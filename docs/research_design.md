# Research Design: Climate Change Discourse Across Digital Registers in Human and LLM Production

## 1. Overview and Research Purpose

This research project investigates the circulation and linguistic constitution of climate change discourses across diverse digital registers and social actors in the contemporary period (2015–2022). It establishes a rigorous, empirically balanced comparative framework between authentic human textual production and corresponding synthetic texts generated by state-of-the-art Large Language Models (LLMs).

By pairing Corpus Linguistics with Multi-Dimensional Analysis (MDA) and discursive analysis, the study assesses how automated linguistic models reproduce, stabilize, shift, or resist the discursive patterns characteristic of human institutional and informal communication regarding the climate crisis.

---

## 2. Research Questions and Objectives

The investigation is guided by four primary research questions:

1. **Circulation of Climate Discourses (RQ1):**
   *What types of climate change discourses are mobilized and circulate across different internet registers by distinct social actors in the contemporary period?*
2. **Human vs. LLM Discursive Differentiation (RQ2):**
   *To what extent do climate change discourses, identified along discursive dimensions across the analyzed registers, distinguish authentic human textual production from corresponding texts generated by Large Language Models (LLMs)?*
3. **Consonances and Discursive Tensions (RQ3):**
   *What consonances (alignments, semantic reinforcements, stabilization of meaning) and tensions (divergences, discursive shifts, resistances) emerge from the systematic comparison between discourses produced by human actors and those generated by LLMs?*
4. **Generation Conditions (RQ4):**
   *How do generic and summary-guided generation differ in their climate change discourses across actors, registers and models?*

### Core Objectives
- Construct a balanced, stratified corpus of contemporary English climate discourse.
- Compare generic and summary-guided generation while keeping persona, register and other shared instructions consistent.
- Quantitatively and qualitatively map linguistic variation across registers, social actors, authorship types, models and generation conditions.

---

## 3. General Corpus Design Principles

The corpus architecture is designed to maximize analytical utility, factorial stability, and comparative validity:

- **Hybrid Stratification (Actor × Register):** Strata are strictly defined by the intersection of *Social Actor* (who produces the discourse) and *Discursive Register* (the genre/communicative variety of the text). Grouping texts solely by social actor without distinguishing communicative register is conceptually invalid (e.g., governmental treaties possess fundamentally distinct functional and linguistic profiles from governmental scientific synthesis reports).
- **Stratum Equilibrium:** Strata are targeted at approximately **200 sampling units** (with empirical adjustments for domain-specific archive ceilings), ensuring robust conditions for stable factor analysis, factor extraction, and lexical comparisons without stratum-size distortion.
- **Paired Generation Conditions:** Each of the **1,191 human units** is assigned one model, which generates one generic and one summary-guided text. Each condition contains **1,191 texts**, giving 1:1 parity with the human subcorpus separately; the combined LLM subcorpus has **2,382 texts**.
- **Methodological Sizing:** The planned total is **3,573 texts**, subject to empirical availability and factorial stability requirements. Combined human-to-LLM parity is 1:2, not 1:1.

```
Total Corpus (N = 3,573 texts)
├── Human Subcorpus (n = 1,191)
│   ├── Government – Minutes / Treaties (n = 84)
│   ├── Government – Synthesis Reports (n = 107)
│   ├── Press – News Reports (n = 200)
│   ├── NGOs – Institutional Stories (n = 200)
│   ├── Academia – Research Abstracts (n = 200)
│   ├── Social Media – Microblog Posts (n = 200 GETTR, provisional)
│   └── Sceptical Blog – Blog Posts (n = 200)
└── LLM Subcorpus (n = 2,382)
    ├── Generic condition (n = 1,191)
    └── Summary-guided condition (n = 1,191)
```

## 5. Sampling Unit Definition and Segmentation Protocol

### 5.1 Rationale for Textual Segmentation
The sampling unit across long-form documents is defined as an **authentic textual segment** rather than an entire document. This dual-criteria decision addresses:
1. **Technical Constraints:** LLMs possess input/output context boundaries and cannot reliably replicate multi-hundred-page treaties or reports verbatim in a single pass.
2. **Analytical Comparability:** Lexical and multidimensional analyses require comparable text lengths and coherent communicative scenes between human source texts and synthetic generations.

### 5.2 Segmentation Procedure
1. Identify high-information, functionally cohesive sections within long-form documents (e.g., Executive Summaries, Introductions, Challenge/Action syntheses).
2. Extract multiple authentic segments per macro-document until reaching the 200-unit threshold per stratum.
3. Formally log precise delimitation tags (`begin` and `end` byte/token offsets) for transparent, automated extraction and reproducibility.
4. Prevent data waste by extracting multiple valid sampling units from high-value scarce sources (e.g., IPCC synthesis reports).

## 6. LLM Subcorpus Architecture and Stratum Allocation

Each human unit is assigned to one model, which produces both conditions for that unit. The matrix below gives the number of human units assigned to each model and, equivalently, the number of outputs **per condition**. Thus each model's combined synthetic output is twice its allocation: ChatGPT 596, Claude 596, Gemini 596 and Grok 594.

- **Cross-Stratum Allocation:** Each stratum is distributed as evenly as possible across the four models. Balance reduces model–register confounding but does not eliminate confounding or establish causation.
- **Paired Conditions:** The same assigned model and compatible persona are used for both outputs. The guided output is linked to its human unit through a summary; the generic output is matched by actor and register, not necessarily by specific facts or topic details.
- **Technical Output Controls:** Use comparable length instructions across conditions and monitor output lengths against the human units; moderate variation is expected.

## 7. Prompt Engineering and Scene Reconstruction

### 7.1 Shared Prompt Design
Both conditions specify a persona compatible with the social actor and register, the climate-change domain, the communicative task and an approximately comparable length. Keep these instructions consistent within each pair without prescribing particular stylistic or ideological traits. Persona, model, instructions and source content can all influence the result; differences cannot be attributed solely to the model.

For example, the shared persona for a Greenpeace institutional story may be an institutional Greenpeace writer or a writer associated with Greenpeace. In both conditions, the model is asked to produce an institutional story; the generated text is synthetic and is not an authentic Greenpeace statement.

### 7.2 Generation Conditions

- **Generic:** Ask the model to freely produce a text in the specified actor and register about climate change, without the source text or its summary. The output is not necessarily matched to the specific facts of its assigned human unit.
- **Summary-guided:** Use the same persona and task, adding a concise neutral summary (at most 100 words) of the corresponding human unit. Ask the model to take the summary into consideration when producing the text in the specified register.

Illustrative paired instructions for a Greenpeace story, with the same length guidance in both:

> **Persona:** You are a writer associated with Greenpeace, producing an institutional story.
>
> **Generic:** Write an institutional story about climate change.
>
> **Summary-guided:** Write an institutional story about climate change taking the following summary into consideration: `<summary>`.

### 7.3 Summary Preparation and Provenance
Use an LLM to prepare one neutral summary per human unit, in its own words rather than copied excerpts. Review summaries against the source for accuracy, missing context and copied wording before generation; retain the reviewed summary for any reuse across models. Record the source-unit ID, summary, summarising model, prompt and version, review decisions, assigned generation model, condition, persona, generation prompt and output. These links support paired analysis and reproducibility without treating content alignment as stylistic similarity.

## 8. Lexical Query Construction and Harvesting Strategy

The search vocabulary for text identification and corpus harvesting is constructed using a high-precision, iterative validation protocol modeled on benchmark linguistic corpora (e.g., Davies' Coronavirus Corpus):

```
Seed Terms (Domain Knowledge)
      │
      ▼
Empirical Validation on Google Scholar (Top 20 results evaluated; keep if ≥ 95% True Positives)
      │
      ▼
Iterative Citation / Keyword Expansion (Extract keywords from highly-cited papers and top 20 citing works)
      │
      ▼
Precision Filtering & Pruning (Prune false-positive generators)
      │
      ▼
Final Query Assembly (Disjunctive Boolean condition: Term A OR Term B OR Term C)
```

### 8.1 Key Criteria
- **True Positive Maximization & False Negative Minimization:** The optimization goal is high precision (≥ 95% relevant hits among top 20 sampled results) and comprehensive recall.
- **Disjunctive Query Logic:** Searches utilize broad boolean disjunction (`OR` logic), avoiding restrictive multi-term conjunctions (`AND`) that bias or artificially narrow the retrieved discourse.

## 9. Analytical Methods and Downstream Processing

1. **Morphosyntactic Annotation:**
   - Preprocessing and Part-of-Speech (POS) tagging via standardized taggers (e.g., TreeTagger, Biber tagger suites).
   - Grammatical, structural, and functional feature extraction.
2. **Multi-Dimensional Analysis (MDA):**
   - Factor analysis on co-occurring linguistic features to identify underlying functional dimensions of variation.
   - Dimension score calculation across all strata and models.
3. **Comparative Discourse Analysis:**
   - Compare human texts separately with generic and summary-guided texts, and directly compare the paired generation conditions across communicative dimensions.
   - Account for model, actor, register and shared source-unit linkage in statistical and qualitative analysis. Generic outputs are actor–register matched; guided outputs are content-aligned through summaries.
   - Examine semantic reinforcements, register conventions and discursive shifts without treating guided content similarity as purely stylistic similarity or inferring causation from balance alone.
