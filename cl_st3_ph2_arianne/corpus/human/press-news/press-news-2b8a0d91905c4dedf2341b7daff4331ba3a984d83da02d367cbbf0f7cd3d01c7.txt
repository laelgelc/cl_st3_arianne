<h> Custom Navigation <h> Climate change and India <p> Despite several international conferences to devise ways to preserve and protect the Earth 's environment and ecology , there has been no appreciable impact on the problem . Global warming continues . A British consultancy firm predicted three years ago that India was among the ' extreme risk ' countries of the world in which the impact of climate change would be keenly felt by 2025 . Kolkata and Mumbai will the two cities in India where impact of climate change would be the highest in the next three decades . The danger is known but the causes that are contributing to the building up of the danger are not being addressed . Destruction of forests goes on . The Ministry of Environment is now nothing more than a costly showpiece because its clearance is no longer necessary where big industrial projects are to be built which involves of destruction of forests . The present paradigm of development considers GDP growth to be more important than the growth of greenery . <p> India 's agriculture , especially the food @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of Nature . Continuous and increasing use of chemical fertilizers to extract more crops from the same land to feed a continuously rising population is gradually reducing soil fertility . A UN report last year predicted that by the middle of this century India would top China as the world 's most populous country . After 2050 China 's population is likely to come down to a billion while India 's will rise to a billion and six hundred million . It is a moot question whether India can feed a population of 1.6 billion by using more and more chemical fertilizers which will reduce soil fertility . Simultaneously , another process is going on . The area of arable land is gradually decreasing -- due to demands of housing and land for setting up factories . <p> The enormity of the danger that is building up on the food front does not seem to have seeped into the minds of the politicians , the policy makers and the general public .
