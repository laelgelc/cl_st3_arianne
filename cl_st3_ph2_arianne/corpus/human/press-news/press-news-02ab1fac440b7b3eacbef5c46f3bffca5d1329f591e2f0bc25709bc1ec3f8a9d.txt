<h> Oilsands will be around for another century , says Suncor CEO <p> Despite unwavering environmental and Indigenous opposition to oilsands expansion , Suncor Energy chief executive Steve Williams predicts the sprawling resource will still produce heavy oil 100 years from now , pointing to bite-sized projects as the next wave of growth . <p> Williams said in an interview that Suncor is gradually " exploding the myth " that the oilsands is a high-cost albatross and an environmental offender . He believes the industry has taken strides on both fronts , noting Suncor can cover its operating costs and dividend at US$37 per barrel this year , besting its rivals . <p> " We 've already got oilsands to a position where ( emissions are ) broadly equivalent to other crudes on this continent , and we 're in a position to take it to an even better position , " he said . <p> " If you think high-cost and carbon intensity are the issues , both of those are being removed as the major issues now . Costs will depend on the size , but they 're expected to carry a tab of roughly $2 billion each . <p> The idea is to design one project and replicate the model for the remaining phases , improving it along the way . This approach cuts costs and scales back the turnaround time from the point of company approval to first oil production , which spanned five to eight years with megaprojects but is expected to last just two or three under this bite-sized style . <p> Suncor expects to decide on whether to start sanctioning these projects late next year or into 2019 . <p> " I expect we 'll see some of these incremental investments in the oilsands as well as ongoing maintenance , " said Jackie Forrest , director of research at ARC Energy Research Institute in Calgary . <p> " But I do n't think these will add up to replacing the kind of investments that we saw in 2014 , in terms of what it means for Alberta .
