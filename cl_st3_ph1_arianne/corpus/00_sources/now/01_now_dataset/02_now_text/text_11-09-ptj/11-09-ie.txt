The annual great exodus from our shores is now in full swing and is reaching its peak as thousands of these summer visitors leave us for warmer climes . It has been estimated that over 100,000 pairs of Barn Swallows breed in Ireland and Britain each summer and that over one million pairs of these birds pass through these islands on migration . <p> September is a time of falling leaves and falling temperatures . As temperatures drop , the number of insects on the wing declines and insect-eating birds like Barn Swallows move on to more bountiful hunting areas . In the past , it was a mystery to people where the birds went to each autumn . Thanks to bird ringing , we now know that many of the Barn Swallows that summer in this part of the world winter in sub-Saharan Africa , some travelling as far south as South Africa . <p> Adult male and female @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ us being the length of their tail streamers ; in males the tail streamers are very long and are wire-thin ( pictured ) , in females they are shorter and broader . In autumn , things are much more relaxed . Time of departure from Ireland for Barn Swallows starts around midAugust and usually peaks around the second or third week of September . Some Irish-born birds are known to cross over to Wales in August , to move on to the south of England and to hang around there for a while before crossing the English Channel at their leisure . <p> Some Barn Swallows do not bother to go all the way to Africa and stay in the Mediterranean or on the north African coast for the winter .
