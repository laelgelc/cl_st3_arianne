I also commend Secretary-General Ban Ki-Moon for his initiative in convening this meeting . <p> It is clear that we are in danger of failing the targets which we set for ourselves at the Millennium Summit 10 years ago . Ten years on , almost one billion people still live in extreme poverty and more than one billion suffer from hunger . <p> In the race to 2015 , many developing countries were lagging behind , even before the global economic crisis . The reasons are many . Inadequate investment in human resource development has left us with weak productive capacity . This , together with an international trading system that treats us as equals when we are @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , more importers than exporters , sustaining jobs elsewhere rather than creating jobs for our own people . <p> Our mounting indebtedness pre-empts the limited resources we have . We are thus not able to devote adequate resources to education , training and infrastructure -- the necessary requirements for investment , job creation and sustained development . <p> Not only is access to financing limited but the cost of borrowing is expensive because we are perceived by our circumstances to be risky borrowers . <p> In our efforts to achieve our millennium targets , we have increased as much as we can our social welfare programmes , but this is , in a sense , illusionary , for it is done at the expense of more self-sustaining , developmental initiatives . Real achievement in the reduction or elimination of poverty is to be measured not by the number of people kept out of poverty by fiscal cash transfers but by the number that escape poverty without the need for such programmes . The millennium goals must therefore be seen as a development imperative , not merely as statistical targets . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ impact of the global crisis on the struggling efforts of developing countries to meet the millennium targets . Most of us are among the least resilient with no surpluses to mount counter-cyclical stimulus initiatives . Many of the gains we made through considerable effort and sacrifice have gone into reverse . For many of us , it is as if we must start all over again . The slow pace of global recovery suggests that it may be some time before we will get going again . <p> This is not the only setback we have suffered since the adoption of the MDGs . The increasing impact of climate change and the costs associated with measures we have had to adopt to counter international terrorism have constrained our progress toward attainment of these goals . <p> What does this mean for us in terms of fulfilling the goals we set 10 years ago ? It requires more than just stock-taking . It requires more than merely appealing for a redoubling of effort . <p> Contraction <p> Make no mistake , without an emergency programme to re-energise the MDG agenda , those @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ fiscal policy requirements attached to the resources that have been made available for developing countries through institutions like the IMF are contracting . <p> They can not assist in meeting our millennium targets in the short run . A way must be found through more concessionary loans and grants and debt-for-equity swaps for the channelling of resources and the fiscal space to support programmes for economic empowerment and capacity building . The Global Partnership for Development aligned to MDG-8 must be reinvigorated . <p> We well appreciate the constraints facing developed countries as a result of the recession . Yet , we urge them not to renege on or postpone their commitments on overseas development assistance . If that was needed when the commitments were made , it is needed even more now . <p> But it needs even more than that . It needs an institutional framework with innovative provisions calibrated toward enabling developing countries to accelerate their development momentum . <p> Time-bind it to create the needed sense of urgency ; establish without excessive bureaucracy clear guidelines for its implementation and monitor them vigilantly . It is a development issue and is correlated to issues such as poverty , investment , competitiveness and job creation . Its links with the large international network of organised crime makes it a matter of global , not just domestic , concern . We urge that this be tackled within the context of the Global Partnership for Development and its incorporation into the existing MDG framework . <p> I continue to appeal for special consideration to be given to small and vulnerable economies like Jamaica which are classified as middle-income countries and therefore not eligible for certain concessionary and development financing .
