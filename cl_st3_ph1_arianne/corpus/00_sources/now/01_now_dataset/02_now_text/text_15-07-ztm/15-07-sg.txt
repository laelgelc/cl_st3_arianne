@@3478408 <p> Work by Singapore scientists on the long-extinct woolly mammoth is opening the door to resurrecting this ice age creature from the dead . <p> The team has produced , for the first time , a high-resolution genome or genetic blueprint of the giant mammal . <p> This is the first of many steps which would make it possible to bring it to life , said Nanyang Technological University 's ( NTU ) Dr Stephan Schuster , who is part of the Singapore-United States team involved in the effort . <p> " I think there is a very deep connection between humans and mammoths . In the past , they provided meat , fur to cover houses , and there were settlements built using the ribcages of the mammoths , " he said . <p> The team has already worked out the gene which gives the mammoth its characteristic fuzzy coat , and is looking at what the other 1,600 genes in the creature 's DNA do . <p> This information would provide an instruction manual of sorts on how to change the DNA of the mammoth 's @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ it more mammoth-like . <p> Professor George Church , a professor of genetics at Harvard Medical School in the US , is keen to do just that . <p> He and his team hope to make hybrids of Asian elephants and mammoths , which would be able to survive in the Arctic 's minus 40 deg C temperatures . <p> " This could save the Asian elephants from extinction due to negative interactions with people , such as farmers , " he explained . <p> The massive woolly mammoths - which could weigh over 6 tonnes and measure more than 4m in length - roamed the frozen tundra of northern Asia , Europe and North America .
