@@316544 <h> Environment Sunday Sermon <p> By Dr Devanesan Nesiah <p> This week is the 21st Anniversary of the first Earth Summit held in Rio de Janeiro , in the first week of June 1992 . Even as World leaders and Environmental experts were addressing ecological problems at that meeting , we in Sri Lanka were in the grips of floods , storms and earth slips , which were symptoms of the global crisis triggered mostly by human activities . This crisis includes global warming and erratic weather patterns , alternating floods and droughts , storms and natural disasters , crop failures and food shortages , air water and soil pollution , sea level rise and fresh water shortages , and the continued loss of bio-diversity . The consequences include the progressive deterioration of the quality of human life and other life forms . Many great rivers will dry up and many lands will go under the sea . <p> What do we learn from our scriptures and our traditions in understanding and responding to these developments ? Thomas Aquinas and many other theologians have favoured a human centred , hierarchical interpretation that has been labelled Dominionship on account of the dominance assigned to humans in that model . In the words of Aquinas , " Imperfect beings serve @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ from the earth , Animals feed on plants and these in turn serve man 's use ... lifeless beings exist for living beings , plants for animals and the latter for man ... the whole material nature exists for man " . <p> Other theologians , scholars and environmentalists have suggested that the Dominionship interpretation of the scriptures has contributed to over consumption , over use of resources , environmental pollution and the ecological crisis we are caught up in . In the Stewardship interpretation favoured by St Augustine , many centuries before Aquinas , humans are merely stewards who may not exercise dominion to further their own interest at the expense of the rest of creation . In recent decades there has been a gradual shift back from the dominionship to the stewardship school , towards seeing the relationship between humans and the rest of creation as unequal but not vertical ; we are not rulers but have lead roles and special responsibilities . Other components of creation also have key roles and unique contributions . One of the central lessons of the Great Flood is of God 's commitment to a covenant with us and the rest of creation . Although God 's commitment is not qualified , a covenant by definition is a reciprocal contract or agreement . God expects from all creation , and particularly from the human population , a similar commitment to that covenant .
