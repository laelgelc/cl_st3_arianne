@@3680472 <h> Helping farmers cope with El Ni ? o <p> Droughts are recurring phenomena which adversely impact agriculture from time to time . They are associated with the so-called El Ni ? o climate events which bring droughts to us in the Western Pacific but dump heavy snow and rains in the Americas in the eastern side of the Pacific Ocean . <p> According to the latest forecast of the US Weather Service , this year 's El Ni ? o will likely be the second most severe since record-keeping began in 1950 . <p> Unfortunately , with global warming and climate change , El Ni ? os and droughts are predicted to become more severe and occur more frequently . We therefore need to institute measures to help farmers cope with drought . <p> There are at least two long-term , permanent ways by which government can help farmers cope with El Ni ? o ( drought ) : <p> 1 ) Providing reliable , timely forecasts , and <p> 2 ) Investing heavily in small-scale irrigation systems . <p> Actually lack of water is a natural @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Since farmers know when the rains come , how much and for how long , they know which crop species/varieties are best for their areas and the optimum time to sow . <p> The planting calendar and farming practices which farmers have developed by trial and error over generations are resilient enough to take care of normal variations on the onset of the rains , the volume as well as duration of the rainy season . The problem begins when the rains depart from the average by 25% or more ( the conventional definition of drought ) . The usual practices wo n't suffice anymore and farmers must take remedial measures to mitigate the impact of drought . <p> Weather Forecasting : Early Warning , the Key <p> Farmers can not prevent drought but they have various ways of dealing with it . As long as farmers are forewarned they can adopt measures to protect their crops and/or embark on alternative enterprises to sustain their livelihoods . <p> For example , if a drought is anticipated , farmers can switch to planting early or short maturing varieties . Early varieties are @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ able to avoid the drought which often comes at the tail of the growing season . <p> In the case of the rice farmers in Bulacan who rely on Angat Dam for their water , since water for the second rice crop is not forthcoming , they can plan on planting mungo which can survive with residual moisture after the first rice crop . <p> Another way farmers can alleviate impact of drought is by the use of farm straws/wastes and plastic sheets as mulches to reduce water loss . Specially for high value vegetables , the extra cost of plastic mulch can be easily be offset by higher yield and higher selling price of produce due to tight supply . <p> The first order of business for the Department of Agriculture ( DA ) and Department of Science and Technology ( DOST ) therefore is to jointly develop a robust , timely weather forecasting system to advise farmers what to expect . <p> However , general broad weather forecasts are not good enough . In order to be effective the weather forecasts need to be timely , more detailed and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is divided into at least four broad climate regimes according to the Corona Climate Classification System . The timing and the severity of drought will be different in regions with two pronounced seasons , dry and wet ( Type I ) ; regions with no dry season ( Type II ) ; regions with short dry season ( Type III ) , and regions with evenly distributed rainfall throughout the year ( Type IV ) . <p> Moreover , the consequences of drought could vary also within individual farms . The fields on the highest part of the topographic sequence will suffer water stress earlier than the fields at the bottom . <p> Also , fields with sandy soils which have little water holding capacity are more susceptible to drought than fields with clayey soils . <p> Small Scale Irrigation Systems <p> The second major initiative to help farmers cope with drought on a permanent , sustained basis is greater investments in the countryside on small scale irrigation systems . These could be small water impounding projects ( SWIPs ) , small diversion dams ( SWDS ) , small farm reservoirs @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and pump irrigation systems . <p> Small scale irrigation systems are not only cheaper than large-scale irrigation systems per hectare of service area but also they are quicker to install . They also provide the farmer more precise control of delivery of water which is vital for multiple cropping and diversification into more valuable crops like fruits , vegetables and even ornamentals . <p> In 1999 of the 1.458 million hectares of irrigated lands , 119,000 hectares were serviced by small irrigation systems . Since then our efforts to develop small irrigation systems have been feeble . For the period ( 2001-2015 ) , the Bureau of Soils and Water Management ( BSWM-DA ) which is responsible for developing small irrigation systems , added only 8,100 hectares . For 2015 the target service area is almost negligible at 5,856 hectares . <p> These targets are minuscule considering the vast potential for SWIPs in the uplands as well as supplementary water sources for the irrigated lowlands served by national and communal irrigation systems . <p> In the uplands with rolling to hilly terrain , there are countless depressions and inland valleys across @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ water for irrigation , for fish culture and recreation as well as for control of soil erosion . <p> The Department of Agriculture ( DA ) therefore , should direct a bigger slice of its General Appropriations ( GAA ) into the BSWM for the development of small irrigation systems . <p> In order to really make a difference , the target should be ramped up to 30,000-40,000 hectares of small irrigation systems a year . However , since BSWM has severe constraint in manpower , it should enter into collaborative projects with the state universities and colleges ( SUCs ) in the region to help organize farmers into irrigators associations .
