@@20039297 <p> " Global growth will be hindered by rising operational costs as global temperatures rise . " This was the conclusion that the Schroders Economics Team arrived at having examined how climate change will shape the global economy.1 Climate change has environmental , social , political , and economic repercussions that directly impact business operations , competitiveness and profits . <p> Building climate resilience can help businesses avoid what Mark Carney , Governor of the Bank of England , described as " the tragedy of the horizon " .2 That tragedy , far from being on the horizon , has already impacted heavily on many businesses in Asia . For instance , the 2011 flooding in Thailand hit the manufacturing industry hard . Tourism , housing and the financial sector were also heavily affected . Overall , approximately 90 per cent of the damage and losses were borne by the private sector.3 <p> Many Asian countries are vulnerable or very vulnerable to the impacts of climate change ( Figure 1 ) . Furthermore , there is evidence of prominent increases in the intensity and/or frequency of many existing extreme events , such as heatwaves , floods , droughts , rapid snowmelt , storm surges and rising sea levels . <p> These climatic events can lead to , or exacerbate , supply chain disruption , raw material and commodity price increases , water scarcity , employee health impacts , forced displacement , political unrest , and more . Examples of climate-related supply chain disruption and global price increases were evident when PC hard drive supplies4 were affected by the 2011 floods in Thailand , and also in the economic losses of USD 15 billion when Russian wheat supplies were affected by a heatwave and the associated drought and wildfires . Therefore , companies operating in this region , or relying on supply chains within it , need to develop climate resilience strategies to manage the risks , and to allow them to seize opportunities when they present themselves . <p> Climate resiliency refers to the ability to withstand and recover from disruptive climatic events , and the capacity of a system to adapt and persevere in the face of change . Building climate resilience is now increasingly recognized as an equally important and complementary response to efforts that reduce greenhouse gas ( GHG ) emissions . However , climate resiliency is often overlooked by businesses , as despite being aware of the risks of climate change , many do not perceive that they are vulnerable to them.5 <p> The Financial Stability Board ( FSB ) set up the industry-led Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures ( TCFD ) . In June of this year , the Task Force published a set of consistent climate-related financial disclosure recommendations6 for use by companies in providing consistent and comparable information to investors , lenders and insurance underwriters about their climate-related financial risks . <p> FSB Chair Mark Carney said , " The Task Force 's recommendations have been developed by the market for the market . They set out the disclosures that a wide range of users and preparers of financial filings have said are essential to understanding a company 's climate-related risks and opportunities . Widespread adoption will provide investors , banks and insurers with that information , helping minimize the risk that market adjustments to climate change will be incomplete , late and potentially destabilizing . " <p> Climate resilience requires a robust climate risk assessment and strategy , which can be incorporated into business continuity planning . As a first step , businesses need to acknowledge that climate change is a business risk . They then need to assess , and build awareness of , the climate vulnerability and risks they could face , as well as potential response options . Finally , they must develop and implement a climate resilience strategy and action plan , which should be monitored , reviewed , and reported on . <p> Carrefour conducts climate-related risk assessments7 at the country level and at the micro level , focusing on direct damages and losses at specific branches or locations with a particular focus on ' sensitive ' sites . These allowed Carrefour to identify water scarcity in Brazil as a key risk , and introduce measures to reduce water consumption levels including the formation of a committee and an online tool to monitor store consumption in real time . A related campaign to raise employee awareness on the issue helped to ensure the strategy was operational at all levels of the business . Water consumption levels fell by 20 per cent in 25 stores . Carrefour Brazil is now being used as an example of best practice across the entire group . <p> In addition to reducing exposure to risk , developing climate resilience strategies can also mean using the findings from vulnerability and risk assessments in combination with innovation to explore new opportunities , create value and increase profits . <p> Unilever is considering how climate change in general , and water scarcity in particular , is affecting its customers.8 As a result , it is developing innovative products , such as detergents that use less water , to address a social need while simultaneously exploiting an opportunity and minimizing its exposure to climate risk . Unilever report that sales are growing and they have a leading position in some markets . A programme Unilever introduced during Vietnam 's worst drought in 90 years saw sales of its Comfort One Rinse detergent grow by 27 per cent compared to the average market growth of 2 per cent over the same period . <p> A sound understanding of how climate change could affect your business and the introduction , or enhancement , of measures for resilience needs to become a key aspect of business strategy -- particularly when operations take place in vulnerable locations . @@20297110 <p> China has the world 's largest green bond market , with more than RMB 200 billion ( US$ 30.3 billion ) worth of green bonds issued in 2016 alone -- almost 40 percent of green bonds issued globally that year . <p> Policymakers are providing financial support and governmental incentives for green bonds issuers in order to attract foreign investors and encourage CSR related projects . The Climate Bonds Initiative ( CBI ) estimates that the annual issuance of green bonds rose from RMB 21 billion ( US$3.2 billion ) in 2012 to RMB 559 billion ( US$84.5 billion ) in 2016 . <p> The policy support and market growth provides foreign investors and businesses operating in China with a wide range of opportunities : foreign investors can profit the support of a state-run program , while China-based businesses can fund eco-friendly projects with relative ease . <h> The Importance of Green Bonds <p> Conventional sources of capital , such as banks and governments , struggle to meet investors ' expectations and satisfy the amount of capital required to set certain projects in motion . External funds , such as green bonds , can represent a powerful tool to make China more green . <p> The People 's Bank of China ( PBOC ) has made it clear that public investment alone is not enough to meet the funding requirements needed to drive green and environmentally-friendly solutions , and has set out to make green bonds a powerful financial alternative to attract foreign investors . <p> China 's environment paid a high price for the country 's rapid economic ascent , bringing air and water pollution to alarming levels over the span of just a few decades . Interestingly , this ongoing threat has yielded opportunities for eco-friendly companies not only to thrive financially , but also play a role in the efforts to combat environmental stress . Therefore , green bonds represent a useful means to combine the government 's efforts with private investment and assist and alleviate the cleanup operation . <p> Sustainable urban planning has been identified as one of the primary methods to accomplish this : rethinking how cities can conserve energy is becoming less of a solution , with developing eco-cities a more effective option for cutting carbon emissions . Regional governments benefit from green bonds as they can help to finance and develop local green infrastructure . As a result , the demand for companies able to provide cities with the latest green-related technology is increasing rapidly . <p> The benefits of supporting green bonds are manifold . Public authorities ' efforts to support sustainable initiatives will allow the country to gain international recognition for green investment and growth , while companies have the chance to implement CSR activities . Green bonds will help businesses empower R&amp;D departments financially , potentially allowing the Chinese market to become one of the most eco-friendly in the world . <h> Regulation <p> China supports the development of green bonds at both a local and national level . Most green bond issuers are headquartered in 14 cities , and more than 80 percent of bonds were issued in Shanghai , Fuzhou , and Beijing for projects in green industries such as clean energy , clean transport , and resource recycling . <p> Depending on the type of issuer , regulation is supervised by the PBOC , which regulates green bonds issued by financial entities , and the National Development and Reform Commission ( NDRC ) , which regulates bonds issued by other corporate entities that do not have listed equity . <p> The regulation requires companies to submit a ' commitment letter ' to CSRC confirming the intention of using raised funds solely for green projects conforming to its guidelines . Companies in industries that have high emissions or energy usage , such as in the oil industry , will not be allowed to issue green bonds . <p> The CSRC 's new guidelines will apply to all green bonds . They state that there are regulators for each category of issuer , and recommend the use of third-party auditors and certifiers to speed up the process . Finally , the guidelines encourage rating agencies to evaluate companies ' performances in order to assess the issuer 's credit risk . <p> The process to become a green bond issuer are as follows : <p> First , companies are required to disclose their projects and the details of its assets ( the same process is undertaken by normal bonds issuers ) ; <p> A company can then chose independent review to secure its reputation or submit details of the project directly to the relevant regulator according to its nature ( see the table below ) ; <p> After being approved , the company can start to issue green bonds . <p> Periodic reporting on the use of proceeds and undistributed proceeds is also required . The issuer should disclose the development status of green projects and their environmental benefits on an annual basis . <p> Though several provincial and municipal governments have stepped up their efforts to follow and support the central government 's green policies , many companies have limited knowledge of green bonds . Special task forces aimed at supporting companies on the green bond issuance process have been set up in first tier cities such as Shanghai and Beijing , but the lack of awareness of opportunities for eco-friendly projects is still too high . <p> Potential issuers may be unaware that green bonds can be a valid tool to re-finance existing eco-friendly projects or assets , or that green bonds are not only issued to green enterprises . As long as the project can be qualified as a green project , green bonds are available to any type of company . For example , a power producer based in Tianjin was allowed to issue " green bonds " worth RMB 1 billion ( US$151 million ) to finance a coal-fired power plant in June . <h> Utilizing government incentives <p> The Chinese government has been making efforts to attract green investors and support green projects at a time when many companies are increasingly taking action to combat climate change . A deeper understanding and holistic approach is required to face this issue ; a different approach that can bring tax incentives and government support , as well as added competitive advantage . <p> Chinese citizens are showing a strong interest in energy-saving and eco-friendly solutions . Companies that will address such interests will not just only be rewarded by the government and potential investors , but by a fast-growing green market . <p> Tax incentives , funding opportunities , government support , and the high demand for these kind of products and services create the right scenario for successful business .
