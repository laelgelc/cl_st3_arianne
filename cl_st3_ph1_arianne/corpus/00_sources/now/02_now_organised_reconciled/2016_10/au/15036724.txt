( This year has been warmer across the globe , but the El Nino cycle is over . ) <p> Scientists warn that as the planet warms due to climate change , bleaching of many types of coral is expected to become more frequent and severe . <p> Coral bleaching occurs when the stressed coral host ejects the tiny marine algae , known as zooxanthellae , that lives inside its tissue and gives it its colour and the bulk of the energy needed for it to grow and reproduce . <p> Most corals rely on zooxanthellae to feed . Without it they start to starve and their tissue becomes transparent . <p> Corals can regain their zooxanthellae and colour if the temperature returns to normal , though some never fully recover . When heat stress continues for eight weeks or more , bleached coral often die . If the mortality rate on a reef is high it can take reefs years or decades to recover . <p> There was historically significant coral bleaching at reefs across much of the globe last year .
