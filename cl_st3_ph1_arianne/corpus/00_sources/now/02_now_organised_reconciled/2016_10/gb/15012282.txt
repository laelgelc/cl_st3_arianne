<h> Steel chiefs to meet government for crunch talks <p> Steel bosses are set for a crunch meeting with Greg Clark , the new Business Secretary , this week as they lay out to him the extent of the crisis the industry is in . <p> The heads of some of Britain 's biggest steelmakers will meet the new Secretary of State for Business , Energy and Industrial Strategy on Wednesday for the first time . Also scheduled to attend is Nick Hurd , minister of state for climate change and industry . <p> The steel chiefs plan to use the talks to detail the knife-edge the industry finds itself on , with thousands of jobs in the sector and its supply chain having been lost over the past year . <p> Redcar-based SSI collapsed into administration last October and was soon followed by Caparo , while Tata put its entire UK steel business up for sale in March , before pausing the disposal as it explored a merger with Germany 's ThyssenKrupp that many fear could lead to closures of British steel plants . <p> Key subjects @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ part of the Government 's forthcoming industrial strategy , with support such as encouraging British-made steel for public procurement projects . <p> The high energy prices the power-intensive industry pays compared with European rivals and calls for a reformed tax regime which encourages rather than punishes capital investment in new equipment , are also set to feature . <p> Stronger trade defences to protect steelmakers from state-backed mills in China dumping excess production in the UK are also likely to be up for discussion . These tariffs are currently imposed on a Europe-wide basis and Mr Clark 's predecessor was reluctant to press for harsh levies to discourage dumping , claiming it could harm UK companies which buy cheaper foreign steel . <p> Trade levies are in focus after the European Commission last week called for an overhaul of tariffs so they mimic tough measures imposed by the US -- which has imposed duties of 500pc in some cases -- saying the current measures are no longer effective . <p> Gareth Stace , director of trade body UK Steel , said : " We will look forward to a positive meeting @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ support this vital industry and make Mr Clark aware of our concerns about what needs to be done to ensure it is sustainable .
