This one was completely black . <p> The black dormouse found in the Blackdown Hills <p> The little creature was carefully manoeuvered into a plastic handling bag so the resident project officer , Conrad Barrowclough , could take a closer look . The animal 's furry tail confirmed it was indeed a dormouse , and further inspection revealed it was male , aged between one and five years , and weighed a healthy 19.5g . But why was it black ? <p> DNA analysis of a fur sample has confirmed that the Blackdown dormouse 's unusual colour is down to a quirk of genetics -- the species has a rare recessive gene that causes it to grow black fur . Dormice , a priority species in the UK Biodiversity Action Plan , are vulnerable to extinction , and numbers in England and Wales have declined by 38 per cent since 2000 , according to the State of Britain 's Dormice report published in September . <p> The factors affecting dormice numbers are well documented -- woodland management , woodland connectivity and climate change -- and efforts to boost populations focus on the first two . <p> An ideal dormouse habitat and feeding ground is " early successional vegetation " , says the UK 's official dormouse officer , Ian White , of the People 's Trust For Endangered Species . This means the understorey of new life that grows when woodland is cut down . Dormice also need plenty of hedgerows to let them move between patches of woodland in safety . The latter fall into the Muridae family , which includes some 1,380 species of mouse and rat , while dormice are the only species in the family Myoxidae . <p> Dormice have double-jointed hind ankles . <p> The presence of dormice can be identified by the distinctive way they eat hazelnuts : they leave a hole in the nut with a smooth inner rim . This effort typically takes a dormouse around 20 minutes . <p> When dormice hibernate , at this time of year , their heart and breathing rates can drop by 90 ? per cent . <p> Apart from hazel dormice , the only other species in the UK is the much larger fat -- or " edible " -- dormouse , so called because they were a tasty delicacy in Roman times .
