<h> Lifetime of Scotland 's nuclear plants could be extended , says EDF <p> EDF 's Scottish Business Director suggests that depending on assessments of how the plants age , their life span could be extended <p> Nuclear power plant - Fotolia <p> It may be possible to extend the operation of Scotland 's nuclear plants past their current projected lifespans , according to their operator . <p> Scotland has two nuclear power stations , both run by French state-owned energy company EDF -- Torness , based in East Lothian , and Hunterston , in North Ayreshire . Hunterston began operating in 1976 and is due to go offline in 2023 , while Torness went online in 1988 , and is due to close in 2030 . <p> But Paul Winkle , EDF 's Scottish Business Director , has suggested that depending on assessments of how the plants age , their life span could be extended . <p> Speaking at an EDF fringe at the SNP conference , Winkle said : " The current life for Hunterston is 2023 and Torness is 2030 , and that is based @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ being absolutely sure that when they are shut down they are still safe to operate . <p> " But to go beyond that we will do assessments and it may be possible to make some small further extensions , but we will not operate them beyond when we are confident they are safe to operate . Our current estimate is , with Hunterston , we get to a point where , if we go beyond 2023 there will be uncertainty . We will do more analysis in due course . Those dates are based on our best judgement . " <p> Torness has originally been expected to close in 2023 , but had its lifetime extended by seven years back in February . Hunterston had expected to close in 2011 , but had its lifetime extended until 2017 . Then in 2012 EDF gave approval for operation to continue until 2023 . <p> As well as running two plants in Scotland , EDF is also behind plans for the controversial Hinkely Point nuclear station , which was given the go-ahead by Prime Minister Theresa May earlier this month . <p> Winkle @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ years ' time , and Torness will be close in about 15 years ' time , and they are producing a large proportion of Scotland ' electricity .
