<p> Image copyrightLeigh StearnsImage caption An investigation has been launched into Prof Gordon Hamilton 's death <p> The family of a Dundee-born scientist who died in Antarctica said they were " immensely proud " of his achievements . <p> Dr Gordon Hamilton , 50 , was killed after his snowmobile plunged 100ft ( 30.48m ) into a crevasse on Saturday . <p> His body was later recovered and the US Antarctic Program has launched an investigation into his death . <p> Dr Hamilton 's family , including his wife Fiona and children Martin and Calum , said in a statement they were " devastated " by his death . <p> Dr Hamilton was a University of Maine professor in the School of Earth and Climate Sciences , and a researcher with the Climate Change Institute . <p> He was part of a team camped in a heavily crevassed area known as the Shear Zone , around 25 miles ( 40.23 km ) south of McMurdo Station , the largest of the three US research stations in Antarctica . <h> ' Immensely proud ' <p> Dr Hamilton 's family @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Harris Academy . <p> After leaving school , he attended Aberdeen University and gained a PhD from Cambridge University . <p> His family said : " During this time he had his first experiences of polar exploration that went on to form such an important part of his life and career . <p> " Although Gordon had worked abroad in Norway and the USA for more than 20 years , he maintained a love of Scotland , always eager to find out the Dundee FC score or to hear how the Scotland rugby team was getting on . " <p> Dr Hamilton visited Scotland in July to celebrate his 50th birthday . <p> His family said he visited the Oor Wullie Bucket Trail in Dundee and saw a concert in Stornaway by Runrig , a band he had followed since his schooldays . <p> The statement said : " His wife Fiona , children Martin and Calum in America , Gordon 's family in the UK and friends throughout the world are all devastated at his death . <p> " However , they are immensely proud of all that he achieved @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Colleagues also paid tribute to the scientist following the tragedy . <p> Paul Mayewski , director of the University of Maine 's Climate Change Institute , said : " You knew that if Gordon came into the tent , that things were going to be fun and pleasant . <p> " They were repeating an activity that they 'd done many times before , but it 's a dangerous area and accidents happen . That 's exactly what this was . " <p> Dr Hamilton spent much of his time in Greenland and Antarctica studying the movement and melting of glaciers and its effect on rising sea levels . <p> The National Science Foundation , which was funding Dr Hamilton 's research , is arranging the return of his body to the United States . <p> Kelly K Falkner , director of the division 's polar programs , said : " The death of one of our colleagues is a tragic reminder of the risks we all face , no matter how hard we work at mitigating those risks , in field research .
