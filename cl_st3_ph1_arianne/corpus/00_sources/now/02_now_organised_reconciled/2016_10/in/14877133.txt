It had not even given any definite time-line for reducing the HFCs ' use . But at Kigali , India showed flexibility by proposing a four-year advancement of the baseline to 2024-2026 and 10% reduction by 2032 . It also linked increase in its ambition with those of the developed countries . <p> " It is good to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ can make big difference to ambitious and equitable outcome on climate change " , said Sunita Narain , environmentalist and director general of the Delhi-based think-tank Centre for Science and Environment ( CSE ) . <p> She also noted that the agreement clearly reflects the principal of common but differentiated responsibility ( CBDR ) -- where developed countries will have to take enhanced actions keeping in view their historical responsibility of using pollutants over the years . <p> " The Agreement also reflects the emerging reality of a world in which China will have to take more and more responsibility to solve global environmental issues , " said Narain , referring to the move to put China in a different category for phase down actions . <p> Acting pro-actively a day before reaching the agreement , India in Kigali announced to make it mandatory for its domestic producers to capture and incinerate the HFC-23 ( the most potent among the group of 19 HFCs ) so that it can not get released into atmosphere .
