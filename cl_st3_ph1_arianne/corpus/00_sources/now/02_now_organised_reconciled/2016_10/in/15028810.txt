<h> Why the Sustainable Development Goals are important for Indian cities <p> Urbanisation has been receiving significant importance globally as cities continue to attract human and economic resources . At the time of writing this article , the HABITAT-III Conference is underway in Quito , Ecuador . Experts from across the globe are discussing the details of a ' New Urban Agenda ' , which pledges to create sustainable cities and human settlement for all . Deliberations on the sustainability of cities have also become important in the context of the Sustainable Development Goals ( SDGs ) , which 193 countries , including India , have agreed to adopt starting January 2016 . Cities and urban areas have received specific focus under Goal-11 of the SDGs , which aims to create inclusive , safe , resilient and sustainable human settlements . <p> The challenges of urbanisation in the 21st century have many facets . The World Cities Report 2016 by UN Habitat points to the inability of today 's cities to respond to emerging challenges such as climate change , inequality in accessing services , resources and opportunities @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ negative social , economic and environmental impacts . Many , if not all , of these challenges are experienced by urban India . A glance at the SDGs shows that these are the same challenges that the SDGs and their respective targets intend to address . Thus it is not only Goal-11 , but all the other 16 goals that matter to Indian cities . <p> Though perceived as being ambitious for developing countries , the SDGs can become important yardsticks for Indian cities at a time when main urban sector programmes envision ' sustainability ' as the panacea . India is expected to become 50% urban by 2050 and is banking on its cities for sustained economic growth . Indian cities are growing bigger in size and number , and the 17 SDGs can provide meaningful policy directions at the national level . In fact , two important aspects of the SDG discourse are the emphasis on the need for localising implementation and recognition of the cross-cutting nature of the goals . <p> India has responded to the call for action demanded by the SDGs by identifying the National Institution @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ nodal agency for co-ordinating the country 's efforts in achieving the SDG targets . Niti Aayog has mapped various ministries at the national level for each of the 17 SDGs . The ministry of urban development has been identified as the nodal ministry for Goal-11 . It is also a part of a list of ' other concerned ministries ' for realising the SDGs related to ending poverty , achieving gender equality , promoting sustainable economic growth , reducing inequality , and ensuring sustainable consumption and production . This mapping is indicative of the interlinked nature of the SDGs . In order to translate this mapping into institutional practices , the involved ministries will be required to co-ordinate their actions , bring convergence in their mandates and design suitable sectoral policies . <p> At present , India does not have an urban development policy at the national level , though separate policies for urban transport , urban sanitation , among others , exist . Urban sector development is broadly guided by centrally designed guidelines for specific programmes such as the Smart Cities Mission , Atal Mission for Rejuvenation and Urban Transformation @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Most of these programme guidelines mention ' sustainability ' as a part of their visions . However , imperative questions such as what facets of urban sustainability do these programmes want to achieve and how to measure them have not been articulated clearly . For example , sustainability can not be gauged only through economic success and environmental efficiency in a city . It also includes gender equality -- an aspect about which the mainstream urban development discourse in India largely remains silent , except for limited interventions by non-governmental and international organisations . <p> India 's urban sector initiatives have also been criticised for measuring outputs in terms of physical infrastructure production , and not measuring outcomes , i.e. , how much progress has been made towards achieving the original objective . For example , the present evaluation mechanisms are inadequate to measure whether the length of a newly laid water supply pipeline has actually increased people 's access to water . <p> For long-term impact , initiatives for achieving urban sustainability must go beyond a programmatic approach and be embedded in robust policy and regulatory instruments . There @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ been historically left out of urban sector initiatives , such as food security , gender equality , biodiversity , peri-urban dynamics , among others . Urban planning Acts and guidelines are critical tools in this regard . India also needs to re-initiate its efforts in formulating a comprehensive national-level urban development policy . This policy should ensure that sustainable urban development includes aspects beyond the scope of municipal service delivery mandates . The current SDG discourse and related research provide a vast pool of knowledge resources to practically attempt this . <p> A number of agencies under and outside the auspices of the United Nations are working towards making the SDGs implementable and measurable . Interesting studies are being conducted to bring forth evidences and model relationships between different SDGs , such as the Millennium Institute 's tool to simulate policy coherence and integration among the SDGs . Significant research is also being done to design measurable indicators for the SDG targets , such as the Inter Agency Expert Group 's study on SDG indicators .
