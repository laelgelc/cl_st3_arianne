This led to a series of water-sharing negotiations that ultimately culminated in the Indus Water Treaty ( IWT ) brokered by the World Bank . <p> In spite of its positional advantage , India has in the past discounted water as a military tool , even in times of war . <p> In fact , India lacks the kind of hydrological infrastructure on western rivers essential for any adventurous manoeuvring of waters in the eventuality of any hostility with Pakistan . <p> India has always handled security and water issues with Pakistan separately . <p> The present attempt @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ complicate the relations with Pakistan and might set off a spiral of discontent and mistrust between the two countries . <p> Safeguard the Treaty A section of the Pakistan establishment already looks at water-sharing with India through the prism of Kashmir . <p> India has to reject this type of dogma to safeguard its political and diplomatic interests with other co-riparian states like China , Bangladesh and Nepal . <p> Chinese hydro plants on the Brahmaputra are already a cause for concern . Linking water to security might suit the interests of hawks in India and Pakistan but India , an emerging power aspiring for a seat in the UNSC , should safeguard rather than violate bilateral treaties . <p> Notwithstanding PM Narendra Modi 's dismissal of any prospect of waging a " water war " with Pakistan , the sabre-rattling continues unabated in the media .
