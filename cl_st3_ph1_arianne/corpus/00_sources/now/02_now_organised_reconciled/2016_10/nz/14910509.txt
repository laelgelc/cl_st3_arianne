<h> Plankton blooms east of NZ evidence of climate change , say Victoria University scientists <p> Satellite observations show coccolith blooms are moving south as the ocean warms and becomes less turbulent , a movement similar to 130,000 years ago . Photo / Nasa <p> White blooms of plankton appearing east of New Zealand suggest the ocean is responding to climate change , according to research by Victoria University scientists . <p> Doctorate student Bella Duncan investigated coccolithophores , a white algae with remains known as coccoliths , as part of her master 's degree . <p> Under favourable , warm conditions , these algae grow rapidly and turn the ocean 's surface milky white , a natural phenomenon known as a " bloom " . <p> Ms Duncan says satellite and ship-based observations show coccolith blooms are moving south as the ocean warms and becomes less turbulent . She says the movement is similar to 130,000 years ago , when the last major warm period occurred . <p> " Our results show that during that last warm period , when the ocean was about one to two @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ mainly made up of coccoliths , " she said . <p> Professor Lionel Carter , who co-supervised the research , said coccoliths were deposited on the ocean floor and formed white mud similar to the White Cliffs of Dover . <p> The iconic British landmark was also formed during a warm period 70 million years ago . <p> " The same process appears to be happening now , suggesting the New Zealand ocean is currently responding the present phase of global warming , " Prof Carter said . <p> " This is also a trend that has been increasingly observed in other parts of the world . " <p> Prof Carter said the ramifications on fish stocks , uptake of carbon dioxide and general ocean health had yet to be evaluated , but " it is clear that change is under way " . <p> The study has been published in the international journal Global and Planetary Change .
