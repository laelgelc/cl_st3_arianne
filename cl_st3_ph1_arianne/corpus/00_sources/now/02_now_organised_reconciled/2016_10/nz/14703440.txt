<h> Better scientific understanding leads to pro-environment politics <p> Massey University doctor of psychology Aaron Drummond has been researching how scientific understanding affects environmental political views . <p> Support for pro-enviromental policies can be increased by a better understanding of how science works , researchers say . <p> Massey University psychology lecturer Dr Aaron Drummond was part of an international team that studied how scientific knowledge affects opinions about environmental politics . <p> The researchers found people supportive of scientific inquiry showed more support for pro-environmental policies . By comparison , people who were knowledgeable about scientific subjects and facts , but not the philosophy of scientific inquiry , did not show the same tendency . <p> " You need public support in a democracy to enact any level of change . So the critical issue is the divide between what is thought by climate scientists ' perspective , compared to that lower level of public support . The study had not found data to show the percentage of New Zealanders who think climate change is an issue , but he estimated an educated guess would be 70 to 80 per cent . <p> The researchers also carried out experiments that showed opinions about pro-environmental policy could be influenced by " brief intervention " . A sample of people given a fact sheet about the value of scientific inquiry then showed a " modest increase in endorsement of scientific inquiry and also of pro-environmental policy " . <p> Interestingly , the subjects ' socio-political attitudes had little bearing on this change in stance .
