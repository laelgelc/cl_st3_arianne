<p> As Canada moves forward with its plan to legalize marijuana , government officials have at least one international conundrum to sort out : what to do about the global treaties Canada has signed that prohibit making pot legal ? <p> A senior government official said there are essentially two options available . <p> On the one hand , Canada could take a " principled stand " in favour of the international legalization of pot . <p> The other , quieter approach , would be to withdraw from the treaties and attempt to re-enter with a special exemption for legalized marijuana . <p> It 's the second option , causing the " least international turbulence , " that the federal government favours , said the source . <p> But at least one Canadian researcher believes that would be a missed opportunity for Canada . <p> Steven Hoffman , a professor at the University of Ottawa , is co-author of a paper that identified two treaties Canada 's new pot policy is expected to violate : <p> In Bolivia , there is a traditional practice of chewing coca leaf @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ convention considers a narcotic .
