<p> Albertans have had a few days now to ponder Prime Minister Justin Trudeau 's abrupt announcement Monday that Canada will impose a universal federal price on carbon beginning in 2018 that will hit the $50 per tonne mark by 2022 for any province failing to meet that benchmark on its own . <p> The unilateral decision -- preceding Parliament 's move Wednesday to ratify the Paris climate change agreement and its 2030 greenhouse gas reduction targets -- falls shy of a full slap in the face to Alberta . <p> The province is , after all , on its way to introducing a $20 per tonne carbon price in 2017 that will grow to $30 per tonne in 2018 . Premier Rachel Notley 's NDP government has already been doing the heavy political lifting on the issue in Alberta . <p> But that does n't mean there 's no sting in Trudeau 's new carbon price point . <p> Albertans are still trying to wrap their heads around the NDP carbon tax and assorted plans to pour money into green energy initiatives and return some money back @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to prepare for a new and even bigger tax in the middle of a recession that has kneecapped the energy industry and pushed unemployment rates up . <p> It 's like being a kid who does well in school , skips parties and consistently has report cards that score in the 80s and 90s , only to have your hard work and triumph greeted at home with the deflating question : " What happened to the other 10 per cent ?
