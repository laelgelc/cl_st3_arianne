<p> Environmental Commissioner Dianne Saxe is warning that forest fires will increase in severity and frequency across Canada , including in northern Ontario , as a result of climate change . <p> Saxe is the province 's independent environmental watchdog , and her report , called the Environmental Protection Report , Small Steps Forward , was issued today in Toronto . <p> " We concluded that more forest fires must be allowed to burn in northern Ontario forests to prevent permanent ecological change and more catastrophic fires in the future , " she reports . <p> There is movement in that direction as the MNRF 's new strategy includes increased flexibility for fire-response decision-making , which enables the ministry to assess each forest fire individually , regardless of its location , to determine the most appropriate response in order to safeguard human life , property and infrastructure , and maintain and enhance forest health . <p> " To this end , the MNRF must follow through on the new strategy 's commitment to build and maintain a workforce capable of executing prescribed burns , and should also create @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ burns could help the ministry protect human values while promoting natural and necessary ecological processes . " <p> Northern communities are also urged to increase their resistance and resilience to forest fires by preparing and implementing prevention and mitigation plans . <p> " The Ontario government , for its part , must ensure all communities near flammable forest become ' FireSmart ' by making prevention and mitigation plans mandatory , and providing adequate funding to communities to develop and implement them . " <p> Fire is an essential ecological process in Ontario 's boreal forest , which covers 50 per cent of the province . <p> " Current levels of fire protection are undesirable ecologically and may become impossible economically , " Saxe warns .
