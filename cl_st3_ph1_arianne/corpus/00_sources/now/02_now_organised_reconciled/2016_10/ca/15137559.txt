<p> Trick or treat is the question Albertans will still be asking , long after tonight 's homage to the pagan lurking in all of us remains little more than a cold , collapsed pumpkin on an early morning doorstep . <p> Suddenly , we 're like that fabled farmer in Saskatchewan , who , staring hard and long enough , can see the back of his head . Yes , the carbon tax is on our collective horizon and moving closer to reality each day . <p> It will define this provincial government 's legacy as it touches every area of our lives , as well as the multitude of nooks and crannies of Alberta 's economy . <p> For the NDP under Rachel Notley , any nasty side-effects of this initial $20-a-tonne carbon levy to be introduced in two months can not be explained away by world energy prices , catcalls of the dangers of brutal cuts ( the two words have become one in the NDP 's lexicon . They should be written " brutalcuts " ) , or the party 's favourite , even @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ went before . <p> No , this baby is all their own . They ca n't even blame the voters , as a carbon tax was never in the party platform . They will sink or swim with its success or failure . It is , for the Notley crew , the ultimate trick or treat . <p> People are worried . The latest poll ( if you can give much credence to polls after the Calgary police " love ' em or loath ' em " contest in recent conflicting tallies ) shows a large majority of Albertans opposed to the carbon levy . Even setting aside polling vagaries , there 's no doubt many ordinary folk are extremely concerned about what this new tax is going to do to such a fragile economy . <p> The timing too is either the most opportune or the most disastrous possible . In the past weeks , we have seen more layoffs at Enbridge while , on the other hand , Precision Drilling is actually rehiring workers . <p> On the international energy front , the Saudis seem to have finally thrown @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ U.S. shale industry crack , but those same frantic frackers in Texas are piling in again which , in itself , will likely keep oil prices below any $60 mark . <p> Meanwhile , unemployment keeps rising here at home -- the dreaded 10 per cent mark is n't too far off in Calgary . Nationally , our GDP numbers are so weak , the Bank of Canada is mulling a cut to interest rates , despite them already being close to zero . Then there 's the whole future pipeline conundrum , an argument dividing the country and one that 'll come to a boil in the months ahead . <p> In fact , Notley is doubling down on her government 's carbon tax move , going well beyond the basic argument that it 's what a responsible government should do to help battle climate change . Even that position is n't an easy sell these days in Alberta . <p> But that is n't enough of a political gamble . She 's now linking the carbon tax to the so-called social license needed to ensure the go-ahead for new @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ economic future . <p> So if the feds say no to the proposed Trans Mountain expansion in a few weeks , or invent enough so-called conditions that the project dies a lingering death regardless , then where will that leave Notley ? It will leave her and her government looking like dead ducks even before we start paying lots more for everything with the carbon levy . Yes , then , go ahead ; try trusting a Liberal prime minster whose home base is in Quebec to do the right thing for Alberta and thereby save your political hide . <p> Alberta is at an economic and social crossroads yet , despite that backdrop , the government plows ahead , with limited public support , to implement a change that will have consequences no one can accurately predict and gives a vote on its success to the federal Grits . <p> Notley is either remarkably bold or unreasonably foolish .
