<h> Pe ? a : Ruminants and climate change <h> Pe ? a : Ruminants and climate change <p> WHEN we hear of climate change , we think about the millions of cars on the roads and fossil fuel-fired power plants . They are the biggest emitters of global warming gas . But did you know that there is a big source of greenhouse gases ( GHG ) which is not man-made , has been with us for the longest time and is a source of livelihood and food ? It 's the livestock industry . <p> Believe it or not , the methane and other gases expelled by some 150 species of animals called ruminants are causing global warming . Ruminants such as cows , goats and sheep do not simply eat grass and swallow it . These mammals ferments grass and other plants in a specialized stomach prior to digestion then brings it back to its mouth to be chewed again . Pig meat and poultry meat and eggs contribute respectively 9 percent and 8 percent to the sector 's emissions . <p> -About 44 percent of livestock emissions are in the form of methane ( CH4 ) . The remaining part is almost equally shared between Nitrous Dioxide ( N2O , 29 percent ) and Carbon Dioxide ( CO2 , 27 percent ) . <p> How can we avoid GHG from livestock ? According to the FAO , the greatest promise involves improving animal and herd efficiency . Collaborating with international seed company DLF , the scientists are working to create a " super grass " that is easier for cows to digest , thereby reducing the amount of gas that builds up in their stomachs . <p> A drastic solution in which all us can do something about is to change our diet . Authors of a study conducted in Europe conclude that it is better for the climate to produce protein from vegetable sources than from animal sources . A switch to less " climate-harmful " such as pigs and poultry is also an option . <p> According to the report from the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change , dietary change can " substantially lower " emissions but there are no UN plans to achieve that . Some scientists have proposed a meat tax to curb consumption .
