<h> Nigeria may lose N69tr to climate change -- FAO <p> Nigeria may lose the sum of N69 trillion to climate change leading to a reduction in the National GDP by 11percent in 2020 and 30 percent by 2050 , the Food and Agricultural Organisation ( FAO ) has revealed . <p> The Minister of State Agriculture and Rural Development , Senator Heineken Lokpobiri , who stated this yesterday in Abuja during the 2016 World Food Day adding that the increase in droughts , floods , erosion , land degradation in the coming decades would significantly impact on the food security , hence affecting the nation 's GDP . <p> According to him , Nigeria is the most populous country in Africa and the most vulnerable to climate change as about 170million people depend on agriculture , as the sector account for 24 percent of the national GDP . <p> He further added " The African Continent according to the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change is the most vulnerable to climate change . It is predicted that by 2050 , the global population would be about 9.6billion . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Lokpobiri stressed the need to fund agriculture so as to adapt to the adverse effect of climate change and as well ensure it becomes more resilient , productive and sustainable , stating that it is the only way to guarantee the well-being of the ecosystems and rural population as well as reduce emissions . <p> The minister also disclosed that a National Agricultural Resilence Framework ( NARF ) has been developed with the aim of setting out approaches that would enhance productivity and boost food security while protecting and strengthening the environment . <p> The Minister said the country has aligned itself to the Adaptation of Agriculture and Africa ( AAA ) initiative which is designed to address the link between climate change and food security in African Countries , adding that the initiative would among other contribute to the Global Climate Action Agenda ( GCAA ) . <p> He said the programme would contribute to promoting African Projects and good practices in soil management , reconversion to fruit trees and agro forestry , agricultural water management , climate risk management and dedicated financing solutions for smallholders
