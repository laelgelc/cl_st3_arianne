<h> 150 nations agree major deal in Rwanda to cut greenhouse gases <p> More than 150 nations meeting in Rwanda hammered out a global deal to cut back on greenhouse gases used in refrigerators and air conditioners , Rwandan minister announced to loud cheers on Saturday , a major milestone in combating climate change . <p> The deal divides countries into three groups with different deadlines to reduce the use of factory-made hydrofluorocarbon ( HFC ) gases , which can be 10,000 times more powerful than carbon dioxide as greenhouse gases . <p> " The amendment and decisions are adopted , " said Rwanda 's Minister for Natural Resources , Vincent Biruta , before applause drowned out the rest of his words . <p> Under the pact , developed nations , including much of Europe and the United States , commit to reducing their use of the gases incrementally , starting with a 10 percent cut by 2019 and reaching 85 per cent by 2036 . <p> Many wealthier nations have already begun to reduce their use of HFCs . <p> Two groups of developing countries will freeze @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and then gradually reduce their use . India , Iran , Iraq , Pakistan and the Gulf countries will meet the later deadline . <p> U.S. Secretary of State John Kerry met officials from China , India and Pakistan during the talks this week . India , the world 's third-largest emitter of greenhouse gases , had asked for more time for developing nations to adapt their industries .
