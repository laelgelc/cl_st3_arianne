<p> Nearly 200 of the world 's nations reached a historic agreement Saturday to reduce greenhouse gases used in refrigerators and air conditioners ; a major step in the fight against climate change . <p> The agreement to reduce hydrofluorocarbons , or HFCs , was announced at a climate meeting in Kigali , Rwanda . HFCs can have an effect that is thousands of times greater than that of an equal volume of carbon dioxide when released into the atmosphere , according to the U.S. Department of Energy . <p> In a statement , President Obama called the pact among 197 nations and governments " an ambitious and far reaching solution to this looming crisis . " <p> " It shows that we can take action to protect our planet in a way that helps all countries improve the lives and livelihoods of their citizens , " the president said . <p> " It 's a monumental step forward , " said U.S. Secretary of State John Kerry , who was in Kigali for the conference . <p> Reductions begin in 2019 <p> The accord divides countries into @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of HFCs . India , Iran , Iraq , Pakistan and the Gulf countries will meet the later deadline . <p> " The global phase-down we have agreed today could knock off up to half-a-degree of warming by the end of the century , " said Miguel Arias Canete , a commissioner with the European Union . <p> Some countries , especially those already feeling the effects of rising sea levels due to a warming planet , hoped for a more ambitious plan to reduce HFCs . <p> " It may not be entirely what the islands wanted , but it is a good agreement , " said Mattlan Zackhras , a representative from the Marshall Islands . <p> Potent greenhouse gas <p> Factory-made hydrofluorocarbon gases , or HFCs , were introduced decades @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ out to contribute to global warming . <p> Their danger has risen as the sales of refrigerators and air conditioners have soared in emerging countries like China and India .
