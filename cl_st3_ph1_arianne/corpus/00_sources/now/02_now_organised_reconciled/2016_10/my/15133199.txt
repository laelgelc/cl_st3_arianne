<p> Nepal 's army says it has finished draining a dangerous glacial lake near Mount Everest to a safe level . <p> The Imja glacial lake , at nearly 5,000m ( 16,400ft ) high , was in danger of flooding downstream settlements , trekking trails and bridges . <p> The lake , which was originally 149m deep in places , has had its water levels lowered by 3.4m after months of pain-staking work , officials say . <p> Imja is one of thousands of glacial lakes in the Himalayas . <p> Many of the lakes are said to be filling up fast because of accelerated melting of glaciers amid rising global temperatures . <p> Last year 's earthquake in Nepal is also feared to have further destabilised Lake Imja . <p> The military said the project to make it safe was the highest drainage project of its kind , with army personnel and Sherpas working for six months to construct an outlet to gradually release the water . <p> After the outlet was constructed , nearly 4m cubic metres of water was released - in a process that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 's manager with the department of hydrology and meteorology , told the BBC the process would now be applied to other lakes . <p> " It was a pilot project that we completed without any unfortunate incident and now this model will be replicated to reduce risks in other glacial lakes as well , " Mr Khatri said . <p> The project was also challenging because of the heavy snow and the dangers of working at high altitude . <p> Officials said teams could only work safely for a few hours each day . <p> " We suffered from some level of stress and fatigue as we were working at this high altitude and extreme location , " Lt-Col Bharat Shrestha , who headed the army team , told the BBC from the Everest region . <p> " In the beginning , a few people had to be evacuated as they suffered from high altitude sickness but gradually we all acclimatised ourselves to this place .
