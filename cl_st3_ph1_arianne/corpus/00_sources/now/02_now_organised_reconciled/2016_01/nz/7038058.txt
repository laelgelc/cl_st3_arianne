All of our breathing years are dedicated to grappling with competition , whether it be at school , on the sportsfield , for employment , business , wealth , prestige , status , or even when searching for the best looking partners to mate with . <p> Competition is everywhere , and even fishing and hunting are not immune . <p> Like a multi-headed monster , competition takes many forms , with some being positive and some negative . The usual excuses of poor land management practises gain most of the airplay , with dairy farming , plantation forestry , and urban development being blamed for a range of ills ranging from nitrate poisoning , sedimentation , eutrophication , increased water temperatures , riparian destruction , to pollution , and more . <p> Fish and Game , an organisation independently funded by recreational licence holders , hit the headlines of recent months when it chose to walk away from the Central Government sponsored Land and Water Forum ( LWR ) -- a theoretically collaborative attempt between stakeholder groups to resolve New Zealand 's freshwater woes . <p> Clearly competition for water usage and quality was part of the reason for bailing out and the best comment I 've seen on the decision was by one Tom O'Connor writing in the Letters of the Editor section the Christchurch Press . In reality , the angling resource has got smaller and what is still worth fishing is getting hit harder by more people than ever before . <p> Competition is fierce on many wilderness streams lately , and it 's common to find sandy beaches covered in bootprints increasing the need to get there early to make sure you are ahead of others . Fish numbers and fish habitat appear to be declining too . <p> Some anglers blame the invasive algae didymo , or massive 1080 poison drops over the back country public commons , but my thinking is that excessive pressure with advanced equipment and technology is an understated issue . Catch and release is practised by most anglers but maybe we 're all loving our trout too much ? <p> Rivers are dynamic systems and another factor I believe is a major issue in fisheries decline is that riverine habitat from headwaters to the ocean has become disconnected . Over the past 30 years of commercial fishing guiding I 've watched increasing high intensity rainfall events , which has led to more riverine flooding and habitat degradation . <p> Rivers have filled in with gravel , pools have disappeared , insect hatches are less common , and there is marginal habitat to support once healthy trout population . These days I mostly fish away from lowland areas and in most places there are no stock , no fences , no farming . I do n't believe water quality is the issue in many areas , it 's more about riverine habitat and because many of these rivers are on conservation land it 's a problem bigger than all of us . <p> With the advent of Facebook , Youtube and the internet , our backcountry rivers are getting fished by more people than ever .
