In the end , leaders must deliver , writes Thomas Walkom . <p> Barack Obama 's final state of the union address was not just the swan song of a U.S. president preparing for next year 's departure . <p> It was also a reminder that the politics of hope -- or what , in Justin Trudeau 's Canada , are called sunny ways -- can not always be counted on to produce results . <p> To hear Obama 's televised speech to the U.S. Congress Tuesday was to recall the heady expectations that surrounded him seven years ago . <p> Not only was he America 's first black president , he was also a leader @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ partisan divisions that tore at his country . <p> Those outside the U.S. cheered him as a president who , after eight years of George W. Bush , could bring a rational approach to American foreign policy . <p> He promised to end the war in Iraq and win the war in Afghanistan . He promised a new relationship with the Muslim world and a more balanced approach to the intractable Israeli-Palestinian conflict . <p> He promised to close the notorious U.S. prison at Cuba 's Guantanamo Bay . <p> He was cheered in Europe and idolized in Canada . Indeed , he is still probably more popular here than in his own country . <p> By contrast , the Obama who spoke Tuesday was restrained -- almost sad . <p> His words were triumphant . He talked of the number of jobs created in the U.S. under his presidency . He bragged about his so-called Obamacare health insurance reforms . <p> But his tone was oddly wistful , as if he himself knew how little he had been able to accomplish . <p> At times , as he strove to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , he seemed almost peevish . " This is good news , people , " he insisted at one point . <p> Certainly , there were gains . With its complications , and exemptions , the health insurance provided by Obamacare may not be as good as that under Canadian or Australian medicare . <p> But for millions of Americans it is signal improvement . <p> On the foreign affairs front , where a president has most leeway , Obama managed to negotiate a far-reaching nuclear deal with Iran . <p> He also defied Florida 's powerful Cuban exile community to re-open diplomatic relations with Havana . <p> And he has avoided going to war with Russia over Ukraine . <p> On the economic front , Obama was far too cautious in his attempts to re-regulate rogue financiers and stimulate jobs . <p> But unlike , say , the European Union , he did n't make matters worse by insisting on fiscal austerity . <p> Yet it is the failures that promise to haunt this president . <p> His vow to close Guantanamo Bay , which he reiterated Tuesday night , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ part , that 's because Congress , now dominated by Republicans , wo n't agree . But in part it is because of the president 's failure to move more deftly when his popularity was still high . <p> His support of the so-called Arab Spring came to naught in Egypt and backfired terribly in Libya . His outreach to the Muslim world fizzled as did his efforts to persuade Israel to refrain from building Jewish settlements on Palestinian land . <p> American troops are still fighting in Afghanistan and have returned to Iraq as " advisers . " <p> Obama 's efforts to deal with climate change , while real , are vulnerable to being overturned by his successor . <p> His repeated calls for gun control have gone nowhere . <p> Most of all , he has never been able to bridge the divide between his Democrats and an increasingly hostile Republican Party dominated by take-no-prisoners conservatives . <p> That in turn , has severely limited his ability to get anything through the country 's Republican-dominated Congress . <p> For Trudeau , that other purveyor of hope , the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ minister can get any measure he wants passed by the Commons simply because his Liberals hold a majority of seats there . <p> Still , Trudeau would be advised to pay attention to what went wrong in Obama 's Washington . <p> The U.S. president 's record is a reminder that fine words and charisma -- while they can motivate voters and win elections -- are never enough .
