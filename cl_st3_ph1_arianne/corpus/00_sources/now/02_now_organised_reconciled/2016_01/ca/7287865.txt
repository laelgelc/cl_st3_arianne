<h> Zika virus outbreak : What you need to know <p> With the second case of Zika virus in Alberta recently confirmed -- contracted abroad in both instances -- the disease spreading across other parts of the Americas is raising questions about the safety of Alberta travellers . <p> Mosquito-borne and similar to other infections such as dengue fever , Zika was first found in humans in 1954 and remained confined to equatorial parts of Africa and Asia until the 21st century . It has since spread to Central and South America and the Caribbean , and has reached pandemic levels . <p> While the mosquitoes that carry the virus ca n't survive in our climate , jet-setters should be aware of the potential risks . <p> Dr. Bonnie Meatherall , an infectious disease specialist and assistant professor at the University of Calgary , answered questions about what the public ought to know . <p> Q : What are the symptoms of the Zika virus ? <p> A : The classic symptoms are fever , and normally it 's a very sudden-onset fever . It also appears that asymptomatic cases are possible . <p> The symptoms for most people are on the mild side . Most people do n't get really sick or need to be hospitalized . The illness is usually done in about a week . <p> Q : Who is most at risk ? <p> A : I think the biggest concern that we have right now is the congenital transmission . If a pregnant woman were to become infected , particularly in the early stages of pregnancy ... that seems to be the most concerning issue . <p> Q : Zika virus has been linked to microcephaly in fetuses . What is microcephaly ? <p> A : Microcephaly means ' small head . ' If the brain is n't developing properly , as a result the brain is small and the head is small as well . At the moment , we do n't actually have good proof of that , we just have some concerning trends . <p> Q : Where is the current outbreak happening ? <p> A : This outbreak is still going on in parts of Latin America , and it 's been on a number of islands in the Caribbean since December . <p> It 's definitely possible that travellers could pick up this virus on holiday . <p> Q : How can people protect themselves ? <p> A : What the Public Health Agency of Canada is suggesting , particularly for pregnant women , is to consider deferring unnecessary travel . <p> If someone has to travel for some reason and they 're pregnant , they need to very cautious about mosquito bite prevention , because that 's the only way of preventing Zika virus transmission . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have medications to take , and there 's no specific treatment if someone does become infected .
