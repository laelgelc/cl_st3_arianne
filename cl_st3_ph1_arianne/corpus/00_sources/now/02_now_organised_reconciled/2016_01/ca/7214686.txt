<h> DEXTER : Ray of hope , note of caution in new federal-provincial ball game <p> Former premier Darrell Dexter : " My government always knew Ottawa wanted to throw shipbuilding work to Quebec and Ontario , or whoever lost the bidding for major contracts . " ( HERALD FILE ) <p> A New Year marks a new beginning in lots of ways . <p> In Canada , among other things , it marks the continued rollout of the work of the Justin Trudeau government and , related to that , a bid to kickstart a new relationship between the federal government in Ottawa and its provincial/territorial counterparts . <p> This is without question a new and different time for intergovernmental relations . Indeed , with the exception of its infrastructure plan , the previous Harper government did not have a defined national program that set goals and standards for provincial-territorial participation and involvement . <p> Even when that government reset equalization , there was a penchant for striking various one-off deals , including with the likes of Nova Scotia . <p> By dealing with the provinces one-on-one @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ over him , and placed himself in the powerful position of someone who would distribute benefits if the terms were acceptable to him . <p> Back in the days when prime ministers and premiers met regularly about the economy and federal-provincial fiscal arrangements and goals for Canada , there were still plenty of side deals , but the context was based on a view that Canada could be greater than the sum of its senior levels of government . Federal action on the economy , as an example , was considered to be more legitimate if undertaken after receiving the advice of premiers , both publicly and privately . <p> After 10 years of a particular style in Ottawa for managing relations with provincial and territorial leaders , these governments are now accustomed to the Stephen Harper approach . For some time , provinces rarely had to deal with issues like federal standards that are too intrusive , or failing to recognize that different provinces can find different means of achieving a shared goal ( though the provinces got a taste of that with the federal greenhouse gas regulations that were @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ schedule , regardless of cost or alternatives ) . <p> Generating real results on a number of the Trudeau government 's biggest and most complicated issues like refugee settlement and immigration , climate change , infrastructure funding , a new health accord and labour market pressures , as examples , all hinge on a productive working relationship between federal , provincial and territorial counterparts . <p> Resuming the approach of regular discussion that the prime minister has committed to wo n't necessarily be easy , since provinces seek leverage by ganging up on Ottawa . My own experience as chair of the Council of the Federation in 2012 , when B.C. essentially walked away from finalizing an updated energy agreement , illustrated the complicated winding path through 14 political and electoral agendas toward consensus and new or updated national goals , programs and standards . <p> In closing , it 's worth highlighting a separate but related issue . Although this can be seen as a transactional procurement matter , the national shipbuilding strategy is actually an industrial initiative as well as a defence initiative . My government always knew Ottawa wanted to throw shipbuilding work to Quebec and Ontario , or whoever lost the bidding for major contracts . <p> All this emphasizes the point that while the relationship between the federal government and the provinces/territories has never been more important , it has probably also never been so complex given the multi-layered and in some cases difficult issues facing all governments in Canada . <p> The renewed commitment to build bridges with provincial and territorial leaders is important and I hope it will go a long way to generating successful outcomes for Canadians . <p> Darrell Dexter is vice chair , Global Public Affairs .
