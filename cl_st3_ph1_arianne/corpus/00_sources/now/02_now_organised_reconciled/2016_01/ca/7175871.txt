<h> A year in review and a year to change <p> Each January I try to take a brief look back at the previous year with regards to heritage , arts and culture ( among other related aspects ! ) and offer my humble opinions on how I saw the year and what I see in the upcoming year . <p> As you might know , the month JANUARY is named ( according to some pundits ) after the god JANUS , who was the god of beginnings , transitions , passages and endings . It is believed , among many circles , that the god Janus had two faces which looked towards the past as well as towards the future . That is what I will attempt to do today . <p> On the national front it promises to be a refreshing new beginning with Justin Trudeau taking power . Although the proof will be in what happens in the next few months , he has had a great start . I once considered him to be a dilettante but I am , so far , impressed @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of our scientists , repositioning Canada as a peace keeping nation , addressing , in a realistic fashion , the serious aspect of climate change and expressing support for the CBC are all most welcome . It is so nice to think that our leadership now actually believes in climate change , does not believe that the earth is flat , watches other channels besides Fox News and can see beyond the bottom line of excessive profits for big business . <p> All of these things represent a much-needed and positive atmosphere in our national government and should translate into a renewed interest and support for arts , heritage and culture . Unfortunately , on the local front we were once again on the wrong side of the election of federal members of parliament . I am not sure why a complete disregard for our part of Southwestern Ontario by the previous government did not translate into a serious sense of disenchantment but ... that was not the case and , as a result , we have what we have . Let us hope that a rising tide will raise all @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ mud . <p> On the local level there have certainly been some positive developments . Dance , drama and artistic endeavours at a local level continue to grow in popularity . More and more young people are appreciating all aspects of local arts , heritage and cultural life and that is indeed promising . Now all we have to do is keep these young people in our local community . There are too many people my age around and not nearly enough young adults . A community can not grow and advance without them . A community with an equal balance of young and old is most desirable for not only arts and culture but for our economy in general but ... you must have good paying jobs to attract an educated , creative and engaged core of young workers . <p> Our local economy continues to falter and our local schools , churches , factories , businesses continue to close at an alarming rate . One is amalgamation that forces individual councillors to vote not for the good of the whole community but for the narrows needs of their area . The other problem is an old one . It is a deeply rooted out-dated philosophy of never spending money on anything resembling progress . The concept of spending money to make money has always by-passed Chatham-Kent . Until we get over that and think in a different more aggressive and strategic manner we are doomed . They both operate in efficient and progressive ways and I have seen them take active roles in the development of the Tecumseh Monument site , our Cemetery Strolls , much-needed Mausoleum upkeep , and our area 's local history in general . Whoever hired these people should hire many more like these two . <p> In addition , the CK Museum Staff ( Stephanie Saunders , Lydia Riedstra and Deanna Bullard ) did a marvellous job of working with Bruce Warwick ( aka " Super Volunteer " ) to put together the 2015 Time Capsule that was recently placed in the Maple Leaf Cemetery Mausoleum .
