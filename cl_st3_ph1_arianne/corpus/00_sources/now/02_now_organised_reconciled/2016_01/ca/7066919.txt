<p> The world of trees is a competitive one with sprinters and marathoners . <p> A recently published major global study on tree competitiveness found these plants ' traits have " trade-offs " to compete for growth depending on the environment they 're in , said Mark Vanderwel , a University of Regina assistant professor in biology and one of the paper 's authors . <p> The findings stem from extensive data sets from around the world featuring four million trees representing 2,500 species . <p> Vanderwel , whose research interest lies in how changes in climate affect forests , collected data from the Canadian and American governments stemming from most provinces and states . He said he went into the project hoping to discern " what are the common factors , common processes that we can use to understand forests globally .
