He died in September last year . <p> POETRY <p> Happiness MARTIN HARRISON UWA Publishing , $24.99 <p> I first met Martin Harrison in a tiny ABC radio studio in Adelaide in 1986 . He was working on an Adelaide Festival edition of an experimental program called Surface Tension , and I was his sound engineer . We were going live to air around the nation and we had several reel-to-reel tapes lined up . He told me to spool them forward , stop them at random and then hit play . When we came out of the broadcast , the guys in master control just stood there silently shaking their heads . Harrison was a radio radical , and as a teacher he always encouraged experimentation . He died in September last year , found in his car by the side of the road after suffering a heart attack . Harrison was deeply concerned with catching the fleeting moment , the exactness of specific times and places and arrangements of the world , particularly in the environment around his home in Wollombi in the Hunter Valley . Here Harrison 's style , his " branching " language , reminds me of the wonderful but sadly neglected Australian poet John Anderson . Like Anderson , Harrison was very interested in how non-Indigenous Australians could be influenced by Indigenous structures and move beyond inherited European mythologies . He was fascinated by the multiple voices of landscape . <p> Happiness also moves beyond Australian shores . A suite of seven Paris Poems is a looser and more experimental offering , employing elements of randomness like those early tape experiments of his . He engages with the politics of climate change , and touches on elements of American and European history : " Himmler 's daughter thought he was a hero/ spent her life trying to clear his name . " <p> Happiness finishes with a series of ( perhaps prescient ) elegies , including two for his lover Nizar Bouheni , who died in 2010 at the age of 26 , and to whom the whole book is dedicated . With a title like Happiness , we 're on the alert for the opposite , but there 's a refreshing lack of irony in this collection . In his last book Harrison moves through the particularities of landscape and season , embracing " the struggle to love and be loved " .
