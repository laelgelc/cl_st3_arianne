<h> The Eiffel Tower lights up with the slogan " Action Now " referring to the United Nations Climate Change Conference in Paris in December . <p> I 've always thought of New Year 's Eve as a time for celebrating the past year and setting our sights on what more we could do in the next one . Instead , Journal Sentinel columnist Ernst-Ulrich Franzen uses his year-ending column to cast a pall of pessimism on some of the year 's most promising climate policy changes ( " My pessimism on climate change , " Dec. 21 ) . <p> Most of Franzen 's column reads like the worst kind of Monday morning quarterbacking . Sure , it 's easy to cast doubt on the efforts of citizens , corporations and nations to prevent climate catastrophe . But it 's just as easy enough to counter every one of Franzen 's fears . <p> Yes , it 's true that the Paris climate pact is ambitious , that it embodies no enforcement mechanism and that Kyoto failed before it . True , Republicans seem bent on frustrating both President Barack Obama 's Clean Power Plan and state legislators have stymied progress toward expanding renewables . But in recent weeks , Congress extended clean energy credits while Madison 's local utility announced new targets for increasing its reliance on renewables . <p> More to the point , Franzen mentions only in passing the surge in popular support for significant action on climate change , helped along no doubt by Pope Francis ' encyclical and public statements .
