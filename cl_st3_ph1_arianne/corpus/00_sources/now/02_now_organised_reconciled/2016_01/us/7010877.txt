<h> Climate Change Could Challenge the Water Needs of Power Plants <p> By Katherine Tweed <p> Posted 4 Jan 2016 20:15 GMT <p> Photo : Rich Pedroncelli/Corbis <p> Most power plants need water . As water resources are challenged in coming decades due to climate change , the bulk of the world 's power plants could see reduced capacity , because of water limitations or temperature changes according to a new study published today in Nature Climate Change . <p> The output of more than than 80 percent of thermoelectric plants could be affected after 2040 , according to the research . However , the technologies to mitigate any capacity reductions due to change in water availability already exist . <p> Thermoelectric power plants , including coal , gas and nuclear , deliver the majority of the world 's power needs today . In 2040 , fossil fuels will still likely make up the bulk of energy supply , according to the International Energy Agency . <p> Most plants that generate steam to make electricity use water to cool and condense the steam . Although some areas will have increased water flow , most of the hydro and thermal plants are located in regions that will see a decrease , the study found . <p> The model shows that by 2050 , thermoelectric plants could face an average capacity reduction of 7 to 12 percent , and hydropower would face a 1 to 3 percent reduction . In some water-constrained regions , affects are already far higher .
