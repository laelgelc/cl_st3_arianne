<h> This island nation just traded a huge chunk of its debt in return for protecting their oceans <p> The Seychelles , a small island nation in the Indian Ocean , did something pretty big . <p> Last month , the country of 115 islands agreed to protect 30 percent of their ocean territory , and in return a foreign investor group , the Paris Club , will cancel $30 million of Seychelles ' debt . <p> It 's quite a deal , and it comes with big potential perks for the planet . Marine reserves are one of the major ways we 've seen countries take action to protect their natural resources in recent years . The 154,000 square miles of ocean named in the Seychelles deal will be protected , with a large area designated as a no-fishing zone , which means fish will get bigger and more abundant , while sensitive habitats like coral reefs will be protected from some damaging fishing practices . It will be the second-largest marine reserve in the Indian Ocean . <p> All benefits supported by the debt restructuring will @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , coral reefs , and mangroves . The financing will promote implementation of a Marine Spatial Plan for the entire Seychelles Exclusive Economic Zone , a territory approximately 3,000 times the size of their land mass . The deal will also ensure approximately 400,000 km2/98.9 million acres ( an area larger than the size of Germany ) will be managed for conservation as marine protected areas ( MPAs ) within five years . <p> Low-lying island nations like the Seychelles depend on , and are at the mercy of , the seas . The Seychelles ' economy is based on tourism and tuna-fishing -- meaning it 's in their best interest to preserve the coral reefs and species that surround them , while they also have a significant financial incentive to catch more and more fish . <p> And of course , the communities of these tropical islands are disproportionately at risk from climate change and sea level rise .
