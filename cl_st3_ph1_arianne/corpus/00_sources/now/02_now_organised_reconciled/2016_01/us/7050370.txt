become rare almost to the point of endangered in Washington : Optimism . <p> In a city where stagnation has long been the standard operating procedure , amid political factions whose mutual disdain for productive compromise has complicated even routine legislating , during a primary season where fear-mongering and boorishness have been the prominent characteristics , the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ days refection on his administration 's achievements these past seven years . <p> While a victory lap would be inappropriate , a reminder that positive change is still possible in Washington , even in the midst of crippling partisanship , reinforces the idea that with cooperation and singleness of purpose -- some would call it optimism -- there are no challenges this nation can not overcome . <p> Just in the past year , the administration has scored victories on both the domestic and international fronts : the Trans-Pacific Partnership trade agreement , a multinational deal to put the brakes on Iran 's nuclear-weapons advancement , a 2016 federal budget , a historic global pact to slow the causes of climate change , new engagement with Cuba and the addition of 2.65 million jobs . <p> It is Congress and the nation as a whole have seen no progress . <p> Unfortunately , such hotly charged issues are unlikely to be given any legislative attention in an election year . <p> And with voting in the nation 's first primaries and caucuses just weeks away ( one of the reasons this year 's State of the Union is being held so early ) , Obama will need to make the most of what will be one of his last national speeches before an audience undistracted by his potential successors . <p> So a wise Obama will avoid laying out an action agenda for his final year . The president needs to reassure a nervous public that his administration is working actively to counter the threat . <p> Foreign policy : Obama 's patience in dealing with global hot spots like Syria , the Ukraine , North Korea and the Middle East has been , frankly , an improvement over the feckless , reckless policies of his predecessor ( echoes of which can currently be heard on the campaign trail from a number of would-be Oval Office holders ) . Statistics on employment , imprisonment , education and opportunity -- not to mention repeated clashes between minority communities and the too-often overwhelmingly white forces that police them -- belie that assertion . What are the president 's thoughts on building a nation that truly offers liberty and justice for all ? <p> Obama will surely touch on other topics . An overview of his administration would be incomplete without reference to the 17 million-plus Americans who now have health insurance thanks to the Affordable Care Act ; to the nation 's resurgent automobile industry , brought back from the brink of collapse by federal intervention ; to the 70 straight months of job growth that grew out of the worst economic calamity since the Great Depression . <p> But it is not past successes that the president should most dwell on but future challenges . America does not want for them . The president must spell them out as well , reminding the nation that it is not without resources -- ingenuity , creativity , diversity -- that can help it overcome any adversity . <p> As he gives his final address from what his Press Secretary Josh Earnest calls , " the grandest stage in all of American politics , " Obama 's greatest challenge is not so much convincing Americans that the state of their union is strong , but offering direction on how to keep it that way . Offering , in a word , optimism .
