<h> Law Speak - China 's ' grand strategy ' <p> IN my last Law Speak column I wrote of the US grand strategy towards China as revealed by the influential US think tank -- the Council of Foreign Relations -- in its March 2015 report . It stressed that China 's own grand strategy was to accumulate " comprehensive national power " -- in economic , military , technological and diplomatic aspects . Once it achieves this power , it will embark to reclaim the primacy it once enjoyed in Asia -- as a prelude to exerting global influence in the future as a world power . This was seen as a threat to US primacy and hegemonic dominance of the world -- and , said the report , had to be faced head on even if it would lead to dangerous consequences . <p> Is this assessment accurate ? Indeed a long time ago China and India were indeed significantly industrialised . In the 18th and early 19th centuries , India produced a quarter of the world 's textiles , much of it exported . In India with British rule in 1857 , British textiles entered India duty-free but Indian textiles were barred altogether from Britain . In fact up to 45% of British cotton textile exports went to India by 1873 . <p> China managed to maintain some measure of independence after the two Opium Wars . Its deindustrialisation process was less than half that of India because the influx of western goods started much later . <p> From 1750 to 1800 , the per capita industrialisation of India and China was at par with the developed states . But by 1860 ( after colonial intervention ) , it dropped to as low as 19% of that of the developed states . <p> India was burdened with maintaining the British expatriate bureaucracy and the military ; and pay interest on British loans . China was devastated with the huge indemnity exacted by western powers and Japan after the Boxer rebellion of 1900 . India 25% . And the developed states 27% . By 1900 the developed states had 89% -- with China and India trailing far behind at 6% and 2% respectively ! <p> This irked these countries . As early as 1988 , Manmohan Singh the past prime minister of India complained bitterly of the rules set largely by the North that constrained the ability of the South to breathe . As then secretary-general of the South Commission he said : " What we in the Third World need is global perestroika , restructuring of international economic relations which would take into account the legitimate aspirations of the four-fifths of humanity that lives in the Third World , for better life for their peoples . " <p> So the need was realised early on for the reformation of global institutions like the World Bank and the International Monetary Fund . But this restructuring was a long time coming . This set the framework for establishing new policy space and initiatives for countries of the South led by this bloc . <p> The idea then was to use their combined weight as a counter-balance to the primacy of the North . China 's strategy was located within this multilateral platform . It was , and is , not abiding its time to knock the US from its hegemonic pedestal at some time in the future . <p> In fact China as a leader of Brics is uneasy with any challenges to the North . They prefer to operate passively , building trade relations and forging a development programme for the South . As a new voice of the South , it advocated the importance of respecting the rule of law , strengthening the UN and the Security Council and prioritising diplomacy to maintain peace and security . <p> In sum the hallmark of China 's strategy -- multi-polarity to counter balance US primacy . China 's prime minister Hu Jin-tao stated after 9/11 : " Multi-polarity constitutes an important base for world peace and the democratisation of international relations is an essential guarantee for that peace . " <p> So international relations must be democratic and therefore multi-polar . Confrontational agenda against the North , and US primacy , is out . The emphasis is on ensuring an end to the unfair practices by the North on matters of trade rules , subsidies , technology transfer and finance rules -- which is hampering the South 's growth . China 's economic strength -- given its vast trade surplus against the Europeans and the US , and its emergence as the second largest economy in the world -- makes it a natural leader of the South . <p> China is projected to become the largest economy in the world by this year . Yet it seems content to go along with the original Brics programme to correct the imbalance of the power of the North over the South . It urged the North to reform its financial policies . It has called for a North-South partnership ; revision of the governance of the IMF and the World Bank and strengthening of UNCTAD -- which empathises with the views of the South . <p> So the thrust is for : a development agenda , financial reform and multi-polar regionalism .
