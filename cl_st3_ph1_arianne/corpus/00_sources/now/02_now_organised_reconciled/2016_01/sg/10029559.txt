<h> While occupying less than 1% of land here , they store equivalent of 3.7% of CO2 emissions in 2010 <p> Mangroves may cover just 960ha or less than 1 per cent of Singapore , but that is enough for them to store 450,571.7 tonnes of carbon . <p> This is equivalent to the annual per capita emissions of 621,089 people , local scientists have found . <p> This amount is also equivalent to 1,652,096 tonnes of stored carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) or 3.7 per cent of Singapore 's national CO2 emissions in 2010 . <p> This ability to soak up carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and store high densities of it has gained mangroves a growing prominence globally for their significant role in protecting the earth from the upheavals of climate change . <p> On top of that , they act as nurseries for crabs , prawns and fishes , and protect coasts against storms and floods . <p> A researcher from the National University of Singapore conducting a carbon stock assessment of the mangrove forests in Singapore .
