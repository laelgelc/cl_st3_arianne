<h> The dirty truth about ' clean diesel ' <p> Volkswagen played a leading role in convincing people to accept a technology that is causing a precipitous decline in air quality for millions of city dwellers across the world : the diesel engine.PHOTO : AGENCE FRANCE-PRESSE <p> MONTREAL * As scandals go , it was a juicy one . Volkswagen , one of the world 's highest grossing automakers , persuaded consumers it had created a new generation of so-called clean diesel cars - until investigators discovered that defeat devices , which activated emissions controls only when the cars were tested , were concealing the fact that its vehicles emitted up to 40 times the permitted levels of pollutants during regular use . <p> Volkswagen has been punished with consumer opprobrium , a costly recall and plummeting sales . Yet the public outrage over the fraud obscures a much larger issue . <p> Volkswagen played a leading role in convincing people to accept a technology that is causing a precipitous decline in air quality for millions of city dwellers across the world : the diesel engine . Last March , Paris was enveloped in a grey cloud of choking fumes that obscured the Eiffel Tower for hours and briefly earned the French capital the title of the world 's most polluted city , beating out even Beijing . An air quality expert in Britain reported that much of it was " stale diesel " from traffic emissions generated in European cities . <p> This diesel pollution is not just unpleasant ; it is also dangerous . The nitrogen oxides produced by diesel engines , which are far more popular in Europe than in the United States , are a potent irritant for asthma sufferers . So , a driver who steps on the accelerator of a diesel car may be filling the lungs of nearby pedestrians , cyclists , infants in strollers and other drivers with potentially deadly particulate matter . <p> According to one study , 9,416 premature deaths in London in 2010 were attributed to nitrogen dioxide and PM 2.5 particles . The annual number of deaths linked to air pollution in Britain as a whole now rivals the death toll in the early 1950s , when " pea souper " fogs caused by coal fires shrouded London for weeks . <p> How did we get here ? <p> German engineer Rudolf Diesel must have thought he was on to a good thing when he built his prototype engine in 1893 . Unlike petrol engines , no spark plugs were needed to ignite the fuel-air mix injected into each cylinder : Pressure and high temperature caused it to combust spontaneously . <p> Diesel 's engine also ran on a grade of fuel @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ internal combustion engines , diesels became the workhorses of the 20th century : They powered ships , trains , trucks and tanks ; they cut trees , dug ore and drove factories . Generations of children in rural areas got to school thanks to buses running on diesel . <p> The diesel engine is inherently efficient : Even a heavy sedan can get up to 5.6 litres per 100km while producing far less carbon dioxide emissions per km . The relatively light carbon footprint of Diesel 's invention meant that in the late 1990s , policymakers in Europe , eager to meet Kyoto Protocol goals , initiated a " dash for diesel " . Consumers got a push from sharply reduced taxes on diesel fuel . <p> In Europe , where tens of millions of vehicles run on diesel , 55 per cent of all new vehicle registrations are now for diesel automobiles . Thanks also to a glut of cheap diesel from Russian refineries , which last year ramped up production to half a million barrels a day , diesel is now a cheap option at petrol stations . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as green . " In the US , which has some of the world 's toughest air pollution laws , automakers worked hard to convince consumers that a new generation of " clean diesel " cars was far less polluting . Volkswagen heavily promoted its " TDI " ( turbocharged direct injection ) technology . <p> We know better now . As anybody who has seen the black smoke spewing out of the pipes of a big rig as it changes gears can testify , diesel has a fatal flaw . It tends to burn dirty , particularly at low speeds and temperatures . <p> In cities , where so much driving is stop and start , incomplete diesel combustion produces pollution that is devastating for human health . <p> Volkswagen sold under half a million of its " clean diesel " cars to the American public before the emissions scandal broke . It turns out opting for diesel may even have failed to limit climate change : Researchers at Stanford found in 2002 that the warming because of soot from diesel emissions may more than offset the cooling because of reduced carbon dioxide emissions over the long term . <p> Europe is now scrambling to undo the damage . In London , Mayor Boris Johnson last year called for a British programme to pay some drivers to scrap their diesel vehicles . In Paris , Mayor Anne Hidalgo has gained broad support for a proposed ban on diesel cars . <p> City leaders have a ways to go . Battling its own smog problem , Bogota has a pilot project to replace its rapid transit system 's diesel-powered buses with Chinese-made electric vehicles . Officials in Madrid have gone further , with plans to make its city centre car-free by 2020 . <p> Last month , the signatories of the climate deal in Paris agreed that the world has to begin a long-term shift from fossil fuels to more sustainable forms of energy .
