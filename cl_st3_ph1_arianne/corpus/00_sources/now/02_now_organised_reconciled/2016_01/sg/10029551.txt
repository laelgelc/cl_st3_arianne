<h> S'poreans can join series of engagement sessions ; Govt will work with individuals to translate good ideas into actions <p> While Singapore has grown into a sterling city in a garden in 50 years , the next phase will be fraught with more acute challenges , driven by increasing land constraints that could mean trade-offs between commercial , residential and green spaces . <p> Climate change will also result in rising sea levels , more intense rainfall and warmer weather , and globally , there will be more pressure on resources such as food and raw materials . <p> But Singaporeans will have a say in how these challenges will be met , and the future they envision , through a month-long series of engagement sessions titled " A Cleaner , Greener and Smarter Home " launched yesterday . <p> The discussions will be based on four main themes : City in a Garden , Vibrant Community Spaces , Eco-smart Towns and Gracious Living , and A Green and Conserving Culture . <p> It will delve into topics , including how to cut down on food and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ more environmentally friendly ; ways in which the community can maintain the island 's greenery ; or how technology can be used to improve the daily commute . <p> They are part of the larger SGfuture discussions that started in November and are expected to run till the middle of this year . Last month , the focus was on building a caring community . <p> To kick-start this round of dialogues , a symposium was held yesterday at Gardens by the Bay , involving some 300 participants , and attended by National Development Minister Lawrence Wong and Environment and Water Resources Minister Masagos Zulkifli . <p> In a call to action , Mr Wong said the sessions " should not just be talk only " , but those with good ideas should come forward . <p> The Government will work with individuals to " translate your ideas into actions " , he added . <p> Ms Masagos gave the example of Mr Tan Ken Jin as an individual who made a difference .
