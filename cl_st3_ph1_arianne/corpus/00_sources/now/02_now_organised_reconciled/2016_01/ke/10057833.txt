<h> Policy To Cushion Farmers Is Timely <p> The purchase of maize worth Sh690 million from farmers and re-introduction of the Guaranteed Minimum Return policy to cushion farmers from losses and motivate them to invest in agriculture is timely . <p> It is also encouraging that the National Cereals and Produce Board has waived drying fees to encourage farmers to deliver more maize . And things have been looking up for farmers since NCPB introduced the cash-on-delivery system . This will boost crop production and food security . <p> The Guaranteed Minimum Return policy , scrapped some years back , will go a long way in helping farmers as erratic rains , especially in the food basket of the Rift Valley , have adversely affected farming . Natural calamities such as disease , drought , and floods are some of the major setbacks that farmers face . The policy will ensure no more Kenyans die of starvation . <p> Kenyans have a right to food security and this can only be achieved if proper policies are put in place to increase crop production . This initiative comes in the wake of discontent by farmers because the sector is facing a crisis unlike when Deputy President William Ruto was Agriculture minister . <p> Farmers have been demotivated by poor prices due to liberalisation . Maize prices in the Rift Valley , for example , have declined to as low as Sh1,700 for a bag as middlemen exploit farmers due to lack of markets for the produce . <p> The NCPB offered Sh3,300 per 90kg in the previous season but delayed to open depots leaving farmers with no option but to sell at throwaway prices to meet their basic needs . <p> Maize production in the Rift Valley has declined from 21 million bags to 16 million , following the outbreak of lethal necrosis disease and erratic weather patterns . Farmers were contemplating reducing acreage under maize cultivation due to lack of capital and rising cost of farm inputs - @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ farmers can not afford practising agriculture as a non-profit venture . <p> The liberalisation of the economy , especially the opening up of the East Africa Community , has exposed farmers to unfair competition . There is a need to introduce incentives to motivate them to continue planting maize to earn a profit and ensure food security . Some traders import maize from Uganda , which harvests earlier than Kenya , and stock up , waiting for the NCPB to open buying stores and make a kill , while farmers continue to live in abject poverty . <p> Most farmers say the Agriculture ministry has not done enough to cushion them . They want better policies to enable them invest in crop production as a protective venture . <p> Malawi , for instance , which was once an importer of maize , has revitalised its agricultural sector and now has a surplus for local consumption and export . Kenya needs to borrow a leaf from Malawi . <p> There is no way we can achieve development and food security if more effort is not put in the sector to increase crop @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ some parts of the country . Apart from subsidised fertilisers , availability of farm implements is one of the issues Ruto had introduced during his term in the agricultural ministry . <p> Lowering taxes on farm machineries and having county governments in grain growing areas prioritise the availability of the resources ( farm inputs and machineries ) will motivate farmers to increase crop production . The establishment of a fertiliser plant in the region will enable farmers get fertilisers at affordable prices besides being availed to them ahead of the planting season . <p> The sector has for long been faced with challenges of adopting modern production techniques to increase crop yield and the time has come for the sector to be revitalised . <p> Farming should be taken as a business venture just like any other sector for crop producers to be motivated to earn better returns . <p> Bethwel Kaino works for the Deputy President 's Press .
