<h> Team SA head for cold , costly Davos arena again <h> South Africa traditionally has a strong presence at the annual World Economic Forum gathering in Davos , Switzerland , and this year will be no different . <p> That 's despite the fact that , thanks to the exchange rate , South African delegates will be paying about 50% more than last year for the privilege of attending the world 's biggest annual gathering of global elites . <p> The South African delegation consists of more than 80 delegates from the government , business and NGOs . Eight cabinet members , including President Jacob Zuma and ministers from his economic , energy and water clusters , will be there , along with the CEOs of Eskom , Transnet and the Industrial Development Corporation . They 'll be joined by the usual crop of mainly banking and insurance CEOs . <p> The theme of this delegation ? " South Africa is open for business " . Not dissimilar from last year 's , when there were fears that South Africa 's power grid was about to collapse @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ then , largely due to a lack of demand because the economy has slowed . <p> The biggest problem facing the South African delegation is that every other struggling emerging market will be trying to convey the same message to the same people in the quaint Alpine village . <p> With more than $500-billion ( about R8.3-trillion ) estimated to have left emerging markets in the past year , all will be seeking investment ; all will be telling potential investors that they are also open for business . <p> But South Africa has been hobbled : with a recession beckoning as early as mid-year , a vulnerable currency and trust eroded by the firing of Nhlanhla Nene as finance minister late last year , Team South Africa are going to have to work extra hard to convince investors of their case when the gathering begins on Wednesday . <p> Another problem facing South African delegates this year is the cost of attending . The Swiss franc has strengthened to almost parity with the dollar , and the rand is considerably weaker than it was a year ago . Private sector @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ representatives will be funded by the taxpayer . <p> Pricing for Davos is opaque . Various estimates put the cost per delegate at $27 000 , which excludes the cost of getting there and other necessities such as board and lodging . CNN had estimated the total cost to be $40 000 per delegate . <p> Using the simplest measure of currency differential , the flawed but amusing Big Mac Index - created by The Economist magazine as a measure of purchasing power parity - Switzerland is four times as expensive as South Africa . A basic hotel room ( with shared bathroom ) for four nights during the Davos week can cost R60 000 . <p> It 's a great place to meet people and be immersed in new ideas and fresh thinking : this years ' theme , " Mastering the Fourth Industrial Revolution " , has attracted some of the world 's top technology brains . <p> In a world moving at an unprecedented speed and scale due the quantum leap in the power of technology , the insights delegates will gain will be unsurpassed . But in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ collapse and climate change , the agenda may seem oddly out of touch . <p> Last week , for example , German Chancellor Angela Merkel cancelled her appearance so that she could focus on domestic issues related to the influx of Syrian refugees . But Davos is not the UN . It 's a place where money goes to meet other money and influence . <p> You never know who you might bump into on the village street . Last year , I was almost steamrollered by IMF head Christine Lagarde and her entourage , swapped pleasantries with the inventor of the world wide web , Tim Berners-Lee , and caught a glimpse of Alibaba founder Jack Ma , who is on the guest list again this year . <p> This week , expect about 2 500 delegates and hordes of hangers-on , yours truly included , from about 1 000 companies and 100 countries , to battle high prices , crowds and maximum temperatures of -3 ?
