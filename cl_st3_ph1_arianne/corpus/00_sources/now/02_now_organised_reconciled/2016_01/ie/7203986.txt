<p> The chair of the new Climate Change Advisory Council has said the group wo n't hold back on criticising the Government for failings in tackling carbon emissions . <p> Professor John Fitzgerald warned the task of combatting climate change would involve " significant policy developments at both a national and an international level " . He said where those policy developments did not materialise , the council would be swift in pointing it out . <p> " That 's our job , to call it as we see it , and with the nature of the personalities on the council , we 're not going to be pulling our punches , " he said . <p> " But we 're not going out to disagree . " We 're going out to influence policy and hopefully the bulk of what we say will find favour . " <p> The 11-member council was set up on an interim basis six months ago but the order legally establishing it was signed by the environment minister earlier this week , meaning it can now begin its work in earnest . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ monitoring Government policy and its impact on the behaviour of industry , agriculture , and the energy and transport sectors in terms of the country 's international obligations to become carbon neutral by 2050 . <p> It will hold its first meeting as a statutory body next month to agree a plan of work for the year ahead and will produce its first annual report in the first half of 2017 . <p> Professor Fitzgerald said there were similarities between the group and the Fiscal Advisory Council . " Government takes on board some of the things its says and others it does n't . Inevitably there will be things which we will recommend which may not find favour . "
