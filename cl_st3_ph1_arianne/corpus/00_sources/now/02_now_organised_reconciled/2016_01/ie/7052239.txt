<h> We will benefit too if we welcome refugees with open arms <p> Developed countries can well afford to aid refugees . In fact , we ca n't afford not to . And the way we respond will tell us a lot about ourselves , writes Peter Sutherland <p> THE Mediterranean migration crisis has delivered two critical lessons . First , Europe and the international community have grossly inadequate systems for protecting vulnerable migrants . <p> Second , in the absence of such systems , populist leaders will prey on fear to gain political support , undermining the liberal , tolerant societies that have taken 70 years of hard work to build . <p> That is why vigorous action at the European and global levels is essential this year . In September , United Nations Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon will convene an extraordinary summit dedicated to building a fair global system for protecting refugees and vulnerable migrants . One hopes that countries will come prepared to make tangible , enduring commitments . <p> Such commitments were sorely lacking in 2015 . That failure could end up costing Germany alone upwards of ? 21 billion annually for years to come . <p> But the financial implications of the crisis pale in comparison to the human and political costs . More than a million people risked their lives crossing the Mediterranean last year , and then endured grueling journeys through the Balkans . Almost 4,000 people died on the way , and many European countries turned their backs on those who survived , refusing them safe haven . <p> Cynical political leaders ruthlessly capitalised on popular anxiety by promoting an odious nationalist vision that either ignores or distorts real-life experiences with immigration . <p> In the United States , for example , not one of the 780,000 refugees resettled since September 11 , 2001 , has executed a terrorist attack . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ they receive in benefits . Nonetheless , extremist forces are dangerously close to taking political power in some European states , and are gaining traction even in formerly liberal bastions . <p> Anti-migrant parties already are in power in Hungary and Poland . Their success is compelling mainstream parties to adopt anti-migrant policies as well . <p> All of this has seriously undermined European cooperation . <p> The EU 's programme to process the million refugees who arrived on its shores has succeeded in ' relocating ' a mere 190 of them . Checks at the borders of six countries within the Schengen Area have been reinstituted , at least temporarily . <p> The most urgent priority is to create safe and legal paths for refugees to reach Europe . This does not imply that every vulnerable migrant must be accepted . But the EU should be more systematically generous in determining how many to admit , and it should implement organised ways to facilitate their entry . <p> Such a system would protect migrants and safeguard Europe by enabling it to vet applicants fully . <p> The second priority for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and other vulnerable migrants . This requires , first and foremost , agreement by more countries to accept refugees . <p> In recent years , the UN Refugee Agency has been able to resettle fewer than 75,000 of more than 20 million refugees annually . <p> Millions end up in protracted displacement , spending an estimated 25 years , on average , stuck in limbo , unsure when they might return home . In 2016 , developed countries should agree to accept a combined total approaching a million refugees annually , either through resettlement or by issuing humanitarian , student , labor , and other visas . <p> With Canada alone saying that it will resettle 50,000 Syrian refugees this year , it is clear that this target is achievable . <p> At the same time , the international community must support the integration of refugees in major host countries like Turkey , Kenya , Lebanon , and Jordan . As it stands , such countries receive just a fraction of the $3,000-5,000 per refugee required annually to provide adequate housing , food , health care , schooling , and job @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ If migrants are viewed as a burden or , worse , a security threat , reactionary political forces will continue to gain ground , cutting off opportunities for newcomers and turning such fears into a self-fulfilling prophesy . <p> If , however , host countries enthusiastically integrate migrants , everyone will benefit -- including home countries ( for example , through remittances ) . Last month in Paris , the international community proved that it could subordinate national self-interest to a greater global goal : confronting climate change . In 2016 , the same thing must happen to forge a better system for protecting migrants . <p> It is a matter of life and death for 20 million refugees and millions of other vulnerable migrants -- and a profound test of the civic health of democratic societies worldwide . <p> Peter D Sutherland is United Nations Special Representative of the Secretary-General for International Migration .
