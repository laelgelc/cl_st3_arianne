<h> Improving earthquake risk management capacity <p> Farid Hasan Ahmed <p> In the early hours of January 04 , 2016 , an earthquake of 6.7 magnitude shook Imphal , the capital of the Indian state of Manipur , bordering Myanmar and Bangladesh . Strong tremors were felt in Bangladesh . Five people died and over 100 were injured in the panic-stricken situation when the powerful earthquake hit Manipur , some 353 kms away from Dhaka . Many people woke up to the tremor at 5:05 local time.The panicking people hurried to the streets while some jumped off their balconies , leading to wounds . <p> An earthquake is a sudden shaking of the ground triggered by the breaking and shifting of rocks beneath the earth 's surface . Earthquakes can take place at any time of the year . This shaking can cause damage to infrastructures , disrupt gas , electric and phone service ; and sometimes cause landslides , avalanches , flash floods , fires , and huge , destructive ocean waves ( tsunamis ) . <p> A large number of people ( an estimated 50 million @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the area , many of whom reside in densely populated locations of Bangladesh , Bhutan , India , Nepal , Afghanistan and Pakistan . Bangladesh has a long history of seismic activity related to its proximity to the Himalayas . Three great earthquakes of magnitudes exceeding 8 were felt in 1897 , 1934 and 1950 , and four others exceeding magnitude 7 were felt between 1869 and 1950 . <p> A huge earthquake occurred in 1762 along the Burma Arc plate boundary . It caused a local tsunami in the Bay of Bengal and caused at least 200 deaths . In 1897 , a large earthquake pushed up the northern edge of the Shillong Plateau . <p> Even though Bangladesh territories have not faced large earthquakes since the 1918 Srimangal shake , it has experienced around 116 seismic turbulences since January 2007 . This is an alarming reminder of the seismic risks in Bangladesh . <p> It is acknowledged by almost all concerned that earthquake risk reduction measures of Bangladesh is not at all up to the mark and it needs to be improved immediately . Heavy density of building and inhabitants - especially in old part of the city , poor construction , poor utility services , narrow lanes , shortage of evacuation spaces are the danger factors of the city . <p> Disregard for the national building code by the builders and related actors and lack of necessary enforcement has left cities at risk with various vulnerabilities . <p> When an earthquake occurs in a densely populated area , it may cause deaths and injuries as well as extensive stuff damage . Most earthquake-related injuries result from collapsing walls or floors , flying glass , and falling objects as a result of the ground shaking or people trying to move during the shaking . <p> In a " worst-case scenario " , more than 100,000 people may die and numerous others wounded if a 7.5 magnitude earthquake from the nearby Madhupur Fault were to hit the capital , according to a 2009 study by the Comprehensive Disaster Management Programme ( CDMP ) under the Ministry of Disaster Management . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ -- Dhaka , Chittagong and Sylhet -- are extremely vulnerable to earthquakes . <p> Taking into account the current capacity , risk and vulnerability of Bangladesh to earthquake , the following steps and measures could be undertaken by the relevant organisations/actors in improving further the emergency and earthquake risk reduction performances . <p> Preparedness for a rapid response encompasses those measures taken before a disaster event aimed at minimising loss of life , interference of critical services when disaster occurs . It includes the formulation of workable earthquake specific emergency plans , maintenance of inventories and training of workforce . It will also require search and rescue measures as well as evacuation plans for communities at risk . <p> Prior to any emergency situation , the contingency plan provides the basis for identifying and further developing response capacity . It is expected that responsible authorities will act appropriately for reducing the risks in line with the Disaster Management Vision of the Government of Bangladesh . The vision is to reduce the risk of people , especially the poor and the disadvantaged , from the effects of natural , environmental and human-induced hazards , to a manageable and acceptable level , and to have in place an efficient emergency response system capable of handling large-scale disasters . The major areas of interventions for reducing earthquake risk could be : <p> 1 ) Integration of Earthquake Risk Reduction in existing development works and future initiatives of different government departments and Non-Government organisations ( NGOs ) of the country is very much important . The government authorities and NGOs need to redesign their earthquake risk reduction programme to ensure that they maximise disaster mitigation potential and incorporate appropriate structural and non-structural measures which are fit technically , environmentally , culturally and economically . <p> 2 ) People should be encouraged @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to build infrastructures considering the risk of earthquakes . It is important to develop culture of earthquake resilience through promoting mitigation measures and raising awareness of all actors including communities at risk . Sensitising key actors for earthquake resistant infrastructures through promoting and practising Bangladesh National Building Code ( BNBC ) is vital to improve earthquake risk management in Bangladesh . <p> 3 ) Infrastructure , particularly electric polls and bill boards should be properly installed , monitored and maintained . It is urgently needed to retrofit public buildings such as hospitals , schools , offices , staff quarters etc . Masons and builders should be trained by relevant authority with a follow up and monitoring mechanism . <p> 4 ) Earthquake preparedness through regular drills and simulation exercise should be organised by local government bodies particularly in the most vulnerable locations with the support of relevant duty bearers . <p> 5 ) The respective authorities at different levels should strengthen their capacity building .
