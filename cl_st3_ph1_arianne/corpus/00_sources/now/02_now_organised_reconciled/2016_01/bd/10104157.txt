<p> A key issue highlighted by the recent Gobeshona conference on climate change in Dhaka is the lack of accountability for commitments made at the Paris COP21 summit to deliver its target of providing $100 billion of climate finance . <p> This is a vital challenge for energy ministers to address at this week 's assembly of the International Renewable Energy Agency . <p> Bangladesh is currently considering the goal of quadrupling renewable solar power generation capacity from 411MW to 1,740MW by 2021 . New capacity like the 200MW plant envisaged at Teknaf and the 7.6MW solar power plant just announced for Sirajganj are steps in the right direction . <p> The government should work on ways to strengthen international commitments further to ensure renewable energy production can be grown much faster , both within Bangladesh and around the world . <p> A global clean-energy revolution is imperative to stem greenhouse emissions and temper the enormous threat posed by climate change to millions of people in our country . <p> The success of organisations like IDCOL in delivering millions of solar-powered home electricity systems proves the multiple benefits @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ they reduce emissions and create jobs , they also raise living standards , which is vital to improve people 's resilience to climate change . <p> Such benefits should be encouraged further by cutting subsidies on fossil fuels and taxing carbon emissions to incentivise greater investment in renewable sources . <p> This will not only help the government meet national targets and support the growing number of jobs in Bangladesh 's clean-energy sector , it will be invaluable in persuading other countries to do the same and ensure all nations maximise efforts to reduce greenhouse gas emission .
