<h> Sanchez : Pensioners <h> Sanchez : Pensioners <p> WHAT have coal-fired plants got to do with the presidential veto of House Bill 5842 that would have given a P2,000 across-the-board increase in retired members ' monthly pensions , raising the minimum pension to P3,200 from the current P1,200 ? <p> Nothing , of course . But the non-relationship would explain President Benigno S. Aquino 's state policy priorities . <p> Two years ago , former Department of Environment and Natural Resources secretary Heherson Alvarez said that " coal and fossil fuels are heavily subsidized , and factoring in the externalities of pollution and climate change costs , these energy systems ( are ) the most costly and , at the same time , most destructive to the environment . " <p> Expanding coal use in all parts of the world will be very costly in terms of environmental ruin , reduction of water supply , and impairment of the food system . These are the environmental and social costs that , in the Philippines , the state will have to pay . <p> In this era of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ coal for their energy needs , the Philippines ' coal-fired plants is projected to grow to 86 percent in 2030 , reducing alternative energy use from the current 6 to a mere 14 percent . <p> Under Aquino 's watch and beyond his term , 23 new coal-fired power plants will be established by 2020 . <p> In other words , the state ( read : the taxpayers ) -- not the polluters -- will shoulder the costs of environmental clean-ups and pay the costs of feeding the citizens . So , in the conflict of interests , it is us , the citizens , versus them , the corporate polluters , and their politician backers . <p> But what has this got to do with social security and the presidential veto ? Plenty . <p> The retirees are going to be left on their own when they are no longer strong and productive .
