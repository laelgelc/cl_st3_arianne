<p> DUBLIN -- The Mediterranean migration crisis has delivered two critical lessons . First , Europe and the international community have grossly inadequate systems for protecting vulnerable migrants . Second , in the absence of such systems , populist leaders will prey on fear to gain political support , undermining the liberal , tolerant societies that have taken 70 years of hard work to build . <p> That is why vigorous action at the European and global levels is essential in 2016 . In September , United Nations Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon will convene an extraordinary summit dedicated to building a fair global system for protecting refugees and vulnerable migrants . One hopes that countries will come prepared to make tangible , enduring commitments . <p> Such commitments were sorely lacking in 2015 . Indeed , the global community could have blunted the crisis by providing even modest support for the three frontline countries -- Turkey , Lebanon and Jordan -- which together host some four million Syrian refugees . More than a million people risked their lives crossing the Mediterranean in 2015 , and then endured grueling journeys through the Balkans . Almost 4,000 people died on the way , and many European countries turned away those who survived , refusing them safe haven . <p> Cynical political leaders ruthlessly capitalized on popular anxiety by promoting an odious nationalist vision that either ignores or distorts real-life experiences with immigration . In the United States , for example , not one of the 780,000 refugees resettled since Sept. 11 , 2001 , has executed a terroristattack . Meanwhile , immigrants typically pay more in taxes than they receive in benefits . <p> Nonetheless , extremist forces are dangerously close to taking political power in some European states , and are gaining traction even in formerly liberal bastions . The EU 's program to process the million refugees who arrived on its shores has succeeded in " relocating " a mere 190 of them . Checks at the borders of six countries within the Schengen area have been reinstituted , at least temporarily . To the rest of the world , the EU appears chauvinistic and inept . <p> Of course , the crisis is not solely for Europe to solve ; responsibility is not defined by proximity . But the EU might now face an existential threat , which it can overcome only with a strong show of solidarity and global leadership . That is why EU states must take the lead in proposing solutions . <p> The most urgent priority is to create safe and legal paths for refugees to reach Europe . This does not imply that every vulnerable migrant must be accepted . But the EU should be more systematically generous in determining how many to admit , and it should implement organized ways to facilitate their entry . Equally important , it would put pressure on the rest of the global community to contribute . <p> That brings us to the second priority for 2016 : building a robust global system to protect refugees and other vulnerable migrants . This requires , first and foremost , agreement by more countries to accept refugees . In recent years , the UN Refugee Agency has been able to resettle fewer than 75,000 of more than 20 million refugees yearly . Millions end up in protracted displacement , spending an estimated 25 years , on average , stuck in limbo , unsure when they might return home . <p> In 2016 , developed countries should agree to accept a combined total approaching a million refugees annually , either through resettlement or by issuing humanitarian , student , labor , and other visas . As it stands , such countries receive just a fraction of the $3,000-5,000 per refugee required annually to provide adequate housing , food , healthcare , schooling , and job training during the first few years of displacement . And that does not account for the costs of building or upgrading infrastructure . Lebanon 's water-supply system , for example , is faltering under the strain of the massive influx of refugees . In exchange for funding , host countries should agree to integrate refugees fully into their schools , labor markets and civic institutions . <p> But integrating migrants effectively will be impossible unless European and other countries change how they perceive migrants .
