<h> Opinion Column <h> LaFleche : On bugs and climate change <p> If you want to know why we need to take climate change seriously , all you need to do is listen closely to Fiona Hunter . <p> Hunter , a medical entomologist at Brock University , is leading a team of researchers working to determine if local mosquitoes can carry or transmit the Zika virus , a pathogen the World Health Organization has classified as a global threat . <p> During a Friday press conference in which Hunter talked about her research , she noted that Niagara is a " gateway to Canada " for bugs . Because of Niagara 's geography and climate , it can be an ideal place for a new arrival to set up shop . <p> While the rest of the press conference returned to the details of her Zika study , the bit about Niagara being a gateway should n't be forgotten . <p> It 's very likely a sign of things to come . <p> When past outbreaks of insect-borne illness happened in warmer climates , like the current @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to fret over . <p> Canadian weather acted as a barrier to all manner of bugs . Even if they arrived , these insects would not be able to get an ecological foothold . It was just too cold for them to survive . <p> Not anymore . Hunter said the warming of the climate allows many critters , including biting insects that carry pathogens harmful to humans , to migrate north . <p> In the specific case of Zika , this might have already happened . Hunter said a known vector for Zika , the mosquito species Aedes albopictus , has already been found in Ontario . It does n't belong here , so she 's trying to find out if the species has established itself . <p> In short , Zika is the problem de jour but the reality of climate change is the driver behind it for Canada . <p> We still have problems coming to grips with climate change . It 's not the first time Canadian scientific ignorance has been on display . <p> In 2007 , an Angus Reid survey found that 42 per cent of Canadians believe humans and dinosaurs co-existed . That belief is a 65-million-year-old error that essentially means a breathtaking number of Canadians look upon the Flintstones as less of a cartoon and more of a docudrama . <p> Not accepting scientific reality taints the way we approach handling the problem . If we deny that human technology is a significant player in climate change , we 're going to be less likely to consider changing how we use that technology . <p> So we dither about , saying things like " Well , yes , climate change might be a problem but we need to create jobs first , then we can worry about it . But she says the community of medical entomologists is very small and shrinking . <p> She told me there just are n't that many people stepping into the field . She 's concerned when her generation retires , there wo n't be enough new entomologists to replace them . <p> Given climate change 's possible negative impacts on human health , we need sentries in the watchtowers .
