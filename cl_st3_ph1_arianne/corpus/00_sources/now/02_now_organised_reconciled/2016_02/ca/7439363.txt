<h> Time to chill : University of Alberta 's ice-core storage and research facility receives $2.3-million grant <p> Construction is set to begin next month on a University of Alberta facility to store and do research on 1,700 metres of core ice from Canada 's Arctic . <p> With a $2.3 million grant from the Canada Foundation for Innovation , plus university promises to pay the rest if the Alberta government does n't come through with its share , the facility 's laboratory and two walk-in freezers should be open to researchers in six or seven months , glaciologist Martin Sharp said . <p> Located in the basement of the South Academic Building , the facility is expected to cost about $4 million and will store the ice-core collection currently located in Ottawa at a Natural Resources Canada site . <p> Many of the researchers there are nearing retirement , so moving the collection to the U of A makes sense , Sharp said , since about six out of eight Canadian ice-core researchers with hands-on experience work out of Edmonton . <p> The U of A also @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ organic contaminants found in ice from the atmosphere , and study how the genomics and biology of the ice is affected by climate change , Sharp said . <p> " It 's a unique , one-off opportunity to save and maintain this collection , " said Sharp , a professor in the department of earth and atmospheric sciences and initiating director of the new facility . <p> " At a time when the ice caps that these ( ice cores ) are collected from are disappearing very fast , we should maximize the amount of information we can gain from them about the history of our country .
