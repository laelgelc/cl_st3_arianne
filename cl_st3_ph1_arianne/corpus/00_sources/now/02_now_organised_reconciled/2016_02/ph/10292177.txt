<h> Gore visit should inspire greater efforts for renewable energy <p> SOMETIME next month , the world 's pioneer in advocating action against climate change , former United States Vice President Al Gore , is visiting the Philippines as part of a worldwide effort to get governments and nations to meet the serious environmental issues arising out of global warming . <p> Gore , founder of two activist organizations that consolidated into the Climate Reality Project , will be here to train a group of " climate reality leaders " who will , in turn educate their communities on the adverse effects of climate change . His visit should inspire more Filipinos to be aware of the threat faced by the world today from melting polar ice and rising sea levels , more devastating storms and floods , and other forms of extreme weather . <p> Members of the House Special Committee on Climate Change , led by Rep. The Department of Energy disclosed that for nearly 5,000 megawatts of power planned in the next five years , over 70 percent of the new power plants are coal-fired . It does not look at all like we are moving in the direction of renewable energy as we committed to do in Paris . <p> It is true we already have a number of renewable energy projects in the country -- including wind power projects in Northern Luzon , geothermal plants in the Visayas , and solar power generating centers in and near Metro Manila .
