<h> Letters <h> Time to stop our wasteful habits <p> TIME and again , we are reminded that Malaysia is a country blessed with abundant water resources . <p> In fact , we are so used to getting water easily we sometimes forget that there are people around the world who are struggling just to quench their thirst with a cup of clean water . <p> Recently , researchers from the Netherlands said that four billion people around the world face the problem of water scarcity . This is quite a big difference from the previous assumption , which was that two billion people did not have enough water . <p> The argument of the new research was simple -- previous assumptions did not account for temporary water scarcity , for example people who did not have enough water for several weeks . <p> This argument makes perfect sense because lack of clean water for even a week can negatively affect a person 's health . This problem is also recognised by the World Economic Forum , which has put water crisis as one of the top risks faced by our planet . It is ranked high in likeliness to happen and high in impact towards society . <p> Not all countries are as lucky as Malaysia in having access to clean water almost every day . According to a report on Palestinian 's access to water in 2015 , people in Palestine use an average of 40 l/c/d ( litres per capita per day ) only . Malaysians , on the other hand , use 232 l/c/d . Penang , the state with the highest water usage , uses nearly 300 l/c/d . <p> The World Health Organisation recommends 150 l/c/d under normal conditions , and 100 l/c/d is set as the minimum for drinking and hygiene purposes . <p> Do you see the difference in water usage between Malaysia and countries that lack access to clean water ?
