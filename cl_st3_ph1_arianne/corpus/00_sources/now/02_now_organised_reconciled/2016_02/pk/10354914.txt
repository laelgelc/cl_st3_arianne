<h> Workshop calls for curbing illegal wildlife trade <p> KARACHI : World Wide Fund ( WWF ) Pakistan initiated a four-day consultative and capacity building workshop to develop a strategy to combat illegal wildlife trade in Pakistan . <p> The workshop is part of the project ' Combating Illegal Wildlife Trade ' by establishing a national monitoring network that benefits local communities and environment , supported by USAID through its small grants and ambassador 's fund programme . <p> Experts from Sindh and Balochistan Wildlife Departments , Ministry of Climate Change , Pakistan Customs , Sindh Judicial Academy and WWF-Pakistan shared the national and provincial efforts being made to combat illegal wildlife trade . <p> He shared a few success stories of illegal wildlife consignment seizure including the freshwater turtle repatriation from China . <p> Speaking on the occasion , Ghulam Ahmad , the director general in Directorate of Research and Training Customs , highlighted that such events also bring an excellent learning opportunity to the departments such as Pakistan Customs , " which are responsible to control illegal trade at our borders and develop synergies with other @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of Climate Change Wildlife Conservator Umeed Khalid pointed out that wildlife traffickers have been adopting new strategies to transport wildlife illegally , thus highlighting the need for law enforcement agencies to adopt new technologies to tackle this challenge . <p> WWF-Pakistan senior Biodiversity Director Rab Nawaz highlighted that the illegal wildlife trade has been recognised as the third most lucrative illegal trade falling closely behind drugs , counterfeiting and human trafficking and generates an estimated $19 billion annually . Muhammad Moazzam Khan , the technical adviser for marine fisheries in WWF-Pakistan , while sharing the scale of illegal fishing practices pointed out a number of " loopholes " in the existing laws and practices which further promotes fisheries crimes .
