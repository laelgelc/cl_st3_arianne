<h> Warm ocean temperatures helped make 2015 a record year for shark attacks <p> Increasingly warm waters around the globe due to climate change and El Ni ? o led to the highest number of shark attacks ever recorded in 2015 , according to data released this week . <p> The University of Florida found that there were 98 unprovoked shark attack incidents last year , surpassing the previous record of 88 in the year 2000 . <p> The numbers follow an upward trend of attacks that scientists at the the university 's International Shark Attack File , which collects and analyzes data on shark attacks , say is due to greater interaction between humans and sharks in the water . <p> That interaction has increased as air and water temperatures have increased , sending humans into the ocean for more months out of the year in more places , and sending sharks into waters that were previously too cold for them , according to George H. Shark populations , meanwhile , are either declining or remaining steady in many parts of the world , he said , due to overfishing , which should theoretically help reduce the number of attacks . But complicating factors have prevented this . <p> The two biggest factors in 2015 were global warming and El Ni ? o , he said , both of which have warmed previously-cool waters enough to make them hospitable to sharks likely to bite . <p> " Warmer temperatures also affect humans , in the sense that we also have a water temperature preference , so we are more likely to go in the water when the temperature is one we 're happy with , " he said .
