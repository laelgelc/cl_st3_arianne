<p> Forget the threat of rising political instability and trade conflict : these are simply the symptoms of a wider disease . The real danger is the rise of wealth inequality across the world in recent decades . Never have so few owned so much , at the expense of so many . This deeply worrying picture has been turbocharged by capitalism allowed to run amok by laissez-faire governments , the rapid growth of multinational corporations spurred by technology and the vast expansion of global finance . <p> That is n't to say capitalism can not deliver significant benefits in terms of personal prosperity . Across the world it has allowed more than a billion people to lift themselves out of extreme poverty since 1990 . Yet there are growing questions about the ability of capitalism to deliver for as many people as possible . <p> A report from Credit Suisse this month found that the top percentile of the world 's super-rich alone accounts for almost half ( 47% ) of all household wealth .
