<p> Saskatchewan Premier Scott Moe accused Ottawa of cynically planning to buy votes with money collected under its federal carbon tax , characterizing it as a " sham . " <p> The federal government announced Tuesday it is accepting the provincial climate change plan , Prairie Resilience , but does not consider it good enough to adequately address pollution , specifically when it comes to electricity generation and natural gas pipelines . <p> To that end , starting in April , Saskatchewan will be subject to a carbon tax on natural gas and electricity production , to be collected from producers and distributors . It 's expected the cost -- starting at roughly four cents a litre on gasoline -- will be passed on to consumers . <p> Federal Minister of Public Safety and Regina-Wascana MP Ralph Goodale was in Regina , speaking to local reporters about the carbon tax . He warned of the " potentially deadly risks " of climate change , estimating the combined direct and indirect cost of the new tax to the average Saskatchewan household would average $403 in 2019 and rise to $946 in 2022 . <p> But a rebate of roughly $600 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ) would also be provided at tax time to that same family , prompting Moe 's accusation of vote buying .
