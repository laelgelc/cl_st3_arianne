<h> University of Oregon museum installs life-size mammoth replicas <h> Monday <p> Oregon 's mammoths went extinct about 13,000 years ago , but they 've left traces of their existence throughout the state . <p> That 's one reason behind the University of Oregon 's Museum of Natural and Cultural History 's unveiling of its permanent , life-size mammoth installation this week in Eugene . <p> Two enormous sculptures of Columbian mammoths have been erected in the museum 's courtyard -- an adult female more than 12 feet tall with a young calf just behind her . <p> The pair were trucked in from Kearney , Mo. , where artist Gary Staab constructed them using steel , resin and fiberglass . <p> The $100,000 project was crowdfunded through community support and donations , said Kristin Strommer , director of communications for the museum . <p> " They really are an icon of Oregon 's ice age -- their fossils are frequently found throughout the state , " Strommer said . <p> In 2014 , paleontologist Greg Retallack and six UO students discovered footprints of a herd of Columbian mammoths at Fossil Lake in south Central Oregon . <p> Retallack @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the museum 's Condon Collection of Fossils . <p> The life-size mammoth sculptures are meant to act as ambassadors for the museum , Strommer said . <p> " They inspire conversations about how species relate to environments and how incredibly interdependent we are with our environment , " Strommer said . " How species can adapt , survive and thrive -- or not -- in a rapidly changing ecological context . " Its fossils , common in late Pleistocene sediments in the Willamette Valley and across Oregon , offer clues about how our environment has changed over the millennia .
