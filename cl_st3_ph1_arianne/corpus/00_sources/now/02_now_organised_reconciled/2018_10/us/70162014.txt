<h> Gardening Etcetera : Reflections of a naturalist at Ghost Ranch , New Mexico <p> Last week I stayed at Ghost Ranch , in Abiquiu , New Mexico , where artist Georgia O'Keeffe had a home and studio . Staff had posted signs around the ranch , " You are in Mountain Lion Country , " and mule deer tracks and scat were everywhere -- including right in front of the door to our little adobe cabin . <p> I knew there had to be a big cat around ; deer are their main prey . I wanted to see if I could find some mountain lion signs -- tracks or scat , so I decided to hike in the deep arroyo right next to the entry road . With the recent rains , the sand substrate would offer me the best chance at seeing tracks . As I walked , I saw a few that might be bobcat or lion , but so many critters were using the arroyo as a trail , including humans and dogs , that I could n't be sure . The scat was segmented and full of pale reddish deer fur . I searched for a deer carcass cached in the old cottonwoods . Nothing . Good . Still , I quickened my pace and scanned the cliffs . The warning sign had said not to hike alone , and here I was . I climbed out as soon as I could . <p> With 21,000 acres and a wide variety of biological communities , Ghost Ranch is heaven for a naturalist . Elevations range from 6,500-7,000 feet , with annual precipitation of about 10 inches . Higher up on the north side of the box canyon , stream-side , you see spruce and pine . Cottonwoods line the perennial creek and arroyo . Pinyon-juniper and scrublands take over at lower elevations away from water sources . <p> As I explored the plant communities , I was surprised to see some of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as we have in Doney Park .
