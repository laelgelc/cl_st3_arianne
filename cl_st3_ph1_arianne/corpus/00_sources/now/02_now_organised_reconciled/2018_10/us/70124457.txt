<p> The Nobel Prize in Economics was awarded on Monday to two influential thinkers who came up with ideas that we must embrace even though they are out of favor right now : 1 . The long-run is important ; 2 . Ideas matter ; and 3 . The commons belong to all of us . <p> Paul Romer and William Nordhaus remind us that the market is n't perfect . Externalities create failures . On its own , the free market wo n't provide enough investment in ideas or technology , nor will it protect the environment . Capitalism needs nudges to deliver the optimal outcome . <p> Before Romer and Nordhaus came along , mainstream economics did n't have much to say about two of the most important factors governing the long-term growth rate of the economy : knowledge and nature . <p> Unlike most economists who are quoted in the media , Romer and Nordhaus were not particularly interested in how fast the economy would grow in the next quarter or two , but in how it would grow over the next decades . <p> Where economists such as John Maynard Keynes were preoccupied with important @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ right now ( during the crisis of the Great Depression , Keynes and other set the future aside because , after all , in the long run , we 'd all be dead ) , Romer and Nordhaus led a revolution in thinking about growth over decades and centuries . <p> Needless to say , neither Wall Street nor Washington seems to care much about anything beyond the next fiscal quarter or the next election . We humans are notoriously short-sighted . <p> When he was still a grad student , Romer wondered why economic growth had been flat for almost all of human history before taking off in the industrial revolution . The explanation was that there had been an explosion of technology , but Romer asked the question : Why did it happen at one time and not another , in one place but not another ? Most economic models take technological change as a given ; sometimes there was lots of change and other times there was n't much , but there did n't seem to be much anyone could do about it . <p> Romer disagreed @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ came from hundreds , or thousands , or millions of people coming up with new ideas , improvements on old ideas , and the adoption of those ideas and technologies . He believed that the right policies could help create the right environment for growth . <p> In the language of economists , Romer came up with an endogenous growth theory , which put human capital , knowledge and technology at the heart of the theory instead of it being something that just happened with no explanation . <p> If you want your economy to grow faster over the next generation or two , you need to cultivate ideas , which means you need the right kind of institutions and incentives . It means sacrificing a little bit of consumption right now in exchange for investing more in the future . <h> Cost of destroying the environment <p> All this may seem obvious , but there 's a strong counterrevolution in place . Lots of Americans do n't believe that pumping money into the economy can pull us out of a depression , or temporarily jolt growth to 3% nearly a decade into an expansion . Lots of Americans do n't believe that humans burning carbon-based fossil fuels is raising the temperature on Earth to catastrophic levels . <p> William Nordhaus was one of the first who attempted to tally the cost of destroying the environment . Like knowledge , nature appears to be something that is just there , like the air . The Earth -- the air , the water , the land and the sea , the plants and animals -- are there for our use . <p> But because no one owns the atmosphere or the oceans , no one has any economic incentive to care for them . In the words of the prize committee : " He thus not only regarded nature as a constraint on human activity , but also as something greatly influenced by economic activity . " <p> Nordhaus developed practical tools to study how carbon levels , temperature , and economic and social costs and benefits interacted with each other . His growth model is an attempt to assess the costs and benefits of various mitigation strategies , or of doing nothing . <p> His models played a central role in the assessment of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change that immediate action is needed urgently to prevent the temperature from rising too much .
