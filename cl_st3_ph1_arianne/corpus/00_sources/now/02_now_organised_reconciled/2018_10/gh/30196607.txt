<h> Cry me a river : Low water levels causing chaos in Germany <p> A new island in Lake Constance . A river in Berlin flowing backward . Dead fish on the banks of lakes and ponds . Barges barely loaded so they do n't run aground . <p> A hot , dry summer has left German rivers and lakes at record low water levels , causing chaos for the inland shipping industry , environmental damage and billions of euros ( dollars ) in losses -- a scenario that experts warn could portend the future as global temperatures rise . <p> The drought-like conditions have hit nearly 90 percent of the country this year . <p> In Magdeburg , the Elbe River has been so low that no ships carrying goods south to Leipzig or on to the Czech Republic have been able to pass through since the end of June , said Hartmut Rhein of the city 's waterways and shipping department . <p> The river 's down to a depth of about 50 centimeters ( less than 20 inches ) there , when at least double that level is needed for normal shipping traffic , he said @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to completely unload ships and transfer their cargoes to other means of transportation , " he said . <p> The situation is similar across Germany . The mighty Rhine has hit its lowest water levels ever at several points , and other major rivers like the Danube , the Weser and the Main are all far below normal . <p> On the waterways that are still navigable , the lower water levels have actually led to increased shipping traffic , as companies pack less weight onto boats so they do n't ride so low in the water . That means they must send more vessels out to carry the same amount of freight . <p> " All the ships on the Rhine are going around the clock to transport goods that would normally be on fewer ships , " said Rolf Nagelschmidt of Cologne 's waterways and shipping office .
