<h> Africa bets on technology to lure youth to farming <p> Since Marie Chantal Akingeneye lost her only cow to an unknown illness , she has no source for manure for her fruits and vegetables - but she hopes a new phone app could help . <p> After attending a training by the United Nations , which developed the technology , she thinks the app will help to keep her goats and pigs healthy and modernise her farm for her six-year-old son to take over . <p> " It tells farmers about symptoms and diseases that attack livestock , " the 28-year-old single mother told the Thomson Reuters Foundation , sitting outside her home in northern Rwanda . <p> " The cow died because I did n't know it was sick . Now I do n't have much fertiliser . " <p> Donors and African governments hope such tools could also lure youth to farming as the continent struggles with rising hunger , unemployment and migration . <p> Africa has the world 's youngest population - 60 percent of its 1.2 billion people are under 25 - but only 3 million jobs are created for some 12 million young @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Development Bank says . <p> The free app , which was created by the Food and Agriculture Organization ( FAO ) , also provides information on weather , market prices for crops , and producing and conserving nutritious foods . <p> " Digital technologies like these can make farming more interesting , " said Andy Jarvis , research director at Colombia-based International Center for Tropical Agriculture ( CIAT ) . <p> " They could be transformational for Africa . A simple text to a farmer just to say , ' The weather outlook for the next three days is this ' can fundamentally change what they do .
