<h> Conserving Africa 's precious resource base while fighting hunger <p> ROSEMARY Chate 's seven children gather around the table inside their home in Malela , a village in Zambia 's remote Northern Province . They dig their spoons into bowls of food prepared by their mother for the second time that day . <p> Felister Namfukwe on her farm . Credit : Self Help Africa <p> Not long ago , Rosemary 's family would assemble to eat just once a day their resources , for many months each year , were so thin that they needed to ration their food supplies to just a single family meal . <p> This is the reality for millions of African farmers like Rosemary . Many challenges are keeping yields on the continent low . Farmers lack access to inputs that farmers in developed countries have utilized for decades , from quality seeds and herbicides , to the right type of fertilizer for their undernourished soils . <p> The hand hoe even in this century is still the main tool for smallholder families . Migration to urban areas and the impact of AIDS have left many rural homesteads with a labour shortage @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and rural families grapple with adaption . Changes in the climate have brought with them not only drought and flooding , but new plant diseases and insect attacks . <p> The fall armyworm in sub-Saharan Africa has caused tremendous damage . This unpredictable reality has made crop management very difficult , and indigenous knowledge alone can no longer suffice . <p> African farmers need scientific innovation from low to high tech to face these challenges . Yet preserving Africa 's environment , its most precious resources after its people , is also a high priority . <p> This is one of the fundamental concerns of agroecology ensuring farmers can produce food and earn a good living , while keeping the natural resource base intact . <p> With the right approaches that blend traditional knowledge with scientific innovation , this can be achieved . <p> At Self Help Africa , we are working with farmers to achieve this through the implementation of conservation agriculture . In Zambia alone , we have reached over 80,000 farmers in the last five years . <p> Conservation farming involves a combination of approaches . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ species , such as groundnuts , which can naturally fix nitrogen to the soil , and cassava , for example . <p> This ensures maximum use of a piece of land that has been cleared producing more food with less resources . Crop rotation and mulching , along with an integrated use of mineral and organic fertilizers are also part conservation agriculture . <p> 59-year old Felister Namfukwe has seen the benefits of this farming approach . Not only are her soils healthier , but her income is as well . With the help of her sons and her profits from groundnuts , she is building a new home made of brick , replacing her previous mud home . <p> " Being part of this ( Self Help Africa ) project has lightened my burden , " she told us . <p> We also work with local farmers to build their capacity to grow good quality seed , and to strengthen community based seed systems .
