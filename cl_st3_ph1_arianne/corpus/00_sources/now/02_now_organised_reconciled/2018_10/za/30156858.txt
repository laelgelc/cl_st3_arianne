<h> ' Year of extremes ' for shrinking Swiss glaciers in 2018 <p> Despite an exceptionally snow-filled winter , Swiss glaciers have lost 2.5 percent of their volume this year , according to a report Tuesday which dubbed 2018 " a year of extremes " . <p> This year 's record-breaking temperatures have greatly contributed to the loss of " a fifth of ( the glaciers ' ) volume over the past decade " , according to the annual study on the state of the glaciers , published by the Swiss Academies of Science . <p> And this despite the piles and piles of snow that fell during the 2017-18 winter season , following three consecutive years of little snow in the Alpine country . <p> Snow acts like a protective covering that prevents glaciers from melting . <p> " Up until the end of March , there was still more than twice as much snow as usual above an altitude of 2,000m , " the report 's authors said . But the exceptional snowfall at the start of the year was matched by soaring temperatures and little to no precipitation after March . <p> According to the Swiss @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ranked among the hottest on record after 2003 and 2015 . And the period from April to September was " by far the hottest ever recorded " in Switzerland , the report said . <p> It pointed to the case of Weissfluhjoch peak ( 2,540 metres ) , which hosts the Swiss Institute for Snow and Avalanche research . The peak saw no snowfall of more than 1cm between May 17 and September 4 - a first in the 81 years of record-keeping , the report said . <p> The heat and lack of precipitation " not only melted large quantities of winter snow ( up to five metres on some glaciers ) , but also melted the ice , " said Matthias Huss , in charge of the Glacier Monitoring Network ( Glamos ) . <p> " The shrinking of the glaciers is directly linked to climate change , " Huss said . If the planet continues warming at the same pace as today , he warned that " many of Switzerland 's smaller glaciers will completely disappear " .
