<h> Why women farmers are paid less than men ? <h> Share this article <p> Women provide crucial support in Indian agriculture and household livelihood ; however , they remain confined as workers . Designed farm tools available are mainly used by male farmers , and rural women are left to use traditional tools and procedures resulting in low efficiency , drudgery , occupational health risks , and low income . Their contributions fall under indirect material income and go unacknowledged even in decision making . Illiteracy , lack of knowledge among rural women , improper training , and less opportunity for skill development worsen their subdued existence . <p> Formal education , availability of economically viable agricultural machinery for women , training , extension services , social engineering , and gender budgeting is need of the hour . Gender budgeting would assess the quantity and adequacy of allocation of resources for women and establish the extent to which gender commitments are translated into budgetary commitments . While earnings of both men and women farmers were increased in the last decade i.e. from 2006 to 2017 , the wage inequality continues and the women farmers still earn less . <p> It is important to mention that the wage gap was highest in 2012 to 2014 period when women farmers received 27 % less than the men . <p> Although there was just a marginal reduction in wage gap ( by 4.43% ) between 2012 to 2013 and 2014 to 2015 , nothing much seems to have changed , proves the data on All India Annual Average Daily wage rate that includes 20 major states . In actual fact , the wage difference once again figured significantly during 2015 to 2016 when our women farmers got 25.37% less than the male farmers . <p> Additionally , in 2011 , women formed 75% of the agricultural sector labor force . While men migrate , the responsibility of taking care of the family lies on women 's shoulders . They prefer to stay and complement family 's income by working on the fields . Migration , hence , has been one of the major reasons for women getting involved in this sector , even though it pays them less . Further , women cultivators are at loss without land titles . They participate in agricultural work as unpaid subsistence labour and are not recognized as farmers . Hence , they are unable to access credits and government benefits .
