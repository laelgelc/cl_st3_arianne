<h> Clipping the wrong end <p> The Centre has given in to pre-poll pressure and cut petrol and diesel prices by Rs 2.50 . This will be welcomed by many quarters . But here are some facts . India 's average annual inflation rate was 7.6% a year over the last 10 years . <p> Nominal prices of all sorts of goods and services are reaching record highs all the time . But has consumer purchasing power been affected by the earlier rise in the recent nominal price of fuels ? <p> In 2005 , an average rural labourer earned a monthly salary in the order of Rs 71 . In the same year , petrol prices were in the order of Rs 41 a litre . In other words , a rural labourer would have had to work more than half a month to buy a litre of petrol . <p> Today , rural wages are in the order of Rs 310 a month , and before Thursday 's cut , petrol price was about Rs 80 a litre . A rural labourer would have had to work about a week to afford a litre of petrol at @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ measured in terms of consumers ' purchasing power , petrol is indeed cheaper than ever before . <p> Except this is not quite true . Since August 2017 , rural wages have lost about 30% of their real purchasing power with respect to transport fuels , as the nominal price increase of fuels has outpaced wage increases . <p> This would suggest two timescales for policymakers to consider . In the long run , as India 's productivity increases and wages rise , consumers ' purchasing power with respect to fuels and transport services will increase tremendously . However , in the short term , a look at past data shows that there have been periods , sometimes extended , when the price of transport fuels grew as fast or faster than wages , particularly those of the weakest in society . <p> Between the oil price nadir in mid-2016 and January this year , the rise of global oil prices drove up petrol prices by about Rs 16 a litre . Exchange rate depreciation is now responsible for just slightly less than half of the rise in fuel prices since January , with the rest being due to the continued rise of international oil prices . This suggests that cutting fuel taxes , as has been done on Thursday , may be counterproductive . <p> In 2016 , fuel import made up almost 60% of the current account deficit ( CAD ) . Thus , a fuel-based tax cut is likely to worsen CAD , potentially contributing to further rupee depreciation . Furthermore , given its importance to government finances , a fuel-based tax cut can now raise concerns about India 's fiscal deficit , spook investors and weaken the rupee . <p> The balance of these considerations would have argued against a tax cut . It does n't make sense for GoI to support the rupee by restricting imports and the net @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , cut the consumer price of India 's largest import . <p> So , are fuels in India excessively taxed ? India 's fuel taxes are in the order of 45-55% of the final price . This is slightly lower than European countries ( 60-65% ) , but higher than Australia and the US ( 20-40% ) . Why should a low-income country like India tax fuels so highly ? <p> Because the consumption of transport fuels has a number of significant externalities . First , in a resource-poor country , it necessitates imports and forex . Second , the current , oil-dependent transport system creates congestion , pollution , infrastructure pressures and contributes hugely to global climate change . <p> Third , transport fuel taxation tends to be more progressive than other forms of indirect taxation . <p> For these reasons , a high level of fuel taxation is justified . It is better that imported fuels are highly taxed , consumption dampened and the benefits of that taxation are recycled back into the economy . Central and state governments should focus on reducing the long-term dependency of the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ transport and infrastructure development for electric vehicles . Higher fuel taxes bring the resources to do so . <p> In the current period of higher global prices and a jittery rupee , India , unfortunately , can scarce afford to consume more imported oil . Thursday 's fuel price cut goes against this economics . It is laudable that GoI wants to protect vulnerable consumers . But it has jeopardised the same people in the long run by cutting fuel price .
