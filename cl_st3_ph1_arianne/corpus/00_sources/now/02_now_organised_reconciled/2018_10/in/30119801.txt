Gupta Use of night soil compost or manure for agriculture in Ladakh As the soils of Ladakh are generally light textured i.e. , mostly sandy type and loamy sand and rarely sandy loam , so are very low in available plant nutrients and organic matter content . Hence , an application of organic manure is primary step to increase the binding capacity of soil particles into aggregates due to its binding effects as well as to enhance the water retention and cation exchange capacities of soils . For this purpose , the farmers of Ladakh nourished their soils with night soil compost . There is no doubt that farmers of Ladakh had devised an organic farming system through application of night soil compost since ages , which is not only a sustainable livelihood option but is also an ecologically suitable . However , this farming system is now loosing its sheen because of a number of reasons , which stand elaborated elsewhere ( Gupta and Upadhaya , 2006 ) . Water conservation from modern to traditional/ancestors footprints With the burgeoning human and livestock population , not only the demand of food and fodder production has increased but the water requirement also goes on increasing simultaneously . The only possible way to boost agriculture production is to ameliorate agricultural technology , extensive and intensive irrigation , and more importantly , water conservation in all walks of life . As has been mentioned in our granthas -- Atharva Veda , that if water of wells , ponds and rivers , etc. , used and managed efficiently , it will reduce the intensity of drought and scarcity of water . Our ancestors were wise enough because they dug wells , constructed bowalies/baulies , ponds and tanks . They had devised old and traditional techniques of conservation of water . In one of the shalokas of Atharva Veda , it is stated that banks of the above said waterbodies must be shaded by planting trees like vata or banyan tree ( Ficus bengalensis ) , plaksha ( Ficus infectoria ) , jambu or black berry ( Syzygium cumini ) , kadamba @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ or mango tree ( Mangifera indica ) , etc. as mentioned by Gupta ( 2005a ) . The basic purpose to plant these tree species was to prevent evaporation and conserve water as well as to improve the environment . It is worthwhile to mention that hitherto the people of Siwaliks of Jammu , especially submountainous region also known as Kandi belt , had been meeting all their water requirements from the ponds which stored rain water ( Gupta , 2010 ) . These ponds , which were mostly built during the Dogra rule ( 1846 to 1947 ) , till as late as about 50 years ago were the major sources of drinking water for the area . Not only this , " many of the peasants used to irrigate their mango trees grown in the fields or in kitchen gardens through pitchers after filling them with water from the ponds during morning hours " , as the elders of Akhnoor tehsil say . Apart from drinking purpose and irrigating the water of these ponds for crops , the people of this belt also used to utilize this water @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , 2010 ) . Similar was also the practice of the people dwelling in Kandi areas of Himachal Pradesh , Punjab and Haryana . Revival of ponds : Owing to climate change , there is now frequent occurrence of drought as has been observed during the years 1998 , 2002 , 2004 and 2009 , especially in north India , where the agriculture production has considerably declined . In the light of above , it is stressed that improvement of indigenous technologies , i.e. revival of ponds , bowalies is now needed to effectively utilize water . Rainwater harvesting : Rainwater harvesting techniques have been used for agriculture in several parts of the world since ancient times . The frequent rains , if harvested over a large area can yield considerable amount of water . The examples of ancient rainwater harvesting involve water/moisture control at a very simple level . It often consists of rows of rocks placed along the contour of slopes . During scarcity of water many of the women of Jammu collected rainwater by spreading a muslin cloth ( chadhar ) after tying its four corners with @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the house or in open varanda ( Gupta , 2009a ) . Nowadays , a number of new water harvesting techniques are being used in a number of cities like Chennai , Jodhpur , Delhi , Meerut and other . The method is site specific and consists of a number of steps ( Gupta , 2009a ) , viz. ( a ) collection of rainwater in collector ( pond ) , ( b ) separation of first rain flush , ( c ) filtration and storage of rainwater , and ( d ) distribution of water . After collection of roof water duly separated of first rain flush in a pond , it must be filtered through sand filter and kept in PVC tank connected to handpump for withdrawal .
