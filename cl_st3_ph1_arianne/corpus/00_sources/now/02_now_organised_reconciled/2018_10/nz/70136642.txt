<h> Petrol price pain shows how to tackle climate change <p> OPINION : It 's often said that climate change is the nuclear issue of our day . <p> Both are existential threats but they have an important difference . <p> The eradication of nuclear weapons does not require us to make any change to our everyday comforts and conveniences . Nor does any ordinary person have to take responsibility for the spread of nuclear arsenals . <p> Climate change , on the other hand , makes nuclear weapons look easy . It stems from the small decisions , habits and actions people make in their daily lives . <p> ? Governments , especially democracies , can influence those decisions but will do so only to the extent that it does not hurt them at the ballot box . We saw an example of this dilemma this week when Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern needed to say something about crippling petrol prices . <p> As luck would have it , she was speaking in the same week the world 's leading climate scientists from the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) warned the world had about a decade @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of 1.5C . <p> 123RF <p> The grass is always greener . <p> The irony could not have been lost on her . On the other , she must have seen the fuel prices as the way of the future and a good lesson for everyone . <p> Her reaction was pure politics . She promised to push through legislation that allows the Commerce Commission to compel fuel companies to provide data to prove they are operating competitively . <p> In fact , no special legislation is needed to show the petrol market is uncompetitive . Apart from a few areas in New Zealand , this has been obvious for years . As an aside , perhaps it 's time to nationalise the country 's fuel distribution system . Make carbon more expensive with something like a carbon tax . Such a tax would reflect the contribution of any product to global warming and would obviously start with oil and coal . <p> If Ardern was consistent , she could have responded to high petrol prices by saying they are just the incentive people need to react to climate change .
