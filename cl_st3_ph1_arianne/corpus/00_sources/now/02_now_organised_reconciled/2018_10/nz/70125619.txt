<h> Rural dwellers less likely to seek help on mental health - survey <p> A survey on critical topics facing rural New Zealand has found stress levels are on the increase . <p> Photo : 123rf <p> The State of the Rural Nation Survey , conducted by Bayer New Zealand and Country TV , showed 70 percent of survey respondents have felt more stress over the last five years . <p> It also showed rural people were less likely than urban dwellers to consider talking to a health professional about stress or anxiety . <p> In addition , 61 percent said living rurally limited access to mental health resources . <p> Agricultural science firm Bayer 's William Malpass said just more than half cited financial pressures as the main reason , while worsening environmental factors affecting people 's work and livelihoods came in at a close second . <p> " Farming is naturally a stressful job , there 's no secure income , you have fluctuating prices of produce - things are always up and down - the New Zealand dollar 's always up and down . " <p> And then , there was the weather . <p> " We 're seeing the effects of climate change more and more these days , with problems such as increased cyclones , more rain , less rain , drought , " Mr Malpass said . <p> Bayer commissioned a research company to do the survey , to which 260 people responded , he said . <p> Two-thirds of Bayer 's business related to the rural sector , through the company 's animal health and crop science divisions , and it wanted a better grasp of the issues facing New Zealand farmers , he said . <p> Mr Malpass hopes the results of the survey will trigger ongoing discussion about what is needed . <p> " The more we can discuss these issues , the more likely we are to come up with some solutions , " Mr Malpass said . <p> " It @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to keep these feelings to themselves , so if we can bring some of the discussion topics to the surface and start talking about them , then that goes a long way to try to solve them .
