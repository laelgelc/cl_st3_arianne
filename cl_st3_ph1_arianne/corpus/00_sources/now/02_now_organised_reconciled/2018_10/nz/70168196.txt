<h> Hawking and the role of science <p> Stephen Hawking , in his wheelchair and with his distinctive voice-generated computer , was the most famous scientist of recent decades . <p> He has been described as a man of mind-boggling achievements and defiance , and he earned enormous respect . <p> His death in March , aged 76 , after 52 years of living with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis , was prominent international news . And then came his " chilling posthumous message " last week . A recording was played at the launch of his final book , completed by his children , Brief Answers to Big Questions . <p> Prof Hawking has for years expressed gloomy views about the future of the world and the human race . His voice from the grave last week was no exception . <p> In particular , he warned that education and science were in danger now more than ever . He cited the election of United States President Donald Trump and Britain 's 2016 vote to leave the European union as part of " a global revolt against experts , and that includes scientists " . <p> He acknowledged science had @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ including climate change , overpopulation , species extinction , deforestation and the degradation of oceans -- but said he could say with confidence the future for young people would depend more on science and technology than any previous generation . <p> He urged young people " to look up at the stars and not down at your feet " . <p> " Try to make sense of what you see , and wonder about what makes the universe exist ... It matters that you do n't give up . " <p> Prof Hawking in his later years stepped up his dire comments . He is not alone in fearing , for example , the dangers of artificial intelligence . AI that reproduces itself could create a new type of life that would outperform humans . <p> Humans were also in danger of self-destructing , and global warming was close to the tipping point where it would be irreversible , in Prof Hawking 's view . <p> Prof Hawking himself was aware his views like all others are " observer dependent " . <p> Indeed , scientists and " experts " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ their culture , upbringing , peers and are loaded with biases . <p> But , at least , through the scientific method and through recognition of bias , a goal of objectivity is sought and means developed to try to achieve that . Controls are instigated and analytical rigour applied . <p> Scientists and experts should not fudge the " facts " , attack the person rather than the argument and they should steer clear of hyperbole . Their views on relevant matters , while often flawed , are nonetheless much better than those based on whim , emotion , blatant self-interest and pseudo-science . <p> While scepticism of experts is in order , it is wrong to dismiss them . <p> Yet , respect for science is slipping .
