<h> District Plan to manage land use and subdivision in the Far North <p> How can we get the right activities happening in the right places ? That is one of the questions the Far North District Council is putting to communities as it creates a new District Plan to manage land use and subdivision in the District . <p> The Council launched a community engagement project called Let 's Plan Together in January 2016 as the first step in a review of the current District Plan . It has since developed a policy framework that addresses Significant Resource Management Issues ( SRMIs ) the community helped to identify . These issues are : <p> Rural Sustainability <p> Affordable Infrastructure <p> Heritage Management <p> Coastal Management <p> Urban Sustainability <p> Indigenous Biodiversity <p> Tangata Whenua Partnerships <p> Climate Change , Natural Hazards and Resilience <p> Landscapes and Features . <p> The Council is now entering the next phase of the District Plan review and would like to know what the community thinks of the policy framework and rule directions it has written . <p> It will use feedback to create zone maps and rules and invite formal submissions on @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ planning standards , in late 2019 . <p> Mayor John Carter says the law requires local authorities to review and update their District Plans every 10 years . <p> " Our District Plan is out of sync with national and regional policy statements , which it must give effect to , and it does n't respond well to a number of issues we currently face in the district . " <p> These issues include the loss of horticultural land , the need to prevent urban sprawl and the need to consider floods and other natural hazards when developing or subdividing land . <p> " The Draft District Plan provides a high level framework and some rule direction that indicate where certain activities should occur and when consent should be required . We will further develop these rules using feedback from the community .
