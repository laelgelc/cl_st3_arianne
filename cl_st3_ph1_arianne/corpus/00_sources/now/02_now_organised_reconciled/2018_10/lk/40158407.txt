The rapid expansion of these projects is exerting much pressure on available groundwater resources . <p> The share of agricultural water demand is higher in Sri Lanka when compared to many other countries in the world , coming behind India by only 3% . The reasons for the increase in demand for water could be the low efficiency of water use in farms ; poor adoption of water saving techniques ; high losses associated with storage , distribution and on-farm utilisation of water ; and the lack of incentives for conservation due to free State-supply of irrigation . In most developed countries , the adoption of technological advances and market-based strategies has reduced the water demand for farming . <p> Water pollution <p> It has been estimated major industrial parks of Sri Lanka generate 30 million cubic metres of wastewater per year ( Source : Central Environmental Authority ) . Estimates for other sectors are not readily available , as data are not compiled by a single agency . <p> Approximately 10% of wastewater produced by industries is discharged to the environment @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . This is mainly by small-scale manufactures operating without formal licenses . <p> Livestock farmers also use their wastewater in their own agricultural farming with minimal or no treatment . <p> Treated wastewater is discharged into nearby natural water streams . Water is then provided from these streams for agriculture and other uses . <p> Effluents from industrial and agricultural sources and from hospitals are considered water pollutants , therefore , comprehensive pollution control measures that go beyond mere disposal of wastewater are necessary to handle the problem of water pollution . <p> The surface water contamination is mainly due to improper disposal of sewage , unmanaged waste from industries , inadequate sanitary facilities , livestock and agricultural run-off . Over-utilisation , particularly through tube wells , is a major problem affecting ground water resources in Sri Lanka . Oil spills , dumping of waste from ships , coral and sand mining activities are the main causes of marine pollution in the country . <p> Except for the pipe-borne water supply , irrigation and hydropower schemes in general and other water resources in Sri Lanka are managed very poorly . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ but enforcement of these regulations is lacking . <p> The ultimate result of degradation and depletion of water resources is an increasing health hazard .
