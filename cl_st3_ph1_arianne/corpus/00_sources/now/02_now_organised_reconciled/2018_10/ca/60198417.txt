<h> OPINION : New fuel charge a concern <h> Marked fuel only allowed in tractors and combines ; must be extended to all farm-plated vehicles <p> BY DAVID MOL <p> GUEST OPINION <p> The Prince Edward Island Federation of Agriculture ( PEIFA ) is pleased that the provincial government has avoided implanting a carbon tax , but remains concerned about the implementation of a new fuel charge . <p> The farming community knows full well the effects of climate change . <p> Extreme weather , cold springs and dry summers have presented challenges in the last two years alone . Combating this challenge is important and the farming community is prepared to do its part . The agriculture industry needs to be part of the solution . <p> The PEIFA has met with senior officials at the Provincial Department of Communities , Land and Environment to discuss the Climate Action Plan and what it will mean to the farming community . The agreement signed this week will allow the province to exempt light oil , propane and marked fuel as well as electricity . <p> This is to be applauded . The PEIFA does welcome the government 's willingness to decrease the excise tax and feels this will help soften the financial costs this policy will cost Island farmers . <p> While the PEIFA is encouraged by the exemptions , marked fuel is currently only allowed to be burned in tractors and combines and must be extended to all farm plated vehicles . The PEIFA membership has called on the government to make this change and remains hopeful that further dialogue can achieve a solution on this front . <p> The farming community is also concerned about the potential of high transportation costs as trucking companies will face increased fuel costs . The PEIFA is pleased to see that the province has committed to work with the trucking sector in reducing this impact . <p> The PEIFA recognizes that yesterday 's announcement does present challenges for the farming community . <p> Further dialogue has been committed to by the province and the PEFIA will continue to voice the concerns Island farmers have with the agreement and work with Government to find solutions that keep the Island 's agriculture sector @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ agreed to speak with the PEIFA board of directors in November and the PEIFA looks forward to these discussions . <p> - David Mol , president of the Prince Edward Island Federation of Agriculture
