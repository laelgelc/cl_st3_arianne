<h> David Jessop The potentially destabilising effect of demographic change <p> Last month , credit ratings agency Moody 's published a report that indicated the potentially negative economic and political implications of demographic change in the Caribbean and Central America . <p> It suggested that if government and the private sector do not make significant improvements in education , healthcare , and other social determinants , many nations will struggle to remain competitive , particularly after 2050 . <p> The report 's findings were amplified just over a week ago in a webinar hosted by Moody 's Assistant Vice-president Marla Dukharan , one of the region 's leading economists , and analyst David Rogovic , who , in an online dialogue made clear the policy issues that needed to be addressed . <h> NET OUTWARD MIGRATION <p> Moody 's research contained potentially alarming statistics and tables that pointed to several critical and so far largely unaddressed long-term policy challenges facing the region . <p> Put simply , it indicated that although declining , the Caribbean still had the highest net outward migration in the world and the highest age-dependency ratio between retirees and citizens who work . As the agency points out , demographics are a secondary force in shaping credit markets , so their study should be seen as an adjunct to the credit ratings they and others provide commercially to enable investors to take longer-term decisions about a country . <h> GOVERNMENTS ' RESPONSE <p> What Moody 's well-researched , statistically based , and accessible report does is explode the myth perpetrated by some governments about their capacity to continue to meet the demands of economic growth , the young , and the ageing through a ' business as usual ' approach . <p> Focusing on the 10 Caribbean countries and five Central American nations for which it provides ratings , the report suggested that apart from improving social and human capital indicators , there are steps that governments might take to improve @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the positive responses governments might make to address demographic change were encouraging inward migration of those of working age or with particular skills ; attracting better-off retirees from other nations able to purchase retirement services and healthcare ; the introduction of electronic government ; and innovative approaches to income redistribution . <p> The ratings agency also observed that while outward migration resulted in relatively stable flows of remittances that supported domestic consumer demand and growth , this exposed the region to external shocks and the potential tightening in immigration policy in receiving countries . <p> What Moody 's makes clear is that the Caribbean is on a wholly predictable but unsustainable path .
