<h> Environment File : climate change and price of beer <h> Climate change threatens beer <p> The price of beer in Canada could triple unless the world acts to curtail climate change , a recent study suggests . <p> Researchers published a study in Nature Plants this week on how extreme heat and drought caused by climate change could affect the world 's beer supply . <p> University of California , Irvine earth science professor Nathan Mueller said he and his co-authors did the study because they liked beer and studied climate change . <p> " Beer 's main ingredient is barley , and we know crops are sensitive to climate fluctuations , " Mueller said . <p> The team used climate models to estimate the odds that barley-growing regions such as Canada would experience severe heat and drought events in the next century under 3 to 5+ C of warming . Researchers agree we 're currently heading for at least 3 C of warming without serious emissions reductions . <p> The team found that the risk of such events rose about four per cent with less than 3 C of warming but 31 per cent with 5+ C @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Mueller said . This warming would chop three to 17 per cent off global barley yields , assuming no change in agricultural techniques . <p> The team then applied these supply shifts to an economic model of beer consumption . Under 5+ C of warming , the team found that global beer consumption would drop 16 per cent -- equivalent to all the beer drunk in the U.S. in 2011 -- as prices doubled . <p> Canadians would have to pay about US$0.72 more per 500 mL bottle of beer if they kept warming to less than 3 C , which would cause most to drink 13 fewer bottles per year ( down from 114.5 ) . With 5+ C , prices jump by US$4.35 and consumption drops by almost a third ( 36.5 bottles ) . <p> " The prices really shoot up for Canada , " said Mueller , rising to about $6.22 per bottle under the worst-case scenario . <p> " We 're talking a tripling of prices .
