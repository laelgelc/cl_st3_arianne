<p> Environment Canada has issued heat alerts in various provinces over the past few years , and started the practice in Nova Scotia this past summer . <p> Now , the province has received $150,000 from the federal government to hire a co-ordinator to get municipalities and emergency management planners working together . <p> Dr. Robert Strang , the province 's chief medical officer of health , said the plan will identify who 's at risk from high heat and humidity , and who can help . <p> Dr. Robert Strang , chief medical officer of health for Nova Scotia , says Environment Canada heat alerts are only the first step in dealing with high temperatures and humidity . It will connect public health officials with local people in the Emergency Management Office , as well as municipal staff in various departments and local sport and recreation organizations . <p> The province plans to choose one or two municipalities to work with initially , and then will roll the plan out provincewide once the details are settled . <h> Climate change creating urgency <p> There is some urgency to get the plan ready , Strang said . <p> The province saw two summers with record high humidex values -- a measurement of combined heat and humidity -- and a new report from the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a critical juncture and only has a few years left to put the brakes on climate change . <p> " If we know the impacts of prolonged heat have significant impacts on health , and with climate change we are going to see hotter summers ... we do need to have these more robust plans in place , " Strang said . <p> The plan will have to be different in different parts of the province , he said .
