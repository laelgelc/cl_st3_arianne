<p> The First World War was one of the deadliest conflicts in human history . But not as deadly as the flu . <p> The Spanish flu , which reached its peak in the fall of 1918 , killed somewhere between 20 million and 40 million people , with some estimates reaching as high as 50 million . In Canada , it killed around 55,000 , mostly young adults . <p> The virus spread rapidly around the globe , carried in many cases by millions of soldiers travelling to and from Europe . <p> And now , 100 years later , scientists are once again looking back to the pandemic and what it can tell us about how to prepare for the next one . <p> Because the question is n't if it will happen again , they say , but when . <p> Warehouses were converted to keep infected people quarantined during the 1918 influenza pandemic . <h> Related <h> A unique virus <p> The Spanish flu was unique in a number of ways .
