<h> OPINION : Please charge me now for my carbon usage <p> Shelley MacDougall <p> Published : Oct 11 at 6 a.m . <p> There is a cost to carbon , whether the government prices it or not , writes Shelley MacDougall - 123RF Stock Photo <p> I am a voter , a taxpayer and a consumer . I want to be charged for the carbon in the products I use . I still use carbon-intensive products : I drive a gas-powered car ; I heat my home with oil ; and I use electricity that is 65 per cent produced by burning fossil fuels , mostly coal . <p> I will continue to reduce my carbon footprint -- for example by replacing lightbulbs with LEDs , driving less , installing a heat pump , drying clothes on a line , turning down the heat at night and when I 'm out . That will save me money , both on the fuel and the price on carbon . <p> I want my government to price the carbon I use . Insurance premiums are going up due to the risks , too , or the policies will not cover these now more-likely losses . <p> There is a cost to carbon , whether the government prices it or not . Cap-and-trade systems have their merits , but they are complicated and expensive to operate . To be sure we are paying the fairest price for carbon , we need to be part of a large cap-and-trade program , not to go it alone in a tiny market like Nova Scotia . <p> Besides , giving free allowances to industrial emitters does not really price carbon . It is essentially regulation , with no revenue to redistribute . Free allowances can end up being a windfall for the companies that can sell unneeded allowances to other emitters , at a price that will , of course , be then passed on to us , the consumers . <p> I realize a carbon tax will cost me and the price per tonne has to go up over time to encourage businesses and households to keep finding ways to reduce emissions . By the same token , we need to levy a carbon tax on products imported from those countries . <p> Here is my condition , though : the carbon taxing system has to be completely revenue-neutral for the government . It does not matter to me whether it is the provincial or federal government . Every penny that gets paid in has to go back out . I want transparency on that , too , through legislation and public reporting . <p> How the government should distribute the money , and to whom , should be the focus of today 's carbon-pricing debate . It could be paid back through lower income tax or paid like the GST rebate or social assistance payments . It could be paid to everyone or it could be means-tested , so the most vulnerable get the most help . There are conservative- and liberal-minded think tanks that are in agreement that a carbon tax is a workable solution to climate change mitigation . <p> We are , and will be , paying for carbon emissions in any case , even if we do nothing -- but doing nothing is the worst alternative , because disaster-relief payments only deal with the effects of global warming , not the problem causing it . Let 's get on with it . <p> Shelley MacDougall , Ph.D , is professor emerita , F.C . Manning School of Business , Acadia University
