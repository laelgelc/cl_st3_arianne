<h> Barnes : Ottawa could become a green capital in the next term of council <p> In the lead-up to the Oct. 22 municipal election , all eyes will be on the tight races and public debates . While the horserace aspect of an election can be entertaining , it 's critical to take a longer-term view . <p> What kind of future is possible if Ottawans elect a greener city council -- one with a level of vision and ambition appropriate for a world-class capital ? What could Ottawa look like in 2022 , at the end of the next term of council ? <p> The next term of council will be tasked with updating the city 's Official Plan . This is no minor job . The Official Plan provides a decades-long vision for the future growth of our city . The Official Plan deals with everything from community design to zoning to transportation planning and protection of natural areas . <p> One of council 's first major Official Plan challenges will be to wrestle with the status of the urban boundary . Low-density , single-use development -- where residences and businesses are linked only by large arterial roads -- guts community life and bakes in too much car reliance . If Ottawa does not act , then congestion will remain a constant problem , transit will be spotty and expensive , greenhouse gas pollution will soar and we will continue to chew through valuable green space . <p> Instead , we should take the opportunity to systematically design our neighbourhoods at human scale , with pedestrians , cyclists and transit users as top priorities . Traffic -- which has been worsened by wider lanes and expanded highways -- will ease as the city connects sidewalks , pathways and bus routes with light rail . In the process , the city will save hundreds of millions of dollars from road widenings , and can redirect it back into a greener transportation network . In 2022 , Ottawa 's streets will be safer and more inviting for users of every age and ability . <p> Where new communities are built , they should be designed around transit connections rather than new roads . And where infill takes place , it must be much more protective of our urban tree canopy . By 2022 , green design elements like green roofs and rain gardens should be built city-wide -- on top of large buildings and alongside new streets . Even as rainfall events become more severe and frequent with a changing climate , our green infrastructure will work to slow down , soak up and filter rainwater where it falls . This will lead to healthier watersheds and fewer floods . <p> Ottawa must also show much greater ambition on climate change action , especially in light of the most recent Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) report . The IPCC warns that the window of time to prevent climate change catastrophe is narrowing fast , and the consequences of failure are dire . A greener city council can dramatically reduce greenhouse gas pollution by targeting the highest-emitting source -- our buildings -- with efficiency investments . Meanwhile , taking lessons from the 2018 tornado disaster , a greener city council will move ambitiously on a Climate Adaptation Plan . This new plan will leave us better prepared for the next climate impact -- whether that means natural disasters , invasive species or prolonged heat waves . <p> In four short years , Ottawa can transform itself into an environmental leader .
