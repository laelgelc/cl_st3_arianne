<h> Federal pollution plan follows Sudbury example <p> Sudbury MP Paul Lefebvre , parliamentary secretary to the natural resources minister , rises during Question Period in the House of Commons on Parliament Hill in Ottawa on Friday.Justin Tang/Canadian Press <p> Last week , the United Nations Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change published a watershed report on climate change , warning that a bigger crisis is coming sooner than we thought . <p> Earlier this month , William Nordhaus won the Nobel prize in economics for incorporating climate change into mainstream economics . His pioneering work integrating climate change into models of economic growth has provided a roadmap for a future where the world 's economic health is directly linked to its environmental action . <p> Closer to home , in Canada , a Conservative-led think tank is heeding these warnings and calling for government-led action on climate change -- sooner rather than later . <p> Canadians realize that polluting is n't free , and that it is a leading contributor to climate change . <p> We know that climate change causes droughts , fires and floods across our country and the world . And we know that all these @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ -- combine to impact our health through issues like asthma , premature mortality and more emergency room visits . <p> We also know that making polluters pay is a critical part of any serious climate plan . It reduces pollution , creates direct financial incentives for companies to innovate and develop clean solutions , and provides certainty to businesses that we are moving to a clean growth economy . <p> Here in Sudbury , we know very well the value of collective action in reducing pollution , and the importance of governments , industry and citizens working together to build a stronger community . From the 1970s on , government standards have increasingly required Sudbury 's mining giants to refine and improve their industrial processes . The result of these changes has been improved production , reduced costs and dramatically reduced emissions , all leading to a healthier more vibrant community and providing uncalculatable benefits to every single citizen in Sudbury . <p> Ask yourself , are Sudburians better off today than before Inco was compelled to build the Superstack ? Are Sudburians better off today because of the efforts @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ stem pollution and re-green our city ? Clearly we are . <p> That is exactly what the research referenced above has concluded , and it is exactly what our government is implementing through its revenue neutral pollution pricing . By making it more expensive to pollute there is more of an incentive to reduce emissions , lessening the impact on the environment . <p> And by returning the money collected directly to taxpayers , we can be certain this is not a cash grab . In fact , that same Conservative-led study , led by Mark Cameron , policy director to former Conservative prime minister Stephen Harper , shows that the vast majority of households , regardless of income level , would receive more money in the form of carbon dividend cheques than they would pay in carbon taxes . <p> And while almost all households would be net beneficiaries , by far the biggest benefit will go to lower income households , Cameron concluded . This exactly the kind of action Canadians want and need from their government to fight climate change on a global stage . <p> Sadly @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ coalition of provincial Conservative governments , now led by Doug Ford in Ontario , have chosen to pretend it is n't real and are telling Canadians to ignore it . At the federal level , Conservative leader Andrew Scheer is scrambling at the last minute to cobble together ideas to excuse the fact that he has absolutely no plan to deal with this issue . <p> The facts are in . The research is done and the call to action is clear . There is a pathway forward , and it is a proven pathway blazed by Sudburians over the last generation . Ask yourself this : If Sudbury had ignored the call to action 50 years ago and pretended nothing was wrong , what kind of city would we be living in today ? <p> Paul Lefebvre is MP for Sudbury and parliamentary secretary to the minister of Natural Resources .
