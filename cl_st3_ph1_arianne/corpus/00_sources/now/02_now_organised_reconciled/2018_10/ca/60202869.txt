<h> Summit hoping to change Sask. climate change narrative <p> Some attendees at a climate change summit held at the University of Regina this weekend are hoping to spark a shift in the way Saskatchewan talks about climate change . <p> The summit was held in response to a report from the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change . The report gave countries an ultimatum : Drastically cut emissions in the next 12 years , or suffer irreversible damage to the environment . <p> Jared Clarke , one of the speakers at the summit , hopes Saskatchewan will change its talk on climate change . <p> " Polling numbers show that we have a lot of climate change denial in this province compared to other places in Canada , so I think that conversation does need to be happening , " he said . <p> Despite this , Clarke is encouraged by some of the renewable options that are being discussed and implemented throughout the province . <p> " It does feel like there is momentum in this province around making change . We 've had the carbon tax come out , and however you feel about that , that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and reduce our emissions , " he said . <p> Some at the summit were also hoping to see an Indigenous perspective enter into climate discussions . <p> Michelle Brass is a committee member for Indigenous Climate Action -- a group that works to incorporate Indigenous perspectives into climate talks . <p> " I would say that Indigenous voices have not been heard , " said Brass . <p> " Indigenous peoples contribute the least to greenhouse gas emissions , but we are impacted the most and often the first with our connections to the land and how we engage with our societies .
