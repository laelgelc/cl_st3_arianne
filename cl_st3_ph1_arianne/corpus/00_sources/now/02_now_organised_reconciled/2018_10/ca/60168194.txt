He blames climate change <p> By By Kendra Pierre-LouisNew York Times <p> Thu. , Oct. 18 , 2018 <p> The biggest number of winter ticks that Peter J. Pekins ever found on a moose was about 100,000 . But that moose calf was already dead , most likely the victim of anemia , which develops when that many ticks drain a moose 's blood . So it was probably a lowball estimate , because some of the ticks had already detached . <p> " It 's about as grody a picture as you can imagine on a dead animal , " said Pekins , a professor of natural resources and the environment at the University of New Hampshire . <p> A moose makes its way through a nursery in Midway , B.C. , Wednesday , May 16 , 2018 . ( Jonathan Hayward / THE CANADIAN PRESS ) <p> Between 2014 and 2016 , Pekins counted ticks on moose calves at two locations in New Hampshire and Maine . He wanted to see how the moose were faring , given that climate change has been delaying snow 's arrival in New England 's winters . <p> The longer-lasting warmth gives the ticks a leg up as they glom onto the moose , their preferred hosts , in the fall . They then feed through winter and hop off in the spring to lay eggs . <p> The moose-tracking exploits of Pekins and his colleagues were published last month in the Canadian Journal of Zoology . They argued that three consecutive years of tick outbreaks " arguably reflects a host-parasite relationship strongly influenced by climate change at the southern fringe of moose habitat . The average number of ticks he found on them was 47,371 . <p> " Anything over 35,000 is trouble for a calf moose , " he said . During the study period , 125 calves died , 70 percent of those being tracked . <p> Article Continued Below <p> In some ways , the moose are a victim of their own success . <p> " Maine and New Hampshire had less than 50 moose in the 1970s , " Pekins said . Over the long term , Pekins does not expect the moose to die off completely , but there will be fewer of them . <p> The ticks do not want the moose to die off completely , either .
