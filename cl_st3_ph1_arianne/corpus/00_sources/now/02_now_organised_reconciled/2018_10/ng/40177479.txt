<p> HARARE -- Zimbabwe aims to clear its $2 billion arrears with the World Bank and African Development Bank in the next 12 months , after securing the support of international creditors and donor countries , Finance Minister Mthuli Ncube said on Friday . <p> Zimbabwe has struggled to access international credit since defaulting on its debts to global lenders two decades ago and running up arrears of nearly $6 billion . <p> Ncube said in an interview the arrears clearance programme had the backing of the United States government , which maintains sanctions against Zimbabwe . <p> Ncube met international lenders as well as representatives of the U.S. and British governments in Bali , Indonesia , last week on the sidelines of the annual Internal Monetary Fund and World Bank meetings . <p> " My intention is that by this time next year we would have paid off the AfDB and World Bank . All options are on the table , including the Highly Indebted Poor Country ( HIPC ) option debt write-off , or the HIPC-lite or the ad-hoc solutions , with sponsors , " Ncube told Reuters . <p> " For sponsors , we will be @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ two of them , or all of them , could sponsor us and give us some lines of credit , bridging finance to be able to clear those arrears . " <p> The IMF and World Bank launched the HIPC initiative in 1996 to help poor countries struggling with external debt get debt relief . <p> Acknowledging the U.S. role in his debt relief plan , Ncube said Zimbabwe needed to embrace Washington 's conditions as spelt out in its Zimbabwe Democracy and Economic Recovery Act ( Zidera ) . <p> That sanctions law was enacted in 2001 in response to alleged human rights abuses by the Zimbabwe government under former President Robert Mugabe . <p> " The Americans are very supportive of Zimbabwe . Of course , there are points of departure , Zidera is the issue .
