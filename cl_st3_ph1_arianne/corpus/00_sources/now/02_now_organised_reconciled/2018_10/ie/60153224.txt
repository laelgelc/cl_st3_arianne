<h> Culture on environment must change <p> Monday , October 15 , 2018 - 12:00 AM <p> Last Monday , the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) , representing nearly 200 governments , warned of the dire consequences of our refusal to do more to limit climate change , a man-made catastrophe . <p> Within hours , our Government published a budget shorn of an expected , 10% increase in carbon taxes , one Taoiseach Leo Varadkar had predicted in August . <p> That budget did not contain a single environmental measure proportionate to the climate-change crisis . <p> This denial provoked considerable anger , but whether that dismay changes the thinking of this **26;3262;TOOLONG administration remains to be seen . Change might have to come through the ballot boxes , which will be dusted off any day now . <p> Sadly , that attitude , which sees habitat and species protection as icing-on-the-cake afterthoughts , shapes the culture of official Ireland . The examples are myriad . <p> Killarney National Park , a jewel that would be cherished in any other country , is an almost perfect example of this <p> neglect . <p> Killarney National @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ invasive weeds ; its magnificent oak woods are threatened by rhododendron ; hill fires are set , but there seem to be no consequences for the arsonists , and the park 's red deer , if , like Innisfallen 's sika deer , they do n't starve to death , are managed cackhandedly . Report after report from the Environmental Protection Agency on the relentless destruction of our remaining ' pristine ' rivers is another indicator of this very poor stewardship . <p> That neglect has contrasting consequences at five , maybe more , river sites in Co Cork . In Bandon , millions upon millions have been spent on flood-protection measures . However , and no matter how sweet the icing on this cake is , those works seem to be destructive on an industrial scale . <p> In Cork , the city council and the Office of Public Works seem determined to press on with ' flood protection ' measures that appear a kind of officially-endorsed vandalism . It has fallen into grave disrepair and represents an almost impassible <p> barrier for the river 's struggling salmon stocks . Its sorry state is also a threat to the water levels needed to support the sports clubs that have played a vital part in building that town 's heritage and wellbeing over generations . <p> That this wier could be repaired for a fraction of the money spent in Bandon reaffirms that the Government 's **26;3290;TOOLONG attitude has permeated local authorities .
