<h> Portlaoise school goes back to blackboards and chalk to save energy <p> A Laois primary school hit the off switch on energy consumption recently , all to try and win their second Green Flag . <p> The staff and students of Portlaoise Educate Together National School held a " No Energy Day " on Thursday September 27 . <p> Young pupils used blackboard and chalk for the first time , and a handheld school bell rung out for the first time too in their modern school . <p> " One could be forgiven for thinking that a low energy day meant that the staff and students lounged around all day , doing little or no work , but the opposite was actually true , " said teacher Claire Romito , who is leading the Green Flag project helped by a young committee . <p> The day was part of their campaign to raise awareness of how much energy is consumed on a daily basis and to encourage the school community to reduce their energy consumption . <p> Lights were left switched off , photocopiers were out of bounds , and lessons were taught without the use of ICT @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ energy use and climate change in geography , and for history they got to experience what school was like long ago with no electricity or computers . <p> Elaine 's Senior Infant class even did their work on a traditional blackboard and chalk , something that the children had never seen before . <p> The staff also brought in lunches that did n't need to be heated in the microwave and tea and coffee consumption was seriously curtailed . <p> The intercom was switched off , so the school resorted to using an old hand held school bell brought in by a Senior Infant students to announce breaks and home time . <p> " Staff and pupils were very impressed with how effectively the day brought home the message about being more mindful of energy consumption , " Ms Romito said . <p> " Low energy day highlights to us as teachers and children the amount of energy we consume unnecessarily on a daily basis .
