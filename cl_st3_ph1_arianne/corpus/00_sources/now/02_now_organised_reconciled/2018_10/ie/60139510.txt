<h> Budget 2019 does nothing to tackle entrenched inequality <p> Wednesday , October 10 , 2018 - 04:47 PM <p> Budget 2019 fails to make any notable impact on Ireland 's entrenched inequalities and fails to tackle any of the major challenges the country currently faces , according to Social Justice Ireland 's Sean Healy . <p> THIS budget pays lip service to social investment and skewed priorities and a reluctance to support much needed tax increases to fund public spending will mean inequality will remain fundamentally unchanged in Irish society . <p> Currently Ireland has 780,000 people in poverty , a quarter of a million of whom are children and over 100,000 of whom have jobs . Ireland also has 700,000 people on healthcare waiting lists , 110,000 households waiting for social housing and hundreds of thousands of people without adequate broadband in rural Ireland . <p> Budget 2019 fails to provide resources on the scale required to address any of these challenges effectively . <p> Social Justice Ireland is glad that Ireland 's economy is growing , that unemployment is low and that emigrants are returning . We need to extend our understanding of fiscal policy beyond economic growth alone and give equal weight to just taxation , decent services and infrastructure , good governance and sustainability . A buoyant economy alone will not deliver a more equal , fairer Ireland . <h> Investment and Taxation <p> We need to take heed of the shocking social exclusion being experienced by so many Irish people and invest in solutions . Investment requires funding and this will only be delivered by increasing our total tax take . <p> This additional revenue and increased investment can be delivered without risk to the economy , without adding to the national debt and without sacrificing the goal of a budgetary surplus . While politically unpopular , with real political leadership this can be achieved by progressive taxation : which has already been done in some of the most successful and stable economies in the world . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ consequences . We can only borrow so much and , as Minister Paschal Donohoe pointed out in his Budget speech , our national debt is already at a dizzying level . <p> While Ireland 's income tax system is progressive , further progressivity on the wider tax front is the route to a broader tax base and resulting funds for social investment .
