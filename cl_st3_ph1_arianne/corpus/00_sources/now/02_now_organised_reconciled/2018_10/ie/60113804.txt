<p> Researchers are speculating that climate change could boost UK production of internationally-bred apples , as a major study gets underway in Kent . <p> A warmer UK climate has already resulted in earlier apple flowering and harvest , and it is allowing late-ripening apple varieties to be produced across more of the UK . <p> Now , one of the UK 's largest experiments into the impact of climate change on crops is beginning at Brogdale Farm in Kent , funded by the National Fruit Collections Trust . <p> The research project seeks to learn how warmer temperatures and altered rainfall patterns , expected in future decades , will affect UK apple production , potentially allowing popular international varieties like Granny Smith ( Australia ) or Golden Delicious ( West Virginia , US ) to be grown more widely in Britain . This would increase UK growers ' share of the domestic apple market . <p> Professor Paul Hadley of the University of Reading , and an NFCT Trustee , said : " Climate change is affecting top fruit already , but this is given less attention by researchers than annual crops .
