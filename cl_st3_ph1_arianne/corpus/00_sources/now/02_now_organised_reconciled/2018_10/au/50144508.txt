<h> Mistake to dismiss Wentworth result as a one-off <h> By Trent Zimmerman <p> 21 October 2018 -- 9:00pm <p> The outcome of the Wentworth byelection has understandably sent shockwaves through the Australian political system . Whether or not postal votes change the final outcome , the size of the swing should be a wake-up call . We saw 20 per cent of our base in Wentworth abandon the government in favour of an independent . As the Prime Minister said on Sunday , this result is on us and we must listen and respond . <p> Around the globe , Australia is recognised as one of the most successful democracies . We are a people admired for both our long-term economic success and for forging a harmonious multicultural society . <p> Key to this success has been the relative stability of our political system since we became a nation in 1901 . <p> Partly this is structural , reflecting the strength of the Westminster hybrid our founding fathers left future generations . Both parties have failed in delivering on that core proposition over the past decade and it is little wonder that voters have moved to minor parties or independents , reflected in the declining primary vote for both Liberal and Labor . <p> Loading <p> If the Liberal Party is to restore faith in its ability to provide the stability that , for most of our history , has been our strength , then change must happen . <p> Loading <p> First , the Liberal Party must look at its rules relating to the leadership . Put simply , the long-term policy interests of Australians are not being well served by a three-year political cycle . <p> There are factors in Wentworth that are unique . Kerryn Phelps was an independent with a strong national profile and undoubtedly held in high regard by residents . Yet it would be a fundamental mistake to dismiss Saturday 's outcome as being a one-off . A large swag of seats held by the Liberal Party share similar traits to Wentworth . <p> For Labor the message is no better and should provide the Opposition with little comfort about their prospects at the next general election .
