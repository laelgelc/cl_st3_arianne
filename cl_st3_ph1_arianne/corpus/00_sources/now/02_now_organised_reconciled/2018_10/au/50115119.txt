<h> Letters : Population growth , Glencore , AFIC and discrimination <h> Population growth only half the story <p> Jennifer Hewett ( " Alan Tudge 's congestion busting a start but needs more work " , October 9 ) rightly points out that the states largely have to pick up the tab for the extra infrastructure associated with population growth . No wonder then that NSW Premier Gladys Berejiklian has called for immigration to her state to be halved as it can not keep up with infrastructure demands . <p> It is infuriating that Prime Minister Scott Morrison can look no further than economic growth fuelled by population growth . He fails to comprehend that total economic growth does not necessarily translate into economic growth per capita , nor that population growth in large cities is associated with diseconomies of scale such as congestion and unaffordable housing . The prime contributor to congestion is high-density planning policies that severely restrict the release of land for development on the periphery of capital cities . High-density cities with transport services vastly superior to Sydney such as New York , Hong Kong , Paris and Moscow experience horrendous traffic congestion and pollution . <p> In contrast , the large low-density sunbelt cities in the southern United States allow any developer on city outskirts to subdivide land subject to reasonable constraints . Compared with Sydney these cities experience much better journey to work travel times and other traffic metrics .
