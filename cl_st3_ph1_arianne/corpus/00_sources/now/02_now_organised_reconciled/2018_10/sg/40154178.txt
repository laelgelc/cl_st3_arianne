<p> Under the surface beauty of words lies the broil and murk of discontent . The lyricism of American writer Lauren Groff 's prose in this short story collection belies the hectoring voices of her protagonists - mostly wives and mothers burdened by their duties at home and at work - and the expectations set by society and self . <p> The collection is named after a place , Florida , an atmospheric setting with its heat , humidity , wetlands terrain and untamed nature - sinkholes , snakes and alligators . <p> Florida is presented as an inhospitable environment where middle-class folk have chosen to settle down with their families . These are tales of marital and professional disenchantment , niggling maternal fears and violence against women and children . <p> Accompanying the collection 's sombre tone is a curious air of detachment and defeatism . In the stories told from the perspectives of anxious , fatigued mothers - Ghosts And Empties , Yport , Snake Stories and Flower Hunters - love seethes with worry and anger at global woes such as climate change and social injustice . <p> Is Florida a metaphor for mankind 's ungainly ineptitude @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ head over and over again in different stories . <p> In At The Round Earth 's Imagined Corners , Jude sells off " precisely calibrated " parcels of his father 's hundred acres of land " overgrown with palmettos and vines " to the university , whetting its " greed " and " gobble " for more air-conditioned space . <p> Groff condemns his capitalism : " Jude 's promise was unfulfilled , the choices made not the passionate ones .
