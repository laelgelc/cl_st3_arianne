" <p> The NutriFish1000 campaign , launched on Wednesday , aims to improve family nutrition in the first 1,000 days of life by getting pregnant women and children to eat small fish - dubbed " nutrient bombs " - grown in ponds and rice paddies . <p> Global hunger has been on the rise for three years , according to the United Nations ( UN ) , which says that one in nine people worldwide do not have enough to eat , and 151 million children under five are stunted by malnutrition . <p> While almost all rural families in Cambodia eat fish from flooded rice fields during the wet season , stocks have been hit by overfishing and malnutrition remains high because of poverty , disease and a tradition of not feeding fish to young children . <h> " <p> We 're now translating that into something that is accessible for the poorest @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ settings , ... to produce these fish in a safe , available , accessible and affordable manner . <p> Pawan Patil , economist , World Bank <p> With financing from the World Bank and the UN , NutriFish1000 projects have been rolled out in six countries , including India and Bangladesh , with plans to expand to Ivory Coast , Malawi and Ghana next year . <p> NutriFish1000 experts have identified 33 species of locally available small fish , up to 10 cm ( 4 inches ) long , that they intend to promote in countries with high malnutrition rates , said Pawan Patil , a World Bank economist . <p> " In developed countries , there 's already the knowledge that eating certain kinds of fish is good , " he said . <p> " We 're now translating that into something that is accessible for the poorest of the poor , especially women and children in rural settings , ... to produce these fish in a safe , available , accessible and affordable manner .
