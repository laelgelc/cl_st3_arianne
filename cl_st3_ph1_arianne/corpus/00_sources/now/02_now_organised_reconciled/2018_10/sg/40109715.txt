( AP Photo/Dan Elliott ) <p> DENVER ( AP ) -- Another rare Colorado River fish has been pulled back from the brink of extinction , wildlife officials said Thursday , the second comeback this year for a species unique to the Southwestern U.S. <p> The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service recommended reclassifying the ancient and odd-looking razorback sucker from endangered to threatened , meaning it is still at risk of extinction , but the danger is no longer immediate . <p> The Associated Press was briefed on the plans before the official announcement . <p> Hundreds of thousands of razorbacks once thrived in the Colorado River and its tributaries , which flow across seven states and Mexico . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Researchers blame non-native predator fish that attacked and ate the razorbacks and dams that disrupted their habitat . <p> Their numbers have bounced back to between 54,000 and 59,000 today , thanks to a multimillion-dollar effort that enlisted the help of hatcheries , dam operators , landowners , native American tribes and state and federal agencies . <p> " It 's a work in progress , " said Tom Chart , director of the Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program . " We get more fish out in the system , they 're showing up in more places , they 're spawning in more locations . State and federal agencies began introducing game fish into the Colorado without realizing they would devour the native fish , Chart said . A spurt of dam-building was a boon to cities and farms but interrupted the natural springtime surge of melting snow , which in turn shrank the floodplains that provided a safe nursery for young razorbacks . <p> Dams also made parts of the rivers too cold for razorbacks , because they release water from the chilly depths of reservoirs .
