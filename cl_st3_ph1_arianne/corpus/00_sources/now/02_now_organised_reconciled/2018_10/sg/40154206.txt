<p> The production of rice , one of the world 's most important crops , is facing major problems such as slow growth and climate change , which could increase its price by more than 30 per cent by 2050 . <p> " We need major changes to our rice and food production systems to make them more resilient to weather disruptions , and also to reduce their emissions and their impact on the environment , " Minister for National Development Lawrence Wong said yesterday . <p> Speaking at the opening of the International Rice Congress , he said that although Singapore is a rice consumer instead of a producer , the country has turned its limited land space to its advantage by experimenting with more productive farming methods . <p> " After all , necessity is the mother of invention , " said Mr Wong , who is also Second Minister for Finance , citing examples of urban farming producing more rice with less land . <p> The congress , also called the " Olympics of Rice Science " , is the world 's largest scientific conference on rice .
