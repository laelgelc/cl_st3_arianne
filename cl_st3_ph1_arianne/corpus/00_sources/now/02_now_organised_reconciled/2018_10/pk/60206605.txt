<h> Email <p> BEIJING : China sent its first ever satellite built in partnership with another country into space on Monday , a device tasked with helping scientists better predict dangerous cyclones and climate change by monitoring ocean surface winds and waves . <p> A Long March 2C carrier rocket blasted off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre in north-west China 's Gobi Desert at 0043 GMT , according to China 's State Administration of Science , Technology and Industry for National Defence . <p> The 650-kg China-France Oceanography Satellite ( CFOSAT ) is the first satellite jointly built by China and France and will allow climate scientists to better understand interactions between oceans and the atmosphere . <p> It 's fitted with two radars : the French-made SWIM spectrometer , which will measure the direction and the wavelength of waves , and China 's SCAT , a scatterometer that will analyse the force and direction of winds . <p> The data will be collected and analysed in both countries . <p> The instruments will allow scientists to collect information about winds and waves of the same location simultaneously for the first time , Wang Lili , chief designer @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , said , according to the official Xinhua news agency . <p> The two countries are also working together on the Space Variable Objects Monitor ( SVOM ) mission , which will launch a satellite in 2021 to detect and study gamma-ray bursts . <p> Chinese leader Xi Jinping and French President Emmanuel Macron exchanged congratulations in a phone call , according to Xinhua . The project began in 2007 . <p> China and France already cooperate in space , with a French cardiovascular device aboard China 's Tiangong-2 space lab to monitor a crew 's hearts .
