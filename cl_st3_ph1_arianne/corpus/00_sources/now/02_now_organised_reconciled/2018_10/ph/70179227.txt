<h> Musings on economic growth and the Nobel <p> This year 's Nobel Prize in the Economic Sciences once again focuses on the long run question of how economies grow . On October 8 Paul Romer ( New York University ) and William Nordhaus ( Yale University ) were awarded for their work on integrating the roles of " technological innovations and climate change , " respectively , with analyzing economic growth . This article is an overview of their works , as well as other related literature on economic growth . <p> Romer 's work can be compared to another Nobel winner , Robert Solow , who was awarded the Nobel Prize in Economics in 1987 for the neoclassical growth theory . In Solow 's work , it is predicted that growth is caused by an increase in total factor productivity , which can be defined as the proportion of growth caused after accounting for exogenous factors such as an increased savings rate , decreased population growth and increased capital accumulation . One version of this model suggests that technology is related to the number of people involved in the research and development sector . This suggests that capital accumulation can also be interpreted as increases in human and knowledge capital , which do not necessarily have diminishing returns . Technology can be assumed to be a nonrival good because knowledge capital can be shared by all once it is developed . This implies that population growth can be beneficial for a country if the level of human capital increases along with the population growth . <p> Nordhaus 's work is under the field of environmental economics , where he developed " integrated assessment models " of climate and economic activity . In particular , many researchers have made use of cointegration methods such as the bounds test developed by econometricians M. Hashem Pesaran , Yongcheol Shin and Richard J. Smith to test the long-run relationships between growth and related sectors such as the trade and financial sectors . In addition , tests developed by econometricians such as Clive Granger ( another Economics Nobel winner ) and the Japanese economists Hiro Toda and Taku Yamamoto have allowed for the examination of causalities between these variables . <p> Theory suggests that the financial and trade sectors have a relationship with economic growth . These arguments suggest that there may be a triangular relationship between finance , trade and growth at the same time . <p> The Australian-based and Pakistani researchers Mohammad Rahman , Muhammad Shahbaz and Abdul Farooq attempted to test this proposed relationship between economic growth , trade and finance empirically in the case of Australia using the methods by Pesaran , Shin and Smith ( 2001 ) and Granger . They found that there was a long run relationship between economic growth , international trade , and financial development in Australia . Furthermore , they inferred that trade and growth have a bidirectional relationship and that financial development leads to both growth and trade in Australia . Therefore , policy-makers have the incentive to push for financial development because it can stimulate growth directly and stimulate growth through further trade activity . <p> As can be seen from all these works , the study @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is not yet completely settled .
