<h> We need more ' provincianos ' in the Senate <p> With the fanfare with which some senatorial candidates have filed their certificates of candidacy in recent days , we often hear hopes that our Senate represent more of the Philippines , because truth is that most of the senators are from Metro Manila . <p> If my math is correct , since the revival of the upper chamber in the 8th Congress in 1987 , only 8 senators from Mindanao and 7 from the Visayas were elected out of the 74 elected to the senate . <p> Moreover , we have had only two Muslims since then , former Senators Mamintal Tamano and Santanina Rasul , whose respective terms ended in 1992 and 1995 . No other Muslim has since been elected to the Senate since them . <p> Everyone else was from either Luzon or Metro Manila , many of those recently elected are children of former Senators . Most have built their political networks in the capital . <p> That said , we in Mindanao often bewail our miniscule representation in the Senate , which mirrors the larger alienation of the island and its unique concerns @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Manila 's elite from across the spectrum prefer to talk about or their preferred perspective when looking at our issues . <p> This exclusion over time may have created the following outcomes : <p> First , almost 3/4 of all national budgets since 1986 go to Luzon and Metro Manila , with the rest of the country sharing what is left despite having a bigger portion of territory and population . <p> This brings us to the second point . Persistent economic inequality was never solved by the governments we elected since 1987 , with the most underrepresented regions in Mindanao remaining the poorest and conflict prone until only recently with the end of hostilities between the MILF and the government . <p> It is not surprising therefore is that 2/3 of our GDP has always come from Metro Manila and nearby Southern ang Central Luzon . Most of our manufacturing remained in these regions where better infrastructure and connectivity is present . Their GDPs per capita mirror that of Thailand while the rest is at ASEANs bottom levels . <p> This raises the argument that if more budgetary support @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and economic growth would be higher , and their poverty levels would be lower . <p> This would have made our economic development more balanced and peaceful over the last 30 years since the EDSA revolution that promised to bring the nation together in a more equitable way . <p> The last two years exposed the truth that much more could have been done by previous governments that seemed blind to this inequality . Had the Senate been more representative of the regions , the argument is that legislative priorities would have given the regions more attention and resources . <p> Persistent inequality despite the presence of these rights allow only a select to enjoy its freedom . This breeds a contempt for the system , creating constant conflict . <p> Now that the Visayas and Mindanao economies are growing in their GDP contribution , the efforts in the last two years to even out the imbalance by granting bigger allocations to the regions are laudable , yet much still needs to be done . <p> Of course , we can always argue that the Senate was never meant to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> More important than formal representation is the opportunity to be heard , especially on the emerging issues such as the need for broadening the peace and development gains of game changers like the Bangsamoro Organic Law , monitoring the effectiveness if Build Build Build and pursuing reforms in modernizing our public transport system , food supply and our countrys readiness for climate change impacts , among others . <p> These are matters that affect us in the regions in a fundamental way . <p> Can today 's senators and likely winners in 2019 help us deal with these ?
