<h> Bangladesh to be 26th largest economy <h> HSBC report says the country will be the biggest mover in global GDP ranking in 2030 <p> Bangladesh is likely to be the biggest mover in the global gross domestic product rankings in 2030 , becoming the 26th largest economy in the world from 42nd now , according to the latest report of HSBC Global Research . <p> The report -- The World in 2030 : Our long-term projections for 75 countries -- showed Bangladesh 's economy would jump 16 notches , the highest by any country , between 2018 and 2030 . <p> Bangladesh would be followed by the Philippines , Pakistan and Vietnam and Malaysia on the list of the biggest risers . <p> " The starting point for a country is a key part of its potential growth . It is very clear that a country such as Bangladesh has far more potential for growth than one like Norway , which is far richer , " said the report , which was published recently . <p> HSBC 's long-term growth model projections showed that the real GDP growth of Bangladesh would be 7.1 percent per year up to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the report . <p> The country is projected to grow 7.3 percent from 2018 to 2023 , 7.0 percent from 2023 to 2028 , and 7.2 percent from 2028 to 2033 . <p> Bangladesh will be a $700 billion economy in 2030 from $300 billion now , according to the report . <p> The research refreshed HSBC 's long-term forecasting framework to make model projections for 75 developed , emerging and frontier economies to assess growth potential and changes in global rankings by 2030 . <p> The report said China is set to continue to be the single biggest contributor to global growth over the next decade and by 2030 , will have become the world 's largest economy . One of the most striking rises amongst the rankings will be by India , which is set to become the world 's third-largest economy in just over a decade , up from seventh today -- leapfrogging the second- and third-largest developed economies of Germany and Japan . <p> The report focuses on six main categories of economic indicators : catch-up potential , population ( size and shape ) , human @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and technology . <p> Better educated workers are more likely to be productive , it said . <p> Poorer countries will have room to catch up by simply adopting best practice elsewhere , and those with strong governance are more likely to facilitate investment and growth .
