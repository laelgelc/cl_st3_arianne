<h> ' Extremely dangerous ' hurricane nearing Mexico 's Pacific coast <h> An " extremely dangerous " category 4 hurricane is nearing the Pacific coast of Mexico , bringing in the potential for life-threatening storm surge , wind and torrents of rain , according to weather authorities . <p> The US ' National Hurricane Centre ( NHC ) said that Willa weakened slightly on Monday afternoon , it was still expected to be a dangerous major hurricane when it slams into Mexico 's central Pacific coast " over or very near " Islas Marias on Tuesday morning , CNN reported . <p> Willa 's maximum sustained winds ticked down from 160 mph to 145 mph , bringing the hurricane down from Category 5 strength to Category 4 . <p> Its current intensity is about the same as Hurricane Michael 's when it made landfall in Florida 's Panhandle two weeks ago . <p> Willa became a tropical storm on Saturday morning and was a Category 5 hurricane in less than two days . <p> As of Monday morning , Willa had swelled by 80 mph in just 24 hours . <p> Storm surge accompanied by " large and destructive @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and southwestern coast , the NHC said . <p> Dangerous surf and riptides were expected along the southern coast of Baja California late Monday . <p> Rainfall ranging from six to 12 inches could spawn life-threatening landslides and flash flooding in portions of the Mexican states of Jalisco , Nayarit and Sinaloa , CNN quoted the NHC as saying . <p> There were 10 major hurricanes this year , including Willa , which ties 1992 as the most major hurricanes seen in the northeast Pacific in one year , CNN said . <p> Increasing numbers of major hurricanes , along with a greater propensity of storms to undergo " rapid intensification " are expected consequences of warmer ocean waters resulting from climate change . <p> The ocean waters off the western coast of Mexico are running 1-2 degrees Fahrenheit above average for late October .
