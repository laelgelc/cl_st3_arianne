<p> ( Bloomberg ) -- Naspers , Africa 's biggest company by market value , expects second-hand clothing sales online to pick up as companies around the world look to cut production of new goods to help address climate change . <p> " We are big investors in trading in second-hand clothes -- we think the world will need more recycling over time , " Chief Executive Officer Bob van Dijk told Bloomberg Television in Davos . <p> " In classifieds , we are helping to reduce the production of new goods . " <p> Naspers , through its majority-owned Prosus NV division , is investing in companies around the world to try and replicate the success of an early involvement in Tencent , now China 's largest company . Another preferred market is food-delivery , where Prosus recently lost an $8bn takeover battle for JustEat in the UK . <p> " Food delivery we think is still at an early stage and can be 30 or 50 times larger , " said Van Dijk . <p> Naspers shares declined 2.7% to R2,451.07 as of 12:20pm in Johannesburg .
