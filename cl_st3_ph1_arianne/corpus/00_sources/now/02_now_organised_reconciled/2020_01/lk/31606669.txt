<p> Climate change over the years has greatly affected the agriculture industry and farmers in Sri Lanka . Haphazard weather patterns , droughts , temperature changes and a number of other phenomena relating to climate change have left local farmers struggling negatively affecting their livelihoods . With adapting to climate change becoming top priority , a world bank funded project is now in the pipeline to assist farming communities to face new challenges brought on by climate change . <p> By June this year , in a bid to uplift agriculture in the country and to make the industry resilient to climate change the Ministry of Agriculture will commence the ' Climate-smart irrigated agriculture project ' . The project will seek to improve the climate resilience of farming communities in Sri Lanka and the productivity of irrigated agriculture in selected climatically vulnerable hotspot areas in Sri Lanka . <p> According to the Deputy Project Director , Dr. Priyantha Weerasinghe the basic idea is to assist farmers and uplift agriculture in areas that are most affected by climate change . According to Dr. Weerasinghe , the main issue faced by farmers due to climate change is the acute shortage of water . " Without water , they can not do anything , " he said . Floods , shorter and more intensive rains along with temperature changes have also become major obstacles to farmers . " When the temperature changes it affects the growth of plants , how nutrients are absorbed and cause a host of other issues , " he pointed out . " The areas chosen for the implementation of the project were done so with these facts in mind .
