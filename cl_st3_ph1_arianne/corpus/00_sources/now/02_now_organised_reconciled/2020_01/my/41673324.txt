<h> 10 reasons why it can not be business as usual <p> With household debt and federal government debt indicators rising and natural resources being depleted , it can not be business as usual for the Malaysian economy . Anil Netto says it is time for a radical re-think of the economy . <p> The Malaysian economy has entered a critical phase . Integrated into the global trading system as part of an export-led economy and driven by foreign direct investment , the economy today is feeling the effects of a global slowdown . <p> Compounding the problem are other structural weaknesses in the BN government 's economic model that tell us that it simply can not be business as usual for much longer . <p> Unfortunately , the mainstream economic framework does not have an integrated world-view that encompasses a broad range of inter-connected issues . The framework does not look at how economic policy is contributing to widening income inequalities , indebtedness ( individual and national ) , the weakening position of labour , sustainable development and climate change . <p> Support the struggle to build a New Malaysia based on Justice , Freedom , Solidarity : @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ analysis . And when it involves government-related projects , the cronyism can be blatant . <p> This is closely related to the phenomenon of ' money politics ' -- a euphemism for the institutionalised intertwining of business and political interests in the country . Neoliberal policies <p> The ' Father of Privatisation ' in Malaysia , former premier Mahathir Mohamad introduced Thatcherite policies in Malaysia as state-owned assets were privatised to private companies , many of them crony companies or linked to politically well-connected individuals . <p> Because some of these were essentially monopolies , they reaped super profits that served to enrich the ruling political-business elite . <p> Having lowered corporate tax and income tax ( for the higher bands ) over the years and with its expenditure now spiralling , the government now finds itself faced with persistent fiscal deficits . <p> It is thus forced to reduce subsidies and find new ways of raising funds . So now the public is faced with the prospect of GST , electricity tariff hikes , and 1Care . <p> Even the acquisition and installation of traffic cameras is reportedly being privatisated in a move that could rake even more money for the government -- and certain companies . <p> State-run essential services -- especially health care and education -- are stuck in a rut and their quality has suffered . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ just the rich , are forced to turn to the private sector , where they have to pay higher tariffs . <p> 3 . Financialisation and speculation driving up prices <p> Property-centric development , low bank interest rates , easy credit and speculation have driven up house prices . High house prices in turn have an inflationary effect on other essential goods while wages simply can not keep pace . <p> One way out of this would be to spread residential and work areas out of already heavily congested urban areas and introduce a better network of public transport . Tighter controls should be introduced to prevent foreign speculation in domestic property and to tighten up bank loans . New rules should be formulated to ensure property can only be sold after construction is completed . <p> In this way , inflationary pressure on urban land areas and property would be reduced . <p> 10 .
