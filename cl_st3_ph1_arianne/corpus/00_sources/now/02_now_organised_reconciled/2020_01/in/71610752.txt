<h> Davos 2020 : Will sweeping protests across the globe lead to a new form of capitalism <h> Last Updated : Thursday 23 January 2020 <p> The World Economic Forum ( WEF ) is not the place to talk ' poverty ' , ' social exclusion ' or ' the death of capitalism ' -- at least that is the moral judgement . But those words are being debated more than anything else at its annual meeting that also coincides with the forum 's 50th anniversary . <p> Another issue with a similar bandwidth is ' climate change ' -- again , discussed and debated in terms of social and economic losses to the world 's poor and marginalised . <p> By the time the WEF annual meeting ends on January 24 , 2020 , ' stakeholder capitalism ' can become a buzzword . A bit of background : WEF founder Klaus Schwab floated the term way back in 1971 . ' Stakeholder Capitalism ' was defined by him as a form of capitalism where the corporations behave or conduct businesses as trustees of society . After half a century , he again forcefully wrote and spoke about it @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the contemporary world 's " social and environmental challenges " . <p> What are these social and environmental challenges being discussed by corporate honchos with unusual fervour ? <p> Across the world there are protests , massive and intense in nature . The youth and children are joining forces to express grievances , starting from university fee hike to loss of resources and a better life in future . <p> Inequality in the distribution of income and resource has become mainstream global issues . Climate change and environmental crisis have already emerged as the most crippling reason for loss of livelihoods for the poor across the world . Children activists are trooping in at various forums , accusing leaderships and urging them forcefully to act to protect their future . <p> In Davos too inequality looms over corporate representatives and the champions of capitalism as a Damocles ' sword . In a way the rising restlessness among the next generation is a threat to both business and profit . <p> How can political leadership in elected governments afford a large , unhappy constituency ? High salaries of corporate executives has been mentioned by Klaus as a point of concern in context of inequality . <p> The new Davos Manifesto wants corporates to pay taxes , not to indulge in corruption and work towards " shared value creation " . This means the corporate factor in social , environmental and governance issues into their profit-making practice . <p> Sounds grand ; the question is who will ensure they do so ? These are all moral principles that just got a general approval . So , is this talk about ' stakeholder capitalism ' a ploy to diffuse anger against capitalists ? <p> If corporations are trustees of society , as according to this new form of capitalism propagates , what is the society 's supervision over them ? And who form this society ? <p> This is a critical aspect . Reports released at the WEF summit show that significant parts of population have been kept out of the society due to various discriminations . That was the argument for adopting wealth-creating capitalism . It has definitely failed .
