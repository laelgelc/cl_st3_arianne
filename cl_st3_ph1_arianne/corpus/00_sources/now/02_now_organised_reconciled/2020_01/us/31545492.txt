<h> Panama marks 20 years in charge of canal , faces climate threat <p> The United States handed control of the Canal to Panama on December 31 , 1999 <p> Panama on Tuesday marks two decades of control over the vital interoceanic Canal following its return by the United States , but worries over weak traffic and climate change loom . <p> On December 31 , 1999 at midday sharp , then-president Mireya Moscoso hoisted Panama 's red , blue and white flag over the Canal administration building for the first time . <p> The entire Central American nation was swept with joy , having finally received sovereignty over the Canal after 85 years of management by the US , which built and inaugurated the ocean route in August 1914 . <p> Twenty years later , recurrent droughts caused by climate change are the main threat to the 80 kilometer-long canal between the Atlantic and Pacific oceans , through which mostly American , Chinese and Japanese merchant ships pass . <p> " The impact of climate change is quite evident on the Panama Canal , " warned its administrator Ricaurte Vasquez . <p> A historic deficit of rainfall has @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Canal and the " challenge is now to find the solution to this lack of water for the next 50 years , " he explained . <p> This aerial photo taken in May 2016 shows the new Panama Canal expansion at the Gatun Locks in Colon , Panama <p> Future uncertain <p> According to the Panama Canal Authority ( ACP ) , the rainfall deficit reached 27 percent in 2019 compared to the average . <p> In addition , the temperature of Gatun Lake , the main tributary of the Canal , has increased by 1.5 degrees Celsius over the past ten years , which causes significant loss of water by evaporation . <p> That has meant the Canal only had three billion cubic meters of water this year , instead of the 5.2 billion cubic meters needed for its normal operation . <p> " Climate change is already hitting us .
