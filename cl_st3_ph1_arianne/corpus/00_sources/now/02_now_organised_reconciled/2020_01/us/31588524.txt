<h> Game of Thrones star travels to the Amazon to find out why the rainforest is burning <p> The devastating reality of climate change became irrefutable in 2019 when the Amazon rainforest erupted in wildfires . Over 30,000 outbreaks were detected in August alone as land was cleared by farmers and trees burnt for agricultural purposes . With fires having spread more rapidly due to the dry season in South America , this rate of deforestation is unprecedented on such a scale . <p> The world watches on as habitats are destroyed and hundred-year-old trees are burnt . Despite covering only 1% of the planet 's surface , the Amazon is home to 10% of the world 's wildlife species , according to WWF . Deforestation not only harms animals by ridding them of their homes , it means we are not benefiting from trees ' " mindblowing " ability to capture carbon dioxide from the air and absorb it , say scientists . The loss is near irreparable as reforestation takes years for trees to reach the same maturity -- some were up to 250 years old when they burnt down . <p> " It would be expected @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ really big happens in the next two or three months to avoid the high season of deforestation that starts in May , " Tasso Azevedo , coordinator of ' MapBiomas ' which monitors the rate of forest destruction , told Reuters . <p> In the same report , prime minister Bolsonaro claims his government is " protecting the rainforest adequately .
