Will Iowa do better in the 2020s ? <p> The Register 's editorial Published 10:00 a.m . CT Jan. 1 , 2020 <h> Editorial : If lawmakers invest in communities , the environment and the people , when the calendar flips to 2030 , Iowans will be able to take pride in a decade of accomplishment . <p> There is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Whether one believes a new decade started on Jan. 1 , 2020 , or will start a year from now , the 2010s are behind us . So this is a fitting time for taking stock , looking back and projecting forward . <p> Ideally , looking back should produce a sense of satisfaction , a pride in a job well done and knowledge that life is better than it was 10 years ago . That satisfaction may be hard to find across Iowa . In many respects , the past decade in this state was lost . It was one of marching in place . <p> Life for the average Iowan is not notably easier or more fulfilling than it was 10 years ago . <p> The minimum wage remains stuck at $7.25 per hour . Median household income has risen faster in Iowa than in the U.S. as a whole from 2010 to 2018 , census figures show , climbing to $68,718 , which is now above the national average . More Iowans gained health insurance , thanks to Obamacare and the expansion of Medicaid . <p> But Iowa 's governors and legislators undertook no great life-enhancing initiatives during the decade , nothing like the Vision Iowa program in the early 2000s that funded quality-of-life improvements all over the state . The focus has been on tax cuts and corporate giveaways , which do not position Iowa for a more prosperous future . <p> The telltale marker of Iowa 's lost decade is the condition of our lakes , rivers , streams and sources of drinking water .
