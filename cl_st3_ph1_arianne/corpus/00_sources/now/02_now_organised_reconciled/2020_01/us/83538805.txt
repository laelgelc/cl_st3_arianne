<h> This artist shows why it 's important to protect wildlife and wildlands <p> By <p> Nicole Javorsky <p> Courtesy of Zoe Keller <p> Story at a glance <p> Climate change threatens wildlife and natural ecosystems worldwide . <p> Art has a unique way of communicating the importance of protecting species and the planet while capturing what is at stake if we fail to prevent the worst effects of climate change . <p> Artist Zoe Keller has been at work in her studio creating drawings that depict at-risk species and wildlands . <p> In the era of massive threats to wildlife and planet Earth from climate change , it 's a tricky puzzle to figure out how best to communicate these threats . <p> Scientists , who have been sounding the alarm about our warming world , are beholden to the standards of their profession . For example , scientific research presents the evidence of the climate change problem alongside transparency about the study 's limitations and information about the likelihood of different scenarios playing out . While that might still be an open question , there 's something about art that can communicate the value of coral reefs , insects or even snakes in a way that 's different than other forms of communication . <p> Zoe Keller is a visual artist based in Portland , Ore. , who does extensive research ( reading , visiting sites or talking with scientists , sometimes all of the above ) before she captures at-risk species and wildlands in her large-scale , graphite drawings . " <p> When Keller starts a project , she goes to the library and checks out upwards of 45 books on the subject , whether that 's about the species of Zion National Park in Utah or Southeast Alaska . <p> " Whether or not I get to visit a place in person , there 's always a lot of reading of journal articles and books . I tease out storylines that I think might work well visually , " Keller says . " Whenever I have the chance , I love to be able to talk to scientists who are working on the ground .
