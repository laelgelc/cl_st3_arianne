<h> Climate refugees can not be sent back home , United Nations rules in landmark decision <p> Refugees fleeing the effects of the climate crisis can not be forced to return home by their adoptive countries , a United Nations panel has ruled , in a landmark decision that could open the door to a flood of legal claims by displaced people around the world . <p> The UN 's Human Rights Committee was making a judgment on the case of Ioane Teitiota , who applied for protection from New Zealand after claiming his life was at risk in his home country of Kiribati . The Pacific island is at risk of becoming the first country to disappear under rising sea levels . <p> The committee ruled against Teitiota on the basis that his life was not at imminent risk -- but it also outlined that countries could violate people 's international rights if they force them back to countries where climate change poses an immediate threat . <p> " Without robust national and international efforts , the effects of climate change in receiving states may expose individuals to a violation of their rights , " its ruling said @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ immediate impact on citizens of other countries , given that even Kiribati 's dire situation did not meet the threshold for Teitiota 's claim to succeed . But the decision could have a significant impact on future claims , as the number of people forced from their homes from the intensifying climate emergency grows . <p> Droughts , crop failure and rising seas are expected to force tens of millions to move to other areas in the coming years . A 2018 study by the World Bank found that 143 million people across South Asia , sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America are at risk of becoming climate migrants . <p> " Given that the risk of an entire country becoming submerged under water is such an extreme risk , the conditions of life in such a country may become incompatible with the right to life with dignity before the risk is realized , " its decision added . <p> The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change has identified Teitiota 's home nation of Kiribati as one of the six Pacific Island countries most threatened by rising sea levels .
