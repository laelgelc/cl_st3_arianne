<h> 90-year-old Alaska native woman will be 1st counted in Census <p> The census , a decennial event , is traditionally begun in rural Alaska out of necessity , which began there when the U.S. bought the territory from Russia in 1867 . <p> Author : Associated Press , TEGNA <p> Published : 1:55 PM PST January 21 , 2020 <p> Updated : 4:28 PM PST January 21 , 2020 <p> A Yup'ik elder born to nomadic parents in wild western Alaska just after the start of the Great Depression is set to be the first person counted in the 2020 Census . Lizzie Chimiugak has lived there for the last 90 years . Her family once lived in mud homes and lived a life following where they could find good hunting and fishing spots . <p> The Associated Press reports that on Tuesday , Steven Dillingham , director of the census bureau , will conduct the first interview . There are federal privacy laws that say the Census bureau wo n't confirm Chimiugak will be the first person counted , but it 's widely known in her hometown that she will be the first person counted . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Toksook Bay , Alaska , will be honored during a ceremony Tuesday at the school in Toksook Bay called Nelson Island School , which is just off Alaska 's western coast in the Bering Sea . <p> Completing the census form is not all the 90-year-old intends to do . She is also taking the opportunity to perform with Alaska Native dancers during the ceremony and raise the alarm of climate change and what it 's doing to the fish and animals of western Alaska to other women at the ceremony . <p> While speaking the Yup'ik language of Yugtun , with family members serving as interpreters , Chimiugak said " Elders that were before me , if they did n't die too early , I would n't have been the first person counted , " the Associated Press reported . <p> Jan. 20 , 2020 photo , a woman walks before dawn in Toksook Bay , Alaska , a mostly Yuip'ik village on the edge of the Bering Sea .
