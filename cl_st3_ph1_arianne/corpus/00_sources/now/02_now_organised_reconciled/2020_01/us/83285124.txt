( Photo : Noah Berger/Associated Press ) <p> An Arizona Republic and USA Today Network investigation that identified hundreds of communities across the West at risk of heavy casualties from wildfires was recognized with national honors . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the nationwide Philip Meyer Journalism Awards , part of the Investigative Reporters and Editors journalism contest . <p> It 's the second national recognition for " Ahead of the Fire , " which in October was awarded " Best Innovation Project " by Editor &amp; Publisher magazine . <p> In the wake of 2018 's fire in Paradise , California , which killed 85 people , journalists at The Republic began to examine whether other Western communities face similar risks . The analysis used a giant data set from a U.S. Forest Service analysis of fire potential for every 18-acre pocket of land in the United States . Reporters determined the wildfire hazard in every populated place in 11 Western states . <p> Then , the team combined U.S. Census and other data to measure such risk factors as evacuation routes , the age and disability status of people in those communities , and participation in the cellular emergency-alert system . <p> The analysis found more than 500 small communities across 11 states had a greater wildfire potential than Paradise . <p> " We were shocked by the destruction in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to other communities , " said Arizona Republic Executive Editor Greg Burton . " And , in the spirit of Philip Meyer , this talented team of reporters combined gumshoe reporting with scientific analysis . " <p> Joined by journalists from other newsrooms across the USA Today Network , reporters and videographers visited some of the at-risk communities to see the threat up close . <p> " The sophisticated graphics and compelling photos helped tell a gripping story that can be replicated in many newsrooms thinking about ways to bring the threat of climate change home to their audiences , " the contest judges wrote . <p> The Meyer award was created to promote journalism that uses empirical methods to explore an issue and illuminate a story . It is administered by the National Institute for Computer-Assisted Reporting , a joint program of Investigative Reporters and Editors and the Missouri School of Journalism .
