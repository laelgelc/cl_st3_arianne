<h> Cutting back the environmental impacts from holiday decorations <p> By Meteorologist Brian Doogs&amp;nbsp&amp;nbsp <p> Posted : Sun 7:08 PM , Jan 05 , 2020 <p> MADISON , Wis . ( WMTV ) -- Now that the holidays have come and gone , you are probably taking down those decorations and even thinking of throwing some of them away . Have you ever thought about if what you are doing is environmentally friendly ? There are simple things you can do to reduce your carbon footprint . <p> Our partners at Climate Central spent time delving into the environmental impact of real vs. artificial trees . This is not an easy question to answer . To assess the carbon footprints of each choice , a few things need to be considered -- the full life cycle of trees , from farming or production to transport , use , and disposal . <p> Because of their PVC production and shipping distance ( usually from China ) , fake trees have a much higher single-year footprint than natural trees . However , if the same artificial tree is used for five or more years , the total footprints are more comparable @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Donating is another option . Many Goodwill 's accept gently-used fake trees in their original box . <p> For natural trees , impacts are highest from transport and disposal . According to one analysis , driving 10 miles round-trip to buy the tree may offset its carbon storage benefits . But there are many ways to give trees a second life , from planting to donating it for ocean dune restoration . Some trees can even be treats for farm or zoo animals -- from goats to otters ! <p> Tree-trimming itself has its own footprint . According to PE Americas , LED lights have one fifth the carbon footprint of traditional incandescent lights .
