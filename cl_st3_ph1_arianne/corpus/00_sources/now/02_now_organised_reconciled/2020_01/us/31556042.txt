<h> Majority of Irish people in favour of wind energy -- survey <h> Research by Irish Wind Energy Association finds that climate change will influence how people vote in general election <p> At a national level , 79 per cent said they were either strongly in favour or tend to favour wind power , according to the survey . Photograph : David O'Brien <p> A majority of Irish people say that they are in favour of wind energy and that climate change will influence who they vote for at the next general election , according to a new survey . <p> The research was carried out on behalf of the Irish Wind Energy Association , ( IWEA ) which lobbies on behalf of the wind energy industry here . <p> It found that some 55 per cent of respondents said climate change will either " greatly " or " somewhat " influence who they are likely to vote for . Among rural dwellers , who live closer to wind farms , results were nearly identical to the national sample at 54 per cent . <p> The survey also polled people 's support for wind energy projects , which have @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the planning process in recent years . At a national level , 79 per cent said they were either strongly in favour or tend to favour wind power . For rural dwellers , the overall figure for support was the same , although 48 per cent said they were strongly in favour against 52 per cent nationally . <p> Support for wind power dropped significantly when respondents were asked about the development of wind farms in their locality . Nonetheless , slim majorities at a national level ( 55 per cent ) and among rural dwellers ( 51 per cent ) still favoured wind farms . The online survey was carried out among a nationally representative sample of 1,019 Irish adults . A supplemental survey sampled 200 residents in rural areas , spread between the countryside , villages and small towns . <h> ' Undermining efforts ' <p> David Connolly , the chief executive of IWEA , said there is a contradiction between the relatively high levels of support for wind energy indicated by the survey , and Government proposals on planning and noise emissions which he said would undermine @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ At the very moment we need to be developing wind energy at record levels the Government , through restrictive new planning guidelines , is undermining efforts to achieve its own targets for renewable electricity . " <p> Minister for Planning Eoghan Murphy and Minister for the Environment Richard Bruton published draft wind energy planning guidelines before Christmas . The guidelines propose new noise standards and minimum setback distances between wind farms and homes of four times the height of the turbines used at the plant . A minimum setback distance of 500m has also been proposed . <p> IWEA said the new noise limits are " potentially the harshest in Europe " and " will cost every person in Ireland an extra ? 550 in order to hit our 2030 climate action targets " . Dr Connolly said that " it is clear that candidates and parties putting forward policies designed to tackle climate change , including supporting renewable energy like wind power , will be rewarded for it on election day " .
