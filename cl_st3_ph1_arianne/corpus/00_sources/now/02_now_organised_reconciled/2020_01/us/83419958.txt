<p> We can breathe a sigh of relief after hearing Gov . Andrew Cuomo 's State of the State speech ( " Cuomo : ' Restore Mother Nature ' " Jan. 9 ) . The governor has committed himself to proposals that might just make possible the state 's goal of net zero carbon emissions by 2050 . <p> " Might " is the operative term . The proposals are hazy . The more specific they could be , the more they might become operational . <p> For instance , the governor 's commitment to building an electric vehicle road map could be elaborated to become part of a plan for a 55 percent reduction of greenhouse gas emissions from on-road transportation by 2035 . <p> Since there are currently 10.5 million fossil-fuel burning vehicles on the state 's roads emitting one third of the state 's greenhouse gases , building infrastructure to support electric vehicle purchases is vital to the state 's overall energy reductions . <p> In his speech , the governor characterized the threat of climate change as " transcendent , " which in this context means " beyond the range of normal human experience . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ need to go beyond our normal experiences of delay and denial to confront this challenge .
