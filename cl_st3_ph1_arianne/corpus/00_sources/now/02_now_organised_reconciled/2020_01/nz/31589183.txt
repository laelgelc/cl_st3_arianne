<p> The museum is currently running an exhibition showing water quality in various parts of the country : farm streams , lakes and harbours . <p> Bottles are artificially coloured to show what they believe is colour of water in those places . It also has an interactive display where people can supposedly improve the environment by touching a screen which says less cows . <p> Te Papa says the Te Taiao Nature exhibition , which includes the controversial dairy element , is designed to take a broad look at NZ 's environment , the threats it faces , and what can be done to protect it . <p> Spokesperson Kate Camp told Dairy News farming is one of a wide range of human activities that is looked at . <p> She says the exhibition content is based on various science and research papers including a report on NZs freshwaters values , state , trends and human impact , by Sir Peter Gluckman , a former Chief Science Advisor in the Prime Minister 's office . <p> She says they also used the Royal Society 's 2016 reports on climate change implications and the transition to a low-carbon economy @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ groups or individuals . <p> Camp says Te Papa 's challenge is to take a complex and varied picture , and present it in a simple , easy-to-understand way , that is still true to the overall picture . <p> " We ca n't be an encyclopaedia that presents the full complexity of an issue , but we can spark interest and get people thinking and talking . <p> " We hope that our visitors will seek out more information , and that they 'll make changes in their own lives to help protect the environment .
