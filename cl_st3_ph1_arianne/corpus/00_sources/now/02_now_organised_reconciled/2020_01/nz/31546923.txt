<h> Weathering new technology in Tonga - NIWA <p> There are about 800km between the southern and northern tips of Tonga - and a lot of ocean . <p> Many of this Pacific archipelago 's 169 islands are remote , exposed , sparsely populated and difficult to access . And when it comes to forecasting the weather in an area known for cyclones and also prone to drought , it 's a significant challenge . <p> However , that is all about to change . <p> NIWA is in the final stages of completing a project with the Tongan Meteorological Service ( TMS ) to install new weather stations and sea level monitoring equipment from one end of Tonga to the other . In what has been a huge logistical exercise six months in the planning and another year in the implementation , Tonga now has 21 new weather stations and two sea level monitoring stations . <p> One of the problems faced by TMS was the difficulty in receiving manual weather observations from parts of the country where there were no staff . <p> TMS head Ofa Faanunu said that challenge with communication , particularly from the outer @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ needed updating . <p> " We are now able to get data from these remote areas and can now work on building the capacity of the Met Office in all areas of forecasting . " <p> The project was part of the Climate Resilience Sector Project , funded by an Asian Development Bank ( ADB ) Grant . It will enable Tonga to be better prepared for extreme weather events and climate change as well as providing improved weather forecasting for businesses such as ferry operators and airlines . <p> NIWA principal scientist Dr Graham Elley , who managed the installations , says the new system was a complex logistical exercise but will help Tonga build resilience with effective warning of extreme weather and coastal events . <p> " The challenges started with how to get the equipment there . In the end we shipped three container loads , which was more than 20 tonnes , to the capital Tongatapu , and then faced the issue of how to get it - and the people we needed - to the outer islands to install it .
