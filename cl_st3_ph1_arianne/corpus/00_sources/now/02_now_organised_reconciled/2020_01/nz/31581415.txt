Through a ruthless private army and a reported " propensity for beheadings " , the clan cultivated a culture of impunity . Now , however , reports David Robie from Manila , a courageous judge has challenged the horror by jailing the masterminds of the 2009 Ampatuan massacre for life . <p> With the judge almost two hours late in arriving at the fortified special courtroom in Camp Bagong Diwa , a police barracks with a jail annex in Manila 's satellite Taguig City , fears were expressed for her safety . <p> The 101 accused ( although three were missing and cited for possible contempt of court ) for the heinous crime , dressed in yellow jail tees , were housed in in a barred cage sandwiched between lawyers and some 200 heavily armed police guards and waiting . <p> Many of the victims ' families burst into spontaneous applause for the jailing of the ringleaders ; others wept for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the accused were relieved with the acquittals . <p> Judge Joycelyn Solis-Reyes of the Quezon Trial Court Branch 221 announced to the court that she could deliver the shortened verdict rather than the full 761-page judgement or " it could take all day " . <p> Judge Solis-Reyes sentenced the 28 principal accused -- including three brothers of the powerful Ampatuan warlord clan from Mindanao -- to life in prison without parole and ordered them to pay a total of more than 155 million pesos ( almost NZ$5 million ) in changes to the heirs of 57 victims killed in the massacre . <p> The judge reduced the " official " death toll from 58 to 57 because the body of photojournalist Reynaldo Momay was never found .
