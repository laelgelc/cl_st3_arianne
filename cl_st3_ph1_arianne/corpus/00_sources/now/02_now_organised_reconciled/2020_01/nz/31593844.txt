<h> Congestion charging ' inevitable , ' but not now <p> A congestion charge is being ruled out by the Minister of Transport for now , but he is thinking longer term about how to use GPS transponders to track car usage and charge road user fees instead of fuel taxes , Dileepa Fonseka reports <p> Minister of Transport Phil Twyford believes congestion charging is " inevitable , " although he is ruling out introducing it in the " immediate future " . <p> The issue has become more pointed in this election year after National suggested in a discussion document late last year they were open to congestion charging . That would have left the way clear for Labour to use the idea without the fear of a ' new tax ' attack in an election campaign . <p> " We have no plans to introduce congestion taxes , " Twyford told Newsroom in a phone interview . <p> But Twyford also told Newsroom it could be part of a future GPS-based road user per-kilometre pricing system aimed at replacing declining fuel tax revenues . <p> " We 're doing some thinking about what might the future look @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ kind of GPS-based transport pricing system based on time and space use of the transport networks , " he said . <p> " If you do move to a system like that where every car has a transponder and so on , that would then give you a pretty powerful tool to do congestion pricing .
