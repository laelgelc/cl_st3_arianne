<h> Pretty average : NZ books in 2020 <p> ReadingRoom literary editor Steve Braunias previews the year ahead in New Zealand publishing . <p> There are some very , very good New Zealand books due to be published in 2020 but also a lot of fairly whatever ones and in general it looks like the year ahead in local publishing is destined to be pretty average , mate . <p> Where 's the new overview of Pacific or New Zealand history that has been years in the making , and is a masterpiece of scholarship and good thinking ? Where 's the new novel by Eleanor Catton ? Ooh , look , there 's the old novel by Eleanor Catton -- The Luminaries featured on the Nielsen best-seller list this month , even though it was published in 2013 . <p> But there are some big names ( CK Stead , Fiona Kidman ) and big projects ( the concluding volume of Peter Simpson 's study of Colin McCahon ) and a big secret . Rumour has it there 's a new biography or memoir in the works of a major , much-loved figure in New Zealand public life @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 's going to be huge , maybe . Lorde ? Taika ? Beaudy ? It 's not Jacinda Ardern , anyway , although a new biography -- another new biography , warm on the heels of Michelle Duff 's panegyric -- is due soon , about March , by Madeleine Chapman . <p> There are definitely going to be some absolutely beautiful art books . There is definitely going to be one new collection of poetry which will generate a good deal of excitement among some youngish people in Wellington . And there is definitely - I think , anyway ; I mean I promised to do it so I suppose it 'll happen -- going to be a kind of sequel to my true-crime book The Scene of the Crime . <p> Throughout , ReadingRoom will do its best to cover , to review , to feature in some which way the works of genius or mere competence that are about to creep into bookstores across the nation . To the specifics ; as follows , a preview of the publishing highlights in New Zealand in 2020 . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ top of the Nielsen best-seller chart for New Zealand fiction is a book that could make its author very , very big on the international market : In The Clearing , the latest crime novel by Melbourne-based author JP Pomare . His last book Call Me Evie was one of the biggest sellers of 2019 in New Zealand , and huge in Australia , too . He 's got a good touch , he knows how to creep readers out , and chooses good subjects . In The Clearing is based on an Australian cult which went horribly wrong . <p> The short story ! It 's awesome , and the proof is in the beautiful images collected in New Zealand Seaweeds : An illustrated guide by Wendy Nelson , published by Te Papa . Over 150 genera and 250 key species are described , with each entry illustrated by underwater photographs as well as herbarium scans , microscopic photographs and reproductions of celebrated botanical artist Nancy Adams ' paintings . <p> Best-selling novelist Nicky Pellegrino returns with what appears to be a tearjerker : Tiny Pieces of Us , published by Hachette .
