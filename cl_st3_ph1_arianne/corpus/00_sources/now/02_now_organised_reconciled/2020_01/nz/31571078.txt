<h> Meet our newest creepy crawly citizens <p> A meet-and-greet with 10 of New Zealand 's 68 freshly minted insect citizens <p> Moths which live on volcanoes , a mite too small to photograph , a flightless stonefly , and a wasp with teensy wings to cope with windy weather are just some of New Zealand 's newest citizens . <p> These finds represent just a smattering of the 68 insects at our feet and in the trees around us which were studied and formally described by scientists in 2019 . <p> Being ' described ' is a little like getting a birth certificate . The characteristics of the species are detailed , it 's slotted into a family and genus and given a scientific name . <p> Thousands of New Zealand 's insects are yet to be formally described by scientists . <p> Manaaki Whenua Landcare Research 's head curator of the arthropod collection Darren Ward said prioritising which species to study and name is a juggling act . <p> Biosecurity concerns can play a part . Identifying what 's native from what 's introduced can be one driver . <p> Many species , few people <p> At @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ will focus on wasps , arachnid buffs on spiders . New Zealand does n't have experts for every insect group , according to Ward . <p> " We do n't have anybody that works on flies . Most people would go ' Flies , who 's really worried ? ' but there are several thousand species of flies and they are really important pollinators . " <p> Aphids and thrips , which can be agricultural pests also lack a local expert to identify natives versus imported biosecurity risks . <p> Even when there are experts , it 's a numbers game . There are around 5000 species of beetles in New Zealand . <p> " There 's a lot of stuff . Not so many people , " said Ward . <p> Why should people care about insects ?
