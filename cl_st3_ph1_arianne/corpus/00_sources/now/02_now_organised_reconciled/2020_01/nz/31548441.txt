Photo : Supplied <h> Food scraps : The man turning our waste into natural gas <p> Auckland Council just announced kerbside food waste collections from 2021 , taking up to 100,000 tonnes of scraps out of landfill . But where 's that rotting waste mountain going to go ? Business editor Nikki Mandow tracked down the guy with the plan and asked him . Spoiler alert : It 's not compost , and it 's quite cool . Or hot . Warmish maybe . <p> It sounds corny , but Andrew Fisher is a man on a mission . Several missions . As a former NZ Army special air services officer , he has done stints in Afghanistan , Papua New Guinea , and East Timor . <p> And the week I meet him at his far-from-swish office/factory in the Auckland industrial suburb of Wiri , he 's off to give as much of his in-demand ' O ' @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ supplies after the volcanic eruption at Whakaari-White Island . <p> But Fisher 's main job these days is trying to tackle the problem of food waste . And hopefully make money out of it . <p> For the last 12 years his company , EcoStock Supplies , has taken up to 35,000 tonnes of waste food a year from supermarkets and manufacturers - bread , chips , vegetable scraps , breakfast cereals , pasta etc - and turned it into animal feed . <p> But there 's a whole lot you ca n't put into animal feed . Meat , for example , or pizza boxes with that scraping of Hawaiian in the bottom . <p> So from next year , EcoStock will be heading into a whole new league - establishing in New Zealand a process which could in the end turn the hundreds of thousands of tonnes of food waste produced each year in this country into an alternative for natural gas . <p> Fisher is the driving force behind Ecogas , a joint venture company which has just started building a $10 million-plus " anaerobic digestion " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ other partner is renewable electricity and gas company Pioneer Energy . <p> Caption : The Ego Gas plant is modelled on a sister plant in Perth ( pictured ) and on similar digesters used for several years in Europe . You put the food in , let the micro-organisms chomp on it for a while , and you get methane gas , CO2 and fertiliser . <p> The main difference between composting and the anaerobic digestion process is , as the name suggests , the presence or absence of oxygen . <p> Composting is aerobic = with air ; anaerobic is the opposite . With the anaerobic digester , the biogas produced from the closed system rises into the dome and gets captured , piped out and used . <p> How it works <p> " You have a big tank and you are continuously feeding it with what looks like a thick spirulina that 's made out of food waste .
