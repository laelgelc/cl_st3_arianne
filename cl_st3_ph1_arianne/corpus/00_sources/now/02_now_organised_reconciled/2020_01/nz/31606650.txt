<h> School , hospital upgrades part of $200m fund to reduce state sector 's carbon footprint <p> The government is this morning announcing money for upgrades at schools and hospitals so they can move to cleaner energy . <p> Climate Change Minister James Shaw says the money for a cleaner public sector is leading by example when it comes to climate change . Photo : RNZ /Dom Thomas <p> Up to $4.8 million will be available for eight schools to upgrade their old coal-fired boilers , and up to $5.2m for upgrades at two hospitals . It is the first confirmed spending from a $200m fund aimed at reducing the carbon footprint of the state sector . <p> " As part of the government 's $12 billion infrastructure stimulus spend that Grant ( Robertson ) is announcing today , $200m of that has been set aside for a clean-powered public service . Today what we 're announcing is that the first projects under that are eight schools and two hospitals . " <p> Shaw said it was believed there were about 200 schools still running on coal . The eight schools represented 4 percent of those . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a good start . The boilers have to get replaced at some time but everyone knows that schools do n't have a lot of money and so for a lot of them they 've been putting it off and putting it off . And we ca n't afford to wait when it comes to climate change any more so we 're basically giving them a helping hand . " <p> The hospitals are Ashburton and Hillmorton . <p> Shaw said the public sector had to lead by example when it came to climate change . <p> Asked if a tougher line needed to be taken - perhaps in the form of a ban on coal - Shaw said : " We sort of already have in the sense that as ... public organisations are replacing their plant in the normal business cycle we 've already said you can not replace that with a new coal boiler " . <p> He advocated for biomass - wood - boilers as they had lower emissions profiles.
