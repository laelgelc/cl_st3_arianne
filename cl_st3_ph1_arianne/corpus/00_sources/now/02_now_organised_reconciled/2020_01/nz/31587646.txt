<h> Platypus numbers on the ' brink of extinction ' , new research finds <p> Researchers say the platypus is being driven towards the " brink of extinction " , with numbers halving since Europeans arrived in Australia . <p> Climate change , dam building and land clearing of platypus habitats may have caused local extinctions across 40 percent of the species ' range , according to a study published in the scientific journal Biological Conservation . <p> And it 's feared that current threats and worsening climate change could cause the platypus population to decline between 51 percent and 73 percent over the next 50 years . <p> The Sydney Morning Herald reports platypus numbers were likely affected by the record heatwave and on-going droughts , even before the bush fires . <p> Richard Kingsford , director of the UNSW centre and one of the study 's authors , said the disruption of their freshwater habitats is affecting their " ability to survive during extended dry periods " , the Sydney Morning Herald reports . <p> " If we lost the platypus from Australian rivers , you would say , ' what sort of government policies or care @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> " What sort of nonchalance and disregard for one of the world 's most important species has allowed this to happen ? " <p> The lead researcher of the study , Gilad Bino , believes there is an urgent need to begin national conservation efforts for the species ' survival . <p> A spokesperson for NSW Environment spokeswoman told the Sydney Morning Herald the government " recognises that a range of factors , exacerbated by the current prolonged drought conditions , may be placing the long term viability of platypus populations at risk " .
