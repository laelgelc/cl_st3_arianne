<h> Gas flaring in Nigeria <p> If you take a long drive along Ebedei , Goi , Oloibiri , Bodo and other villages in the Niger Delta region , plumes of smoke and flames will punctuate your journey . Those are gas flares and they are lighting up the skies like a thick fog . Nigeria has been exporting oil for more than 60 years , and the sector accounts for more than half of the government 's revenue . <p> In a simple term , gas flaring is the burning of natural gas , coal and petroleum . Experts say flaring is the easiest method producers use as they do n't have to stop pumping oil . Flaring is preferred because methane is burned off . <p> According to www.bloomberg.com , gas flaring globally emits more than 350 million tons of carbon dioxide equivalent in a year . In Nigeria , oil companies flare over 313 million standard cubic feet of gas annually , according to satellite data estimates from Nigeria 's environment ministry . The flared gas in turn results in the emission of 16.5 million tonnes of carbon dioxide . <p> What is the effect of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . People are living under the shade of hazard because of gas flaring . Exposure to air pollutants released by gas flaring has been linked to cancer and lung damage , as well as neurological and reproductive problems . Kester Chuwkuma who farms in Goi said , the mangrove forests are caked in bitumen . Even when he managed to plant crops , they shoot up like rocket on mission but barren like a winter rain . When he reverted to fishing , his village 's fishing creek was contaminated ; now , he must revert to other means of business to survive . Much more is the incessant noise that sends animals scurrying and people must yell to be heard over the growling flames . In the rainy season , no one needs a microscope to observe evidently black rainwater . The World Bank notes that gas flares contribute to climate change through the emission of millions of tons of C02 to the atmosphere . <p> Besides , it wastes a valuable energy resource that could be used to support economic growth and progress .
