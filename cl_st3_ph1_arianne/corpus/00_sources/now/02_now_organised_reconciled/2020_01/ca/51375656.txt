<p> Why it matters : Nexii and Gregor Robertson say the company 's proprietary building material can have a positive environmental impact <p> Vancouver 's former mayor is taking to the corporate world once again . <p> Nexii Building Solutions Inc. announced Tuesday ( January 21 ) it 's tapped Gregor Robertson as the construction firm 's new executive vice-president of strategy and partnerships . <p> The company , which was founded in Moose Jaw before relocating its head office to Vancouver , is known for developing a building material known as Nexiite that can be used as an alternative to cement and concrete for floors , walls and roofs . <p> Cement has a large carbon footprint , due to its energy intensity . <p> About 900 kilograms of CO2 is produced for every tonne of Portland cement , according to a 2018 study published in Science Direct . <p> Materials that have similar properties as cement , but lower carbon intensities , are being pursued as a way to decarbonize the construction sector . <p> " I 'm proud to be part of a Canadian company with significant potential to make a huge positive impact on the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ statement . <p> " We are currently facing a global housing shortage , and faster construction processes and retrofit solutions for existing buildings will help improve cost efficiencies and drive housing supply . " <p> The Nexii job is n't the former mayor 's first foray into the corporate world . <p> Since announcing in 2018 he would not seek re-election for the city 's top job , Robertson has been working part-time with the Global Covenant of Mayors for Climate and Energy as the organization 's ambassador . <p> Nexii employs about 50 workers at its head office in Vancouver and another 35 at its manufacturing plant in Moose Jaw .
