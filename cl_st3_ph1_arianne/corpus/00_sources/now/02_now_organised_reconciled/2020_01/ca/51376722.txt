<h> Owners of Collingwood 's former Goodyear site facing charges , jail time over toxic transformers <p> The property at 101 Mountain Rd. , used to be a Goodyear plant . Erika Engel/CollingwoodToday <p> The director of a Collingwood corporation is facing jail time for failing to remove a highly toxic substance from a Mountain Road property after receiving an official order from Environment Canada . <p> Collingwood Prime Realty Holdings Corp . and its director , Issa ( Chris ) El-Hinn , were sentenced in Ontario court earlier this week with a combined penalty of $420,000 and 45 days jail time for El-Hinn to be served on the weekend . <p> The charges stem from old electrical transformers and capacitors in use on the former Goodyear property at 101 Mountain Rd. , which is now owned by Collingwood Prime Realty . <p> The transformers contain high concentrations of polychlorinated biphenyl ( PCB ) , which has been prohibited for use in Canada since the 1980s . <p> David Rennie is operations manager of Environmental Enforcement for Environment and Climate Change Canada . It has been known to cause cancer in animals and is suspected of causing cancer in humans . <p> An expert witness in the case against Collingwood Prime Realty suggested just a drop could cause significant damage and poses a threat to humans . <p> If a transformer containing PCB catches fire , the burning PCB creates another toxic substance known to cause birth defects and impair cognitive function . <p> " If there was a fire ... it could have been disastrous , " said Rennie . " If it leaked and got down to ground water it could have poisoned the town 's water easily . " <p> He said nothing was leaking on the site , and the transformers and capacitors have been safely removed and disposed of now . <p> Rennie suggested the transformers could have been manufactured in the 1960s and were located outside exposed to the elements . <p> Rennie said Environment Canada first got involved in November 2013 when an inspecting officer issued a written notice to Collingwood Prime Realty to have two transformers and two capacitors removed and safely disposed of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ transformers . <p> Today , transformers must be filled with mineral oil , PCBs are not permitted in new transformers . <p> " It costs more than $110,000 to safely remove and dispose of PCB-containing materials , " said Rennie . <p> That 's added to the cost of replacement . It takes approximately six months to order new transformers . <p> An Environment Canada officer returned to the site in September 2014 to find the previous written notice was ignored and the PCB material was still on site . <p> Rennie said at that time , the officer issued an Environmental Protection Compliance Order to Collingwood Prime Realty and its director El-Hinn . <p> In April 2015 , Environment Canada determined the order was not followed . <p> " At the end of the day , the company as well as the director completely ignored and refused to follow the order that the officer had written up , " said Rennie . <p> Environment and Climate Change Canada started an investigation .
