Hell , even as a 2010s-era indie music snob , it only took a handful of listens for me to brand myself a Belieber and brag to anyone who would listen that Stratford , Ont. , was only about half an hour away from my hometown . ( Years later , I would sit on the Avon Theatre steps where Justin used to play his acoustic guitar and fight back the urge to tell anybody who passed me that he was my son . ) It was such an easy song to like . And ultimately , it was like a mainline to being young again . <p> Because that 's what made a song like " Baby " so easily digestible : where the magic of crushes and innocent heartbreak that accompany low-stakes relationships tend to fade the further into adulthood you go , Bieber 's premiere single served as a delightful time machine . It evoked emotions many of us had n't felt since junior high and tapped into a nostalgia that ended up defining the decade in its own way . It sounded like butterflies , the first time holding hands , and quiet but bold -- albeit still very harmless -- emotive declarations . It was a tiny boy singing about how much he liked someone and how much he hoped they could be together despite knowing it might never be so . It was the most fun . <p> As we know , not falling in love with the song proved impossible . It debuted at No. 5 on the Billboard Hot 100 and was the most-viewed video on YouTube until Psy 's " Gangnam Style " surpassed it in November of 2012 . True , the song -- and Bieber -- obviously followed the formula that pop music has long been built on ( a young star sings about puppy love as a means to launch a brand he 'll inevitably abandon in a handful of years as he grows up ) . But in 2010 , it felt brand new -- and newer still for Canada , a nation that 's global musical offerings at the time felt far removed from the majesty of a young pop singer who 'd used the internet to earn the attention of Usher and , subsequently , the audiences Usher had placed him in front of . It felt like a chance to relive the ' 90s and the young generation of pop stars it produced ( Britney Spears , *NSYNC , Backstreet Boys ) , but under an updated , improved Canadian lens . It felt like we could learn from old industry mistakes . It felt like a new era -- the dawn of a new day . <p> And it was so easy to sing and dance to , and as a result , easier still to defend to naysayers whose problems with the song seemed rooted in cynicism and bitterness . Unfortunately , because these works did n't fit colonial ideas of what constituted " art , " they were labeled either " crafts " or anthropological artifacts for centuries -- not art worthy of examining , studying and displaying in art museums . This extended to the literary realm as well . In her seminal book Survival : A Thematic Guide to Canadian Literature , Margaret Atwood theorized that the central symbol in Canada , and therefore Canadian literature , was survival . This was based on early settlers ' experiences of " bare survival in the face of ' hostile ' elements and/or Natives . " Though they did exist , Indigenous writers were not considered ; we only ever appear in the book as " hostile Natives " that white Canadians must survive . <p> In her pivotal 1991 paper " The Politics of Inclusion and Exclusion : Contemporary Native Art and Public Art Museums in Canada , " Mohawk curator Lee-Ann Martin named the ultimate effect of this erasure : " The exclusion of the arts of Native peoples implies that the artistic and cultural contributions to Canadian history by Canada 's First Nations Inuit , and M ? t is are non-existent . " Because our art and literature were not acknowledged , as far as the Canadian public was concerned , it did n't exist . And since Indigenous peoples ' supposed lack of history , art , politics , science , medicine and culture was often used to excuse Canada 's systematic attempts to wipe out our nations , this sort of erasure had concrete effects on our lives . <p> This colonial mentality -- that Indigenous people are " backward " or " uncivilized " or a " problem " that needs to be solved -- directly resulted in the creation of government-mandated residential schools , drastically interrupting our nations ' cultural and artistic development .
