The polarization of wealth and power <p> In December 2010 , a 26-year-old fruit vendor set himself on fire to protest the cruelty and unbridled wealth and authority of the powerful in his home country of Tunisia . Mohamed Bouazizi 's sacrifice sparked a movement that came to be known as the Arab Spring -- a contagious energy that toppled dictators and sparked pro-democracy protests but at the cost of thousands of lives , millions of refugees and a rage that still burns across North Africa and the Middle East . <p> Such rage burned closer to home too as in September 2011 , when Occupy Wall Street broke out in protests that spread to nearly 1,000 cities around the world . The language of the " 99 per cent " influenced struggles for an increased minimum wage , the reduction of student debt , and spurred larger conversations around inequality and economic justice .
