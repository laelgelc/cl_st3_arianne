" <p> For about a year , Martin and a small crew have been running a demonstration algae plant out of a small tent at his company 's cement factory in St. Marys , Ont. , about 170 kilometres west of Toronto . He pointed out that cement is the most manufactured product in the world , used to build things like hydroelectric dams . <p> " We see a smokestack and we say , ' Look , that 's evil , that 's got to go , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cement , you make a tonne of carbon dioxide . So we need to find a way to use that , and luckily , nature has provided algae . " <p> Pipes from the smokestacks at the cement plant run to Pond 's tent , transporting CO2 into a giant , 22,000-litre tank known as a bioreactor . The algae then does what it does best , according to project manager Tim Everett : It gobbles up the CO2 . <p> The result is about 20 kilograms of a thick green paste produced daily . " It really is almost a limitless byproduct , " said Everett , a mechanical engineer . <p> The team then uses that paste to cook up a series of green superfoods , including chlorella and spirulina , as well as feed for farm animals . <p> Martin admitted the technology is n't new , and Pond Technologies is n't the only one doing it .
