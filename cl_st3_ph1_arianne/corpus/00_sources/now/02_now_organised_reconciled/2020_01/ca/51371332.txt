<p> OTTAWA -- Prime Minister Justin Trudeau is reviving his practice of holding periodic cabinet retreats outside the nation 's capital -- an exercise in regional outreach that his office maintains is worth the added cost of ferrying ministers around the country . <p> Beginning Sunday , he and his 36 ministers will be holed up in Winnipeg for three days to discuss priorities and plot how to bring them to fruition in a House of Commons where the Liberals hold only a minority of seats . <p> That is to be followed by a three-day caucus retreat with Liberal MPs in Ottawa , in preparation for the resumption of Parliament on Jan. 27 -- its first extended sitting since the Liberals were reduced to a minority in the October election . <p> The choice of Winnipeg is a nod to the East-West divide exposed in the election . The Liberals shut out entirely in Alberta and Saskatchewan , where voters were irate over environmental policies they believe have gutted the energy industry . <p> Manitoba , where the Liberals lost three of six seats , is somewhat friendlier territory .
