<p> KINGSTON -- As the effects of climate change continue to chip away at food security , the area 's public health officials are calling on the federal government to commit to local measurement of food insecurity . <p> The board of Kingston , Frontenac and Lennox and Addington Public Health adopted a motion Wednesday afternoon calling for the government to make the Household Food Security Survey Module a core module in the Canadian Community Health Survey and to incorporate updates to Canada 's Food Guide into the model used to determine the price of nutritious food . <p> The motion came as public health released its 2019 cost of eating healthy report , showing that 13 per cent of households in the Kingston area , or about one in eight , experience food insecurity . <p> The monthly cost of healthy food for a family of four last year was $955 , a nine per cent increase from the previous year and a 21 per cent increase since 2012 . <p> " Data from 2012 to 2019 for the KFLA area show that increasing food costs along with income inadequacy make food insecurity an issue for our community @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the board stated . " Income security responses are needed to ensure vulnerable populations have adequate incomes to pay for basic expenses , such as the cost of healthy food . " <p> The direct and indirect effects of climate change , locally and abroad , are contributing to the higher costs . <p> Wildfires in California have begun to compel farmers there to change the crops they are planting , board member David Pattenden said . <p> John Wise , a farmer and councillor and board representative for Lennox and Addington County , said the impacts of climate change are already being seen locally . <p> He pointed to last winter , when a cycle of freezing and thawing hit his winter wheat crop and was followed by a spring so wet that planting was delayed by about five weeks . <p> " It is going to get worse and worse , " Wise said . " It 's the weather , driven by climate instability .
