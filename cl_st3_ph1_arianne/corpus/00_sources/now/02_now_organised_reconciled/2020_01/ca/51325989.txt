The damage was man-made , caused by chlorofluorocarbon chemicals found in things like spray cans , fridges , foam insulation and air conditioners . <p> At its worst , in the late 1990s , about 10 per cent of the upper ozone layer -- approximately 15 to 30 kilometres above Earth -- was depleted . An international agreement called the Montreal Protocol compelled businesses to come up with replacements for those harmful products . One hundred and 80 countries eventually signed the protocol , agreeing to phase out chlorofluorocarbons . <p> Had there been no correction , had the destruction of atmospheric ozone continued , the hole would have grown by some 40 per cent by 2013 . In our lifetime . Like " AIDS from the sky , " frantic scientists warned . Unhalted , there would have been an additional 280 million cases of skin cancer in the U.S. alone , and the world , those same scientists predicted , would be at least 25 per cent hotter by now .
