<p> The 2019 fire season in Western Canada has begun , but this year some former residents of Alberta and B.C. wo n't be there . <p> They 've lived through the last two years of devastating wildfires and have moved on , for good . Seeking safety for themselves and their families , they 've picked up stakes , often taking an economic hit . <p> " I sold my home and got out of it , " says Darlene Powell , 73 , who has moved from Kelowna , B.C. , to Carleton Place , Ont . " I just could n't handle how bad the air quality was . My breathing was getting worse and I ended up on a puffer to ease the tightness in my lungs . " <p> There are no hard statistics on the number of people leaving Western Canada due to the smoke from wildfires , but there 's evidence that residents like Powell , young and old , often on fixed or limited incomes , are making tough choices about where they want to live and to work .
