<h> Opinion : Vehicle choice seems lost in the concerns for the environment <h> Opinion : Vehicle choice seems lost in the concerns for the environment <p> Vehicle manufacturers have done a good job of marketing the perceived benefits of SUVs and light trucks , but it seems few purchasers have considered what their purchase does for the environment , says columnist.Sun Yilei / REUTERS <p> Despite indications to the contrary , Canadians across the country do agree on some things . And here , I 'm referring to more than just hockey and Tim Hortons . <p> Take vehicle choice , as informed by Statistics Canada vehicle sales data for 2010 to 2018 . The statistic that leaps off the page is the growing proportion of light trucks in the overall sales figures . While this might be interpreted as a trend unique to Western Canada , there is consistency in the data across all regions . <p> For Canada as a whole , this segment increased from 54 per cent to 69 per cent . <p> Car manufacturers have been remarkably successful in creating demand for vehicles that offer many of the same comfort and convenience features @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ And indeed , it is the passenger car segment that has seen a corresponding decline in sales share . <p> SUVs have gained status as an acceptable , if not the preferred , form of urban transportation . As for trucks , they are n't just for the farm anymore . Their heavier platforms offer a perception of greater passenger safety . <p> There are implications of such a strong shift in consumer preference . Increasing vehicle weight suggests that Canada is moving directionally opposite to its greenhouse gas ( GHG ) emissions targets in the 2015 Paris Agreement . <p> Statistics from the Global Fuel Economy Initiative ( GFEI ) back this up . GFEI is a 2009 partnership between international agencies , which promotes action to improve energy efficiency of the global light-duty vehicle fleet . Its target is to halve average fuel use per kilometre by 2030 , against a 2005 benchmark . <p> The latest results are not encouraging . Canada was one of only four countries in the GFEI group to see an increase in its average fleet fuel consumption between 2015 and 2018 . <p> Care to guess which country has the highest average CO2 emissions per kilometre ? Why , it 's Canada . For the last three years , in fact . Our average emissions in 2017 were higher even than the U.S. , a country often associated with a preference for large , inefficient vehicles . <p> One potentially puzzling aspect of the vehicle sales trend is the implication that Canadian consumers do not take account of the environmental impact when making their purchase decision . If true , how does this square with the prevailing view that we are becoming ever more concerned about climate change ? <p> Perhaps the Canadian vehicle buyer who does care about action on climate change is making a flawed assumption . Vehicle comfort , safety , convenience and cold weather performance are also important . <p> On the other hand , if decisions for larger and heavier vehicles are being made by those claiming to be serious about climate action , but with full knowledge and disregard for the higher emissions that will result , then it suggests a level of hypocrisy that is difficult to comprehend . <p> It will take concerted effort by everyone to meet Canada 's GHG emission reduction goals .
