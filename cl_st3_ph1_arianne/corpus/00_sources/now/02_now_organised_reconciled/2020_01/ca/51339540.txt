<h> The Coal Industry Is Dying in Spite of Trump <h> A huge reduction in coal use in 2019 brings some not-horrible climate change news , for once . <p> Here 's some not bad climate change news : Carbon emissions in the U.S. dropped in 2019 , driven by a record reduction in the use of coal power . <p> Coal use dropped by a full 18% last year , according to a preliminary estimate from the Rhodium Group , a private research group that studies energy markets and climate policy . That caps off a decade in which domestic coal use was cut in half , as renewables and gas power picked up the slack . Mostly thanks to the coal drop , carbon emissions in the power sector declined by 10% , a big turnaround from 2018 , when they ticked up by 1.2% . And coal use dropped by just 5% in 2019 . <p> The bad news : The rest of the economy -- including the transportation and manufacturing sectors , and buildings -- is still heavily reliant on fossil fuels . <p> Coal plants have closed in record numbers across the country even @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ President Donald Trump 's replacement for the Obama administration 's Clean Power Plan -- which , though it was never implemented , would have shifted the energy sector away from coal -- was designed to allow new coal plants to open and existing plants to run longer . <p> Trump also appointed Andrew Wheeler , a former coal lobbyist , to run the Environmental Protection Agency . <p> Sixty-one coal plants have announced plans to shut down since Trump took office , according to the Sierra Club , and 2019 saw shutdowns of some of the biggest U.S. plants . But 231 plants are still operating without plans to shut down . Coal just has n't been able to keep up with the cheaper energy that 's available from renewables and natural gas -- the latter of which is more than 30% cheaper than coal . <p> In April of 2019 , renewables produced more energy than coal for the first time ever .
