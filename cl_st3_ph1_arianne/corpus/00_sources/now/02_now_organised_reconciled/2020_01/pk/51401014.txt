<p> ISLAMABAD : In a major development on Tuesday , a parliamentary committee approved the much-awaited Geographical Indications ( GI ) Bill , 2019 , to protect the commercial heritage of goods produced in the country . <p> The legislation has remained pending for over 18 years . <p> According to details , the Senate 's Standing Committee on Commerce and Textile approved the GI Bill , which will now be presented before the upper house of parliament for its consent . The bill will then be forwarded to the National Assembly ( NA ) for deliberations . <p> The bill 's draft was earlier approved by the Cabinet Committee for Disposal of Legislative Cases ( CCLC ) . <p> Once the bill is passed , Pakistan will be able to claim and use GI for goods such as Basmati Rice , Sargodha Kinnow , Peshawari Chappal , Hunza apricot and many other products . <p> Geographical indication is a broad term which includes indications of source and appellations of origin . Since the majority of the goods being produced in India and Pakistan are the same , the neighbouring country 's exporters claim that the product is of Indian origin and indication causes damage to Pakistan internationally . <p> " We have studied the GI bill in detail . We expect that after the law 's approval from parliament , the legislation will benefit not only exporters and traders but also the growers of the country , " he added . <p> Members of the committee also suggested that the IPO and Commerce Ministry add protection of products under the GI law while keeping climate change in mind . <p> In the absence of GI legislation , international brands continue selling Pakistan-origin goods such as Paul Smith 's Peshawari chappal @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of getting premium on its GIs in the international market as member countries of the World Trade Organisation ( WTO ) are required to protect GIs under Article 22-24 of the Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights agreement . <p> The GI bill entails that once a GI is registered , an infringement action can be initiated both by the registrant and authorised users . <p> The proposed bill also provides for both civil and criminal remedies in cases of infringement . Civil remedies include imposition of fines , forfeiture to the government of all goods and things , means of which the offence had been committed , damages , an account of profit , together with or without any order for delivery of the infringing label and indications for destruction or erasure while criminal remedies include imposition of fine or imprisonment or both . <p> All suits for infringement shall be filed before the Intellectual Property Tribunal ( IPT ) whereas appeal against the decision of the registrar shall lie before the high court .
