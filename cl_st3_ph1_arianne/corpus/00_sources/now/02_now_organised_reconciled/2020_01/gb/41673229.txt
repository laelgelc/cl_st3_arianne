<h> Why worry over a warming world ? <p> It seems barely a day goes by without a news story about climate change . Rewind even just a few years and this was not the case . As we enter a new decade , we are beginning an era of environmental awakening . People are realising that climate change is the number one issue of our time , regardless of your age , location , background , or income -- we are all affected . <p> The world is warming with dangerous consequences and it is happening as a direct result of human activity . The science on the matter is unquestionable . The more we burn coal , oil and gas , the hotter the planet is getting and the more damage is done to humans and wildlife alike . But the solutions are at our finger tips . Clean energy , such as wind and solar power , is often cheaper than generating energy from fossil fuels and creates lots of jobs . Here in the south west we have some of the best renewable resources in Europe , if not the world . <h> Read More @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ change begins <p> Every country in the world signed the Paris agreement , which aims to rapidly cut greenhouse gas emissions , particularly carbon dioxide to keep global warming below 2 degrees Celsius . Even Saudi Arabia , which relies so heavily on oil production for their wealth , agreed to this . <p> To stabilise temperatures , decisions over the next few years are crucial . We need to dramatically reduce carbon emissions pumped into the atmosphere if we are to stand any chance of protecting the planet for future generations . Wide ranging impacts are already affecting our lives and will continue to worsen for our children and grandchildren as the world heats up . Certain parts of the city , including Ashton , Bedminster , Stapleton , Eastville and St George , are particularly vulnerable to flooding , along with areas near the River Avon and harbour . Bristol City Council estimates that around 1,000 properties in the city are currently at risk from tidal surges caused by storms . <p> At the same time , water supply in the city is also under stress as the climate changes , bringing heatwaves and droughts . Bristol 's rich habitats include the limestone grassland of the Downs and Gorge and mudflats and saltmarshes of the Severn Estuary . These are home to all sorts of species from water voles and otters to skylarks , peregrine falcons and rare wildflowers . Climate change means that new species will come to thrive in the city , with potentially negative consequences , while other species rapidly decline . Migration patterns are changing , affecting winter bird populations on the Severn Estuary . Certain tree species have become more susceptible to disease and the sea level rise threatens estuary biodiversity and other low-lying habitats . <p> Farmers are already battling the extremes of recent weather <p> Concerns are also rising around food security . Farmers are already battling the extremes of recent weather and smaller harvests mean increased food prices . Only five per cent of produce sold in Bristol comes from local farms . Industrialised food production , which the city is dependent on , results in more greenhouse gases , loss of healthy soil and loss of biodiversity .
