<h> A budget for a sustainable future <p> Supporting frontline services which protect vulnerable children and adults is the priority within the Cabinet 's budget plans for 2020/21 . <p> The proposals were presented at a Cabinet meeting on Monday 13 January 2020 , marking the start of a 28 day public consultation . <p> The Government has not yet announced the level of funding for local authorities beyond 2020 . <p> This uncertainty means that this year 's Cabinet budget proposals cover only the spending and savings decisions required in 2020/21 for which funding levels are known . <p> The Council continues to face extreme budget pressures as the current level of demand for children 's and adults ' social care is unprecedented , particularly from those with complex needs . The Council will have to continue to take action to address these pressures and make use of any additional funding available to it . <p> The Cabinet will be required to make a ? 745,000 saving to ensure the budget is balanced by March 2021 . It is proposed that this will be achieved by : <p> reducing the Council contributions to the Council 's pension fund @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ one off savings relating to the West Yorkshire Combined Authority 's Transport Fund and <p> replacing the proposed Council funding for market town improvements with the Government funding announced for Elland , Halifax , Sowerby Bridge , Todmorden and Brighouse . <p> To help the Council maintain essential local services the Cabinet 's budget proposals include a 1.99% rise in Council Tax . <p> The Cabinet is also proposing to use the Social Care Precept to raise Council Tax bills by a further 2% so that the Council can continue to provide high quality care for frail and vulnerable people , and to help meet increasing demand as the population ages . <p> An additional ? 1 million has been allocated within the proposals to support children 's social care and an extra ? 2.5 million will be invested annually in adults ' social care to help these services remain sustainable in the future . <p> Leader of Calderdale Council , Cllr Tim Swift said : <p> " Although the Government has announced the end of austerity and the provisional Local Government Finance Settlement for 2020/21 confirms some additional funding @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ care precept to the budget , this only provides a fraction of the level of funding which we have lost since 2010 . <p> " Our priority must always be to protect those services which support the most vulnerable people in our communities . We will allocate additional funding to social care but at the same time we are transforming the way in which we provide services . We will also protect and sustain our prevention and early intervention services so that we do all that we can to reduce and manage demand . " <p> The Cabinet has highlighted four areas of focus once future funding is clear : <p> Addressing local poverty -- the Council will work with partners to tackle homelessness and support the poorest households in our communities . <p> Taking action on the climate emergency -- the Council has declared a climate emergency and its Climate Change Working Party is developing the Council 's response .
