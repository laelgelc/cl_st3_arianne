Fully electric propulsion after that could eliminate fossil fuels completely . <p> Even with the advent of electric airliners by mid-century , the huge cost and long lifespan of commercial jets means it could still take decades to wean fleets off fossil fuels . <p> A shorter-term solution might be replacing fossil fuels with " sustainable aviation fuels " such as biofuels made from plant matter . But in 2018 just 15 million litres of aviation biofuel were produced -- less than 0.1 per cent of total aviation fuel consumption . The problem is it costs significantly more than standard kerosene-based aviation fuel . Greater use depends on the price coming down , or the price of fossil fuels going up . <p> Research into biofuels made from algae and other plant matter could prove a viable alternative to fossil fuels . Right now , though , cost is a major hurdle to uptake . This is the basis of all market-based solutions to reduce carbon emissions . <p> One way is to impose a tax on carbon , the same way taxes are levied on alcohol and tobacco , to deter consumption as well as to raise revenue to pay the costs use imposes on society . <p> The key problem with this approach is a government must guess at the price needed to achieve the desired reduction in demand . How the tax revenue is spent is also crucial to public acceptance . <h> Trading and offsets <p> Greater outcome certainty is the reason many economists champion an emissions trading scheme ( also known as " cap and trade " ) .
