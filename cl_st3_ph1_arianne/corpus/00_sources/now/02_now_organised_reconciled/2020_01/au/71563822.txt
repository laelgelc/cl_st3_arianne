<p> Kochland tells the astonishing story of Charles and David Koch , known simply as the Koch brothers . Charles , who might be described as a modern-day robber baron , gets much of the focus . His outstanding entrepreneurial skills generated wealth beyond the dreams of avarice . But what sets him apart is not his commercial skills , but the unelected political power he wields through his political network and his " advocacy philanthropy " -- strategic donations designed to further his doctrinal libertarian philosophy . <p> Fred Koch , a founding member of the anti-communist John Birch Society , bequeathed to his four sons a substantial industrial enterprise , mainly in oil refining and oil pipelines . Charles , the most entrepreneurial of the brothers , expanded it into a wide-ranging conglomerate . <p> Out of this early business experience , he formed a powerful doctrine in which the unregulated free market was the core belief . As commodity markets developed in the 1980s , Charles took these insights beyond oil and gas into other newly deregulated commodity markets . <p> Deregulation and privatisation took off in America in the 1970s and gained extra momentum during Ronald Reagan 's presidency in the 1980s . But what has distinguished them from other successful entrepreneurs is their far-reaching political activism . <p> For many years , they retained a low public profile . This was intentional : " It is the spouting whale that gets harpooned " was a favourite maxim . Nevertheless , they were active in politics from the start , backing a self-serving libertarian agenda of low taxes and deregulation . <p> The narrow focus of this agenda gained little popular traction . Tactics changed , with political contributions directed towards " advocacy philanthropy " to influence the political structure and wider public debate . Political donations were aimed at replacing centrist Republicans in Congress with hard-right candidates . They funded libertarian think-tanks and encouraged university scholars to promote their agenda in public debate .
