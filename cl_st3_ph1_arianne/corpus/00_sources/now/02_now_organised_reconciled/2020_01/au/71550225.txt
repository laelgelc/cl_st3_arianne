<h> Opinion <h> Mallacoota : this is what climate change looks like <h> By Linden Ashcroft <p> There is a tradition among some residents and visitors to Mallacoota to ring in the new year with a swim in the lake . <p> Locals watch the skies after the worst of the fires in Mallacoota on New Year 's Eve.Credit:Rachel Mounsey <p> The midnight splash is special because instead of fireworks in the sky , they are all around you as shimmering blue bioluminescence . The microorganisms react to your movement , allowing you to swim through your own personal galaxy . It is the closest thing I 've ever seen to real magic . <p> In fact , Mallacoota is the closest I 've ever been to real paradise . My family 's small holiday house on Karbeethong Hill has resident lyrebirds , koalas , goannas and gliders . It is a haven filled with clear waters , loud birds and clear , bright stars . <p> But instead of seeing those stars and welcoming in 2020 by swimming with ephemeral blue , locals and tourists farewelled the decade prepared to tread water to survive below a raging sky @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ climate scientist , I am trained to assess the latest research and objectively analyse data and facts . <p> But as someone who just spent the past three days fleeing fires that have mauled my favourite place on Earth , I confess it is it hard to do anything but feel . <p> I feel heartbroken for those who have lost houses , futures and family members . <p> I feel inspired by the compassion and leadership shown by people across the country in times of crisis but furious that we can not seem to replicate this on the global stage . <p> And I feel afraid of what the next few months , years and decades will hold . <p> The climate science of what 's happening this summer involves many components . Cool waters off the coast of Indonesia have meant less moisture available for our weather patterns , on top of the drought already strangling eastern Australia . Unusually strong westerly winds around Antarctica in the past month have exacerbated this drying by shifting weather patterns and bringing enhanced bushfire risk . It 's hard to identify the fingerprints of climate change in fuel loads , for example , or ignition sources . <p> But the big picture is crystal clear : this is what climate change looks like . The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change concluded with virtual certainty that our bushfires will become more intense and more frequent in the future . And by future , they were n't referring to a far distant problem . They meant 2020 to 2070 . <p> The future is here , and it 's hard to breathe . <p> As we move into the new decade , I find myself scared of our country and how much damage our wide brown land can inflict on communities .
