<h> The stage show that 's a lament for the planet and a spark of hope <p> The timing is terrible , but perfect for the world premiere in Melbourne of The Planet -- A Lament , showing in mid-February at the Asia TOPA festival . <p> It comes at the tail end of an Australian summer marked by drought and bushfire catastrophe , a summer in which smoke haze put Melbourne and Canberra at the top of the rankings for the worst air quality in the world ; a summer when Australia came face to face with the impact of climate change as the world watched in horror . <p> " I do n't think any of us could have foreseen what it meant to premier a work like this in Melbourne now , " says Planet producer Jala Adolphus . " To lament the planet , to mourn the state of the planet ; I could n't imagine what else would be appropriate . " <p> " The Planet -- A Lament " draws on music and dance from the east Indonesian provinces of Papua and East Nusa Tenggara . <p> The production , bringing together Melanesian @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ drawn from the east Indonesian provinces of Papua and East Nusa Tenggara , offers a rare opportunity to hear elements of the lament tradition sung by a choir on an international stage . The story of a a human survivor struggling to preserve the promise of future life , it 's a deceptively simple show that has taken seven years to come together . <p> Director Garin Nugroho was inspired to create Planet by an Easter procession in a football stadium in Larantuka , in East Nusa Tenggara -- a living theatre with a young choir , as he recalls it . The award-wining Garin is famous across Indonesia as a master mixer of forms , and Planet features film , song and dance drawn from the director 's travels through the Indonesian archipelago , including to Aceh after the 2004 tsunami . <p> Garin knew he wanted to showcase the lament tradition , which uses pentatonic and tetratonic scales and repetition to convey feelings of sadness , loss and hope -- just as the blues uses particular chord progressions and rhythms to convey lost love . She grew up in a Maluka family that settled in Papua and for whom singing lament compositions was part of life . It stayed part of hers as she became a musician and scholar focused on the southern Papua lament tradition . She is one of the few capable of transferring a lament song to the confines of sheet music so it can be performed by a choir . <p> So it is fitting that Septina opens Planet with her song and is surrounded at various points in the 70-minute production by the 16-voice choir for whom she arranged and composed the music . <p> Advertisement <p> This is a church group that Garin discovered in the East Nusa Tenggara city of Kupang .
