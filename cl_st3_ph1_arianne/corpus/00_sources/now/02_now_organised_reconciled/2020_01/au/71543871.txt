<h> BoM : 2019 was the hottest and driest year we 've ever endured <p> The data is in and 2019 has topped the Bureau of Meteorology 's ( BOM ) charts for average and maximum temperatures as well as the lowest annual rainfall across the country . <p> It will come as no surprise to those suffering through this horror fire and heatwave season that the conditions leading up to it were the worst on record . <p> The new figures arrived as large parts of the country brace for more dangerous fire conditions over the next few days . <p> BOM will comment when its official report comes out next week , but the numbers speak for themselves . <p> Australia 's annual mean temperature was 1.52 degrees Celsius above the 1961-90 average of 21.8C -- well above the previous hottest year ( 2013 ) at 1.33C . <p> Also , the daytime temperature record was smashed , with the average maximum temperature of 30.69C coming in 2.09C above the 1961-90 average . <p> The previous record , set in 2013 , was 30.19C . Image : NASA <p> The record-breaking year was especially noteworthy because it occurred without an El Nino event , the climate driver most commonly associated with hot dry conditions in Australia . <p> While the Pacific Ocean remained neutral , the main climate driver was the incredibly strong positive Indian Ocean Dipole ( IOD ) . <p> The IOD has been working to generate rising air and rain over eastern Africa but hot descending air over Australia . <p> It is associated with drought and hot conditions over eastern Australia as well as with a delayed monsoon , all of which have been the case this summer . <p> Late last year , an unusual sudden stratospheric warming ( SSW ) event also added to the hot dry conditions by shifting the westerly winds , which usually lurk over the Southern Ocean , up onto the continent . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the Southern Annular Mode ( SAM ) , again encouraging hot dry conditions . <p> And this is all on top of the anthropogenic global warming trend ; BOM records show Australia has warmed by just over a degree since 1910 , with most of the warming occurring since 1950 . <h> What now ? <p> Thankfully the positive IOD and negative SAM have decayed and the BOM 's outlook for the next three months is decidedly more neutral for rainfall , although temperatures are likely to remain above average for most of the country . <p> It is finally beginning to look like more tropical weather is returning to our north , with the chance of a cyclone developing early next week and tropical rainfall expected to move into inland Western Australia over the next few days . <p> But for those in the south , while a return to neutral conditions is better than drier than average , average conditions over summer are still hot and dry . <p> Until there is widespread rainfall , fire and heatwave conditions will continue .
