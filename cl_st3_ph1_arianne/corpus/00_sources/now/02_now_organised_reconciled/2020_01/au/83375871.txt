In some regions it can disappear within a decade . <p> And that can alter the local cost-benefit mix of tree-planting programs , says plant scientist Laura Bentley , first author of a paper in the journal Global Change Biology . <p> While it is well known that planting trees reduces the amount of water flowing into nearby rivers , until now there has been no understanding of whether and how this changes as forests age , Bentley says . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the world where forests have been established , using river flow as a measure of water availability in the region . <p> They found that flow had reduced by an average of 25% within five years of planting trees , and 40% after 25 years . In a few cases , it had dried up entirely . The biggest percentage reductions in water availability were in areas of Australia and South Africa . <p> Land type was found to be a factor . Trees planted on natural grassland in healthy soil decrease river flow significantly , but if land has been degraded by agriculture , establishing forest helps repair the soil so it can hold more water , and river flow decreases by a lesser amount . <p> Counterintuitively , the effect of trees on river flow is less in drier years than wetter ones . <p> When trees are drought-stressed they close the pores on their leaves to conserve water , and as a result draw up less water from the soil .
