Sean Davey/AAP <p> A key question facing us all after Australia 's unprecedented bushfires is how will we do reconstruction differently ? We need to ensure our rebuilding and recovery efforts make us safer , protect our environment and improve our ability to cope with future disasters . Australia could learn from the innovative approach India adopted in 2001 after the nation 's second-most-devastating earthquake . <p> The quake in Gujarat state killed 20,000 people , injured 300,000 and destroyed or damaged a million homes . My research has identified two elements that were particularly important for the recovery of the devastated communities . <p> First , India set up a recovery taskforce operating not just at a national level but at state , local and community levels . My research examined post-disaster reconstruction processes that influenced community recovery -- physical , social and economic . The findings from Gujarat 13 years after the quake were then compared with recovery processes seven years after the devastating 2008 Kosi River floods in the Indian state of Bihar . <p> Of my key findings , two are most relevant to Australia right now . <p> India 's government set up a special recovery taskforce within a week of the earthquake . The taskforce was established at federal , state , local and community level , either by nominating an existing institution ( such as the magistrate 's court ) or by establishing a new authority . <p> The Australian government has set up a National Bushfire Recovery Agency , committing A$2 billion to help people who lost their homes and businesses rebuild their communities .
