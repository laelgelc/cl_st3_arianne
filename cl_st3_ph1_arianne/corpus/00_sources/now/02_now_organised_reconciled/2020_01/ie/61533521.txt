Will he still be Taoiseach and FG leader after February 8 ? <p> With the phoney war finally over , and the General Election upon us , the election and its aftermath is now the only thing occupying the minds of politicians and political anoraks . However it is by no means the only thing that should interest us . <p> What then can we expect from , and what should we look out for , in 2020 the political year ? First , you probably have to go back to 1997 for the last occasion on which the outcome of a General Election - at least in terms of who would lead the next government - was so unpredictable . <p> With the two main parties seemingly evenly matched , and with an abundance of other parties and groupings in the field , there is no clear favourite , and if @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ it make take weeks to put together a government afterwards . <h> Sparring points <p> In 2011 , Insider remarked that FF was about as popular as the Black and Tans -- little did anyone expect FG to have its own problems in this regard ahead of this contest ! On a more serious note , some of the sparring is predicable . Expect FG to call into question FF 's record on economic management with many references to the economic crash of 10 years ago . Expect FF to retort that it has acted responsibly over the past four years in facilitating the Government , and to question why FG was willing to accept this support if FF was not to be trusted . <h> ' With both FF and FG ruling out coalition with SF , putting together a majority government will be a challenge ' <p> Expect SF to criticise both parties - FG for failing to tackle the key issues and FF for acquiescing with it . As Insider put it to one contender last week , the vote in provincial Ireland in particular will be split between ' Eff Eff , Eff Gee and Eff You !
