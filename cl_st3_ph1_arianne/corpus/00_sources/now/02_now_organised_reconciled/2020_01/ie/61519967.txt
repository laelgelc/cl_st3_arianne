<h> Scottish rocks reveal undiscovered ice age in ' boring billion ' era <p> The site in the northwest highlands where the important rocks were found . Image Prof Adrian Hartley <p> Scottish researchers have uncovered evidence of a previously unknown ice age during a supposed lull in the planet 's evolution . <p> Rocks unearthed from the north-west highlands of Scotland have caused quite a lot of excitement among scientists from the University of Aberdeen . Analysis of these rocks shows evidence of debris dropped from melting icebergs in lakes . <p> This is significant because the rocks have been dated to between 1,800m to 800m years ago , during a period dubbed the ' boring billion ' . This was coined by geologists to describe what was thought to be a relatively calm period in Earth 's evolution with little climatic upheaval . <p> However , this new discovery shows that this time may have included a previously undiscovered ice age . <p> " Throughout this so-called ' boring billion ' , the global climate was temperate and unchanged . This , in turn , deformed even older layers of sediment . <p> While similar studies have helped researchers reconstruct the planet 's glacial history , this goes much further back in time when Scotland was at about the same latitude as South Africa is today . <p> " It 's the first evidence globally for glaciation at this time in Earth 's history -- proving it was n't such a boring billion after all , " Hartley said . <p> This is not the first discovery in Scotland to significantly change the geological history books .
