<h> Square Cut : Corporate governance , like climate change , concerns all <p> Wednesday , Oct 30 <p> Corporate governance issues are not just boardroom issues . They have now made their way into the living room of ordinary folks . Recent developments have shown that blindly trusting one 's savings with those having fiduciary responsibility could lead to grief . It is time for everyone to be financially literate and ask questions because , like climate change , it involves all of us . <p> By Krishnadevan Vijayaraghavan <p> Protecting the unsuspecting from the unscrupulous is everyone 's responsibility . <p> Acts of negligence , by design or default , by those entrusted with fiduciary responsibility could end up negatively impacting your investments -- in debt or equity . <p> Take for instance the Employees ' Provident Fund Organisation that now invests over 15% of its incremental inflows in equities , which is estimated to be over 180 bln rupees annually . <p> This investment is mostly through exchange-traded funds based on the Sensex and the Nifty 50 indices . ICICI Bank , YES Bank , Sun Pharmaceutical Industries and Indiabulls Housing Finance , when it was part of the benchmark indices , are some of them . <p> The latest one being Infosys , which lost 530 bln rupees in market capitalisation in a day , after a whistleblower made public the contents of a letter addressed to the US Securities and Exchange Commission and a director in the company . <p> Let us not buy the argument , if proffered , that the weightage of some of these stocks in the indices is not significant enough and therefore , only a small percentage of total portfolio is at risk . <p> Also , do n't buy the argument that the EPFO is investing only 15% of incremental flows into equities and that core investments are not affected . Look at how it ended -- tattered reputation for the bank and decimated portfolio for investors . <p> Being watchful for departures from good corporate governance is everybody 's job , especially when your savings are at stake . <p> Or , take the case of Sun Pharmaceutical Industries . The company did not report <p> 58-bln-rupee worth of related party transactions till a whistleblower called it out . This resulted in the Securities and Exchange Board of India asking for a forensic audit of the company 's financial statements for three financial years to March 2018 . <p> Events such as these make it imperative for all stakeholders to keep a watch on the companies that their funds invest in .
