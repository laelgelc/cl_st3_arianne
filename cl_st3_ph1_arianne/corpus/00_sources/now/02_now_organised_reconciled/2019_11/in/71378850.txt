<h> ' With the rising issues of climate change , slum settlements in urban areas will be impacted the most ' <p> " There is a huge possibility to see the impact of climate change in the urban regions of South Asia by flooding , rise in vector borne diseases like malaria , dengue , water scarcity , heat stress and so on in the coming years . The most will be impacted are the slum settlements in urban areas , " said Bijal Brahmbhatt , Director , Mahila Housing Trust ( MHT ) , which is all set to host International Grassroots Conclave titled ' My Home , My City , My Voice : Women as Change makers ' on November 20 at the Sabarmati Riverfront Environment Center . <p> Brahmbhatt stated , " The doors and windows are made of glasses in buildings , too much use of tar on making roads , less number of trees , etc . The urban people have an access to the technology but the urban slum settlements does n't have a reach of it . Ever wondered how will they combat it ? Research is being done on how minimal use of energy takes place in affordable housing and the type of code needed for it . <p> Bijal said , " Villages have greenery but cities have more construction and so there is more heat . With these , they will use more energy and this will lead to carbon emission and ultimately rise in climate change . So , there is a need for the construction where less energy is consumed . Both government and experts have no answer for it . Research is on . " <p> For this , at least two instruments of electricity loggers have been installed in each house at three affordable housing schemes such as Laxminagar , Meghaninagar , Santadeep Co-operative Housing Society , Ambawadi and Kimadri Mill . The conclave will provide an inclusive platform to highlight the grassroots perspective on key habitat challenges , challenges to responsible urbanisation and service delivery challenges being faced across urban slums . <p> MHT with the overall objective of improving habitat conditions of poor women in the informal sector was established .
