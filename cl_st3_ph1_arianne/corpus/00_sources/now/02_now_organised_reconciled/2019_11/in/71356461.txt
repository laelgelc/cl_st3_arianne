<h> Salt-tolerant kewda gene to be used in rice <p> According to scientists , by 2050 nearly 50 per cent of cultivable land will be salinised due to climate change . Therefore , the recent finding by researchers from the botany department at Savitribai Phule Pune University ( SPPU ) and National Chemical Laboratory ( NCL ) , Pune , is good news for agriculture business . They have identified a plant species from the coastal region of Diveagar that has a high salt tolerance capacity . Their findings could be put to use to increase crop productivity in soil high on saline contents . <p> The species , Pandanus odorifer , commonly known as kewda ( screwpine ) , is a monocot variety that belongs to the family of pandanaceae and grows naturally along the coastal regions of Asia , Southeast Asia and Polynesia . <p> " Salinity is one of the major causes of huge loss of crop productivity arising mainly due to natural human activities and rampant use of chemical fertilisers , " said a professor at the department of botany , Altafhusain Nadaf . <p> Nadaf also said , " The fruit and flower-bearing plant @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that the species harbours salt-tolerant genes .
