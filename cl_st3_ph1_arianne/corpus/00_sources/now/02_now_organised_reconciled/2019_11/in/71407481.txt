<h> Last Updated : Monday 15 April 2019 <p> The Ministry of Environment , Forests and Climate Change ( MoEF&amp;CC ) has started gearing up for the upcoming Supreme Court ( SC ) hearing on the petition challenging the constitutional validity of Forest Rights Act ( FRA ) . <p> On March 7 , 2019 the Forest Survey of India ( FSI ) -- a body under the MoEF&amp;CC which conducts surveys and assesses forest resources in the country -- wrote to the Principal Chief Conservator of Forests ( PCCF ) of all states requesting for the geo-referenced location of " encroachments " . <p> Quoting the SC order of February 13 , the letter says that the apex court had given the direction to FSI to conduct satellite survey and place on record the encroachment positions . MoEF&amp;CC and the Ministry of Tribal Affairs ( MoTA ) are respondents in the case filed by wildlife conservation organisations and former forest department bureaucrats . <p> " It is requested to kindly provide the geo-referenced digital vector boundaries of the encroachment positions relevant to the above order in respect of your state to enable us to initiate action in the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , " states the letter sent by Subhash Ashutosh , Director General , FSI . <p> Geo-referencing is the process through which the latitudinal and longitudinal coordinates of an area are assigned . It can be done using satellite imagery and a geographic information system ( GIS ) . However , due to the poor implementation of FRA , the states are facing problems in undertaking this activity . <p> For example , there are no digital records of claims filed , rejections and appeals available with the state of Jharkhand . As a knee-jerk reaction to the SC order , the state is currently undertaking a massive exercise of digitising the documents , starting from 2008 when FRA was implemented . <p> The Odisha government , meanwhile , has told FSI that they do not have the capability to create geo-referenced boundaries .
