<h> Last Updated : Wednesday 13 November 2019 <p> Four years after the Paris Climate Change Agreement was signed , the global energy sector is yet to make significant headway in achieving its goals , a new study has revealed . <p> Researchers from the London School of Economics , Oxford University and the Transition Pathway Initiative found in a survey that just 13 of the 132 largest publicly listed coal , electricity , oil and gas companies have committed to net zero carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) emissions . <p> They surveyed management at 132 electricity , coal , oil and natural gas companies on the following : <p> Does the company acknowledge Article 2 of the Paris Agreement on climate change as the objective of international climate action ? <p> Does the company state its support for Article 2 of the Paris Agreement ? <p> Does the company explicitly acknowledge the need for net global CO2 emissions to reach zero ? <p> Does the company commit to a year ( for instance 2050 ) , or a temperature rise ( for instance 1.5 degrees Celsius ) , by which point its emissions should be net zero ? @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ net zero , or thereafter ? <p> Does the company explain what the offset mechanism will be and who will pay for it ? <p> Article 2 of the Paris Agreement commits countries to " holding the increase in the global average temperature to well below 2 ? Rapid decarbonisation implies coal use needs to drop much faster than oil and natural gas use .
