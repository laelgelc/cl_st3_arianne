<h> IEA projects rapid increase in installed solar capacity by 2040 <h> Last Updated : Tuesday 26 November 2019 <p> The International Energy Agency ( IEA ) has projected a rapid increase in installed capacity of solar power by 2040 , from 495 GW now to 3,142 GW , in its latest World Energy Outlook ( WEO ) . <p> This is a massive departure from its previous stance as the projected capacity addition trend now is almost the exact opposite of the projection made in 2018 . <p> The strong growth of solar has now been projected on account to optimistic policy changes around the world , including China 's partial reversal of a decision to reduce subsidies ; India 's announced target of 450 GW of renewable energy capacity by 2030 ; and strengthening of some renewables portfolio standards in the United States . <p> Despite turning bullish on solar , IEA remains conservative in its projections compared to other energy thinktanks such as the International Renewable Energy Agency ( IRENA ) which in its recently released ' Future of Solar Photovoltaic ' report projected solar growth to be over 6,000 GW by 2040 . <p> IEA @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development ( OECD ) . The WEO is its flagship publication . <p> In the 2019 edition , IEA projects a ' profound shift ' in global energy systems , with rapid increase in the installed solar capacity by 2040 , flattening of coal-based power generation and a decline in reliance of oil . <p> However , it expects ( CO2 ) emissions to continue rising for decades unless polices reflect greater ambition on climate change . This is primarily due to the longevity of the existing stock of coal-based plants , which account for 30 per cent of all energy-related emissions . <p> In the developing countries of Asia where the majority of the new plants are located , the average age of coal-based plants is just 12 years . <p> Mapped climate scenarios <p> The report models three policy scenarios . The first is the Current Policy scenario where the world continues to function according to the old regulations and the energy demand rises annually by 1.3 per cent by 2040 . <p> The second scenario is the Stated Policy Scenario , formerly @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ already announced by governments , where the energy demand rises by one per cent per year . <p> The third , more ambitious , Sustainable Development Scenario ( SDS ) scenario charts out a path aligning with the Paris agreement to maintain the global temperature well below two degrees Celsius ( ? C ) and pursuing efforts to limit it to 1.5 ? C. <p> However , the report does not define a pathway for the world 's temperature increase to remain below or at 1.5 ? C from the pre-industrial levels . <p> Additionally , the SDS comes quite close to the Stated Policies scenario , with the additional reliance on carbon sequestration beyond 2050 to overcome the overshoot . IEA points to the difficulty in the sustainable delivery of an extensive end-of-century deployment plan . <p> Importantly , IEA does n't see energy-related CO2 peaking by 2040 due to economic growth and population increases . <p> Change in energy mix <p> Under the Stated Policy scenario , solar surges ahead of coal and gas to become the largest source of installed power by 2035 , if stated policies @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is expected to decline marginally post-2020 , and remain around 2100 GW . <p> As for generation , the share of renewable generation could nearly double , from 26 per cent today to 44 per cent to 2040 , surpassing coal-based generation in 2026 . <p> IEA 's past projections <p> The IEA has long faced criticism for being overtly bullish with its prediction of coal and other fossil fuel dependence , and underpaying the importance being gained by solar PV . <p> For instance , in its WEO 2012 report , IEA had suggested that solar capacity will grow by 15 GW in 2015 . However , in reality , the solar capacity in 2015 was 52 GW . <p> Even in successive publications following the 2012 report , the projected amount for 2015 remained lower than actual .
