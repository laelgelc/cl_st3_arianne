<h> Global industrial emissions : Steeling through ? <h> Last Updated : Friday 15 November 2019 <p> Emissions from manufacturing industries and construction accounted for nearly 19 per cent of global carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) emissions from fuel combustion in 2016 . When emissions from power and heat are allocated to end-use sectors , its share rises to over 36 per cent , making it the largest such sectoral emitter . <p> Of all greenhouse gas ( GHG ) emissions as a whole ( excluding land use change and forestry ) , industrial processes and onsite energy emissions from industry accounted for over 20 per cent in 2014 ; a share which has worryingly increased from 16 per cent in 2000 . <p> This continued high growth of industrial emissions comes even as major historic climate polluters have decreased their industrial emissions . <p> Even the United States ( US ) reduced industrial GHG emissions by 12 per cent between 1990 and 2017 though , at over 7 per cent it still accounted for a share higher than its share of global population . <p> But the reduction in emissions has been largely due to the so-called deindustrialisation @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ economies in the past few decades , rather than sustained progress towards decarbonisation of processes .
