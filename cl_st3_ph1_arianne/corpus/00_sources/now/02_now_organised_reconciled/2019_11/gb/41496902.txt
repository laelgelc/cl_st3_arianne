<p> Multiple areas of Northern Ireland are already in crisis or heading towards breakdown , with urgent action required -- yet with no devolved government for more than 1,000 days . <p> That is the message from a report to be published today by a new non-partisan Northern Ireland think tank which has examined the state of public services , the economy and society . <p> The report overwhelmingly draws on material which is already known and it is not an in-depth examination of each of the issues . <p> However , it presents a grim picture of a society in which massive decisions are in many cases not even being discussed , still less being taken , because there are neither devolved nor direct rule government ministers to take them . <p> It warned that Northern Ireland is " unprepared for a fast-changing world and faces serious problems unless there are big changes in policy " . <p> Among a series of areas of concern , the report by the think tank Pivotal sets out how : <p> * The mental health system is " creaking " ; <p> * A rapidly ageing population where the number of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 106% but the total population grows by less than 6% between 2018 and 2043 , bringing knock-on impacts on public services ; <p> * Of the just over 1,000 schools in Northern Ireland , this year 451 of them went over budget , with a total funding shortfall of ? 62.6m ; <p> * A decade after the UK Climate Change Act , Northern Ireland has set no emissions targets -- unlike Scotland and Wales -- and enacted no legislation , with far poorer performance in protecting the environment than in the rest of the UK . <p> Nine months ago , the head of the Northern Ireland Civil Service , David Sterling , spoke of the " slow decay and stagnation " in public services in the absence of ministers . <p> Mr Sterling , who is now the most senior government decision-maker but is democratically unaccountable , said he was concerned that the absence of ministers could " become the new normal " .
