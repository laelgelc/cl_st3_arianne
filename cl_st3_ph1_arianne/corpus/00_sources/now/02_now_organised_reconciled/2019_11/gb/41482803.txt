<p> British pensioners are increasingly at risk from the growing threat of heatwaves as the climate changes , health experts have warned . <p> And children are particularly at risk from air pollution , mostly caused by transport and burning coal , which stunts their lung development and has lifelong impacts on physical and mental health . <p> But taking action to tackle climate change is " sensible " to deliver public health and economic benefits and relieve pressure on the NHS . <p> The 2019 Lancet Countdown On Health And Climate Change report from 35 global institutions examines 41 indicators up to the present day across areas including the impacts of rising temperatures , resilience , and economics . <p> It finds climate change is already damaging the health of the world 's children , and threatens them throughout their lives unless action is taken to limit temperature rises to well below 2C in line with global commitments . <p> Professor Elizabeth Robinson , from the University of Reading , said there was a " clear upward trend " in the number of people exposed to heatwaves . <p> And Europe was the most vulnerable out of all @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in cities , the high temperatures in 2019 , putting older people who are increasingly suffering ill health at a growing risk . <p> Air pollution is also a major health problem to the UK , with fine particulate air pollution contributing to over 20,500 premature deaths in 2016 , and coal linked to 60 deaths a week , the study said . <p> People on an Essex beach during a heatwave last year ( Nick Ansell/PA ) <p> The UK faces increasing threats to crop production , and the spread of infectious diseases as temperatures rise -- with the presence of tick-borne encephalitis virus confirmed in the UK for the first time . <p> The report also warns of rising exposure to threats such as wildfires globally . <p> To illustrate the future health risks and opportunities , the report sets out two pathways for the health of a child born @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ today could live into the 2090s , potentially experiencing a world with temperatures 4C above pre-industrial levels , and affected by increasing infectious diseases , malnutrition and ongoing air pollution . <p> But in a world that takes steps to keep temperature rises to 2C , a child born today would see the phase-out of all coal in the UK by their sixth birthday and see the UK reach net-zero emissions by the time they are 31 . <p> Dr Nicholas Watts , executive director , Lancet Countdown , said : " When you look at the technology available , the economics of the issue , the finance available , the technical questions , you realise that ( depending on ) which of these two pathways we pick , climate change is an enormous threat to public health or the response to climate change is the greatest public health opportunity we have in front of us . <p> " Which one of those pathways we pick is entirely a political question .
