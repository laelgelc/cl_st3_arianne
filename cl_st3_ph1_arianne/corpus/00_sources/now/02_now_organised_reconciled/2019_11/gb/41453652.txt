<h> Study explores Scottish landmarks to be affected by rising sea levels <p> A report published using data from a number of climate change studies has revealed certain Scottish landmarks may be affected by rising sea levels . <p> Using information from Climate Central 's Surging Seas Map , renewable information portal GreenMatch has created an interactive map detailing sites which may be endangered by sea level rise by the year 2050 and 2100 . <p> These include Fife 's Forth Road Bridge , and the UK 's tallest mountain , Ben Nevis , located in the Scottish Highlands . <p> The report states that by 2050 , Ben Nevis 's peak may not be directly affected , but its surrounding habitat will be affected by rising seas . <p> It says milder winters and decreased snowfall will likely affect the surrounding flora and habitat , stating the sea level rise in the Highland area is at a greater rate than the rebound since the last ice age . <p> By 2100 , it claims there will be an even higher risk for other habitats near to the mountain . <p> The Forth Road Bridge , labelled the sixth @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ also be affected , claims the report . <p> It says the area in which the bridge sits will be affected by the process of coastal erosion and flooding , meaning the Scottish landmark will be at risk by 2050 . <p> The report details 19 other sites across the UK which will be affected by climate change , with coastal cities and areas of flat , low altitudes most likely to be affected . <p> These include London 's Tower Bridge , the Palace of Westminster , Brighton Palace Pier , Blackpool Tower and the White Cliffs of Dover .
