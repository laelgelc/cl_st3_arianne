<h> FIS Looking for New Boss <p> ( ATR ) Gian Franco Kasper will step down as president of the International Ski Federation ( FIS ) next year , ending 45 years with the organization . <p> Gian Franco Kasper has been FIS President since 1998 ( ATR ) <p> Kasper , who is 75 , received a standing ovation at the FIS Council meeting in Germany over the weekend when he announced that he will officially leave office after presiding over the 52nd FIS Congress in Pattaya , Thailand , from May 17-23 , 2020 . <p> He stated that making his decision public now will allow National Ski Associations plenty of time to prepare any applications for candidacies . <p> His successor will be elected during the Congress and will become only the fifth president of FIS since the organization was founded in 1924 . <p> Sarah Lewis , the current FIS secretary general , would appear to be an obvious candidate to take over . The Briton has been in her current position since 2000 and began working for FIS in 1994 . She will turn 55 later this week . <p> Kasper served as secretary @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to replace fellow Swiss Marc Hodler . <p> The outspoken Kasper was granted IOC Honorary Member status in 2018 after completing his IOC term due to age limit statutes in the Olympic Charter . He had become an IOC member in 2000 . <p> In February , the IOC disavowed comments made by Kasper in an interview with a Swiss newspaper . Kasper spoke of " so-called climate change " , the gigantism of the Winter Olympics , and how administering the Games is " easier for us in dictatorships " . <p> Kasper apologized for the comments , saying they " were not meant to be taken literally but this was not clear in the final story " . <p> He was chosen in June to represent IFs on a new IOC commission formed to handle future Winter Olympic and Paralympic bids . The commission will first consider candidates for the 2024 Youth Olympic Games before turning its attention to the 2030 Winter Games .
