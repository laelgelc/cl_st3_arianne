<h> Three ways to build a more sustainable IT model <p> Reports suggest 8-million data centres are active today , and we 'll see billions of devices connected in the very near future . <p> By Brendan McAravey , regional director : sub-Saharan Africa Citrix <p> Further , when factoring in the millions of people who travel to and from their workplaces every day , in the tech industry and beyond , it 's fair to say the growth of digital technology is having an unprecedented impact on the environment . <p> Those of us in the technology industry must be prepared to help address this issue . Climate change is already causing a rise in global temperature . <p> With no time to lose , the technology industry must increasingly prioritise thinking about how we can reduce carbon emissions , improve sustainability and embrace greener practices . <p> There are three initial steps that organisations can take to begin building a more sustainable IT ecosystem . <p> Transition from On-Premises to Hyperscale Hosted Cloud Environments <p> Organisations should review their existing infrastructure and evaluate their efficiency . Many will realise they can cut their impact on the environment @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ data centres and migrating to hyperscale hosted cloud services . <p> As exponential demand for technological innovation continues , hyperscale service providers are increasingly turning to renewable energy to reduce their greenhouse gas emissions and create a sustainable approach to data centre computing . <p> As an example , Google achieved its 100% renewable energy pledge in 2017 , acquiring more clean energy than it had consumed that year . To achieve this , Google used power purchase agreements ( PPAs ) to buy and distribute renewable energy that sufficiently covered consumption of location-based electricity . <p> Alongside such " offsetting " , hyperscale hosted cloud services are typically more efficient than traditional on-premises data-centres -- as well as being powered by more sustainable energy than the national grid . <p> Ditch the legacy end-user devices <p> Many older end-user devices ( traditional laptops , for example ) use a significantly higher amount of computing power than necessary to perform everyday tasks . <p> While this might seem like a small issue , when you consider the number of knowledge workers using legacy laptops and docking stations for 40-50 hours @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ amount of unnecessary kW hours consumed . <p> Adopting a thin-client approach -- in which the computing power is performed centrally in the cloud , such as with Chromebooks -- reduces reliance on the device for performance , enabling a more efficient and less energy-intensive way of working . <p> Embrace a flexible working culture <p> Making this change will likely have the most far-reaching consequences , in terms of an altered working culture , more efficient and happier teams , and a reduced negative impact on the environment . <p> It 's no secret that the roads are getting busier than ever , with South Africans spending countless hours commuting to the office and back each day . All of this time adds to our overall emissions , as well as potentially reducing our productivity . <p> Adopting a culture where your team can work anywhere , anytime reduces this strain on the environment and also removes the onus of lengthy commutes each day . <p> We firmly believe that work is something you do , not somewhere you go .
