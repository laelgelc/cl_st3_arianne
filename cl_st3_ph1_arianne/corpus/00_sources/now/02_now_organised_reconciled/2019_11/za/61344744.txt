<h> Colds and flu ; never the twain shall meet -- expert advice <p> OK , time to throw away all that Googled and/or anecdotal stuff upon which we base so many of our unhelpful responses when we or our loved ones come down with a cold or the flu . Here 's top advice from three clinical experts , ( an epidemiologist , and two respiratory specialists ) , on what to do -- and the difference between the two . They go to some lengths to dispel six common and widely circulating myths , some as ubiquitous and seasonal in their manifestation as the flu virus itself . With climate change resulting in unexpected cold spells , flu is no longer confined to winter . It 's not ' just a bad cold ' ; a glance at the symptoms below puts that misconception to bed . A cold is generally signalled by a runny nose or congestion and does n't result in serious complications . -- Chris Bateman <p> Whenever there 's an outbreak of flu -- usually over the winter months -- various myths begin to circulate , some as contagious as the flu itself . These range from confusion about what flu actually is , to speculation about how it 's transmitted . <p> Flu is a contagious respiratory tract illness caused by the influenza virus . There are three different strains that can cause seasonal flu in humans . They are called influenza A(H1N1) , influenza A(H3N2) and influenza B. Here are six common myths which regularly do the rounds . <h> Flu is just a bad cold <p> False : Both flu and colds are respiratory illnesses , but they are caused by different viruses . Flu is generally worse than a cold . Flu presents with a sudden onset ( within a few hours ) of some or all of the following : fever , upper respiratory symptoms @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , tiredness , muscle pains , body aches , headaches , cold shivers and hot sweats . <p> A cold presents with a runny nose or congestion and generally does not result in serious complications . <p> Flu can lead to serious complications including pneumonia . Pneumonia is an infection of the lung which results from either the influenza virus alone ( viral pneumonia ) or from co-infection with a bacteria ( secondary bacterial pneumonia ) . Other possible serious complications triggered by flu can be heart , brain or muscle tissue inflammation and multi-organ failure . This happens when the flu virus in the lungs causes an inflammatory response throughout the body causing a number of organs to fail . This could include respiratory and kidney failure . <p> A serious flu infection may require admission to hospital especially in high-risk groups for severe influenza complications . These include pregnant women , people over 65 , children under 2 , people with underlying medical conditions such as kidney or lung disease and diabetes . <p> Most people will recover from flu within three to seven days . <h> The @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ not contain live virus so you can not ' catch ' flu from the vaccine . The flu season coincides with the cold weather and so naturally people associate the two . <p> The influenza virus spreads mainly by droplets from infected people when they cough , sneeze or talk . You can also come in contact with the influenza virus through contaminated surfaces or objects ( for example , door handles ) and then touching your mouth , eyes or nose . <h> People can get swine flu <p> False : The term swine flu is sometimes used to incorrectly refer to the influenza A(H1N1) strain which emerged globally during the 2009 pandemic .
