<p> Boreal caribou have been declining for decades , but public awareness and government action does n't seem to change , according to Rachel Plotkin , a caribou expert with the David Suzuki Foundation . <p> Now , the foundation has created an interactive map that visualizes the impact of human activity on caribou habitat . They spent three months building the map using data from the federal and provincial governments , as well as non-profits like the Canadian Wildlife Federation and Global Forest Watch Canada . <p> There are 34,000 boreal caribou that continue to live across the country . The World Wildlife Foundation estimates their population has declined 30 per cent over the past two decades . <p> The map , launched on Tuesday , shows a correlation of weaker populations and more degraded forests with higher levels of oil-and-gas activity around the northern border between British Columbia and Alberta . In 2016 , there were 728 boreal caribou across five herds in northeastern B.C. <p> Plotkin said experts hold the data necessary to enforce solutions to save caribou , such as setting limits on logging and investing in reforestation .
