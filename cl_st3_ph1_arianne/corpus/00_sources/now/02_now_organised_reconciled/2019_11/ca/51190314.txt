<p> A new report from one of the globe 's most prestigious medical journals says Canada 's health-care sector produces the third-highest per capita greenhouse gas emissions in the world . <p> The Lancet Countdown on Health and Climate Change 's annual report for 2019 , published Wednesday , claims that the global health-care sector is responsible for approximately 4.6 per cent of total emissions , with emissions from the U.S. " substantially higher than those of any other country and have risen steadily " over the study period of 2007 to 2016 , driving up some 19 per cent . <p> According to the study , in 2016 , the U.S. health-care sector produced nearly 1,800 kilograms of CO2 equivalent per capita , with the next biggest producer , Japan , contributing around 1,000 kilograms per capita , though this represents a 37 per cent increase from 2007 . <p> Canada 's health-care sector was the next biggest emission producer , contributing some 900 kilograms or so per capita of CO2 equivalent , representing a far more modest rise over the study period compared to its Japanese and American counterparts . <p> Dr. Jean Zigby , a member @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Physicians for the Environment ( CAPE ) , told iPolitics on Thursday that the major issue that needs to be addressed to reduce the sector 's carbon footprint is " purchasing itself , " arguing that with the health-care field representing some 10 per cent of Canada 's economy , it has an " enormous opportunity to give the market a signal " to lower emissions . <p> Dr. Zigby , a family physician in Montreal , called on governments in Canada to " give a signal " to administrators , hospitals and health-care centres that reducing emissions is a priority , urging them to set " quantitative and robust " directives , such as devising new environmental criteria to assess when selecting vendors . <p> " Right now governments are telling health-care ( providers ) just go for what 's most economically efficient , " he said in a phone interview . <p> " Administrators and health-care workers are ready for it , they want it . There 's just no signal from above that it 's important . " <p> The Lancet Countdown on Health and Climate Change @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the strategic and financial support from the Wellcome Trust . It was created to hold world leaders to account as they pursue the emissions reductions they pledged to embark on as part of the Paris Agreement . Each report , a new set of indicators are studied . <p> Collectively , the report suggests that if the global health-care sector was a country , it would be the fifth largest emitter on the planet . Perhaps unsurprisingly , 71 per cent of the health-care sector 's global climate footprint is attributed to its supply chain , including the production , packaging , transportation , and disposal of goods and services . <p> Transportation , in particular , was identified in the report as a major driver of Canada 's emissions , while conversely , sustainable transit was identified as the key to lowering the country 's carbon footprint . Around one quarter of Canada 's greenhouse gas emissions originate in the transportation sector , with a large majority attributable to use of personal vehicles .
