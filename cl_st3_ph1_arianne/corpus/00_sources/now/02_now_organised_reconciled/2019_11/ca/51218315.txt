<p> A North Atlantic right whale is entangled in fishing gear in the Atlantic Ocean . - Center for Coastal Studies <p> CLIVE DOUCET <p> Global warming is bringing all kinds of changes to the Atlantic Ocean , the atmosphere and the land -- everything from the bogging of abandoned farms and disappearance of coldwater species in the Gulf of St. Lawrence to severe coastal erosion and hurricane-force storms driven up from the overheated Caribbean seas . <p> All of these are pressing concerns , but the most immediate is the death of the right whales that have moved north chasing food as the ocean heats up in the Gulf of St. Lawrence . <p> It is the most pressing because every time a right whale is reported dead from crab trap entanglement , as recently occurred with the whale nicknamed Snake Eyes , it brings us closer to an American boycott of Nova Scotia crab . The whales ' population is down to 400 , and its perilous position has become an icon marking environmental degradation . The threatened boycott led by American environmentalists is very real . The timeline is two fishing seasons to eliminate right whale @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ said over and over again during the recent election , the Canadian government needs to get out ahead of this one . We ca n't wait until the boycott is unleashed , as Canada did with the Brigitte Bardot/Greenpeace-led boycott of seal products . Then it 's too late . Public opinion , once formed , is often impossible to rewind . <p> The European Union is no longer interested in seeing seal products for sale in Europe , and with that , the entire predatory hierarchy of the Gulf was thrown off kilter and remains so to this day . Human beings are predators . Remove the largest predator from an ecosystem and there will be an immediate problem , as was demonstrated here . <p> We risk the same kind of disaster with the crab fishery if the Canadian government does not take the American threat seriously . It needs to get a delegation together now , travel to Washington to hammer out a " share the seas " agreement with organizations like the Sierra Club . It should n't be hard . There are ways of fishing @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . We have already reduced the number of deaths from entanglement considerably , but we can do better . <p> A " share the seas " agreement does n't have to be complex . It does n't even have to be nation-to-nation , but it has to include all the major environmental organizations and the major crab fishery organizations . It 's about managing expectations as well as physical changes to cordages and weighting . <p> It 's clear from the Bardot/Greenpeace attack on the seal fishery that neither had the slightest idea of the havoc the destruction of that fishery would create for human communities , as well as the ecology of the Gulf . <p> A boycott of the crab fishery would be worse . Crab and lobster fisheries employ thousands of Atlantic Canadians in a well-paid , essential , renewable and sustainable industry . With the disappearance of so many once profitable species , shellfish have become the pillars of the entire Atlantic economy . Each spring , everyone waits with anticipation for " setting day . The only thing that is possible is to slow the rate of change , but we can devise a made-in-Atlantic Canada adaptive plan that includes a " share the seas " agreement that will give the right whales the space they need and fishermen access to the crab fishery they need . <p> It 's hard to think of anything more important for our newly elected MPs than beginning this process of creating an environmental adaptive plan for Atlantic Canada .
