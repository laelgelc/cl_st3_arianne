<p> Some in the Holland Marsh call it the " main drain . " To others , it 's the West Holland River . <p> But to Glenn Harrington , of consultants Harrington McAvan Ltd. , it 's a lake . <p> As Harrington explained at a public meeting held upstairs in Bradford 's old Community Centre this week , the entire Holland Marsh basin is a former extension of Lake Simcoe that , since the retreat of the glaciers , has filled in -- first with sediment , and then with organic material produced by an expanding swamp . <p> And as the soils subside -- a result of erosion , compaction , decomposition and oxidation - the drains must be cut deeper and deeper to maintain drainage , requiring more and more effort to pump the water from the low-lying area . <p> The Holland Marsh basin remains a ' sink ' , and the river , in its present form , lacks the power to either carve out a clear channel , maintain its flow , or flush out the sediment that builds up . <p> Harrington McAvan Ltd. has been retained by the Holland Marsh @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to " come up with an improved , if possible , management mechanism ... for the drainage scheme , " Harrington said . In particular , they are looking at the effectiveness of the main drain " and how to use it . " <p> Harrington noted the polders in the Holland Marsh were inspired by those in the Netherlands -- a country where high water levels and shallow soils have led to the abandonment of some fields , which are " now used for pasture . " <p> " Slowly but surely " the Holland Marsh is moving toward the same fate . " There is a time limit on how long these fields are going to be functioning . " <p> The study being undertaken has a number of objectives -- to maintain the ability of the drainage scheme to lower the water table in the fields ; to provide a reliable source of water for irrigation ; to manage soil movement and reduce erosion ; to minimize areas requiring dredging and therefore minimize costs ; and to adapt the system to the pressures of climate change , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and stronger winds . <p> In the audience were landowners and marsh farmers . They were asked to respond to a number of possible solutions , and to share their knowledge of the Holland Marsh . <p> Were they in support of vegetation on the dykes to stabilize the banks and prevent erosion ? Were they in support of windbreaks ? <p> Did they want soil materials dredged from the main drain or canals spread on their lands , as a valuable resource , or stockpiled elsewhere ? <p> What about the excavation of " sumps " in the marsh , at the end of every drain , to capture eroding soils ? What about re-introducing marshland , in certain locations along the main drain to improve water quality , and narrowing the channel to improve flow ? <p> The main drain " is no longer a river , it 's a lake -- and not a very good lake , " Harrington pointed out , suggesting that restoring some marshland and changing the dimensions of the channel could provide improvement . <p> " You guys have the on-site @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have to tell me . Although grower Brian Visser warned against the introduction of species , for bank stabilization or windbreaks , without a full understanding of potential problems , disease vectors , and hosts for insect pests . <p> " We do n't want to introduce the wrong plant material into the marsh , " he said . <p> Rather than traditional windbreaks -- almost impossible , when neighbouring fields may be only 18 inches ( 46 cm ) apart -- growers have created hilled rows at fixed intervals to increase wind resistance and reduce erosion . <p> Growers pointed out that improved farming practices -- including irrigation when the soils become too dry , and use of cover crops -- have actually reduced wind erosion events in the marsh , in part counteracting the impact of climate change @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ one key piece of the puzzle was the amount of water used by marsh farmers for irrigation purposes .
