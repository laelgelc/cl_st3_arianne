VENTURAS <p> Climate change could be good news for plants due to increased atmospheric carbon dioxide , which encourages photosynthesis . But it could also lead to higher temperatures and dried-out soil -- conditions that hinder plant growth . The degree to which climate change helps or hurts plants depends in part on whether forests can acclimate to shifting conditions , according to a study published Monday ( November 25 ) in PNAS . <p> Researchers led by ecologist John Sperry at the University of Utah created models of how groups of trees in 20 forested areas across the US would respond to climate change . They focused on regionally abundant types such as birch , spruce , aspen , poplar , and pine . In some of the simulations , the team enabled @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and tree growth with the constraints of reduced water and increased heat . In other scenarios , these characteristics remained the same , representing an inability to adapt to a shifting climate . In the simulations , forests that acclimated were still able to thrive , but only if there was enough of an increase in carbon dioxide to keep up with rising temperatures . <p> J.S.
