<h> Ottawa ' fixes ' the pipeline process -- and the lobbyists order champagne <p> It 's okay , I suppose , for a politician or a government to take a position against pipelines . It 's not a position I would take , but it would be consistent with a climate change policy framework calling for the development of green energy alternatives while keeping oil and gas reserves in the ground . The federal NDP has a policy along those lines . <p> The Liberals , meanwhile , claim to be in favour of pipelines -- at least those that can be built in an environmentally sustainable way . But the Trudeau government 's announcement last Wednesday of a new interim regulatory process for major energy projects leads me to believe that the government does n't want to see new pipelines built -- that what it truly wants is a pipeline moratorium . <p> Projects currently under National Energy Board consideration , such as Kinder Morgan Trans Mountain and Energy East , will now face the additional hurdle of having the Department of the Environment and Climate Change assess greenhouse gas emissions that would be the consequence of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ indigenous consultation ; right now , only groups with a direct interest in the project are granted intervenor status by the National Energy Board . The new process is being layered atop the existing NEB assessment process and will occur only after the regulator has completed its review . <p> Critics of the NEB -- and opponents of pipelines in general -- argue that climate change was never considered by the NEB . Pipeline proponents , alternatively , argue that the process is time-consuming , onerous and offers little in the way of certainty for investors . <p> A pipeline proponent is like a hamster on a wheel , running flat-out to get nowhere . The process is a mirage . The final decision rests with the federal cabinet , which will make a decision based on what it sees as the ' national interest ' without telling the rest of us what that means , or how the economic benefits balance off against the environmental risk . <p> I agree with both sides , actually . But rather than fix a regulatory process that seems to lack the support and confidence of both sides of the debate , the government chose instead to superimpose a new system on top of a process that was complicated enough already . <p> The result most certainly will be more layers of bureaucracy , more process . More assessments will need to be done , more reports will need to be filed . Paralysis by analysis . Expert consultants and lobbyists familiar with the process will be the only people to benefit . <p> I remember travelling to Inuvik on behalf of the Progressive Contractors Association of Canada a dozen years ago when the Mackenzie Valley Gas Pipeline was still on life-support . I was amazed at how the project had spawned an entire cottage industry of consultants , all studying aspects of the project and advocating one approach or another on how -- or whether -- the project should proceed . Many of them had no interest whatsoever in actually seeing the process come to a conclusion . Why would they ? As long as more and more hearings were being ordered , as long as the ' consultations ' continued , those involved kept getting paid . Once a pipeline project is either approved or rejected , an entire cadre of expert consultants has to pack up and find another gig . <p> A pipeline proponent is like a hamster on a wheel , running flat-out to get nowhere . After studying and debating the project for forty years , shale exploration elsewhere has softened demand for natural gas . Notwithstanding regulatory approval in 2010 and the federal cabinet signing off in 2011 , the Mackenzie Valley Gas Pipeline did not get built . In all likelihood , it never will be . <p> The process , in other words , is a mirage . The regulator does n't decide -- it only reports .
