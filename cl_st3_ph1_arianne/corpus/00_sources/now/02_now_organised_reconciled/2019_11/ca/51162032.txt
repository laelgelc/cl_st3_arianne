Call me heartless -- and no doubt plenty of people will -- but the photos of sad-looking cows that tend to be featured on ads for veganism usually just make me hungry . <p> Once I learned about the general cruelty of factory-farming , I limited my meat purchases to those farms which raise animals ethically ( I 'm not a total monster ) , but this only limited my meat consumption to the extent that " happy " food is usually also expensive food . <p> Although I know humans can live healthy lives without eating meat , the basic concept of eating animal flesh has always seemed like a reasonably natural part of human life @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ some scorn for vegan activist campaigns . I found it hard to respect the way they tended to lean heavily on juvenile sentimentality and blatantly false equivalencies . Sorry , but turkeys are not " just like us . " <p> I now realize that my focus on the " sad-cows " aspect of veganism allowed me to ignore the bigger problem . Animal products are one of the leading producers of greenhouse gas emissions , meaning that they are a leading contributor to the current climate crisis . This is now common knowledge -- or at least it should be . <p> Despite being a reasonably well-informed person , I managed to hide myself from this knowledge for many years . When I finally paid attention to the rumblings and read up on the data behind the meat industry and climate change , I realized that I had to do what I never thought I could bring myself to do : go vegetarian , and move toward vegan . I love meat so much -- not only the taste of it , but how it makes my body feel -- that I 'm not yet at the point where I have the strength of will to refuse it if it 's offered to me , even on a menu .
