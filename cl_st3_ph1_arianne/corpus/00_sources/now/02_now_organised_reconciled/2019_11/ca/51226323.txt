<h> Do we have enough scientists in Parliament ? <p> " Science must be better represented on the multidisciplinary team we elect to put forth policies to address the challenges we face today and tomorrow . Just as MPs with experiences in agriculture , trades , and other fields bring valuable perspectives to the House of Commons , so too do scientists . " <p> The 43rd Parliament , which convenes in Ottawa next week , shoulders the responsibility of addressing our biggest challenges , chief among them climate change . Grand challenges like these require the fullest suite of skills , expertise and knowledge we can offer . More urgently than ever , this includes science . <p> But when it comes to our federal parliamentarians , science appears to be lacking from the team . <p> Let 's look at the data . <p> Of the 338 Members of Parliament ( MPs ) , we were able to find discipline-specific post-secondary education information for 287 of them . We identified 21 MPs ( 7.4 per cent ) whose highest accreditation is in a science , technology , engineering or math ( STEM ) field , four of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ PhDs . An additional 14 MPs ( 4.9 per cent ) have some form of secondary qualification in a STEM field . In contrast , 48 MPs ( 17 per cent ) have business degrees and 59 ( 20.9 per cent ) have law degrees . <p> Among the general population , 18.6 per cent of Canadians aged 25-64 with post-secondary qualifications graduated from STEM fields . However , 22.4 per cent and 57.8 per cent of all master 's degrees and PhDs earned , respectively , were in STEM fields . Contrast this to four per cent ( of 100 ) and 15.4 per cent ( of 13 ) , respectively , found in Parliament . <p> Evidently , scientists are under-represented in elected office . So what ? Why do we need more scientists in Parliament ? Surely it is not reasonable to expect MPs to have specific expertise on every file they shepherd . After all , policy-makers regularly consult subject matter experts when needed . <p> Indeed , lowering the barriers between MPs and the scientific community is an ongoing endeavour that is tackled from multiple @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the Chief Science Advisor and the subsequent development of the network of departmental science advisers , to periodic gatherings of scientists and MPs on the hill like Science Meets Parliament and the Bacon and Eggheads series , to the recent Evidence for Democracy report on how MPs gather and use evidence . <p> But a scientist 's value-added is more than the technical service she can provide based on her area of expertise . It is about understanding the scientific method , the limitations of experimentation and the uncertainties in our knowledge . It is a way of critical analysis and problem solving . <p> The importance of having more scientist MPs is about growing and sustaining a culture of science , and increasing the diversity of intellectual approaches in Parliament . <p> So why do n't more scientists run for office and how do we change that ? The issue is complex but here we offer two observations . <p> It may be that scientists are difficult to recruit because the conventional trajectory of an academic career is not well-suited to public office . There are plenty of skilled professionals with advanced STEM training in all sectors of society . This is a much bigger talent pool from which to draw that is , by the way , much richer in diversity compared to STEM faculty members . This pool gets even deeper if we include those with master 's degrees . <p> On the flip side , scientists also have a visibility problem and typically do not enjoy name recognition , a serious disadvantage if running for office . On this , scientists could do with some reflection on why they are not as integrated in their communities as they could be . The narrative of elitists in ivory towers is probably ungenerous . Rather , scientists face a number of disincentives , including that activities like policy engagement are not valued in the existing academic system . <p> However , this in no way absolves scientists of civic engagement , in whatever form that may take . Indeed , many scientists are already politically engaged -- publicly and privately -- but an ingrained belief that " the science speaks for itself " persists , though there are signs of improvement . <p> To be sure , parachuting scientists into Parliament on its own is hardly a cure-all . Scientists also have much to learn about the complexities of policy . <p> But this much is clear : science must be better represented on the multidisciplinary team we elect to put forth policies to address the challenges we face today and tomorrow . Just as MPs with experiences in agriculture , trades and other fields bring valuable perspectives to the House of Commons , so too do scientists .
