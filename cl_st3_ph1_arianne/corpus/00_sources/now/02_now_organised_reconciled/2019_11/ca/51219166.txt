<p> Accelerating the Coal Phase-out : Nova Scotia and the Climate Emergency is a technical report by the Ecology Action Centre that lays out how Nova Scotia could reduce its total carbon emission to 69 per cent below 2005 levels by the end of the next decade . - Tim Krochak / File - Tim Krochak <p> A new report by the Ecology Action Centre is proposing changes that would drastically decrease the province 's carbon emissions by 2030 . <p> " When it comes to climate change , there is a critical difference between doing something and doing enough , " reads the report released Monday . <p> " Moving forward , we must have the courage to look toward deep , system-level changes regarding the ways we produce , transport and use energy in order to set and meet climate targets that keep global temperature rise to below 1.5 ( degrees ) C. " <p> The report proposes a sweeping overhaul that would see us transition from generating nearly half our electricity using coal , where we are now , to 90 per cent renewables by 2030 . It would mean adding 800 megawatts of wind power @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ solar generation . <p> The need for consistent supply , when the wind is n't blowing or the sun not shining , would be obtained by building a 200-megawatt electrical link to New Brunswick that would allow access to hydro power coming from Quebec to complement the recently completed Maritime Link bringing power generated in Labrador to Nova Scotia via an undersea cable . <p> As well , the report proposes the addition of two huge batteries with combined capacity of 480 megawatt hours of storage . <p> To offset the costs of higher electricity bills , the report also proposes a wide expansion of the Efficiency Nova Scotia program to do deep retrofits of more homes and businesses to reduce energy consumption . <p> That program would see buildings switch from electrical baseboard and oil for heating both air and water to more efficient heat pumps . <p> " An enormous part of this report is the energy efficiency programs we have as part of the pathway ; more than 80 per cent of houses and businesses get deep energy retrofits , " Stephen Thomas , energy co-ordinator for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Increasing efficiency to the point they are using less than half of what they were before the retrofit . " <p> Currently , about 25 per cent of Nova Scotia 's carbon emissions are generated by burning fossil fuels in personal vehicles and to heat buildings . <p> The report comes with a hefty estimated price tag of $200 million a year to make all the proposed changes . <p> " That 's less than 0.5 per cent of Nova Scotia 's GDP , so it 's certainly a cost , but we think it is doable considering the benefits , " Thomas said . <p> His hope is that the provincial government and Nova Scotia Power will take the report into consideration as they plan future changes to electrical generation .
