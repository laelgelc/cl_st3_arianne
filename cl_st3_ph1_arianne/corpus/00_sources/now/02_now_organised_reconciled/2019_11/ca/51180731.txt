COURTESY OF MOHAMMAD AKBARZADEH AND SOBHAN KOUHESTANI <p> Road management , the climate crisis , and machine learning are three things which may not seem connected , but they do to Dr. Madeh Piryonesi , a University of Toronto civil engineer who defended his PhD this year . <p> This June , one of his papers , co-authored by Professor Tamer El-Diraby at U of T 's Department of Civil &amp; Mineral Engineering , titled " A Machine-Learning Solution for Quantifying the Impact of Climate Change on Roads , " won the Moselhi Best Paper Award at the Canadian Society for Civil Engineering 's annual conference . Piryonesi created a model to predict how roads would deteriorate due to change in climate , and implemented it as an online tool that will be accessible to policymakers . <p> Piryonesi 's research has been funded by the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council and the Ontario Good Roads Association . <p> How roads @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 's model , users are treated to a visual interface where they can input a road 's name and see that road pop up on Google Maps . They can then enter the parameters for a future climate -- such as an increase in temperature and precipitation -- and see the projected future deterioration of the road . <p> The model can make predictions for roads in many locations , thanks to the wealth of data Piryonesi had access to . His machine-learning algorithms were trained on data provided by the Long-Term Pavement Performance program , which is managed by the US Federal Highway Administration . The program stores data -- including traffic and weather information -- on more than 2,500 road sections across Canada and the US , and dates back over 30 years . <p> " Using this very well-spread data kind of makes sense for climate change analysis , " said Piryonesi to The Varsity . <p> The model 's predictions depended strongly on location . He tested the tool on roads in both Texas and Ontario . In everyday life , decision trees -- a kind of flowchart -- let us visualize how outcomes or costs depend on sequences of events that take place . <p> In machine learning , decision tree algorithms are fed existing data , learn from it , and reverse-engineer a decision tree that predicts unknown data . To amplify the low accuracy of a single decision tree , Piryonesi 's model also uses ' bagging , ' a process in which hundreds or thousands of ' learners ' construct separate trees , and then hold a ' vote ' on the best one . <p> This approach can produce predictions that are not single numbers .
