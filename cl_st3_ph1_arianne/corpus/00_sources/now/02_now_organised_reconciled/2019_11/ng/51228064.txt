<h> ISIL is not dead , it just moved to Africa -- Rashid Abdallah <p> Illegal armed groups are opportunistic by nature . They usually start their operations and recruit followers in countries where there is poverty , corruption , religious conflict or ethnic strife , and where the security forces are unable to keep the public safe and illegal formations under control . <p> The rise of the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant ( ISIL , also known as ISIS ) in the Middle East was a textbook example of this trend . Since the occupation of Iraq by US forces in 2003 , the region has been stuck in a vicious cycle of conflict , sectarianism and regime change . It is in the shadows of this crumbling landscape that ISIL first began to emerge , nourished by the increasing frailty and incompetency of Arab states in revolt or at war . <p> Over the last few years , regional and global powers , aided by non-state actors , managed to eliminate ISIL from most of Iraq and Syria . The Islamic State in the Greater Sahara ( ISGS ) , the prodigy of ISIL there , is going from strength to strength , bolstering its membership and carrying out attacks . <p> There are several reasons for ISIL 's ongoing success in this vast region that runs west to east across the continent from Senegal to Sudan . <p> Most of the states that have territory in the Sahel are grappling with the destructive effects of climate change , poverty , food shortages , ethnic conflicts and lack of effective democratic governance . There is little opportunity for the people in the region to receive an education and find work that would allow them to sustain their families . All this creates an ample opportunity for terror groups like ISIL to expand their influence over the region . <p> Burkina Faso , for example , is on the verge of becoming " another Syria " , according to the UN 's food agency . <p> The landlocked West African country has long struggled with myriad political , economic and humanitarian problems , but terror was not a significant threat there until early 2015 . Since then , however , fighters linked to al-Qaeda and ISIL have taken advantage of the government 's inability to control vast areas in its north and east to infiltrate the country . <p> Combining hit-and-run tactics with road mines and suicide bombings , the insurgents have killed hundreds of people and managed to recruit local youths looking for a way out of unemployment , poverty and uncertainty . <p> The attacks forced almost half a million people from their homes and turned a third of the country into @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ abandon their fields and farms , agriculture collapsed , causing malnutrition to reach emergency levels . While no group claimed responsibility for the attack , analysts said they believe ISIL was responsible . <p> ISIL and al-Qaeda 's interest in Sahel 's goldmines has long been known . Mali , too , has long suffered insecurity which has allowed the country to become a playground for groups like ISIL and al-Qaeda . Earlier this month at least 53 soldiers and a civilian were killed in an ISIL attack on a military post in northeast Mali . The attack came a month after two similar attacks killed at least 40 soldiers near the country 's border with Burkina Faso . <p> Its parent may be dead , but ISGS is very much alive and well . The insecurity in the Sahel gives the group free rein to entrench itself in the region and work towards establishing a new " caliphate " there . <p> As world leaders pat themselves on the back for " destroying ISIL " in Syria , the group is openly building up its strength in Africa . The international community needs to take urgent action to stop ISGS in its tracks . <p> First and foremost , it must do everything it can to support the Force of the Group of Five for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , Chad , Mali , Mauritania and Niger in its efforts to combat armed groups , cross-border organised crime and human trafficking . Moreover , international organisations and states need to increase their efforts to provide the necessary aid and assistance to governments in the region . <p> If effective measures that address not only the ongoing insurgency but the core problems that allowed it to prosper in the region are not implemented right away , the destruction and suffering caused by ISIL in Syria and Iraq will be repeated in the Sahel . More and more people will try to escape their predicament by embarking on deadly journeys towards Europe . A few will make it there , while tens of thousands of others will either die horrible deaths at sea or languish in outrageous refugee camps in Africa . <p> ISIL 's short but brutal reign in the Middle East will long be remembered as one of the darkest hours of our recent history . We should do everything we can to stop it from happening again .
