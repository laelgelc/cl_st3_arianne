<h> Strategically Speaking : Union Investment <p> Although Union Investment is not an iconic name in German finance it has become a substantial force in asset management . It might not have the global recognition of Allianz or Deutsche but it is , without doubt , one of the largest German asset managers . <p> Of course , its exact ranking depends on the particular metrics used . According to IPE 's Top 400 Asset Managers 2016 , it was ranked 58th globally with ? 261bn in assets under management ( AUM ) at the end of 2015 . <p> According to the group 's own figures , it had AUM of ? 275bn at the end of June 2016 , of which about ? 160bn is managed on behalf of institutional clients ( as of 30 June 2016 ) . The remaining ? 115bn was accounted for by its approximately 4.5m German retail customers . <p> Its capabilities in terms of asset classes are extensive . DZ Bank , an umbrella organisation for the co-operative banks , is its main shareholder . It , in turn , represents over 1,000 co-operative banks in Germany . <p> One of the group 's main missions is to provide retail investment products for the customers of this network . Through the co-operative retail network , it serves literally millions of clients . Union 's retail funds are only available through the co-operative banks , although these institutions are also free to sell products from other asset managers . <p> Although Union Investment itself was founded in 1956 , the roots of the co-operative banking movement go back to the mid-nineteenth century . Back then , Hermann Schulze-Delitzsch and Friedrich Wilhelm Raiffeisen independently formed co-operative banks in the spirit of self-help , self-responsibility and self-management . Schulze 's emphasis was on helping small business owners in cities while Raiffeisen focused on the rural poor .
