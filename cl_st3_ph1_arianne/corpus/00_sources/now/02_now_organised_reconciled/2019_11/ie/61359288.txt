<p> The 550 finalists who will represent their school and community at the 2020 BT Young Scientist &amp; Technology Exhibition ( BTYSTE ) have been announced . Selected from over 1,800 entries , these projects represent 244 schools across 31 counties . As BT , the organiser of the event delivered the good news over the past week , students have already begun preparing to exhibit their projects and meet the judges at the 56th annual Exhibition on 8th January 2020 . <p> A significant number of the projects this year delve into pressing social issues such as mental health , transgender rights and the effects that social media has on young people . Topics such as these are prevalent in the Social &amp; Behavioural Science category but are also increasingly prevalent in the remaining three categories -- Technology , Chemical , Physical &amp; Mathematical Sciences and Biological &amp; Ecological Sciences . <p> Shay Walsh , Managing Director at BT Ireland said , " It is a trend in itself globally that it is our young people who are now leading the charge when it comes to taking action on issues such as climate change and mental health . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ when I see this same passion and concern coming from our own young people as evidenced in their project ideas .
