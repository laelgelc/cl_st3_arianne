<h> Vivaldi reworked to ' make climate change audible ' <p> A classical orchestra in Germany aims to " make climate change audible " with a reworking of Antonio Vivaldi 's famous violin concertos " The Four Seasons " using algorithms based on climate data . <p> The project reimagining one of the most widely recognised classical works is entitled " For Seasons " and will be performed in Hamburg on Saturday . <p> A collaboration between musicians , artists and software developers , the composition has been described by its creators as " disharmonic " and " uncomfortable " . <p> " The piece makes the facts of climate change obvious : the climate and our seasons have grown unbalanced , " the organisers said on the project website . <p> Using data relating to species extinctions , rises in global temperatures and extreme weather events , the composers claim to have developed algorithms which can " influence the notes on the original sheet " . <p> Changes to Vivaldi 's original score from 1725 include a blurring of the lines between spring and summer and the removal of notes which represent birdsong . <p> The new work @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> " Almost everybody in the room will recognise the original , but then quickly think : ' Hang on , this sounds different , somehow disturbing ' , " said Yuri Christiansen , the orchestra 's solo cellist . <p> Tickets for the performance at Hamburg 's modern , waterside concert hall will be free of charge , and the event will also be streamed by regional public broadcaster NDR.
