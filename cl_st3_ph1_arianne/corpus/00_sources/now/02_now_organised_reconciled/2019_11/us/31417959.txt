<p> Most of the cities in Michigan will be dealing with harsh consequences of climate change , and vulnerable groups who are disproportionately affected by it will continue to do so now and into the future , according to a new University of Michigan study . <p> U-M researchers looked at future temperature projections , tree canopy and hard surface data , as well as location of vulnerable populations , to create heat vulnerability maps for the state . <p> The maps -- available online as an interactive tool , The Mi-Environmental Project -- will help policymakers and community organizations focus their efforts on strategies that help cushion the blow of changing climate , said Trish Koman , research investigator at UM 's School of Public Health and first author of the study published in Health and Place . <p> " Climate change often feels very far away . Some people think it 's in the future and that it 's something happening to other people in other parts of the world , " she said .
