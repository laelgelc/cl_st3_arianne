<h> 2019 Might Just Be Earth 's Second-Hottest Year On Record <p> Climate change is real and we might be living through the planet 's second or third warmest calendar year on record . The data released by the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration ( NOAA ) highlights the growing influence of long-term global warming , reports the Washington Post . <p> As per the report , there 's an 85% chance of 2019 getting ranked as the second-warmest in NOAA 's data set and will , overall , become the top-five warmest year for the globe . October 2019 was found out to be the " 43rd-straight October " that 's warmer than the 20th-century average , and is also the 418th straight warmer-than-average month . NOAA also found that the average global land and ocean surface temperature for October was 1.76 degrees ( 0.98 degrees Celsius ) above the 20th-century average , which is 0.11 of a degree shy of the record warm October back in 2015 . <p> Furthermore , NOAA found out that record warm October temperatures existed across several parts of the North and Western Pacific Ocean , northeastern Canada , parts of the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ East , the Indian Ocean and South America . Western U.S. was the only region where rockies were found to be record cold for the month . <p> The report also adds that there 's been an increase in the amount of greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide in the atmosphere . Moreover , human activities like burning fossil fuels for energy are the main contributors of greenhouse gases . This buildup of greenhouse gases is further pumping extra heat in the atmosphere which is being absorbed by the oceans .
