Photo : Mark Mulligan , Houston Chronicle / Staff photographer <p> Many people think the story behind Accel Lifestyle starts with a stinky shirt . <p> The basis of Megan Eddings ' business was concocted after she could n't quite get the smelliness out of her husband 's workout apparel . But her interest in science started years before at the University of Virginia , when she was a biochemistry student , and continued after she graduated and worked as a laboratory assistant at Brown University analyzing proteins to study how they affect cancer cells . <p> That was a few lifetimes ago , the Rhode Island native explained , a version of herself that she did n't expect to find again . <p> Eddings had worked for years in medical equipment sales , a job that brought her to Houston about 15 years ago . She was active in social and volunteer circles and named one of the most eligible bachelorettes in Houston about a minute before meeting her now-husband Kyle . <p> " We both come from families where fitness is a big part @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ play sports -- baseball , soccer , volleyball ; our house would shake when we punch the punching bags . The older I got , I found fitness is more than the physical benefits , but the mental . " <p> Then came the stinky shirt conundrum . <p> " We got married four years ago , started living together , and I began washing his workout clothes -- those dry-fit wicking polyester shirts that everyone wears because it ' does n't hold onto sweat . ' Anyone who has worn them knows the same smell : disgusting , musty , mildew mixed with vinegar and B.O. " <p> According to Science Daily , a study involving T-shirts from 26 healthy individuals following an intensive , hourlong bicycle spinning session that were then incubated for 28 hours before being inspected by a " trained odor panel " concluded that polyester clothes smell worse than cotton following intensive exercise because bacteria that cause odor grow better on the material . <p> " Freshly secreted sweat has little odor , because the long-chain fatty acids the axillaries secrete are too big to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ break these , however , as well as hormones and sulfur compounds , down to waftable sized , odoriferous molecules . " <p> The first synthetic-knit fabric to have " superior wicking and moisture management properties " and " particularly well-suited for athletic wear " was patented in 2002 by inventors Robert Kasdan and Stanley Kornblum . <p> " It is an object of the invention to provide an improved fabric having superior wicking properties for improving evaporation of body moisture , " the patent materials state . " Still another object of the invention is to provide an improved and moisture management fabric prepared from two different types of polyester yarns knit by an irregular pique stitch . " <p> The moisture-wicking fabric was soon a mainstay of every athlete 's closet , including Eddings and her husband 's . But the revolutionary fabric did n't address the issue of stink . <p> So , she donned her figurative lab coat again and got to work . <p> " I started thinking about the science of why our clothes smell . I started adding scented pods ; I would @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ smell , " she said . " Specifically , the shirts bothered me . But nothing ever worked . " <p> Eddings asked her friends if they all had this same problem and realized that everyone had to deal with what she called " activated " clothing -- meaning a moisture-wicking shirt would have about 10-15 good wears before it was too smelly to work out in anymore and would need to be thrown out and replaced . <p> After researching anti-stink or odor-repellent fabric for about eight months , she had some luck with the stink factor , but the clothes felt synthetic and harsh on the skin . She wanted a cottonlike soft material that did n't smell or retain moisture , and she knew she had to create it herself . <p> Four years ago , Eddings left medical sales to pursue Accel Lifestyle full time . <p> " I wanted it to be empowering , inspiration and feeling of positivity , " she said . " It was originally Accelerate Health and Happiness , but I shortened it to Accel . It was important that the factory use safe , ethical and fair labor practices and operate with the human workers ' health and the environment in mind . <p> The combination of fabrics and knit pattern is currently awaiting a patent in 120 countries , but Eddings shared what makes her athletic wear special . It 's a combination of supima cotton , which gets softer the more times it is washed , and high-end silver-polyester threads . The goal was to find a configuration that felt luxurious and did n't have bacteria bleed into the material and cause an odor . <p> She used a knitter on the East Coast and the material was tested for sweatiness and smelliness at MicroStar Lab , an antimicrobial laboratory that is accredited by the American Association for Laboratory Accreditation and is equipped to test military products for the federal government . <p> " We kept having to change the percentage of each fiber and thread in the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ from the lab would take four months , and multiple times , the results came back not favorable , meaning that bacteria got into the fabric . " <p> In May 2018 , they found the winning fabric ( named Prema ) , and a year later they had a soft-launch for the men 's line . Currently , the company is sticking to blacks , whites and grays but plans to eventually incorporate more colors . <p> Again , colorful dye is scientific . <p> " There 's a lot of science to figure out how to dye fabric uniformly . For the black , the mill would dye the fabric , and it would have a stripe effect , " Eddings said . " For a startup , you have to order at least 2,500 yards of fabric in one color . The sizes are extra-small to extra-large . <p> Did Eddings think her life would be full of clothing pop-ups , calls with textile mills and smelling T-shirts for a living ? No , but she 's glad for it . <p> " I learned a lot along this journey about patience , resiliency , remaining positive and asking for help , " she said .
