<p> As California is living through the most dangerous time of the year for wildfires due to dry and windy conditions , experts say a trio of factors make America 's most populous state more at-risk than ever . <p> Despite several recent wildfires outside of San Francisco and Los Angeles , there have been no significantly deadly and destructive blazes so far in 2019 . Nonetheless , the long-term trend in the Golden State is toward bigger , faster-moving and more destructive wildfires , due to a combination of overgrown forests creating more fuel , climate change that causes higher temperatures and less snow , and housing construction in fire-prone areas . <p> " There 's no simple problem and no one simple answer , " said Max Moritz , statewide fire specialist based at the University of California , Santa Barbara . " It 's all of these things mixed together .
