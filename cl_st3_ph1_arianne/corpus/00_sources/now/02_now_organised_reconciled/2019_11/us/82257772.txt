Check out what 's clicking on Foxnews.com <p> Archaeologists have recovered from a cliff edge in Wales the centuries-old skeletal remains of at least six people , who may be the victims of a shipwreck . <p> The remains were recovered in July and September from a cemetery on an eroding coastal cliff at Cwm Nash in Glamorgan . Other remains recovered from the site have been dated to the era of the British Tudor and Stuart monarchs in the late 16th or early 17th centuries . <p> Cardiff University participated in the excavation with the Glamorgan-Gwent Archaeological Trust , Natural Resources Wales and climbing experts from The Adventure Alternative . <p> " Many of the skeletons have lost bones to the sea , but early analysis of the burial positions of the individuals has been revealing , " said Jacqui Mulville , professor of bioarchaeology at Cardiff University , in a statement . " There is a single younger individual , buried away from the rest , with others buried side by side or even together @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ these are Tudor or Stuart men who may have been the victims of shipwreck . We aim to tell more of their stories and return their identities to them through ongoing post-excavation analysis . " <p> The skeletons were found at Cwm Nash on the Welsh coast . ( Cardiff University ) <p> Cardiff University bioarchaeologists will be performing a detailed analysis of the newly discovered remains into next year , according to the statement . <p> Experts faced a race against time due to coastal erosion and climate change to recover the latest remains , according to Cardiff University . <p> In a separate project , archaeologists in Wales have been working to uncover the secrets of an ancient coastal hillfort before it falls into the sea . Perched on a slowly collapsing cliff edge , the mysterious fort at Dinas Dinlle is believed to date back to the Iron Age , which began 800 B.C. in Britain .
