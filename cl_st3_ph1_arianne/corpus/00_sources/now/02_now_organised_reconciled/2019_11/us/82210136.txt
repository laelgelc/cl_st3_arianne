( AP ) -- Republican Florida Gov . Ron DeSantis on Monday proposed a $91.4 billion state budget for the fiscal year that begins in July , a spending plan he says will help attract teachers to the state while getting rid of more pythons . <p> The proposal is about $400 million over the current Florida spending plan . One of the largest increases in the proposal is $600 million to raise the minimum salary for public school teachers to $47,500 . DeSantis said that means 101,000 teachers would see a pay increase . <p> It also includes a $1 million boost to hunt down and remove pythons that have overrun the Everglades . <p> " We are upping the investment to help eradicate the Burmese python out of the Everglades , " DeSantis said . Rick Scott , in pushing environmental issues . While Scott was a climate change skeptic , DeSantis has said it 's one of the low-lying coastal state 's top concerns . <p> DeSantis said he wants to spend $625 million a year for the next three years on environmental projects . <p> " That 's over $300 million for Everglades projects , $150 million for water quality improvements , $50 million to reduce nutrients in the water , $50 million for springs restoration , more than $22 million to combat the effects and impacts of algae and red tide , over $50 million for beach renourishment , $9 million to protect coral reefs , " DeSantis said . <p> DeSantis is also proposing creating 549 new state jobs , mostly for the Department of Corrections and to staff new nursing homes for veterans . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ per capita spending , " DeSantis said , noting the state 's population growth over the year . " <p> The proposal is simply a recommendation , and the state 's spending plan will largely be shaped by the Legislature after it begins its session Jan. 14 . Once lawmakers present him with a budget , DeSantis will be able to veto individual items . <p> Last year , DeSantis originally proposed a $91.3 billion spending plan . The Senate proposed a $90.3 billion spending plan and the House suggested $89.9 billion .
