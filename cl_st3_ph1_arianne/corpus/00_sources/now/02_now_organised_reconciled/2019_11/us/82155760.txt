Behind this fascination lies his sense of the fragility of our reality . History might always have turned out otherwise ; and all that we deem solid might melt into air -- or burn to ash . " All civilisations consider themselves invulnerable , " a rare document from our 21st century states in " The Second Sleep . " Mr. Harris knows , and shows , the folly of that belief . His 2003 novel " Pompeii " turned the volcanic terror that engulfed the Roman town into a warning of the fate that may consume even the wealthiest of cultures . In " The Second Sleep , " a Pompeii-like doomsday has wrecked a globalized , technology-based way of life in an " overwhelming , instantaneous and universal " blow . This calamity , it turns out , took place in the year 2025 -- according to our calendar . <p> A master of slow-burn revelation , Mr. Harris gradually discloses the nature of the disaster that has pushed England , and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , packhorse transport and theocratic law . Neither nuclear war nor extreme climate change -- the drivers of so many dystopian novels -- have triggered this downfall . Mr. Harris 's West of England , with its olive groves and local red wine ( rather than the white currently made there ) , feels only a shade warmer than today . In its subsistence economy , traditional crops and beasts still thrive . " The grass grows . The sheep feed . The loom turns . " The mighty Church with its " tribunals of inquisition " controls human minds and roots out heresy . Along with " sacred personage " of the King , it works vigilantly to see " the glory of Old England restored after the chaos of the Apocalypse . " <p> In this bucolic landscape , with the depleted population of England now 6 million rather than 60 , young Father Christopher Fairfax sets out on a mission across the hills and valleys of Devon . He had allied with the persecuted " Antiquaries " to research the times before the Apocalypse of ( our ) 2025 . History is now " a patchwork of voids " filled up by faith . To show any interest in the past is to take " a step on the path to Hell . " The Church , with its dogmatic " rejection of scientism , " treats all such curiosity as grievous heresy . Most ordinary folk slumber in a " second sleep " of ignorance and obedience . The breakdown , panic and slaughter of the post-Apocalypse period lie far in the past . Mr. Harris 's portrait of a narrow , becalmed social order has more in common with the vision of , say , Margaret Atwood 's " The Handmaid 's Tale " than the more florid dystopian imagination of Cormac McCarthy 's " The Road . Mr. Harris notes the stubborn endurance of plastic artifacts -- garage signs , shredded bags , polystyrene packaging -- while the reinforced concrete of multi-story buildings has crumbled to nothing . Mr. Harris 's final , galloping pages have the almost cartoonish exuberance of a neo-medieval Indiana Jones adventure . Yet " The Second Sleep , " a novel as rich in ideas as it is in suspense , leaves us with gnawing questions .
