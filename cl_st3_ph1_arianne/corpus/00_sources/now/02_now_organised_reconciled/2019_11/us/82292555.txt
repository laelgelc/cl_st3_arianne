<h> News <h> University of Surrey to Develop Tool to Help Predict the Efficiency of Wind Farms <p> GUILFORD , England , Nov. 21 -- The University of Surrey issued the following news release : <p> A team from the University of Surrey has been awarded pound sterling150,000 from the Engineering and Physical Sciences Research Council to develop an innovative tool that predicts the efficiency of wind turbines and farms in both stable and unstable atmospheric conditions . <p> In 2017 just over 29 per cent of electricity generated in the UK came from renewable sources , with 21 per cent of that coming from offshore windfarms . The UK government has stated that it wants to deliver 30GW of offshore wind electricity by 2030 , which is hoped will generate pound sterling48bn in UK infrastructure investments . <p> By gathering data from multiple turbines in different wind conditions - from those typical of daytime to those characterising night-time ( i.e. with atmospheres at different temperatures ) - the team from Surrey will test whether an industry-wide assumption of how turbines behave and age ( the Sandia method ) stands up to scrutiny . By interrogating the Sandia method , we hope to provide engineers and decision makers with more robust and reliable tools for designing new turbines and wind farms .
