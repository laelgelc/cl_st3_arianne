<p> Fire , flood , insect infestations , drought and other calamities of nature can wreak havoc on the environment . But every natural disaster seems to open the door to new beginnings for an ecosystem . Fresh starts for the natural world are the theme for a new exhibit at the High Desert Museum . <p> Nature 's Resilience , which opens Nov. 23 , will highlight the valuable role that ecological disturbances play in our environment . The exhibit will feature large-format photography to illustrate the dichotomy of devastation and new life . <p> The exhibit comes at a time when ecological crisis makes daily headlines , from fast-moving disasters such as floods and wildfires to slow-moving ones like climate change and drought . While the consequences of a natural disaster can be dire , the exhibit aims to show the power of mother nature to quickly recover . <p> The exhibit will show how periodic fire , floods and pine beetle outbreaks are a part of the natural cycle and necessary for ecological health -- a sort of detox diet for the natural world . <p> Wildfires are particularly healthy for forests and forest creatures . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of native plants such as manzanita . Wildfires also create standing dead trees , known as snags , which offer homes to woodland creatures . <p> As Deschutes County residents have come to understand , controlled burns are necessary to not only clear fuels but also improve the long-term health of mature trees . As brush fires take out smaller , less mature trees , it reduces competition for water for older trees . <p> " In the high desert landscape , change is constant and dynamic disturbance is both a necessity and a reality , " said Dana Whitelaw , executive director for the museum . " The view the vibrant new growth alongside the destruction reminds us of the power of nature and time . " <p> The exhibit will also address unintended consequences that occur when humans alter nature .
