<h> Why Financial Markets ' New Exuberance Is Irrational <p> Owing to a recent easing of both Sino-American tensions and monetary policies , many investors seem to be betting on another era of expansion for the global economy . But they would do well to remember that the fundamental risks to growth remain , and are actually getting worse . <p> NEW YORK -- This past May and August , escalations in the trade and technology conflict between the United States and China rattled stock markets and pushed bond yields to historic lows . But that was then : since then , financial markets have once again become giddy . US and other equities are trending toward new highs , and there is even talk of a potential " melt-up " in equity values . The financial-market buzz has seized on the possibility of a " reflation trade , " in the hope that the recent global slowdown will be followed in 2020 by accelerating growth and firmer inflation ( which helps profits and risky assets ) . <p> The sudden shift from risk-off to risk-on reflects four positive developments . And , lastly , the US Federal Reserve , the European Central Bank , and other major central banks have gotten ahead of geopolitical headwinds by easing monetary policies . Whenever the next severe downturn occurs , high and rising private and public debts will prove unsustainable , triggering a wave of disorderly defaults and bankruptcies . <p> The disconnect between financial markets and the real economy is becoming more pronounced .
