<h> Shell is test-driving a number of eco-friendly business models before deciding which one to buy into <p> Royal Dutch Shell RDS.B 0.33% knows that climate change means business change , but is n't sure how . Like a savvy car buyer , it is testing out a few eco-friendly models before it chooses one . <p> The approach is sensible for Europe 's most valuable oil and gas producer , given that we do n't know what a lower-carbon future really looks like . At some point , though , investors need to brace for a big decision . <p> All countries except the U.S. have promised to cut their carbon emissions to comply with the 2015 Paris climate accord . Extreme weather events , ongoing climate protests and shifting public opinion are now pushing governments to deliver . The oil and gas business is on the front line of the drastic economic changes required . <p> Equally , though , there is great uncertainty about the pace of the transition and how lower-carbon economies will eventually be structured to produce and sell power , food and transportation . <p> Faced with this conundrum , Shell has promised @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 2016 level . It has gradually moved away from oil toward lower-carbon gas and has been adding electricity to its product mix . It is also applying its trading and retailing expertise to products other than oil and gas . With 45,000 stores -- more than Starbucks or McDonalds -- Shell serves 30 million customers daily . <p> In recent years , the company also has invested an average of between $1 billion and $2 billion annually on small-scale experiments in new energy sources including power , biofuels , solar , wind , batteries and hydrogen . Most do n't make a profit , but do n't cost too much relative to the more than $20 billion a year that Shell generates in free cash flow . <p> The experiments give the company information about new markets and the opportunity to shape customer expectations and regulations as they develop .
