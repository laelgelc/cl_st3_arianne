<h> Climate Adaptation Now <p> Governments and the private sector are feeling increasing pressure from the public to reduce greenhouse-gas emissions and strengthen environmental conservation policies . But the world also can not ignore the more immediate need for investments in climate resilience . <p> LUXEMBOURG -- As world leaders gather in Madrid for the United Nations Climate Change Conference ( COP25 ) , they must address more than future targets for reducing greenhouse-gas emissions . They must also address the harm to people and livelihoods that climate change is already causing . <p> Strengthening our ability to adapt to climate change has never been more urgent . Many regions are experiencing major difficulties as a result of higher global temperatures and changing weather patterns . We must do more to help citizens and governments tackle issues such as rising sea levels , wildfires , hurricanes and other natural disasters , and increased coastal erosion . Unless action is taken , climate change will reduce global GDPper capita by more than 7% by 2100 , with equally severe consequences for countries , whether they are rich or poor , hot or cold . <p> The economic and broader social benefits of adaptation to climate change are clear . In September , the Global Commission on Adaptation issued a report calling on governments , businesses , and communities to take urgent action to step up adaptation measures . The report finds that an investment of $1.8 trillion across five key areas -- early warning systems , resilient infrastructure , agriculture , mangrove protection , and resilient water supplies -- from 2020 to 2030 could generate $7.1 trillion in benefits . <p> Still , today 's investment gap is huge . The climate challenge demands much deeper cooperation between the public and private sector to increase green investment .
