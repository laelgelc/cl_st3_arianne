<h> Readers respond : Swan Lake needs PGE 's support <p> For years , urban Oregonians and policy makers have pursued clean-energy and greenhouse-gas reduction policies to address climate change . Rural communities are ill equipped to absorb the financial impact of these policies . Mutually beneficial energy projects can help bridge that divide . <p> Dozens of solar farms have been built in Southern Oregon , and major natural gas infrastructure is close to being approved . These projects create well-paying construction and maintenance jobs , as well as revenue diversity for landowners and an added tax base for counties that support essential government services such as education and law enforcement . <p> Klamath County is poised to benefit from the largest pumped hydro energy facility ever built in Oregon . The Swan Lake Energy Storage project is expected to drive nearly a billion dollars of investment in the state 's clean energy economy and generate 3,363 full-year-equivalent jobs during its four-year construction period . <p> Once operational , Swan Lake is expected to generate about $6.2 million in payroll and taxes annually , money that can support Klamath County schools , roads , libraries and vital services @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , but ca n't move forward until one or more utilities commit to purchasing some of Swan Lake 's power . <p> Portland General Electric has identified pumped hydro as an important part of its resource portfolio , and many studies point to the need for resources like it to ensure the reliability of our electrical grid . <p> Rural Oregon 's attempt to reinvent itself in a 21st century economy while maintaining its identity has been a challenge that has spanned decades . Energy projects like Swan Lake can be a vehicle toward stability . <p> Klamath County asks PGE to seek approval from the Oregon Public Utility Commission to contract for power from the Swan Lake project . This can be a huge win-win for PGE customers and Klamath County . Let 's get started !
