To date , more than S$6 billion in green bonds have been issued in Singapore . <p> While green bonds have been gaining ground in the region , Mr Ong acknowledged that green lending is still at a nascent stage , and noted that this is an area with " significant potential " for growth . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ lending , " he said . <p> In the real estate sector , for example , developers the likes of CapitaLand and City Developments Ltd have secured green loans from banks to build greener buildings , install solar panels and retrofit buildings with energy-efficient equipment . <p> In order to access lending , firms will need to incur costs to develop sustainability frameworks and engage external reviewers . <p> MAS will develop grant schemes to help defray these costs to make green and sustainability-linked loans more accessible , said Mr Ong . <p> " To make finance green in Asia , we need to make green lending not a niche activity , but mainstream , " he added . <p> In the face of climate change , Singapore 's financial system must also be resilient enough to weather the imminent risks . <p> Old fossil fuel assets can become stranded , in turn devaluing loans and investments .
