<h> Opinion <h> What goes around comes around : The world does not care about us <p> The tyranny of distance was supposed to be dead , killed by cheap air travel and digital telepresence , but it 's back . <p> I 've been feeling it for two weeks . While you 've suffered through heat waves , smoke haze and bush fire , I 've been in the South Korean capital of Seoul , where the average day-time temperature has mostly lingered around the low single digits , before plunging below zero as the sun falls . <p> People overseas care about our injured koalas , such as " Peter " at the Koala Hospital Port Macquarie , but about us ? The rest of world can probably find some momentary feels for the plight of our dying koalas , but for us ... not so much . <p> It says nothing in particular about us . It is just a matter of human nature . As a species we care about what 's close . For a long time this meant Australia was an unusually lucky country . History largely ignored us . <p> But as the catastrophe of climate change accelerates , and for the first time we find ourselves on the hard , cutting edge of human affairs , it means we will get to experience the lack of interest and compassion that has often characterised our own lack of regard for the fate of others . <p> Which others , you demand ? <p> The island people of the South Pacific come to mind . All of the wretched and the dispossessed we have of late imprisoned in our tropical gulags . The first Australians @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have felt our lack of fellow feeling . <p> And now , I suspect , our turn comes around . There is no doubt Australia has suffered these past few weeks . The land and people both . Just as there is little doubt that , in reality , nobody else cares . <p> In the abstract , yeah , sure . Buzzfeed can probably harvest a nice crop of pity-clicks for a burned koala listicle , but the world has its own sorrows to attend to and their attention will pass on . <p> Meanwhile , the bush burns and the reef turns white and dies . <p> This is the object lesson of our imminent demise . Just as we do n't care enough about starving polar bears , melting ice caps , Californian megafires , thawing tundra or drowning islanders because they seem so far away , nobody else should be expected to care about us as we eventually expire in ashes and dust . <p> John Birmingham is a columnist for the Brisbane Times .
