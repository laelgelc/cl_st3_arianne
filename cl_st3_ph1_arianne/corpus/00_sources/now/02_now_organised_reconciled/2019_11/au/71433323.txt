<h> Opinion <h> Australia has only empty words for children <h> By Freyana Irani <p> November 19 , 2019 -- 11.00pm <p> Today , the world celebrates the 30th anniversary of the most widely accepted human rightsdocument in history -- the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child . Insetting out minimum standards for the care and protection of all children , the convention explicitlyrecognises children 's independent agency and the value of their views , thoughts andexperiences . <p> Australia was one of the first countries to sign and ratify the convention . In doing so , we committedto creating a better future for all our children . We promised we would listen to our children.We now rank average at best and are commonly at the wrong end of the spectrum whencompared with the outcomes achieved for children by similarly developed peers , includingthe other 35 OECD countries . <p> School students participating in the Strike for the Climate in Sydney in September . And in Australia , there is so little publicdiscussion of the issues affecting our children 's lives . <p> Last year , on behalf of UNICEF Australia ( the United Nations children 's agency ) and theAustralian Child Rights Taskforce , I travelled to 30 cities and towns to hear from more than500 Australian children . Across the country , children consistently took issue with their lackof influence and consideration in decision making at all levels , including government , community , school , and within the family . They questioned their value . They spoke offeeling " disrespected " , " excluded " , and " powerless to make change " . They asked to be @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ big things " . This means taking an active interest in the issuesaffecting children in this country , and demanding action and improved results fromAustralian governments . It requires a change in adults ' attitudes towards children'sparticipation , and developing processes to maximise children 's agency within existing social , political and governance structures . It means letting children know that we see them , hearthem , and want something better for them . <p> For 30 years , Australia 's commitments to children have been empty words and unfulfilledpromises .
