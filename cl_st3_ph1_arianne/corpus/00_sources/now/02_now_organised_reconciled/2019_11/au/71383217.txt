<p> The Caribbean , the Great Barrier Reef , the South Pacific , Antarctica -- these picturesque locations are some of my favourite places , with their stunning beaches , aqua blue water , and incredible , almost indescribable landscapes . <p> Yet all of these places have a serious common problem . In fact , everywhere in the world , including your local community , has this problem : plastic pollution . <p> Four-wheel driving to a remote beach in Turks and Caicos in 2012 on the hunt for pristine beach to myself really opened my eyes to the problem . <p> After journeying an hour over rocky terrain , I was met by piles of plastic debris , with hardly a spare patch of sand in sight to lay my towel . There was everything from huge degrading shipping ropes to toothbrushes . My oldest find was a hairspray bottle from the 70s , all the way from the UK -- needless to say , fish do n't have hair . <p> Surveying the uninhabited outer islands of our Great Barrier Reef last year made me acutely aware of just how much plastic we use each day @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ globally to stem the tide of it . <p> Water bottles , thongs , household detergent bottles , fishing equipment , plates , plastic cutlery , plastic chairs , pegs , ice cream containers -- you name it , we found it , both from overseas and from our own Australian shores . <p> Locations uninhabited by humans are marred by traces of our convenient plastic-filled lifestyle . <p> Plastic pollution has become so rife that every ocean on Earth is teeming with plastic , including micro plastics and nano-plastics so small they 're invisible to the naked eye . Even the deepest location on our blue planet , the 11km deep Mariana Trench , bears witness to our throw-away lifestyle . <p> The oceans have no boundaries , which means what happens overseas affects us in Australia , and what we do here affects others worldwide . <p> Plastic travels across our oceans on currents and by wind . Each works a little differently , but in essence people receive a 10c refund for each eligible bottle or can returned . <p> Currently , more than five million containers are returned each day across NSW , with this number expected to increase over summer and hopefully reach over seven million per day . In ACT , around 100,000 bottles and cans are returned daily . These are huge numbers and represent an incredible amount of plastic , glass and aluminium being kept off our beaches , out of our waterways and out of landfill . <p> Because Return and Earn takes clean containers and sorts them carefully , ensuring no contamination from other non-recyclables , it provides a reliable , high-quality stream of recyclable product , which is in high demand from industry .
