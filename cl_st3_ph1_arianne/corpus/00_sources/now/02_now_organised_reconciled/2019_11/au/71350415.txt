<p> Roughly a third of the Netherlands lies below sea level . Even when water is n't physically present , the spectre of it is . <p> That feeling of looming water is central to Studio Roosegaarde 's installation , Waterlicht , which made its U.S. debut this week at Columbia University . Viewers stand amidst a sea of ethereal fog that slowly rises to meet blue LED projections that undulate above viewers ' heads . The effect gives the sense of being underwater . <p> Above the digital water , the buildings of Manhattanville rise into the sky . Rather than feeling stifling , the space is meant to open up a sense of possibility and contemplation in the face of one of humanity 's greatest challenges . <p> " The Dutch have such a big tradition in art and design and architecture , because we need to be creative in order to survive , " studio head Daan Roosegaarde told Gizmodo .
