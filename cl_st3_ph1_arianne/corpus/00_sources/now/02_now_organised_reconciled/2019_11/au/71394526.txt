<h> What Is A ' Mass Extinction ' And Are We In One Now ? <p> For more than 3.5 billion years , living organisms have thrived , multiplied and diversified to occupy every ecosystem on Earth . The flip side to this explosion of new species is that species extinctions have also always been part of the evolutionary life cycle . <p> But these two processes are not always in step . When the loss of species rapidly outpaces the formation of new species , this balance can be tipped enough to elicit what are known as " mass extinction " events . <p> A mass extinction is usually defined as a loss of about three quarters of all species in existence across the entire Earth over a " short " geological period of time . Given the vast amount of time since life first evolved on the planet , " short " is defined as anything less than 2.8 million years . <p> Since at least the Cambrian periodthat began around 540 million years ago when the diversity of life first exploded into a vast array of forms , only five extinction events have definitively met these @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have become part of the scientific benchmark to determine whether human beings have today created the conditions for a sixth mass extinction . <p> An ammonite fossil found on the Jurassic Coast in Devon . Each event itself lasted between 50 thousand and 2.76 million years . The first mass extinction happened at the end of the Ordovician period about 443 million years ago and wiped out over 85% of all species . <p> The Ordovician event seems to have been the result of two climate phenomena . First , a planetary-scale period of glaciation ( a global-scale " ice age " ) , then a rapid warming period . <p> The second mass extinction occurred during the Late Devonian period around 374 million years ago .
