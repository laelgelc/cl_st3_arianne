<p> Despite bearing the brunt of climate change and the blame , farmers are embracing new ways to think about carbon . A series of workshops , led by the University of Tasmania and the Australian National University aimed to shed light for producers on the benefits of carbon sequestration . IMAS/UTAS Professor Philip Boyd and ANU Professor Michael Ellwood lead the workshops . Professor Boyd said agriculture had been highlighted as an industry that could benefit from carbon sequestration . " What 's pretty clear is that we need to do more than cut emissions and we need to actively take that carbon from the atmosphere and store it elsewhere , whether that 's in the ground or somewhere else , " he said .
