<p> It 's an economic inevitability that global demand for coal is going to drop significantly , but when it does , the renewables boom will open a whole new market for Aussie startups to dominate , according to Ramez Naam , co-chair for energy and environment at SingularityU at the NASA Ames Research Centre . <p> Speaking at theSingularityU Australia Summit in Sydney last month , Naam noted the importance of coal to the Australian economy , as what is currently one of the most commonly used fossil fuels in the world . <p> While there are a few things driving this , the change can mostly be attributed not to changing social mindsets , but to the steep decline in the cost of clean energy . <p> The prices of both solar and wind energy are " in freefall " , Naam explained . <p> People are naturally going to buy the cheapest energy they can , he added . <p> " To disrupt an energy sector you have to be cheaper ... it 's fundamentally about economics . " <p> Already , in sunny parts of the world , solar power is the cheapest type of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ sunniest parts of the world -- the price of building and operating new high-scale solar resources has dropped to as little as $0.03 per kilowatt-hour ( kWh ) , wholesale . <p> That means building and operating new solar facilities is already cheaper than building and operating a new coal plant , " and edging on cheaper than the operational cost of a coal plant " . <p> Currently , solar power equates to only a little over 2% of global electricity , but that has increased 1,000-fold in the past 20 years , Naam says .
