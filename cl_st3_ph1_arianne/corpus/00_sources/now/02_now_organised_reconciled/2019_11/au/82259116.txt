They account for 50% of carbon dioxide sequestered by oceans -- despite covering just 0.2% of the world 's total ocean area -- and absorb carbon dioxide up to 40 times faster than forests on land . <p> They do this by trapping particles from water and storing them in the soil . This means tidal marsh , mangrove and seagrass ecosystems bury organic carbon at an exceptionally high rate . <p> Since European settlement , about 25,000km ? of tidal marsh and mangroves and 32,000km ? of seagrass have been destroyed - up to half the original extent . Coastal development in Australia is causing further losses each year . <p> When these ecosystems are damaged -- through storms , heatwaves , dredging or other human development -- the carbon stored in biomass and soils can make its way back into the environment as carbon dioxide , contributing to climate change . <p> In Western Australia in the summer of 2010-11 , about 1,000km @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to a marine heatwave .
