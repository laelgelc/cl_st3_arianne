<h> Australia scraps carbon floor price , links ETS with EU <p> Australia says it will scrap the proposed carbon floor price of its emissions trading scheme as part of a deal to link with the EU ETS , and will restrict access to Kyoto credits . The Australian carbon price will now be set in Europe . All eyes on Poland ! <p> The Australian government has announced it will scrap the proposed floor price of its emissions trading scheme as part of a deal to link the scheme with the European Union , and take some of the political heat about the carbon pricing debate . <p> Climate Change Minister Greg Combet announced the move in Canberra today , saying that the floor price of $15 a tonne of CO2-e would not come into effect from July 1 , 2015 , as previously agreed . <p> Instead , Australia has agreed to link its scheme with that of the EU , and will allow Australian emitters to buy up to 50 per cent of their liabilities from international markets , but only 15 per cent of the liabilities can be met from " Kyoto units " -- @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ schemes . <p> The decision follows concern about how Australia would transition from a fixed price of $23 ( rising each year to 2015 ) , yet allow its emitters to buy permits in CDM , which are currently trading at a level of around $3.50/t . The EU price is currently trading at less than $10/t . <p> The linking will inititally be a " one-way " link that will allow Australian businesses to buy permits from the EU scheme . They will be able to buy future permits from today . It is envisaged that European emitters will be able to buy permits from the European scheme by July 1 , 2018 . <p> " Linking the Australian and European Union systems reaffirms that carbon markets are the prime vehicle for tackling climate change and the most efficient means of achieving emissions reductions , " Combet said in a statement . <p> EU Climate commissioner Connie Hedegaard said the linking would be significant for both Europe and Australia .
