<h> The Australian Museum launches ' mysterious ' ad for Frog ID week <p> The spots , created by 303 MullenLowe , ask viewers to solve the mystery of what is making the sound . <p> The Australian Museum ( AM ) has launched a ' mysterious ' new online community service advertisement ( CSA ) promoting its national Frog ID campaign created by 303 MullenLowe . <p> The 15 , 30 and 60-second spots ask viewers Can You Solve the Mystery ? as sounds of a dog barking , an owl hooting , a motorbike roaring and a car alarm sounding ring out ... all mimicking the calls made by frogs . <p> The campaign is in support of Frog ID week , November 8 to 18 , when Australia 's 240 frog species hit their peak of calling and the public are invited to download the free Frog ID app and record the calls . <p> 303 MullenLowe CEO Nick Cleaver says his team jumped at the challenge to help protect one of Australia 's most threatened groups of animals , frogs , whose biodiversity is being impacted by habitat loss and climate change . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ variety of Australia 's unique frogs in a mystery ' movie-trailer ' that looks and sounds believable , until we reveal the twist : that every sound you 've just hear was from a frog , " Cleaver says . <p> " One of the best ways to accurately identify a frog is by its call , and to ensure that viewers know we did n't make this up , we replayed the scenes with an in-set clip of the species of frogs behind the sound . " <p> Australian Museum director , marketing , communications and partnerships Jacinta Spurrett says the idea was to create intriguing content that would live on-line well past Frog ID week and be used to promote the Citizen Science project all year round . <p> " Frog ID now has more than 106,000 participants across Australia and has identified locations of over 87 species of frogs , " Spurrett says . <p> " The online clip is a call to arms to the community to help us document and save our frogs by downloading the AM 's free Frog ID app .
