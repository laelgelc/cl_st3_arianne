<p> Thirteen leading economists have declared their hands in the standoff between the government and the governor of the Reserve Bank over the best way to boost the economy . <p> All 13 back Reserve Bank governor Philip Lowe . <p> They say that , by itself , the Reserve Bank can not be expected to do everything extra that will be needed to boost the economy . <p> All think that extra stimulus will be needed , and all think it 'll have to come from Treasurer Josh Frydenberg , as well as the Reserve Bank . <p> All but two say Frydenberg should be prepared to sacrifice his goal of an immediate budget surplus in order to provide it . <p> The 13 are members of the 20-person economic forecasting panel assembled by The Conversation at the start of this year . <p> All but one have been surprised by the extent of the economic slowdown . <p> The 13 represent 10 universities in five states . <p> Among them are macroeconomists , economic modellers , a former government minister and former Treasury , IMF , OECD and Reserve Bank officials . <h> The 13 economists surveyed @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the government 's contention , spelled out by Frydenberg 's treasury secretary Steven Kennedy in evidence to the Senate last month , that there is usually little role for government spending and tax ( ' fiscal ' ) measures in stimulating the economy in the event of a downturn . <p> Monetary policy -- the adjustment of interest rates by the Reserve Bank -- is nearing the end of its effectiveness in its present form .
