<h> China 's ' business of greening ' , and what it means for the world <p> As a country with massive energy requirements and a top-down approach to action , China is in a unique position on the issue of climate change - and the motivations behind its shift to renewable energy will benefit the rest of the world , one expert says . <p> In many ways , China seems a paradox when it comes to climate change . <p> As both the world 's largest consumer of coal and the biggest manufacturer of solar panels , it is simultaneously a major threat to future generations and a potential source of their climate salvation . <p> Without the democratic dimensions of the climate debate that have slowed action in some nations , the CCP has been able to push ahead with change - for reasons of self-interest as much as moral values , but change that may nevertheless benefit a number of other countries . <p> China 's work on climate action , and what it means for the rest of the world , was at the centre of a conference this week run by Victoria University @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> John Mathews , a professor of strategic management at Macquarie University , said China 's move to a green growth economy was driven more by its scale , which made the use of renewable energy and a circular economy " an inescapable choice " . <p> " It 's moving to green its economy not so much because of a moral or ethical imperative , but because of an energy security and resource security imperative .
