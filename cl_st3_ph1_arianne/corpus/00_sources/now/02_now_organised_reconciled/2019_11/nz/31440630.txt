<h> Coroners balance culture and process after attack <p> No two disasters are the same for those who respond . When a man walked into two Christchurch mosques on March 15 , 2019 and opened fire , New Zealand 's coroners had to balance the intensive and crucial disaster victim identification process with Muslim cultural considerations . <p> " On day five , they were pushing at the gates , and knocking at the doors . They were going to take their bodies ; they were going to drag them off -- their culture demands them . " <p> Deputy Chief Coroner Brandt Shortland said there was a lot of pressure on coroners in the wake of the of the Christchurch mosque attacks in March . <p> It 's no secret frustrations were high in the days immediately after the attack . <p> Muslim tradition dictates the body of the deceased be buried by sundown on the day of death . <p> After a couple of days of no bodies being returned to families , and very little information as to why , tempers began to rise . <p> On the Sunday , Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern told the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and the rest would be released by the following Wednesday . <p> In the background , those running the coronial process had their heads in their hands .
