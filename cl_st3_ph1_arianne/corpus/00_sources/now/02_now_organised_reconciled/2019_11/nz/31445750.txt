<p> The New Zealand Antarctic Society ( NZAS ) with support from Land Information New Zealand ( LINZ ) has produced a new custom-designed wall map of Antarctica to mark the anniversary of the Antarctica Treaty on 1 December . <p> " Sixty years ago , in 1959 , the signing of the Antarctic Treaty laid the foundation for the international science cooperation that exists today - and New Zealand has been a signatory to the Treaty right from the start " says Dr Linda Kestle , NZAS President . <p> Over the next week , the large printed map will be sent to all 2700 primary and secondary schools in the country . Themes on the map address climate change issues , such as the ice sheets , ocean currents , sea ice , and sea level rise . <p> " The NZAS have this year been working in a range of partnerships , to ensure that every school in the New Zealand will receive a copy of this brilliant new wall map of Antarctica . It will hopefully inspire and also be an educational resource for our future generations of scientists and decision makers . It will be on display alongside some of its historic predecessors in a three-week long exhibition called ' Mapping Antarctica - the frozen continent revealed ' until 12 December . <p> Tui Te Hau , Director Public Engagement National Library says , " We 're delighted to host this temporary exhibition that highlights how our knowledge and understanding of Antarctica has strengthened over time . " <p> LINZ says the map is a redesign of traditional cartographic conventions , incorporating the latest satellite technology with the most accurate elevation data . Uncertainties in the altitude of the ice sheet have been reduced to less than one metre . <p> " To support the NZAS schools ' initiative we wanted to present a more realistic tone and use data to demonstrate interesting features , with colours derived from aerial imagery . Tools are getting so much better , offering a wide range of techniques to employ . Therefore , we can be more complex in our designs , incorporating more and more features .
