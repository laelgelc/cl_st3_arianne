<h> PMDD : When hormones can be deadly <p> A little-known hormone-related condition affecting up to 8 percent of people with periods is sometimes mistaken for bipolar disorder and incorrectly treated . Farah Hancock reports . <p> *Warning : This story contains references to self-harm and suicide* <p> Lisa* is fighting for her life . She desperately wants to live but her body 's reaction to hormones makes it a battle . <p> Just before her period she plunges into a suicidal depression . What starts with feeling a bit teary one evening develops into days of what she describes as the deepest depression imaginable , and it continues for four to seven days from when her period starts . <p> " There 's depression , extreme thoughts of suicide , I 'm crying a lot , I ca n't look at other people . I do n't want to be around people . " <p> If she 's not at work , she 's in bed . If she is at work , she 's thinking of " novel ways to kill myself " . <p> Lisa does n't just have a nasty case of PMS . She @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ mental health condition . Her hormone levels are the same as other people , but her body 's response to the hormones is very different . When oestrogen levels plummet so does her mood . <p> The catch is that when it happens , she does n't recognise it for what it is and does n't seek help . <p> She 's trying various treatments and recently opened up to friends about her condition and explained the warning signs . She 's begging them to call the police or the " white coats " if they suspect she 's a danger to herself . She would rather be committed than dead . <p> " If I 'm hospitalised , or tied to someone 's fridge ... if I 'm being watched - then I ca n't do it . " <p> However , there 's a risk her condition might get misdiagnosed . Premenstrual dysphoria disorder ( PMDD ) affects between 3 to 8 percent of people who have periods but it 's not widely-known . The lack of knowledge around the condition means it sometimes gets incorrectly diagnosed as bipolar disorder . <p> This happened to Sarah* , who attempted suicide during a bout . She was taken into police custody and spent a night in a holding cell . A psychiatrist from the hospital brought in to give an urgent assessment diagnosed bipolar disorder . <p> At this stage , Sarah had already been diagnosed with PMDD . She was released after her duty solicitor got in touch with her GP . <p> " I 've certainly seen a lot of women spend time in psychiatric hospitals because they 've been misdiagnosed with rapidly-cycling bipolar disorder or something like that . " <p> A wrong diagnosis can mean a prescription of drugs that are n't necessarily effective for PMDD . <p> " That 's obviously been quite distressing because they ended up being on all sorts of heavy-duty psychiatric drugs that perhaps would n't be needed . However , Fenton said often it 's patients who bring the diagnosis to GPs after researching their symptoms online and realising what they are experiencing goes beyond typical PMS symptoms . <p> " I think generally - not only in New Zealand - it 's written off as being part of a woman 's lot . " <p> She explained that during a normal cycle , oestrogen levels peak during ovulation and then free-fall . The drop in oestrogen is thought to be a trigger for PMDD . " <p> Treating PMDD <p> There are two main ways to treat PMDD . One path tackles hormones , the other mood . <p> Treating the mood can help ease people through the symptoms . To date , the treatment used has been prescribing SSRIs ( selective serotonin re-uptake inhibitors ) , a group of antidepressant drugs similar to Prozac . For PMDD , research has shown these can be taken just at the danger times , rather than continuously . <p> Cognitive behavioural therapy can also be useful as part of a treatment strategy . <p> There are several options for managing hormones , and most are based on stopping ovulation . <p> Yaz , a contraceptive pill , which has fewer sugar pills than the normal pill and is the only one with research backing its effectiveness . Fenton said for most patients visiting a doctor , this was the standard treatment given . " <p> Fenton has been researching Cyproterone , a drug which prevents ovulation . She says it smoothes out hormones to a greater degree than the contraceptive pill and is helping around 90 percent of patients who try it . <p> " We use that with a bit of low-dose , plant-based oestrogen and then that can be incredibly effective . " <p> For severe cases there 's Zoladex , a pellet that is injected regularly .
