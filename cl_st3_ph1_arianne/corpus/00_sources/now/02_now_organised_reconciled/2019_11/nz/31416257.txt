<h> Uplift mother gets second child back <p> WATCH the video story above of the young girl 's return and interaction with her mother and baby brother , by Melanie Reid . <p> The young Hastings mother who resisted attempts by Oranga Tamariki to uplift her newborn baby - sparking four inquiries into the agency and an urgent Waitangi Tribunal hearing - is to have her first baby returned to her as well . <p> Her daughter , who was uplifted from the mother a few days after being born 20 months ago , has been in the care of a couple who believed they would have her permanently . <p> The little girl is being ' transitioned ' back to her 20-year-old mother , who now lives independently in Hastings . <p> Six months ago the mother came to the nation 's attention when she defied several attempts by Oranga Tamariki to uplift her second child , a boy , a week after he was born at Hawkes Bay Hospital 's maternity unit . <p> The mother 's whanau and her midwife , Ripeka Ormsby , were locked out of the hospital while Oranga Tamariki social workers and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ baby . <p> The tense standoff , with the mother physically clutching her baby and refusing to let go , ended at 2am after local iwi leader Des Ratima convinced police not to enforce the uplift order . <p> Video of the mother 's late-night ordeal , and earlier attempts by social workers uplift the baby , was at the centre of a documentary by Newsroom 's investigations editor Melanie Reid . <p> Reid 's investigation highlighted the number of Maori babies being taken into state care -- three a week -- and saw Maori leaders adopt the call ' not one more Maori baby ' . <p> This first inquiry , an internal one by Oranga Tamariki , is released today . The mother refused to participate in the inquiry , saying it was not independent and she did n't trust the process . <p> The mother , who is now being supported by a new social worker , left hospital with her baby boy , and spent four months at a home for young Maori mothers . She also cited drug use , lack of parenting skills , and transient home environments as reasons to uplift . <p> The mother 's whanau and the midwives disputed the seriousness of the claims . <p> The mother 's lawyer , Janet Mason , says since the documentary ran , Oranga Tamariki , which has legal custody of the children , has been supportive of the mother and her children . <p> " We want to do this ( have both children live with their mother ) with consent - the best outcome is for everyone to do it together .
