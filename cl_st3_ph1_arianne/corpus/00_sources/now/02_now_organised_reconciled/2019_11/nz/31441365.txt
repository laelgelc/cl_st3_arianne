<h> Zero Carbon Bill a missed opportunity ! <p> OPINION : The Government failed to take on board common sense suggestions for the improvement of the recently passed Zero Carbon Bill ( ZCB ) . <p> It had a golden opportunity to pass a Bill that was fit for purpose . It could have taken a bipartisan approach to climate change and could have taken farmers along as well . <p> On November 1 , the primary sector put a proposal to the Government which would have achieved the Zero Carbon Bill 's aims and built on the good faith established by the industry-Government climate change commitment -- He Waka Eke Noa . <p> That was a sad day for common sense . The Government not only walked away from an important part of our commitment to the 2015 Paris Agreement , which requires all its signatories not to forsake food production for climate goals . <p> But it also relinquished the opportunity to be true leaders and adopt targets for methane which truly reflect its actual warming impact . <p> Federated Farmers was also deeply worried by a comment made during the debate on the ZCB by Labour @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Deborah Russel , who questioned the usefulness of New Zealand 's ability to produce food . <p> Russel said : " I 'm not sure that we have a responsibility to feed as many people as possible . We certainly want to ensure that we produce food -- it 's one of the things that we export -- but it 's not clear to me that we need to continue producing food at that level . " <p> The 2050 24-47% reduction target for biogenic methane remains eye-wateringly hard for farmers to achieve and correspondingly dire for our economy to withstand . <p> With the current tools in the farmer 's toolbox , the only way to meet the top end of the target ( 47% ) is to halve the size of our livestock sector . Even if some of those tools become available , they are not going to universally fit into all farming systems . <p> New Zealand farmers are proud to be the most carbon efficient farmers in the world . Forcing them to reduce production is not only going to make New Zealand poorer , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ effectively be shooting ourselves in both feet .
