<h> Two Auckland land occupations : Two very different agendas <p> Two very different pieces of land in Auckland ? Owairaka and Ihumatao ? are under occupation by two protest groups with different motivations and different demands . Yet , both occupations have come about due to the same decades-long process of redress under the Treaty of Waitangi for colonial wrongs . The outcomes of both occupations could make or break this essential nation-building process . <p> For the past four decades , the Waitangi Tribunal has investigated claims and since the 1990s , the government has negotiated settlements with iwi . This process has not been without controversy or opposition . To make settlements palatable to the Pakeha majority , governments promised the settlements with iwi were to be full , final and fair . They also promised not to include private land in settlements to further assuage fears . And despite the big dollars involved in Treaty settlements , the total monetary compensation paid out to date has been less than a tenth of the claimed land 's current value . <p> Maori have been more than accommodating and generous throughout the process . Iwi have been @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the return of land to resolve historic grievances in exchange for some recompense and closure . Added to this has been an increasing time pressure to resolve claims , with settlement deadlines being set and extended with Brexit regularity . <p> Despite this , iwi have prospered . Reports have shown iwi post-settlement assets now sit at $9 billion in wealth , nine times the amount paid in monetary compensation , and ironically a significant contributor to the Treasury 's coffers . The current government inherited a process that was drawing to a close , with one major iwi - Ngapuhi - still to settle . The process , however problematic , was trucking along . <p> Ihumatao presents the government with an unenviable challenge . Under the Te Kawerau a Maki Settlement of 2014 , the alienation of Te Kawerau a Maki lands by the government from 1856 onwards was redressed . The land at Ihumatao was not part of the settlement , being privately owned . In the same year Te Kawerau a Maki settled their claims , the land at Ihumatao was sold to Fletcher Building and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Auckland Council . As a result , an occupation to prevent the construction of housing began . <p> The contemporary occupation at Owairaka has a similar timeframe to the one at Ihumatao . In 2014 , 13 mana whenua iwi and hapu in Auckland agreed that the maunga - 14 iconic volcanic cones on Auckland 's landscape - be returned to them and vested collectively between them . A co-governance authority , the Tupuna Maunga Authority ( TMA ) was created to govern the maunga . <p> The TMA 's planned restoration of 13,000 native trees and shrubs to the maunga , requiring the clearance of 345 exotic trees from one of the maunga , Owairaka/Mt Albert , has sparked an incredibly hostile reaction . This hostility has manifested itself in a highly unusual occupation , led by mostly Pakeha , despite Forest &amp; Bird and the Tree Council backing the iwi . <p> Resolving Ihumatao will require the government to either break or dance around past promises that Treaty settlements are full and final . The argument could be made that so long as Ihumatao was excluded , the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ set a precedent right across the country to relitigate previously agreed compensation and redress . <p> The government seems dead set on resolving Ihumatao before Christmas , negotiating directly with Fletchers to either compensate them for the land or provided capital for some kind of loan . Whatever the means of reaching a new agreement , the potential for damage to the settlement process is huge . <p> So too with the occupation at Owairaka . The occupiers are essentially demanding that the TMA reverse its decision to plant native trees and shrubs , on the basis that a climate change emergency has been declared , nesting birds need protection and the aesthetics of the maunga during the regeneration of native trees takes precedence over mana whenua and the TMA . All of this regardless of what the Treaty settlement states . Agreeing to the occupiers ' demands not to remove exotic trees at Owairaka would mean going back on a Treaty settlement not even five years old . <p> To resolve both occupations , some honesty is required by each group : Owairaka 's occupation is the product of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in mana whenua . Ihumatao 's occupation is in the unhappiness by some Maori that privately owned land , even when that land was acquired following confiscation , is excluded from Treaty settlements . Both challenge the Treaty settlement process . The central government now must face this challenge with a view to finding lasting solutions to each . We can only wish them well .
