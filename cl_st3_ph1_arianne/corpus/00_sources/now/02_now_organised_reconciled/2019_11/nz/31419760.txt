<h> Holey data leaves us ' flying blind ' on environment <p> How can a country solve its environmental issues if it does n't really know what they are ? It ca n't , says a bracing new report by the Parliamentary Commissioner for the Environment . <p> New Zealand knows far too little about whether its soil , water , air or native creature populations are getting better or worse , says a lacerating report by the Parliamentary Commissioner for the Environment . <p> Simon Upton has called for the law to be changed so that next April 's Ministry for the Environment/Statistics NZ joint report on freshwater is the last to be done under our current , hole-ridden system . <p> Instead , he wants environmental and statistics staff taken off the " treadmill " of constant reporting that , in his view , does n't tell us much of what we need to know . <p> His report has been backed by several environmental scientists , including some involved in the monitoring process . <p> Patchy and inconsistent data make it impossible to construct a clear national picture , his report says , so policy-makers are @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ information when they make crucial decisions . You rapidly run out of things to say , once you 've said it once , " he told Newsroom . <p> Upton 's report on New Zealand 's environmental monitoring system says governments have for decades failed to seek out and pay for the information we badly need , despite a newish Environmental Reporting Act that aims to improve things . <p> He contrasts our patchy environmental data with the screeds of long-running , high-quality data collected about the economy since World War II . Virtually the only environmental trends with any comparable long-term data are coastal sea level rise and the world-renowned atmospheric CO2 record from Wellington 's Baring Head , he says .
