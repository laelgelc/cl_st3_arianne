<h> A weak climate law based on a feeble consensus is no ' nuclear-free moment ' <p> The Zero Carbon Bill is bland and ineffective . Do n't fall for the spin , argues Steve Abel of Greenpeace . <p> It was hard to not be buoyed by our House of Representatives , seemingly aligned in their commitment to climate action , at last week 's passing of the Zero Carbon Act . Bold expositions of pride and parliament united across divides , as in wartime . Missives of hearty congratulations on the climate activist email lists . <p> But scepticism lurked . I sincerely hope we are on the right track . Here are my doubts . <p> For the last couple of years the climate movement has called for : 1 ) political consensus on a 2 ) strong and 3 ) binding climate law . What we got was one out of three . But without being strong and binding , consensus is meaningless . The reason is , the law barely touches them . <p> Green co-leader James Shaw 's comparison of the act to the winning of suffrage for women , or the ban on nuclear ships , is some pretty sweet spin . Those meaningful social changes in New Zealand 's political history , of which we are rightly proud , were not achieved by consensus -- exactly because they were tough choices . <p> The winning of votes for women was a close run thing with vehement opposition , and required multiple attempts at passage through the all-male House .
