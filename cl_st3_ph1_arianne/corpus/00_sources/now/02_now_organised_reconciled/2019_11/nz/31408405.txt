<h> Court to council : sort out dam , fast <p> An Environment Court judge has stern words for local and central government departments who failed to monitor the impact of an illegally-constructed dam <p> An illegally-constructed dam that is affecting the food source of critically-endangered fairy tern should be removed , according to the Environment Court . <p> Only 37 breeding age fairy tern remain . The dam , which was constructed on public land by the golf course development company associated with US billionaire Ric Kayne , blocks the passage of inanga to upstream spawning grounds . <p> Last year , the birds experienced their worst-ever breeding season with only two chicks surviving . There 's concern a lack of live fish , the birds ' main food source , could be a factor . <p> For years the New Zealand Fairy Tern Trust has been raising concerns about the impact the dam was having on inanga to get upstream to spawning ground . <p> The court case was borne out of frustration of being ignored , said trust convenor Heather Rogan . <p> " Nothing else has worked . During the course of the hearing the court found the dam in its current state breaches the Auckland Unitary Plan . The court recommends this be urgently addressed . <p> Judge Jeff Smith 's decision expresses surprise at the lack of response of council and government departments to the trust 's concerns . <p> " Given the critical nature of the fairy tern population and related inanga breeding at Mangawhai , we are surprised that none of these departments have taken action .
