<h> Old weather records could predict snow in Auckland <p> Machines will transcribe handwritten weather records from as far back as 1860 to help predict future weather patterns <p> On the subantarctic Campbell Island , a worker in charge of the daily recording of weather observations started his job with excellent penmanship . <p> After three years living on a remote island where rain falls 325 days of the year and wind gusts reach 96 kilometres per hour , his penmanship devolved into " chicken scratches " . <p> His weather observations and accompanying comments are soon to be deciphered in a project aiming to use artificial intelligence to transcribe the handwriting used in weather records from up to 150 years ago . <p> The information he and hundreds of others recorded at various weather stations around New Zealand could give insights into future weather . <p> NIWA principal scientist Dr Andrew Lorrey explains : <p> " Old weather data and old scientific data are really useful from a perspective of getting as much in-depth knowledge as far back in time as we can go ...
