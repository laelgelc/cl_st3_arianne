<h> On the 6th anniversary of the Rana Plaza tragedy -- how transparent are fashion brands being now ? <p> Wednesday , April 24 , 2019 - 09:59 AM <p> With thousands of people taking to the streets to protest climate change and hundreds arrested over it , conversations around the impact we 're having on the environment have reached fever pitch . <p> And few meaningful dialogues about tackling the environmental issues facing the planet would be complete without mentioning fashion . As part of the week , it has released the fourth annual Fashion Transparency Index , where 200 brands are ranked not just in terms of environmental @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is leaving behind huge amounts of waste ( Fashion Revolution/PA ) <p> It 's also six years since the Rana Plaza tragedy in Bangladesh which killed 1,135 people working in unsafe conditions in garment factories supplying cheap clothing to some UK high street brands . So what does the index teach us about what 's changed and what should consumers and brands be doing to help create a more transparent fashion industry ? <h> The Fashion Transparency Index <p> The Fashion Transparency Index is n't about listing which brands are the most eco-friendly or who is doing the most for human rights . It 's all about how much information is publicly available regarding these topics : policy and commitments , governance , traceability , supplier assessment and remediation and spotlight issues ( which focus on the UN 's Sustainable Development Goals ) . <p> Fashion Revolution has chosen 200 of the world 's largest brands and retailers across luxury and fast fashion , and has evaluated how open they are about these key issues , giving an overall transparency percentage . Sports and outdoor brands lead the charge with @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ transparency -- it 's the first year ever that brands have scored more than 60% . ASOS , Puma , Nike , The North Face and Marks &amp; Spencer were all in the 51-60% range , but depressingly the average score was just 21% . <p> Although the index might not immediately improve the state of the industry , it will hopefully drive change by showing brands where they can do better and making them accountable for their actions . <p> Founder of Fashion Revolution Carry Somers says : " We believe transparency leads to a greater accountability " , which will in turn lead to positive change in the industry . <p> Fashion Revolution wants the industry to adopt more concrete and measurable goals , both in terms of the environment and human rights . There has been an improvement in transparency of many of the brands listed on the index -- particularly with luxury labels like Gucci and Bottega Veneta -- but there is still a lot to be done . One particular issue which has come out of the report is a lack of information around how brands are working for gender equality , which is troubling as women make up the majority of the fashion industry , from the factories to the shop floors . <p> What the fashion industry does with excess stock is still a relatively murky area , which is surprising considering the uproar around the news of Burberry burning its unsold stock last year . Only 26.5% of the brands on the index are open about what they do with defective or surplus stock , the rest choosing not to disclose any details . <p> Fashion Revolution hopes to push more brands to publish their supply chains and make them easily accessible for anyone who wants to hold them accountable . <p> Some brands are taking these issues seriously .
