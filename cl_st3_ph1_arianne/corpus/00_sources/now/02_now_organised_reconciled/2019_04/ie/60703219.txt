<h> European cities to slash carbon emissions by 40 percent <p> Filipe Araujo , Chair of the EUROCITIES Environment Forum and Vice President for Innovation and Environment for the City of Porto <p> A network of European cities has urged climate change leaders to do more to include them in global efforts to limit rising temperatures . <p> The call for greater inclusion in climate negotiations comes after 12 European cities agreed to cut their carbon emissions 40 percent by 2030 , at the EUROCITIES Environment Forum in Genoa . <p> " By committing to strong actions on climate change , cities are showing that the local level can lead the way , " said Anna Lisa Boni , Secretary-General at EUROCITIES . " Now we need national and European leaders to recognise this and step up their long-term commitments towards a climate neutral Europe in 2050 . " <p> Speaking to Cities Today , Filipe Araujo , Chair of the EUROCITIES Environment Forum and Vice President for Innovation and Environment for the City of Porto , said cities will prioritise the " decarbonisation of mobility " . <p> Araujo said specific actions to halt rising temperatures will also @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ investment in green infrastructure , and the affordability of public transport , among other things . <p> " In Porto this year we will put 1MW of energy on top of our buildings , especially our schools , " said Araujo . " Changing bus fleets to electric or less pollutant buses is also very important and then there is also a big issue regarding the price of tickets . " <p> To address the affordability of ticket prices , Aurajo said cities in Portugal have cut the cost of travelling across major metropolitan regions like Porto and Lisbon from " hundreds of euros " to " 30-40 euros " , with families benefiting most from the change . <p> He also confirmed that the cost of achieving the new 2030 carbon emissions targets in Portugal , is " in the hundreds of millions " . <p> " The cost of subsidising ticket prices on public transport across Portugal is more than ? 100 million .
