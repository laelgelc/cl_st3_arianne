<h> The future of CCS for the UK <p> Following The House of Commons Business , Energy and Industrial Strategy ( BEIS ) Committee 's report titled Carbon capture usage and storage : third time lucky ? Scottish Carbon Capture Storage ( SCCS ) have responded with its thoughts on the future of CCS in the UK . <p> The House of Commons Business , Energy and Industrial Strategy ( BEIS ) Committee has advised the UK Government that it need to take quick action on vital technology to tackle climate change . <p> Carbon capture and storage ( CCS ) , the topic of the Committee 's report , is the most cost-effective -- and for some industries , the only -- solution to this . The UK is well placed to develop CCS technology , meaning the country can create a new industry permanently storing carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) and exporting the technology to capture and store CO2 to the rest of the world . <p> " We strongly welcome the Committee 's report and urge the government to act on its recommendations . We are pleased to see the Committee focusing on the ' how @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " said Stuart Haszeldine , Professor of CCS at the University of Edinburgh and SCCS Director . <p> " It has been shown time and time again that CCS is not just the lowest cost way of decarbonising the UK economy . It will be essential if the world is to achieve the climate ambitions of the Paris Agreement . " <p> " There are five potential CCUS clusters of high-emitting industries , which want to reduce their emissions by capturing and permanently storing the CO2 they emit . Each cluster has different and complementary strengths , and the UK Government needs to support them to collaborate , not pit them against each other in a competitive arena . " <p> Get in gear and release the handbrake <p> Having read this forthright retort from SCCS , I have to say I could n't agree more .
