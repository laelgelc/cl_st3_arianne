<h> 20% of global deaths linked to poor diet <h> Low intake of healthy foods a major concern <p> Apr 04 , 2019 <p> Deborah Condon <p> Some 20% of deaths that occurred worldwide in 2017 were associated with poor diet , a major new study has found . <p> The Global Burden of Disease study tracked dietary trends from 1990 to 2017 in 195 countries . It estimated that one in five deaths globally in 2017 - that is 11 million deaths - were associated with poor diet . <p> In 1990 , the figure was around eight million . <p> The researchers said that people in almost every part of the world could benefit from improving their diets . They also noted that in 2017 , more deaths were caused by diets too low in whole grains , fruits , nuts and seeds , than by diets containing high levels of foods such as trans fats , processed meats and sugary drinks . <p> " This study affirms what many have thought for several years , that poor diet is responsible for more deaths than any other risk factor in the world . The biggest excesses were seen for sugar-sweetened drinks , processed meat and sodium . <p> In fact , the world consumed , on average , just 12% of the recommended amount @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ amount of sugary drinks . <p> Meanwhile , the world consumed only 16% of the recommended amount of milk and 23% of the recommended amount of whole grains , compared to 90% more than the recommended amount of processed meat , and 86% more sodium . <p> Uzbekistan was the country with the highest rate of diet-related deaths in 2019 , while Israel was the lowest . Ireland was ranked 24th on the list , just behind the UK and ahead of Sweden . <p> The researchers said that the findings show that many existing awareness campaigns have been ineffective and new interventions are needed to rebalance diets worldwide . <p> However , these interventions need to be sensitive to the environmental effects of food systems , which can have an impact on things like climate change , soil degradation and the loss of biodiversity , they added . <p> Details of these findings are published in the medical journal , The Lancet .
