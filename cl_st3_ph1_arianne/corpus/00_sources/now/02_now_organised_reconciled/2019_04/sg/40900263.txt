<p> Seventy years ago , Alaska 's Excelsior Glacier stretched its cold fingers from a vast plain in the state 's southern edge nearly all the way to the North Pacific Ocean . Now , the glacier is separated from the sea by a meltwater lake more than five times the size of New York City 's Central Park.In a recent blog post on the American Geophysical Union ( AGU ) website , glaciologist Mauri Pelto of Nichols College in Massachusetts shows how that relatively new lake -- now called Big Johnstone Lake -- has more than doubled in size over the last 24 years as rising global temperatures force Excelsior Glacier into a hasty retreat .
