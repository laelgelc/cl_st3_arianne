<p> SYDNEY - Australia 's ruling coalition yesterday ( Tues ) ( April 2 ) revealed plans to deliver the nation 's first surplus in more than a decade , releasing a federal budget aimed at reversing its poor standing in opinion surveys ahead of an election to be held within weeks . <p> In his first - and possibly last - budget as prime minister , Mr Scott Morrison , who became leader of the Liberal-National coalition last August , presented tax cuts and big-spending infrastructure projects targeting middle class and regional voters who will be crucial to his election prospects . <p> Following a decade of consecutive deficits , the Treasurer , Mr Josh Frydenberg , said that Australia will next year record a surplus of A$7.1 billion ( S$6.8b ) , the first since 2007-08 . The deficit this year is expected to be A$4.2 billion , aided by extra commodities exports and tax revenues . <p> " Tonight , I announce that the budget is back in the black and Australia is back on track , " Mr Frydenberg told Parliament . <p> " For the first time in twelve years , our nation is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ progress , but we know the job is not done . The unemployment rate is at the historically low level of 4.9 per cent . <p> But the nation is facing serious challenges , including plummeting house prices , stagnant wages , losses due to drought and flooding , and global concerns such as China 's slowdown . <p> " The global economy is slowing ...
