Any plastic waste factories outside the boundaries of the park will be deemed illegal . <p> In an exclusive interview with Malay Mail recently , Zuraida elaborated on her proposal , saying that the waste park will also include scrap metal recycling , particularly from irreparably damaged vehicles . <p> " The ministry gives licences to the import of clean plastics . This is where the clean plastic comes in , we recycle and export back as resins and pallets . This will be turned into consumer products -- Nike T-shirts and all those -- and it 's worth RM30 billion . <p> " So , what will @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ which we ride on to bring income into the country ? In two years , I 'm going to centralise all these factories into one designated area -- waste parks . <p> " Then it will be easier for us to monitor and manage . Anything outside this waste park will be illegal . So now they have to start thinking , correct themselves if they are illegal , and start applying for legal licences , " said Zuraida . <p> She said that all state governments have been notified of the ministry 's plans . <p> By the end of this parliamentary term , almost every state will have its own waste park . <p> Acknowledging the logistical challenges , however , she foresees some states such as Selangor and Johor requiring two parks , while small states or those that do not produce as much plastic may share one with their neighbour . <p> The parks will focus on upcycling waste products and turning garbage into money . The three northern-most states in West Malaysia -- Kedah , Perlis and Penang -- might share a waste park located in Kulim . <p> Selangor will probably see a waste park in Port Klang while Johor will have one in Rapid Pengerang . The Perak state government is still in the process of identifying a suitable site . <p> Similar to waste parks , Zuraida also plans to introduce a waste-to-energy ( WTE ) policy and construct an incinerator in each state . <p> " At one time , people were against incinerators .
