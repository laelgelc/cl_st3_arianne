<p> OTTAWA ( AFP ) - Canada is doing too little to combat climate change , a parliamentary report warned on Tuesday ( April 2 ) , a day after government scientists warned the country was warming at twice the global rate . <p> The rebuke came as Canada 's independent environment auditor released a new report on government efforts to end fossil fuel subsidies , and as climate change shapes up as a divisive issue in elections seven months from now . <p> " For decades , successive federal governments have failed to reach their targets for reducing greenhouse-gas emissions , and the government is not ready to adapt to a changing climate , " wrote environment commissioner Julie Gelfand . " This must change . " <p> A report by Environment and Climate Change Canada , details of which were released on Monday , showed that average temperatures in Canada have risen by 1.7 deg C since 1948 , about double the global average of 0.8 deg C. <p> In northern Canada , approaching the Arctic Circle , temperatures rose on average by 2.3 deg C over the same period . <p> Canada has pledged to meet @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ per cent from 2005 levels by 2030 . <p> On Monday , Prime Minister Justin Trudeau - who has declared climate action a top priority - slapped a carbon tax on four provinces that had not fallen in line with his emissions reduction strategy . <p> But the government 's overall efforts have been panned by the environment auditor , and in December , the environment ministry acknowledged a best case scenario of a 19 per cent emissions cut . <p> In her latest report , Ms Gelfand outlined a badly defined process , a lack of good data and little progress in making decisions to meet Canada 's G-20 commitment to eliminate inefficient fossil fuel subsidies by 2025 . <p> She also slammed the government for looking only at the economic impact of fossil fuel subsidies , without considering potential social and environmental damage . <p> For fossil fuel producers , subsidies can take the form of tax breaks , cheap loans , protection from competitors , or favorable trade restrictions . <p> For consumers , they generally result in below-market prices for oil , gas or electricity . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ year estimated the global value of fossil fuel consumption subsidies in 2016 at about US$260 billion ( S$352 billion ) , down from US$310 billion the year before . <p> Electricity and oil each accounted for just over US$100 billion , with natural gas topping US$50 billion .
