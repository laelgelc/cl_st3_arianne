<h> War of Words over Iran Floods Indicates Tehran will not Improve its Environmental Record <p> Although it has a reputation for water shortages and aridity , Iran must now deal with the consequences of excess water , as a series of flash floods devastate the country . The floods , which started in Golestan this March , have killed 70 people , displaced thousands more and destroyed infrastructure . Golestan received 70 per cent of its annual rainfall in a single day when the floods began and more rain is expected this month . According to the Iranian Red Crescent , damage to infrastructure and agriculture now totals millions of dollars . Most provinces have been affected and the most recent flood warnings for Khuzestan have led to evacuations in the already-inundated province . <p> Comment <p> Ordinarily , rain would be a source of celebration in water-scarce Iran . As the rains have continued , however , the situation has become increasingly alarming . Villages have been destroyed , homes have been submerged under mud and thousands of people have been displaced . A number of narratives have emerged from the disaster , each placing blame on @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ on both climate change and the United States , as the current US sanctions have prevented financial aid from reaching the country . The US ( among others ) , meanwhile , has made claims that the Iranian regime itself is responsible for the floods , through continued mismanagement of urban planning and the environment . None of these claims is necessarily inaccurate , but individually they do not fully address the complex causes of the floods . <p> It is true that climate change has certainly made Iran more vulnerable to flash floods . In general , Iran has become hotter and dryer in recent years , but weather extremes have also increased . Although the overall pattern of rainfall has decreased , the frequency of extreme and intense precipitation events has increased . In that sense , the Iranian Government narrative is correct . Climate change has made the country more vulnerable to floods and natural disasters . Tehran 's response to climate change , however , has been insufficient , especially considering how quickly the regime has identified that phenomenon as a cause of its current predicament @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ has been less than successful , dismissing proposed sustainable water management policies , in favour of building infrastructure , including wells and dams . This approach has led to the over-exploitation of aquifers and has prevented water from reaching lakes and rivers . Flood management policies are significantly lacking and there is little to mitigate the impact of rural land use , which contribute significantly to flooding . Over 2,000 hectares of forest has been cut down in a 25-year period in northern Iran , which has increased surface runoff and , in turn , increased its susceptibility to flooding . Although building dams had mitigated flooding to some degree , the extent of the rainfall Iran has received has meant that the dams are no longer able to prevent floods . Currently , many dams are more than 95 per cent full , risking more downstream flooding . <p> By blaming climate change and the US , Iran has demonstrated that it is unlikely to implement policy reforms that could help mitigate future flooding events . It has also spent a number of years imprisoning and harassing its environmentalists , to push its economic agenda at the expense of its environmental one . Unless the Islamic Republic dramatically changes its attitude towards the environment , droughts and floods will continue to devastate the country . <p> Any opinions or views expressed in this paper are those of the individual author , unless stated to be those of Future Directions International .
