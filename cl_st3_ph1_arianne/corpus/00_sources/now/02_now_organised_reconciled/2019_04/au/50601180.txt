<p> The Indian Ocean has many unique characteristics compared with the other ocean basins . The most striking factor is the presence of the Asian continental landmass , which results in the absence of a northern ocean basin and generates monsoon winds . <p> As a result of the former , there is no gyre in the Northern Indian Ocean , and so there is no garbage patch . The latter results in reversing ocean surface currents . <h> How does it move through the oceans ? <p> The Indian and Pacific Oceans are connected through the Indonesian Archipelago , which allows for warmer , less salty water to be transported from the Pacific to the Indian via a phenomenon called the Indonesian Throughflow . <p> This connection also results in the formation of the Leeuwin Current , a poleward ( towards the South Pole ) current that flows alongside Australia 's west coast . <p> As a result , the Southern Indian Ocean has poleward currents on both eastern and western margins of the ocean basin . <p> Also , the South Indian Counter Current flows eastwards across the entire width of the Southern Indian Ocean , through @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ tip of Madagascar to Australia . <p> The African continent ends at around 35 degrees south , which provides a connection between the southern Indian and Atlantic Oceans . <h> Follow that rubbish ! <p> In contrast to other ocean basins , the Indian Ocean is under-sampled , with only a few measurements of plastic material available . As technology to remotely track plastics does not yet exist , we need to use indirect ways to determine the fate of plastic in the Indian Ocean . <p> We used information from more than 22,000 satellite-tracked surface drifting buoys that have been released all over the world 's oceans since 1979 . This allowed us to simulate pathways of plastic waste globally , with an emphasis on the Indian Ocean . <p> We found that unique characteristics of the Southern Indian Ocean transport floating plastics towards the ocean 's western side , where it leaks past South Africa into the South Atlantic Ocean . <p> Because of the Asian monsoon system , the southeast trade winds in the Southern Indian Ocean are stronger than the trade winds in the Pacific and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ further to the west in the Southern Indian Ocean than they do in the other oceans . <h> So the rubbish goes where ? <p> This allows the floating plastic to leak more readily from the Southern Indian Ocean into the South Atlantic Ocean . <p> All these factors contribute to an ill-defined garbage patch in the Southern Indian Ocean . <p> In the Northern Indian Ocean our simulations showed there may be an accumulation of waste in the Bay of Bengal . <p> It is also likely that floating plastics will ultimately end up on beaches all around the Indian Ocean , transported by the reversing monsoon winds and currents . Which beaches will be most heavily affected is still unclear , and will probably depend on the monsoon season . <p> Our study shows that the atmospheric and oceanic attributes of the Indian Ocean are different to other ocean basins and that there may not be a concentrated garbage patch . <p> Therefore the mystery of all the missing plastic is even greater in the Indian Ocean . <p> By Mirjam van der Mheen is a PhD candidate @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ at the University of Western Australia .
