Bloomberg <p> You probably should , if only because we will have to go back to something very much like the Gillard scheme if we want to minimise the cost of emission reduction . <p> So should the Coalition parties , because Gillard 's scheme could have made the coal debate much easier for them . Think about it : under the Gillard scheme the government would have set the annual quantity of allowable emissions and issued the necessary tradeable permits . A new coal-fired power station , for example , would not have affected the quantity of emissions , because that was set by the government . The cost-minimising power of the schemes increases as their coverage of the sources of greenhouse gases is broadened . The trade in permits effectively focuses the national emission-reduction effort on activities where the cost of reduction is lowest . <p> However , the schemes have other useful characteristics . For example , they give the public an independent estimate of the cost of emission reduction . <p> First Gillard and Abbott , and now Morrison and Bill Shorten have been guilty of misleading the public about the cost of converting our heavily carbon-dependent economy . <p> Renewable energy is cheap to generate because the fuel ( sunlight and wind ) is free .
