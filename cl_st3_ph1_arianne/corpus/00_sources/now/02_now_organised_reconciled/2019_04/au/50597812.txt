<h> Time to call more climate strikes ! <p> On March 15 , more than 150,000 school students walked out of class across Australia to demand real action over climate change . A total of more than 60 rallies were held . <p> The actions in Australia were part of a global day of school strikes to stop climate change . It was the biggest single day of climate protest ever , with more than one million students participating across 125 countries . <p> It is not hard to understand why so many students responded to the call . January 2019 was the hottest month in Australia in 100 years . Extreme heatwaves , droughts and bushfires raged across a number of states , while cyclones and floods devastated big parts of north Queensland . <p> Young people see a terrifying future with a climate breakdown happening right in front of them . At the same time no politicians are proposing a viable way forward . <p> The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) estimates that there are only twelve years left to avoid devastating consequences from climate change . Taking action is extremely urgent . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that more than 63% of Australians believe we need immediate and serious action to address the issues . <p> Pressure from public opinion has forced Scott Morrison , Tony Abbott and Peter Dutton to back off somewhat from their ardent support for coal . The Liberals face electoral losses because of their out of touch views . <p> While the Labor Party pose as more progressive , Bill Shorten has not come out clearly against the Adani coal mine , and Labor 's emission reduction targets are inadequate . Labor is torn between trying to win people 's votes and keeping their big business backers happy . <p> Strikes and protests are the best way for school students to exert pressure on both the major parties despite not yet having the right to vote . More mass actions can cause further rifts within the Liberals and Labor and expose their real nature . <p> Strikes also make the political establishment worried because they have the potential to grow and draw wider layers in . Some are beginning to recognise that the capitalist system , with its private control of industry , prevents us from solving this problem . <p> Not only is big business trashing the planet in the pursuit of profits , but capitalism also pushes for competition rather than cooperation . This is at odds with a high level of international cooperation needed to deal with global warming . <p> While many already sense that the system is to blame , there is still a question about what type of change we actually need . Socialists say that the only alternative is to bring the top corporations into public hands , under democratic control . <p> This would eliminate the reckless competition for profits and lay the basis for a planned economy .
