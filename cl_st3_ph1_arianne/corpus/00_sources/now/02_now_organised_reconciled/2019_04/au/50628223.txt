By Martin McKenzie-Murray . <p> What is next for the Australian Greens <p> The year 2010 appears now as a high-water mark for the Australian Greens . The party secured sole balance of power in the senate , won almost 12 per cent of the primary vote in the lower house , and in Adam Bandt won its first seat in the house of representatives . While Bandt has since fortified the seat of Melbourne -- privately , Labor concedes his supremacy -- the Greens ' electoral fortunes have oscillated . <p> The balance of power won in 2010 was lost to successive parliaments ' unpredictable mix of minor parties and independents in the senate . In 2012 , the Greens lost its broadly respected leader , Bob Brown , to retirement . Increasingly , the Labor Party has successfully , often cynically , exploited perceptions of Greens ' sanctimony -- though the Greens has had its share of substantive scandals .
