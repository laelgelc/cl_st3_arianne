<h> Day to highlight plight of koalas <p> ROSEBUD resident Dirk Jansen is organising events to highlight the plight of koalas on the Mornington Peninsula , including a koala day next month . <p> " Many people are surprised to find out there are still koalas living on the peninsula , " he said . <p> " There is something special about observing wild koalas in their natural habitat and residents and visitors are always delighted when a koala visits their backyard or is spotted on one of the many bush walks . " <p> Mr Jansen said the peninsula 's small population of koalas has been declining rapidly . <p> " Like everywhere else on the east coast of Australia , koalas face many challenges , such as habitat loss due to land clearing and climate change ; vehicle strikes , disease and dog attacks , " he said . <p> He said long-term residents are concerned at the lack of koala sightings in recent years , prompting fears that the remaining population is too small to be viable . <p> A 2016 study by Deakin University found that koalas were not thriving on the peninsula , with @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ trees are being lost through removal and dieback , and parks and reserves are not connected well enough for animals to move from one reserve to another , " Mr Jansen said . <p> Koalas are regularly sighted at Somers , Balnarring , Arthurs Seat State Park and Greens Bush , with occasional sightings at Mt Eliza , Mt Martha and Tyabb , Red Hill and around Flinders .
