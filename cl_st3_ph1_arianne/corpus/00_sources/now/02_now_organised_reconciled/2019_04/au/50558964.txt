<h> Rescue climate from Green extremes and coal obsessives <p> The climate change policy wars have helped end the career of every Australian prime minister since John Howard in 2007 . <p> Apr 2 , 2019 -- 12.00am <p> Imagine it 's the end of 2009 . Labor 's climate change plan includes elements of an emissions trading scheme . The Coalition claims it is " a Trojan horse for a carbon tax " that will jack up the price of everything , while the crazy Greens reckon that it apes climate change-denying conservatives and would contract out Australia 's responsibility for saving the planet to pig farmers in Ukraine . The miners do n't like it , while the Business Council wants more detail . Yet it is 2019 and Labor is in opposition , though favoured to get into government by the end of May . And its cobbled-together policy retains at least the core of an emissions reduction scheme that would encourage the market to find the least-costly way to reduce the economy 's carbon intensity . Transitioning a fossil-fuel rich economy while also exporting more coal and gas to help alleviate power poverty in Asia has proven too much of an intellectual and practical leap for Australia 's political class . Amid the policy debacle , Australia has lost its traditional cost advantage in cheap electricity . <p> Mr Howard refused to ratify the Kyoto agreement while announcing an emissions trading scheme ( ETS ) for the 2007 election . Kevin Rudd ran away from his similar Carbon Pollution Reduction Scheme after claiming that climate change was the great moral challenge of the age . Julia Gillard introduced a carbon tax after promising not to . Tony Abbott smashed the bipartisan consensus , replacing an explicit carbon price with an Emissions Reduction Fund that slugged taxpayers and an increased Renewable Energy Target that force-fed too much unreliable wind and solar into the system , prematurely displacing coal-fired plants and destabilising the electricity grid . It would amplify a so-called baseline and credit scheme embedded in a " safeguards mechanism " implanted under Mr Turnbull . That would provide a form of emissions trading for the 250 biggest " polluters " . In turn , that would generate a price signal to help businesses discover the cheapest way to reduce emissions . As with the initial CPRS , Labor would at least partly exempt so-called emission-intensive and trade-exposed industries . Otherwise these heavy industries would simply relocate to countries without any carbon constraint .
