<h> Spores that can grow inside us <p> A mould , Aspergillus thrives in warm dark places with organic matter to decompose . <p> It 's the main player in a compost heap . <p> However , its spores can be inhaled and it will grow inside our bodies too , given the right conditions ( such as weakened immunity , or lungs damaged by disease , smoking , or pollution ) . This leads to disease ranging from isolated fungal balls through to deadly pneumonias . <p> Aspergillus infections are treated with a class of anti-fungals called azoles and occasionally surgery . <h> Growing resistance <p> These azoles are cheap , potent , and non-toxic -- they are one of the main medical weapons against fungi . <p> However , they are not only relied upon by doctors to treat patients , but by farmers who need to protect their crops . Azoles are among the top used pesticides globally , from the tulip growers of the Netherlands to rice farmers of the Mekong . <p> Such wide-spread use of azoles in agriculture has had the unintended effect of pushing Aspergillus to develop resistance . <p> Extensive trade @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Now , across the world , patients who have never taken anti-fungal drugs are turning up with infections that are already resistant to azoles . <p> Many of the remaining drug choices are toxic , expensive , or need to be given intravenously . <h> One of the biggest HIV killers <p> Before Candida auris and azole-resistant Aspergillus , there was cryptococcal meningitis . <p> Although this bug was known for hundreds of years , it only leapt to prominence with the HIV epidemic , where it was one of the biggest killers . Although most cryptococal infections occur in HIV , one member of the family , Cryptococcus gattii , causes outbreaks in healthy individuals , especially in tropical and subtropical areas . <p> It is endemic in Australia , where researchers showed its preferred home was the eucalyptus tree . <p> Here , it was long known as a rare but devastating cause of meningitis in forestry workers and koalas . <p> In 1999 , the global story of Cryptococcus gattii changed . <p> This low-level background infection gave way to an outbreak , which started in Vancouver @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ North America . <p> This new strain was stronger than the strains endemic to Australia , and more fertile . In that new temperate environment it went on to kill hundreds of people , as well as a wide spectrum of animals including dolphins . The outbreak is ongoing . <h> Where will the next threat come from ? <p> Together Candida , Aspergillus , and Cryptococcus kill over one million people each year . <p> But as these three stories illustrate , the disease they cause is unpredictable . <p> They can move into new environments , or new hosts , or develop resistance . Man-made impacts may play a role too -- contamination of the environment with anti-fungals and climate change being the obvious examples . <p> A fungal disease outbreak in frogs illustrates the worst case scenario .
