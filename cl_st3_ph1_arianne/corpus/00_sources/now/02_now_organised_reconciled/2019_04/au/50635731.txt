<h> Energy a big deal for SMEs <h> Topics <p> As we creep closer to the federal election , it is clear that energy costs are not dominating the political landscape to the degree they have over the past few years . <p> While there may be other more newsworthy issues such as the small business-crushing proposed changes to wages , personal income taxes , negative gearing and franking credits , and a somewhat political compromise on the National Energy Guarantee , the impacts of high energy prices on South Australian businesses remain very real . <p> During the March quarter , in fact , the average wholesale market price for South Australia hit a new record . <p> While the average punter is often told he or she does not pay the wholesale price , you can bet your bottom dollar consumers eventually do end up paying , it 's just that those costs do n't get passed on immediately . <p> Price rises for small consumers , including small businesses using less than 160 MWh per annum , only occur annually on 1 July . <p> The forecast electricity supplied through the grid is also dropping faster @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ more of the tab . In the latest network tariff update for South Australia , across the board price rises for 2019-20 were nearly 10 per cent . <p> That might only impact about a third of the bill , but consumers still have to find the money to pay for it . <p> One of the key challenges for South Australian businesses in recent years has been to embrace a low-carbon electricity market when the result has been such a significant hit to their bottom line . <p> In a recent Business SA survey , more than 60 per cent of members said the Federal Government should continue to adhere to the Paris Agreement to reduce Australia 's carbon emissions by 26 to 28 per cent by 2030 . <p> Businesses in general want to support climate change policies , but that has to be balanced with the reality of remaining solvent and keeping people employed . <p> While many businesses are taking matters into their own hands by installing solar , there are many more leasing businesses which rely on the grid . <p> Even with solar , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for businesses does not yet stack up . <p> More than half of Business SA 's members support the National Energy Guarantee and while this was always a political compromise and never the optimal model , having some certainty on energy policy is what the electricity market needs to continue to invest and ensure consumers receive competitively-priced power . <p> Business SA encourages voters to ask political candidates how they will work collaboratively across both state , federal and party lines to ensure Australia can move towards a low-carbon electricity market in a way which does not push avoidable costs onto consumers , including businesses . <p> From our experience as a business lobby group , it is often the lack of political compromise on energy policy , which causes the biggest increase in costs , not the actual cost of solving the underlying problem .
