<h> Ocean currents bring good news for reef fishes <p> James Cook University researchers have discovered some good news for fish populations living on coral reefs hit by climate change . <p> JCU PhD candidate Renato Morais from JCU 's Centre of Excellence for Coral Reef Studies led a study that looked at how fishes on a bleached coral reef get their food . <p> " We already knew that coral reef fish rely on food drifting in from the sea , such as plankton . But we did n't know exactly how important this was for fishes , " he said . <p> Mr Morais and JCU 's Professor David Bellwood combined high-resolution surveys and individual biomass production estimates to generate the first map of where the energy comes from for all fishes on a coral reef . <p> " We looked at everything from gobies to coral trout and large jacks , assessing more than 18,000 fish from over 300 species . We found that various transport mechanisms , such as currents and tides , interact with the reef and bring in vast amounts of plankton , " said Mr Morais . <p> The pair found that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , more than 400 grams of that kilogram relied on food derived from the open ocean , rather than the reef itself . This rises to almost 600 grams for fishes on the side of the reef facing the open ocean . <p> " This means , that for many reefs , food from outside can sustain fish populations , even when the coral is badly damaged , " said Professor Bellwood . <p> The scientists found that areas of the reef that were more exposed to the open ocean produced the largest quantities of fish -- with reef slopes being the most fruitful . <p> " The discovery that reef fishes get so much of their food from off-reef sources was encouraging , especially because many fish species that feed on oceanic material have a history of disappearing after coral loss , " said Mr Morais . <p> " This is the first time we have been able to put all species in perspective , " said Prof. Bellwood .
