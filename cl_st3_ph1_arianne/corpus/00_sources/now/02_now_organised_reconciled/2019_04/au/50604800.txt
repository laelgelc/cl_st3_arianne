<h> You are here <h> Why climate change will dull autumn leaf displays <p> Every autumn we are treated to one of nature 's finest seasonal annual transitions : leaf colour change and fall . <p> Most of the autumn leaf-shedding trees in Australia are not native , and some are declared weeds . Nevertheless , Australia has a spectacular display of trees , from the buttery tresses of Ginkgo biloba to the translucent oaks , elms and maples . <p> Autumn colour changes are celebrated worldwide and , when the time is right , autumn leaves reconnect us to nature , driving " leaf-peeping " tourist economies worldwide . <p> However , recent temperature trends and extremes have changed the growing conditions experienced by trees and are placing autumn displays , such as Canberra 's , at risk . <h> The subtlety of change <p> We learnt in childhood autumn colour change follows the arrival of cooler temperatures . Later we learnt the specifics : seasonal changes in day length and temperature drive the depletion of green chlorophyll in leaves . Temperature plays a role here too -- intensifying colours as overnight temperatures fall . <p> We 've also come to understand the role of a leaf 's environment . Anthocyanin production is affected by light intensity , which explains why sunny autumns produce such rich colours and why the canopies of our favourite trees blush red at their edges while glowing golden in their interior . <p> However , early signs show this year 's autumn tones will be muted . After the record-breaking heat of summer and prolonged heat of March , many trees are shrouded in scorched , faded canopies . The ground is littered with blackened leaves . <p> But we 're kidding ourselves if we believe this last summer was normal or recent temperature trends are just natural variability . If this is a sign of seasons future , we need to prepare to lose some of autumn 's beauty . <h> Lost synchronicity <p> Long-term and experimental data show that the sensitivity of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . While large-scale meta-analyses point to a delay in the arrival of autumn colours of one day per degree of warming , individual genera may be far more sensitve . Colour change in Fagus is delayed by 6-8 days per degree . <p> In addition , as warm weather extends the growing season and deep-rooted trees deplete soil moisture reservoirs , individual trees are driven by stress rather than seasonal temperature change and cut their losses . They shed leaves at the peripheries of their canopies . <p> The remainder wait -- bronzed by summer , but still mostly green -- for the right environmental cue . <p> For years , careful species selection and selective breeding enhanced autumn colour displays . This rich tapestry is now unravelling as hotter summers , longer autumns and drought affect each species differently . <p> At the centre of many urban-tree plantings , our long association with elms faces a threat . Loved for the contrast their clear yellow seasonal display creates against pale autumn skies , elm canopies have been ravaged by leaf beetles this year . However , that too may be tempered by the increased risk of colour-sapping pathogens , such as poplar rust , favoured by warm , moist conditions . And there are also negative consequences for autumn colour associated with elevated carbon dioxide concentrations . <p> Of course , we need to keep it in perspective -- the dulling of autumn 's luminescence is far from the worst climate change impacts . Nevetheless , in weakening our link with nature , the human psyche is suffering another self-inflicted cut as collective action on climate change stalls .
