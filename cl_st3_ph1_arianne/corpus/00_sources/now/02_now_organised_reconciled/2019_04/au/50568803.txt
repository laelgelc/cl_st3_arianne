<p> But this is about more than the fringe . Parts of the political mainstream seem disturbingly unmoored . Take , for example , how many in the Coalition have publicly suggested the Greens pose an equivalent extremist threat to One Nation . Some in the media fervently believe that reformist environmentalism is as evil as white nationalism . <p> Consider as well the shifting ideological ground over climate change . It 's a strange world when the supposedly pro-market Liberals are backing state subsidies for coal investment , and when it 's now left to the Greens to back a carbon price as the best response to climate change . <p> It should be enough to convince us there 's a certain madness in our politics . At least , a great deal more than usual . We usually tolerate some absurdity because we reassure ourselves there 's some ultimate high purpose in politics . That 's less easy to do when so much of politics now resembles bad reality TV . <p> How did we get here ? <p> The problem is a structural one , going back to the 2007 election . The arrival of the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the 24/7 news cycle , a certain hyperactive short-termism . <p> The Gillard years saw a new toxicity added . Gillard 's prime ministership was undermined not only by misogyny , but by Tony Abbott 's relentlessly brutal negativity as opposition leader . Politics grew even more tribal and polarised . <p> It got worse when Abbott became prime minister , and continued under Malcolm Turnbull . Driven by the right wing of the Liberal Party and its media supporters , conservative identity politics has trumped the concerns of the mainstream . On issues such as climate change , same-sex marriage , race and free speech , conservatives have revealed themselves to be out of touch with the rest of the country . <p> There is a certain irony in this . Those on the Australian right are obsessed with attacking leftist identity politics . They castigate the left for supposedly succumbing to divisions based on identity , and for failing to provide a vision for the common good . It is conservatives who are the most egregious practitioners of identity politics . Their identity politics is about putting minorities in their place , about convincing majorities that they 're the real victims in society . It 's about a world that is upside down : one where men are an endangered species because of gender equality , and where racial majorities are the targets of " reverse racism " . <p> In their prescient work of 2008 , The Times Will Suit Them , Deakin University scholars Geoff Boucher and Matthew Sharpe observed the rise of " postmodern conservatism " . Indeed , many conservatives seem to care more about their partisan identities and emotions than they do about truth and reason . As issues such as climate change show , what matters is their version of reality -- not what experts would say is happening . <p> Loading <p> The result : debates are being reduced to hyperpartisan contests , rather than contests about policy and the public good . The prospect is a very different one under a possible Shorten Labor government . There 'd be an opportunity to reset the national debate . A chance to purge from the political system the nastiness and disorienting madness that has come to define politics for the past decade . <p> Ultimately , though , Australian democracy has some fundamental questions to ask itself . Is what we 're seeing a sign of the exhaustion of representative democracy as we 've known it ? <p> It 's a problem that our major political parties are no longer representative of the community .
