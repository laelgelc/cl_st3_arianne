<h> Wellington in the race to reach zero carbon <p> Wellington City Council is seeking feedback on Te Atakura -- First to Zero : its blueprint for a zero carbon capital city , which was released today . <p> The blueprint outlines key activities relating to transport , building energy , advocacy and other areas to reduce emissions that cause climate change . It balances activities that reduce emissions , like using electric cars , with others that absorb them , like planting trees , to reach its goal of zero carbon in Wellington city . It also outlines a plan for the Council itself to reach zero carbon . <p> Mayor Justin Lester says this is the perfect time to be talking about how Wellington can lead the way to a zero carbon future . <p> " The city is already preparing for a further 80,000 more people to make Wellington their home and the Council 's Planning for Growth consultation will guide where and how the city should grow . <p> " Te Atakura -- First to Zero will help shape the Council 's view of what the city needs to look like in the future . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Wellington City Council 's Climate Change Portfolio lead , Councillor David Lee , says the conversation with the public is a chance to hear a range of views on how to move to a zero carbon goal . <p> " Science is telling us our current low-carbon plan is n't ambitious enough . We need to do more , and we need to do it faster , " says Councillor Lee . <p> " If we want to keep temperatures within the climate ' safe zone ' we need to act more quickly to lower our carbon emissions by about half within 11 years , and to zero as soon as possible . " <p> The Council 's plan is ambitious and it needs to know it has the support of Wellingtonians to take strong , fast action . <p> Councillor Lee believes choosing not to act is not an option .
