<h> Farmers warned of climate change risk <p> Ag Minister Damien O'Connor has warned farmers that if they do n't take account of climate change in their production systems they could be jeopardising access to major markets . <p> He says customers worldwide expect producers to look at the implications of climate change . If they fail in this NZ could face more trade barriers in negotiations with the EU and UK especially . <p> " They are expecting us to be part of the climate change movement , " he told the Agricultural Climate Change conference in Palmerston North last week . <p> Farmers may not always get an extra dollar for doing this but must make some changes to be allowed to keep operating . However some of the changes may see farmers get paid more for the value they offer . <p> O'Connor concedes that some farmers find dealing with greenhouse gas issues scary , as do many people . <p> " Farmers are dealing day to day with climatic issues and uncertain pricing ; so to add another layer of complexity with climate change obligations is a big concern . But we have to address @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ them pathways to try to address these things . " <p> O'Connor says farmers have been receiving confusing signals , but there are many young farmers out there , and older ones , who get it . <p> " They just want to know what they have to do to meet the obligations and they must get the clarity they seek via guidelines and advice . " <p> It 's important to ensure that if farmers make change they will be heading in the right direction , O'Connor says . <p> " We ca n't expect them to keep chopping and changing on the basis of different science or different levels of knowledge . " <p> It 's important to make the Emissions Trading Scheme work for farmers , he says . <p> " It may not be simple but it has to be workable and pragmatic with better outcomes . "
