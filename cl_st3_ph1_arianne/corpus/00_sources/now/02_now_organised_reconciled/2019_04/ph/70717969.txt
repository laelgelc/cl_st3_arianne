<h> Antarctic penguins suffer ' catastrophic ' breeding failure <p> The second largest Emperor penguin colony on Earth has suffered a " catastrophic " breeding failure after nearly all chicks born over three years died as their icy Antarctic habitat shrinks , researchers said Thursday . <p> The Emperor penguin is the largest of all penguins but they appear to be particularly vulnerable to climate change ( AFP Photo/MARCEL MOCHET / MANILA BULLETIN ) <p> The British Antarctic Society ( BAS ) used satellite imagery to study the behavior of the Halley Bay colony in the Weddell Sea due south of Cape Hope , which normally sees up to 25,000 penguin pairs mate each year . <p> They found that in 2016 when abnormally warm and stormy weather broke up the sea-ice on which the penguins normally raise their young , almost all the chicks died . <p> This pattern was repeated in 2017 and 2018 . <p> The BAS said the colony at Halley Bay has " all but disappeared " . <p> " We have been tracking the population of this , and other colonies in the region , for the last decade using very high-resolution satellite @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> " These images have clearly shown the catastrophic breeding failure at this site over the last three years . " <p> The team noted however that a nearby penguin colony at Dawson Lambton had " markedly increased in size " , indicating that many of the Halley Bay birds had migrated to safer breeding conditions . <p> BAS penguin expert Phil Trathan said it was impossible to know for sure if the break up of sea-ice at Halley Bay was caused by climate change . <p> " But such a complete failure to breed successfully is unprecedented at this site , " he said . <p> The Emperor penguin is the largest of all penguins but they appear to be particularly vulnerable to climate change . <p> Their population , centered around Earth 's extreme south , is set to decline up to 70 percent by the end of the century as the planet continues to warm .
