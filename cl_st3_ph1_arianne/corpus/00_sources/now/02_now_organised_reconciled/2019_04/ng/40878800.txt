<p> Francis Odim is the Chief Executive Officer at African Sustainable Energies . He is an environmentalist and a lover of nature who has always advocated for the preservation of the environment by using clean energies . <p> In this interview , he told Daily Sun how he started with very little capital he gathered from other people and today , he is turning his passion for the environment into a gold mine . He also enumerated the challenges and gains inherent in his line of business . <p> Background <p> I grew up in Umuahia , Abia State , in my early years before I moved to Jos , Plateau State . Growing up in the East and the North was an avenue to meet people in the different zones and ethnic groups . This has been of great help for me in my dealings with people till date . When I got to Jos , I attended Air Force Military School ( AFMS ) , Jos , where I studied under rigorous military training . I always say that AFMS played a huge part in shaping me into who I am today . I basically grew up in Jos Air Force barrack . <p> Starting up <p> I have always had a flair for engineering . I loved fixing things . Even if it was working fine , I would dismantle it to see what is inside then couple it again . I always wanted to experiment . Curiosity and boredom made me read them and that was how I became the engineer of the house . I fixed the television , laptops , lights and every appliance I could lay my hands on . Studying Engineering in school only fueled the already ignited fire . <p> What got you interested in sustainable energy <p> Unfortunately , I got admission to study Petroleum Engineering in Federal University of Technology , Owerri . I always wanted a more practical engineering course but this is Nigeria , you get what they give . I had no choice but to accept the admission . Although I did not like Petroleum Engineering as a course , it helped me realise that petroleum as a source of energy is diminishing . In a couple of years , petroleum would not be used . I started researching and gathering knowledge on climate change and clean energy sources . After graduating , I started doing some courses in renewable energy and I kept growing more passion for it . I saw that in the next five to 10 years , nobody would be buying fuel , or using other unrenewable energy sources . Renewable energy is the future . Renewable energy is the new oil . <p> Business capital <p> When I discovered all these , I had to align myself with this new form of energy . I contacted some friends I had in the United Kingdom , Netherlands and America and we shared ideas . They had an enabling environment and opportunities to do the same thing over there . Looking at Nigeria , it was a perfect market for solar energy due to the unstable power supply . We decided to hook up -- they bring the technology ; we bring the financing and opportunities . In early 2014 , my uncle in Asaba saw my efforts and asked me to give it a try and install the solar system in his house . I gave him the quotation and he paid in advance and even supported with more . <p> Challenges <p> One of the major challenges we have been facing is the high cost of getting materials . There are no policies on importation duties for people going green . In most countries , there are reductions given to you just because you are helping the environment , just to support the movement and make it affordable for middle and low-income earners . In Nigeria , there is a 20 percent tax on solar batteries and 10 percent tax on panels , while @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ government does not want to go green and it does not speak or support it in any way . Another challenge is the rapid technological advancement in renewable energy . Things are changing fast ; even in this sector and to be a major player , you have to be ready for change . So , we need to keep training our staff and engineers . They need to keep taking courses to ensure that they are current . I do n't think ignorance is a major challenge because we sensitise people on the need to go green . Most people know about it , but they do n't have the resources to pursue it . There are no institutions that are interested in helping with the sensitisation on the need to use renewable energy , neither do they help people get it . So , we are left to bear the brunt of it all . We have some companies that we partner with that assist with some subsidies and costs sharing just to ensure that people actually afford it .
