<h> Chennai ROV monitors climate change in Antarctica , awarded <p> TNN Updated : Apr 4 , 2019 , 9:03 IST <p> The underwater ROV studying coral reef in the Andamans <p> CHENNAI : An indigenous underwater remotely operated vehicle ( ROV ) developed by a city lab , which has helped Indian scientists conduct climate change studies in the Indian and polar regions , has won an award from the ministry of science and technology for innovation . The shallow water ROV or polar ROV ( PROV ) developed by National Institute of Ocean Technology ( NIOT ) , which will be soon available for Indian oceanographers for research , was given the National Meritorious Invention Award by National Research Development Corporation ( NRDC ) , an enterprise of department of scientific and industrial research under the ministry . <p> NIOT director M A Atmanand said they have signed a non-exclusive MoU with L&amp;T and BHEL to transfer the technology and commercially manufacture the ROV . The 10-member team that was involved in the design and development of the ROV was awarded Rs 3 lakh cash . " NIOT has done the system engineering for the ROV , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 500m . We have designed , developed , tested and commissioned it , " he said . The technology transfer was facilitated through NRDC . The ROV was operationalized in 2015 after it underwent successful trials in North Antarctica . Since then , the ROV has been deployed in both Indian and polar regions for climate change studies . In Antarctica , the vehicle was deployed in Priadarshini Lake on the Schirmacher Oasis , which is a source of water for ' Maitri ' , India 's second base in the icy continent . The ROV captured video of the lake bed and penetrated through crevices of an ice shelf to collect data on various parameters through its sensors to help scientist study the shape , size and the history of the ice mass . <p> The vehicle was deployed to monitor the health status of coral reefs in the Andamans and sea weeds in the Arabian Sea . Scientists said the ROV can be used not only for research and scientific exploration , but also for several engineering applications .
