<p> Ironically , what threatens the future of penguins and other species seems to have come as a boon for the endangered kakapo ( pictured ) ( Shutterstock ) <p> Human beings remain a divided tribe over climate change . The climate sceptics ' club has some influential members . The president of the United States of America , who infamously stated that he does not believe in the phenomenon , is one of the patrons of the institution . Pitted against Mr Trump and other non-believers are activists , scientists and -- is this not heartening ? -- Greta Thunberg , a sixteen-year-old in pigtails , hailed as the future face of climate activism . <p> Consensus over climate change is , arguably , equally elusive in the world of animals . Penguins , residents of a melting Antarctic , have every reason to denounce the changing weather . It has been reported that the second largest colony of Emperor Penguins has suffered catastrophic changes , causing nearly every chick to perish in the last three years as their kingdom of ice continues to shrink at an alarming pace . The avian species , which finds itself on the endangered list , has been experiencing an unprecedented mating frenzy , which , scientists believe , could have been fuelled by climate change . Ironically , what threatens the future of penguins and other species seems to have come as a boon for the kakapo whose passions would put even randy rabbits to shame . <p> The asymmetry integral to climate change -- spelling doom for some and bestowing bounty on others -- is also playing out in the case of the global economy . The findings of a recent study by two researchers from the US show that economies located in colder climatic zones -- those of Canada , Britain , Sweden and Norway -- are poised to receive a boost as global warming intensifies , bringing in its wake , erratic weather . Warmer countries -- India being one -- are expected to fare much worse . The end result is , unfortunately , the intensification of global inequality . Only this time , climate , rather than colonialism , seems to hold the key to its perpetuation . <p> The differential consequences of climate change within human and -- it has now been proved -- animal societies reveal significant challenges . The discovery of these diverse impacts is consistent with assumptions that human knowledge of the impending crisis is still in its infancy , making both prediction and intervention unreliable -- as unreliable as the planet 's fickle seasons . Worryingly , meaningful deterrents are being undermined by the benefits that climate change is bringing to a handful of constituencies -- polluting industries and economies better off than the ones in the developing world . The global inertia against formulating corrective action against climate change can be partially explained by those who have spotted profit even in doomsday .
