<p> The scene is a curled-up cat lazing around on a wooden stool , a steaming kettle and a boy awaiting a hair cut in an open barbershop . This is the routine of life in Dilli and there is nothing remarkable about it . Yet it 's one of the most popular backdrops for a selfie in Delhi today . To witness it , you need to stroll around the Lodhi Estate Art District . <p> The residential quarters of Lodhi Estate came up in the 1940s to house babus serving the British Raj , being one of the last creations of the Raj . It was a quiet peaceful neighbourhood then , and nearly eight decades hence remains much the same . But in the last couple of years , it has gone through a visible change and is changing even today . It has metamorphosed into perhaps the first art district in India . <p> The buildings here , created in the British colonial architectural style , have lent their walls as huge canvases for the imagination of a number of Indian and international artists . Nowadays it is going through a fresh stint of painting and repainting .
