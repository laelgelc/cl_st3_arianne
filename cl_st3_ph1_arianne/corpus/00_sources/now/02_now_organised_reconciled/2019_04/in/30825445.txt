<h> Do ' mechanical trees ' offer the cure for climate change ? <p> NEW YORK , April 29 ( Thomson Reuters Foundation ) - A Dublin-based company plans to erect " mechanical trees " in the United States that will suck carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) from the air , it said on Monday , in what may be prove to be biggest effort to remove the gas blamed for climate change from the atmosphere . <p> The company , Silicon Kingdom Holdings ( SKH ) , will build 1,200 carbon-cleansing metal columns within a year with which it hopes to capture CO2 more cheaply than other methods , following a successful test in Arizona over a two-year period , it said . <p> That is enough to suck up nearly 8,000 cars worth of emissions per year of CO2 . <p> " We have to figure out how to act to get to a climate that is safe , " said the technology 's inventor , Klaus Lackner , a professor at Arizona State University . <p> SKH 's pilot would be the world 's largest " direct air capture " operation to date , said Jennifer @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Worcester Polytechnic Institute , who is not involved in the project . <p> Carbon capture is gradually gaining momentum , with the United Nations saying in a report last year that the technology is likely needed to keep the rise in global temperatures below catastrophic levels . <p> SKH expects its two-year pilot , possibly in California , to capture about 36,500 metric tons of CO2 a year , it said - the equivalent of nearly 7,750 vehicles driven for a year . <p> Full-scale farms would be 100 times bigger . <p> The company 's " mechanical trees " , as the firm has dubbed them because they are tall and slender and absorb CO2 just like trees , are fitted with filter-like components to absorb the CO2 , a photo of a prototype showed . <p> The device uses wind to blow air through its system rather than an energy-intensive mechanism , it said . <p> While capturing CO2 from industrial facilities and power plants has a decades-long commercial history , " direct air capture " , which pulls the gas directly from the atmosphere is a burgeoning @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> Swiss firm Climeworks has so far led the market , alongside Canada-based Carbon Engineering and U.S.-based Global Thermostat , she said . <p> The companies compress the high-concentration CO2 they capture and then can sell it for use in industrial applications , including making drinks fizzy , creating fuel and extracting oil . <p> While the high price of direct air capture has long been viewed as an impediment to scaling up the technologies , SKH 's costs is less than $100 per metric ton for pure CO2 , it said . <p> " The $100 a ton is important because I think that 's the point where things start to get economically interesting , " Lackner told the Thomson Reuters Foundation . <p> " You can buy liquid CO2 which is delivered by truck in order to fill fire extinguishers and myriad other things for prices between $100 and $200 a ton .
