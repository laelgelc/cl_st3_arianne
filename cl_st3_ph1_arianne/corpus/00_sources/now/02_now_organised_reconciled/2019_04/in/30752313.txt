<p> The Red Fort and Chandni Chowk areas in Delhi being fumigated after a rise in dengue and malaria cases in the capital last year . <p> ( Photo : ANINDYA CHATTOPADHYAY/ BCCL Delhi ) <p> Humankind 's battle with malaria is one of the world 's oldest continuing feuds . Thousands of years since it first began to strike down unsuspecting or helpless victims , this dreaded affliction still threatens about half the world 's population , principally in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia . <p> Malaria favours tropical and sub-tropical regions , so its historic prevalence in India is no surprise . In 1935 , malaria reportedly killed one million people in the country . Thanks to modern science , that number has since dropped to 17,400 fatalities in 2017 , i.e. four percent of the recorded global malaria deaths , according to the World Health Organisation 's ( WHO ) World Malaria Report 2018 . <p> Bear in mind , this is an illness that , according to some sources , may have been first described during Vedic times as the ' king of diseases ' , around 3000 years ago . Once in the body , the parasite attacks liver cells and eventually red blood cells , and feeds off the latter 's contents , all the while multiplying rapidly . Its speed gives it a lethal advantage . <p> A person affected by malaria may experience fever , head and body aches , and chills . Depending on which species of parasite the person gets ( Plasmodium falciparum is the deadliest ) , one could fall severely ill , or if treatment is not started in time , even die from it . We have attacked the causes and spread of malaria from every direction . And to good effect . <p> In 2017 , global malaria cases stood at 219 million , a drop of 20 million since 2010 . Knowledge is power , so here are some ways you can keep malaria at bay in your home and surrounding areas . <p> Keep mosquitoes in check : Fogging and use of insecticides can help control mosquito populations . Check with your municipal authority about mosquito-prevention action in your area , particularly in warmer and rainy seasons and especially focused on wet ground , puddles and rubbish dumps . <p> Fortify your house : Mosquito breed in open , standing water , including clear water that 's been left around for a few days . Keep exposed water tanks , flowerpots and utensils covered , and drain stagnant water immediately . Cover your windows with mosquito-blocking nets . <p> Be careful , especially at night : The Anopheles mosquito is a nocturnal feeder , so if you live in ( or are travelling through ) a region with known cases of malaria , avoid going out after sunset without the proper precautions .
