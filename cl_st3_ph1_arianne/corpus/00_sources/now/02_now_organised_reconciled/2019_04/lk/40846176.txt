<h> Sri Lanka 's geographic conundrum : Lessons for the future <p> Sri Lanka is an island nation in the Indian Ocean located at the tip of the South Asian Subcontinent . Often praised as the pearl of the Indian Ocean , it 's strategically located among important sea lanes of communication that passes through strategic chokepoints including Strait of Malacca , Bay of Bengal and Gulf of Mannar . <p> Sri Lanka is thus located close to important bodies of water , making the island geographically prominent but also geographically vulnerable due to several contentious issues : The Palk Strait conundrum , Sethusamudram Ship Canal Project , and climate change . <p> Sri Lanka 's geographic blessings <p> Historically the Strait of Mannar has been a short cut between the Far East and the Indian Ocean , posited in a manner important for intercontinental shipping . <p> The Bay of Bengal is becoming increasingly strategic owing to several facts : being the largest bay in the world , with important trade routes traversing through it , and being associated with the Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectorial Technical and Economic Cooperation ( BIMSTEC ) . Without a shipping route through the Palk Strait , ships must navigate around the southern tip of Sri Lanka which is a longer route . <p> The Sethusamudram Ship Canal was proposed to cure this deficit . The low-lying coastal lines will be subject to shoreline erosions damaging the coastal eco-systems and livelihoods . <p> Sri Lanka has suffered much during the severe flooding and severe droughts in various parts of the countries .
