<p> The Seychelles president has gone below the surface of the Indian Ocean to call for better protection for the world 's seas . <p> Danny Faure said that a healthy ocean was " crucial for the survival of humanity " in a broadcast made 124m ( 406ft ) below sea level . <p> He had joined a British-led expedition exploring the ocean 's depths . <p> PROTECTED AREAS <p> Last year , the Seychelles created protected areas of the ocean that were " the size of Great Britain " . <p> During the live broadcast Mr Faure could be seen in the submersible wearing a Seychelles T-shirt . <p> He told viewers that the ocean was " the beating blue heart of our planet " and said that it was " under threat like never before . " <p> " We have managed to seriously impact this environment through climate change . I can see the incredible wildlife that needs protection . Over the years we have created these problems , we must solve them and we must solve them together . " <p> PUBLIC SUPPORT <p> The broadcast was part of an expedition by Nekton Mission . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the Seychelles . <p> The goal is to gain public support for the country to protect 30 percent of its national waters by 2020 . <p> It will then explore other areas of the Indian Ocean ahead of a summit in Oxford in 2022 . <p> In February 2018 the Seychelles protected 210,000 sq km ( 81,000 sq miles ) of ocean in exchange for getting some of its national debt paid off . <p> The reserves limit tourism and fishing activities in the country to halt further damage to aquatic life . It was the first debt swap designed to protect ocean areas in the world . <p> According to the UN , only 16 percent of marine waters under national jurisdiction are covered by protected areas . <p> The Seychelles aims to protect 30 percent of its ocean space by next year . <p> Oceans are one of the seven main themes of this year 's UN climate summit in Chile in December . <p> Small island nations like the Seychelles are among the most vulnerable to the rise in sea levels caused by climate change .
