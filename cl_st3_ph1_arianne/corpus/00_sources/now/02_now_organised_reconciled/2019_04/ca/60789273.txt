<h> Investor-state dispute system puts strain on poorer countries : report <p> A new report by the Canadian Centre for Policy Alternatives is raising concerns over the impact of Canadian companies launching investor-state dispute settlement suits abroad on the public policies of poorer countries , particularly policies meant to protect the environment . <p> Investor-state dispute settlement ( ISDS ) systems allow foreign firms to sue governments of a host country in a private tribunal over policy measures that it feels have unjustifiably hurt their investments . <p> Most trade and investment agreements that Canada is a signatory of have ISDS provisions , including NAFTA . The payouts can be in the hundreds of millions or even billions of dollars for a winning suit . <p> The report , published today by the left-leaning think tank , analyzed the practices of Canadian companies that employ ISDS in developing countries and suggests a power imbalance between Canadian corporations and governments of poorer nations . <p> It found Canadian investors have initiated at least 43 ISDS claims against non-North American countries , with almost 70 per cent of claims regarding resource management and environment policies .
