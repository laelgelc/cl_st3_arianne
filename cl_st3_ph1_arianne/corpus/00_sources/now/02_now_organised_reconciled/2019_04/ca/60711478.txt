<h> Port Moody trailers getting sent to wildfires <p> Randy Cowling checks out the interior of one of the wildfire trailers his company is manufacturing in Port Moody . The trailers , equipped with hoses , sprinklers , valves and giant water bladders , are being deployed across the province for use by BC Wildfire Services and various fire departments to combat wildfires . <p> Photograph By MARIO BARTEL/THE TRI-CITY NEWS <p> Climate change might be bad for Randy Cowling 's grandchildren . But it 's good for his business . <p> Cowling 's company , WASP Manufacturing , is launching production of its new wildfire protection trailer at its Port Moody facility on Spring Street . <p> The 8,000-pound trailers are equipped with storage racks and bins that contain everything needed by crews fighting wildfires in remote and interface areas to protect structures and suppress the flames to where they can be managed . That equipment includes up to 7,000 metres of hose , heavy-duty pumps , sprinklers , valves , and splitters , as well as a chainsaw and toolkits for repairs and modifications along the way . <p> Cowling said the trailers are small enough to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ require a special driver 's license and can be maneuvered in and out of the bush and along backroads and trails quickly . Once on the scene of a wildfire , crews can deploy the hoses and sprinklers to dampen the area around a blaze so it does n't alight . Water can be pumped from a nearby lake or stream , or from one of the giant orange bladders in the trailer that can hold up to 3,000 gallons . <p> Cowling said speed and maneuverability are key to fighting wildfires and limiting the damage they can cause . <p> " You have to get it out there as quickly and easily as possible , " he said , adding the equipment can also be used to pump away water from floods . <p> And the need to deploy such specialized equipment is growing as the effects of climate change take hold . Summers are getting drier and winter and spring rainy seasons are getting wetter .
