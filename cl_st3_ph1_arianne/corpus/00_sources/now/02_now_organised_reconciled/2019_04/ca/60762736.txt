( York University/CBC ) <p> A team of researchers at York University has warned that the American bumblebee is facing imminent extinction from Canada , and this could lead to " cascading impacts " throughout the country . <p> The imminent extinction classification is considered the highest and most at-risk classification before extinction . <p> About 42 of the more than 850 species of bees in Canada are bumblebees -- important pollinators needed to grow crops , including apples , tomatoes , blueberries and legumes , as well as trees , shrubs and wildflowers . <p> Professor Laurence Packer said quite a few of the 42 species of bumblebees are exhibiting substantial declines . <p> " We 've got a situation where ... the number of species that you can find in an area has decreased , " Packer told CBC Toronto . <p> These kinds of declines in important pollinators @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ - Professor Laurence Packer <p> He said with fewer bumblebee species around , plants will be pollinated in a different way . <p> Packer explained that bees have different preferences , so when some species are lost , the flowers that they prefer are likely to be less frequently pollinated . <p> " These kinds of declines in important pollinators are going to have cascading impacts throughout the entire ecosystem . The new study relies on the annual data that she and her fellow researchers have collected . <p> The study 's research team -- led by Victoria MacPhail , Colla 's doctoral student , and including a scientist from the University of Vermont -- used data from three sources . <p> They analyzed southern Ontario data from the citizen science program Bumble Bee Watch , a collaboration of volunteers who submit bumblebee photos through a website or phone app for experts to identify . The researchers used the Bumble Bees of North America database to obtain records of bumblebee species in Ontario and Quebec dating back to the late-1800s .
