<h> Flooding , climate change force Quebecers to rethink relationship with water <p> MONTREAL -- Quebec 's history , mythology and culture are intertwined with its rivers and lakes , but recurring floods that experts say will only get worse because of climate change are forcing Quebecers and their political leaders to rethink their relationship with water . <p> In 17th century New France , land was distributed to settlers in long , rectangular strips with access to the river . More than 300 years later , Quebec entered its modern era in large part through the development of its massive hydro-electric potential . <p> Alexandrine Bisaillon , a researcher with the climate change think tank Ouranos , said the province was founded on the belief that water can be controlled . But as increasingly frequent spring floods force evacuations and damage property , Quebecers are being forced to adapt . <p> " It 's a question of whether we want to be masters of water or live with water , " she said . <p> Rivers across the province are being monitored closely and authorities say they fear water levels over the weekend could rise as high as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of homes were flooded around the Montreal area . <p> Jason Thistlethwaite , professor of environment and business at the University of Waterloo , said Quebecers can expect flooding to get worse because climate change is making winter temperatures more volatile . <p> Sudden waves of warm weather followed by quick drops in temperatures increase the risk of ice accumulating on the rivers in the winter , he said .
