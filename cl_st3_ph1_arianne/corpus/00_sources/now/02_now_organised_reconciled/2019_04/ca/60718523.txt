<h> Letters to the Editor : April 9 <h> Helping environment personal choice <p> Kingstonians last weekend had the opportunity to read our MP 's newsletter about the Federal Climate Action Incentive and putting a fair price on pollution . In reading the MP 's letter , I was puzzled about how this plan was going to motivate residents , primary generators of greenhouse gas , in the fight against climate change . <p> Taxes have been increased on gasoline . On the other hand , all Ontario residents will get a federal rebate to compensate them for this cost . As consumers , we are not being asked to pay for the greenhouse gas cost of gasoline . In the past , we have seen higher gas prices , but this did not cause Canadians to choose more fuel efficient vehicles . <p> In 2017 for the first time in history , more than two million new vehicles were sold in Canada . Of these vehicles , 68.6 per cent were SUVs and light trucks . The government newsletter suggested we can save " lots of money in the long run " if we drive an electric vehicle . Well , it seems we like the off-road capacity , additional comfort and space of our SUVs and light trucks more than we like electric vehicles . Chev is cancelling production of the Volt . Some people need these bigger , heavier vehicles , but for many of us it was the luxury , comfort and perhaps status of ownership that drove us to this choice . It seems we have been more concerned about ourselves than concerned about the environment of our children and well-being of others . <p> A couple of weeks ago , I attended a meeting of Kingstonians called 350 Kingston . This is a small group of citizens concerned about climate change . This cross-section of residents was very educated about climate change and the solutions to combat it . Through the couple of hours of emotional discussion , I heard these people talking about how every consumable item and activity we undertake has a related carbon imprint . I heard that the solution to this crisis will take more than the politicians ' suggestion of turning off the lights or insulating the attic -- though both are good ideas . In expressing their concerns and proposed solutions , I heard background stories of personal sacrifice and a level of attention to solving this problem far deeper than I had previously considered . <p> One solution seemed practical and measured . It was suggested that citizens be challenged when buying a new car to seriously consider if they could sacrifice and make do with a vehicle one level more efficient than they currently drive . If they drive an SUV or light truck , could they get by with a sedan ? If they drive a sedan , could they buy a hybrid or electric car ? The government can not fix this problem alone . The problem can not be fixed by giving rebates with dollars that increase the federal deficit . There needs to be buy-in on the part of the Canadian consumer if personal carbon footprints are to be reduced .
