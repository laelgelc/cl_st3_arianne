<h> Ottawa announces stronger preservation measures for chinook <p> Ottawa has announced stronger measures to preserve endangered populations of Fraser River chinook salmon , placing new restrictions on commercial and recreational fishing . <p> Last year , the Department of Fisheries and Oceans aimed to restrict wild chinook harvesting across B.C. by 25 per cent to 35 per cent . But Rebecca Reid , DFO 's regional director general for the Pacific region , said these reductions were not enough to protect the rapidly declining salmon stocks . <p> " Unfortunately , we are at the point where bold action is required , " Ms. Reid said . <p> Story continues below advertisement <p> Of 13 wild chinook populations in the Fraser River , only one was assessed in November by the Committee on the Status of Endangered Wildlife in Canada as not being at risk . The population declines are attributed to the loss of freshwater habitats and warming ocean temperatures due to climate change . <p> The measures announced Tuesday include habitat protection and restoration , improved stock assessment and research on the role of hatcheries and how seals and sea lions may be affecting chinook . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ announced . Commercial troll fisheries for chinook will be closed until Aug. 20 , after migrating populations have passed through . The wild salmon are anadromous , which means they spawn in freshwater river systems before migrating to the northeast Pacific . <p> For recreational fishing , anglers must catch and release in areas where at-risk chinook are found until midsummer . These areas include the west coast of Vancouver Island , the northern and southern Strait of Georgia , the Johnstone Strait and the Strait of Juan de Fuca . Afterwards , they can retain one or two chinook a day until the end of the year , depending on the region . <p> Fraser River recreational fisheries will remain closed for salmon until at least Aug. 23 . Over all , the total annual limit for chinook that one person can retain in a season is being reduced from 30 to 10 . <p> According to Owen Bird , the executive director of the Sport Fishing Institute of B.C. , the recreational fishing sector is invested in improving the protection of wild salmon species .
