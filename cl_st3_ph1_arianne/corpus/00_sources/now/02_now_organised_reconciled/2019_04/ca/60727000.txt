<h> Announcements , Events &amp; more from Tyee and select partners <p> We 're already deep into a new era . Police -- and businesses and other organizations -- have the ability to track citizens in ways unimagined even a decade ago . Social media , the internet , cellphones , algorithms and analytics have given the state extraordinary power to monitor what you think and predict what you might think or do . <p> And governments have taken no effective steps to protect citizens from round-the-clock , random surveillance in the internet age . They have quietly surrendered our rights . <p> The Tyee 's Bryan Carney revealed the RCMP 's Project Wide Awake , its expansion , and the lack of oversight to protect Canadians ' rights . <p> The RCMP , Carney reported , had launched a sweeping project to monitor Canadians ' internet use , especially social media . At first , the goal was to help investigators solve crimes . Then the police decided to expand the monitoring to try and assess whether people might commit crimes . <p> Basic privacy considerations around the expanded program have not been completed . <p> Why should @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ whole new kind of surveillance . They enter keywords like pipeline , protests , Trans Mountain , oil spills , climate change . Officers can cross-reference that list with other searches using terms like Indigenous rights , and other files . <p> And they can share the information -- and the list of names of people expressing opinions and what they say -- with other police forces and agencies . <p> Your interest in pipelines , for or against , or even discussing the issue , could land you in an RCMP file . <p> The risk to the Charter of Rights and Freedoms is n't theoretical .
