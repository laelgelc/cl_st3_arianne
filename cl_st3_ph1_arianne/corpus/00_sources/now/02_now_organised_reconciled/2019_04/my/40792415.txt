<p> Europe remains the biggest region for sustainable investors with about US$14 trillion devoted to these strategies , up 11% from 2016 , according to the biennial report from the Global Sustainable Investment Alliance , which aggregates data from regional sustainable investment groups around the world . <p> Other markets , while smaller , are growing faster . Japan saw the biggest jump , with assets in sustainable strategies up fourfold to US$2.2 trillion . They now represent 18% of the professionally managed money in the market , up from just 3% two years ago . <p> The country 's corporate governance code overhaul in the past few years has encouraged focus in this area and the 150.7 trillion yen ( US$1.4 trillion ) Government Pension Investment Fund signed the United Nations-backed Principles for Responsible Investment in 2015 . <p> Money managers around the globe said clients were increasingly asking for sustainable strategies and that climate change became a leading issue for investors this year . Retail investors bought up more ethical funds , according to the report , and now account for about 25% of assets , up from 20% in 2016 . <p> So-called negative screening , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ investments , such as tobacco , gambling or fossil fuels , remains the most popular strategy . Nearly two out of every three dollars -- US$19.8 trillion in total -- were invested this way in 2018 , the group said . <p> Issues often overlap . Investors who integrate environment , social and governance principles into their portfolios , now represent about US$17.5 trillion , up 69%from 2016 . Assets that focus on corporate engagement and shareholder activism around environmental and social issues rose to US$9.8 trillion in 2018 , up from US$8.4 trillion .
