It will be evident in the geological and fossil record , just as the Age of the Dinosaurs is apparent . <p> In the future , paleontologists will note that wild animals now make up less than 5% of the mass of land vertebrates , with humans being about one third and the rest being our domesticated species -- cattle , pigs , sheep , goats , chickens and so on . <p> But the Anthropocene is more than just a geological phenomenon ; We do not see ourselves as part of the great web of life , but somehow separate from it . We do not value the fundamental role the environment plays in our lives , and thus we exploit and damage it in the name of growth , progress and the pursuit of wealth -- ultimately , to our own incalculable harm . <p> As a public health physician , my concern is with the health consequences of the Anthropocene , both for the population as a whole @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ few decades there has been a growing concern with the so-called social determinants of health ; those social factors that to a large extent determine our health -- income , education , housing quality , food availability , living and working conditions and so on . <p> The World Health Organisation even established a Global Commission on the Social Determinants of Health , which issued its report in 2008 . Its key conclusion , which I strongly support , is that inequality is killing people on a massive scale , and that this is due to unhealthy public policies . <p> But in focusing on these social determinants , we have lost sight of an equally important and in many ways more fundamental set of determinants .
