<h> Our ruined , lifeless planet <p> Thinking seriously about climate change can bring on two distinct but related feelings . One is a sense of the disorienting triviality of most political conversation , like someone fretting about a chipped nail when their hair is on fire . The other -- in the face of ubiquitous single-use plastics , or headlines about vanishing insects , new Arctic tipping points or acidifying oceans -- is despair at the scale and speed of change needed . <p> Assuming you do n't accede to a burn-the-planet hedonic nihilism , the possible responses to the problem of speed and scale fork along two paths -- one legislative , one extra-parliamentary . The legislative path assumes that the power of the state can be used to reform and constrain major emitters ; it is encumbered by the slow-moving machinery of democracy , and the dilution of achievements by powerful hydrocarbon interests and delinquent nation states . XR protesters differ from their recent predecessors in their active courting of arrest , and their ingenuous friendliness to individual officers .
