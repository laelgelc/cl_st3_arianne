<h> Sustainable agriculture in India : the new policies to address the challenges of climate change <h> The Government of India is implementing sustainable agriculture policies under the National Mission of Sustainable Agriculture ( NMSA ) . <p> According to the Joint Secretary , Department of Agriculture , Cooperation &amp; Farmers ' Welfare , Government of India , the government is " Foreseeing the future risks of climate change " which is likely to impact agricultural growth by establishing policies for sustainable agriculture in India . <h> The climate change related agricultural challenges in India <p> The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) notes that agriculture is a contributor to climate change , with annual emissions from agriculture expected to increase further if the global food demand continues to escalate . <p> The director general of The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations , Jos ? Graziano da Silva , said : " Ensuring food security for all is both a key function of , and a challenge for agriculture , which faces ever-increasing difficulties . " <p> According to the Joint Secretary , some of the major challenges faced in districts in India include @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Increased requirement of water for irrigation ; <p> Over-exploitation of groundwater resources ; <p> Declining organic matter in soil ; and <p> Multiple plant nutrient deficiencies . <h> The future of smart agricultural practices in India <p> The Joint Secretary concludes : " As we propagate climate-smart agricultural practices in India , few last-mile delivery mechanisms need to be strengthened ... Analysis and scaling of such measured sustainable agronomic practices should form the benchmark for convergence of schemes in this area of immense future relevance , especially for small and marginal farmers . " <h> The Indian agricultural market <p> Financial Express argues that : " India is primarily an agricultural country , as the sector provides livelihood to more than 50% of the population and contributes 17-18% to the country 's GDP . However , India lags behind many other countries as far as agricultural productivity is concerned ... Agriculture will always remain a booming industry because food will never go out of demand . Hence , there is an immense potential for growth of this sector in India . "
