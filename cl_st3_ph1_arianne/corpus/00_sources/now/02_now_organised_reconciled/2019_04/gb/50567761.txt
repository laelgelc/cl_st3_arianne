<h> Highland nature enthusiasts asked to keep an eye out for rare butterfly <p> Nature enthusiasts in the Highlands are being asked to look for a butterfly which is making a comeback in Scotland thanks to climate change . <p> Butterfly Conservation wants to know where the Comma has been seen in the Highlands this year , so experts can track the butterfly 's progress as it continues to spread northwards across the UK . <p> The Comma disappeared from Scotland nearly 150 years ago , but is now regularly seen in the south of the country . <p> The butterfly made its return to the Highlands in 2004 and since then , there have only been 23 confirmed sightings . <p> Of these , 78% were in Badenoch and Strathspey , including Kingussie , Nethy Bridge and Boat of Garten . <p> The butterfly has also been spotted in Mallaig , Fort Augustus , Daviot and Aberlour , and the first Comma of 2019 was recorded near Grantown-on-Spey in February . <p> Dr Tom Prescott , of Butterfly Conservation , said : " The Comma hibernates during the winter and comes out when the weather is warmer , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in February . <p> " Now that we 've come into spring , we are hoping to see more adults emerging , so we are asking people to keep an eye out for them in April and May and to let us know if they see any . <p> " We think climate change is the reason behind this butterfly moving northwards , so we need to keep track of its progress to learn more . Sightings can be submitted using the free iRecord butterflies app or by contacting your local butterfly recorder . " <p> The Comma visits gardens in the spring , late summer and autumn . The butterfly is easily identified by its ragged wing edges and the distinctive white comma-shaped marking on the undersides of the wings . <p> When at rest , the shape and colour of the Comma 's wings look like a tattered dead leaf , providing camouflage against predators .
