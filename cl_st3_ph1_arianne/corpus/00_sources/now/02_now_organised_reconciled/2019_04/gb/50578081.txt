<h> Adani 's Australian coal mine wins further environmental approval <p> MELBOURNE ( Reuters ) - India 's Adani Enterprises has taken a step closer to the construction of a controversial thermal coal mine in Australia after winning federal government approval for its groundwater management plan . <p> Indian billionaire Gautam Adani speaks during an interview with Reuters at his office in the western Indian city of Ahmedabad in this April 2 , 2014 file photo . REUTERS/Amit Dave/Files <p> Adani wants to develop the Carmichael coal deposit in Queensland state , but has faced fierce environmental resistance that has turned the project into a political hot potato ahead of a federal election due next month . <p> Environment Minister Melissa Price said on Tuesday the project still needed nine further approvals from the Federal and Queensland state governments before mining can start . <p> " This decision does not comprise the final approval for this project , " she said . <p> The groundwater management plan also requires approval from the state government , Reuters was told last week by the state 's environmental department , which is also assessing a management plan for an endangered bird . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ strong support in the region , the ruling conservative Liberal-National coalition and opposition Labor Party both fear losing votes in major cities where voters are worried about climate change . <p> Adani welcomed the approval . <p> " We 're ... looking forward to delivering the thousands of jobs our project will create for people in north and central Queensland , " Lucas Dow , chief executive of Adani Mining , said in a statement . <p> A Queensland government official told Reuters in February that Adani may have to wait up to two years for some environmental approvals . <p> Adani said last year it would fully fund the coal mine and rail project on its own . It did not give an updated estimate of the cost of the mine , previously estimated at about A$4 billion ( $3 billion ) . <p> Reporting by Melanie Burton and Sonali Paul in MELBOURNE ; Additional reporting by Mayank Bhardwaj in NEW DELHI ; Editing by Richard Pullin and Tom Hogue
