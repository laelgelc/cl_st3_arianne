For full details see our Privacy Policy . <p> BEHIND THE SCENES WITH THE UNICORN OF THE SEA <p> We like to call narwhals the unicorns of the sea . <h> Narwhal 's tusks <p> Narwhal 's are arguably the most iconic whale on Earth because of their beautiful counter-clockwise spiralling " horn " , which can be up to 3m long . This horn is in fact a tusk -- a canine tooth protruding from the whale 's upper lip . It 's mostly male narwhals who have a tusk - sometimes they even have two . They use their tusks for fighting and foraging , but the tusk also helps them detect changes in the water around them . <p> Recent footage has discovered a narwhal stunning fish with the side of its tusk . <h> Perfectly evolved to thrive under ice <p> In summer , narwhals can be found in shallow , ice-free bays , but they 're totally adapted to living amongst thick sea ice . They evolved without a dorsal fin @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ heat loss . In the winter , narwhals move deep into ice-covered ocean with very little open water , where they feed on squid , shrimp and fish , possibly at the bottom of the sea . <p> Narwhals are incredibly social creatures , using whistles to call to each other . They form groups of up to thousands when they migrate . <h> Climate Change <p> Killer whales , with their tall dorsal fins , are n't designed for swimming amongst thick sea ice . But melting ice means these predators have a whole new feeding ground to hunt in . And they hunt narwhal . <p> Satellite tracking of both narwhal and killer whales at the same time shows that narwhal behaviour is changing as killer whales encroach , moving them to shallower waters where food is more limited . <p> Narwhals evolved to use sea ice as protection against predators - now hundreds of thousands of years of evolution is worthless . In mere decades their advantage is being lost . <p> Climate change can also cause sudden local changes in weather and sea ice cover , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ whales use to breathe air . Hundreds of narwhals can become entrapped in small openings and die . <h> Ocean Noise <p> Until recently , the Arctic Ocean 's ice cover made it a natural " acoustic refuge " for marine animals for much of the year . <p> Sound travels about 4.5 times faster and 60 times further in water than in air . It can travel the length of 15 football fields in one second . <p> Shipping , infrastructure development and oil and gas exploration cause underwater noise pollution . Since whales depend on sound to communicate , any interference can negatively affect their ability to find food and mates , navigate , avoid predators and take care of their young . <p> Human-made noise is only set to worsen as the Arctic becomes more accessible , and Arctic shipping traffic is expected to quadruple by 2025 . <h> What do we do <p> We 're working to limit noise pollution in Arctic waters by making parts of the ocean off-limits to particularly loud industrial activities . <p> We 're advising communities and governments on the risks @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ created maps and posters for Canadian ships to help mariners identify and avoid marine mammals . <p> We 're tracking narwhals from space -- we 're working with partners to fit satellite radio transmitters to narwhals to investigate their seasonal movements , to understand whether habitat use has changed with changing sea ice , and which areas are vital for protection . <p> We 're looking at how shipping routes overlap with narwhal habitats . If areas are important for raising calves for example , we can use this information to advocate for protection from shipping . <h> From the field <h> Narwhal Camp ... <p> Melanie Lancaster , our Arctic species expert , took part in the 2017 and 2018 " narwhal camp " , in Tremblay Sound in Canada 's high Arctic .
