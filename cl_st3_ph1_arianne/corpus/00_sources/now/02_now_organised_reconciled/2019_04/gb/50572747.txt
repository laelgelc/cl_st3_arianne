<h> Watch : Man swims with huge jellyfish at Chesil Cove <h> Watch : Man swims with huge jellyfish at Chesil Cove <p> If you have been out enjoying the sun down at the beach during the last week , you may have noticed the return of the huge barrel jellyfish to Dorset 's coast . <p> This species is the largest jellyfish found in British waters . They can grow up to a metre wide , two metres long and can weigh up to 35 kilograms . <p> Though common to find along our shores during May and June , they are more of a rare find this time of year . <p> Although their sting is relatively harmless to humans , it is advisable to not approach them because they could be mistaken for other , more dangerous species . <p> These pictures were taken by Wareham-based wildlife photographer and author , Steve Trewhella . He thinks the recent unseasonable weather may have something to do with the jellyfish returning earlier than usual : " This seems to be becoming a more regular occurrence and it is interesting that they have turned up again , especially this @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ after it shot up from the weather we had last week . It is generally warmer than it has been in previous years , so I think it has to be to do with climate change and rising water temperatures . " <p> In 2014 there were record numbers of barrel jellyfish as they swarmed the coast in huge numbers . Steve said : " This is what happened in 2014 , we started seeing them in drips and drabs earlier on but then the next minute there were hundreds and thousands of them . I think we could be seeing similar numbers again this year . <p> " We saw eight in Chesil Cove but we were n't there long and it was quite bad visibility . <p> " People see these amazing and exotic animals on the telly but do n't realise the incredible marine life they have on their doorstep . <p> " They know more about clownfish in Australia than they do about these jellyfish that they can actually see . <p> " I would actively encourage everyone to go down to Portland and just watch @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ you are bound to see them . <p> " If you see one when you are out swimming or whatever do n't throw your arms up in the air , they are virtually harmless and you do n't scream and shout every time some stinging nettles sprout up at the end of the garden .
