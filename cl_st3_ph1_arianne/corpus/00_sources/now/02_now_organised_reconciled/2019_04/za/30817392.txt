<h> #KZNFloods : Adaptable pigeons feed among debris <p> Durban - Pigeons that have been flocking in great numbers to the shallow waters around floating water hyacinth are probably there as a result of the floods having washed out their normal feeding grounds . <p> This is according to Professor Peter Ryan of the FitzPatrick Institute of African Ornithology , who added that it was normal for birds to look elsewhere for " bits and bobs " in such instances . <p> He suggested the birds , regarded as pests , would be quite hardy when it came to sifting their way through the pollution . <p> " They have learned to cope well in a human-adapted environment , " he said . <p> However , he stressed that climate change could lead to greater run-offs of plastic , which affected birdlife in various ways . <p> He said that even well-managed landfill sites would be prone to " losing " garbage . <p> Plastic pollution posed a threat to birds : they were often entangled in things like ropes and cable ties , as well as ingesting plastic carrying toxins and filling up their stomachs so they would @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ said deep sea species , such as petrels , would probably be the most vulnerable to the plastic pollution off Durban , while on shore , gulls were good at regurgitating the unwanted parts of their meals , such as tin foil , plastic and more natural exoskeletons of creatures they may eat .
