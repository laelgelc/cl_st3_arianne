<h> Amnesty International calls for SA to do more to uphold human rights <p> Cape Town -- Now more than ever , there is a pressing need for global leadership that advocates for human rights , and South African leaders can be pivotal in this . <p> These were the sentiments of executive director of Amnesty International SA Shenilla Mohamed as they released the State of Human Rights report yesterday . <p> The manifesto indicated that South Africa still had a long way to go in addressing inequalities , but some progress had been made . <p> " Especially in developing a robust Constitution and progressive legislation to promote and protect the human rights of all those who live in South Africa . <p> " However , it 's the implementation of these laws that is often lacking . South Africa has made significant progress in increasing access to education since the end of apartheid . <p> " Educational participation among 5-year-olds has increased from about 40% in 2002 to nearly 90% in 2017 . <p> " Primary school and secondary school completion rates have increased considerably over time , especially among black South Africans .
