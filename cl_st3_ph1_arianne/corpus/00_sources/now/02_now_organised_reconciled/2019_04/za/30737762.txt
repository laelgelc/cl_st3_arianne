Although Kieswetter takes over only on May 1 , the contradiction could not have escaped those beleaguered taxpayers frustrated again by poor service delivery . The strike is over and the ultimate impact on those affected probably minimal , but the memory of another let-down in service will linger . <p> In a similar vein was Finance Minister Tito Mboweni 's rebuke of the South African National Roads Agency ( Sanral ) for allowing a stay of e-toll prosecutions . The minister seems determined to paint himself as a technocrat rather than a politician -- an extremely difficult task in election time . In addition , the absence of competition and the prevalence of corruption invariably leads to abuse of the user-pays system , as we have seen with Eskom . <p> The above are just a few of the latest anecdotal snippets that show how the social climate changes and becomes warped by electioneering . Despite its laudable goals , electioneering more often than not brings out the worst of the worst , especially in politicians . The harm comes in three categories : perceptions , polarisation and expectations . <p> Perceptions <p> Electioneering rhetoric , hyperbole and hysteria fuel obfuscation , division and confusion -- compounded significantly by fake social media postings to the extent that the Electoral Commission of South Africa ( IEC ) has established a panel to monitor them . Problems are starkly highlighted and solutions disbelieved . It plunges the country into a division between optimism and pessimism ; between hope and despair . In normal times , it would have received scant attention as inconsequential , but in election times -- and against the heated debate around land reform through expropriation without compensation -- it had some impact on the rand , triggering some debate around how far the country will go in confiscating resources from private ownership . The electioneering process is no doubt going to further escalate capital outflows and depress business and consumer confidence . It is simply about perceptions already darkened by load shedding , commissions exposing deep and widespread corruption , civil unrest and high crime levels . <p> Polarisation <p> South Africans are polarised as never before . It lingers and festers . <p> What the country did not need was for the Human Rights Commission to deliver a puzzling exoneration of Julius Malema 's racist rants as " hate speech " , while still deeming them to be offensive and unacceptable . To a rational mind there should be no difference , and it shows the extremely difficult and tricky path we are going to follow in regulating what people can and can not say . It becomes even more difficult when the regulations are vague and tailored to suit intent and context . Laws are never that flexible , and the only real answer is for society as a whole to be repulsed by such utterances and show their displeasure in the moment . We have become known as the ' protest capital of the world ' , and market research firm Ipsos expects an increase as we approach election day . In the majority of cases , protests are fuelled not only by authorities failing to keep promises but by communities ' reactions to the politicians who made these promises in the first place . Promises create expectations , and discontent is fuelled by the gap between these expectations and the reality people experience . The bigger the gap , the greater the discontent . That adds to the trust deficit , which has become far more significant to our economic well-being than the budget and trade deficits . <p> Conversely , when expectations are lower than reality , it creates a sense of well-being and contentment . That utopian condition is never sustainable because reality itself will lift expectations . <p> The real danger lies in both expectations and reality being low . That in turn demands inspirational leadership : not one that makes promises , but one that can engage the nation to aspire to greater things . <p> It is easy to inflame , but more difficult to inspire . The former is the mark of the politician ; the latter the mark of the true leader .
