<h> An immersive experience <p> With climate change buckling up for the endgame one should be concerned about , and trailers coming in thick and fast in the form of erratic weather patterns , rising sea levels and salinity , environmentalists have their work cut out for them in convincing the rest of the world to assemble and tackle this massive problem . The particular challenge of journalists presenting all the arguments is turning the facts and jargon into a concrete experience that makes the readers sit up , take notice , and actually effect a change . <p> The National Geographic 's interactive editorial " What happens to the plastic we throw away " might just be an effective way to get the job done . The publication 's website has used a combination of infographics , satellite imagery , drone videos , maps and models to create an immersive experience that takes the reader across the world , and into the seas , highlighting the scale and scope of plastic pollution , arguably one of the biggest environmental crises we are facing today . <p> The kinetic presentation , which invites the reader to scroll up and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ chunks of data appear on the screen , feels less like a magazine editorial than a video game , and is part of ' Planet or Plastic ? ' National Geographic 's multiyear effort to raise awareness about the global plastic waste crisis . <p> The editorial recently won the People 's Choice award at the 2019 Webby Awards ( " The Oscars of the Internet " ) in the ' Best Individual Editorial Feature ' Category .
