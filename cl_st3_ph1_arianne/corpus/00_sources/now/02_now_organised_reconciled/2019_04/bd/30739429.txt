<h> Climate change blights children 's lives in Bangladesh <p> While monsoon rains frequently cause flooding in Bangladesh , the problem has been exacerbated by climate change , which is now affecting the country 's social development ( AFP Photo/STRINGER ) <p> AFP , Dhaka <p> Environmental disasters linked to climate change are threatening the lives and futures of over 19 million children in Bangladesh , including prompting many families to push their daughters into child marriages , Unicef said today . <p> " Climate change is deepening the environmental threat faced by families in Bangladesh 's poorest communities , leaving them unable to keep their children properly housed , fed , healthy and educated , " the UN children 's agency said in a report . <p> " In Bangladesh and around the world , climate change has the potential to reverse many of the gains that countries have achieved in child survival and development . " <p> Around 12 million of the children most affected live in and around the powerful river systems which flow through Bangladesh and regularly burst their banks , the report said . <p> Another 4.5 million children live in coastal areas regularly @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Rohingya refugee children from neighbouring Myanmar living in fragile bamboo and plastic shelters . <p> A further three million children live further inland , where farming communities suffer increasing periods of drought . <p> Bangladesh 's flat topography , dense population and weak infrastructure have long made it vulnerable to extreme weather events , but experts say that these have become more frequent in recent years as global temperatures have risen . <p> In 2007 , a powerful cyclone killed nearly 4000 people and affected hundreds of thousands more . The most recent major flooding of the Brahmaputra River in 2017 inundated at least 480 community health clinics and damaged some 50,000 tube wells , essential for meeting communities ' safe water needs . <p> The report says that climate change is a key factor pushing poorer Bangladeshis to head to the capital Dhaka and other major cities , where children risk being pushed into dangerous forms of labour and early marriage . <p> It cites research showing that Bangladesh has six million climate migrants already , a number that could more than double by 2050 . <p> The connection @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ access to education is evident in various parts of Bangladesh , according to the report . <p> " Climate change makes people poorer , " said Gyas Uddin , a specialist on child protection issues , " and poverty is a major factor behind child marriage " . <p> Despite making impressive gains in many social indicators in recent decades , child marriage remains rampant in conservative , Muslim-majority Bangladesh . <p> Bangladesh currently has one of the world 's highest rates of child marriage with nearly a third of girls being married before the age of 15 . <p> According to Unicef Bangladesh Child Protection Specialist Kristina Wesslund , climate change is one reason why an estimated 3.45 million Bangladeshi children are involved in child labour . <p> " Climate change is undoubtedly increasing the number of children who are pushed into the workplace , where they miss out on an education and are terribly exposed to violence and abuse , " Wesslund said .
