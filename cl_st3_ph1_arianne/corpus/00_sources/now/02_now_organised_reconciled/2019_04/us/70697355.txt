<p> Ian McEwan 's latest novel , Machines Like Me , is a fiction about science -- specifically , artificial intelligence . It is set in an alternative reality where Alan Turing does not kill himself but invents the internet instead ; where JFK is never assassinated and Margaret Thatcher 's premiership ends with the beginning of the Falklands war . The near future of the real world becomes the present of the novel , giving McEwan the space to explore prescient what-ifs : what if a robot could think like a human , or human intelligence could not tell the difference between itself and AI ? <p> Machines Like Me is not , however , science fiction , at least according to its author . " There could be an opening of a mental space for novelists to explore this future , " McEwan said in a recent interview , " not in terms of travelling at 10 times the speed of light in anti-gravity boots , but in actually looking at the human dilemmas .
