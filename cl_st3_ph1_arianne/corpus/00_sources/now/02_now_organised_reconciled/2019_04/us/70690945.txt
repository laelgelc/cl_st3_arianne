<h> Maths shows the nature of ' tipping points ' for climate and eco crises <p> Humans need to be wary of breaching a ' point of no return ' that leads to ecological disaster such as loss of rainforests or irreversible climate change , according to the most detailed study of its kind . <p> The thin line separating the Earth 's current climate from a frozen one -- the so-called snowball state -- has been explored in new research led by the University of Reading that combines mathematics with climate science . <p> Researchers analysed how random events and human action could combine to reach a tipping point , where one natural state transitions to a very different one . <p> The findings , published today in the journal Physical Review Letters , can be applied to the Earth 's climate , landscape features or ecosystems like a rainforest to aid our understanding of how they can be altered or destroyed after reaching a point of no return . <p> Valerio Lucarini , Professor of Statistical Mechanics at the University of Reading and lead author of the study , said : " Changes in climate or catastrophic @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ fashion similar to a journey in a mountain region . This helps outline a safe operating space , enabling us to tailor our behaviour to remain within this and to realise when a transition could occur . Cutting down trees , damaging ecosystems or altering the climate could all cause us to stray too close to a tipping point , risking dramatic and irreversible change .
