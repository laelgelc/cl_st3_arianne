For Our Contest , We Got Thousands Of Them <p> by Steve Drummond , Clare Lombardo NPR April 6 , 2019 3 a.m . <p> NPR , LA Johnson <p> We asked teachers and students to put on their headphones and turn their ideas into sound for our first-ever NPR Student Podcast Challenge -- and boy , did they . We got nearly 5,700 entries , from all 50 states and the District of Columbia . <p> Podcasts that explored climate change . Podcasts about gun control and mental health . About great books and mythology . Hedgehogs and history . <p> Teachers and their students at 1,580 schools participated : all told , roughly 25,000 students nationwide . <p> Middle schoolers in Denver tackled an age-old subject : bathroom passes . Eleventh graders in Morehead , Ky. , told us about hanging out in the Walmart parking lot . And three fifth graders in Harrison , Ark. , grilled their principal about their school 's shortage of playground equipment . <p> Student journalists took on topics important to them : social media and its impact on youth , smoking and vaping addiction @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ explorations of video games , especially Fortnite . <p> Along the way , at least one student made headlines . For his podcast assignment , a seventh grader in New Orleans did some investigative reporting and discovered traces of lead in his school 's drinking water . As a result , the New Orleans schools are taking action . <p> Now that the entries are in , we 've begun listening to each one . Later this month , we 'll announce two grand prize winners , one from grades 5-8 and one from grades 9 through 12 . And keep an ear out for other standout entries and honorable mentions in the coming weeks . <p> Missed the deadline ? We 're hoping to hold the contest again next year . Teachers , get your podcast ideas ready .
