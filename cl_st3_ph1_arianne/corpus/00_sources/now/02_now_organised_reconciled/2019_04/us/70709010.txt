<p> Greenland , home to Earth 's second-largest ice sheet , has lost ice at an accelerating pace in the past several decades -- a nearly sixfold increase that could contribute to future sea-level rise , according to a new study based on nearly a half-century of data . <p> The findings , published Monday in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences , estimate that Greenland 's glaciers went from dumping about 51 billion tons of ice into the ocean between 1980 to 1990 , to 286 billion tons between 2010 and 2018 . <p> The result is that out of nearly 14 millimeters of sea-level rise in total caused by Greenland since 1972 , half of it has occurred in the past eight years , researchers found . <p> And the ice losses are likely to get worse . The regions with the biggest potential ice loss -- the frigid far northwest and northeast of the island , which sit up against the Arctic Ocean -- have not changed as quickly as other parts of Greenland .
