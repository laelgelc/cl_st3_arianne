<p> Hey , if Matt Damon can go from mopping the halls at MIT to growing potatoes on Mars , then someone , somewhere can figure out how to stabilize the climate of one little planet . With that in mind , let 's take a look at the new GCxN clean tech innovation accelerator , which the US Department of Energy 's National Renewable Energy Laboratory launched last September with a little help from Royal Dutch Shell ( no , really ! ) . <h> What Is This GCxN Energy Innovator Of Which You Speak ? <p> GCxN is short for " Shell Game Changer Powered by NREL . " The basic idea is for Shell to provide financial support and let NREL do the rest . <p> Program manager Adam Duran of NREL graciously took some time out from his planet-saving duties last week to explain what 's going on to CleanTechnica . <p> " There have always been long term relationships between the national laboratories and industry stakeholders , but this one is unique . It 's a technology incubation approach , " Duran said . " Shell is our sponsor but they are letting NREL @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ validate in the lab to determine if these technologies are viable and sustainable . " <p> Unlike other accelerators , NREL does not coach participants on business plans , and it does n't ask for a stake in the company . GCxN is designed to throw a lifeline to innovators who are staring down the mouth of the Valley of Death , meaning they have a great idea and no funding . Duran explains : <p> What makes everything go is that we 're tied into the entrepreneurial community . All too often a researcher will get their first grant in graduate school , but what do you do next ? You have to piece things together , maybe with the help of things like small business grants . In contrast , we provide support for that great idea . <h> On Beyond Pumped Hydro Energy Storage <p> Currently the only available form of long duration , utility scale energy storage in the US is pumped hydro , which is generally source neutral . That 's not the greatest news for skeptics of nuclear energy 's role in the sparkling @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> For those of you new to the topic , pumped hydro literally involves pumping water uphill to an upper reservoir . When an extra jolt of hydroelectricity is needed , gravity ferries the water downhill to waiting turbines . <p> The tricky part is pumping the water uphill , which requires energy . Nuclear stakeholders like that idea . It provides a way to valuate all those atoms jostling around at night when ratepayer demand is low . <p> Nevertheless , renewables can take the bulk energy storage ball and run with it . Pumped hydro provides opportunities to soak up extra kilowatts from wind and solar farms during periods of low demand . <p> This is all well and good , but GCxN is looking way beyond pumped hydro for bulk storage . One idea that caught NREL 's eye last year was a zinc-based chemistry approach from Toronto based e-Zn . The other was a solar powered system from Antora Energy in California , which involves heating blocks of carbon . <p> NREL also threw down an innovation challenge for novel grid control systems . One @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ system that works like a " mini beehive , " from the Israeli company Electrical Grid Monitoring Ltd . The other one , from California-based Feasible , Inc. , is an acoustics-based system aimed at improving battery pack performance . <p> The themes for the next GCxN round are going to be fast charging and grid control , from home systems to microgrids , neighborhood grids , and on up to utility scale , so stay tuned for more this summer . <h> What 's Up With Shell ? <p> Shell is not the only oil and gas legacy company to dip into the renewable energy revolution and other clean tech in recent years . <p> In fact , with some notable exceptions ( looking at you , ExxonMobil ) , the global oil and gas industry is beginning to get super serious about ramping up the energy transition and branching out into electric vehicles , too . <p> " When you look at the strategy and actions of oil and gas companies , they are starting to see that a cost-inefficient competitor is now directly competitive , " Duran @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Wherever the energy comes from , if it meets the customer 's needs in the market , they 're on it . " <p> It also helps that shareholders are beginning to get nervous about stranded assets , but that 's a whole ' nother can of worms . <h> Clean Power In The USA <p> That point about customer demand is a big one , and we 're not just talking about your neighbor down the block . Here in the US , companies of epically big proportions are on the prowl for renewable energy . They are seeking out states where the pickings are good , and they are also figuring out ways to support clean power for smaller companies . <p> One especially interesting example of the energy transition is the very topmost US coal state of Wyoming , where a sudden surge of interest in wind development has been fueled by demand for clean power in the neighboring state of Colorado . <p> If you 're wondering where oil and natural gas fit into this picture , that 's a good question .
