Adrian Wyld / The Canadian Press <p> On Monday , April Fool 's Day , the federal carbon tax kicked in , in the four provinces that do not have their own carbon taxes -- Ontario , Saskatchewan , Manitoba and New Brunswick . <p> The federal carbon tax ( technically the " federal carbon-pricing backstop " ) kicks in at $20 per tonne , rising by $10 per year to reach $50/tonne in 2022 ( Alberta 's provincial carbon tax is scheduled to rise in lockstep with the federal plan ) . <p> Where it will go after that is anyone 's guess . <p> Some economists defend the federal tax , proclaiming it to be " revenue neutral " since some 90% of revenues will be rebated to households in lump-sum payments that somehow will magically actually exceed the carbon taxes paid by households @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ exceeds the tax . ) <p> Because there will be lawsuits over federal carbon pricing ( and a possible imminent court challenge of Alberta 's carbon tax ) , let 's review the necessary attributes of a textbook carbon tax that can justify it 's an " efficient " way to reduce greenhouse gas emissions . <p> First , economic carbon tax theory does n't simply call for some arbitrarily defined " revenue neutrality , " it calls for carbon tax revenues to be used to reduce other economically distortionary taxes such as personal and corporate income taxes . <p> Some economists argue that a border carbon-adjustment system would fix these problems , so imports would face a carbon tax and exports to countries without comparable carbon pricing would be exempt from a carbon tax . <p> But Canada has not done this . <p> While the federal carbon tax includes measures @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the government 's approach targets high-emitters , meaning that service and low-emitting manufacturers will pay more in energy costs thanks to the carbon tax . <p> Finally , importers to Canada will gain the advantage over Canadian producers since the federal plan does not include an import carbon tariff . <p> To make matters worse , exporters who can not pass their extra carbon costs to world markets will likely see their competiveness wane since the federal carbon-pricing plan does n't issue export
