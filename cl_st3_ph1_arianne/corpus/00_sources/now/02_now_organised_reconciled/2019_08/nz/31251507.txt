<h> Wellington regional council declares climate emergency <p> Greater Wellington Regional Council has joined the chorus of local authorities declaring a climate emergency . <p> Today the council voted unanimously to declare a climate emergency . <p> It also voted to set a target for the council to become carbon neutral by 2030 . <p> While the vote was unanimous , some concerns about the declaration were raised during the meeting . <p> Councillor Daran Ponter made it clear the council had no legislative support when it came to putting a " climate change lens " on everything council was doing . <p> Speaking after the meeting , he explained his position . <p> " We have legislation , for example , that says that we can control slope erosion on steep hill country in the Wairarapa , but we do n't have any legislation that says ' you must plant trees because climate change is a-coming ' . " <p> Neither was there any legislation that allowed regional councils to add a congestion charge to control traffic , despite cars being some of the biggest polluters . <p> He said the declaration came with a challenge to central @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and how it would work with local authorities to give them more tools , so they would be able to get the job done . <p> Councillor Sue Kedgely , who was not running for re-election this year , said in declaring the emergency the council would join a global movement . <p> " I think climate declarations around the world are having an effect , people are waking up and starting to understand the gravity of the threat . " <p> Roger Blakeley said the council could not continue with ' business as usual ' , and the cost of inaction was much greater than the cost of taking action . <p> Councillors around the table also spoke about taking a leadership role when it came to reducing climate change
