<h> Gardening Q&amp;A : Follow either of 3 methods for milkweed propagation by seed <p> By Ken Spencer <p> Correspondent <p> Aug 03 , 2019 7:00 AM <h> Ask Ken <p> Q. Couple of years ago , I purchased some butterfly milkweed at Farmers Market in Chesapeake . I would like to spread the butterfly milkweed ; does it go to seed ? If so , when and how do I collect the seeds , and when and how is best way to return them to the ground for sprouting . Looking at Google , I never realized that there were so many different types of milkweed. -- W. Smith , Virginia Beach <p> A. Yes , according to " Monarch Watch , " there are 73 native milkweeds . Many of these , 30 of which are utilized by the monarch butterfly , are either rare , threatened or endangered . And as you may know , this is certainly not good news for the monarch . The Center of Biological Diversity estimates that monarch populations have declined 80% to 90% over the last 20 years . <p> Advertisement <p> In the Midwest , the conversion of native @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ development , has resulted in the loss and fragmentation of the monarch 's habitat and food source -- the milkweed . The use of nonselective herbicides in agriculture has resulted in the ever-increasing loss of milkweed , their host plant , and it has been devastating . <p> Climate change is growing threat that has impacted the monarch by disrupting stable weather conditions and thus the predictable flowering of the milkweed that monarchs depend on to complete migration . Scientists believe that their Mexican overwintering grounds , located in the high altitude mountain states of Mexico , will no longer be suitable for them by the end of the century . <p> So , what about propagating this precious monarch food source ? The plant produces a football-shaped fruit that is a follicle . Follicles are dry , dehiscent ( ones that split , like pods ) fruits with a single chamber containing two or more ( in this case , many seeds ) . These generally ripen late-summer to fall , depending on location . As the follicles begin to mature and dry , they split and release numerous @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ aids in their dispersal by wind . Thus , if you want to collect the seeds , the key is to monitor the capsules as they dry and time your collection before the follicles split , releasing their seeds . Some collectors will tape , band or enclose the follicle to help prevent inopportune dispersal of seeds . <p> After securing the seeds , there are several relatively easy propagation options . First , you could just let nature take its course and let the seeds disperse themselves . Alternatively , you could collect the seeds , separate from the cottony material and disperse them where you would like . Lastly , you could collect and save the seeds , germinate and produce transplants . <p> In any case , it is important to recognize that milkweed seeds have an internal dormant mechanism , the purpose of which is to time germination until the following growing season when conditions are best for their growth and survival . Thus , they need stratification , a cold/moist treatment , of several months to release them from this dormancy and allow germination . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ After collecting and cleaning , the seeds can be planted ( or covered with soil ) to a depth of about a half inch , then with a light mulch . In other words , spread them around as you would like . They will receive their stratification naturally over the winter and should germinate in spring . <p> Should you want to germinate them yourself , you will need to provide for stratification by stashing them in your refrigerator for two to three months to simulate their winter chill . To accomplish , place the seeds in a freezer-grade plastic bag , a on damp paper towel , at 41 degrees Fahrenheit . Next , using a sterile germination media , sow the seeds at a depth of a half inch , lightly cover and mist , while maintaining at 75 degrees Fahrenheit . You should begin to see germination 7 to 10 days . Transplant to larger containers when plants have sufficient roots and top growth . <p> American Meadows ( bit.ly/sowmilkweed ) provides more detailed and nicely illustrated instructions for both methods .
