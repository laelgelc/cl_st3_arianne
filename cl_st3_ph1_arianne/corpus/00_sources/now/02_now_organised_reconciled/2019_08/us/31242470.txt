<p> A housing developer whose project was stopped by Charleston 's new flood rules is suing the city for $8 million . <p> Builders of the Harmony project -- a group of more than 200 homes slated for the flood-prone Church Creek basin in outer West Ashley -- were told by city staff in late January their building plan no longer complied with the city 's rules . <p> A consultant for Charleston previously pegged fill dirt -- a cheap way to raise homes to federally required heights -- as a driver of the flooding on the outer edges of the city . <p> But the Harmony builders , whose project was initially approved in 2015 , say in a complaint filed Aug. 12 that the city 's policy changes have been " arbitrary and capricious , " and have robbed the company of its development opportunity . <p> They argue the rules of four years ago should apply to the project , not the new standards . <p> The city did not respond to the suit directly but said protections from flooding is the focus . <p> " While the city will not comment on the pending litigation @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the protection of neighborhoods and businesses , whether existing now or to be developed in the future , from the many adverse impacts caused by flooding , " Daniel McQueeny , an attorney for Charleston , said in an email . <p> The city has not yet filed an answer to the complaint in state court in Charleston County . <p> It comes as little surprise that Harmony has chosen to challenge the rules .
