Pollination is where insects move pollen from one plant to another , fertilising the plants so that they can produce fruit , vegetables , seeds and so on . If all the bees went extinct , it would destroy the delicate balance of the Earth 's ecosystem and affect global food supplies . <p> There are more than 800 wild bee species within Europe , seven of which are classified by the International Union for Conservation of Nature ( IUCN ) as critically endangered . A further 46 are endangered , 24 are vulnerable and 101 are near-threatened . In fact , honeybees are responsible for only one third of crop pollination and a very small proportion of the wild plant pollination . There are a diverse range of other insects including butterflies , bumblebees and small flies that do the rest of the work -- and it looks like these insects are in trouble too . <p> A bumblebee , pulling it 's weight . This species preys on honey bees , and a single hornet is capable of killing an entire hive . <p> Of course , in the past bees have coexisted with these pathogens . The fact that scientists have seen more bees lost to these diseases in recent times is probably linked with the bees ' increased exposure to pesticides , which can damage their immune systems . <h> Pesticides <p> Pollution -- particularly from exposure to pesticides -- is a key cause of pollinator decline . These weed killers target a huge variety of the wild plants that bees need to forage . <p> The most cutting-edge research suggests glyphosate ( the most commonly used weed killer ) can impact the gut microbes of bees , which can have devastating implications for their health . <p> Although exposure to herbicides and pesticides used by farmers is likely to be one of the main causes of pollinator decline , the chemicals used by city authorities and civilian gardeners might also be harming bees and other insects . So , for the bees ' sake , it 's best to avoid using them where possible . <h> Climate change <p> Global warming is believed to be a major driver of wild bee declines .
