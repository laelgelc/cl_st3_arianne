<h> Technology and spirituality : can they be happy bedfellows ? <p> What can technology learn from spirituality ? For most Wired readers , the default answer is probably " nothing " . But a conference called Wisdom 2.0 -- attended by Ford CEO Bill Ford and Twitter co-founder Evan Williams -- is trying to change that . <p> Wisdom 2.0 has grown very fast in only four years . <p> From its first panel discussion in May 2010 , between Google VP Bradley Horowitz and zen teacher Joan Halifax , the conference has stayed focused on its signature blend of technology and spirituality . In February 2013 Wisdom 2.0 filled the Concourse Exhibition Centre in San Francisco with some 1500 attendees , attracted by speakers including Ford CEO Bill Ford , Twitter co-founder Evan Williams , Huffington Post editor-in-chief Arianna Huffington and members of US congress Tulsi Gabbard and Tim Ryan . In his opening address at Wisdom 2.0 in 2012 , Buddhist teacher Jack Kornfield talks on the case for wisdom in the modern world . In his early twenties Kornfield trained as a Buddhist monk in Thailand , Burma and India before returning to America to complete a Phd in clinical psychology . Through the 1970s he taught meditation in the United States and co-founded the Insight Meditation Society . Kornfield has since become a central figure in the introduction of Therevada Buddhism to Western society , and the spread of " vipassana " or " insight " meditation in California and across America . <p> Western attitudes to Buddhism are coloured by our attitudes to religion , in turn shaped by Christianity and the other monotheistic traditions . Buddhism understands itself not as a religion but as a philosophy . Buddhism as taught by Siddhartha Gautama around 2500 years ago is a non-theistic philosophy and set @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ mind that aims to alleviate human suffering . As such , Buddhism bares more comparison to the psychological theories of Sigmund Freund , or even the political ideology of Marxism , than it does to religion . The teachings of the Buddha include a system of meditation designed to give practitioners greater insight in to the workings of their own mind , and change their relationship to the thoughts that cause suffering in response to pain . This system , stripped of the mythological framework that accrued around it over two and a half millennia , is the basis of mindfulness training today . <p> Of course re-casting our understanding of Buddhism from religion to philosophy does not in itself prove any of its claims . But the mounting evidence of neuroscience might . The Mind &amp; Life Institute , established to create dialogue between the Dalai Lama and leading physicists and other scientists , now connects research and academic study of mindfulness and contemplative science at a wide range of institutions .
