<h> A no-deal Brexit is n't worth the risk <h> Share <p> The heightened rhetoric of the past few days , from talk of collaborators , saboteurs and government of national unity , prompt me to set out what I believe are today 's risks , from one who voted to Remain in the EU , but has accepted the result and voted accordingly in Parliament to leave with a deal ever since . <p> Let me deal with the balance of risks as I see them , saying clearly that this is my opinion , and that there are others contrary to mine . But I 'm telling you what I think and why . <p> Firstly , no deal is economically damaging to the U.K. The publication in the Sunday Times of the Government 's own document on potential effects , ' Operation Yellowhammer ' , suggests a rational awareness of what could happen . Businesses do not know on what terms their goods will be traded . Tariffs will be imposed on some goods , where none exist now . Agriculture and food will be hardest hit . And the U.K. will lose money , which will impact on jobs and investment . <p> If none of the above were true , the Government would have no need to prepare for no deal , and would not be setting up compensation funds to cope , nor working out new freight pathways for medical supplies . <p> So it will hurt , and the only dispute appears to be as to how much and for how long . Why take the risk ? <p> Secondly , there 's the risk to the border between Northern Ireland and the Republic . The U.K. and the Republic will be in different customs and market zones when we leave the EU . Different regulations and tariffs apply and we are a ' third country ' to the EU . This is our doing , as we have voted to leave and chosen to leave the single market and customs union . And Northern Ireland , which also did not vote for Brexit , might increasingly feel its interests are better served within the Republic and the EU . <p> I do n't know whether either of these will come to pass , and am at a loss at polls which suggest Conservative voters just do n't care about the break up of the U.K. , but why take the risk ? <p> Fourthly , there is the political , diplomatic and security relationship with the EU itself .
