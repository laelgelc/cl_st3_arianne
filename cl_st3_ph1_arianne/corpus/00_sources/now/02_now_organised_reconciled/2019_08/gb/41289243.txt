Because the UK has a constitutional monarchy , the ability to make and pass legislation belongs to Parliament rather than the Queen . <p> However , the monarch retains a symbolic role in government . The Queen formally opens Parliament every year , and when the Government passes a bill , it can not become an Act of Parliament until it receives her stamp of approval , a process called Royal Assent . In reality , though , no monarch has refused to give Royal Assent since 1708 , when Queen Anne did so only at the behest of ministers . <p> As such , Queen Elizabeth II 's formal duties are largely representational , such as embarking on goodwill visits abroad and hosting foreign heads of state .
