<p> Ministers have been urged to tackle the " scandal " of falling happiness among children by making schools measure pupils ' well-being every year . <p> The Children 's Society said a short survey of all secondary school pupils would link wellbeing data with factors such as demographics , attainment , school moves , and social indicators like eligibility for free meals . <p> It came as the charity found that a quarter of a million UK children are unhappy with their lives , with happiness at its lowest since 2009 . <p> Their study revealed that the gap between boys ' and girls ' happiness relating to their appearance had narrowed , despite it being traditionally less of a worry among boys . <p> Meanwhile over-13s were significantly less happy with how they look compared to 10-12 year-olds , with one pupil citing the role of Instagram on body expectations . <p> The Good Childhood Report also found a third ( 33% ) of 10-17 year olds have concerns about whether they will have enough money in the future , with more than a quarter ( 29% ) worrying about having a job . <p> Elsewhere it @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ were crime ( 42% ) , followed by the environment ( 41% ) and information sharing online ( 37% ) . <p> The group said a national measurement of well-being for all children aged 11-18 would enable the experiences of young people to be recorded and for issues to be dealt with for the benefit of future generations . <p> Labour said the report should be a " wake-up call " for the Government and said it would support the introduction of such a policy . <p> Mark Russell , Chief Executive at The Children 's Society says : " Modern childhood is a happy and carefree time for most , yet for too many it is not .
