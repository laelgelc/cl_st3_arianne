At the Office for National Statistics we have been working to develop measures of these so-called ' missing capitals ' . Top of that list is the thing that sustains all activity on our planet ; the natural environment . Dr Adam Dutton explains how the ONS is part of a growing international movement that is seeking to find the economic value of our " Natural Capital " . <p> The environment includes every aspect of the system that supports life on earth . That might mean anything from localised air pollution to global climate change . <p> Our natural capital estimates seek to value the natural world in terms of its impacts on people including health , carbon removal and tourism . But as well as valuing the ' stock ' of our natural capital these figures reflect the costs of environmental degradation and the investment needed to undo any damage . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ all the benefits we get from our environment from producing food to being an area for children to make dens . Once we have the list of human impacts we link them to environmental changes , such as how much air pollution is removed by vegetation and then factor in the health benefits . To calculate this we need detailed information on the natural world in the UK and its condition , along with scientific information on the links between various elements . <p> These data give us the information we need to produce ' physical accounts ' . Only then do we move on to the potentially more controversial aspect of assigning a monetary value to those habitats and their impacts on us . <p> We can estimate how much we spend on taking our children to make dens in the woods or how much money the NHS saves in health costs as air pollution is reduced .
