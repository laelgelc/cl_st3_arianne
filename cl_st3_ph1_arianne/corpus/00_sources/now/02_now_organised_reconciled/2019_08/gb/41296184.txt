<p> One of the key roles of transport planners in large cities is to ensure that high levels of mobility are maintained while traffic congestion is kept to a minimum . Sarah Colenbrander and Catarina Heeckt write that accessibility within cities has n't become better despite urban planners ' best efforts . Creative solutions are needed to create better interconnected and concentrated cities . <p> Over the past fifty years , transport planners have tended to focus on reducing congestion to improve people 's mobility within cities . But increasing road capacity , vehicle speeds and parking spaces have not solved urban traffic . Building more roads and parking just attracts more cars and locks cities into expensive , unsustainable sprawl . <p> Today , transport planners are recognising that they need to work more closely with other sectors to improve accessibility within cities . Cities such as Singapore , Bogota ' , Stockholm , Vienna , Hong Kong , and San Francisco offer a high quality of life built around vibrant , walkable neighbourhoods linked by efficient , high-capacity public transport . <p> In these compact , connected cities , people can live , work , study , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ also tend to produce fewer greenhouse gas emissions from transport , so compact and connected cities can also help to tackle climate change . <p> City governments have led the way in creating more compact , connected cities , building on success and learning from each other . Since Curitiba in Brazil piloted the first Bus Rapid Transit system , a further 170 cities around the world have built their own . Since Paris in France and Amsterdam in the Netherlands first introduced bike-sharing schemes , over 500 cities have emulated them . <p> But city governments can not improve urban accessibility on their own . They need supportive policies at all levels of government to encourage mixed-use development , discourage private car use , boost public transport use and make it easy to walk or cycle . National governments have a particularly crucial role to play when it comes to reforming policies and governance systems to nurture compact , connected cities . <p> New research presents the results of a survey of 77 transport experts from 26 countries . Roads are important : they can connect low-density areas , and they can support buses and bikes as well as cars . <p> But national governments can shift their spending away roads towards infrastructure that primarily favours public transport , walking and cycling .
