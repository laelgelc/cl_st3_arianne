<h> Tree watch <p> The tree is the protagonist in the play that compares the cycles of growth of a tree and a human ; Inset : Shrikant Prabhakar <p> By Kinjal Sethia <p> Watch a movement-based performance that explores the relationship of humans and trees <p> Time moves differently for trees , slower than for humans . A 100-year-old banyan tree might well be in its youth . Forests are full of younger trees that are forced to grow up faster due to climate change and global warming . The idea that time as a concept is equally applicable to trees as it is to humans prompted theatre practitioner Shrikant Prabhakar to humanise trees in Tree Story , a non-verbal piece of physical theatre . " Humanising trees makes one realise that their younger generation has basic primal needs , which are not as materialistically inclined as the modern generation of humans . This is the source of the increasing gap between the trees and humans , who were once closer companions , " shares Prabhakar , who has conceptualised and directed Tree Story , which will be presented by Dhyaas Performing Arts in the city today . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is the protagonist , the play compares the cycles of growth of a tree and a human . The story of the tree starts from a seed , then it germinates and grows until ultimately it is cut down for human needs . On the other hand , the growing human 's needs are nurtured by materialistic things , which stunts human sensibilities towards the natural environment . " People have become so selfinvolved in their daily routine that they do not find time to connect with nature . Even if someone wants to do something for the environment or for animal welfare , they can not do it as they are trapped in the cycle of their needs , " says Prabhakar . The 33-year-old director previously directed other works of physical theatre , such as The Last Colour and The Transparent Trap , which were also centred around environmental issues . <p> As trees are deprived of verbal language , the format of physical theatre seems apt to portray their story . Primarily using movement , mime or dance , physical theatre is a highly visual form of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Madhura Joshi , a Kathak artiste who plays the role of the tree in Tree Story , has blended contemporary and classical dance for the portrayal of the protagonist . " We all know the tragic consequences of deforestation , and yet we do very little for the cause of environmental protection beyond talking . The play makes a comment that as analytical , intelligent beings , humans should fulfil their duty towards nature , " says Joshi , 24 , who was trained in dance by Pandita Vidya Hari Deshpande and later at the Gandharva Mahavidyalaya . <p> Movement is the source of the play 's sound too , generated from everyday actions and objects . " Sounds from claps , Velcro material , tin boxes and Djembe beats are used to interpret moods playing out on the stage , for instance happiness or rage , " says Omkar Kudale , 25 , a Djembe player who has designed the live sound for Tree Story . Apart from the live sounds , recorded music is employed in the play to create an ambience . <p> As the story @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ they too are a part of the natural world . The tree envisages its death , but the story ends on a note of hope , that humans can still rectify their mistakes and save the forests in time .
