<p> With summer comes longer days and sunnier skies . It 's an opportunity to shed our winter clothes and get outside to go for a run , get on a bike or play pick-up sports with friends . Indeed , summer is when we are most active . <p> The other thing that comes with summer is heat and humidity . As Europe and North America grapple with recurring summer heatwaves , we all need to take care when being active in the summer heat . <p> In addition to sweat , blood is diverted to our skin 's surface to cool and recirculate throughout our body . It 's the reason why many of us become flushed in the face when we 're active . <p> How much each of these two methods contributes to cooling can vary from person to person . Some people are profuse sweaters while others turn red and hardly sweat at all . <p> The effectiveness of our body 's cooling also depends on the ambient conditions . Diverting blood to our skin to cool means less blood ( and oxygen ) going to the working muscles . <p> Sweating also reduces the amount of water in our body and if this lost fluid is not replenished , blood volume goes down . This can lead to lower blood pressure and increased heart rate . At the very least , this results in a decrease in performance .
