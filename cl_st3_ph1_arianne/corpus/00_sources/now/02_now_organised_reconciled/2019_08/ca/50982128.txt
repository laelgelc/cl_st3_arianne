<p> This month , the UN published the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change report that highlights plant-based diets as our best weapon against the climate crisis . Specifically , reducing the quantity of meat in our diets could have a significant impact on both our health as well as that of the planet . This dietary shift could not only free up millions of square kilometres of land by 2050 -- which could be put to better use given the current rate of population growth -- but it would also cut anywhere between 0.7 and 8.0 gigatonnes of carbon dioxide emissions a year . <p> The reduced consumption of meat is a trend we are already seeing in Canada . According to a food consumer report from the University of Guelph , almost 85 per cent of Canadians are eating at least one main meal per month without animal protein . The report also notes that consumers have become increasingly concerned with the source and method of production behind the food they consume . " Alongside vegetarians and vegans , " meat minimizers , " " conscious carnivores " or " flexitarians " -- those who still eat meat , but are consuming less of it -- are driving changes in meat consumption . Flexitarianism allows for a shift toward a regime that can involve more people and be less rigid about the rules of meat consumption . <p> In an effort to meet rising consumer demands for the plant-based diet , restaurants are adapting their menus and grocery stores are welcoming more meat substitutes . This past April , Beyond Meat , a plant-based substitute for the most popular meat products such as sausages and burgers , has made its way into both markets . It is now readily available at a variety of grocery stores such as Loblaws and Safeway , as well as in restaurants such as White Spot , A&amp;W and Earls . <p> Even McDonald 's and Tim Hortons are adjusting to meet the demand for meatless patties .
