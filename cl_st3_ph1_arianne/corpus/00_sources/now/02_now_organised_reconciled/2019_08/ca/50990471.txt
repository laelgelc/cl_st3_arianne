As of this summer , only 2.2 per cent of new car sales in Canada are of zero emission vehicles ( ZEVs ) . Norway , on the other hand , has the highest rate of ZEV sales , at 58 per cent . <p> In an attempt to boost sales of ZEVs , earlier this year the federal government launched a financial incentive program that provides rebates of up to $5,000 for eligible vehicles . The government 's modest goal is for these vehicles to reach 10 per cent of sales by 2025 . Hence , even in an optimistic scenario , 90 per cent of new vehicles on the road will still be polluting @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ serious program in place to incentivize those who buy gas-based cars to opt for less polluting ones . This must change . <p> Without financial incentives to avoid the most polluting cars , Canadians are choosing trucks and SUVs . According to Driving.ca , only 10 years ago passenger cars accounted for more than 50 per cent of all new vehicle sales in Canada . But an obsession for big vehicles , coupled with moderate gas prices and economic growth , led to a decade of ever-increasing sales of trucks and SUVs . Today , the market share for sedans is only 25 per cent of total car sales . And the two best selling vehicles in Canada are highly polluting pickup trucks -- Ford F-series and RAM . <p> Although it is little known , there is actually an excise tax on " fuel-inefficient vehicles " in Canada . This dated tax was designed back in 2007 and , amazingly , was never updated . It states that automobiles that have a weighted average fuel consumption rating of 13 or more litres per 100 kilometres are subject to an @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the tax is so poorly designed that it has no effect whatsoever on people 's choices . <p> First , very few vehicles are subject to it . Out of a list of 873 vehicles that were sorted based on their fuel efficiency , only 51 are subject to some tax , with the majority subject to the minimal $1,000 tax . Only four vehicles are taxed at the highest amount of $4,000 . One of those four vehicles is a Bugatti Chiron with a price tag of $2,980,000 . Does anyone in his right mind think that a person who is about to purchase a vehicle for nearly $3,000,000 would care about a $4,000 tax ? This must be a joke . <p> Even worse than that is the fact that pickup trucks , which are the most polluting vehicles , are completely exempt from the excise tax . This is absurd . Sure , some people are buying them for their business or for work in the farm . But the vast majority are buying them for fun , completely ignoring their environmental impact . <p> A mid-size @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ litres of gas per 100 km . The government has put in place incentives to increase sales of ZEVs , but switching from a Honda Civic to an electric car has almost the same impact as switching from a Ford F truck to a Honda Civic in terms of emissions . <p> Based on recent data reported by the International Energy Agency , Canada has one of the highest average fuel consumptions and carbon dioxide emissions per kilometre driven in the world . Given that and the fact that gas-based cars still account for 98 per cent of sales , a tax must be introduced to incentivize drivers to choose less polluting vehicles . The magnitude of the tax should be a function of the fuel-efficiency of the vehicle . More pollution equals higher taxes .
