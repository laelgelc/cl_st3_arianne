<p> On an unnamed island , in an unnamed country , many kinds of things are disappearing , a phenomenon that seems to be accelerating since it started , about 15 years before this novel opens . It takes in whole classes of things : birds , ferries , photos . Put like that , the premise of The Memory Police -- a novel published in 1994 by the Japanese author Yoko Ogawa but only now translated into English , by Stephen Snyder -- sounds as though it could extend in nearly any direction . It may stand in for fascism ; mass extinction and climate change ; the heat death of the universe ; the ugliness of ageing ; cultural memory and national forgetting ; concentration camps : you name it . <p> While fascism takes the main share of the metaphorical territory , The Memory Police also performs the magic of extending in all those ways at once , which has its risks and benefits -- one of the latter being that risky fiction is often inherently interesting , whether or not the reader judges the risk to have paid off .
