<h> US conservatives embrace carbon tax : Is there a message for Australia ? <p> With the Trump presidency now underway , this would seem to be a singularly unpropitious moment to revive the idea of a carbon tax to address global warming . Yet this seemingly spitting-into-the-wind proposal has been put forward by a high-powered group of Republicans , including James Baker , George Shultz , economic heavyweight Martin Feldstein and top academic Greg Mankiw . <p> This has not only had widespread backing from other Republicans ; leading Democrat economist Larry Summers has given the idea unstinting endorsement . And there is a political economy lesson here for Australia . <p> We suggest a solution that rests on four pillars . First , creating a gradually increasing carbon tax . Second , returning the tax proceeds to the American people in the form of dividends . Third , establishing border carbon adjustments that protect American competitiveness and encourage other countries to follow suit . Even so , there is a powerful incentive to reduce carbon emissions . Individuals can reduce their carbon tax by cutting back on carbon emissions and still receive the same dividend : the harder you work on getting your emissions down , the more you become a net cash beneficiary . <p> Republicans have an innate dislike of progressive taxes , but might be attracted to the income-distribution implication of the proposal .
