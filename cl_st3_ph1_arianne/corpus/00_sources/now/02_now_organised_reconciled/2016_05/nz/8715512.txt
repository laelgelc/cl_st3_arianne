<h> Cause and effect <p> Pulitzer Prize-winning novelist Jane Smiley is the author of dozens of novels and non-fiction works . Her latest novel , Golden Age , is the final instalment of a multigenerational trilogy set in 20th-century America . ODT book reviewer Jessie Neilson questions her before her Dunedin appearance next week . <p> Q Your output includes novels for adults and children , short story collections , essays and non-fiction . Like Pulitzer prize-winning A Thousand Acres , " The Last Hundred Years ' ' trilogy is set in the rural Midwest , an area you clearly know intimately . What prompted such an ambitious undertaking at this particular stage in your career ? <p> A I did n't really think of it as ambitious , only as interesting and worth doing . I am interested in food , cooking , climate change , farming , politics , and the decline of the US . To tell that story , and thus explore cause and effect seemed like a good idea , not so much ambitious as fascinating . Then I got really interested . <p> Q The trilogy follows members of the Langdon clan from 1920 , and their descendants ' lifestyle choices , crises , mishaps and exuberances until 100 years later . What a remarkable achievement to keep such a firm hand on multiple characters as we follow them through year by year . What did your pre-writing phase entail in order to map out the step-by-step maturing of so many characters so convincingly ? <p> A The main thing , to begin with , was to think about how each of the characters would have been as infants . For a few of those years , I lived outside of town .
