<h> Breaking News <h> Climate change sinks islands in the Solomons <p> Five entire islands in the Solomons have gone underwater in recent years , and six more are slowly being swallowed . <p> Scientists combined satellite and aerial imagery dating back to the 1940s with local knowledge and data , to show how rising sea levels and coastal erosion have eaten away at the Pacific island chain . <p> Writing in journal Environmental Letters , they say the sea around the Solomons has risen between 7mm and 10mm a year since 1994 . <p> The study focused on two areas , Isabel and Roviana , consisting of 33 islands . Of the original 20 reef islands in Isabel , five had been completely eroded away . Another six were 20 percent or more smaller than they were in the 1940s -- two of them losing more than half their size . <p> Of those in Roviana , six increased in size slightly ( due to improved coral cover ) and six decreased . <p> More than half the houses on the 33rd island , that of Nuatambu @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ damage was incremental -- not the result of a particular adverse weather event -- and happening " over several years " . <p> In 1962 it measured just over 30 square kilometres . In 2014 , it had shrunk to just under 14 . <p> How Nuatambu has shrunk over the years ( supplied ) <p> Some residents have been forced to relocate to neighbouring volcanic island Choiseul , but others have built temporary housing further inland . <p> In Mararo , a village on Malaita , the entire community decided to up sticks and move 20m further inland . <p> " The sea has started to come inland , it forced us to move up to the hilltop and rebuild our village there away from the sea , " Sirilo Sutaroti , 94-year-old chief of the Paurata tribe , told the researchers . <p> Sea levels are rising faster now than they were mid-20th century .
