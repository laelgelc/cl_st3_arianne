<p> Jenny Jiva , a master 's student from the University of the South Pacific in Suva , is giving Pacific climate change a voice on the world stage . <p> Her master 's research concerns the loss and damage impacts related to climate change , as an issue , which can include the loss of livelihood , territory and property . <p> Jiva 's research focuses on the Pacific 's role in getting loss and damage issues on the negotiating table , and successfully into the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change ( UNFCCC ) outcomes and documents . <p> In 2015 , the 23-year-old was selected as a country delegate to represent Fiji at the COP21 climate change conference in Paris , a global meeting where world leaders reached a legally binding agreement to address climate change . <p> Fiji 's Jenny Jiva in Paris for COP21 . " <p> A goal for Jiva is to attend COP22 in November this year , which will be held in Marrakech , Morocco . <p> She told Asia Pacific Report that this year the conference would be reviewing the Warsaw International Mechanism for Loss and Damage , a policy that aims to address climate-related loss and damage in developing countries vulnerable to extreme effects of climate change . <p> In 2013 , in Warsaw , the Pacific fought strongly for this mechanism , she says . <p> The young activist is also a member of Pacific Islands Climate Action Network ( PICAN ) , an organisation that brings together Pacific NGOs and civil society actors who advocate for climate change . <p> " Climate change is a very real thing , we now know that it is happening , it 's not debatable anymore , " she says . <p> TJ Aumua is Pacific Media Watch freedom project contributing editor for the Pacific Media Centre ( 2016 ) .
