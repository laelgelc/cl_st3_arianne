<h> Climate may have defeated Mongol invaders <h> Climate fluctuation not only may have paved the way for Genghis Khan 's conquests of Asia in the 13th century , sudden climatic change may also have halted the Mongol invasion of Europe , according to new research . <p> Two scholars - one skilled in historical documents and another in interpreting tree rings to deliver weather reports through history - say that cold and heavy snowfalls may have blighted the pastureland of the Great Hungarian Plain in 1242 . <p> This would have produced marshy conditions that would have made it difficult or impossible for 130,000 horsemen to campaign or even survive so far from home . <p> Genghis Khan 's vast but fleeting empire began in 1206 , when the leader united the Mongol tribes , and by 1279 one hitherto impoverished group of nomads had swept across China , Russia , central Asia and Iran .
