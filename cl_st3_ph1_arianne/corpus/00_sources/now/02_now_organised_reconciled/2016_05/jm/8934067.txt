<h> Columns <h> Straightening out the land we love <p> IN a few days we will be called upon to give a little " time and sweat " to straighten up as much of the environment as we can for what we still call Labour Day . Would n't it be great if we could really show some interest in straightening out this Jamaica , land we love ? Seriously now . <p> One of the reasons , I believe , why we have been letting things go is that many of us have very little knowledge of what is happening around us . Most of us seem to believe that " farrin better than the land of our birth " . Well , Labour Day used to be a time of pride , to get together and put things right in our communities . It ai n't necessarily so now . For some people , it is easier to blame " di Govament " than to do for ourselves . <p> In a recent drive along the eastern coast , I was looking at the hills which @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Kingston , and I noted two huge white wounds from scarred hillsides . How much will the rock take ? For how long will the hills endure ? Thick white dust is everywhere . What of public health regulations ? <p> Best known to me is the area called Bull Bay . It should be interesting to know how residents of the communities manage a daily battle between dust and lungs . What protection is given to the communities ? <p> Businesses and residences alike are coated with the white , dusty cover , thicker than I remember . However , life still goes on for people who are survivors . Despite inconveniences , construction of houses and shops of varying sizes are developing rapidly . On one hillside , as we proceeded along the road , a large sign announced the opening of a hotel . <p> Water is a necessity that is still not accessible to all . How people manage in the continual drought conditions raises questions , but people are too busy going about their business to spend time answering . I seem to remember that , at one time , hope was being set on the harvesting of krill , that tiny sea creature , which could be used to feed pet fish , and serve other purposes which could earn us revenue . The usual talk-talk kept going for a while , then faded like the white foam on the surface of the ponds . <p> The road surface , near Pomfret , has faced its own trials over the years with the dissonance caused by drivers of big trucks overladen with gravel , sometimes called shingle , used in the construction industry . It can test the sanity of any driver to be trapped behind a truck from which , annoyingly , pebbles spill . Damage to the road surface has also brought argument and promises to do this , do that . Where has the water gone ? Why diminished so much ? As for the beaches , it is painful to see how much of the sand has gone , how dull and dreary the area has become . Picnics are but memories of the past . <p> Question : What if a day came when the sea carves so deeply into the land to the extent that the road is no longer there ?
