<h> Shell CEO warns renewables shift could spell end if too swift <p> THE HAGUE : Royal Dutch Shell can not switch too quickly to producing renewable energy without risking its dividend payments and even its very existence , the oil and gas group 's chief executive warned . <p> Major investors , including Dutch pension fund PGGM , have criticised Shell 's climate change policy in recent months , saying it should do more to mitigate climate change risks . <p> However , 97 percent of Shell shareholders at its annual meeting on Tuesday rejected a resolution to invest profits from fossil fuels to become a renewable energy company . The Anglo-Dutch firm had previously said it was against the proposal . <p> Chevron and Exxon face similar climate change resolutions at their annual meetings on Wednesday , highlighting growing investor concern about oil and gas companies ' exposure to a warming climate after world powers agreed to tougher emissions cuts in Paris last year . <p> Shell Chief Executive Ben van Beurden said that the oil and gas industry will nevertheless need to invest up to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ goal of curbing carbon emissions to limit the rise in global warming . <p> Making a switch to other forms of energy would take time , Van Beurden said , adding that all the top 10 solar companies in the world represent $14 billion ( 10 billion pounds ) in capital employed and invested $5 billion in solar energy last year , but none had so far paid any dividends . <p> " We can not do it overnight ( transition to renewables ) because it could mean the end of the company , " he said . <p> And growing demand for oil and gas in emerging economies means investments in the oil industry will have to continue . <p> " It will take an unprecedented amount of effort to bring about a net zero emissions future , " he said . " If collectively we find a way to stay within the 2 degree ( Celsius limit ) , we will still need significant investment in oil and gas ...
