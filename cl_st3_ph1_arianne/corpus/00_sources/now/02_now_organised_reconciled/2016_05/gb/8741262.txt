<h> Kew Gardens plant study reveals more than 2,000 new species <p> More than 2,000 new plant species were discovered last year , the first study of the global status of plants has found . <p> The " state of the world 's plants " report by the Royal Botanic Gardens , Kew , estimates there are 391,000 vascular plants - which exclude mosses and algae - in the world , with 2,034 new species found in 2015 alone . <p> New finds include a huge insect-eating sundew discovered via Facebook , five new types of onion , a three-metre ( 10ft ) tall slipper orchid , and a close relative of the sweet potato . <p> But the report warns that many plants are living on borrowed time in the face of climate change , as they respond only slowly to rising temperatures and changing rainfall and have limited ways they can adapt . <p> Many plants are already in " extinction debt " , it suggests , hit by global warming but with the true impacts not set to be revealed for some time . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , with increased plant growth or " greening " of the Arctic since 2001 and elsewhere loss of mangroves and forests . <p> Some 1,771 areas of the world have been identified as " important plant areas " ( IPAs ) , but very few have conservation protection , the study said . <p> In the UK , which has important habitats such as Atlantic woodlands and Celtic rainforests , 165 IPAs have been identified , from the Lizard Peninsula in Cornwall to the west coast of Scotland , while work is being done to identify sites in UK Overseas Territories . <p> Globally there are 4,979 invasive plants , with the problem of non-native species such as cheatgrass , spotted knapweed and Japanese knotweed costing ?
