If you know that ocean acidification is caused by carbon pollution from burning fossil fuels and cutting down rainforests , you 're practically a scholar . " Unlike with topics such as climate change or air pollution , this is not an issue that has been covered much in the media . " <p> The world 's oceans sequester around a third of the carbon dioxide emitted into the atmosphere . And as global society has emitted more CO2 , the oceans have kept pace . But that comes with a big price : seawater chemistry is literally changing . Over time the carbon in the oceans turns into carbonic acid , lowering pH levels and making the water more acidic . Over the last thirty years , the ocean 's acidity has jumped 30% . <p> Although ocean acidification has been occurring since the beginning of the Industrial Revolution , it has only been studied in depth recently . <p> " Ten or fifteen years ago @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ progress has been made on public understanding , " Phil Williamson with the UK Natural Environment Research Council based at the University of East Anglia said , referring to the poll . Williamson was not involved with the study . <p> Ocean acidification is expected to hurt a huge wide swathe of marine species and potentially undercut the workings of whole ecosystems . Marine species that depend on shells -- via calcification -- are likely to be hit hardest , including molluscs , crustaceans , corals and some plankton . In 2014 , scientists found that the shells of tiny marine snails were already dissolving due to ocean acidification and a study this year found that coral grew more slowly in waters with lower pH . Over 96% of marine species were obliterated . <p> Despite all this , only 14% of Britons know a little or more about ocean acidification , according to the psychologists , who interviewed 2,501 participants . The poll also found that respondents often held incorrect views of ocean acidification , for example , that it is caused by oil or chemical spills and not carbon emissions . While research on ocean acidification has exploded over the last 15 years , scientists said that it has clearly not trickled down to the public yet . <p> " The UK government and media ca n't be accused of dropping the ball in communicating ocean acidification , since arguably they have never really picked it up in the first place , " Williamson said .
