Photo : AP <p> Beijing : India and China on Thursday decided not to allow mutual differences to affect their bilateral relations . This emerged after talks between President Pranab Mukherjee and his Chinese counterpart Xi Jinping in Beijing on the third day of the former 's visit to China . <p> Mukherjee also met Premier Li Keqiang and Zhang Dejiang , chairman of the standing committee of China 's National People 's Congress . <p> Foreign secretary S. Jaishankar said in Beijing that the two sides held " open , constructive " talks on all irritants that had upset ties in the recent past , including China 's veto on India 's membership of the Nuclear Suppliers Group ( NSG ) and China putting a technical hold on India 's efforts to have Pakistan-based militant leader Maulana Masood Azhar declared a terrorist . <p> " On the larger relationship , the phrase that I would say @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a strong foundation on mutual trust , " Jaishankar said . " The talks were very comfortable , very candid and there was an acknowledgement that as neighbours , we will have differences , but the key was to manage the differences and expand the convergences , the common ground that we have . " <p> Chinese foreign ministry spokesperson Xiao Qian told reporters after the Xi-Mukherjee talks that the two leaders decided to carry forward the strategic comprehensive partnership to new heights . " For example ... on the political front , the momentum of our leaders ( meetings ) will be maintained ... and we will work to improve our bilateral relationship ... of the dialogue mechanism between our two countries to improve mutual trust , " Xiao said . <p> Mukherjee 's visit is the first in a series of high-level interactions between India and China in 2016 . Prime Minister Narendra Modi is set to visit China in September for the G20 summit while Xi will visit India in October for the BRICS summit . <p> " On the disagreements between our two countries , the two @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ but at the same time be realistic . It means that we will well manage the issues that can not be agreed on a very short time so that these disagreements will not stand in the way of our development and cooperation , " Xiao said . <p> One of the irritants that upset India involved China raising objections to India 's application to become a member of the NSG that controls global nuclear commerce . India believes this is at the behest of Pakistan , which is also keen to become a member of the grouping . China and Pakistan have a publically professed " all-weather friendship " . <p> According to China , a country that is not a member of the nuclear non-proliferation treaty ( NPT ) -- like India -- can not be a member of the NSG . India has cited the example of France , which became a member of the NSG prior to signing the NPT . <p> According to Jaishankar , Mukherjee raised the nuclear issue in the context of predictability of global rules in nuclear trade . President ( Mukherjee ) asked President Xi to give it his personal attention , " Jaishankar said . <p> Another irritant has been China putting a " technical hold " on India 's attempt to designate the chief of Pakistan 's Jaish-e-Mohammed ( JeM ) militant group at the UN . India accuses the JeM of carrying out the 2 January attack on its Pathankot airbase . The Chinese have sought direct dialogue between India and Pakistan on the issue . <p> " Essentially on terrorism , what was conveyed by the president was that this was a global fight , that all countries , including India and China , were affected by this and that there was a very strong @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and trans-border terrorism did require international collaboration and that there was no such thing as ' good terrorist ' and ' bad terrorist ' ... we must have an understanding bilaterally and multilaterally to fight terrorism , " said Jaishankar . <p> The discussion also focused on maintaining peace and tranquillity on the border besides border management , he added . <p> Despite countless rounds of talks , the two sides have not been able to resolve their border disagreement that stems from a brief but bitter war in 1962 . <p> On trade imbalance , both leaders agreed substantial investments from China into India can provide a solution . China evinced interest in many of India 's flagship programmes like Make in India , Digital India and Smart Cities , said Jaishankar.
