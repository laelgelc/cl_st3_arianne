<p> New findings show that heat stress nephropathy--chronic kidney diseases--are on the rise , especially in many rural communities in hot regions . <p> With rise in temperature worldwide , dehydration and heat stress are likely to take a toll on the kidneys , emerging as a major cause of poor kidney health in the near future . <p> " A new type of kidney disease , occurring throughout the world in hot areas , is linked with temperature and climate and may be one of the first epidemics due to global warming , " said Richard Johnson , from the University of Colorado in the US . <p> Also , global warming and a rise in extreme heat waves have increased the risk of kidney disease , especially for the agricultural workers , who are exposed to the heat for longer duration . <p> Decreasing amounts of rain contribute to the growing epidemic of the chronic kidney disease consistent with heat stress--by reducing water supplies and quality as temperatures rise , the researchers noted . <p> " We were able to connect increased rates of chronic kidney @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ dehydration--and to climate , " Johnson said . <p> The findings will be detailed in forthcoming issue of the Clinical Journal of the American Society of Nephrology ( CJASN ) . <p> Governments and scientists need to work together to learn more about this threat and take action to reduce the risk of climate change-linked kidney disease , the researchers said .
