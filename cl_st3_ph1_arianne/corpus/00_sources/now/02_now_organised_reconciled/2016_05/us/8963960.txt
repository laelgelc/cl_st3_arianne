<h> SHARE <h> Environmental groups should come to the aid of coal country <p> After working in environmental advocacy for more than 25 years , I 've begun to believe that many of us who are fighting with all of our might against climate change and for a transition to a clean energy future are not thinking about rural communities in the right way . We have not prioritized the people caught in the transition as much as the transition itself ; we have often failed to acknowledge the workers and families who have mined our coal and generated our electricity for more than 150 years . <p> It 's time that environmentalists have a come-to-Jesus moment about coal and the communities that have traditionally depended on it . <p> Let me back up , and state a few facts for the record . First , coal is on the way out and market forces are driving that shift . If there is a " war on coal , " it 's being waged by natural gas companies via cheap gas and coal industry executives ' own bad @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and even fatal health problems , like particulate matter in the air that can lead to lung and heart diseases . This is why regulations to clean up pollution from energy infrastructure are valuable , and I have supported them in the past and will continue to do so in the future . Third , climate change is happening . Our winters are shorter and warmer and our weather is getting more extreme . Anyone who tries to convince us otherwise is peddling false doubt for their own gain . For the sake of our kids and grandkids , it is essential we beat the threat of climate change . <p> Given the imperative to shift to cleaner sources of energy , it is also time that all of us in the environmental sphere put real energy and resources into partnering with communities on how they can not only weather , but thrive and make progress in the 21st century economy . <p> This starts with saying thank you . Thank you to the men and women whose work keeps our lights on . Thank you to the miners who pioneered @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for making our nation a leader throughout the industrial revolution and two world wars . And thank you for helping to make Colorado , the West , and America what it is today . <p> Then , it means supporting policies that empower communities to adapt , thrive , and get a fair shot . I 'm proud to announce that over the coming year and beyond , my organization , Conservation Colorado , will devote more resources to fighting for policies that support rural Colorado . We will make rural economic revitalization one of our top priorities for the 2017 legislative session , and will work to pass bills that provide access to financial support and other tools with which to build rural economies . This is because we believe that all people , whether they live in urban or rural areas , deserve the same opportunity to prosper in the new energy economy . <p> Economic revitalization of coal country could mean a variety of things , and will differ from place to place depending on the needs and desires of particular communities . It may mean regional economic @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . It may mean policies that prioritize people , such as ensuring retirement security , permanent health benefits , and long-term unemployment for miners . It may mean finding the cash to build infrastructure to modernize rural areas , like roads , airports , and rural broadband . And it means urging policymakers -- from the local to the federal level -- to support economies that are based on sources of revenue other than mineral receipts , which ebb and flow with boom and bust cycles . <p> Some attempts have already been made at economic revitalization policies . One example is the federal " Partnerships for Opportunity and Workforce and Economic Revitalization ( POWER ) Initiative , " which leverages federal funding to help coal communities . But , as one commentator put it , " most of assistance money goes toward Appalachia ... while only limited funds are making their way West . " These types of policies must be strengthened , expanded , and made to fit the needs of the West . <p> Being a fierce advocate for coal communities also means standing up to the the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ workers as major coal companies go bankrupt in the face of change . Consider this : when Alpha Natural Resources went bankrupt this past summer , it paid its executives $14.8 million in retention bonuses while cutting retirement benefits for workers . <p> There are many options on the table for rural economic redevelopment , and we must listen to and work with communities to make way for them to rise in the new energy economy . <p> The bottom line is this : All of us who desire and are pushing for a major global energy transition have a responsibility to ensure that the men and women of coal country are not left behind . Coal country has played a major role in the success of our state and our nation , and we must continue to ensure that it has a bright future . <p> In order to achieve this , we need to have a fundamentally different conversation , one that acknowledges that the reason many of us do environmental work is not just for nature , but for people .
