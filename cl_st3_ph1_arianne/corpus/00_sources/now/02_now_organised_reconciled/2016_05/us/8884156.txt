John Byrd , director of the Clinch River Environmental Studies Organization , will be the May program leader for a class in the Great Smoky Mountains National Park that will focus on the park 's diverse population of salamanders . <p> A new series of science programs at the Great Smoky Mountains National Park are taking a close look at the natural ingredients that give the park world-class status when it comes to biodiversity . <p> The next program is scheduled for 1-3 p.m . Friday at the Sugarlands Visitor Center , and the topic will be salamanders . John Byrd , director @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ why the Smokies are known as the salamander capital of the world . The program will begin with an indoor introductory slide show , followed by a short hike in search of the 30 species of woodland and aquatic salamanders that reside in the Smokies . <p> Byrd is active in Anderson County Schoolyard Wetlands , a project that builds wetlands behind elementary schools to introduce students to the natural world in their backyards . <p> The " Discover Science at Sugarlands " series takes place the third Friday of every month at the Sugarlands Visitor Center near Gatlinburg . The series is hosted by Discover Life in America , the nonprofit that in 1998 launched an ambitious survey of all life-forms in the Smokies . <p> To date , the All Taxa Biodiversity Inventory has cataloged 970 species in the Smokies that are new to science and 9,140 species that are new to the park . <p> Scientists estimate the Smokies are home to 60,000-80,000 species of living organisms , everything from slime molds to land snails . <p> Todd Witcher , executive director of Discover Life in America , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ small , often unsung life forms in the park that visitors often overlook in favor of more charismatic species like black bear and elk . <p> " Part of what we do is engage the public through education , " Witcher said . " Our goal is to raise awareness of biodiversity , its importance and it plight . " <p> Future Discover Science at Sugarlands dates are June 17 , July 15 , Aug. 19 , Sept. 16 and Oct. 21 , with topics to include " Pollinators in Trouble " and Climate Change in the Smokies . " <p> Biologists consider the Smokies to be one of the most biodiverse places in North America . In recent years the park has devoted an increasing amount of time and resources toward developing checklists , maps , databases and other tools for enhancing park management and providing biodiversity information to a wide audience . <p> The Sugarlands programs are free to the public .
