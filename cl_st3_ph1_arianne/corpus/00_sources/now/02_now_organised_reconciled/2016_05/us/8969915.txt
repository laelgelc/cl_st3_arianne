( Jeff Scheid/Las Vegas Review-Journal ) <p> By HENRY BREANLAS VEGAS REVIEW-JOURNAL <p> TUCSON , Ariz . <p> On the fourth floor of a research facility at the University of Arizona , the remnants of a famous Nevada tree anchors a display on one of modern science 's most notorious blunders . <p> Arizona 's Laboratory of Tree-Ring Research calls it the Currey Tree after Donald Currey , the researcher at the center of the controversy . But most people know the beloved bristlecone pine by another name : Prometheus . <p> Currey was a graduate student in the summer of 1964 when he came to Eastern Nevada 's Snake Mountains to study ancient trees and prehistoric climate change . <p> During his fieldwork , he persuaded the U.S. Forest Service to cut down a large bristlecone near the tree line on Wheeler Peak so he could count @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ casualty turned out to be approximately 4,900 years old , making it the oldest known tree on Earth at the time . <p> " The cutting of the so-called oldest tree in the world just dismayed people , and it was a major learning episode , " said Gretchen Baker , ecologist for Great Basin National Park . <p> The incident prompted tighter restrictions on the destruction of old trees and contributed to the eventual creation of the national park 300 miles northeast of Las Vegas . <p> Currey would go on to a distinguished career in geography , where he was known among academics for his extensive research on the relics of ancient Lake Bonneville in the eastern Great Basin region . But Prometheus would haunt him until his death in 2004 . <p> His story has taken on an almost mythic quality -- one fueled by the tree 's dizzying age and the folly of killing it to learn how long it had lived . <p> According to some accounts , the first man the Forest Service brought in to do the job took one look at the tree @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ until the next day so another crew could be found . <p> Several of those involved in the tree 's dissection are said to have later died under unusual circumstances . <p> " There 's enough mystery to keep people interested , " Baker said . <p> But there 's no mystery to why the lab at the University of Arizona ended up with a piece of Prometheus . <p> " That is the pre-eminent tree-dating lab in the country , " Baker said . " They have done a lot of the age-dating on bristlecones throughout the West . " <p> In 2013 , researchers there took a fresh look at Currey 's tree and determined it to be older than previously thought , something on the order of 5,000 years . <p> " No other bristlecones , living or dead , are known to have reached this age , " according to the display beneath the lab 's cross section of Prometheus . <p> Another piece of the tree is on display at the main visitor center at Great Basin National Park ; a third slab of the wood @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Ed Spear , the convention center 's executive director , said the tree has been there for decades , though it spent a few years in the lobby of Ely 's Hotel Nevada . <p> He said they get inquiries about it " pretty regularly , " including the occasional call from outside the country . <p> Spear is happy to tell people what he knows and defend the tree 's honor when necessary . Like when people bring up much older " clonal colonies " of plants , including a ring of creosote bushes in the California desert that 's said to be 11,700 years old . <p> " If it 's a clone , it 's not older .
