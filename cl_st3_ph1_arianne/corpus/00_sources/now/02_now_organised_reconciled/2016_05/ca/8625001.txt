<h> Dry conditions spark fire ban in some areas of province <p> Controlled fires within the City of Winnipeg and much of southern Manitoba have been suspended due to above-average temperatures , high winds and dry topsoil . <p> On Monday , the provincial ministry formerly known as Manitoba Conservation and Water Stewardship announced it will no longer issue burn permits in central , eastern and some western portions of the province and cancelled existing permits in those regions . <p> While campfires in enclosed pits are still permitted , open-air burns are forbidden from the U.S. border in the south to Berens River in the north , from the Ontario border in the east to the Interlake in the west and in Spruce Woods Provincial Park , Duck Mountain Provincial Park , the Porcupine Hills and along the west shore of Lake Winnipegosis . <p> The City of Winnipeg followed suit on Tuesday , announcing it , too , will no longer issue burn permits . Low humidity , high winds and above-average temperatures will keep the fire risk high until shrubs and grasses turn green later this month , he said . <p> So far this year , 26 wildfires have been reported within Manitoba , Friesen said . The normal count for this time of year is 60 , he said . <p> While Friesen called some fire crews back to work in March , one month ahead of schedule , cool weather , rain and snow in April helped keep the number of wildfires down , he said . <p> Thanks to a weekend-long rainstorm in April , total precipitation across southern Manitoba has actually been close to normal over the past @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ preparedness metereologist for Environment and Climate Change Canada . <p> Nonetheless , the dry topsoil presents a risk in the short term , Friesen said .
