<h> Registration up by 20 per cent in Whistler , Pemberton and Squamish <p> Residents are encouraged to trade in their car keys for two wheels next week and ride bikes to work or school as part of Bike to Work and School Week , taking place across the province from May 30 to June 5 . <p> The event encourages people to pedal their commute as a way of promoting healthy lifestyles and prevention of health issues , as well as reducing the impact of carbon emissions . <p> " This is a great event because it helps people overcome some of the obstacles with biking , " said Kim Slater , executive director of the Sea to Sky Clean Air Society . " A lot of behaviour is based on habit , so if you 're not in the habit of doing something , there 's always that sort of initial support that is needed to get you into a positive habit , and that 's what this event provides . " <p> Bike to Work Week was started in Victoria in 1995 , and has @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ than 40 communities in B.C. participating this year . <p> Students are now also being included in the event , with organizers encouraging them to participate in Bike to School Week , which was started by the Bike to Work Society in an effort to expand their mandate to look at opportunities for young people to get involved . <p> The Sea to Sky Clean Air Society is organizing the events in Whistler , Squamish and Pemberton this year as a part of their mandate for clean air and climate action . Their role , said Slater , is to work in partnership with other organizations , such as the Resort Municipality of Whistler to look at ways that the community can address climate change , both in terms of mitigation and adaptation . <p> " Our effort as coordinator for the communities is to try and get as many people as possible to participate , " she said . " So that 's reaching out to businesses and organizations and schools as well to get people riding . " <p> Slater hopes that people will be inspired to keep riding @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ you bike instead of getting in your car for those short distances , it does all add up , " she said . <p> A significant amount of carbon emissions in the Sea to Sky corridor are a result of commuting to work and school , and by choosing to ride or walk instead people can do their part in reducing their carbon footprint . " Unfortunately we do n't live in a super dense , urban area where it 's feasible for everyone to bike , " said Slater . " But that being said , I think it 's sort of this opening into thinking about those other choices as well , and doing the best you can . " <p> With the new addition of Bike to School Week this year , Slater expects the event to grow considerably now that young people can be involved . <p> This year marks the fourth year of participation for Whistler , Squamish and Pemberton , with registration growing by about 20 per cent each year . <p> Riders who register online will be entered to win the provincial grand prize @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Exodus Travels .
