<p> A typical Alberta family will pay between $70 and $105 extra per year for consumer goods and services as a result of the province 's new carbon tax , the NDP government said Tuesday in introducing its new climate legislation . <p> It 's the first time the government has put a figure on such " indirect " costs of the new levy , which will increase the price of transportation and heating fuel for most Albertans starting in January . <p> While some groups such as farmers will be exempt from the levy , most businesses , municipalities and other organizations will have to pay . Questions have arisen around how much of these extra costs of fuelling transport trucks and heating retail shops will be passed onto consumers . <p> To answer those questions , the government said it conducted an internal economic analysis of 473 commodities specific to Alberta . " <p> However , Wildrose Leader Brian Jean said his party believes the NDP government is dramatically low-balling the levy 's impact on already cash-strapped Alberta households . <p> " Our calculations indicate that it 's more around the $1,000 mark per family , but it 's good to see the government is finally acknowledging there are indirect costs , " Jean said . " The cost of everything will go up .
