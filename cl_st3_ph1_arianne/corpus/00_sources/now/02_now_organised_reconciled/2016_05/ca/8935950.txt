<p> A recently released report by all three North American governments warns of impending extinction of over one-third of the 1,154 native migratory bird species . Hardest hit are seabirds and those birds making tropical forests their home . The report is the first of its kind to include all North American species . Data was collected via surveys and counts dating all the way back to the 1970 's . <p> " The trend lines are continuing down . They have to be turned around or will fall below a threshold where they can be recovered , " Steven Price , president of Bird Studies Canada told CBC . <p> The news comes on the heels of the 100th anniversary of the U.S.-Canada Migratory Bird Treaty . This treaty is widely credited with helping to increase the populations of endangered wood ducks and whooping cranes from their dismal 19th century levels . <p> Climate change is the main culprit for the species on the endangered list , but the birds also face challenges from energy development .
