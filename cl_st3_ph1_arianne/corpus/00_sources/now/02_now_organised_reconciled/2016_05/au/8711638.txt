<p> Australia deserves a better and more accountable government after the July 2 double-dissolution election . <p> With trust , it could be delivered by either major party . We have two potential prime ministers whose instinct lie in the centre , not the extremes . Liberal leader Malcolm Turnbull stresses the immense opportunities for Australia . His Labor rival , Bill Shorten , has a positive policy platform . <p> The Senate to be elected in eight weeks will better reflect the will of the people . Some of the older guard across the Parliament will not recontest , allowing fresh ideas and less questionable behaviour . <p> Advertisement <p> But voters are feeling less than buoyant . Wages growth is stagnant , the economy is flat and Australian businesses are exposed to a higher currency as well as a slowdown in China . While the Australian economy is still performing above the world average , budget repair has been rightly delayed . Mr Turnbull offers jobs and economic growth , warning voters not to risk that with Labor . He needs a mandate in his own right rather than Tony Abbott 's so as to secure his position in the party and perhaps deliver some of the policies voters had assumed he would . <p> Mr Turnbull has copied many policies of Mr Shorten , who has been brave in rolling out costed policies that play to his party 's strengths : fairness , education and health . <p> The offering in the upper house -- crucial to policy compromise and holding government to account -- is strong . The Greens have a moderate leader in Richard Di Natale . The constructive Nick Xenophon is spreading across state borders and into the lower house .
