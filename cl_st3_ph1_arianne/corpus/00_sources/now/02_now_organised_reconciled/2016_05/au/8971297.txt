But while they may be punishing him in the polling , many other voters would have been left nonplussed by the events of last week , writes Paula Matthewson . <p> A corner of the internet went into meltdown last week when the Australian Federal Police raided the office of a federal Labor politician and a staffer 's home as part of an investigation into a series of leaks from the Government 's NBN Co . <p> It was the confluence of progressive concerns that whipped up the social media frenzy . Not only did the raid dramatically pit the physically intrusive powers of the surveillance state against the high morals of an apparent whistleblower , it was a reminder that Malcolm Turnbull had snatched away the dream of fibre-optic cable to every Australian home when he was Tony Abbott 's communication minister . <p> Even so , Turnbull might have been forgiven for this apparently heinous crime if he had delivered on other progressive issues once becoming Prime Minister . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ marriage , the Republic , climate action and asylum seekers . <p> Today 's Newspoll shows the extent of voters ' disappointment . After the hypothetical allocation of preferences , this gives Labor a 51-49 lead for the third fortnight in a row . <p> More alarmingly , Turnbull 's net approval rating has dropped 50 percentage points from the peak of +38 in late November last year .
