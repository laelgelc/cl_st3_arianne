<h> UK Queen wants son to succeed her as head of the Commonwealth <p> LONDON -- Queen Elizabeth told the heads of more than 50 states Thursday that she wants the heir to the British throne , her eldest son Prince Charles , to succeed her as head of the Commonwealth . <p> The British monarch made her plea at Buckingham Palace where she hosted the opening ceremony of the 2018 Commonwealth Heads of Government meeting . <p> Although Queen Elizabeth succeeded her late father , King George VI , as head and has led the Commonwealth since the 1950s , there is no written rule that the job should automatically go to the serving British monarch . <p> Royal commentators described the Queen 's comments as a rare intervention , just days ahead of her 92nd birthday this weekend . <p> The heads of governments will be debating the succession of their head when they hold a private retreat Friday at Windsor Castle . <p> The Commonwealth is an intergovernmental organization of 53 member states that are mostly former territories of the British Empire , with a combined population of 2.4 billion , a third of the world 's population . <p> In her speech , the Queen said : " It is my sincere wish that the Commonwealth will continue to offer stability and continuity for future generations , and will decide that one day The Prince of Wales should carry on the important work started by my father in 1949 . " <p> Hannah Furness , royal correspondent at the Daily Telegraph newspaper , said the message from the Queen was the clearest signal yet about the future of the head of the commonwealth . <p> " Her highly significant speech , met with warm applause , is the most explicit statement she has made to date of her hopes for the Commonwealth 's future , and the honorary and not hereditary position of its head , " wrote Furness . <p> British Prime Minister Theresa May , speaking at the opening ceremony , said the meeting was taking place at a time of significant global challenges . <p> " The rules-based international system , which has consistently delivered both prosperity and peace , faces threats in many forms and on many fronts , " May said . <p> " Climate change and extreme weather continue to take lives and damage livelihoods across the Commonwealth . And the new opportunities afforded by the digital world have brought with them new risks , with our cyber security under attack from individuals and state actors . " <p> May has called on commonwealth countries to join in the fight against plastic pollution . She cited the decision by the British government to end the sale of plastic straws , drink stirrers and plastic-stemmed cotton buds . <p> May urged Commonwealth countries to join a newly-formed Clean Oceans Alliance and take action , with bans on microbeads , a commitment to cutting down on single use plastic bags , or other steps to eliminate avoidable plastic waste . **25;213;TOOLONG
