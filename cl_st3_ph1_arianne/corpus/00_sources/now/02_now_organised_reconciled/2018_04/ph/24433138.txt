<h> DepEd sticks to June school opening <p> Education Secretary Leonor Briones on Monday said that the school opening for this coming school year ( SY ) 2018-2019 will remain in June amid proposals to move the academic calendar of basic education to later months . <p> " We do n't have plans to move the calendar as of this time , " said Briones in a phone interview with the Manila Bulletin . She noted that there will be no changes in the academic calendar for basic education this coming school year citing that the month of June remains the ideal time to open classes -- particularly in public schools . Private schools have until August 4 , 2018 to open their classes . <p> " You have to bear in mind that if we open in August , that means we will be open up until the summer months , " Briones said . Classrooms in public schools , she said , are not suitable for the hot dry season -- which is also commonly referred to as " summer " in the Philippines . <p> Briones noted that the physical construction of school buildings and classrooms in public schools are not designed to hold classes in " summer . " Thus , DepEd can not move the school calendar to later months since " our facilities are not favorable to classes until May which is the height of summer heat . " <p> Spare the kids , teachers <p> Without school buildings that are designed to withstand extreme heat , Briones noted that students will ultimately suffer should the school calendar be moved to later months . <p> " Maawa naman tayo sa mga bata Let 's have pity on the children , " Briones said . With the climate change that is " exacerbating the issue , " she noted that the learners ' health will be compromised . " If we will see the original classrooms , with a slight change in temperature , the school rooms are going to be like an oven , " she added . Given this , she stressed that changing the school calendar or moving the school opening will only invite " our children to a great deal of discomfort as well as exposure to the typical summer diseases . " <p> Briones noted that climate change is also another major consideration why DepEd has decided to stick to the June school opening . " The June opening will help to avoid diseases and other related illnesses during summer like sore eyes , stomach ache , dengue and others , " she said . <p> Briones said that DepEd is also giving both teachers as well as learners the time to plan their summer vacation . " That 's the time that they are also able to relax and attend to their personal interests , " she noted . <p> Changing the school calendar , Briones said , will only make " life quite complicated " for both learners and students . " I believe there was logic in originally opening the classes in June so we will abide by it , " she added . <p> Last year , bills were filed in Congress seeking the synchronization of the start of academic year in all Philippine schools beginning 2018 . <p> Senator Francis Escudero , chair of the Senate Education Committee , filed Senate Bill 1432 that seeks to mandate " all public and private schools start their school year on the second Monday of August but not later than the second Monday of September . " He noted that a majority of the top institutions in the world start their academic calendar in August or September , thus , aligning the academic calendar of the Philippines along with the rest of the world might be " beneficial " for the country . <p> In the House of Representatives , Rizal Rep. Michael John Duavit also filed House Bill 5802 citing that the shift in the academic calendar for all schools might create " a more favorable environment for attracting a pool of talented students and faculty . " He also noted that adjusting the school year might also mitigate the effects of the rainy season because classes are often suspended due to inclement weather and other related disasters . <p> Other stakeholders are also pushing for the academic calendar shift to make the transition easier for students who wish to study abroad . <p> Briones noted that while it is true that there is an increasing number of students going abroad to study , " their number is not relative " to millions of learners in public schools . " August is for those learners going abroad to study so maybe we will conduct a study to find out how many students really go abroad to study , " she added . <p> When it comes to higher education institutions ( HEIs ) shifting their academic calendars , Briones said that is their prerogative . " We have n't changed our calendar because we have to be more or less , harmonized to the local situation of learners compared to those who are going abroad to study , " she said . " Unless we totally change the physical structure of our school buildings which may not happen in a short period of time , it 's not possible , " she ended .
