<h> In the article , the writer says it is time for countries to think beyond borders and work in the interests of a wider community . <p> Laurence Brahm <p> BEIJING ( CHINA DAILY/ASIA NEWS NETWORK ) - The dragon and the tiger are two ancient Asian symbols that express dynamism and strength in both China and India . <p> The ancient Chinese science of geomancy or feng shui always necessitated having dragon and tiger elements in balance and synergy to assure both prosperity and peace . <p> According to Hindu tradition , the god Vishnu assures maintenance , sustainability and prosperity of all existence , by floating dreamlike upon a bed of entangled Naga or serpent dragons . <p> Such symbolic innuendo will not be lost among the leaders of two nations that share over five thousand years of unbroken history , cultural and economic exchange among themselves , having both served as main sources of our world 's heritage and culture . <p> As China 's President Xi jinping and Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi meet in Wuhan on April 28-29 , 2018 , it potentially heralds a re-start of the ancient Silk Road . <p> For millennia the Silk Road network of trade and commerce represented the economic order of our planet . <p> From the historical perspective of both China and India , the two-hundred plus years of colonial , post-colonial and neo-liberal eras that sidelined these traditional exchange routes , marked only a very brief disruption in the overarching sequence of their shared history . <p> As President Xi and Prime Minister Modi shook hands in Wuhan last weekend , it could represent a historic moment . Yes , an ancient yet new strategic alliance that could prove a global game changer . <p> These two powers through policy coordination could implode a regional mega-trend of smart infrastructure integration , enhanced through technology , telecommunications , and digital finance . <p> Today China stands as the largest single global investor in artificial intelligence and quantum communications . <p> India is the world leader in software outsourcing and IT consulting . <p> Both nations are the largest innovators and users of digital financing networks and cell phone banking systems , pioneering financial empowerment into the most isolated villages where their low-cost smart phone penetration is already strong . <p> The future of innovation is no longer in Silicon Valley , but rather in the arc spreading from China 's Great Bay ( Shenzhen-Hong Kong ) to India 's Bangalore . <p> The question now , is how to better integrate it . <p> China 's Belt and Road offers a blueprint for integration . <p> So does India 's Act East policy . <p> For consecutive years , the Himalayan Consensus Summit held annually in Kathmandu has called for both policies to become integral and synergistic rather than viewed as separate and isolated from each other . <p> To say that India is not a participant in China 's Belt and Road Initiative ( BRI ) is to ignore the fact that alongside China , India is the second largest investor in both the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank and the ( BRICS ) New Development Bank . <p> Both serve as the leading financial institutions for the BRI . <p> In the area of infrastructure and communications , both nations will only gain from coordinating and synergising their respective development policies . <p> Such new infrastructure will need to be smart , green and blue . <p> Certainly China and India are now leaders in scaling renewable energy innovations as viable commercial solutions . <p> Environment and climate change are the most critical issues requiring information and policy coordination . <p> Both nations are the treasurers of our planet 's vital resource , the Himalayan glaciers . <p> River systems from shared glaciers are the main water source for one quarter of the world 's population stretching across Central , South and Southeast Asia . <p> Thousands of trans-boundary river systems pose challenges of both water supply and sanitation . <p> As the two leading regional powers , both China and India must act together in averting imminent water crisis that could pose unprecedented costs of water , food and health security along with natural disasters , migration , and regional instability . <p> For this very reason , Professor Mahendra Lama , one of India 's Eminent Persons and member of Himalayan Consensus board , proposed at the 2016 Himalayan Consensus Summit that an alternative people-to-people and think tank mechanism should be established to mitigate crisis and avert conflict by addressing root causes rather than effects . <p> Nirupama Rao , former Indian Foreign Secretary and member of Himalayan Consensus board , called for a Himalayan Charter for the establishment of a Himalayan Council where regional governments can convene on climate concerns as proposed by ICIMOD Director General , David Molden , also Himalayan Consensus board member . <p> In this time of global volatility , when western leaders and policy makers are lost amidst political myopia , disregard of both long-term climate and human dignity in favour of short-term greed , our planet is in search of a new direction to avert climate crisis and war . <p> That voice of sanity may very well come from two ancient nations who have historically offered the world some of its deepest philosophical foundations . Europe may need to think about rebalance . Africa , whose largest inbound investors are China and India , can see the equation clearly . <p> South America may be thinking along the same lines . <p> This is a time when no nation can put itself first , and everyone must coordinate and work together for the sustainability if not existence of our planet . <p> It is time to realise a " community of shared future for mankind " as a vision to shared among us all . <p> Maybe now is time to " act East " and not look west . <p> The writer is the Founding Director of Himalayan Consensus , an environmental think tank , and International Research Fellow with the China Center for Globalisation .
