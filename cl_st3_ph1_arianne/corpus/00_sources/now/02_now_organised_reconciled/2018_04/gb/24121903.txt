<p> Dr Ellen Fry , from the University of Manchester , said : " Previous studies of the impacts of drought on flowers and bees have looked at individual species , often in the laboratory , but we used an experiment with rain shelters to examine the effects on real communities of plant species living in chalk grassland . <p> " The level of drought that we looked at was calculated to be a rare event , but with climate change such droughts are expected to become much more common . " <p> Results of the study suggest that chalk grasslands may support lower pollinator populations in the future . <p> However , the scientists warned the findings are likely to be broadly applicable to other regions and habitats . <p> The research was part of the Wessex Biodiversity and Ecosystem Service Sustainability project , and was funded by the Natural Environment Research Council . <h> Ipsoregulated <p> This website and associated newspapers adhere to the Independent Press Standards Organisation 's Editors ' Code of Practice .
