<p> French President Emmanuel Macron has said he may have failed in efforts to persuade Donald Trump to stick to an international nuclear deal with Iran . <p> " My view is ... that he will get rid of this deal on his own , for domestic reasons , " Mr Macron said at the end of a three-day state visit to the US . <p> Mr Trump has until 12 May to decide on the deal , which aimed to prevent Iran acquiring nuclear weapons . <p> He has been a strong critic of the accord , calling it " insane " . <p> On Wednesday , Mr Macron used similar language to describe frequent shifts in the US position on global issues , including climate change and the Iran agreement . <p> " It can work in the short term , but it 's very insane in the medium to long term , " he is quoted as telling reporters following a question-and-answer session with students at George Washington University . <p> Mr Macron made changing the US president 's mind on Iran a top priority for his visit but ended up admitting there was a " big risk " Mr Trump would abandon the deal . <p> Media playback is unsupported on your device <p> Media captionA timeline of what Trump 's said about the Iran deal <p> He agreed with Mr Trump that any deal should include a wider agreement on Iran 's influence in the Middle East and should cover the country 's nuclear activities longer term , as well as its ballistic missile programme . <p> He said he would work with Mr Trump to build a " new framework " in the Middle East - and especially in Syria . <p> His comments came at the end of a state visit in which the two leaders discussed differences on global trade and the environment , including the possibility of the US rejoining the Paris climate accord . <p> Under the deal , Iran 's ability to enrich uranium is severely restricted - the number of centrifuges it can install has been limited and its uranium stockpile is being drastically reduced .
