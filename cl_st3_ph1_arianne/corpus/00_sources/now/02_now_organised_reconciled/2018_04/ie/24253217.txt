<h> Staff at Irish Water regulator enjoy bonus payments of ? 238,885 in spite of losses more than doubling <p> By Gordon Deegan <p> The agency that regulates Irish Water paid out ? 238,885 in bonus payments to staff in 2016 in spite of losses more than doubling at the regulator . <p> According to the 2016 annual report just published by the Commission for Energy Regulation ( CER ) , the bonus payments to staff in 2016 follow bonus payments of ? 342,656 in 2015 . <p> In 2014 , ? 297,720 was paid out in bonuses and this followed ? 273,798 paid out in 2013 . <p> Over the past eight years , the CER - since renamed the Commission for Regulation of Utilities ( CRU ) - has paid out almost ? 2.1m in bonuses to staff in a scheme that has approval from the Dept of Public Expenditure and Reform along with the Dept of Communications , Climate Change and Environment . <p> Numbers employed by the energy regulator increased from 86 to 90 in 2016 and staff costs reduced from ? 6.5m to ? 6.36m . <p> The annual report shows that the CER decreased its bonus payments to workers as revenues declined by 14.4% from ? 16.34m to ? 14.9m . <p> The chief factor behind the drop in revenues was license income dropping from ? 15.8m to ? 14.2m . <p> The CER 's expenditure totalled ? 16.2m and this resulted in losses more than doubling going from ? 535,000 to ? 1.295m . <p> The CER is responsible for setting prices for semi-state companies , such as Bord Gais and Irish Water . <p> The annual report states that one of its key achievements during 2016 was delivering savings of ? 165m for customers through a review of Irish Water costs . <p> The report states that it drove efficiencies at Irish Water by requiring the utility to reduce operational costs by 20% between 2015 and 2018 while delivering better outcomes for customers . <p> The performance-related pay model was introduced when the regulator was established in 1999 and does not apply to senior management . <p> The performance-related pay element is reserved for lower grades and has strict criteria and is not guaranteed . <p> Referring to the bonus pay model previously , CER stated that its " staff are specialist and highly marketable and retention of expertise can be supported by such a pay model " . <p> CER 's costs are not paid for directly by the taxpayer , but were funded by a levy on " industry participants " . <p> Consultancy and legal fees incurred by the CER decreased going from ? 7.1m to ? 6.6m that included legal fees reducing from ? 981,000 to ? 515,000 . <p> Six employees at the regulator enjoyed salaries in excess of ? 100,000 last year .
