<h> Matter of Faith : Combining imagination , know-how <h> Pastor : By doing so , we can tackle almost just about any problem <p> When you hear the word " imagination , " what comes to mind ? If anything came to you , then you have an imagination ! Imagination is critical to science , engineering , art , journalism , exploration and so much of human endeavor . It also is necessary for social change . <p> Imagination fueled the great social justice leaders in human history -- from the prophets Isaiah and Amos to Jesus and Mohammed to Gandhi and Martin Luther King , Jr . -- and modern leaders like David Hogg and Emma Gonzalez of #NeverAgain , the women leading the charge in the #Metoo movement and the #BlackLivesMatter leaders , all asking us to imagine a world in which people are safe , treated equally and have dignity . Then to do the hard work to make that imagined world a reality . <p> Their imaginations show a profound trust in human ingenuity and ability to solve difficult problems and to move beyond apathetic acceptance of the status quo as the only possible option . To the naysayers , they say " This can be done " and then they make it happen . <p> When I think of the power of imagination and our capacity to solve problems , I think of the space race of the 1960s . For millions of years of human evolution , we were tied to this planet , but through the courage of imagining otherwise , we are now a people of space . President Kennedy imagined it could be done and trusted our scientists and engineers to make it happen even though the majority of the technology had n't yet been invented or envisioned . <p> The space race then inspired a new imagination of a cleaner world . One of those men on the moon turned his camera toward earth and gave us an image of earth with no lines between countries , but instead a flow of blue , green , brown and yellow . A pale blue dot , as Carl Sagan called it , which is singularly , in an unfathomably immense universe , the only place humanity has ever called home . An infinitesimal speck of dust in an unremarkable galaxy is the birthplace of every person who has ever lived . <p> The Earthrise photo -- offering a view of ourselves we had never seen , stirred our imagination -- and this , in part , also spurred the ecological movement , including the creation of Earth Day , which we celebrate on Sunday . <p> Back in 1970 , a handful of people of vision , including Wisconsin 's own Gaylord Nelson , imagined that the smog , air pollution , burning rivers , poisoned land and dying animals did not have to be the inevitable result of our modern way of life . They imagined we could be who we are but without devastating our only home and that we could be good stewards of God 's creation while maintaining our standard of living . <p> Almost 50 years ago , many of us were inspired ; President Nixon created the Environmental Protection Agency and began to clean up our mess . Our rivers no longer burn , Los Angeles rarely has smog , the almost-extinct bald eagles are thriving . Locally , we have Phoenix Park built on land that some said was an unfixable toxic wasteland . <p> When our country focuses our imagination and our creativity on a problem , we get it solved . <p> Today 's problem is climate change , a real and immediate threat to life on this planet . We need not feel hopeless against it or that fossil fuels are our only option , nor should we fearfully invent reasons to convince ourselves it is n't happening . <p> We are a creative and intelligent people . We just need religious and political leaders -- and engaged citizens -- with the imagination to make it happen . When the full power of our imagination and scientific know-how is rallied together , we can do just about anything , including increasing our standard of living while reducing our damage to our only home . <p> The Rev. David Huber is pastor at Plymouth United Church of Christ , Eau Claire .
