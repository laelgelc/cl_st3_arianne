<h> Mangroves and mapping help Mozambique tackle climate ... <p> BONN , Germany , April 27 ( Thomson Reuters Foundation ) -- M ozambique not only has one of Africa 's longest coastlines ; it is also the final destination for at least nine transnational rivers . <p> That makes it a hostage to geography , says Manuel de Araujo , the mayor of Quelimane , a port city of 450,000 people that lies about 1,000 kilometres ( 623 miles ) north of the capital Maputo . <p> The fact that 60 percent of the country 's population lives along the coast adds to the challenges of managing climate change , he said in Bonn on Friday at an international conference on building resilient cities . <p> With frequent extreme weather threatening the delicate urban balance of Quelimane -- most of which lies below sea-level -- the population is extremely vulnerable to climate risks , he said . <p> " The city is easily flooded both from rain and from marine floods and tides -- sometimes they all happen at the same time , " he said . <p> In Quelimane , one solution involves restoring hectares of mangroves , which act as a nature-based solution against flooding , helping to stem the tide by preventing soil erosion . <p> " Mangroves are our first line of defence , " Araujo said of the scheme , which is supported by the U.S. development agency USAID . <p> The project has a social dimension too : mangrove nurseries are planted in the poorest parts of the city , where residents , particularly women , manage and conserve the young plants . <p> High levels of poverty meant people were used to cutting down mangroves for cooking or building , he said . <p> But working with schools and religious leaders , and putting out a conservation message in the local language via radio , means the community now knows to protect mangroves . <p> " There is a political buy-in given that community members and leaders are involved in all stages of the restoration process , " he said . <p> " This is very important as it brings ownership to the people . " <p> MAPPING RISK <p> A further 670 kilometres northeast of Quelimane is the popular tourist city of Pemba . Its mayor , Tagir Carimo , said nearly one-third of the city is vulnerable to climate threats such as flooding and soil erosion . <p> On the very day that Carimo was sworn in as mayor , Pemba was pummelled by heavy rain and flooding , the consequences of which he is still fixing . <p> One major effort by his city has been to use satellite imagery and local knowledge to map parcels of land and neighbourhoods at higher risks of climate effects , such as flooding , he told the conference in Bonn . <p> " The main gains are that from ( maps ) we can see properly what type of land we can use and advise people on which kinds of materials they should use to build houses , roads and so on , " he said . <p> Whether building climate-proof homes or city-wide drainage systems , he said , the population now feels more secure knowing that they are developing land in safe or low-risk areas . <p> Mozambique is one of the world 's poorest countries , with an average life expectancy of 58 , according to the World Bank . That makes it ill-equipped to fund adaptation to climate threats , said Carimo . <p> " The main difficulty is how to provide long-term finance , " Carimo told the Thomson Reuters Foundation , particularly when short-term survival often seems more pressing . <p> However , Araujo noted , the two might soon merge : disasters cost Mozambique between 1 and 5 percent of GDP annually , with that figure set to rise due to climate change , he said . <p> Given the country 's growing population and increased migration to its coastal cities , both mayors said a shift in mindset was needed from funders to allow climate change adaption and development to go hand-in-hand .
