But this is just the first step in a longer and larger struggle to close down the oilsands industry . <p> Let 's be clear ; the oilsands are not in the national interest . This , in turn , depends on our ability to keep much of the known fossil-fuel reserves where they are -- in the ground . <p> It is particularly important to keep the oilsands in the ground because according to the Energy Education team at the University of Calgary , " the greenhouse gas emissions for oilsands extraction and processing are significantly larger than for conventional crude oil . " In a 2012 paper published in his role as a University of Victoria climate scientist , Green Party Leader Andrew Weaver and his co-author noted : " If the entire Alberta oilsand resource ... were to be used , the associated carbon dioxide emissions would induce a global mean temperature change of roughly 0.36 C. The Energy Education team also reports that the volume of water used in the oilsands operations in a year is equivalent to that used by 1.7 million Canadians , and that " if water use is not changed , then the downstream flow of the Athabasca River is expected to decrease by 30 per cent " by 2050 . <p> Once used , that water is contaminated with chemicals that are " toxic to animals , particularly aquatic organisms , " so it ends up stored " almost indefinitely " in tailings ponds that , together with their infrastructure , cover an area " about 1.5 times larger than the city of Vancouver .
