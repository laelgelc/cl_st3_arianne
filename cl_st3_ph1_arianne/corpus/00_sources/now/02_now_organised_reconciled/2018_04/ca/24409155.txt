one that cuts the industry some slack -- more than $2-billion in assistance , including a five-year exemption from the carbon tax . <p> As a greenhouse gas , methane is 25 times more potent than carbon dioxide over a 100-year period , making it an important focus of both federal and provincial plans to mitigate the effects of climate change . <p> In statement released with the draft regulations , Alberta energy minister Margaret McCuaig-Boyd expressed confidence in the oil industry 's ability to meet the challenge with the province 's homegrown solution . <p> " Their early action and commitment to working with us to get this right means we are well on our way to an Alberta-made plan that puts the jobs of hard-working Albertans and a strong economy front and centre , " McCuaig-Boyd said . <p> While the regulator was drafting the regional plan , the cost of which it pegs at $780 million , the Canadian Association of Petroleum Producers ( CAPP ) , Canada 's main oil and gas lobby , made a public appeal in October for the industry to regulate its own methane emissions . <p> CAPP argued with its own plan , which it claims can be accomplished for $700-million , it could achieve the same results , save jobs , and keep the industry as well as the province competitive . <p> Patrick MacDonald , CAPP 's director of climate change and innovation , said while the regulator 's plan does n't totally align with what the lobby proposed in October , CAPP still supports a " made-in-Alberta " solution and sees the draft as an opportunity to continue working with the province . <p> " The federal government regulations were issued as final , " MacDonald said . " We 're still in an ongoing feedback process for Alberta , so I 'm not sure where these are ultimately going to land , but there ( are ) more flexible components in there . " <p> For example , CAPP called for a " fleet-average , " approach to reductions , which would monitor and regulate emissions across the industry as a whole , rather than at specific sites . <p> Shelley Svetanova , public relations adviser with the AER , said the provincial plan incorporates both approaches , according to how the methane is released -- intentionally , accidentally or otherwise . <p> Environmental groups have been advocating for more accurate ways to measure , monitor and report methane emissions . <p> Duncan Kenyon , the Pembina Institute 's director for responsible fossil fuels , issued a statement that called Alberta 's site-based draft regulations " novel and unique , " but in need of verifiable monitoring and reporting methods in their final form . <p> " As such , it will be judged on whether it can balance this regulatory approach with a reporting system that achieves the highest level of accuracy , transparency and simplicity of site-level reporting and compliance assurance , " Kenyon said . " Without a change to site-level ( monitoring and reporting ) based on real-time , continuous measurement , Alberta 's approach to managing methane emissions will continue to be unverifiable and inaccurate . " <p> Despite the inclusion of site-based monitoring , MacDonald believes the draft regulations leaves enough room at the table to talk , but admits that CAPP still needs to carefully consider the proposal . <p> " There are components in there that we think do give some flexibility , but we really need to do a deep dive to evaluate the full impact of those regulations , " MacDonald said . <p> Along with the draft regulations , the Alberta Energy Regulator announced that it is seeking public feedback on the plan until May 28 . <h> More from the Toronto Star &amp; Partners <h> LOADING <p> Copyright owned or licensed by Toronto Star Newspapers Limited .
