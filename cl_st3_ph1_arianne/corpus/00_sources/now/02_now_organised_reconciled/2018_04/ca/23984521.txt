<h> Court the left , but do n't underestimate Ford <p> The Ontario Liberals are from the government and they 're here to help . <p> Seriously , their pre-election budget last week was n't an attempt to blatantly buy votes in the June 7 election , so much as to please progressive voters on the centre and left of the political spectrum . <p> The Liberals are n't concerned about Progressive Conservative voters on the centre right . Kathleen Wynne is obviously quite content to leave them to Doug Ford , and to let him rail on about the deficit and the debt . It 's Andrea Horwath 's NDP base the Liberals are courting with their Plan For Care And Opportunity . <p> There 's something here for everyone . <p> Free child care for kids aged two and a half to kindergarten . <p> Free prescription drugs and dental care up to $400 per person , $600 per couple and $700 for a family of four . <p> Free meds for people over 65 , to go along with the previously announced free prescription drugs for young @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for up to 225,000 post-secondary students . <p> And $19 billion for hospitals over the next decade . <p> Not to mention the Wynn government 's raising the hourly minimum wage to $14 this year and $15 in 2019 . <p> All of which gets the attention of voters on the left , whom the Liberals will need to overcome a hefty Conservative lead in the polls . <p> Of course , there 's also the nagging problem of the deficit and debt . After a decade of deficit spending , the Liberals finally balanced the books in the fiscal year that ended last week . <p> In the current fiscal year , they 're going to run a deficit of $6.7 billion , followed by $6.6 billion and $6.3 billion deficits in the coming two years . <p> Ontario 's debt has tripled to $325 billion in the nearly 15 years the Liberals have been in office . That 's now a debt-to-GDP ratio of 37 per cent , due to rise to 39 per cent by 2021 . But there 's a deep attitudinal desire for a change of government . A Leger Marketing poll of 1,000 respondents from March 12-14 found the satisfaction level with the Wynne government at only 23 per cent , with the dissatisfaction level at 67 per cent . <p> Asked who would make the best premier , 24 per cent said Ford , 15 per cent said Horwath and only 12 per cent chose Wynne . <p> Asked which party represented change , 31 per cent chose the PCs , 20 per cent the NDP , while only 10 per cent said the Liberals . <p> In a change election , the governing party can make a good case for continuity .
