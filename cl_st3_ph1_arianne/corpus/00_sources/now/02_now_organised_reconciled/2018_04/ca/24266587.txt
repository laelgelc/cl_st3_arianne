<h> SURETTE : Bravo to B.C. for bucking pipeline mania <p> Cedar George-Parker addresses the crowd as protesters opposed to the Kinder Morgan Trans Mountain pipeline extension defy a court order and block an entrance to the company 's property , in Burnaby , B.C. , on April 7 . ( DARRYL DYCK / CP ) <p> So the embattled oil pipeline through B.C. is now a matter of " national interest " and must be built no matter what . So said Prime Minister Justin Trudeau just before hopping off to France where I saw him on French TV getting a round of applause in the National Assembly for a rousing declaration in favour of controlling climate change . <p> The contradiction here -- grand but hollow speeches on the environment while promoting some of the biggest , dirtiest extraction projects on Earth -- has been going on through a generation of prime ministers , but is now coming to a head . Justin Trudeau is wearing it : the increasing clash between our energy consumption and the environmental unsustainability of it . <p> The passions appear to be frighteningly absolute . Alberta claims the virtually divine right to get tar sands oil -- considered the world 's environmentally dirtiest -- to tidewater . There have even been suggestions that the army be called in to get the job done . Meanwhile , in B.C. , where some people , including Green Party Leader Elizabeth May , have already been arrested , chiefs of native territories that the pipeline would go through are basically saying , " over my dead body . " <p> If this pipeline is considered of vital national interest , it means that the entire country has given in to the Alberta oil myth : the idea that Alberta 's wealth is the unquestionable foundation of the nation 's economy , and sustains the equalization system that have-not provinces depend on -- all of whom had better drop their fussiness and have some respect or else , environment be damned . If these British Columbian insurgents are n't put down , apparently , the country 's sunk . <p> That Alberta is rich and economically important is not in doubt . That it will remain rich is also not in doubt , whether or not this pipeline is built . In fact , the projections are for Alberta oil production to nearly double to over five million barrels a day by 3030 , no matter what -- this is oil that world environmental authorities say should stay in the ground for the climate to have any chance of not careening completely out of control . <p> Even now , Alberta has a capacity to export up to four million barrels a day , even before the famous Keystone XL pipeline ( now delayed by Nebraska landowners , but likely going ahead ) and other pipeline upgrades . The B.C. pipeline would add only 600,000 barrels a day to that , but would reach tidewater , theoretically freeing the oil from the American single market and raising the price , but with no indication that the Asians would actually pay more than the Americans for this problematic stuff that needs specialized refining . <p> Other expert voices say even partially upgrading oil sands oil in Alberta , making it flow without having to add expensive natural gas condensates , would increase existing pipeline capacity by 26 per cent and free the natural gas for other things . <p> That argument , however , does n't fit into pipeline mania and ca n't get going . CN Rail also claims to have invented a process where the pre-refined product can be shipped as " dry goods " in regular freight cars , without danger of exploding or seeping into the environment . Given these other possibilities , the battle over the pipeline looks more political than real . <p> The calculation by the oil companies , prepared to spend billions on these projects that would n't be economic at recent oil prices , seems to be that a half-dozen years from now , " peak oil " -- the idea of world reserves drawing down -- will return with a vengeance . <p> The American frackers , who put off the day of reckoning , will be running out of steam . Oil prices , the calculation goes , will skyrocket and oil from any source will be at a premium . <p> This explains also BP drilling off Nova Scotia , companies eyeing the Arctic and so on , in a new environmentally destructive frenzy that presumes that we will not be able to curb our oil appetite -- which , in fact , shows no sign of abating . It 's up to 90 million barrels a day and rising . The worsening climate ? Melting Arctic ? From this point of view , forget it , apparently . The oil lifestyle is more important than flooded downtowns , half-continents ablaze and destructive weather . <p> As for the narrative that we slackers all live off the hard work of entrepreneurial Albertans , you might check out one Trevor Tombe , economist at the University of Calgary , who 's been doing some figuring to the annoyance of the political hyperventilators . <p> Albertans do pay far more in federal tax than they get back -- but that 's because they 're the youngest and most highly paid population . ( Even most retirees apparently take off for B.C. ) <p> So there 's less federal spending on old-age pensions , unemployment insurance , social assistance and what have you . But as far as actual equalization to other provinces , only about 10 per cent of Alberta 's federal taxes go to that . An Albertan pays the same taxes , with the same proportion going to equalization , as a person making the same amount in Nova Scotia or anywhere else . A lot of this sounds like rich people whining that they 're not rich enough , and that any restraint on their hubris is outrageous . <p> I do hope all this gets worked out peacefully , but for the moment I 'm saying hooray for B.C. for calling out this phoney " national interest . "
