<h> Tories demand government explain how much carbon price will cost Canadians <p> Conservative finance critic Pierre Poilievre will ask the House of Commons this week to force the government to show Canadians how much more they can expect to pay for gas , heat and groceries once every Canadian will be charged a $50 per tonne carbon tax . <p> Poilievre says he knows the government has the information because access to information requests he filed produced a finance department memo that says there is an analysis of the potential impact of a carbon price , based on household consumption data across different income levels . <p> However , he says the actual data from the analysis is blacked out . <p> Poilievre says he has given notice of the intention to file a motion demanding the government table documents in the House that show how much a $50 per tonne carbon price will cost a median income Canadian family . <p> The federal government requires every province to put a price on carbon by the start of next year or it will impose one itself . Legal authority to do so is laid out in the government 's budget implementation bill currently working its way through Parliament . <p> Poilievre says the carbon price legislation takes up 200 pages of that bill , but nowhere does it say how much it will cost people . <p> " Government can not tax what Parliament does not approve , but Parliament can not approve what it does not know , " Poilievre said Sunday . " Right now , Parliament is being kept in the dark . " <p> In refusing to provide the analysis done by the department of finance , the government says it can be withheld because it is either advice to government or information that can possibly harm the Canadian economy . <p> " If the knowledge of the cost of the carbon tax in itself would cause injury to the Canadian economy , then that cost must be even worse than we have imagined , " said Poilievre . <p> He also filed a complaint with the information commissioner , asking her to investigate the government for its refusal to release the information in the access request response . He said Caroline Maynard has agreed and has started looking into the matter . <p> Four provinces already have carbon pricing systems in place , but the federal requirement will mean on January 1 , every province has to have a system that applies a price of at least $20 per tonne of carbon emissions . The price has to rise $10 per tonne per year until it reaches $50 per tonne in 2022 . At that point , Ottawa will review the system . <p> The documents outlining the proposed federal pricing system , to be used only in provinces that do n't have their own plan , do provide information about what impact the price will have to the cost various types of fuel such as gasoline , natural gas , propane and coal . <p> However , Poilievre says it 's not detailed enough for individual families to understand what it means to them , because the price will not only affect the price at the pump but most of what people buy as the cost to produce and ship products also goes up . <p> Environment Minister Catherine McKenna said Sunday a price on carbon has a low cost and high impact . <p> " We know polluting is n't free -- there is a cost , " said McKenna . <p> She said carbon pricing pushes for innovation to find ways to reduce emissions in order to pay less , and is one of the ways Canada will meet its international commitment to reduce climate-changing emissions . <p> McKenna has also been criticized for not answering questions about how much emissions the carbon price will cut . <p> " We will be releasing more information shortly , " she said Sunday . <p> The Pan Canadian Framework on Clean Growth and Climate Change estimates 86 million tonnes of emissions will be eliminated in Canada from the measures contained within , but it does not specify how much of that is to come from carbon pricing . <h> More from iPolitics <h> 1 comment on " Tories demand government explain how much carbon price will cost Canadians " <p> Mr. Poilievre already knows all too well what the impact of a carbon tax will be , because his own government had already performed this experiment .
