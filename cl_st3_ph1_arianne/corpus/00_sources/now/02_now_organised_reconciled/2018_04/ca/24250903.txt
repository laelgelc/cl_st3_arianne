<p> And these rising prices are aided by the provincial carbon tax , which on April 1 was hiked to $35 per tonne of carbon-dioxide emissions , the highest such tax in Canada . <p> Those increases arrived in time to join other tax and fee hikes that are not balanced out by a commensurate rise in wages . <p> The B.C. NDP 's carbon tax is unlike its format under the previous B.C. Liberal government and will take a larger financial bite out of the budgets of British Columbians already squeezed to the point of absurdity . <p> ( It also represents a broken NDP campaign promise in that the party pledged to begin carbon tax hikes in 2020 . ) <p> Gone is the revenue-neutral component via corresponding income-tax cuts and credits , which turns B.C. 's carbon tax into a general tax on living . <p> Meanwhile , the province continues to export massive amounts of both thermal and coking coal to the rest of the world , surely raising skepticsm about embracing this **25;859;TOOLONG mindset . Vancouver is now North America 's largest coal-exporting port . <p> Are we really making a difference in switching to energy-efficient light bulbs when a few shipments of coal leaving Deltaport essentially negates annual green efforts in Kamloops ? <p> Is there really a point in the myriad efforts to go green when it has been demonstrated neither the city , nor the province , nor the country will come anywhere close to meeting their greenhouse-gas emissions reduction targets ? <p> Can we truly go green locally when city hall continues to approve development in tick-infested areas where transit dares not venture ? <p> Canada accounts for less than two per cent of the world 's greenhouse-gas emissions , which is a fact pounced on by critics who counter that Canada is among the leading nations in per capita emissions . <p> But Mother Nature does not compartmentalize emissions based on a country 's population . <p> All emissions end up in the same place , regardless of per capita data . All that coal heading across the Pacific Ocean is not separated by Mother Earth based on where it is being burned for electricity or used in steelmaking , nor does nature care from where that coal originates . <p> Most of B.C. 's coal is coking coal , used to make iron and steel , as opposed to thermal coal , used to generate electricity . <p> According to the Massachusetts Institute of Technology , production of a ton ( 2,000 pounds ) of steel generates almost two tons ( 4,000 pounds ) of CO2 emissions , with steel production comprising up to five per cent of the world 's total greenhouse-gas emissions . <p> Any effort to reduce greenhouse-gas emissions is laudable , but there is a serious disconnect when those efforts are negated by larger forces , yet fragile bottom lines of households continue to be eroded . <p> Aaron Wudrick , federal director of the Canadian Taxpayers Federation , summed it up well in a recent commentary : <p> " Carbon tax advocates need to go big or go home . They can either admit to Canadians that slashing carbon emissions will be expensive , difficult and painful and all the sacrifice could well be for nothing if other countries are n't doing the same . Or they could just admit this is nothing more than a plan for filling up government coffers . " <p> The reality is going green in Kamloops and B.C. makes one feel good , but does little else to combat climate change when our coal exports and the belching giants of China , India and the U.S. are considered . <h> Comments <h> NOTE : To post a comment you must have an account with at least one of the following services : Disqus , Facebook , Twitter , Google+ You may then login using your account credentials for that service .
