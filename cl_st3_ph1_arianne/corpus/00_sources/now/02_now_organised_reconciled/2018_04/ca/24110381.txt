But you might be confused into thinking so by the inflamed rhetoric coming out of Ottawa and Alberta @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ national crisis over the resistance to the proposed Kinder Morgan pipeline , following an ultimatum by Houston-based Kinder Morgan that it 's suspending long-stalled building plans unless it 's assured a green light by May 31 . <p> With both Alberta and Ottawa offering full-throttle support for the project , including potential financial backing , a chorus of rage has arisen from the country 's business and political ranks over B.C. 's refusal to fall into line . <p> Some commentators insist this shows Canada is broken , even implying Prime Minister Justin Trudeau is a sissy unless he gears up to send in the army to restore the rule of law . <p> So what precisely has B.C. done that it is trampling on the rule of law ? <p> Article Continued Below <p> After hiring the distinguished former B.C. Supreme Court justice Thomas Berger as a legal adviser , B.C. 's NDP government sought and won intervener status in a federal court case involving the pipeline , and it has announced plans to consult the B.C. public about developing new regulations to protect its environment from oil spills @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a legal showdown , Ottawa would almost certainly win , since it has constitutional power over projects it deems in the national interest . But without a Supreme Court ruling , it does n't seem outrageous for B.C. to test its right to protect its environment . <p> Business pundits complain that there 's no time to seek such a ruling since we 're staring at the May 31 deadline . <p> No doubt , Kinder Morgan has shareholders to satisfy , but should the timetable here -- given the momentous issues involved -- really be driven by the needs of a foreign company ? <p> The pipeline would dramatically increase oil tanker traffic in the sensitive Burrard Inlet -- to more than 400 supertankers a year -- risking potentially devastating , long-lasting impacts from an oil spill that put the May 31 deadline in perspective . <p> Justin Trudeau says the federal government remains " determined " to see the Trans Mountain pipeline expansion built after Kinder Morgan hit pause on the project , but the prime minister would n't say what Ottawa 's next steps would be . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Andrew Weaver , who holds the balance of power in B.C. 's government , notes that the diluted bitumen travelling through the pipeline from Alberta 's oilsands is more dangerous than regular oil if spilled into water . <p> " It sinks . We can not clean up a spill , " said Weaver , an internationally recognized climate scientist , in an interview on CBC-TV 's Power and Politics . <p> Weaver , who was part of a team sharing a Nobel Peace Prize with Al Gore , dismissed the scientific review of the Kinder Morgan project carried out by the National Energy Board as a " sham . " <p> Then there 's the fact the pipeline would go through unceded Indigenous territory , where resistance to it has been fierce . <p> Martyn Brown , who was chief of staff to former B.C. Premier Gordon Campbell , argues that B.C. should go further and bring in legislation giving Indigenous people shared jurisdiction over environmental protection related to oil spill prevention and cleanups . <p> Then , of course , there 's the matter of climate change ; @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , making it harder to reduce our carbon emissions . <p> Weirdly , Trudeau fashions himself as a champion of climate solutions . According to his backflip of an argument , the pipeline is key to an implicit bargain that allows Alberta to get its oil to market , in exchange for Alberta 's support for a carbon tax . <p> Only by pumping more carbon into the atmosphere can we hope to stop climate change -- sort of like only by smoking more cigarettes can we hope to stop cancer . <p> Trudeau 's bargain has always seemed full of holes , but perhaps it has taken the Kinder Morgan ultimatum to finally out it as the truly unworkable , fraudulent deal that it is . <p> It 's premised on the notion of compromise , which is n't necessarily a bad thing .
