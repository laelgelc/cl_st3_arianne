<h> Alberta experiences decrease of more than 62,000 EI recipients in early 2018 <p> According to Statistics Canada , Alberta saw a significant decrease in employment insurance ( EI ) beneficiaries in February of this year , ending a four month trend of relatively stagnant recipient numbers . <p> In February 2018 , there were 62,100 fewer EI recipients in Alberta than the month prior , a 1.9 per cent decrease . <p> From February 2017 to February 2018 , Alberta experienced a 26.7 per cent decrease in EI beneficiaries as the province 's unemployment rate dropped from 8.2 per cent to 6.7 per cent . <p> Sharlene Massie , with About Staffing says the numbers are good news for the province . <p> " It make sense because they 're getting jobs and they 're negotiating pay and we have n't been able to do that for a long time . It was the other way ; it was going down with wage rollbacks . " <p> She says that the jobs are coming in from all around the province in every sort of industry . <p> " It 's not just your typical construction pockets that come up this time of year when the sun shines , it 's pockets of all kinds of roles including management , HSE , accounting . We 've got all these unique jobs like mediator , mortgage broker and physiotherapist , all these really interesting roles that are coming in from everywhere , so it 's fun . " <p> Massie says that there has also been a change in the mindset of employers too . <p> " There are n't any employers that are saying ' yeah , I can wait for the right one ' , they are saying , ' no , I am ready to hire right now ' which is also a big switch . " <p> Ron Kneebone , professor of economics at the School of Public Policy , says that a drop in EI numbers can be a sign of good news or bad news . <p> " You come off of EI for two reasons ; one is you got a job , which is the good news , or you reached the end of your benefits , which is the bad news . " <p> He says that while EI has dropped , he is also seeing a shift of people going on social assistance . <p> " We 've reported from the school before that social assistance caseloads are substantially higher than they were before this recession and they are n't coming down very quickly . " <p> As for the long and short term in Alberta , Kneebone says there are too many variables to accurately predict how things will turn out . <p> " It depends on whether or not the pipelines get pushed through , it depends on what we 're going to do in terms of climate change initiatives , it depends on what Donald Trump does in respect to trade policy . There are so many uncertainties which is a real problem for the economy . " <p> Unfortunately , Massie also says that the rising economy also shows a slip back into bad habits in Alberta . <p> " We certainly did n't learn anything . The big energy companies , they know how to attract people and it 's buckets of money and that 's what they 're doing again . They 're coming in and stealing the people that are getting new jobs and are stealing from the small businesses and the entrepreneurs , so we 're back in the cycle again , which is terrifying . " <p> Across Canada , there were 480,200 EI recipients in February , the lowest level on record since 1997 when Statistics Canada began collecting EI data . <h> Photos <p> Experts say a drop in employment insurance numbers can be a sign of good news or bad news .
