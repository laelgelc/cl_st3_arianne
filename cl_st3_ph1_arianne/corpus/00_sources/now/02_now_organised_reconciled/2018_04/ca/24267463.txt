<p> Climate change has had a negative effect on the whole planet , but coral reefs are among the most damaged . They 've been exposed to warmer ocean temperatures , to pollution of water , low tides and a long exposure to sunlight , bleaching them . The bleaching process was the expelling of algae in their tissue , losing the color , nutrients and becoming fragile . After a while , in these conditions , corals starved and died . <p> But Emily Darling , SFU alumnus , and Isabelle C ? t ? , a marine biology professor , have hope that they could save coral reefs . They have recently published a study in the journal Science , in which they talk about ways of saving the reefs from climate change . <h> Saving Coral Reefs For a While <p> The study argues that scientists can help coral reefs by selectively breeding or genetically engineering the corals to increase their resistance to oceans that are warmer and more acidic . Of course , they admit that these solutions are n't going to help enough with solving the issue . Emily Darling said that : <p> " Right now , it is n't realistic to think that we can cost-effectively restore high-diversity reefs at scales of hundreds or thousands of square kilometers using these methods . " <p> But there is one other solution . There are some rare coral reefs that have low exposure to bleaching or predators . They 're called ' reefs of hope ' , and these corals can travel via currents to other safe places . The idea the authors propose is to ensure that these reefs are protected and that their coral larvae can travel safely to a good location . <h> Eliminating Emissions Will Save Coral Reefs <p> Global conservation is very important , so the authors said that " finding and protecting even the smallest of resistant refuges is an urgent priority " , but we should also eliminate greenhouse gas emissions . <p> If we do not take measures , C ? t ? and Darling said that reefs will transform in the next decades , they will grow very slow and they will erode or break apart . This means the ecosystem of the ocean will be in a critical situation , also affecting humankind , says Darling : <p> " These changes will force some societies to transform away from current reef-dependent livelihoods , like fishing or tourism . " <p> C ? t ? said that selective breeding and other strategic choices to protect the reefs are only buying us time , " but at the end of the day , if we do n't curb emissions , we will lose reefs as we know them today .
