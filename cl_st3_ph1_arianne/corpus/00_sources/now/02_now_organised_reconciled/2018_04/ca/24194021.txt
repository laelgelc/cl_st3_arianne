The earth has been going through significant warming and cooling cycles for millions of years , and I believe it is hubris to think we can stop those cycles . <p> Whereas I agree that humans have to do something about carbon emissions , along with all other forms of pollution , can we not be realistic about it ? Stopping the pipeline developments will have minimal effect on global emissions . If we do not ship our bitumen for export to countries that want it , they will simply buy the bitumen from someone else @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ our own ( few ) refineries , we will continue to buy ever more of the bitumen and refined oil products from someone else , as evidenced by the growing amount of crude oil imports into Canada . Because , like it or not , until someone comes up with a viable alternative , we still have to keep heating our homes and our infrastructure , operating our vehicles , etc . Believing that carbon pricing will significantly change that behaviour when there are no viable alternatives at this point is wishful thinking . Urban dwellers , especially those in major cities , appear to be most guilty of this unrealistic approach to the issue of carbon emissions . Easy to be holier-than-thou with short commuting distances and public transit systems . But residents of rural areas and small towns have little choice currently when it comes to operating our vehicles and heating our homes and businesses . With no access to natural gas ( which of course is also a contributor to carbon emissions ) and electric heating being costly and of dubious efficiency , oil becomes the necessary @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ can come out with mass production of electric vehicles which are capable of long distances between charges , and improved fast-charging times , small town and rural residents have no choice but to operate gasoline-powered vehicles . Not to mention the huge expansion of the power grid that will be required . The construction industry and the long-distance transportation industry are even more difficult to convert to alternate power sources . <p> It 's time to be realistic . We live and work in a cold climate , with huge seasonal variations in our ability to use solar power . Crippling our economy and implementing carbon pricing will do nothing significant to address the carbon emissions problem . In my opinion , diverting the always huge bureaucratic expense of implementing and operating a carbon pricing system toward research and development of viable energy alternatives would be a much more rational approach .
