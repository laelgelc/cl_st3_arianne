<h> Province launches reference case before the Saskatchewan Court of Appeal <p> Saskatchewan Premier Scott Moe and Canada 's Minister of Environment and Climate Change Catherine McKenna have been locked in a stalemate over Saskatchewan 's refusal to sign onto the country 's climate change plan and to implement carbon pricing . ( CBC ) <p> Saskatchewan 's opposition to a federally imposed carbon tax has reached the courts . <p> On Wednesday , the government submitted a reference case to the Saskatchewan Court of Appeal to see if the federal carbon tax is unconstitutional . <p> Meili added there 's a " very great likelihood " the federal government will impose its own plan on the province before the court action is resolved . <p> " We 're very likely to have by the end of this year a plan that 's been designed by Trudeau and the Liberals in Ottawa rather than something that 's designed with the best interest of Saskatchewan , " he said . <p> A spokesperson for the premier 's office said the reference case was launched using internal staff of the government . <p> Part of Saskatchewan 's argument is that a federal carbon pricing plan would treat each province differently and thus go against federalism . <p> " It 's certainly , I think , a novel argument , " said Eric Adams , an associate law professor at the University of Alberta . " But we 're in a novel area of the law generally . There are not clear precedents obviously in place , given that this is really the first major foray into carbon pricing by the federal government . " <p> University of Alberta associate professor Eric Adams thinks it 's unlikely the Saskatchewan government will convince the courts of its case . ( CBC ) <p> " There 's no section of the Constitution that says all provinces must be treated exactly the same by every piece of legislation , " he said . <h> Premier ran on anti-carbon pricing campaign <p> Last fall , the province of Manitoba sought a legal opinion to see if it would win a court challenge against the carbon tax . After receiving the opinion , it decided to sign the federal framework . <p> " We do not believe the federal government has the constitutional right to impose the Trudeau carbon tax on Saskatchewan against the wishes of the government and people of Saskatchewan , " Premier Scott Moe said . <p> When Moe won the party leadership in January , he promised to fight the carbon tax . <p> " Justin Trudeau , if you are wondering how far I will go , just watch me , " Moe said during his victory speech . <p> On Wednesday , Moe defended his government 's plan . <p> " We have a made-in-Saskatchewan plan to reduce emissions and fight climate change , and that plan does not include a job-killing carbon tax on Saskatchewan families . " <h> Could win , minister says <p> Former premier Brad Wall long resisted a carbon tax in Saskatchewan and threatened legal action back in 2016 . <p> In March , Meili called the fight against carbon pricing " pointless " and asked what the legal costs would be of taking on Ottawa in court . <p> The federal plan sets a starting price at $10 per tonne of carbon dioxide emissions this year , escalating to $50 per tonne in 2022 . The money collected by the tax would go back to the province . <p> On Wednesday , Duncan said Saskatchewan " is the solution , not the problem . " <p> Duncan also pointed to the agriculture industry sequestering " nearly 12 million tonnes of CO2 annually " and a carbon capture system at Boundary Dam 3.
