( Mackenzie Scott/CBC ) <p> Researchers have been coming to communities in the Beaufort Delta since 2013 , asking residents one question : " How does weather affect what you are doing ? " <p> They are trying to come up with a better way to predict weather in these communities , because research shows northern weather forecasts are not detailed enough . The University of Victoria project is focusing on the N.W.T. communities of Sachs Harbor , Tuktoyaktuk and Ulukhaktok . <p> David Atkinson , associate professor in the department of geography at the University of Victoria , said community knowledge is key to their research since community members " have a culture of observing . " <p> I 'm hoping Environment Canada learns a thing or two about what the people on the ground are reporting for weather. - Manny Kudlak <p> He said the project has been driven by the people in the communities . <p> The researchers have also teamed up with Environment and Climate Change Canada . <p> They are choosing a local research assistant from the Inuvialuit Regional Corporation as well as from each of the three communities . The assistants will go to Edmonton and Victoria in December to learn more about weather forecasts . <p> " People from the communities will see how the forecasts are being done and we 'll establish that two-way linkage between the communities and met(eorological) service of Canada , " said Atkinson . <h> People do n't trust long-term forecasts <p> Armel Castellan is a warning preparedness meteorologist with Environment and Climate Change Canada . He joined Atkinson and fellow researchers on their most recent trip up North . <p> Castellan said community members have told him they 're worried about bringing families out on the land because they do n't trust the long-term weather forecasts that are provided . <p> Ulukhaktok , N.W.T. , the day after a blizzard in March 2018 . ( Mackenzie Scott/CBC ) <p> " They 're getting hotter weather in the summer , they 're getting stronger blizzards , " he said . " They 're on the land so they are much more vulnerable to the weather . So it 's really important to cater to their lifestyle . " <p> He said his department is looking to revamp and improve services across Canada , and part of that push is an effort to create technology that is meant specifically for northern weather . <p> " We are trying to tailor our forecasts to the realities of each location , " he said . <h> Hopes gov't learns ' a thing or two ' <p> Manny Kudlak is the community corporation manager for Sachs Harbor , and was selected to be the research assistant for the community . <p> Kudlak said he worked at the airport for about 10 years , and found that there was a error rate for the weather forecasts of " being 60 per cent wrong and 40 per cent right . " <p> " I 'm hoping Environment Canada learns a thing or two about what the people on the ground are reporting for weather ... the weather phenomenon we have up North , they never really happen down south , " he said .
