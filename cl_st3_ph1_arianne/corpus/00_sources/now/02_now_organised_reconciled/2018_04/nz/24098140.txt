<h> Farmers map out dairying 's future at forum <p> Farmers will discuss how DairyNZ 's new strategy , Dairy Tomorrow , will be achieved at the Farmers ' Forum in early May . <p> The future direction of the New Zealand dairy industry will be up for discussion at a Farmers ' Forum in May . <p> The two day DairyNZ-run event will include Minister of Agriculture Damien O'Connor and Minister for Climate Change James Shaw as well as future of food specialist Julian Cribb , economist Cameron Bagrie , future thinking , strategy and innovation leader Roger Dennis and mental health campaigner Mike King . <p> A panel discussion of dairy company leaders will look at what the Dairy Tomorrow strategy will mean for farmers . <p> DairyNZ chief executive Tim Mackle said Farmers ' Forum at Mystery Creek Events Centre would set the sector up for future and discuss how its new strategy , ' Dairy Tomorrow ' , will be achieved . <p> " The forum will explore where dairy is headed . Understanding our sector 's future from a range of viewpoints is so important @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as well as our markets , drive what we do daily on farms New Zealand-wide . The dairy sector is dynamic and challenging , and as we plan for the future through the strategy it 's a great time to discuss and debate all the issues and opportunities we face . " <p> The forum is expected to attract more than 700 farmers and bring together influential leaders and commentators . <p> O'Connor will discuss the future of dairy farming from the government 's perspective and priorities for food production , while Shaw will talk about darying opportunities for climate change and greenhouse gases . <p> The forum 's first day looks at what is driving change in the global markets , the changing political environment , the implications for New Zealand dairying and how the sector can adapt . <p> On day two the schedule will include new technologies and techniques to help farmers respond to challenges and opportunities , along with tips , tools and advice for boosting farm businesses . Farmers can choose from eight workshops .
