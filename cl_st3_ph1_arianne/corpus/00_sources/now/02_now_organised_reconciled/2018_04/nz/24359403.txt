<p> The government have dropped charges against Greenpeace but two climate activists will still plead guilty for their part in the seismic blasting ship protest . <p> Dr Russel Norman in the ocean off Wairarapa near the Amazon Warrior . <p> Source : Supplied <p> Greenpeace said they were advised on Friday that the Ministry for Business , Innovation and Employment will not pursue the charges laid under the Anadarko Amendment which carry a fine of up to $200,000 . <p> However , Greenpeace executive director Russel Norman and climate activist Sara Howell will still face charges for their involvement . <p> Greenpeace said they will plead guilty and seek a discharge without conviction on the basis their conduct was necessary to bring about required change to government policy . <p> Dr Norman and Ms Howell swam in front of the largest oil exploration ship in the world , the Amazon Warrior , last April and were subsequently charged under the Anadarko Amendment which prevents protest against oil ships at sea . <p> " We have never denied the facts , that we defied the ban on protest at sea , " Dr Norman said . <p> " We see the best course of action now is to plead guilty while making a plea in mitigation in court on the grounds that our actions were reasonable and honourable given the threat of climate change . " <p> Dr Norman said he remained hopeful the court would agree with them . <p> The dropping of charges against Greenpeace was a " breakthrough " Dr Norman said . <p> Dr Norman and Ms Howell will appear by video link from Auckland District Court to lodge their pleas on Monday and the case will be adjourned for sentencing on a date yet to be fixed .
