<h> Climate change puts more pressure on New Zealand 's ecosystems <p> The remnants of ex-cyclone Fehi at Abel Tasman National Park paint a grim picture . <p> The iconic location , known globally for its azure blue water , golden sand beaches and native bush was ravaged by the storm . Slips , damage to the tracks , loss of vegetation , and flooded camp grounds dotted the park . <p> These weather phenomenons , like ex-cyclone Fehi and Gita , are on the rise . Scientists are attributing this to climate change , and the oceans warming up . <p> " It 's no good to go ' right , we 'll just rebuild , we 'll just rebuild ' , because we 'll run out of money . So we need to think about using it really smartly , " DOC conservation services manager Golden Bay Andrew Lamason said . <p> " I think nature has got amazing ability to be resilient and bounce back , all we need to do is to give it the space and capacity to do those things . " <p> ABELTASMAN.COM <p> Abel Tasman National Park was ravaged by ex-cyclone Fehi . <p> Scientists also point out that climate change will have a flow on effect on New Zealand 's native species and ecosystems , putting them under even more pressure . <p> But , Lamason did n't think climate change meant the end of national parks as New Zealanders knew them . <p> " It 'll find a new baseline , there will be some changes . I do n't think it 's going to be the end of the world , I 'm sure national parks have had many times they 've been impacted by different events and they 'll bounced back . " <p> " It 's inevitable there will be more damage on the tracks , particularly in the Abel Tasman , there will be more swells , a higher sustained sea level and stronger winds , so we need to think about how we manage all those things . " <p> Trampers in the Abel Tasman National Park wade through water after a bridge near Marahau was washed out by Cyclone Fehi . A temporary boardwalk has since been installed . <p> Summer 's storms illustrated the impacts climate change is likely to have on biodiversity and infrastructure , Department of Conservation director of operations planning Darryl Lew said . <p> Across New Zealand there was track damage , coastal erosion , and washed out bridges , he said . <p> The storms highlighted the issue of balancing future proofing with the visitor experience . <p> " From a biodiversity perspective , DOC is concerned that climate change has the potential to compound existing issues as well as create a variety of new issues for native species and ecosystems . <p> " Warmer than usual summers has been tough on many native species , for example the Otago Peninsula northern royal albatross have experienced chick hatching failure , as a result of warmer summer temperatures and extreme weather events . " <p> SUPPLIED <p> Northern Royal Albatross chicks fail to hatch due to warmer summer temperatures and extreme weather events . <p> Other victims were West Coast Kiwi chicks , starving because of spring drought , and Northern Royal Albatross chicks failing to hatch due to warmer summer temperatures and extreme weather events . <p> Landcare Research researcher and ecologist Dr Susan Walker said people were aware of the plight of kiwis because they were monitored , but the impact of changing weather patterns on many other species was unknown . <p> " Greater unpredictability , and greater unprecedented extremes will have a whole lot of unpredictable flow on effects . <p> " The more extreme variability and the unpredictable combinations of conditions that we see translates into greater pressure on native species and ecosystems that are already under pressure .
