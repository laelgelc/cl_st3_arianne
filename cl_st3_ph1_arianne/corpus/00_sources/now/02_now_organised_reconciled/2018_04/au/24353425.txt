<p> Fracking has been used around Australia but current regulations are uneven and inconsistent in each state and territory . <p> Last year , Victoria became the first state to permanently ban fracking on land ( or onshore ) , in what then-Minister for Resources Wade Noonan called " a historic day for our state " . <p> There are moratoriums on fracking in Tasmania and Western Australia , while New South Wales has applied certain restrictions on fracking . <p> The new South Australian Liberal government is planning a 10-year ban on fracking in much of the state 's south-east . <p> Queensland more broadly allows the practice and the Northern Territory announced on 17 April its ban on fracking will be lifted . <p> An anti-fracking protest outside the 2017 Annual General Meeting for Origin Energy in Sydney . <p> AAP <p> The federal government is pushing for far more fracking . <p> " I would like all moratoriums and bans across Australia lifted , because more gas is good for jobs and it 's good for energy security and supply , " Minister for the Environment and Energy Josh Frydenberg has said . <h> Do other countries frack ? <p> The US in particular has experienced a fracking boom over the past decade , with widespread use of the practice opening billions of barrels of oil and trillions of cubic feet of natural gas to production . <p> Time Magazine said the US adoption of fracking has meant the country " is on the verge of transforming the world 's energy markets " . <p> A fracking operation in Colorado . <p> Getty <p> But other countries and jurisdictions have been far more hesitant around fracking -- it is banned in many places including New Zealand , Germany , France and Spain . <p> Following a recent fracking ban in Scotland , US senator and 2016 presidential contender Bernie Sanders called on his country to follow suit . He posted on Facebook : " Congratulations to Scotland for taking the very significant step to ban fracking following enormous public opposition to the practice " . <h> What is the economic case in Australia ? <p> While encouraging the Northern Territory government to lift its fracking ban , Prime Minister Malcolm Turnbull pushed the economic opportunities around fracking . <p> " The jobs , the investment , the opportunities that come with opening up 180 years of gas that you 're sitting on is endless , " he said . <p> The Coalition is also hailing gas as key to improving the stability and affordability of electricity . <p> But Associate Professor Gavin Mudd from the RMIT School of Civil and Infrastructure Engineering challanged this theory . <p> " We have a gas crisis because industry started exporting it and quadrupled the price of gas overnight , " he told SBS News . <p> " This is entirely a crisis generated by industry , and now they 're proposing to solve it with more gas ... It 's absolutely insane . " <h> What are the risks of fracking ? <p> Fracking involves injecting very large amounts of water with a small amount of chemicals deep underground . Environmental groups say drinking water and aquifers can become contaminated during this process . <p> Spokesperson for Australian anti-fracking group Lock the Gate , George Woods , told SBS News that shale fracking in particular " is an industry that is very thirsty and creates a considerable amount of waste water " . <p> One research project by the US Environmental Protection Agency has found that fracking can impact drinking water and has resulted in some private drinking wells being unusable . <p> And Ms Woods said there were also many unknowns around water contamination . <p> " The gas gets extracted and the money made in the first short years of boom time , but the long-term change to groundwater takes place over decades and centuries , " she said . <p> " Australia 's environment laws are simply not equipped to deal with the damage that occurs over these time frames . " <p> She also said the " flaring and venting of gas " from fracking projects releases volatile compounds into the atmosphere , which can similarly be dangerous for the environment and human health . <p> One recent study by the Concerned Health Professionals of New York and the Nobel Peace Prize-winning group Physicians for Social Responsibility was particularly scathing about fracking in the US . <p> British actress Emma Thompson at an anti-fracking protest in the UK . <p> Getty <p> Drawing on news investigations , government assessments and more than 1,200 peer-reviewed research articles , its conclusion was " our examination ... uncovered no evidence that fracking can be practiced in a manner that does not threaten human health " . <p> The study found that - at least in the US - there were increasing dangers to health around fracking , including respiratory disease , cancer risk , and low birth weight and preterm birth . <p> One of the report 's co-authors Dr Sandra Steingraber said " what impressed us , as we reviewed and compiled the data , is just how extensive the impacts from drilling and fracking processes are " . <h> What about climate change ? <p> As an energy source , gas is sometimes considered cleaner than coal as it does not produce the same level of carbon emissions as coal when burned . <p> Material from oil and gas company Santos says " natural gas plays an essential role in reducing global carbon emissions " . <p> But RMIT 's Dr Mudd told SBS News this is a " huge " misconception . <p> He said burning black coal leads about one tonne of carbon emissions for every megawatt hour of electricity generated , while that figure is 0.7 tonnes for gas and less than 0.1 tonnes for renewables . <p> " So gas still leads to at least seven times more carbon emissions than renewables , " he said . <p> " Thinking that gas is a clean form of energy is tragic ...
