<h> Energy blueprint hangs in the balance ahead of crucial talks <h> By Nicole Hasham <p> 13 April 2018 -- 5:00pm <p> The Turnbull government 's energy blueprint promises to make the electricity sector more transparent and competitive but the policy hung in the balance on Friday night , as states and territories awaited critical detail on how emissions would be slashed . <p> A draft design of the government 's controversial national energy guarantee has been circulated to energy ministers ahead of critical negotiations next Friday . <p> Energy ministers from across Australia will next week decide whether to adopt the federal government 's national energy guarantee . <p> Photo : Chris Ratcliffe <p> The policy will set reliability and emissions standards for electricity retailers . It aims to make electricity more affordable and secure , as well as cutting dangerous greenhouse gas emissions in line with Australia 's obligations under the Paris climate deal . <p> Environment and Energy Minister Josh Frydenberg must undertake the delicate task of winning consensus support from the states and territories -- including allaying concern from some quarters about inadequate emissions reduction -- @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Council of Australian Governments energy ministers will be asked to approve more detailed design of the policy , ahead of a final design to be presented in August . <p> Advertisement <p> Under the policy , retailers will be required to enter contracts with generators for power that meets emissions and reliability criteria . <p> There were widely-held fears this would place an onerous compliance burden on small retailers and make it harder to buy the power they need , which could reduce competition and put upward pressure on power prices . <p> The draft design by the independent energy security board shows this concern has been heeded . Among the measures proposed , contracts will not require retailers to specify the physical generators the power is being sourced from -- a move which is expected to avoid entrenching the market power of larger electricity retailers , especially the " gentailers " AGL , Origin and Energy Australia which also generate electricity . <p> The policy will also require retailers to support a range of power generation - not just coal-fired electricity - through their contracts . <p> Energy and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 's backbench to make the policy more coal-friendly . <p> Photo : Alex Ellinghausen <p> The ACT government has been the most vocal critic of the national energy guarantee . The territory 's Climate Change and Sustainability Minister Shane Rattenbury said the draft contained helpful detail , and he welcomed efforts to improve transparency in gentailer electricity transactions . <p> But as of Friday afternoon , the federal government had not revealed the emissions reduction requirements of the policy , including how the national emissions target will be set . <p> Mr Rattenbury said those issues were " fundamental to how this shapes up " and his government could not form a view on the policy until the details were known . <p> Labor governments in Victoria and Queensland , which both have ambitious renewable energy targets that could be affected by the national policy , declined to comment on the draft design . <p> Mr Frydenberg said the guarantee would " get the market working again for consumers delivering the right investment signals to generate the right investment in the right place at the right time " . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ advised the ACT government on the national energy guarantee , said the design paper addressed " a lot of the concerns we had ... the policy looks a lot more workable than it has previously .
