" <h> Policy experts apply pressure for change <p> Energy policy experts have argued the energy sector should be expected to make much deeper cuts than other sectors of the economy because the growth in renewables made it cheaper for them to achieve those cuts . <p> Experts including Anna Skarbek from ClimateWorks Australia at Monash University have argued if the electricity sector was not required to do more , then others like farmers and the transport industry would be forced to do extra work . <p> The ACT , Victoria and Queensland have higher targets for cutting their emissions , and are angry Mr Frydenberg will not agree to the NEG recognising the extra work they propose to do . <p> ACT Climate Change Minister Shane Rattenbury said that would undermine work done by the ACT , which has a target of 100 per cent renewable energy by 2020 . <p> John Grimes from the Smart Energy Council called the NEG a diabolical policy . <p> He said the nation was on track to be meeting the 26 per cent emissions reduction from the electricity sector by 2020 , so he argued there would be no new projects built in the following decade . <p> " This policy is designed to put a cap on renewable energy and lock in the central place of coal , " Mr Grimes said . <p> " It will not encourage uptake of renewables . It will not provide investment certainty . The only certainty it provides is no large-scale projects will be built . " <p> Mr Grimes called the emissions reductions targets a joke and said electricity emissions should be cut by more than 50 per cent by 2030 . <p> The renewable energy industry is planning a crisis meeting on Monday to discuss the issue . <p> If there is agreement with state and territory governments across the nation there is still another hurdle to overcome . <p> The plan also needs to gain support from the federal Coalition party room , which includes a number of outspoken backbenchers who are concerned the changes would diminish the role of coal-fired power .
