<p> Fiji 's prime minister has said the Pacific island nation is in " a fight for survival " as climate change brings " almost constant " deadly cyclones . <p> Frank Bainimarama said Fiji had entered a " frightening new era " of extreme weather that needed to be confronted . <p> His comments came after Cyclone Josie caused deaths and flooding on Fiji 's main island , Viti Levu , at the weekend . <p> In 2016 , a cyclone hit Fiji leaving 44 people dead and wiping out one-third of the nation 's economic production . <p> Four people have died in severe flooding caused by Cyclone Josie , according to Reuters news agency . <p> " We are now at an almost constant level of threat from these extreme weather events , " Mr Bainimarama said on Tuesday , adding that powerful cyclones in the region were becoming " more severe " as a result of climate change . <p> " We need to get the message out loud and clear to the entire world about the absolute need to confront this crisis head on , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ are starting to build our resilience in response to the frightening new era that is upon us , " he added . <p> Last November , Mr Bainimarama took a leading role at the UN 's Climate Conference in the German city of Bonn . <p> The planet 's climate has constantly been changing over time . However , the current period of warming is occurring more rapidly than many past events . <p> The changes could drive freshwater shortages , bring sweeping changes in food production conditions , and increase the number of deaths from floods , storms , heat waves and droughts . <p> Climate change is expected to increase the frequency of extreme weather events - though linking any single event to global warming is complicated .
