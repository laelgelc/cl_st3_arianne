<h> ECOWAS , NEMA collaborate on disaster risk management <p> Stakeholders at the capacity workshop organised for National Emergency Management Agency staff and others on Tuesday in Abuja <p> The Economic Community of West African States ( ECOWAS ) has called for the strengthening of the capacity of disaster risk management agencies at local , states and national levels as well as other stakeholders in disaster response . <p> Dr Siga Jagne , ECOWAS Commissioner for Social Affairs and Gender , made the call at a three-day capacity building workshop organised for National Emergency Management Agency ( NEMA ) staff and other stakeholders . <p> The workshop , which began on Tuesday in Abuja , was organised by NEMA in collaboration with ECOWAS commission and the World Bank . <p> The workshop is tagged : " Integrating Climate Change and COVID-19 Vulnerability Assessment and Adaptation Planning for Disaster Risk Management in Nigeria . " <p> Jagne said that the challenges posed by the climate change and COVID-19 had significantly undermined development and sustainability in the West African sub region . <p> She said that the ECOWAS commission was committed to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ coordination , planning and policy advisory . <p> " It is , therefore , necessary that for the implementation and adaptation of actions , the capacity of disaster risk management agencies at the local , states and national levels and other stakeholders be strengthened . <p> " Hence , there is a need to build capacity on the understanding of issues concerning COVID-19 and climate change awareness or relevance . <p> " Also on vulnerability assessment and adaptation planning as well as COVID-19 recovery need assessment for disaster risk reduction in Nigeria , " she said . <p> According to her , Nigeria , in particular , is one of the countries in the sub-region seriously affected by natural disasters , epidemics and human-induced calamities . <p> " A combination of these events has led to huge economic losses and unavoidably deaths , " she said . <p> Speaking , Retired Air Vice Marshal Muhammadu Muhammed , Director-General , NEMA , said that climate change had added to the problems of environmental degradation and rapid unplanned urban growth . <p> He said that the situation had further reduced communities ' @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as increased drought in some regions would lead to land degradation , reduced crop yield among others . <p> The director-general said that it was important for disaster managers to evolve from the traditional ways of handling disasters and quickly adopt new ways to reduce hazards . <p> " The impacts of climate change and COVID-19 cut across all known boundaries , spatial or geographical , economic , political technological or social and cultural . <p> " Developed and developing nations are not immune to these adverse effects . <p> " The risk and dynamics of COVID-19 pandemic and climate change vulnerability have given us the warning that we have to quickly adapt to the new situation or we suffer the devastating consequences , " he said . <p> The News Agency of Nigeria ( NAN )
