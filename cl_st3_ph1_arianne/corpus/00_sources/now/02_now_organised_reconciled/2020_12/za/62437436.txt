<h> The UK 's commitment to ban the sale of new petrol and diesel vehicles from 2030 to combat climate change means South Africa 's automotive industry needs to shift gears , and fast . <p> News that the UK plans to ban the sale of new internal combustion engine-powered ( ICE ) vehicles from 2030 ( and hybrids from 2035 ) might simplistically be dismissed as " First World problems " . However , if 6.4% of your GDP comes from vehicle manufacturing and your largest export market is the UK , this requires pause for thought . <p> The UK 's moving deadline mirrors the aggressive move to electric vehicles ( EVs ) in the EU . In the bloc , incentives to move motorists into diesel cars in order to reduce carbon emissions had the unexpected side effect of creating health problems related to the particulate and NOx emissions . As a result , France has said it will ban the sale of ICE cars in 2035 , and Germany in 2030 as well . <p> Dr Norman Lamprecht , National Association of Automobile Manufacturers of SA @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , says three out of every four cars exported from SA go to the EU and the UK . In 2019 , more than 100,000 cars built in SA were delivered to UK customers alone . <p> The UK 's move is designed to kick-start investment and innovation in a post-Brexit paradigm and to meet its climate change agreements . <p> " The UK 's commitment to ban the sale of new petrol and diesel vehicles from 2030 is part of our ambitious response to the threat of climate change , " says UK High Commissioner to South Africa Nigel Casey . <p> " As a major market for , and partner with , South Africa 's automotive industry , we hope this will help advance consideration of investment in the production of electric vehicles here , both to protect export markets and to support the use of electric vehicles locally , " says Casey . <p> There are other factors at play too .
