Reuters <p> The COVID-19 pandemic has fueled a 40% increase in the number of people needing humanitarian assistance around the globe , the United Nations said on Tuesday , as it appealed for roughly $35 billion to help many of those expected to be in need next year . <p> " If everyone who will need humanitarian aid next year lived in one country , it would be the world 's fifth largest nation , " UN aid chief Mark Lowcock said . <p> " The pandemic has wreaked carnage across the most fragile and vulnerable countries , " he added . <p> The United Nations has set out 34 humanitarian response plans covering 56 countries for 2021 , aiming to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ million most vulnerable people worldwide facing hunger , conflict and the impacts of climate change and the coronavirus pandemic . <p> " We always aim to reach about two-thirds of those in need because others , for example the Red Cross , will try to meet the remaining gap , " Lowcock said . <p> He said this year donors gave a record $17 billion to fund humanitarian operations and data showed that aid reached 70% of the people targeted . <p> While Lowcock noted the $35 billion needed for 2021 was a lot of money , he said it was a " very small " amount compared to what rich countries have spent protecting their citizens during the pandemic . <p> Key among the concerns for Lowcock is averting famines in countries including Yemen , Afghanistan , northeast Nigeria , South Sudan , Democratic Republic of the Congo and Burkina Faso . <p> " There is a clear and present danger of really a large scale famine in Yemen now and the single biggest reason for that is because some very important countries who provided a lot of assistance @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ done that in 2020 and those are the countries of the Gulf , " he said .
