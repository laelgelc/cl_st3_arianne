<p> The current pledges by governments across the world to reduce emissions are insufficient to keep global warming within safe levels ( that is , below 2 ? C ) . Although with recent developments in China and the US , the target might once again be within reach if all nations actually deliver on their pledges . <p> More radical proposals to reduce climate change risks are being debated . Among these is solar geoengineering ( also called solar radiation management or modification ) . The most commonly proposed solar radiation management technique involves the deliberate injection of sulfur aerosols into the stratosphere -- the layer of the atmosphere around 15km above Earth 's surface -- to create a thin veil that would reflect a small amount of sunlight back to space . This in turn would cool Earth and offset greenhouse gas warming . <p> Although solar radiation management has never been tested outdoors , large volcanic eruptions that have injected aerosols high into the atmosphere and briefly cooled the climate are evidence that increasing stratospheric aerosols could cool the planet .
