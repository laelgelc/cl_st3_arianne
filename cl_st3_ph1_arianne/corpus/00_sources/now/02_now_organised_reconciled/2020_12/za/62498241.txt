<p> NIAMEY , Dec 23 ( Reuters ) -- In a year marked by setbacks for West African democracy , Niger is poised to hold an election on Sunday that will lead to the country 's first transition of power between two freely elected presidents . <p> President Mahamadou Issoufou is stepping aside after two five-year terms , in contrast to leaders in Ivory Coast and Guinea who used constitutional changes to extend their tenures to three terms . <p> That represents a milestone for Niger , which has experienced four coups since gaining independence from France in 1960 . The practical significance , however , is less clear . Mohamed Bazoum , Issoufou 's handpicked successor and the favourite in the race , is campaigning on a message of near-total continuity . <p> " We are going to consolidate the gains of the past 10 years , " Bazoum said on the campaign trail . " We are going to continue in the direction set by President Mahamadou Issoufou . " <p> The new president will inherit a security crisis . Twice the size of France , Niger touches @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ western border with Mali and Burkina , where jihadists linked to al Qaeda and Islamic State are active , and another near its southeastern border with Nigeria , where Boko Haram operates . <p> In the past year alone , hundreds of Nigerien soldiers and civilians have been killed in attacks . The latest , blamed on Boko Haram , killed 28 people on Dec. 12 . <p> Pervasive corruption has undermined the fight . A government audit this year found almost 40% of the $312 million Niger spent on defence contracts in the past three years was lost through inflated costs or materiel that was not delivered . <p> The economy will be another challenge . More than 40% of the population lived in extreme poverty in 2019 , according to the World Bank . The COVID-19 pandemic , low prices for top export uranium and the impact of climate change have all further hit the economy this year . <p> Bazoum will face 29 other candidates , including Mahamane Ousmame , who was president from 1993-1996 , and Seini Oumarou , a former prime minister . The runner-up @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ from running because of a criminal conviction . <p> Only Bazoum has waged much of a campaign so far , owing to the ruling party 's vast financial advantages . The election will go to a second round if no candidate receives more than 50% of the vote . <p> " We think a transfer of power is a good thing , but if Bazoum is elected , he will need to combat many ills , including corruption and the politicization of the civil service , " said Bibata Garba , a nurse in the capital Niamey .
