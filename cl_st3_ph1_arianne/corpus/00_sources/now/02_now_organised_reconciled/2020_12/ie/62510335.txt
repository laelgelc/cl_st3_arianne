<h> ' State has to act on the level of toxins in our rivers ' <p> Mountcollins/Brosna Anglers ' Association PRO Brendan Danaher responds to the dire warning from the Environmental Protection Agency on the need for speedy action in protecting our biosphere -- not least its rivers , just 20 of which remain ' pristine ' <p> As salmon and sea trout anglers we are acutely aware of the issues affecting water quality , particularly in relation to agriculture and other land management practices which are key drivers behind the recent increases in nitrates and phosphorous entering our rivers . <p> The recently published EPA report clearly indentifies forestry as a prevalent pressure causing damage to our rivers and affecting 16 per cent of water bodies . The River Basin Management Plan 2018 - 2021 has already identified water quality issues in local tributaries of the Feale such as the Clydagh , the Caher and the Breanagh . All three tributaries have one thing in common and that is that their catchments and uplands in particular are covered in forestry . <p> These blanket coniferous plantations are destroying the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and siltation , physical alterations in spawning habitat and can cause organis enrichment which leads to oxygen depletion in feeder streams for smolt and parr . Salmon anglers are aware that a huge amount of chemicals are used on Sitka Spruce plantations . Salmon anglers find it astonishing that Coillte is being granted derogations by the Forest Stewardship Council ( FSC ) for the use of a chemical called cypermethrin which was banned in France , Germany , Sweden and Italy ten years ago . <p> Cypermethrin is sprayed on forestry by Coillte to prevent and control the harmful pine weevil pest decimating non-native spruce and pine trees . <p> It is regrettable that Government agencies still continue to allow the planting of Sitka Spruce in the forlorn belief that it helps to meet our climate change obligations , even though they must surely know by now that such plantations are doing immense damage to our finest salmon rivers . <p> We as anglers believe that action speaks louder than words and if politicians and Government agencies are serious about protecting the environment then they need to take our concerns @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ idly by and see our finest rivers and lakes being destroyed before our eyes . <p> I know and they know that a new sense of urgency is needed to address the issues raised in this article . They can no longer pretend to be unaware of the seriousness of the challenge . <p> It is no surprise to anglers that the EPA Director General stated recently that Ireland is good at signing up to EU directives but that the aspirations do not meet reality and that only 20 pristine rivers are left in Ireland . <p> Can we seriously portray ourselves as the saviours of a clean , green island ? Cherish the thought .
