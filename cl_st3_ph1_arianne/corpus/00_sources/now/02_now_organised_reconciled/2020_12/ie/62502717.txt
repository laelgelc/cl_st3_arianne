<h> 2021 BT Young Scientists protect farmers and animals <p> Students showcasing their projects at the 2021 virtual BT Young Scientist and Technology Exhibition , which will run from January 6 to 8 , have demonstrated a growing awareness of dangers on the farm , delivering solutions and opportunities to protect farmers and animals from accidents and theft . <p> Projects for 2021 vary from new devices to assist in the safe transporting of cattle to a sheep tagging device to prevent theft . <p> These projects also feed into a wider concern for pollution and climate change -- a continuing trend from previous years -- with several projects examining the sequestration of carbon on Irish farms , while others examined the possible move towards biodegradable netting of hay bales . <p> In addition to farming-specific projects around issues such as equipment and machinery , there are a number of projects included in this year 's exhibition which would impact , improve or alter farming and agriculture activity including projects based on weather forecasting , food longevity and sustainability . <h> Trends <p> A vast array of topics of interest @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ below . <p> Loreto Secondary School , Balbriggan , explored whether vertical farming can be used as a more efficient farming method than conventional arable farming in Ireland . <p> Templeogue College , Dublin , explored at epigenetics and its role in helping crops deal with climate change . <p> St. Brigid 's Secondary School in Kerry asked if vertical farming could be the future of healthy schools .
