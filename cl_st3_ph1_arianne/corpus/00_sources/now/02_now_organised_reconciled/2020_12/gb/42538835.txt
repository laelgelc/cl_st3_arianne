<h> Teresa Ribera <p> THE GREEN ENGINE <p> Doer No. 8 -- Spain <p> Teresa Ribera is the rare policy theorist who 's been entrusted with the keys to the car -- in this case the Spanish economy she hopes one day will hum along like an electric vehicle . An architect of the Paris climate accords , Spain 's minister for ecological transition is laying the concrete policy foundation to make Brussels ' plan to " build back better " go from mantra to post-pandemic reality , while also creating a new model for economic transformation in Southern Europe . <p> Around a third of the EU 's planned ? 750 billion recovery fund is devoted to tackling climate change , thanks in large part to Ribera 's quiet but forceful style of diplomacy . She was one of the first to call for a green coronavirus comeback , and also one of the most credible . Ribera 's Southern European credentials help her make the case for decarbonization from one economically fragile country to another -- she 's on the phone regularly with her Eastern European counterparts . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ wealthy capitals like Copenhagen , which is usually met with eyerolls in less prosperous corners . <p> At home , Ribera , 51 , has worked out deals to get rid of coal and phase out nuclear power , and she made sure chunks of next year 's budget were earmarked for renewable energy and sustainable housing . Her vision : Make Spain the Denmark of the south by seizing on the coronavirus crisis to shift the tourism- and service-based economy to green tech . Portugal , Greece and Italy are watching closely . <p> Her success comes with a downside . Ribera has become Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez 's go-to for difficult long-term problems -- and that could pose its own long-term problems : In addition to climate , she 's also been tapped to deal with the broader coronavirus recovery , as well as the fallout from young people 's exodus from rural areas . The risk is that the weight of her portfolio turns out to be unsustainable.
