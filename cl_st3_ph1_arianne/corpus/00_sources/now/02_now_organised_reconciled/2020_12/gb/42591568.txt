You can unsubscribe at any time . <p> Hundreds of new trees have been planted at a Nottinghamshire nature reserve after volunteers banded together in a bid to improve habitats on the site . <p> Volunteers at Nottinghamshire Wildlife Trust 's Skylarks Nature Reserve have planted 750 trees after the reserve was awarded a grant from the Tree Council 's Branching Out Fund . <p> Regular volunteers and the reserve 's Young Ranger group were joined by family members for a series of socially-distanced tree planting sessions at the site . <p> The trust says Skylarks , in Adbolton Lane , Nottingham , has become " hugely popular " as a place to take exercise and connect with nature since the first national lockdown began in March . <p> Over the course of three days , the teams saw a wide range of weather conditions including sleet and rain -- but all 750 trees were planted , giving the reserve 's wildlife a much-needed boost . <p> Erin McDaid , Nottinghamshire Wildlife Trust 's head of communications , said : " Like many of our nature reserves , Skylarks has proved hugely popular with local visitors during the pandemic , especially during the lockdown periods and as many people have discovered new natural green spaces on their doorstep . <p> " Thanks to the support of the Tree Council and our wonderful volunteers , we 've been able to give the site a bit of much needed TLC . " <p> As well as absorbing carbon and helping offset impacts of climate change , it is hoped the new trees will provide food , shelter and nesting sites for a wide range of wildlife . <p> The reserve at Holme Pierrepont , created in 1982 , is believed to be the first of its kind in the country laid out specifically for the benefit of wheelchair users . <p> Once a commercial gravel pit , the area has since been colonised with plants and animals . A range of wetland birds can be seen on the reserve , such as great crested grebe , kingfisher and reed bunting . <p> The site was significantly expanded in 2014 and now covers 115 acres , providing a natural haven , protecting important habitats and access to nature for the 100,000 people who live within 10 minutes of the reserve . <p> Skylarks Nature Reserve has a variety of routes and walks , including a two-mile footpath linking viewing screens and board-walks , situated at prime locations around the site , providing views and access to lakes , woodlands , ponds , reed beds , meadows , scrapes and islands .
