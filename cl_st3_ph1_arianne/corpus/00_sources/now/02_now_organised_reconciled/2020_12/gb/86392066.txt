<p> Every morning , students in Assam 's Pamohi village go to school clutching a bag of plastic waste , in exchange for which they will get their day 's lessons . <p> Akshar School , founded by Mazin Mukhtar , 32 , and his wife Parmita Sarma , 30 , has turned its pupils into ecowarriors by waiving school fees and helping to stop local people burning used plastic . <p> The village 's toxic bonfires were causing such an environmental hazard , children were coughing and wheezing in their classrooms . <p> The school was founded to provide an education for children in the area , most of whom were working in the local stone quarries , earning about $3 ( ? 2.25 ) a day . Few of the hard-pushed parents were keen to send an earning member of the family to study . <p> Mazin Mukhtar , who with his wife Sarma founded Akshar school in Pamohi . Students are taught how to install solar panels and attend carpentry and electronics workshops , says Muhktar , an African-American who came to India in 2013 from New York to work on a school project in Assam where he met Sarma , a student of social work at Guwahati University . The couple set up Akshar in 2016 , raising money to build it and funding from private donors to run it . <p> From the original 20 students , Akshar now has seven teachers managing 110 children aged from 4 to 15 , and a 100-strong waiting list . <p> " We try to teach students to take responsibility for their surroundings and to strive @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ we collect at least 25 units of plastic every week from each student , we are able to muster upwards of 10,000 pieces of plastic each month .
