<h> Lessons and resolutions learned for farming industry <h> Great quality food at the centre of farming <p> HEAD of UK Agriculture for Virgin Money 's Clydesdale and Yorkshire Bank , Brian Richardson writes he has seen how farmers have had to be more resilient than usual . <p> Starting off the year with record-breaking floods across Yorkshire and Wales , followed by periods of very dry weather in the Spring , it was soon clear that 2020 was going to be the year to really test everyone . <p> Of course , then the coronavirus pandemic came , initially disrupting farming operations and just-in-time supply chains . Never will we forget the images of thousands of litres of milk being wasted and going unused , as cafes and restaurants went into lockdown and the hospitality sector demand was put on hold . Nor will we forget the impact on beef prices , with the foodservice market for highest value cuts collapsing overnight . There is no doubt that the years ahead of us will have their ups and downs -- much like the pandemic . However , we now know that preparing for potential shocks is key to an adequate response . So to keep the industry resilient , we should heed the lessons of the last nine months and take steps to adjust ahead of time . <p> As a bank , we are focused on supporting our customers and the agricultural sector on planning for decarbonisation and moving towards sustainable farming outside of the EU CAP . For example , agri-technology can pave the way for new forms of knowledge-sharing among farmers .
