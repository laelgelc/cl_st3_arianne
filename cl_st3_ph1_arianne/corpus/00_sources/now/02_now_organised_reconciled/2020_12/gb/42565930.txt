<p> Scientists in South Korea have developed a team of aquatic robots dedicated to thinning out the numbers of jellyfish swarms . Known as the Jellyfish Elimination Robotic Swarm ( or JEROS ) the sea-bound robots use a combined GPS and camera system to detect jellyfish before catching them in nets . <p> The JEROS system is estimated to be three times more economical than manual removal of the jellyfish , with the robots -- which travel at a speed of 6 knots or 7mph -- eliminating around 400kg of the invertebrates an hour . <p> The team has been led by Professor Myung Hyun , who has been working in response to the growing danger to businesses and individuals from swarms -- or blooms , as they are technically known - of jellyfish . <p> " To overcome this problem , " writes Hyun , " a jellyfish removal system with trawl boats equipped with the jellyfish removal net has been suggested .
