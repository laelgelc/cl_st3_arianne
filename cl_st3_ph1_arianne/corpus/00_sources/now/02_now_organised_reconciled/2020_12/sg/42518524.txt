<p> Lab-grown meat , particularly ' chicken , ' will soon be available in restaurants in Singapore , after the city-state became the first to green-light meat created without slaughtering any animals . <p> US start-up Eat Just have announced that its meat had been approved for sale in the city-state as an ingredient in chicken nuggets . <p> The news marks a " breakthrough for the global food industry , " said the company , as firms increasingly try to find less environmentally harmful ways of producing meat . <p> " I 'm sure that our regulatory approval for cultured meat will be the first of many in Singapore and in countries around the globe , " said Josh Tetrick , co-founder and CEO of Eat Just . <p> Consumption of regular meat is an environmental threat as cattle produce methane , a potent greenhouse gas , while logging forests to make way for animals destroys natural barriers against climate change . <p> Demand for sustainable alternatives to meat is rising due to growing consumer pressure , but other products in the market are plant-based . <p> There were @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ spokesman for Eat Just said the company had made " considerable progress " in lowering the Eat Just 's lab-grown chicken in nuggets after concluding it was safe for consumption . <p> The agency said it had put in place a framework for " novel foods " which do not have a history of being consumed by humans to ensure safety standards are met before they go on sale . <p> The high-tech city-state has become a hub for the development of sustainable foods in recent years , with local start-ups concocting dishes suited to Asian palates . <p> These range from lab-grown " seafood " to dumplings made with tropical fruit instead of pork .
