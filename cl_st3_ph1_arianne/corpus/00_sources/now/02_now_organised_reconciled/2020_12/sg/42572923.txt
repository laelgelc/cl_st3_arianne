<h> Climate summit recognises US deal <h> Share <p> Delegates at the Copenhagen summit have passed a motion recognising a US-backed agreement on climate change . But correspondents say it is not yet clear whether the motion is unanimous or what its legal implications are . <p> Delegates at the climate summit had been battling to prevent the talks ending without reaching a final deal . <p> Earlier , a US-led group of five nations - including China - tabled a last-minute proposal that President Barack Obama called a " meaningful agreement " . <p> However , it had been rejected by a few developing nations which felt it failed to deliver the actions needed to halt dangerous climate change . <p> The majority of nations had been urging the Danish hosts to adopt the deal . <p> " The conference decides to take note of the Copenhagen Accord of December 18 , 2009 , " the chairman of the plenary session of the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change ( UNFCCC ) declared on Saturday morning , swiftly banging down his gavel . <p> To be accepted as an @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ by all 193 nations at the talks . <p> On Friday evening , the US , China , India , Brazil and South Africa reached a last-minute agreement on a number of issues , such as a recognition to limit temperature rises to less than 2 deg C. <p> However , a number of developing nations were angered by the draft proposals . <p> BBC environment correspondent Richard Black said the language in this text showed 2C was not a formal target , just that the group " recognises the scientific view that " the temperature increase should be held below this figure . <p> The five-nation proposal had promised to deliver US$30bn of aid for developing nations over the next three years , and outlined a goal of providing $100bn a year by 2020 to help poor countries cope with the impacts of climate change . <p> The agreement also included a method for verifying industrialised nations ' reduction of emissions .
