<h> Choice and opportunity for African farmers will transform Africa <p> ' A hungry man is not a free man . He can not focus on anything else but securing his next meal . ' So proclaimed the late Kofi Annan . <p> In 2003 , Kofi Annan and a like-minded group of African leaders recognized hunger as a complex crisis on the continent . <p> They saw the eradication of hunger as not just an end in itself -- but the first step towards sustainable development and progress , requiring the transformation of African agriculture . <p> In order to address this , three momentous events occurred at that time . In 2003 , the Comprehensive African Agriculture Development Programme ( CAADP ) was launched to provide a policy framework for the transformation of African agriculture . <p> We need African solutions to African problems . When an African farmer has access to better technology and finance , they see improved productivity , food security and income . Most of the big mistakes in development have happened when external actors have foisted their ideas and ideologies on the continent @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in Africa ( AGRA ) , the organization I lead , was established to turn these ideas into reality . We are founded on the belief that the only way to do this is at scale -- and yet with a focus on the farmer . <p> And the Africa Fertilizer conference was held , to increase access to crop nourishment -- identified as the weakest link in the farming chain . <p> These measures have reaped rewards . <p> Across Africa , we have directly reached millions of farmers with increased access to technology , investment in research , financial support or training . <p> For instance , we have helped establish over 110 African seed companies , with some 700,000 tonnes of seed now available to 20 million farmers . Countries like Ghana and Mali had no seed suppliers , and now have an average of six each . <p> Healthy soil is fundamental to a productive global food system . However , many smallholder farmers do not have means to prevent or address soil degradation problems . As the world commemorates the World Soil Day , we @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ reverse decades of soil depletion wherever we work . <p> We have taken the lead in providing evidence to governments on the value and challenges of subsidies being used in agriculture . We advocate for national policies that benefit smallholder farmers . We support upgraded storage facilities , better market information systems , stronger farmers ' associations , and more credit for farmers and suppliers . <p> There is still much to do , however . There are approximately 45 million farmers on the continent -- African governments and investors must reach all of them if we are to see an end to poverty and hunger . <p> There are also new challenges . Climate change has the potential to reverse the continent 's hard won gains . <p> Desertification threatens productive lands . Locusts , armyworm and diseases like the Maize Lethal Necrosis wipe out the livelihoods of hundreds of thousands . Currently , COVID-19 is pushing tens of millions more into malnutrition , while farmers see their choices diminished . <p> As a proud African , I share Kofi Annan 's optimism and conviction . The eleven countries we support have all advanced in the last ten years through hard work and investment . With ten years to go to meet the Sustainable Development Goals , it is important now to reflect on progress , and positioning for future gains . <p> Inclusive agriculture transformation is not a quick fix . It requires a long-term focus . They do not want to be stuck with 40-year-old seed varieties .
