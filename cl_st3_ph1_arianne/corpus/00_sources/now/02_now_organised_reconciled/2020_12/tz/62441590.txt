<h> World soil day focuses on soil management challenges <p> The Alliance for Food Sovereignty in Africa ( AFSA ) joins the international community to commemorate the World soil day which is to be celebrate this Saturday ( December 5th ) to highlight the growing global challenges in soil management <p> AFSA strongly believes that conserving agricultural biodiversity and maintaining soil fertility is among the most urgent and grave challenges of the global fight against food insecurity and climate action <p> In his remarks , the AFSA General Coordinator , Dr. Million Belay , said , " ... soil is the foundation of life . Our life on earth depends on the life and health of our soil . No aspect of human life is more dependent on soil than agriculture . A farming system that does n't care and nourish the soil will collapse agricultural productivity and contribute to climate change . " <p> " Agro ecology offers the best and systemic soil biodiversity and water resource management solution . Agroecology is the future to cool the planet , nourish our soils and feed the world with healthy and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ increasing the biodiversity of soils through agro ecological practices , smallholder farmers and food producers in Africa could sustain food and water security while contributing to climate change mitigation and adaptation . " <p> He said this year 's celebration is critical to highlight how the industrial agriculture paradigm is poisoning the Soil , polluting the air and depleting biodiversity by excessive use of agrochemicals and intensive , high-input , monoculture farming . <p> Bridget Mugambe , Program Coordinator of AFSA , said , " The campaign for " Agroecology for Healthy Soil " is particularly relevant to AFSA to show the urgent need to transition to agroecology to save our soils , solve the climate crisis , revive biodiversity , improve food security and create a pathway for a healthy and resilient future for humans and all life forms on earth .
