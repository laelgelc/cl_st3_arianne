Yves Herman , Reuters/file <p> BARCELONA - Heat-related deaths are surging around the world , particularly among older people , scientists said on Thursday , warning of growing pressure on health systems hit hard by COVID-19 . <p> Almost 300,000 people over the age of 65 died from extreme heat in 2018 - a 54 percent rise in two decades , said a report on the links between health and climate in The Lancet medical journal . <p> Higher temperatures are also making it impossible for people to work outdoors in sweltering conditions , with 302 billion work hours lost in 2019 , up from 199 billion in 2000 , it said . <p> " Climate-induced shocks are claiming lives , damaging health and disrupting livelihoods in all parts of the world right now , " said Ian Hamilton , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ mosquito-borne diseases , said the study by 35 academic institutions and UN agencies . <p> The new coronavirus pandemic has thrown a spotlight on the ability of health systems to cope with the sorts of health shocks climate change could cause in future , said Hugh Montgomery , Lancet Countdown co-chairman . <p> " A nation 's wealth offers no protection against the health impacts of even a 1.2C global average temperature rise , " he said , referring to the level of warming since preindustrial times . <p> " Flames , floods and famine do not respect national borders or bank accounts , " said Montgomery , a professor of intensive care medicine at University College London who has been caring for COVID-19 patients . <p> The growing health threats linked to climate change could overwhelm
