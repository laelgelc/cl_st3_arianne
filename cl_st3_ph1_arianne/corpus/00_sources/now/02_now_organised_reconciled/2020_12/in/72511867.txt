<h> The New Wilderness , Diane Cook 's Booker Prize-nominated novel , holds up a mirror to the not-so-distant future of mankind . <p> In a world increasingly ravaged by climate change and degradation of the environment , the plot of Diane Cook 's novel -- The New Wilderness ( published in 2020 ) -- is not very hard to envisage . <p> Set in the future that is closer than we would like to believe , the world ( or should we say the City ) is engulfed in smog and pollution . The City is a metropolis that is over-populated and to meet the housing and other needs of the inhabitants , is consequently overbuilt . And here is where the human population is concentrated . <p> Children are suffocating in the poisoned air from the day they are born and one among them is five-year-old Agnes . Her mother Bea can not bear to see her daughter and her tiny lungs suffer , and the mother-daughter duo shifts to the Wilderness State along with 18 other volunteers . And they have one rule -- the newcomers can not leave any trace of their existence in the wild . <p> The twists in plot explore the relationship between Bea and Agnes as the latter gets increasingly close to nature , having been raised in its lap . On the other hand , Bea is unable to shake off her urban roots . As they grow apart and distant , their relationship meanders through heartbreaking turns where the choice blurs between who is right and who is not . <p> Cook 's Booker Prize-nominated debut is visceral . The chilling semblance to our everyday reality and survival instinct thereof speaks to you through every page describing the City . As you travel with the team in the Wilderness State , you can not help but wonder if that is the future we are headed towards . And if we are , will we be Beas -- unable to shake off the urban shackles and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ with our souls turning increasingly rustic ? Will we be able to live the life of nomadic hunter-gatherers and adhere to the laws of nature where the stakes lie between life and death ? <p> The New Wilderness pushes you to think beyond this day and this moment .
