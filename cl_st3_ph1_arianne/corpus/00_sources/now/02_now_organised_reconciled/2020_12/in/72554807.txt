<h> Decarbonizing the future of power sector in India <p> As we wind down for 2020 , a dynamic year that took the world by surprise , it would be interesting to evaluate the trends which impacted the power sector globally and in India . According to International Energy Agency 's 2020 World Energy Outlook the global energy demand is expected to return to its pre-COVID 19 levels by early 2023 . However , as the economic activities pace up , global CO2 emissions would continue to tread a downward trend reflecting the role of flourishing Renewable Energy ( RE ) in the energy mix . As per a recently released position paper by GE titled ' Accelerated Growth of Renewables and Gas Power Can Rapidly Change the Trajectory on Climate Change ' , 41 percent ( 13.7 Gigatonnes or Gt ) of the global CO2 emissions from fuel combustion are attributable to the electricity and heat production sector . This is followed by industry and transport sectors , with 26 percent and 25 percent respectively . <p> Certainly , significant efforts across sectors are needed to tame down the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ targets . The explosive growth in the adoption of wind energy and solar PV capacities has been primarily due to continuous reduction in cost , technology advancements to improve capacity factors and progressive policy measures , and positive public sentiment . The LCOE ( Levelized Cost of Electricity ) of onshore wind and utility solar PV are expected to drop further between 2020 and 2040 , due to reduction in CAPEX and economies of scale . In its efforts to provide a clean energy future to its people , India has set an ambitious RE installed capacity target of 450 GW by 2030 , which is currently at 90.39 GW as on 30 November 2020 , and the country is taking several other measures at the policy level to reduce CO2/NOX emissions . <p> While it sounds promising , there is a caveat . With higher penetration of renewables , the Indian grid would face complexities of frequency and voltage fluctuations due to the sudden loss of generation and reduced system inertia . Due to the variability in the generation pattern of solar and wind energy , the system requires @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ capacity . As per the Government of India projections , the RE curtailment would be 27% at the point when RE installed capacity reaches 175 GW by 2022 and 50% at the 450 GW level , adding to the losses for the power generators . Hence , the future grid will need more dispatchable synchronous generation as spinning reserve to provide load following and frequency response services . <p> To the case in point , if we consider providing the necessary base load generation capacity using gas-based power -- through a RE + Thermal Hybrid solution , India can really see itself at the top spot in terms of meeting its energy transition objectives . As per GE estimates , a RE + Gas Power hybrid modelled for 400 MW RTC solution , can provide highly competitive 1st year composite tariff of INR 2.89 per kWh and additional benefits on higher annual availability , maximized RE share of Annual Energy Production ( AEP ) with over 75% , improved plant load factor , reduced RE curtailment to less than 10% , and lower CAPEX and land requirement by 40% and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ inherent flexibility and dispatchability , as gas power plants can come online quickly due to higher ramp rates . Gas-power plants have higher availability , providing dependable capacity 365 days in a year . <p> Upcoming technologies , like Lithium-ion battery energy storage system ( BESS ) are increasingly gaining popularity to support the growth of RE . However , battery storage will not be competitive , versus gas-based power , on an LCOS ( Levelized Cost of Storage ) basis for durations greater than 8 hours until there is a significant technology breakthrough resulting in a reduction in cost . <p> It 's encouraging to see that the Government of India plans to infuse USD 66 billion to further develop the gas infrastructure in the country including gas-pipeline network and LNG regasification terminals to take the share of natural gas in its energy basket to 15 percent by 2030 from the current 6.3 percent . The point to be noted here is that , even if evolved fuels like Green Hydrogen make ways into the power generation segment , the advanced gas turbine technology can burn Hydrogen mixed with Natural Gas to generate clean power .
