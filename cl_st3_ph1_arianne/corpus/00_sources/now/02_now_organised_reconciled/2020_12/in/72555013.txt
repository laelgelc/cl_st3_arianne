<h> PointBlank : Beyond COVID -- Time to run the talk , eye marathons ahead <p> Monday , Dec 28 , 2020 <p> The pandemic may have run its course . The vaccine is in sight . The marathons for human survival are , however , yet to be mapped . <p> By Ranjana Chauhan <p> The world has been fighting its battles to win the war against the COVID-19 pandemic -- enforcing successive lockdowns , building health infrastructure , with laboratories working at a frenetic pace to develop a vaccine . Victory now appears tantalisingly near . But will the vaccine be the one-shot victory for humankind ? <p> As we are set to neutralise the enemy virus , it 's time to look beyond at the war brewing on three fronts core to human existence -- hunger , climate change , and liberty . <p> When we were first hit by our own pain of being locked in , we learnt what real pain was -- from visuals of teeming migrant workers carrying all they had in precious little bags and bundles , walking hundreds of kilometres back @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ fear was not the deadly virus but hunger . They were returning home , hungrier than ever before . There was no number put on these migrations or deaths . <p> These visuals exposed the frailties of an economy that was lauded as among the world 's fastest growing not too long ago . But poverty and unemployment-triggered hunger is not just a pandemic fallout . India 's malnutrition level is almost twice that of many African countries . <p> The Global Hunger Index 2020 report ranks India 94th among 107 nations , faring much worse than its smaller neighbours Bangladesh , Pakistan and Nepal . This , when the country 's granaries are overflowing with stocks , and much of it going waste . <p> As we combat the health crisis , persisting climatic calamities continued to wreak havoc , leading to volatile weather events.Climate change brought in unprecedented cyclones , rain , floods and droughts , accompanied by extreme temperatures , glacial melting , bush fires and also opening up of new habitats for pests such as locusts that grew into serious threats to global food supply . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ dioxide emissions from fossil fuels and industry are set to fall 7% in 2020 due to the lockdowns -- the biggest decline since the World War-II . Experts , however , say a one-year decline will not really slow the pace of global warming . <p> And then , there is the inevitable environmental fallout of the hygiene regime for the pandemic . Plastic is increasingly littering water bodies , and there is a surge in consumption of single-use plastic and improper disposal of items like masks , personal protective equipment , and food packaging . Without much hue and cry , recycling of plastic has almost been suspended in the battle against COVID-19 . <p> Beyond the basic survival needs of food and environment , there is another factor key to flourishing of our species as humans -- that of liberty . These included freedom of movement , expression and press . <p> The report was not just about specific actions taken during the pandemic , as it said the crisis was only " an accelerator of trends that were in place before the virus struck " . <p> Even as the immediate focus is indeed to conquer the lethal virus , the world must not lose sight of the long run . <p> It is time to make the right policy choices to avert inequalities and environmental disasters , says a recent UN report , warning of the undoing of any progress made earlier and a future clouded by crises . <p> These challenges require more of us to join in the marathon .
