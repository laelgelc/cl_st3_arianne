<p> Almost three years ago when Sameer Hans , along with his partners , Sanu Ratho and Suchismita Naik , started the Bhubaneshwar-based Bocca Cafe , serving great food was not the only goal . In their own words the trio wanted to serve " an eco-revolution on a plate " . <p> From eco-friendly coir coasters , cornstarch and sugarcane pulp packaging , paper straws to a zero plastic policy , they want to set a positive example for the city 's food and hospitality industry . <p> Sameer also added , " the fight against environmentally degrading practices by adopting sustainable methods is a continuous process , which will require commitment and a willingness to learn and change . " <p> And , that is why , when Punyasloka Panda , the lead coordinator at Youth for Sustainability , approached Bocca Cafe to help raise awareness about the harmful effects of unsustainable oil palm cultivation , Sameer and his team were quick to extend their support . <h> Positive change , one plate at a time <p> Sameer was shocked to realise that palm oil was not just @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ other products like bread , ice-cream , chocolate , and even shampoo and soap . It is one of the most commonly consumed edible oils in the world . <p> " It is a welcome realisation that there are so many indirect ways of consuming palm oil , be it through ingredients , cleaning products or other packaged items . This is a constant process of learning and improving , and we are doing our best to accommodate and evolve , " says Sameer . <p> But , the problem is not the use of the oil , but the production .
