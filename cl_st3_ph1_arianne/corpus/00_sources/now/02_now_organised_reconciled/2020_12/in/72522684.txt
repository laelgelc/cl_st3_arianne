Private agencies and NGOs have been roped in to analyse the camera images . Sariska gets fewer tourists than Ranthambore , but requires rigid monitoring because of the movement of locals . Experts say the delay in relocation of tigers has disturbed their gender ratio in Sariska , currently one male tiger to eight females . However , relocation poses major risks to big cats . In 2018 , tranquiliser overdose killed a tiger that had strayed out of Ranthambore park . <p> SPECIALISED VETS NEEDED <p> The lack of expertise in handling big cats is most glaring in the absence of vets qualified and trained for the job . Usually , vets on wildlife duty hold university degrees in livestock and poultry . In most states , forest departments take them on deputation and put them on the job after short trainings in wildlife . Sreya Guha , principal secretary ( forest and environment ) , Rajasthan , says the forest department has only six vets on deputation , against a requirement of 23 , and no assisting staff when 33 are needed . Only one vet in the state is considered an expert in tranquilising big cats . <p> In October last year , the Union ministry of environment , forest and climate change instructed the NTCA to deploy a vet in every tiger reserve .
