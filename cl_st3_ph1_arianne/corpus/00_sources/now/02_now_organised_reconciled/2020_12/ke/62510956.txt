<h> Do n't let perfection be enemy of your progress <h> Summary <p> It 's easy to fall into the temptation of always finding fault in everything and everyone and being ' blind ' to bright spots around us , however few or modest . <p> Most often we fail to notice progress happening in the society until the very end when the evidence is absolutely clear to ignore . <p> It 's easy to fall into the temptation of always finding fault in everything and everyone and being ' blind ' to bright spots around us , however few or modest . <p> Most often we fail to notice progress happening in the society until the very end when the evidence is absolutely clear to ignore . This is largely because important undertakings stretch over long periods of time to start taking shape and thus call for patience . It is exactly this understanding that gave rise to the saying , ' Rome was n't built in a day . ' <p> No one can transform everything at once . In fact , the hardest thing about leading change is managing the complexity -- breaking it down into manageable portions as parts of a whole . <p> Instead of unrealistically pursuing radical plans that promise overnight transformations , it is much more sustainable to give chase to a series of minor reforms deployed consistently and strategically over time . This is because if you only focus on a complete overhaul of things or a revolution , you 're more likely to miss the small , detailed tasks that should serve as the binding glue of the bigger picture . <p> In other words , do n't let the perfect be the enemy of the good ! <p> It 's much better to take small , steady steps forward than wait to take a giant leap at once , not knowing whether it would end up being a flight or a tumble . Small steps allow faster trouble-shooting of loose ends early on in the pursued model , enabling timely adjustments at the lowest cost possible . <p> Also , if the Covid-19 disaster has taught us anything it is that grand ideas can fail miserably in practice . We do n't often have much control over things as we think we do . Thankfully , we have almost infinite power to adjust , survive and bounce back , as many households and businesses have in the wake of the health pandemic . <p> When applied to progress on the social scene , transformation in human development indicators such as poverty reduction , access to quality healthcare and education as well as housing should be approached in a methodical and consistent way with the resources available . It is better off to make ' baby steps ' towards a desired level with whatever little resources that can be mobilised than to wait until all the needed resources are in place before moving into action . <p> For this reason , many people fail to notice the small , steady progress made by governments and organisations over time . They instead expect to see radical changes , almost overnight to believe it . <p> To be clear , not all governments and businesses have the interest of the public at heart and would rather maintain the status quo out of selfish interests than invest in projects designed to uplift the society 's welfare . But for those inclined towards positive transformation , their commitments may initially seem unnoticeable , drawing backlash from the impatient public and shareholders for their apparent inaction . To this end , it should be remembered that transformation is hardly a destination , but a journey full of twists and turns requiring steady hands to manouvre through . Change is never a switch , but a transition . <p> The story is the same in the sustainability space . Steering a business towards sustainable practices where profit , people and planet goals neatly align is a complex process to be approached incrementally over time . <p> As countries and enterprises stare down a public health crisis , another crisis is brewing up -- climate change , one that is threatening the society 's future survival . <p> The deadly threat of global warming requires enormous capital flows to set the planet on the path towards net zero greenhouse gas emissions soonest . This would require transformations in the systems of energy , transportation and production as well greening the building and construction sector across areas such as climate action , health and wellbeing , and resource efficiency and circularity . <p> While there is a clear sense of urgency in getting our planet in order environmentally and social-wise , the best approach is evolutionary , adjusting in lockstep with expected shifts across the sands of time . Adopting a revolutionary mindset , often not-well thought through , should be discouraged as it might further upset the human-induced imbalance in nature , from which recovery may not be possible . <p> Identifying and diagnosing a problem is the first step towards finding it possible solutions . The next step is mapping out a blueprint , picking the right tools and drawing an able talent pool to execute the project , while measuring progress every step of the way . Real , sustainable change is a long game , a marathon . As such , it requires staying power among those implementing it to see it through to fruition -- a new brand of sustainability leadership .
