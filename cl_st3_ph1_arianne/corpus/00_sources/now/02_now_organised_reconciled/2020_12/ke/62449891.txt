<h> Summary <p> The greatest recipients of the expanded water , sanitation and hygiene infrastructure will be the public schools where the bulk of the students are . <p> Water insecurity has become a clear and present danger . With climate change , populations increase and extractive human activities , water availability continues to diminish , further eroding the gains on the sixth UN sustainable development goal ( SDG ) . <p> UN Environment status report on the achievement of SDG 6 in Africa calls for sufficient financial resources to be allocated to sanitation to meet national targets . Specifically , focus should be on data provision , financing and investments , capacity building , cooperation and monitoring and reporting . <p> For poor nations , this deteriorating water situation exacerbates poor access to quality water and sanitation . <p> Data on least developed countries ( LDCs ) show 27 percent of the population as having basic handwashing facilities , while 26 percent have handwashing facilities lacking soap or water . The remaining 47 percent have no facilities . The situation is not any better for learning institutions . <h> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ water point is a catchphrase in learning institutions . According to Unicef , about 584 million children lacked a basic drinking water service at their school , including 297 million whose schools had an improved source with no water available , and 287 million whose schools still had no water service . <p> In East and Southern Africa , only 13 percent of schools reported providing learners with hand-washing facilities in 2014 . In Kenya , however , data on water , sanitation and hygiene infrastructure in schools is inconsistent and unreliable . <p> A survey on the sustainability and impact of school sanitation , water and hygiene in 2018 conducted by V Njuguna and others found that only five percent of 1,000 schools sampled provided soap for hand-washing , and only two percent of students reported using soap while washing their hands . <p> Fortunately , Kenya 's education system has witnessed increased commitment to improving water , sanitation and hygiene in schools . Through support of development partners such as Unicef , approximately 500,000 children have access to water , sanitation and hygiene facilities and education in schools @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in the wake of the Covid-19 pandemic where water use has more than doubled , more will still need to be done . If done well , perhaps , water , sanitation and hygiene programmes will be the silver lining to the Covid-19 pandemic in schools . <p> The greatest recipients of the expanded water , sanitation and hygiene infrastructure will be the public schools where the bulk of the students are . <p> The advantage of public schools is their ability to cushion vulnerable and economically disadvantaged learners . This group fundamentally lacks generational inherited privileges and bear the brunt of business-as-usual water , sanitation and hygiene management and neglected water infrastructure in schools . <p> In most cases their average coping costs for unreliable and distant supply is comparatively higher . Learners in structurally marginalised rural areas are often forced to bear the burden either by fetching water from rivers or by carrying water from home , further exposing them to other risks . <p> The dangers of water fetching on children though not documented , can be far-reaching . These included but were not limited to broken limbs , drowning in wells or rivers , and sexual assault . <h> Basic sanitation <p> Today , the importance of hand-washing , a basic sanitation practice , in the prevention of diseases can not be overemphasised . However , hand-washing comes at a cost , and with the Covid-19 pandemic here to stay , these costs may skyrocket . To arrest the situation , we have to explicitly estimate the investment costs of new water-efficient technologies required to reach water security . <p> With focused planning and political will , we should emerge out of the pandemic stronger than before and relentless in our efforts to ensure universal access to safe drinking water , especially in school settings . <p> There will undeniably be a bigger water gap in terms of supply and demand when normal learning resumes in January 2021 .
