<h> Glen and Kristy Badger : Caring for our caregivers <p> THE HOLIDAY SEASON is upon us and the winter weather is slowly moving in across New England . Under normal circumstances , we would all be planning large family gatherings , office parties , and getaway trips . This year , however , is like no other in modern history . We know what we must do for ourselves and our communities to slow the spread of the coronavirus and to help protect our most vulnerable loved ones . At the same time , we must not forget that we are also responsible for caring for our caregivers . <p> In eldercare communities that provide every level of care across New England and indeed the entire country , our caregivers have faced the most dangerous threat ever to their lives , their livelihoods -- and importantly -- the people in their care . These professionals work long hours and weeks under tremendous stress , facing down a dangerous illness , and often for very modest pay . The physical toll is obvious , but perhaps it can be easy @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ resident -- a friend , mother or father , grandmother or grandfather , a mentor -- - become ill and die . <p> With talk of a vaccine by the end of the year or early 2021 , it can be tempting to let our guard down and forget about the needs of our long-term care communities , their staff , and residents . Recently , Governor Chris Sununu reactivated New Hampshire 's Long-Term Care Stabilization Program , which will offer stipends to frontline health-care workers at Medicaid facilities until the end of December . This is a weekly $300 stipend for full-time workers and $150 for part-time workers . We applaud this modest but necessary action , but are mindful that so much more is needed to ensure our caregivers are protected and compensated adequately for years to come . <p> The Union Leader recently reported ( Oct 26 , 2020 ) that New Hampshire lost 709 licensed nursing assistants over a recent 12-month period . " <p> Second , we need our elected officials to convene a task force made up , at least partially , of knowledgeable caregiving specialists , to study the short and long-term effects this pandemic is having on our caregivers . The coronavirus is causing talented professionals to leave their jobs and you can bet it is also deterring others who might have been considering a career working in a long-term care facility . The time to address this problem is now , not when our communities no longer have the staff to continue caring for our seniors . <p> Finally , we recommend full support for our eldercare communities as they recover and reexamine their best practices for future health care emergencies . This year , we must also endeavor to show our appreciation for caregivers by taking the necessary steps to improve their daily lives .
