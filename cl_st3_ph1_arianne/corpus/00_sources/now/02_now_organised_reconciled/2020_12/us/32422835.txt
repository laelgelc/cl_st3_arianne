<h> Opinion : Mold and the insidious dangers of climate change <p> Sandra Edwards stands in a room in her home which still has mold growing , Wednesday , August 26 , 2020 , in Houston , which is still damaged from Hurricane Harvey , three years ago . " The Harvey Forgotten Survivors Caucus , a group of 15 to 25 home repair applicants that have been working since October of last year trying to help each other get their homes back together and demanding better service and equitable outcomes from the city 's housing department and Texas General Land Office <p> Photo : Karen Warren , Houston Chronicle / Staff photographer <p> The 2020 hurricane season was one for the record books . An all-time high of 30 named storms formed -- and 12 of them hit the U.S. mainland . As anyone who lives in the Houston area knows , these tropical storms are fierce . While the gulf states have always dealt with hurricanes , scientists are now telling us that global warming will bring even more extreme weather on top of rising sea levels -- @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ We all remember Hurricane Harvey , which stalled and dumped multiple feet of rain on Houston in 2017 . It was a tragic harbinger of what 's to come . The Earth has already warmed 1 degree Celsius since pre-industrial times . Like a steamy bathroom after a hot shower , our planet 's warmer atmosphere is now holding more moisture , which is increasing the number and severity of humid days as well as turbocharging rainfall . The city 's creeks , bayous and human-made storm management systems simply ca n't handle such intense deluges , so even when we survive the initial storm damage , we can expect the battle against water in our homes and businesses to become even more difficult in the coming years . <p> All that moisture points to a silent and underappreciated impact of climate change that we must recognize : mold . Our city , already known for its hot and humid weather , will get even hotter and wetter in the future , creating increasingly conducive conditions for the growth of mold and fungi . While these destructive spores may not @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ their impact on human health is severe and widespread . <p> As these storms continue to grow in intensity , that post-hurricane musty smell or those black specks that show up on a wall will also mean expensive home repairs ahead . Stripping out drywall and reconstructing our houses and commercial buildings will become an increasingly frequent necessity in years to come , and it 's simply an unsustainable situation for many families and small-business owners . And this remediation will further the cascade of health impacts . <p> Although we used to think of global warming as an issue in the future for our children and grandchildren , the reality is we are feeling the impacts here and now . The untold story of the health impacts of mold on Texas communities deserves to be researched and shared with our fellow citizens . <p> And it 's clearly time for our leaders in Congress and the state legislature to act on climate . We must accelerate the transition to renewable energy , which will have the immediate positive impact of cleaning up the air while slowing climate change and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ leaders can start by providing a level playing field for clean energy and stop unwarranted subsidies for the fossil fuel industry . <p> James is an allergist-immunologist who is interested in the intersection between climate change and health .
