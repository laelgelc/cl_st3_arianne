<h> Conservationists sue feds over lack of wolverine protections <p> By NICHOLAS K. GERANIOS , Associated Press <p> Published : December 14 , 2020 , 7:19pm <p> Share : <p> SPOKANE -- Conservation groups filed a lawsuit Monday challenging the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service 's decision to withhold Endangered Species Act protection from wolverines in the lower 48 states , where no more than 300 of the animals are thought to remain . <p> The groups said wolverines face localized extinction as a result of climate change , habitat fragmentation and low genetic diversity . <p> The lawsuit was filed in federal court in Missoula , Mont . and contended that climate change is diminishing the mountain snowpack that wolverines rely on for their primary habitat . Wolverines in the lower 48 seek out areas with persistent spring snowpack to dig dens to birth and raise their young . <p> " The wolverine is a famously tough creature that does n't back down from anything , but even the wolverine ca n't overcome climate change by itself , " said Earthjustice attorney Amanda Galvan in a press release . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ on how wolverines are severely affected by climate change , " said Andrea Zaccardi , attorney at the Center for Biological Diversity . " The future of the wolverine in the lower 48 now stands on a knife edge . " <p> The Fish and Wildlife Service received a petition to protect the wolverine under the Endangered Species Act in 2000 , and has fought the designation for years . <p> The agency issued a statement Monday saying it stood by its recent decision not to list wolverines for protection . <p> " New research and analysis show that wolverine populations in the American Northwest remain stable , and individuals are moving across the Canadian border in both directions and returning to former territories , " the statement said . " The species , therefore , does not meet the definition of threatened or endangered under the Endangered Species Act . "
