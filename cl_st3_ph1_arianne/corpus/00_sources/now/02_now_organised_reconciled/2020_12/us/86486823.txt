<h> Column : Dump HB 6 -- for the bailout and the lousy energy policy <p> The Columbus Dispatch <p> If there 's anything the GOP-controlled Ohio General Assembly opposes almost as much as abortion and gun control , it 's saving energy and encouraging new jobs . <p> Not that lawmakers would say they 're for wasting energy ( and money ) and keeping Ohio 's economy stuck in the past , but that 's exactly what they 'll be demonstrating if they vote to let House Bill 6 live on under a new name . <p> House Bill 798 has been sold as a delay of HB 6 , apparently in an effort to appease those across the political spectrum who want HB 6 repealed in light of federal allegations that its passage was aided by industrial-scale corruption . <p> Along with compelling electricity ratepayers to fund a $150 billion bailout of two nuclear power plants , HB 6 makes permanent the smaller subsidies for two coals plants ( one of which is in Indiana ) and essentially wipes out Ohio 's efforts to encourage renewable energy and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 're heeding the public call to repeal the corruption-tainted bailout , but HB 798 changes nothing . The bailout payments to Energy Harbor -- owner of the nuclear plants and former subsidiary of Akron-based FirstEnergy -- are scheduled to start in January 2022 . <p> There 's an audit process that is supposed to ensure that Energy Harbor really would go under without the bailout -- in other words , that it wo n't use the payments to pad profits and reward shareholders -- but the audit would be largely secret . The bill does n't describe any circumstance in which the payments do n't start a year from January as scheduled . <p> Assuming a certain level of cynicism , it 's not hard to guess why lawmakers are so keen not to let down the corporate offspring of FirstEnergy , which has contributed generously to so many of their campaigns . <p> What 's harder to understand is the undying drive at the Statehouse to wipe out anything that smacks of smart , forward-looking energy policy . <p> Backers of HB 6 make much of the handful of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ new fee to support the bailout is slightly smaller than the old fees that support efficiency and renewables . But those fees enabled utilities to help consumers buy energy-efficient appliances and otherwise use less electricity . And over 10 years , those measures caused Ohio residents and businesses to use 67.8 million fewer megawatt hours of electricity -- a savings of $7 billion , or about $7 per month per household . <p> The fees just are n't that important . But the large-scale drop in energy use is very important in the fight to limit the damage from climate change . And the good jobs that could come from turning Ohio 's manufacturing muscle in a serious way toward the emerging renewable-energy industry could make all the difference for struggling Ohio towns . <p> Our state is older , less educated , lower paid and less diverse that the U.S. as a whole .
