<h> Birds able to adjust egg-laying date <p> Many birds are able to change their egg-laying date to cope with variable conditions , new research shows . <p> The study examined birds and mammals , and found birds in particular showed the ability to find the " optimum " laying date . <p> However , the research team -- led by the University of Montpellier -- say this ability may not be able to keep pace with the current rate of climate change . <p> " The environment is constantly changing , creating variation in natural selection acting on populations , " said Dr. Simon Evans , of the universities of Exeter and Oxford . <p> " For example , laying eggs earlier in the spring than others might be advantageous for a bird in one year but disadvantageous in another . <p> " Each year therefore has its own optimal timing for reproduction , with lower reproductive success for individuals breeding earlier or later than this . <p> " As a result , individuals reproducing at the optimum time contribute more surviving offspring to the next generation than those @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ variation in natural selection has been very difficult to measure , particularly because each species has its own unique reproductive behaviors , making it difficult to compare studies from around the world . " <p> To remedy this , the study combined records from 39 long-term projects that have each monitored the reproductive timing in their bird or mammal population for several decades , to allow general conclusions to be reached . <p> The results show that the optimal timing of reproduction varies between years in most populations . <p> The study also looked at individuals reproducing over two consecutive years , to assess how well they adjust their reproduction date from one year to the next to match changes in climate , a phenomenon called " phenotypic plasticity .
