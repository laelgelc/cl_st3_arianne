<h> Lowry : The economic cruelty of the pandemic <p> The pandemic has been a neutron bomb , destroying jobs for service workers and minorities , as well as small businesses . The Biden administration must prioritize economic recovery . <p> Photo : Nam Y. Huh / Associated Press <p> Just when it seemed some of the most disheartening trends in the U.S. economy were finally beginning to reverse , COVID-19 arrived to entrench them . <p> The pandemic has been a neutron bomb targeted at the prospects of lower-income working people . They had finally begun to benefit from the recovery from the Great Recession when the virus ravaged sectors of the economy that disproportionately employ them . <p> The Washington Post has called the resulting economic damage " the most unequal recession in modern U.S. history . " As the paper puts it , starkly , " the less workers earned at their job , the more likely they were to lose it . " <p> The pandemic has hammered restaurants , hotels and places of entertainment , all of which do n't pay high wages and tend @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ swath through small business . It has slammed workers who ca n't retreat to home offices for Zoom calls . <p> In short , it has taken all of the tendencies of our knowledge economy that benefit the better-educated and disadvantage non-college-educated workers and has made them more pronounced , amid a public health crisis that has also hit the most vulnerable the hardest . <p> According to a Gallup Poll earlier this year , 71 percent of people in the top income quintile said they were working from home , whereas 45 percent of people in the bottom quintile stayed at home and were unable to work . <p> A National Bureau of Economic Research working paper published in May found that workers in high-proximity jobs impossible to perform from home tend to be " less educated , of lower income , have fewer liquid assets relative to income , and are more likely renters . " Workers in such jobs were more likely to become unemployed , and non-college-educated workers experienced a 4 point larger drop in employment than college-educated workers . <p> Jobs at the top have @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ more wealth than ever , given the increase in home values and frothy stock market . <p> The story is different further down the income scale . According to the Washington Post analysis , Hispanic Americans experienced the sharpest loss in employment with the onset of the pandemic . The young were particularly hard-hit ; 20 percent of those ages 20 to 24 lost their jobs . Mothers with children ages 6 to 12 , called upon to fill the gaps created by school closings , have been another hard-hit group . <p> Similarly , mom-and-pop businesses have fared poorly . A survey by Alignable , a small-business social network , found nearly 50 percent of small businesses say they are generating less revenue than they need to stay in business , with travel businesses , gyms and beauty salons at particular risk . <p> The pandemic has torn apart what the long recovery since the Great Recession had slowly , too slowly , built . <p> Since 2010 , a lion 's share of new jobs were created in the services sector , which has been shredded by the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . It plummeted last spring . Positive trends in labor force participation have been stymied , with the labor force participation rate the lowest it 's been in about 50 years . <p> Mass vaccination next year should take the edge off this economic dislocation , but it 's harder to create than destroy . The Federal Reserve estimates that employment wo n't fully bounce back until 2023 . <p> What is to be done ? Policymakers need to realize that when they promulgate COVID-19 restrictions , they are asking the people with the least economic margin for error to sacrifice the most . Congress needs to pass a new stimulus bill to cushion the blow of a natural disaster that has immiserated many millions of people through no fault of their own . And the incoming Biden administration ideally would realize that fashionable causes like climate change need to take a back seat to the pursuit of full economic recovery . <p> The economic pain is not the worst that the pandemic has wrought , but it can not be ignored .
