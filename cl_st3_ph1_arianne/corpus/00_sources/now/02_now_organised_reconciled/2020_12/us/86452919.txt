<h> OP-ED : Nature and the pandemic <p> Kathleen Johnson , Ellen Mass <p> As the pandemic rages , evidence points to virus roots in animal pathogens as the culprit of this pandemic and we realize that the continuation of habitat removal is what is driving the global transfer from wild animal hosts such as bats or pangolins to human animal hosts ; thus , COVID-19 . <p> Our recklessness with the environment is causing Corona strains to flourish . Scientist David Quamman in his book , " Animal Infections and the next Human Pandemic , " has a recent article in American Scientist confirming these non-human sources that are killing so many humans . <p> Today , we are being warned by a flock of Alewife bird watchers that many solvable but present dangers are facing the ducks , geese and the nesting and roosting great blue herons , found at Jerry 's Pond , Yates Pond , Blair Pond and Little Pond . <p> These winged creatures face fires , tree removal , increased pollution , fishing debris , heavy silt depleting oxygen , trash in the pond , contaminated stormwater runoff and even a surplus of snappers at Yates . The chainlink fence is about the only protection for the 90 species of birds and 20 mammal species found throughout the heavily vegetated Alewife area . <p> Alewife development plans are rapidly going forward at former W.R. Grace and the western reservation 's Mooney Street or Fresh Pond floodplain area , which is also the Quadrangle flood plain . Pressure for the city 's planning board to permit private companies continues unabated . They ignore ponds , rivers , streams , wetlands and an entire watershed that feeds the Boston Harbor . <p> Also , lack of public health mitigation and enforcement of conservation guidelines have eroded our existing ecosystem foundations which water quality testing has exposed . <p> Advocacy and education are needed to retain what was once a healthy floodplain , functioning for thousands of years in a by-gone era that nourished nearby towns and cities with small plot truck farming and a rich Alewife fish economy . The regional floodplain of the Mystic River watershed 's " Upper Basin " is over 500 acres when including Somerville . <p> We 'll only know the stress level and tipping point of Alewife 's Cambridge flora and fauna by their emission of putrid smells , heavy algae cover and bringing illness to creatures who touch or drink e-coli water . Water quality data has shown city failure of EPA standards via Massachusetts Water Resources Authority 's testing results on contamination and toxic residues from over-development . <p> This environmental damage over many years , with over eight state variances awarded the city , stems from excess permitting without proper zoning scrutiny and impact studies needed as stated in the city 's " Alewife floodplain zoning " regulations , often circumvented at permitting time with " special permits " with a small percentage of " affordable " units . <p> Mitigation linkage costs to any development proposal is a natural part of other city permit requirements in California . Remittances and protective funds should be extracted to cover any natural resource damage in the future , whether affordable housing is built or not . <p> Cambridge has received ample " outside " advice to upgrade the Alewife corridor without hesitation from MIT , Harvard , Tufts and Earthos Institute scientists , and from town officials from neighboring Arlington and Belmont . <p> In 2017 , a region-wide Alewife Corridor Symposium with city officials participating , informed over 200 persons of the fragility and " tipping point " conditions of the broad Alewife corridor and its impact , not to mention a hazard to residential property . <p> In the future , " mitigation linkages " for developments or hefty fees will save the city enormous public safety costs with sea rise on the horizon by retaining the watershed sponge wetlands that hold back flood waters . Local and state climate committees and biodiversity for climate change has recommended expanding not shrinking floodplain capacity . The Charles River Watershed Association purchased 800 acres of floodplain wetland upstream for flood attenuation to protect the city . <p> A council resolution is pending action by the city manager to tie the Jerry 's Pond open space and its interconnected ecology and hydrology to the larger DCR 115 acres of reservation as a whole . Wildlife protections via DEP guidelines are missing from the resolution and must sustain priority status in this discussion because developers are planning larger and taller building spaces adjacent to rich habitat woodlands surrounding Fresh Pond Apartment and Cambridge Park Drive : alongside ponds , meadows and the river . <p> Over-development inevitably creates dysfunction of existing natural systems in the diminishing open spaces of Cambridge . Local scientists making the case of needed Alewife conservation for co-existence of people and wildlife might give our native species a chance for survival and thus meet the ominous threats of climate change . <p> The 1995 North Cambridge News published a 40-page paper focused on the extraordinary environmental value of this floodplain to the city . Recreation at Jerry 's Pond should be a third level of consideration within the existing urban wild setting because safety comes first for living creatures and that includes us . Regional agreement must move forward . <p> Kathleen Johnson is a resident in the Alewife area and has monitored wildlife for over 10 years in various reservation locations , including Jerry 's Pond . <p> Ellen Mass is a resident and former president of Friends of Alewife Reservation , which is now a subsidiary of Green Cambridge
