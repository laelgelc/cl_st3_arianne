<p> The world cut its carbon dioxide emissions by 7% during a locked-down , pandemic-stricken 2020 , marking the biggest drop ever recorded , new preliminary figures show . <p> The Global Carbon Project , an authoritative group of dozens of international scientists who track emissions , calculated that the world will have put 37 billion U.S. tons of carbon dioxide in the air in 2020 . That 's down from 40.1 billion U.S. tons in 2019 , according to a study published Thursday in the journal Earth System Science Data . <p> Scientists say this drop occurred chiefly because people are staying home and traveling less by car and plane , and they cautioned that emissions are expected to jump back up after the pandemic ends . Ground transportation makes up about one-fifth of emissions of carbon dioxide , the chief man-made heat-trapping gas . <p> " Of course , lockdown is absolutely not the way to tackle climate change , " said study co-author Corinne LeQuere , a climate scientist at the University of East Anglia . <p> The same group of scientists months ago predicted emission drops @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ COVID-19 . That 's because China had an earlier lockdown with less of a second wave . Also , China 's emissions are more industrial based than other countries , and that industry was less affected than transportation , LeQuere said . <p> The calculations -- based on reports detailing energy use , industrial production and daily mobility counts -- were praised as accurate by outside scientists . <p> Even with the drop in 2020 , the world on average put 1,185 tons of carbon dioxide into the air every second . <p> Final figures for 2019 published in the same study show that from 2018 to 2019 , emissions of the main man-made heat-trapping gas increased only 0.1% , much less than annual jumps of around 3% a decade or two ago .
