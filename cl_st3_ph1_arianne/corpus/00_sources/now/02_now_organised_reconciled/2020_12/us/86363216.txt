<p> MELBOURNE ( Reuters ) - The health of Australia 's Great Barrier Reef , the world 's most extensive and spectacular coral reef ecosystem , is in a critical state and deteriorating as climate change warms up the waters in which it lies , an international conservation group said . <p> The World Heritage-listed site off Australia 's northeastern coast has lost more than half its coral in the past three decades . <p> Coral-bleaching in 2016 , 2017 and 2020 has further damaged its health and affected its animal , bird and marine population , the International Union for Conservation of Nature said in a report . <p> Such bleaching occurs when hotter water destroys the algae which the coral feeds on , causing it to turn white . <p> The union moved the reef 's status to critical and deteriorating on its watchlist . <p> Some activities which threaten it , like fishing and coastal development , can be tackled by the management authorities , the union said . <p> " Other pressures can not be addressed at the site level , such as climate change , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ said . <p> Progress toward safeguarding the reef under a long-term sustainability plan through to 2050 has been slow and it has not been possible to stop its deterioration , it said . <p> The turtle populations - including loggerhead , hawksbill and northern green - as well as the scalloped hammerhead shark , many seabird populations and possibly some dolphin species are declining . <p> Efforts to safeguard the reef are rising , however .
