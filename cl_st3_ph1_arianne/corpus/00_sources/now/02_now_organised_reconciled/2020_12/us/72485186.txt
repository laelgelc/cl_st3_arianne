<h> Scientists say rules laying out what can be marketed as a sustainable investment are too lax <p> By Kate Abnett <p> BRUSSELS , Dec 7 ( Reuters ) - More than 100 scientists have urged the European Commission to address urgently a " critical oversight " in its planned rules on sustainable finance that they say could undermine the bloc 's climate change goals . <p> The European Union last month published a first set of draft criteria for its sustainable finance taxonomy - a system of classifying investments that can be marketed as sustainable . <p> In an open letter to the Commission on Monday , 123 scientists from 27 countries said Brussels ' proposal is not compatible with the EU 's target to reach net zero greenhouse gas emissions by 2050 . <p> The scientists ' concerns centre around a plan to class gas-fuelled power plants as sustainable if they meet an emissions limit of 100 grams of CO2 equivalent per kilowatt hour . <p> Brussels ' advisers - some of whom signed the scientists ' letter - had recommended this as a starting threshold that must @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> The Commission 's proposal omits the requirement for the limit to decrease over time . <p> " We assume this omission can only be an unfortunate administrative oversight in these extraordinary times that , however , needs urgent correction , " the scientists said . <p> The green finance rules should make " absolutely clear that the threshold has to decline " to net zero emissions by 2050 , Andreas Hoepner , a professor at University College Dublin , said . <p> He led the initiative to send the letter with Joeri Rogelj , a climate scientist at Imperial College London . Hoepner sits on an expert group that advises the Commission , the EU executive . <p> The Commission is taking feedback on its proposal until Dec. 18 , before writing the final rules . <p> Another statement from a group of NGOs , seen by Reuters and due to be released next week , backs the scientists ' call for a declining emissions threshold , and asks for bioenergy and livestock farming to face tighter environmental criteria or be denied a green investment label .
