<h> How can we heal a fractured America ? This Victorian-era disease detective offers clues . <p> More than 150 years ago , a renowned physician named John Snow walked the gritty streets of London 's working-class Golden Square neighborhood , not far from his office in the city 's Soho district , knocking on the doors of residents felled by the cholera epidemic . Why Soho was so hard-hit had vexed London 's health officials , but Snow , known today as the " founding father of modern epidemiology , " used maps , public records , and his sleuthing skills to find the source of that devastating outbreak : contaminated water from a public water well pump on Broad Street . To find answers , he became an intrepid , Victorian-era medical detective who went door-to-door collecting information on residents ' water sources to determine how the deadly disease spread . He was as comfortable in the Queen 's palace as he was on the bleak streets of Golden Square . Snow brought not just his insights as a working physician , but also the social connections he had with residents , his roots with the working poor , and his local knowledge of the streets that he canvassed .
