<p> Over the last decade in Ghana , a country in West Africa , authorities found that three endemic diseases have a nationwide prevalence of chronic infections in 170 districts . <p> These chronic infections are known as schistosomiasis , onchocerciasis ( river blindness ) , and lymphatic filariasis . They can be fatal . Schistosomiasis is caused by parasitic flatworms known as Schistosome haematobium and S. mansoni . This disease afflicts around 7 million residents . Also known as snail fever , this disease infects the intestines or urinary tract . It causes stomach aches , diarrhea , and blood in the stool or urine . <p> Onchocerciasis , or river blindness , affects approximately 8.2 million people in 73 districts . It is caused by the parasitic worm Onchocerca volvulus . It is the second most common cause of blindness caused by infection . Symptoms include severe itching , bumps under the skin , and blindness . <p> Lastly , Lymphatic filariasis , also called elephantiasis , affects the highest number of Ghanaians in 74 districts . It is caused by the parasitic worm Wuchereria bancrofti .
