Much of traditional economic theory is based on the " rational actor , " who responds to incentives in a consistent manner . <p> In simplified terms ( because in economics , there are always qualifications and nuances ) , raise the price of a good and the rational actor will buy less ; raise it even further and they will buy even less . Of particular relevance here , it can be applied to how people process new evidence and how that might affect their existing beliefs . Someone who , based on widely available information , doubts the connection between human-generated greenhouse gas emissions and climate change will , if given more of the same type of data about the link , maintain their original position -- i.e. , reject the connection .
