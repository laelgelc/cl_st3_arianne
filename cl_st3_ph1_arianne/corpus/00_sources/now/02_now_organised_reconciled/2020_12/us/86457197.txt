On Dec. 9 , 2020 , the comptroller announced the pension fund would divest from all fossil fuel stocks by 2025 . <p> Syracuse , N.Y . -- The New York pension fund is getting out of the business of Big Oil . <p> The $226 billion fund set a target to be net-zero emissions by 2040 , starting with a review of its riskiest oil and gas holdings by 2025 , state Comptroller Thomas DiNapoli announced Wednesday morning . <p> " New York State 's pension fund is at the leading edge of investors addressing climate risk , because investing for the low-carbon future is essential to protect the fund 's long-term value , " DiNapoli said in a statement . <p> Under DiNapoli @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ will be reviewed by 2025 and investments that do not meet climate risk standards will be divested . This year alone , the comptroller divested from 22 coal companies and is currently reviewing investments in nine oil sands companies . <p> Any divestment will first be subject to fiduciary review . <p> By 2040 , the fund will transition its portfolio to net-zero greenhouse gas emissions , according to DiNapoli . That goal is intended to protect the fund 's long-term health , DiNapoli said , as the world 's economy moves to eliminate greenhouse gas emissions as part of the Paris Agreement . <p> The New York pension fund is the third-largest in the nation , behind two in California . It pays pensions for more than 1.1 retired municipal workers . <p> Last year , the fund included investments in 539 fossil fuel corporations , accounting for about $12.9 billion , or 6.1% of that fund .
