<p> Granholm , 61 , was attorney general of Michigan from 1999 to 2003 and the 47th Governor of Michigan from 2003 to 2011 . She was the first woman to serve as Michigan 's governor , where she made clean energy development a hallmark of her administration . <p> With a budget of roughly $31 billion and a workforce of about 14,000 , the Department of Energy regulates the nation 's varied energy portfolio , including fossil fuels and renewable sources such as solar , and nuclear energy . <p> Granholm is experienced in dealing with the auto industry , which could come as an advantage to Biden 's administration , as he seeks to make clean energy a priority in his administration including efforts to increase fuel efficiency standards for cars and light trucks . <p> Much of the department 's focus involves development of cutting-edge fuel sources that could play an important role in Biden 's pledge to address climate change by reducing carbon emissions produced by coal , oil and gas . <p> The department also regulates the minimum energy efficiency of light bulbs and appliances @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that collectively contribute to the carbon footprint that has added to the planet 's warming temperatures . <p> Since she left the governor 's mansion , Granholm has been involved in several initiatives focused on transforming the nation 's energy industry from one focused on fossil fuels to one expanding renewable sources , such as wind and solar . <p> She founded the American Jobs Project , a 30-state multi-university research project focused on advanced manufacturing jobs in clean energy . <p> Scott Segal , partner at the Washington law firm Bracewell LLP , which represents energy sector clients , said he was n't surprised by the choice of the former governor . <p> " Granholm is well positioned to run DOE , " he said . " She has been a leader in the clean energy transition back in Michigan , addressing some of the same key issues that will confront development and implementation of the Biden plan .
