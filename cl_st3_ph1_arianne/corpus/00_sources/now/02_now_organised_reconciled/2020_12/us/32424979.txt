A South China Sea shark , last seen in 1934 and only formally described last year , is thought to be " possibly extinct . " <p> Craig Hilton-Taylor , head of the IUCN red list , said the impact of human activity was a driver for many species nearing extinction . <p> " All of these things are down to human activities , whether it 's direct hunting or fishing or harvesting of the species , to introducing invasive species , changing habitats to agriculture , urbanization , climate change , " he said in a video interview . " The human footprint is everywhere . The red list breaks down threatened species into vulnerable , endangered and critically endangered categories -- the last of these meaning those closest to extinction . <p> The European bison is making gains -- more than tripling its population since 2003 to more than 6,200 last year -- because of conservation management , IUCN said . <p> Once widespread across Europe several centuries ago , the continent 's largest land mammal was hunted , and its numbers declined dramatically . <p> After World War I , the remaining @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ North American bison -- was hunted out , leaving the species extinct in the wild and only surviving in captivity . Reintroduction efforts began in the 1950s , and today 47 free-ranging herds now roam the continent . <p> " There 's a huge push across Europe , what they call re-wilding , trying to restore habitats that have been transformed by people back into wild habitat again and bring back a lot of these species that have been lost , " Hilton-Taylor said . <p> IUCN , which was founded in 1948 , brings together more than 1,400 member organizations and 15,000 experts and bills itself as one of the world 's largest environmental networks .
