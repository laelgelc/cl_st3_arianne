( Peter Weber / Lake County News-Sun ) <p> There 's an irruption going on this winter in the world of nature -- and you might even be able to witness it in your own backyard . <p> I 'm not talking about a volcanic eruption , but rather an irruption of birds , and it 's not an Alfred Hitchcock experience . It 's a gift from the north , as songbirds such as evening grosbeaks , crossbills , red-breasted nuthatches and pine siskins have been flying south to find food . <p> Advertisement <p> This winter , pine siskins are staging one of their largest invasions recorded in the nation . Every perch was occupied , and about 100 more siskins were on the ground below the feeders picking up fallen food . The huge flock is gone , but some siskins are still around at other feeders as well as in natural areas . On a visit to Lakewood Forest Preserve near Wauconda , we heard and saw dozens upon dozens of pine siskins flying over trees and dipping in to eat seeds from pines , spruces and alder trees . <p> A common redpoll visits a feeder in Lake County . These birds are the same size as siskins , but they have a reddish cap and blackish throat . They also eat pine seeds , as well as those of birch and alder . One winter , we had dozens of redpolls at our thistle feeders , but we have n't seen many of the sporadic visitors in our backyard since . <p> This year , we are being treated to views of three red-breasted nuthatches at our peanut feeders . Their larger cousins , the white-breasted nuthatch , visit feeders and live in northern Illinois year-round . The white-breasted is decked with a deep blue neck and head , softer blue back and white belly . The red-breasted has a black cap and black eye stripe , soft blue back , white throat and reddish-orange breast . These delightful birds show up one year in winter , then not the next , and then again another year . They give calls like someone playing a toy tin horn as they probe conifers for food . ( Steven D. Bailey / Lake County News-Sun ) <p> Several other rarer winter visitors have been spotted in the northern Illinois region this year . One of them is the red crossbill , another is the white-winged crossbill . Both have reddish orange bodies , but the white-winged has , as its name implies , two white wing bars on dark wings . Both have noticeably crossed bills that allow them to pry open cones from evergreens , including pines and spruces . Red crossbills make loud " kip , kip , kip " calls as they fly in small groups over the tops of evergreens looking for cones . The red crossbill has been seen at Lakewood Forest Preserve near Wauconda and white-winged crossbills have been spotted at Illinois Beach State Park in Zion and Lyons Woods Forest Preserve in Waukegan at the end of November . The evening grosbeak looks like a giant male goldfinch , with similar colors but a much larger bill . Evening grosbeaks , which also live and breed up north , sometimes make erratic movements south into the continental United States . <p> They breed in spruce-fir , pine-oak forests of northern America and leave the region when food is scarce . Evening grosbeaks have found bird feeders in the north woods to help keep them supplied with energy in the winter , but sometimes they fly farther south looking for food .
