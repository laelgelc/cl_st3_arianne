<h> Abstract <p> Climate change is a well-documented driver of both wildlife extinction and disease emergence , but the negative impacts of climate change on parasite diversity are undocumented . We compiled the most comprehensive spatially explicit data set available for parasites , projected range shifts in a changing climate , and estimated extinction rates for eight major parasite clades . On the basis of 53,133 occurrences capturing the geographic ranges of 457 parasite species , conservative model projections suggest that 5 to 10% of these species are committed to extinction by 2070 from climate-driven habitat loss alone . We find no evidence that parasites with zoonotic potential have a significantly higher potential to gain range in a changing climate , but we do find that ectoparasites ( especially ticks ) fare disproportionately worse than endoparasites . Accounting for host-driven coextinctions , models predict that up to 30% of parasitic worms are committed to extinction , driven by a combination of direct and indirect pressures .
