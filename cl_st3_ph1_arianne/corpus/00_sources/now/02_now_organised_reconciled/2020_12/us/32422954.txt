( Gary Coronado / Los Angeles Times ) University High School <h> Opinion : The abolishment of capital punishment is long overdue <p> As Martin Luther King Jr . said , " returning violence for violence multiplies violence , adding deeper to a night already devoid of stars . " This exemplifies my position regarding the barbaric nature of capital punishment . <p> The death penalty was used during the Reign of Terror in France , during the Dark Ages of the Medieval Times , during the Salem Witch Trials . Should we , in 2020 , still be holding on to such a primitive practice ? <p> The answer is simple : no . <p> The death penalty has been a part of American society since its inception . When European settlers came to America , they brought with them their laws and values , one of which is the practice of capital punishment . A study by the National Academy of Sciences titled " Rate of False Conviction of Criminal Defendants Who are Sentenced to Death " found that at least 4.1% of defendants sentenced to death in the United States are innocent . <p> Recently , on Dec. 23 , 2019 , the Philadelphia District Attorney 's Office dropped all charges against Christopher Williams who had been wrongfully convicted of a triple homicide in 1989 , according to the Death Penalty Information Center . He spent 26 years on death row despite being completely innocent . <p> This is simply unacceptable . <p> America is the only modernized Western nation to hold on to this practice , with 29 states still allowing the death penalty as a form of punishment as of Dec. 12 , 2019 . Many cite the deterrent effect as a reason for why we should hold onto capital punishment . The rationale behind the deterrent effect @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cause people to be less likely to commit egregious crimes . <p> However , numerous studies have disproven the deterrent effect and its modern applications in criminal psychology . In a study conducted by Michael Radelet and Traci Lacock published in the Journal of Criminal Law and Criminology , it was found that " 88.2% of respondents do not think that the death penalty deters murder -- a level of consensus comparable to the agreement among scientists regarding global climate change . " <p> Within the death penalty system itself , inherent injustices are being committed . In 1976 , in Gregg vs . Georgia , the Supreme Court approved the modern death penalty statutes and ensured that they were no longer arbitrary and discriminatory . <p> The Court stated that " capital punishment is an expression of society 's moral outrage at particularly offensive conduct . " Yet the evidence from the past 44 years clearly shows that the death penalty does indeed remain arbitrary and discriminatory . <p> According to the American Civil Liberties Union , blue-ribbon study commissions have shown that across the country , a defendant @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ receive the death penalty than a defendant who kills a person of color , and the racial configuration most likely to result in a death sentence is a black-on-white crime . <p> Next , the death penalty places unnecessary economic burdens on American taxpayers . According to the Death Penalty Information Center , keeping inmates on death row in California costs taxpayers $114 million a year beyond the cost of imprisoning convicts for life . <p> This significant figure comes from legal costs , pretrial costs , jury selection , trial costs , incarceration , and appeals . Money that could be used for public education , healthcare , social services , and other underfunded areas is instead being put to waste by keeping a few inmates on death row for years on end . <p> On Jan. 25 , 2018 , former Governor of California , Jerry Brown , stated that California 's prison budget in 1970 was about 3 percent of the general fund . Now , it 's 8.9% , about $12 billion . <p> These billions of dollars being spent to keep inmates on death row @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ according to Governor Gavin Newsom , ranks 41th in the nation in per-pupil funding . With California being the world 's 5th largest economy , this should not be happening . <p> Furthermore , investing in education can prevent crime later down the road . As a study by the Economic Opportunity Institute shows , children who did n't participate in the preschool program , who therefore missed out on some important opportunity for early childhood development , were 70% more likely to be arrested for a crime by the age of 18 . <p> Therefore , by investing in education , the government will be preventing crime , thereby reducing the prison population and the money needed to sustain keeping those inmates in prison . <p> The dispensation of justice in America is inherently flawed and the systemic issues that plague our justice system directly threaten the integrity of this institution .
