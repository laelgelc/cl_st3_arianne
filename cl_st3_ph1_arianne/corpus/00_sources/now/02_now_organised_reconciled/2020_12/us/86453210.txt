<h> After warmer temps , sprinkle of rain possible in the Valley later this week <p> The Valley can expect hotter weather and a slight chance of rain this week . <p> The higher temperatures will come ahead of a storm system approaching from the Pacific Northwest that may also bring precipitation closer to the weekend , according to Paul Iniguez , a meteorologist for the National Weather Service in Phoenix . <p> " It 'll be a little breezy mostly tomorrow , kind of a warm breeze , " Iniguez said looking ahead to Monday . " It could potentially hit 80 degrees at some point over the next few days , but upper 70s are a lock . " <p> This heat will be significantly higher than the average temperatures for this time of year , which are usually in the upper 60s . Thankfully , though , Iniguez does n't expect any record-breaking highs . The all-time hottest temperatures during early December are in the mid-80s , he said . <p> The storm system moving southward from Oregon may brush by the Phoenix area and bring rain @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a graphic that showed Phoenix has a 36% chance of getting a tenth of an inch of rain on Thursday . <p> " If we do get rain , it looks like it would be pretty light , which would still be of note -- it has n't rained in almost four months at this point , " Iniguez said . <p> Sunday was the 108th straight day without measurable rain in Phoenix . In Yuma , it 's even worse , with Sunday marking their 237th day without rain , Iniguez said . <p> According to measurements from the weather service , 2020 has seen a little more than 4 inches of rain , just two-thirds of the normal 6 inches Phoenix typically sees by this time of the year . <p> Human-driven climate change has been pushing up average temperatures throughout the year and bringing warmer days in the fall on average . The urban heat island effect , wherein asphalt absorbs the sunlight during the day and radiates heat throughout the night , also contributes to rising temperatures in the Phoenix metro area .
