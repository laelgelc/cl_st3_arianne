<p> The United States Fish and Wildlife Service said on Monday that a seismic survey planned for the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge in Alaska this winter would have " no more than a negligible impact " on the numbers of polar bears in the region . <p> The finding was contained in a proposal by the agency to allow up to three incidents in which bears could be inadvertently harassed or disturbed during the survey work , which would take place over several months and is meant to detect signs of oil and gas reserves underground . <p> The agency said that a few incidents of unintentional harassment , by , for example , coming too close to a bear and causing it to flee or interrupt feeding , would not affect survival . It added that it did not expect any bears to be physically injured or killed during the survey . <p> The proposal is to be published in the Federal Register on Tuesday , after which the public will have 30 days to comment . With climate change reducing the extent of sea ice , their main habitat , the subpopulation in the southern Beaufort Sea declined by about 40 percent from 2001 to 2010 .
