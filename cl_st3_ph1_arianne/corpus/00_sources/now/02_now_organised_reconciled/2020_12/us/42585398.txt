<p> Fifty years ago this month , the Clean Air Act was signed into law . Half a century later , the success of this landmark public health law is astounding , and its mission and promise of providing clean air for all has never been more important . <p> For decades , the Clean Air Act has done its job , safeguarding our health and environment by working to clean up our air . In fact , the Clean Air Act has been estimated to have prevented 230,000 early deaths in 2020 alone . Furthermore , from 1990 to 2020 the Clean Air Act resulted in nearly $2 trillion worth of economic benefits , due to improved health and worker productivity , and savings on medical expenses for air pollution-related health problems . <p> As a lifelong advocate for lung health , I have seen first-hand the benefits of the Clean Air Act . I first began my career as a health educator , developing , implementing and evaluating programs for adult and pediatric lung disease -- diseases made worse by air pollution . Between 1970 , when the Act was signed into law , and by 2019 , aggregate emissions of common air pollutants dropped 77% , all while the U.S. GDP grew 285% . <p> Americans are breathing healthier air now because of the Clean Air Act , but there is much more work to do . Nearly half of Americans are still breathing unhealthy air , according to our 2020 " State of the Air " report , and climate change poses new challenges to cleaning up air pollution . Rising temperatures are contributing to higher levels of ground-level ozone pollution , and more frequent and powerful wildfires are leading to more spikes in particle pollution .
