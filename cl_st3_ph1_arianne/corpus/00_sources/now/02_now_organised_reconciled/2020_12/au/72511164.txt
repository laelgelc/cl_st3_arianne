<p> Small water-reliant communities will need more targeted support as climate change batters the Murray-Darling Basin . The Murray-Darling Basin Authority has released its latest evaluation of the $13 billion plan for more water to be returned to the environment across the river system . The report found the overall impacts on communities were positive but water-dependent small towns were doing it tough . MDBA executive director Vicki Woodburn said more targeted support was needed to help parts of the basin struggling to adapt . " Some towns are struggling while others are growing and thriving , " she told reporters . Climate change information for basin communities , industries and governments is set to be crucial to the future management of the river system . " The climate is changing . It 's no longer an over-the-horizon challenge , " Ms Woodburn said . MDBA chief executive Phillip Glyde conceded the plan had been poorly explained to communities most affected by returning more water to the environment . " It 's absolutely astounding to me that this change has been made - that 20 per cent of water has been taken out of consumption to be provided to the environment - and we have n't had revolution in the streets , " he said . Mr Glyde did accept there were some communities seething with the implementation of the measures . " It 's understandable there 's a lot of anger . You do n't make a change of this scale without causing a lot of grief , " he said .
