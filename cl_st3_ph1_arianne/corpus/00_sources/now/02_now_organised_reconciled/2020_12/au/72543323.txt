<h> Will Biden 's EPA Take On Wood Burning ? <h> Dharna Noor <p> Last week , President-elect Joe Biden nominated Michael Regan , who now heads North Carolina 's Department of Environmental Quality ( DEQ ) , to lead the Environmental Protection Agency . One of the top issues he grappled with in North Carolina could also be prominent at the EPA as the Biden administration tries to reduce carbon emissions : burning wood for energy , which could lead to climate doom if not properly regulated . <p> Regan 's record at DEQ has garnered praise from policymakers and activists alike for creating his agency 's first Environmental Justice and Equity Board in 2018 , as well as helping win settlements to clean up PFAS and coal ash pollution . But on biomass , it 's more mixed . <p> Burning wood pellets for energy has been touted as a way to reduce emissions by the industry . Trees planted to replace those chopped down for wood pellets , in theory , suck up carbon dioxide , making the process carbon neutral . But the door is open for Biden 's EPA to overturn that rule , and science would be on its side . <p> The EPA could remove wood burning from its list of carbon-neutral fuel sources , disincentivizing states to give contracts to wood pellet facilities .
