<h> Synthetic meat is the new frontier of food <p> For thousands of years , humans have had to kill animals to eat meat . This is no longer the case -- at least in Singapore . Cultured meat is now legal in the city-state . <p> The Singapore Food Agency has approved chicken nuggets from a San Francisco-based company called Eat Just , which is known for its cultured meat . This is a world first , according to the agency . <p> Experts in food science , toxicology , nutrition , epidemiology and other fields in Singapore have deemed the product safe for human consumption . <p> So now laboratories are making meat . Indeed , synthetic agriculture appears to be the new frontier in food . <p> The cell-cultured chicken will be licensed to a local manufacturer and sold under the GOOD Meat brand . Most products will be sold to restaurants . <p> No animals are killed to make the product . The process begins with cell isolation , where cells are sourced from a live animal . The process allows anyone to design the perfect meat product , from iron-rich chicken to pork with all the vitamins you need . Anything is possible . <p> The timing is no coincidence and numerous factors are behind this technology . <p> Consider what happened in abattoirs around the Western world during the first wave of the COVID-19 pandemic , with several plant closures , vulnerabilities and disruptions in the supply chain . It seems these problems can be avoided with lab-produced meat alternatives . <p> This is particularly interesting for the cattle and hog sectors , which are still dealing with overwhelming backlogs caused by processing plant closures in the spring and summer .
