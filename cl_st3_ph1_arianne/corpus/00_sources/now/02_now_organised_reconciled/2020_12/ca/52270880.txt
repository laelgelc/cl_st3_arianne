<p> Ben bakes bread in the off-season , and Mark enjoys woodworking . We both read and occasionally we -- gasp -- watch TV . <p> Now that the garden is put to bed , we finally viewed a documentary that has been highly recommended to both of us : Kiss the Ground , featuring Woody Harrelson as commentator . <p> This documentary is a compelling discussion about the state of a most valuable natural resource -- soil . Specifically , the planet-saving power of soil . <p> Kiss the Ground is a treatise to the value of soil . It begins as many documentary films do , with an overview of our dire straits . The facts they lay at our feet are disturbing : since the 1970s we have lost 33 per cent of existing soil worldwide . This is called desertification and it is what happens when we remove supporting plant life like forests , tropical jungles and native prairie grasses and create open soil , farmland . Since the " dust bowl " in the American and Canadian prairies in the 1930s , which was caused @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ than 40 million people have been pushed off their land worldwide . At the current rate of desertification , two billion people will be " desert refugees " by 2100 . The other issue is climate change , which we know about but most of us do not fully appreciate its relationship to soils . <p> One enemy is open cultivation . When we cultivate soil , we release carbon and kill microbes in unthinkable numbers . Microbes , which include bacteria , protozoa , fungi , algae , amoebas and slime molds , are the building blocks of life . When you hold a handful of healthy soil in your palm you are holding more microbes or microorganisms than there are people on earth . Soil is not only the basis of life , but healthy soil is teaming with life . <p> Harrelson predicts that all our topsoil will have completely disappeared in 60 years if soil continues to erode at the current rate . This is important as soil is the bedrock ( poor analogy , we know ) of virtually all the food that we eat @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cows and pigs eat plants , which grow in soil . <h> Hopefulness <p> As Harrelson says , " there is another path " . Traditional methods of farming and food gardening can change and there are people who have embraced new ideas that eliminate the need for cultivation and maximize the amount of carbon that farmers and gardeners return to the soil . These modern-day soil-stewards produce more food per square metre and enhance the ground that they grow in . They do this by minimizing tillage and allowing livestock to graze like the wild buffalo did , eating their feed out of the pasture and leaving only their nutrient and carbon-rich droppings to feed the soil versus being fed corn or soy in feedlots . <p> Kiss the Ground provides some science lessons that were not lost on us . For example , the human body is " one per cent human and 99 per cent microbes " . Microbial activity is perhaps the least understood and under appreciated function in the natural world . <p> We know that plants produce all the oxygen that we breath . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of microscopic life under the surface of the soil and in our oceans that enables plants to exchange carbon dioxide , the gas that we breathe out , for oxygen , gas we breathe in , while sequestering carbon at their root zone . <p> Paul Hawken , an environmentalist and sometimes entrepreneur , is interviewed in the film and suggests that we can reduce , even eliminate the effects of climate change by employing " technology that has existed for millions of years " .
