<p> A recent debate on a Chinese state-owned company 's interest in acquiring TMAC Resources , a Canadian-based mining company that owns the Hope Bay Project in Canada 's Arctic backyard , has rekindled our existential Northern sovereignty anxiety once more . This time the duel is between Dr. Michael Byers on one side , and retired major-general David Fraser on the other . Byers takes the position that the purchase is no threat to Canadian sovereignty while the retired Major-General argues that it does . <p> Of course , in any great debate , including one that seems to reappear every decade or so such as Canada 's sovereignty in the Arctic , there are elements of truth in both positions . However , missing from both points of view is the articulation of Canada 's short , medium and long-term interest in the Arctic and those of its residents . <p> The government released its Arctic and Northern Policy Framework on the eve of the last federal election , noting it was short on details and timelines .
