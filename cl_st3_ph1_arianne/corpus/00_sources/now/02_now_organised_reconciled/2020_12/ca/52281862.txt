In today 's reactors , we take a heavy radioactive material , such as uranium , and use it to generate heat , which is used to create steam to turn turbine generators . Uranium is a large atom that is just on the verge of breaking up . We give it a gentle tap with a neutron and the atom splits into pieces , releasing energy . This process is efficient but leaves behind radioactive waste and damaged reactor vessels . <p> Fusion , on the other hand , uses light elements , such as isotopes of hydrogen and lithium . Simply put , it is the energy that fuels the stars . By putting together the equivalent of four hydrogen atoms , we create one helium atom that weighs slightly less than the four hydrogen atoms . This difference in weight is converted into energy and can be considerable . And why have we not been using fusion all this time ? <p> Stars use the pressure of gravity and about 15 million C to smash together the atoms . The sun converts a little more than four million tonnes of hydrogen into energy every second . This results in enough energy to power all the processes on our world and to keep us toasty warm . Doing that on the Earth is indeed a daunting task . <p> Because we can not achieve the massive pressures in the core of the sun , we need to heat the hydrogen to about 100 million C to get the atoms to fuse . This creates a problem of containment . Any container you put this in will be instantly vaporized . We can contain this high-temperature gas using magnetic " bottles , " but they are hard to control and keep stable while the atoms fuse . Another approach is to use high-powered lasers to smash atoms together in a process called inertial confinement . <p> There are several government and private labs that are working on this now and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ put into the system .
