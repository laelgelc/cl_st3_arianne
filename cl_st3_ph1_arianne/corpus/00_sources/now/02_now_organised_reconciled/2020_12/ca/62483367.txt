Even in a year of shocking news stories , this one carries high voltage . At first glance , introducing a tax hike of this magnitude might seem like touching the third rail for a minority government contending @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ every product or service that uses petroleum-based fuels would become more expensive . And while that might rile fiscal conservatives , many progressives will , instead , feel betrayed by the relative lack of new money in this plan -- $15 billion -- to stimulate post-pandemic , climate-friendly economic growth . <p> From this perspective , Trudeau deserves credit for a bold policy change that finally makes it possible for Canada to keep the promise it made to the world in the Paris Agreement of 2015 . At that time , Trudeau pledged that by 2030 , Canada would reduce its carbon emissions by 30 per cent from what they were in 2005 . No Canadian federal government before now has come close to making such a major cut in the country 's greenhouse gas emissions . <p> The United Nations warned last week that the world 's temperature is on track to rise by three degrees Celsius this century -- a planetary warming that could bring catastrophic results . Without the kind of radical changes Trudeau now proposes , Canada will fail to meet the goals it has agreed @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> That 's why Trudeau 's plan deserves Canadians ' support . It 's meant to change how we live , not raise more cash for Ottawa . Consumers can look forward to receiving carbon-tax rebates that should result in financial gain for many , if not most , households . <p> Even with the rebates , the rising costs of petroleum-based fuels will encourage Canadians to profoundly change their lifestyle . Some will buy electric vehicles . Others will rely more on public transit or retrofit their homes . Industries will change how they operate , too . New technologies will be perfected and become profitable as they drive the transition to a low-carbon , and eventually carbon-neutral , economy . <p> This is a market-driven approach to battling climate change that 's been endorsed by many economists . Indeed , it 's one the federal Conservatives might have advocated themselves . Regrettably , it appears they 'll continue to oppose carbon taxes without offering a viable alternative of their own . <p> While eminently sensible , however , Trudeau 's plan is not a done deal . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of federal carbon taxes , and the Supreme Court will have to side with Trudeau for his plan to survive . Meanwhile , a federal election is highly likely next year . If that happens , voters can provide their own verdict on whether Trudeau 's carbon-tax increases live or die after 2022 . <p> The prime minister knew all this before he rolled the dice on carbon taxes .
