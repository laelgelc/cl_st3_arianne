This is because of a phenomenon known as homeostasis . Homeostasis is a balance of competing interests . <p> We speak of the balance of nature and this is a very real thing . We have certainly seen this with people who went from a small population of only about 2,500 individuals a few tens of thousands of years ago , to more than six billion today because there were few things to stop the population growth of humans . <p> Humans have no predators and can reproduce as fast as the available resources allow . Our ability to produce enough food is perhaps the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ technology has allowed the planet to support many more individuals than would be possible in a natural ecology . <p> This balance can be profound if it is tampered with . <p> The absence of wolves in Yellowstone Park allowed a huge increase in the deer population . This in turn changed the dynamics of the forest as young tender plants were browsed to death . This also had great effects on the deer population as they went from population boom to starvation , to massive die-off , to the next population boom . <p> When wolves were reintroduced , the deer population started to decline due to predation . The lower numbers allowed the trees to recover and new forested areas were developed . As well , the wolves caught and ate the weaker of the deer population , so the quality and strength of the surviving herd increased dramatically . Yes , after a time , a new balance point will be established that will allow life to continue , but it will never be the same . <p> Of course , things are not quite as simple as the Yellowstone wolves . In many cases , species have been eliminated , not because of direct human predation , but because of the disappearance of habitat , climate change , and predation of species the predators relied upon for food . <p> A good case in point is the Australian Tasmanian Devil . This creature is the largest marsupial carnivore on the planet . <p> At about the size of a small dog , they have a reputation for ferocity that is almost unmatched in the animal kingdom . They can bite right through bone and are relentless predators as any watcher of cartoons in the 1960 's will remember . <p> They disappeared from the Australian mainland for unknown reasons , but most likely human predation of their primary food source was the cause . The only remaining population of these creatures is in Tasmania on the southern coast of Australia . <p> To the dismay of the feral cat population in southern Australia , the Tasmanian Devil is being re-introduced to the mainland . The cats formally filled an ecological niche that the Devils used to inhabit . In response , the cats have turned to daylight hunting , leaving the night to the Tasmanian Devils . <p> Nature is a complex web of interlocking relationships that we tinker with at our peril . Any small change can have dramatic effects on the world -- effects we will not know about until they happen . <p> Unfortunately , in some cases , it might be too late to walk the changes back .
