<h> Climate emergency or not , data shows New Zealand has one of the worst climate records in the industrialised world <p> Following this week 's climate emergency declaration , New Zealand will have to face up to the fact it has one of the worst climate records of industrialised nations . <p> Of 43 industrialised countries -- known as Annex 1 countries -- 31 are experiencing a drop in emissions . But 12 have seen net emissions increase between 1990 and 2018 , and New Zealand is near the top of this group . <p> As part of the Paris Agreement , countries were asked to submit emissions reduction targets . These Nationally Determined Contributions ( NDCs ) are a measure of a nation 's commitment to contribute to the goal of limiting warming to well below 2 ? . <h> Related News <p> New Zealand submitted its NDC in 2015 , with a headline target of bringing emissions down over the coming decade to 30 percent below 2005 levels . But this is not what it seems . <p> New Zealand 's NDC confuses the issue by adopting @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ baseline of gross emissions in 2005 . This target actually allows New Zealand to increase net emissions . <p> Last year , New Zealand introduced the Zero Carbon Act , making it one of few countries to have a zero-emissions goal enshrined in law . But current short-term policies do not yet keep up with the ambition to reach net zero emissions by 2050 . <h> Fair and ambitious climate action <p> It was clear at the time of the Paris Agreement that countries ' initial targets would be woefully insufficient for limiting warming to well below 2 ? . Therefore , the agreement requires countries to show a " progression over time " to reflect each country 's " highest possible ambition " . <p> In addition to increasingly more ambitious targets , countries were also asked to explain why their intended contribution to the common aim was fair .
