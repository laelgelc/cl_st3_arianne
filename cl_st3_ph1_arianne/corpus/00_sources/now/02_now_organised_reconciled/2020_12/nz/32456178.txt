<h> stuff family <h> Editorial : the high costs of winter storms <p> The Interislander ferry sails for Picton on Wednesday morning before bad weather forced its cancellation . <p> EDITORIAL : Snow in Dannevirke and Rotorua , strong winds in Wellington , stranded tourists in the South Island , hail the size of golf balls and trampolines that are flung from suburban backyards -- we are weather obsessed in New Zealand . Not just because snow is picturesque and floods look dramatic on TV , or because we still think of ourselves as primarily rugged outdoor types dependent on agriculture , but because very bad weather can stop everything here . <p> Every winter reminds us that we are a country at the mercy of changeable weather and natural disasters . When the Kaikoura earthquake closed the main freight line between Christchurch and Picton in November 2016 , it was noted that the longer , inland route through the Lewis Pass was often closed by snow in winter . <p> At such times , entire supply lines are disrupted . Flights were delayed and cancelled at Wellington Airport and , as noted , ferries were cancelled . Wellingtonians do not need to be reminded that their precarious location is especially vulnerable to weather events and potential disasters . <p> The conventional wisdom is that storms will only get worse and more frequent due to climate change . Rising sea levels will increase the risk of flooding . Discussions of climate change in New Zealand have focused mainly on the risks of drought in summer but we should also prepare for greater storms in winter . <p> Do we need better contingency planning in New Zealand ? Are we as prepared as we should be ? And are we too reliant on long distance freight rather than goods and products that are locally sourced ? <p> These are not just questions for local and central government . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ even households . <p> In the immediate wake of the Canterbury earthquakes , there was a flurry of national interest in checking whether households were equipped with emergency survival kits . Is your phone always charged and within reach ? Do you have bottles of water stashed ? How about food and candles ? Could you be self-sufficient for days , if you had to be ? <p> Being ready for anything came and went , as all fads do . But weather events such as the storm that hit New Zealand this week remind us of the importance of being prepared and of changing and updating plans when necessary . Above all , it reminds us not to be complacent . <p> It also reminds us to listen to experts and to take them seriously . How many of the drivers stranded on South Island roads thought that warnings did not apply to them ? Or thought that flake news was fake news ? We have seen enough disasters to know it is not worth taking risks .
