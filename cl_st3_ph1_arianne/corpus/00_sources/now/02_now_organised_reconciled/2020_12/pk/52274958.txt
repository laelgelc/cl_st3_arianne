PHOTO : ABID NAWAZ/EXPRESS <p> LAHORE : <p> Coronavirus-induced lockdowns have helped abate Punjab 's smog intensity to a significant degree this year . Where a trend like that would often point towards better health reports , an unforeseen increase in respiratory illnesses in the region has raised new concerns for the medical community . <p> On top of that , experts believe the reduction in air pollution and subsequent drop in smog levels is rather a temporary change . It is feared that air quality might start worsening as soon as the lockdowns are permanently lifted , which is likely to further aggravate the spread of respiratory illnesses <p> According to Mayo Hospital Medical Officer Dr Salman Kazmi , despite clear skies from recent rains and reports of increased air quality , 40 per cent of daily patients in Lahore 's emergency wards are those with aggravated asthma and other respiratory diseases . <p> " The reduction in smog and better air quality should have been beneficial for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is spreading rapidly in winters , has become more dangerous than smog for those with any kind of breathing disorders , " said Dr Kazmi . <p> Environmental experts believe that emissions of harmful gases have reportedly decreased in countries where people have been told to stay indoors in a bid to curb the pandemic 's spread .
