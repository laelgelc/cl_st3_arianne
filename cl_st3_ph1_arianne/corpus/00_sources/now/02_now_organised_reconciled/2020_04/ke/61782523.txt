<h> Summary <p> Youths drawn from 13 counties have come up with a green initiative to make low carbon charcoal from waste materials that contain high fibre content , such as husks from wheat , coconut and coffee , sawdust , maize cobs , hyacinth and peelings from sweet potato and cassava . <p> Operating under the umbrella body , the Kenya Climatic Action Teams ( Kenya CAT ) , the youth 's aim to reverse the adverse effects of climatic change and reduce rates of unemployment in the country . <p> Environmental conservationists recognise use of recycled materials to make charcoal as a major factor in curbing extensive deforestation as well as reducing emission of greenhouse gases , which add up to climate changes . <h> Youths recycle waste to make environment-friendly charcoal <p> Products that the Green Platinum Project Demo makes from recycled matter on display at the stand at the Nakuru Agricultural Show . Photo/Suleiman Mbatiah <p> With the increasing cost of paraffin and cooking gas , any venture to produce an affordable source of fuel would be a relief for Kenyans , especially the low income earners . <p> It is to this end that youths drawn from 13 counties have come up with a green initiative to make low carbon charcoal from waste materials that contain high fibre content , such as husks from wheat , coconut and coffee , sawdust , maize cobs , hyacinth and peelings from sweet potato and cassava . <p> Operating under the umbrella body , the Kenya Climatic Action Teams ( Kenya CAT ) , the youth 's aim to reverse the adverse effects of climatic change and reduce rates of unemployment in the country . <p> Environmental conservationists recognise use of recycled materials to make charcoal as a major factor in curbing extensive deforestation as well as reducing emission of greenhouse gases , which add up to climate changes . <p> Every youth group is a unit of Kenya CAT and each has a production site where they make the charcoal , sold in the form of briquettes , thrice a week . <p> In a day , each group , with an average of 30 members , makes around 1,500 briquettes . The briquettes cost Sh5 apiece as compared to the commonly used wood charcoal that costs an average of Sh40 per kilogramme . <p> Cost- effective <p> " Three pieces of briquettes can cook a complete meal for four people . You will have spent only Sh15 . This is very cheap considering that the least you can spend to buy a tin of charcoal is between Sh 30 to Sh 40 , " says Ruth Chege , the Naivasha Green Platinum Project coordinator , one of the Kenya CAT groups . <p> Not only is the charcoal cost effective , Ms Chege says it can cook for six hours continuously unlike wood charcoal which lasts less than an hour . <p> To cook githeri ( a mixture of beans and maize ) , which may take probably five hours for example , she says one only needs to use five briquettes . <p> " The charcoal is well compressed and emits more energy , which means it cooks faster for fewer hours . You can use five blocks for cooking githeri which is only Sh30 . You will use at least two tins of wood charcoal to cook the same , " notes Ms Chege . <p> She says foreign tourists , who are their main customers , prefer the charcoal for warming their houses as it has a low carbon emissions . <p> " The briquettes are compressed , which means the biomass is compacted and hence releases less carbon dioxide unlike the wood charcoal which is not and wastes a lot of energy , " ex plains CAT-Kenya project officer Kevin Ochieng . <p> The location of a group determines the kind of raw materials it uses . <p> " Our main concern is the content of fibre contained in the raw material . The higher the fibre content , the higher the biomass and the more the energy contained , " says Mr Ochieng . <p> The supply of the raw materials , Mr Ochieng says , is consistent as the groups collaborate with large scale farmers growing the high fibre crops and with waste disposal agencies . <p> Though they began extensive production 15 months ago , Mr Ochieng says they have not been able to penetrate the market due to lack of awareness among the target market . <p> " Our greatest challenge is selling the charcoal mainly because most people are unaware of its existence while others are reluctant . It takes time to convince the people that the charcoal can ideally work and it is environmental friendly , " says Mr Ochieng . <p> So far the highest consumption rate has been in Naivasha , Nakuru , Embu and Gilgil . Most of their clients are in the hospitality industry . <p> To root for market and increase conservation of the environment , Mr Ochieng says the youth groups undertake aggressive training drives at the grassroots . <p> " The youth groups not only make use of the waste but also go out to the community to create awareness on not only the availability of the charcoal but how and why they need to protect and conserve the environment . Knowing the measures they need to adopt to address the issue of climate change will make them understand why the charcoal is the better option , " says Mr Ochieng . <p> The project is sponsored by UK Aid and trainings on charcoal production are jointly done by UN-Habitat and Unep . <p> " Using household or farm waste in the charcoal is efficient instead of cutting down trees , which in the long term results to deforestation . Bare lands do emit a lot of greenhouse gases which escalate climatic changes , " says Nakuru County National Environment Management Authority director Wilfred Osumo . <p> Though no official national statistics have been released , it is estimated that more than 92 per cent of the waste from industries and households remains piled up in dumpsites and open fields . <p> The Kenyan government is yet to legislate policies to specifically address matters relating to recycling biological or electronic waste and reduce emission of greenhouse gases . <p> In Africa , only South Africa -- which is the continent 's highest producer of greenhouse gases -- has a carbon tax introduced through the Carbon Tax Policy Paper . The South African government intends to implement the taxation from January 2015.
