However , it has been naturalised in many other parts of the world , such as Australia , Brazil , New Zealand , Argentina , Southeast Asia and Africa . <p> The tree first came to Africa via South Africa in 1907 before spreading to the rest of the continent . <p> In Kenya , it is the most important pine species , not just because of its premium products like timber , but also for its ability to remove carbon from the atmosphere . <p> Its local names include msindano ( Kiswahili ) , mucinda-nugu ( Kikuyu ) and omobunduki ( Kisii ) . <p> According to the Kenya Forest Research Institute 's ( Kefri ) Guide to Tree Planting in Kenya , it grows in ecological zones of between 1,600 to 3,000 metres above sea level and it is moderately drought-tolerant . The average annual rainfall in its native habitat is from 750mm to 2,000mm . <p> But in Kenya , it grows in zones with annual rainfall ranging from 800mm to 1,400mm . <p> Like the blue gum , it is fast-growing and thus uses a lot of water . Whereas it is good for soil stabilisation because of its fibrous roots , it should not be planted near water sources or with crops . Its fast growth makes it useful in climate change control . <p> Its fibre is used in the commercial manufacture of pulp in the paper industry and boards . <p> The tree grows up to 30 metres tall . For pulp , it can be harvested at between 16 and 20 years and 20-30 years for timber . <p> Its timber is soft and is rated at a higher premium than that from the whistling pine as it sells at an average of Sh55 a foot , the same price as cypress . <p> Climate change <p> When tapped , Pinus patula yields oleoresin , which is distilled to give turpentine , and rosin , which are used in paint and batik ( a technique of wax-resistant dyeing applied to whole cloth ) industries <p> The tree is one of the most important in carbon sequestration , a crucial service for regulation and mitigation of climate change through reduction of greenhouse gas emissions . <p> A study carried out by Forestry Research Network of Sub-Saharan Africa in Kiambu and Nyeri counties found that the tree removes high amounts of carbon from the atmosphere . <p> It involved 99 plots measuring 20 by 50 metres , which were established in government-managed forest plantations of selected species . <p> There were significant differences in the amount of carbon sequestered among species across sites . <p> But eucalyptus saligna had the highest amount of carbon sequestered in Nyeri South followed by Pinus patula in Nyeri North and Cupressus lusitanica in Kiambu . <p> Significant contribution <p> The study concluded that carbon sequestered from selected tree species in central Kenya demonstrated a significant contribution towards reduction of harmful gases , specifically carbon dioxide , which is among greenhouse gases that trap heat in the atmosphere and drive climate change , causing floods , droughts and wildfires . <p> CarbonBrief , a climate change publication , estimates that emissions will drop by 5 per cent this year due to the economic impact of the coronavirus disease ( Covid-19 ) . <p> The United Nations says the world needs a drop of 7.6 per cent annually until 2030 . <p> Propagation <p> Pinus patula is propagated through seeds , which go for up to Sh7,000 a kilogramme at Kefri shops . <p> Pre-sowing treatment is not necessary , according to the Seed Handbook of Kenya ( second edition ; edited by Omondi , W , Maua , JO , and Gachathi , FN . ) However , germination may be boosted by soaking in cold water for at least 24 hours . <p> The germination rate can be low . To test the viability of the seeds , put them in a container filled with water and separate those that sink from those that float . The seeds that float are generally the ones that are least likely to germinate . <p> The bed should be covered with mulch and kept moist . The seeds take between seven to 14 days to germinate . Transplant when a foot tall . The ideal spacing while planting is three metres .
