<h> Memories of World War II-beleaguered Pangasinan and Gen. Douglas MacArthur ; praying for former Senator Heherson Alvarez and wife Cecile <p> The atmosphere of fear and insecurity brought about by the dreaded Coronavirus ( COVID-19 ) pandemic reminds this columnist of the World War II-beleaguered Pangasinan , my beloved home province . <p> We were five years old when the Pacific War broke out in 1941 , following the bombing of the Pearl Harbor by the Japanese forces . In due time , the Japanese troops invaded Manila and then advanced towards the north including Pangasinan . <p> Fearing for our lives , our family joined thousands who hurriedly left our hometown Dagupan and our province . Our father , who was then provincial fiscal of La Union , thought that the safest place to escape would be to our family 's farm in Santa Barbara , 15 kilometers west of Dagupan . We recall leaving home in a horse-drawn calesa , crammed with a few personal belongings . <p> Life during the war was hard . At an early age , we had to help our family by cutting grass some days and sell it to calesa owners to feed their horses . <p> A year after , in 1942 , our family decided to go back to Dagupan . We are delighted to note that at an early age , we had begun to develop a simple flair in entrepreneurship , which earned us a modest profit each day . <p> On the ground floor-corner of our two-story house , we set up a wooden pool table which we rented out by the hour ; on the side we had comic books and magazines that visitors could read for a fee . The comic books and magazines were gifts from our paternal uncle Zosimo , our father 's younger brother who studied fine arts in the United States and returned to the Philippines as a soldier with the liberation forces of the great General Douglas MacArthur , supreme commander of the Allied Forces in the Pacific . <p> We were nine years old when the returning American forces , led by General MacArthur , established a foothold at Lingayen Gulf on January 9 , 1945 . It is still an enigma where MacArthur actually landed in the Gulf area but we are certain he stormed ashore at our hometown Dagupan , confirmed by the fact that he set up headquarters in our elementary school . <p> For nine days leading to the American landing , US warships bombarded the areas in and around Lingayen and Dagupan . American planes droned overhead and excited Pangasinenses waved at them from the ground . A good number were killed or wounded because the US ships offshore did not know the Japanese had already fled to Mountain Province . <p> We knew they were American planes and American troops that were coming to liberate us . The bombers attacked enemy positions in the Lingayen airfield and the ships shelled us in Dagupan . The heavy shelling left many civilian casualties . There were no guerrillas around to inform the Americans that the Japs had already fled eastward to the Mountain Province . <p> On January 13 , 1945 , MacArthur waded ashore on Lingayen Gulf and headed toward a schoolhouse in Dagupan where he set up his temporary headquarters . This was my school , the Dagupan Elementary School , now West Central Elementary School . The great American general was met by jubilant civilians bearing fruits and crying with joy . <p> The home economics building of our school was where MacArthur set up his command post near the Church . We remember the general pacing back and forth or puffing on his corncob pipe or poring over documents in the home economics building , which was right beside our classroom . On occasion he would look up from his papers , gaze down at us , and give a little wave and smile . He was indeed larger than life . <p> We were also ecstatic at this time because we were getting lots of chocolates from American soldiers because we spoke good English and could give them directions or point out certain locations . <p> The Japanese were routed and on general retreat then . Fighting would still drag on for several months in the mountains northeast , but the war was essentially over , because the Japanese forces had been subjugated or fled and then regrouped in Mountain Province . <p> On a sentimental journey to the Philippines 15 years later , in 1961 , MacArthur , considered by Filipinos as the " Liberator of the Philippines , " rode the train from Manila to Dagupan . <p> We were then a young journalist and we covered the general on this trip . We were also privileged to have been allowed by the protocol officer to spend a few minutes with the general . <p> After his sentimental journey to Pangasinan years later , the great general , then 81 years old , spoke to a million people at the Luneta Park by the Manila Bay , in front of the historic Manila Hotel , scene of one of the fiercest battles in the liberation of the Philippines . <p> In addressing the Filipino people , he said , in the now immortal words , " I must say with a sense of sadness that the deepening shadows of life cast doubt on my ability to say once again , I shall return . " The great General died three years later , in 1964 , in Washington , DC . <p> Coincidentally , today , April 5 , is the death anniversary of General Douglas MacArthur -- supreme commander of the Allied Forces in the Pacific , " Liberator of the Philippines , " and , to quote the title of his biography written by William Manchester , the " American Caesar . " <p> *** <p> My wife Gina and I and our family join the family and friends of former Senator Heherson " Sonny " Alvarez and his wife , Philippine arts and theater advocate Cecile Guidote Alvarez who , as of this writing , are both in critical condition at the hospital . <p> Sonny Alvarez , an audacious freedom fighter during martial law , faithfully served our beloved country as senator , secretary of agrarian reform and of environment , Isabela congressman , and climate change commissioner . <p> Cecile Alvarez , an equally courageous democracy advocate , is a multi-awarded theater artist , founder of the highly-regarded Philippine Educational Theater Association ( PETA ) , and former executive director of National Commission for Culture and the Arts ( NCCA ) . <p> Both Sonny Alvarez and wife Cecile continue to serve our country with distinction even in their senior years and in their private capacities . <p> We pray as well for the recovery of our fellow Filipinos and other peoples throughout the world who are afflicted by the raging global plague .
