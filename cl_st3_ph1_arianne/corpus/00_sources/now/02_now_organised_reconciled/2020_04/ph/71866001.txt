" <h> LEADERSHIP <h> RICHARD HEYDARIAN <h> AUTHOR , COLUMNIST , POLITICAL WRITER <p> " Across late-developing countries , from Czarist Russia to post-Marcosian Philippines , there is this obstinately enduring myth that what we need is a ' strongman ' -- a single-minded , decisive leader who can , almost magically , carry a nation through the valley of tears and plunge it into the stream of modernity . What this dangerously simplistic narrative misses is that the success of nations is anchored in strong institutions , not strongmen . What we desperately need are visionary leaders who invest in the bureaucracy and organs of the state as well as sincerely engage and collaborate with the civil society as the intermediaries of collective national development . Leaders , even the best of them , are ultimately fallible mortals , who can deteriorate and become drunk on power if left unchecked . For every Lee Kuan Yew , there are dozens of Mugabes and Gadhafis who devastated their once promising nations . The only way to perpetuate a good legacy across generations is via strong institutions . Even more so in the 21st century , where the very physics of power have shifted beyond our wildest imaginations , thanks to technological disruption , democratisation of information , and climate change . Thus , collaborative teamwork and optimal delegation are sine qua non for good leadership . We need young , dynamic bright leaders willing to look forward and at the big picture .
