If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <h> Kettle : Telecommuting would benefit county after pandemic has waned <p> Highway 101 in Ventura had few cars at noon on March 23 as residents were told to stay home to stop the spread of the coronavirus . ( Photo : GRETCHEN WENNER/THE STAR ) <p> It was fall 2007 when I first came to the Ventura County Transportation Commission as its new executive director . As I introduced myself around the county the first thing people would say is , " Whatever you do , please fix the 101 . " And that request has not let up for the past 13 years . <p> However , I have not heard that phrase once in the past five weeks . <p> Most effects of COVID-19 have been awful . But one of the few positives has been the decrease in traffic on our roads and highways . ( And along with that has been a decrease in smog and greenhouse gas emissions that lead to climate change . ) <p> So when this pandemic finally wanes and we can all return to life as we knew it , what if we made " life as we knew it " something else ? Many of us have learned how to productively work from home . So what if we as employers and employees commit to working from home one day per week ? We would reduce traffic during the morning and afternoon peak gridlock periods by taking 20% of those daily commute trips off our streets and freeways . <p> Most drivers would acknowledge ( perhaps with some guilt ) that it is a pleasure to drive the freeways these days when we have an essential trip . We are not bumper-to-bumper crawling to our final destination . It is tragic that it took a deadly pandemic to teach us what life on the freeways could be like without this traffic , but perhaps something good can come out of this . <p> Nearly 80% of us drive to work each day alone . Our research at VCTC shows commute times have continued to lengthen in the last decade . The imbalance between the cost of housing and the salaries paid in the county leads many to travel long distances to find a job that pays well , and vice versa , many live where housing costs are cheaper and drive here for their job . <p> When this all ends our region and California will recover and again prosper . And with that prosperity so will come the need to move freight safely and efficiently through our region , reopen the front door to California 's Central Coast to hospitality and tourism , provide access to new affordable housing , and , yes , all of those who have lost their jobs because of this mess will go back to work . So , yes , we still have work to do to improve the 101 , 118 and 23 freeways even if we telecommute . <p> But technology has shown us through this public health crisis is that it can be a tool to not immediately find ourselves back staring at the brake lights of the car ahead of us on our way home . The air would be cleaner . Those on the roads would be getting where they need to go faster . In fact , the commuter would spend less money in reduced fuel costs and lower auto maintenance . <p> Technology is our enabler . Broadband high-speed internet can help us get this done . As challenging as they have been , look at all the changes we have made as we figuratively turned on a dime : <p> Businesses stayed connected to their clients and workers through technology . <p> We closed school doors , but we did not close schools . Our incredible teachers and education administrators quickly figured out ways to get lessons to our students . <p> Churches and social organizations ( even such vital self-help groups such as Alcoholics Anonymous ) turned to Zoom , Google Hangouts and Microsoft Teams to keep people connected . <p> Government agencies and boards conducted public meetings . While they were not always as ideal as an in-person meeting , the critical work got done . <p> Just a word to acknowledge that technology can not save every business and we know of many restaurants , hotels , retailers and service industry providers who were deeply hurt by the virus . And we are exceptionally grateful to our healthcare workers , public safety officials and others who continued to put themselves at risk to ensure we are safe . <p> But in a similar manner as we move forward , organizations might consider identifying employees whose commute and physical presence is essential and encouraging other employees who can productively work from home a day or two a week to do so . <p> Ventura County and the California Central Coast are special and unique places and smooth sailing on freeways like the 101 makes them even more that way . Both the public and private sectors should engage in a discussion about how best to leverage what we have learned in this unexpected moment . <p> Darren Kettle is the executive director of the Ventura County Transportation Commission .
