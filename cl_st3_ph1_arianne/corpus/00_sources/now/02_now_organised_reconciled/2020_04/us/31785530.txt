<p> ANN ARBOR , MI -- How Ann Arbor will weather the impacts of the coronavirus COVID-19 pandemic is becoming a major theme in this year 's election . <p> Two candidates seeking an open 5th Ward seat on City Council shared their views on the topic , as well as housing affordability , new development and the city 's carbon-neutrality plan , during a virtual forum this week . <p> Seeking the 5th Ward seat being vacated by Chip Smith are Erica Briggs , a city planning commissioner and longtime walking and cycling advocate , and Dan Michniewicz , a Zingerman 's Bakehouse baker and democratic socialist . <p> Both are running as Democrats and will compete in the Aug. 4 primary . <p> A potential third candidate , David Silkworth , has pulled petitions but has not filed . <p> " Let me begin by acknowledging what an awful time we 're in , " Briggs said . " So many are facing profound loss right now and we 're fearful about what the future holds . " <p> A lot of people are yearning for a return to some sort of normalcy , Michniewicz said . <p> " I 'd like to challenge this and note that , before coronavirus , it was normal for people to be kicked out of their homes because they ca n't pay their rent , " he said . <p> " It was normal for a percentage of our community to have no home to begin with . It was normal for people living in mixed-immigration-status communities to be fearful of leaving their homes . " <p> The world has shifted under everyone 's feet with COVID-19 and it 's evident how segments of the community are vulnerable and how there are disparities in health outcomes , Briggs said . <p> She talked about providing bridge support to struggling small businesses and said she does n't want to return to " the old normal , " citing income disparities . <p> As for how to manage potential city revenue declines , Michniewicz said " it 's the capitalist business cycle " to go through times like this about every 10 years . <p> " This is a conversation that is happening alongside our attempt to address the climate crisis , " he said , adding the city should look at how public land is used and how it delivers services . <p> Michniewicz said he wants to address the role police play in society , noting police services account for a large chunk of the city 's annual budget -- about $30 million . <p> He expressed concerns about " militarism and imperialism , " suggesting the city should reallocate police department funds to social services to address root causes of poverty and economic inequality , rather than " using the police department as a mop and a bucket to clean up after the injustices . " <p> Ann Arbor ward boundaries.City of Ann Arbor <p> Briggs recalled moving to Ann Arbor two decades ago as a 23-year-old college graduate and single mother who had fled an abusive relationship and was feeling hopeless . <p> " I came to Ann Arbor because I had family support here , but what I found was a community that was ready to lift me up , " she said . <p> She said she put herself through graduate school , obtained a master 's degree in public administration , launched a career in public service , and has been working with state and local government on land-use issues . She 's now director of statewide environmental nonprofit Scenic Michigan . <p> She said she 's fortunate to call Ann Arbor home and the city is doing a lot right , but it has some real problems . <p> " In addition to planning for our economic recovery from the coronavirus , we have a housing shortage , we have an affordability crisis and climate change is upon us , " she said . <p> The city needs level-headed council members willing to be bold and make sometimes politically difficult decisions , she said . <p> " So I 'm skeptical that if we have the same group of folks around the table that we 're going to be able to take any bold steps , " she said . <p> Michniewicz said he 's running , in part , because he hears local politicians talk about people like him . <p> " I 'm furloughed from my job making bread right now and I 've made a living since moving to Ann Arbor working in restaurants , delis , kitchens in general , " he said , arguing many people who commute to work in Ann Arbor would like to be able to live here . <p> Michniewicz said he works with those people and organizes politically with people of the same economic stature . <p> " We can do a better job of advocating for our own interests and advancing our own causes than our current political leadership can , " he said . <p> Michniewicz said there are two dominant political factions in the city and he 's not part of either . <p> " We see these two dominant political factions , you know , basically sometimes taking stances on issues just based on what the other side is saying , and it really does n't seem like they are really tied to their convictions , " he said . <p> Michniewicz said he wants Ann Arbor to pursue clean energy through creation of a locally owned public energy utility . <p> The city 's ability to do that and take control of its energy future is enshrined in the state constitution , he said . <p> The city needs to take on DTE Energy and be real about divisions between the community the investor-owned utility that " does not share the priorities that we do as a community , " he said . <p> Both candidates had a lot to say about housing and new development . <p> Briggs said she supports more density , saying it 's proven that increased supply helps address affordability , in addition to other benefits . <p> " It provides better options for transportation when you have more people grouped together , " she said . " You have more services , so we like the idea of neighborhoods where you can go to the corner grocery store , you can go to a bakery , go to a department store . " <p> Greater density and more mixed-use development , particularly along transit lines , also help the city achieve its climate-action goals and fund services residents care about , Briggs said , noting some of the city 's top taxpayers are new apartments . <p> " We need to recognized that if we care about beautiful public spaces , if we care about affordable housing , if we care about our climate-action goals , this is where we 're going to find funding for it , because our budget is tight , " she said . <p> A lot of people have concerns about what new development looks like , Briggs said , saying the city needs to look at its code and address those . <p> Michniewicz said he wants to maximize the amount of " social housing " in the city . <p> " I 'm going to prioritize the creation of social housing like we see through the Ann Arbor Housing Commission , " he said . " I 'm somewhat pleased with the route that the current City Council is taking by looking at things like surface-level parking lots , property that we already own , and figuring out how we can maximize the amount of affordable housing that we put on those lots . " <p> The private sector dominates the housing industry and a lot of new construction is based on " people building things to make sure there 's a return on their investment , " he said . <p> " And when I 'm on City Council , you wo n't see me opposing what people say are luxury apartments just because people like me ca n't afford to live here , " he said . <p> " We ca n't just focus on new potential residents , but we have to pay attention to the needs of existing residents , " he added . " You know , a lot of people are on fixed incomes . " <p> Michniewicz brought up the city 's living wage ordinance , saying a lot of jobs are exempt from it and he wants to see it extended to all city employees . <p> Expressing concerns about entering a recession , Briggs said unemployment is skyrocketing and people are going to be facing financial distress for a while . <p> Though evictions are on hold , Briggs said , she 's worried when the governor 's stay-home order is lifted landlords may move to evict hundreds of people . <p> Council needs to be working on that and looking forward at what 's ahead , she said , noting a majority of residents are renters.
