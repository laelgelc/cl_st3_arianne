If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <h> Five Foothills Christian video production students recognized in national competition <p> Students in the video production class at Foothills Community Christian School were recognized for the second year in a row for their work in a national C-SPAN StudentCam documentary competition with over 2,500 submissions from nearly 5,400 students across the country . <p> This year 's StudentCam documentary competition focused on the topic " What 's Your Vision in 2020 ? " to allow students to explore issues they want presidential candidates to address during their campaigns . Reese Broadbent , Paxton Lauver and Alex Schuman 's video earned them 3rd prize in the Western Division winners with their team 's video titled " Simmering the ' Stir Pot ' of America . " Caed Melone and Vittoria Mendez earned Honorable Mention for their video , " Agriculture and Climate Change : A ' Hot Topic . ' " <p> The videos are the only prize winners from Montana . <p> " I 'm super proud of these kids , they put a lot of time into this and they deserve everything , every bit of recognition they get , " said Dustin Bauer , Foothills Community Christian 's video production instructor . <p> Bauer has worked over the last eight years to develop the video production program at the school . This is the fourth year students from Foothills Community Christian have participated in the competition and the second consecutive year of awards for the school . <p> " Now we 've kind of made a habit of it ( placing ) , " said Bauer . " Now , the expectations are high . " <p> When he first received the email notifying him that his team had earned 3rd place in the competition , Schuman thought it was a joke or a scam and deleted the email . When he pulled up the email later he realized it was for real . <p> Broadbent said that he found out during early in the morning during praise and worship practice . <p> " We were kind of surprised , but we were all excited by the results , " said Broadbent . " There was a lot of people who entered in and we had confidence in ourselves , but I guess we were n't really expecting to get there . " <p> Their video , " Simmering the ' Stir Pot ' of America , " focuses on immigration policy in the United States . The students worked together to produce a documentary-style video that interviewed Great Falls Mayor Bob Kelly and former Montana Gov . Stan Stephens . <p> The team even put in a request to speak with President Donald Trump . Although they were unable to arrange and interview with him on the topic , they did receive a signed letter in response . <p> " They 're definitely topics that need to be discussed instead of being brushed under the rug , " said Broadbent . " I kind of enjoyed getting it out there and seeing other people 's opinions and what they think and how they think we can fix these issues . " <p> Bauer appreciates the way the C-SPAN competition challenges students and pushes them to engage in a productive dialogue about difficult subjects . <p> " They get to be out there and interact with people that do n't have the same beliefs as them , " said Bauer . " The thing with C-SPAN videos is they have to be unbiased , so they get to experience the perspectives of the world , compared to their perspective . So It 's kind of a cool thing for them to be challenged like that . " <p> It was the first time Broadbent , Schuman and Lauver were in the video production class at Foothills Community Christian . For Broadbent , the most challenging part of the experience was knowing where to start . <p> He said that he appreciated the opportunity to speak with Mayor Kelly for the project . Overall , Broadbent and Schuman agreed that the teamwork and persistence were key in their team 's success for the competition . <p> Students in the video production class work on their projects for months . Bauer said that students produce story boards for their projects to ensure that they are thought out before they begin shooting video . Though the process may seem tedious , it 's worth it in the end , he said . <p> " It was very rewarding at the end when we had gotten it finished and we could kind of take a step back and say , ' well we accomplished this and we 'll see what happens from there , ' " Broadbent said . <p> Skylar Rispens reports on education and breaking news for the Great Falls Tribune .
