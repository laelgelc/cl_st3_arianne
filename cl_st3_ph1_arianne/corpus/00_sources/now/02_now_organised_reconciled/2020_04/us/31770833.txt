Blue/The New York Times ) <p> The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency recently announced that , during the coronavirus outbreak , it would not " seek penalties for violations of routine compliance ... in situations where EPA agrees that COVID-19 was the cause of the noncompliance . " <p> Clearly , the current public health crisis needs to be our nation 's paramount focus right now . Nevertheless , we are concerned that this open-ended declaration is part of an unfortunate and yearslong pattern of systematic weakening of federal environmental protections . <p> Advertisement <p> Indeed , there is an aggressive campaign to severely roll back our federal system of environmental enforcement . Recent national reports show that both criminal and civil enforcement to keep our waters clean and our air safe to breathe have plummeted under this administration . Failing to hold polluters accountable puts public health at risk . <p> Fortunately , families across our region have a couple of safety valves to help ensure clean air and water . The first is that in Connecticut and New York , the Clean Water Act and Clean Air Act are implemented and enforced not primarily by the federal government but by the states . Thus , the Lamont and Cuomo administrations can continue to enforce these key statutes . <p> However , there is still a caution here : Gov . Andrew Cuomo recently noted that his state is likely to face massive budget deficits post-COVID-19 due to the unprecedented health response underway , and the federal Care Act passed last week offers a tiny fraction of the funds states like New York and Connecticut will need to reimburse these expenses . We worry about the future of state environmental enforcement when things return to " normal , " as we 've often seen cuts to environmental agencies and state enforcement activities in lean budget years . <p> The second safety valve is the ability to bring citizen suits under the Clean Water Act , Clean Air Act and other environmental laws . These laws permit organizations and citizens to take legal action themselves when the government fails to address a violation of federal environmental law . <h> Latest Op-Eds <p> From our inception , Save the Sound ( previously known as Connecticut Fund for the Environment/Save the Sound ) has brought citizen suit cases in federal court and won extensive cleanup and injunctions against further pollution . We have harnessed the citizen suit provisions of these acts , passed by a far-sighted Congress that recognized that the government needed a back-up enforcement force . <p> As a result , Long Island Sound , which was clogged with sewage in the 1970s and ' 80s , has had a remarkable recovery , including the return of whales and dolphins . Communities across our state have seen relief from the toxic legacy of industrial pollution contaminating soil and groundwater . The Naugatuck River -- which once turned different colors depending on what manufacturers were producing on any given day -- is now home to trophy trout fishing . Save the Sound 's Soundkeeper now patrols the Connecticut and New York shorelines to identify pollution hotspots . And our attorneys are investigating those hotspots and using the Clean Water Act citizen provisions to secure orders preventing millions of gallons of raw and under-treated sewage from being discharged into the region 's rivers , lakes and Long Island Sound . <p> Maintaining clean water and healthy air is a basic public health need , not an optional convenience for when times are easy . We can feel powerless in the face of massive health events and social upheaval , but all is not lost . Local nonprofit environmental enforcers are still assuring that the power of the Clean Water Act and Clean Air Act continue to protect your family . <p> Roger Reynolds is senior legal counsel at Save the Sound , an environmental nonprofit that fights climate change , saves endangered lands , protects Long Island Sound and its rivers , and works with nature to restore ecosystems throughout Connecticut and the entire Long Island Sound region .
