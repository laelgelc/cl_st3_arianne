( AP ) -- When their daughter announced last year she 'd be leaving soon for a job at a remote base on Antarctica , Cheryl Guentzel and Ken Gansen of St. Peter were n't too surprised . <p> " It 's in her nature , " Gansen said about 33-year-old Kris Sack 's personality that led to a three-month stint on the icy continent . <p> " I 've seen her bucket list , " Guentzel said , then mentioned a few accomplishments from the more than 100 adventures Sack 's promised herself . <p> " Kris drove a truck ... and she taught in Japan -- straight out of high school , " Guentzel said . <p> " This is n't her first foray into the cold , " Gansen said . <p> Sack was working at Seward , Alaska , in 2010 when she first set her sights on working at research stations on the opposite side of the world , the Mankato Free Press reported . <p> " I kept trying for 10 years until I was accepted , " she said about her job as a radio dispatcher at an Antarctic research hub about 850 miles north of the South Pole . <p> " I 'm from Minnesota , mostly I wore fleece and flip-flops , " she said , describing her workplace attire . <p> She spent September through February working at McMurdo Station on the southern tip of Ross Island . She was hired by a contractor to handle typical 911 calls -- medical , fires , domestics . <p> Her previous experience as a dispatcher for the Nicollet County Sheriff 's Department was very useful at McMurdo . <p> Sack is thankful she did not have to deal with a major disaster while she was on duty in Antarctica where " every event has the potential to become more extreme . " <p> The research station 's remoteness from the rest of the world -- flights take at least five hours to reach the nearest large community -- Christchurch in New Zealand -- means calls for help may quickly turn into serious emergency situations , Sack said . <p> Dispatchers are a vital part of the communication system on a continent without cellphone towers . Sack 's duties included passing on messages to scientists and assisting them with flight check-ins . <p> " We also keep track of researchers when they 'd go out in the field . " <p> McMurdo is home to a variety of climate and geological research projects as well as wildlife studies . <p> About 90% of U.S. Antarctic Program participants -- 130 to 200 in the winter and up to 1,000 in the summer -- stay at the station . The government organization manages all U.S. scientific research and related logistics in Antarctica as well as aboard ships in the Southern Ocean . <p> When Sack returned to St. Peter in February for a mandated break all employees must take , she brought along loads of educational materials to use during classroom presentations . <p> USAP supplied her with free fact sheets about research , Antarctica and its wildlife , and a knapsack full of winter gear like that worn by the scientists at McMurdo . <p> Two researchers went " above and beyond " to help Sack understand the basics of their research so the projects can be described to schoolchildren . They also sent photos , videos and other materials to use during classroom presentations . <p> " If I was a kid , I would find those kinds of things really interesting , " said Sack , who had completed at least 13 presentations in a variety of classrooms in St. Peter and Nicollet before schools went to long-distance learning because of the coronavirus pandemic . <p> Sack was able to discuss with older students ' projects dealing with climate change research and Jupiter 's moons . <p> " Kris was able to describe her real life experiences by using language they could understand . For example , she used a tape measure to show the length of a seal , " said Erin Stueber who teaches preschoolers in St. Peter . <p> " At the end of her presentation , Kris let them try on some winter gear -- and she offered to come back next year , " Stueber said . <p> Sack is scheduled to return to Antarctica this fall . In the meantime , she 's a management consultant for the St. Peter American Legion . <p> She 's also enjoying time with her husband . Being married to a person who 's curious to explore the world for a couple of months while you stay home in St. Peter has required some assistance from family and friends , James Sack said . <p> His in-laws have helped a lot with taking care of the couple 's two big dogs . <p> He 's got lots to keep busy with in St. Peter -- his job , fishing , hunting and a new smokehouse . Traveling to Antarctica soon is not in his plans . <p> Some patience and a lack of wanting to change someone has been key to their sometimes long-distance marriage . <p> " I know if that 's what she wants to do , then she 's going to do it , " he said . <p> When Kris is back to work at McMurdo , the couple will communicate regularly despite the 10,000 miles between them . She 'll purchase minutes on phone cards so they can talk in real time . <p> " And we 'll be emailing when we have some downtime , " James said . <p> Besides family and friends in Minnesota , she 'll miss having access to fresh fruit and vegetables when she returns to Antarctica , Kris said . <p> She knows what she will and wo n't need for her next stint . <p> " For my first trip , I took all kinds of clothes . That wo n't be the case ( this fall ) . I am going to download a lot more music and books to take with me. "
