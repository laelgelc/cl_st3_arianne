If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <p> Fifty years ago , something so extraordinary happened in the United States it 's almost unfathomable today . <p> Twenty million people , fully 10% of the United States population at the time , turned out at thousands of events coast-to-coast in support of a single goal : a cleaner environment . <p> They did n't sit at home and connect through social media . They actually showed up and participated . <p> The date , April 22 , 1970 , would come to be known as Earth Day . <p> With the country lashed by oil spills , mounting levels of pesticides , smoggy skies and polluted waters , a fuse had been lit on America 's environmental consciousness . <p> The spark was a Wisconsin man , Sen. Gaylord Nelson . <p> A native of Clear Lake in the state 's north woods and a former Wisconsin governor , Nelson was frustrated by the lack of action in Washington on pressing environmental issues during his seven years in Congress . <p> After a 1969 trip to view the oil-sullied Pacific Ocean coast of Santa Barbara , Calif. , Nelson got the idea of " environmental teach-ins " at schools to galvanize change . <p> " If we could tap into the environmental concerns of the general public and infuse student energy into the environmental cause , we could generate a demonstration that would force the issue onto the national political agenda , " Nelson said . <p> The plan proved so popular Nelson set up an independent organization , Environmental Teach-In , Inc. , to handle a flood of queries . He recruited Denis Hayes , a former student body president at Stanford University , to act as coordinator . <p> Nelson also established a steering committee of scientists , academics , environmentalists , and students , and tapped Rep. Paul McCloskey , a Republican from California , as co-chair for the event . <p> Nelson said a key for the event would be " inclusivity " and that the national office would not try to shape a uniform protest . The national day for the environment was to be a day for people to act locally . <p> " This is the time for old-fashioned political action , " Nelson said . <p> Members of the Environmental Action Coalition prepare posters and placards for Earth Day demonstrations in New York in 1970 . ( Photo : Journal Sentinel files ) <h> Huge parades , small class outings <p> Nelson received more than 500 invitations to speak . <p> On the eve of the event , he spoke in Atlantic City , N.J. , to more than 3,000 delegates at the United Auto Workers convention . The workers that year unanimously passed a resolution calling on industry to develop a pollution-free vehicle engine . The Apollo 13 mission had just ended , and there was much talk of NASA 's space program . <p> " I admire the technology and bravery of those who went to the moon , " Nelson said as he addressed the UAW . " But that moon will still be there 25 years from now . But this country may not be , if we postpone dealing with the problems we face today . " <p> Nelson then flew to Wisconsin , where he first spoke at UW-Madison . A planned parade was cancelled hours before his arrival out of fears of disorder . <p> He next drove to Milwaukee , where an evening presentation drew a packed house of 1,700 people at Milwaukee Area Technical College 's Cooley Auditorium . <p> Carmen Ninham , a member of the Oneida tribe , hands a pamphlet to U.S. Sen. Gaylord Nelson of Wisconsin as he arrives at Milwaukee Area Technical College to speak at a rally on the first Earth Day in 1970 . ( Photo : Journal Sentinel files ) <p> " In order to enhance the quality of our environment , it 's time we started to place our gross national quality on a par with gross national product , " Nelson told the audience . <p> True to Nelson 's wishes , the events varied widely across the nation . <p> There were large gatherings , such as on New York 's Fifth Avenue , where traffic was blocked off and a massive crowd paraded down the urban canyon . <p> And many smaller ones , including in Shorewood , where the junior class of Shorewood High School picked up 2.75-tons of trash along the Milwaukee River . <p> Father Quentin Quesnell officiated at a special Mass at Marquette University 's Brooks Memorial Union ballroom . Quesnell prayed over a globe , a jug of water , a balloon to symbolize air and a pile of dirt . The ceremony had to be moved from intimate Joan of Arc Chapel to accommodate the large crowd . <p> That same day Boy Scouts in Wauwatosa collected tin cans from parks and ditches ; local businesses paid them one penny apiece . <p> In all , the first Earth Day included events at 10,000 elementary and high schools , 2,000 colleges and in more than 1,000 communities . <p> Nelson described it as " nothing short of incredible . " <p> Students were well-represented , but labor union members , housewives , farmers , scientists and politicians of all stripes -- from Barry Goldwater to Edward Kennedy -- made up the mosaic of faces in Earth Day crowds , according to a Wisconsin Historical Society account . <p> But the questions being asked in the wake of the April 22 events were : Will it last ? Is it just another fad ? Will politicians be as sincere about saving the environment after the fall elections ? <p> " We are being overwhelmed with oratory -- a lot of hot air , " said Sen. Abraham Ribicoff , a Democrat from Connecticut . " Industry has to be taken by its heels and shaken up . Pollution is a good issue for the politicians -- a safe issue . Now the question is to make them deliver . " <p> In what contemporary Americans would find hard to believe , in 1970 the environment was a bipartisan winner . <p> Even though President Richard Nixon did not take part in an Earth Day event , he said " the activities show the concern to people of all walks of life over the dangers to our environment " and it " should be more than a one day event and should be the beginning of a new and sustained effort . " <p> A movement had crystallized . The country would never be the same . <p> Sen. Gaylord Nelson holds a jar of contaminated water from the Potomac River sent to him by a tourist to Washington in 1973 . ( Photo : Journal Sentinel files ) <h> Numerous pieces of legislation passed <p> Earth Day was a watershed moment for environmental politics , kicking off what is now termed the " Environmental Decade " of legislative reforms . <p> In the following years Congress passed the Clean Water Acts , Clean Air Act , the National Wild and Scenic Rivers Act , the Federal Pesticides Act , the Environmental Education Act , the National Hiking Trails and the National Scenic Trails Acts . <p> Under Nixon , the federal government also created the Environmental Protection Agency . <p> A witness to the change was George Meyer , then a 22-year-old law student at the University of Wisconsin and part-time employee of the Department of Natural Resources . <p> " It was a transformative era , " Meyer said . " It was the golden age of environmental protection in this country . " <p> Meyer started work at the DNR 12 days before the inaugural Earth Day and did not take part in a formal event . However , he will forever be linked to it . <p> After he finished his law degree in 1972 , Meyer was hired full-time by the DNR , one of three staff positions funded through the Clean Water Act . Meyer would go on to become DNR Secretary and is now executive director of the Wisconsin Wildlife Federation . <p> Earth Day has since become an international event celebrated in more than 180 countries . <p> The occasion helps draw attention to current environmental issues , including climate change , loss of biodiversity and extinction of species . <p> Arguably the country -- and the world -- needs change now more than ever to confront the crises of the day . This year , however , there will be no large in-person gatherings due to the coronavirus pandemic . <p> Many have asked if Americans in 2020 are capable of the kind of activism and cooperation required to meet daunting challenges such as climate change . <p> In 1970 , Nelson said Earth Day worked because of the spontaneous response at the grassroots level . <p> " We had neither the time not the resources to organize the 20 million demonstrators who participated from thousands of schools and local communities , " Nelson said . " That was the remarkable thing about Earth Day . It organized itself . " <p> Would that be enough today ? <p> Meyer , for one , sees reason for optimism in part caused by the COVID-19 outbreak . <p> " Peoples lives are being changed and we 're seeing the need for science in a very immediate way , " Meyer said . " If we as a nation continue to value science to address these larger , longer term problems like climate change , I think we could see another time of significant progress . " <p> In 1970 , Nelson said millions of Americans were mobilized for a cause affecting every one of them , keeping the earth livable . <p> As Earth Day turns 50 on Wednesday , Nelson not only leaves a legacy of environmental activism and success but provides a shining example that change is possible . <p> Our subscribers make this reporting possible .
