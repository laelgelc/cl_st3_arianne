ET April 14 , 2020 <p> CLOSE <p> Jill Buhler and her sons join in neighborhood effort to offer messages of inspiration and encouragement while under stay-at-home order during coronavirus panedemic . York Dispatch <p> A thick blanket of smog and dust is shown on the outskirts of New Delhi , India , in 2017 . Air pollution around the world has been markedly reduced during the coronavirus pandemic . ( Photo : Altaf Qadri ) <p> Opportunity can present itself during the most difficult of times . <p> Now is such a time . <p> There is no doubt that the coronavirus pandemic is a worldwide challenge that has left hundreds of thousands dead , millions sick and billions afraid . <p> Unfortunately , it may take years to fully emerge from this scourge -- economically , physically and mentally . <p> Still , when we are finally permitted to fully emerge from our house-bound cocoons , we will likely encounter at least one pleasant surprise . <p> Our environment will be noticeably and pleasantly cleaner . <p> After just a month of forced confinement , our planet is enjoying a much-needed respite from the daily pollution that normally plagues our skies , our water and our land . Reduced traffic on our roads and reduced economic activity have drastically reduced our use of fossil fuels . <p> Not surprisingly , reports are pouring in from across the world about the improved air quality . <p> Los Angeles , which has had some of the tightest coronavirus restrictions in the nation , also normally has some of the worst air quality in the nation . Now , L.A. has turned into one of the nation 's cleaner cities , according to IQAir . In fact , L.A. has enjoyed its longest stretch of " good " air quality since 1995 . <p> Similar reports are being recorded in other international megacities , such as Bangkok , Beijing and New Delhi . <p> There 's little doubt that our oceans , rivers , lakes and streams are also benefiting from this momentary reduction in the toxins we normally spew into our environment . Scientists have even noted that noise pollution has been significantly reduced . Even the litter on our roadways has lessened . <p> All of those factors should help us in our ongoing battle against climate change . <p> Economic cost : Of course , this decrease in pollution is coming at an enormous economic cost . Millions of folks are out of work and hundreds of thousands of businesses have been put at risk . <p> Once the coronavirus crisis abates , many of those people will , hopefully , go back to work . Traffic will obviously increase and our factories will again , hopefully , roar back into action . <p> And the pollution , unfortunately , will return , but it does n't have to return at the previous levels . <p> Over the past month , we 've had the opportunity to see what can happen to our environment in just a short span . Our planet 's ability to rebound can often be quite remarkable . <p> We must chart a new path : That 's why , when the coronavirus pandemic passes , we should do our best to chart a new course in an effort to reduce the pollution pandemic that harms our health daily in ways that may not be immediately tangible , but are nonetheless quite real . They eventually catch up to us through increased levels of respiratory illnesses , cancerous growths and other diseases . <p> During the current crises , many businesses have likely discovered that many of their employees can work quite effectively from home , thus reducing the traffic on our roads and the pollution in our air . That is a trend that should most definitely continue . <p> Those same businesses should also more vigorously pursue alternative forms of energy that do less harm to the environment . <p> Additionally , on a more personal level , we have all likely learned over the past month that many of our car trips are , quite simply , unnecessary . In the future , we need to walk or bike to our destinations whenever feasible and car pool whenever possible . When you do venture out on errands , try to combine those chores into one trip . <p> Taking advantage of this opportunity : We have been given an opportunity and our planet has been given a desperately needed , short-term break . <p> We ca n't afford to mindlessly return to our old habits and allow this opportunity to pass us by .
