If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <h> New Mexico relief for oil and gas industry called a ' bailout ' by environmentalists <p> The move , announced by the New Mexico State Land Office in early April , saw broad support among oil and gas companies welcoming the relief and environmentalists who sought to limit or stop oil and gas production all together during the pandemic . <p> As of Monday , domestic crude oil was trading at about $18 per barrel , with New Mexico dropping to just 84 active drilling rigs -- the lowest number all year . <p> As part of the rule-making process , the New Mexico State Land Office held a public hearing on Friday , soliciting comments from oil and gas leaders and activists throughout the state . <p> The emergency rule would allow state land lessees to apply to the State Land Office to shut in oil wells , while maintaining the terms of the lease for 30 days , with an option for the Office to extend the time to 120 days . <p> Royalties paid on a shut in well would be calculated on a well-by-well basis but would be capped at twice the amount of the annual rental rate . <p> Annual rent payments range from 25 cents to the cap of $1 per acre , meaning the royalty owed on a shut-in well would be capped at $2 per acre . <p> CLOSE <p> Oil prices are down due to high supply , low demand and coronavirus . Cincinnati Enquirer <p> Ryan Flynn , executive director of the New Mexico Oil and Gas Association said the temporary regulations allowing operators to voluntarily curb production until the market stabilizes was a necessary step to ensure one of New Mexico 's biggest economic drivers remained viable . <p> He said the industry would be key to New Mexico 's economic recovery once the virus was contained . <p> " New Mexico 's oil and natural gas producers agree that the State Land Office 's emergency rule to allow lessees to voluntarily curb production is a prudent and necessary step amid a historic drop in the global demand for energy , " Flynn said . <p> " This temporary rule will help preserve the long-term value of resources on State Trust Lands for both the state and energy producers . It is critical that we continue to work together to fight and end the coronavirus pandemic in order to give our state the best opportunity to bounce back . Oil and natural gas producers will continue to be here , as we always have , to help fuel our recovery and the lives of New Mexico 's families . " <p> Oil companies Chevron and Marathon both voiced their individual support of the measure , hopeful the efforts could reduce the economic impact of the low market value for domestic oil and protect their bottom lines . <p> But environmentalists , while they agreed less oil and gas should be produced on state land , argued the reduction in costs for shutting in wells was an unfair " bailout " for the industry that supported what opponents called the dangerous process of hydraulic fracturing . <p> " While the whole world is dealing with a public health crisis , the oil and gas industry is looking for another bailout to continue fracking forever , " said Rebecca Sobel , senior climate and energy campaigner for WildEarth Guardians . <p> " These proposed rules respond to the current economic collapse but fail to consider the fact that the State of New Mexico is actually losing money by allowing more oil and gas extraction , while contributing to toxic pollution and climate change . " <p> Margaret Wadsworth , senior organizer for Food and Water Action said the State Land Office should also adopt rules to not only shut-in wells but remediate the environmental impacts they pose to ground water and air pollution . <p> " While a step in the right direction , we 're asking Commissioner Garcia Richard to adopt rules that do n't just allow companies to stop production , but that require the shut-in and environmental cleanup of wells that are economic losers for New Mexico , " Wadsworth said . <p> Ally Beasley , an attorney for the Western Environmental Law Center said New Mexico should also move away from oil and gas extraction and transition to renewable energy , rather than continuing to support an industry she said created a problematic level of pollution . <p> " For our health and economic stability , New Mexico leaders must chart a transition away from fracking and oil and gas , " Beasley said . <p> " We understand the emergency nature of this rule , and now is the time to diversify New Mexico 's economy , not create loopholes and bail out polluters . New Mexicans require protection as the industry collapses . " <p> New Mexico State Land Commissioner Stephanie Garcia Richard providing relief for oil and gas producers was needed for the State Land Office to continue supporting public schools and hospitals with state oil and gas revenue . <p> " Here in New Mexico , the ripples of this situation hit hard , not only when thinking about the state budget , but within communities where people rely on the boom for jobs to support their families , " Garcia Richard said . <p> " Due to these factors , I 've determined that it is in the best interest of the beneficiaries of state trust land -- our public schools , hospitals , and universities -- as well as the employees dependent on this industry , that we allow companies to apply for these temporary shut-ins until we can better predict the future of the Permian Basin .
