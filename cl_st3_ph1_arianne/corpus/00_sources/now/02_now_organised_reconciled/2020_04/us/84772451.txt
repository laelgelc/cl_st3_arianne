If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <p> Farming is a way of life in the Hawkeye State , and to commemorate Earth Day on April 22 , Cherokee County farmer Nathan Anderson received shine from People magazine for his climate action efforts on his 1,000-acre farm . <p> " It 's a little bit surreal . I feel pretty undeserving of being in a spot like that just because there are hundreds of farmers that I look up to and learn from that are equally or more deserving to be in that than I am , " Anderson said . <p> " I get that at the same time it 's a great opportunity for a farmer to be in a national magazine and have a little bit to say on an issue that is impacting a lot of people in agriculture and food production . So pretty humbling overall . " <p> Cherokee County farmer Nathan Anderson was featured in a recent issue of People magazine . ( Photo : Iowa Interfaith Power &amp; Light Facebook/Special to the Register ) <p> In 2012 , Anderson said that his grassland and pastures , hay , and some corn were negatively affected by the drought in his northwest Iowa farm . <p> " It was like a slow-motion train wreck , " Anderson said through an email sent by People . " Then , in 2013 and 2014 , we had some pretty severe flooding that drowned out our crops and left a lot of debris in our fields . " <p> The devastation of his crops convinced Anderson , who has been farming for a decade , to create a resilient and enduring farm . <p> Anderson graced a page in the magazine with the help of the Iowa Interfaith Power &amp; Light executive director Matt Russell , who saw Anderson 's work of helping to find a solution to the issue of climate change by reducing emissions and using systems to capture carbon . <p> The organization is dedicated to empowering people of faith and conscience " to take bold and just action on climate action , " the IPL website reads . <p> For Anderson , his farm 's mission as it pertains to the involvement with IPL is to " honor God , our family and community by caring for the resources that we 've been trusted , " he said . <p> So far , 50 farms across Iowa are helping IPL develop the message , Russell said . Anderson uses several practices beneficial from a climate standpoint that can build and improve soil health , so it can hold more water in times where there is a lot of rain or not enough . <p> Anderson plants his crops ( soybeans and corn ) by tilling not to disturb the soil and uses livestock grazing to restore needed prairie grasses . <p> Cherokee County farmer Nathan Anderson ( Photo : Nathan Anderson/Special to the Register ) <p> " Perennials on the landscape are more effective at pulling carbon out and storing carbon from a longterm standpoint , " Anderson said . " The managed grazing that we do where our cows move from spot to spot on a regular basis allows a lot of those deep-rooted native grasses to put carbon back into the soil . " <p> Russell said that farmers could use five leading practices to reduce climate change and help agriculture : conservation tillage , integration livestock , permaculture , extending crop rotations , and generating energy . <p> As the executive director of IPL since April of 2018 , Russell 's goal for the organization is to get the right leaders of farmers to share the five methods and instill greater climate action . <p> Sierra Porter covers entertainment for the Des Moines Register .
