If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <h> City of Ventura settles lawsuit , agrees to turn over records related to Thomas Fire <p> The city of Ventura has agreed to turn over records to county Supervisor Steve Bennett , who sued for their release after failing to get answers on how critical infrastructure fared during the Thomas Fire . <p> In particular , Bennett wanted to know why the city 's water system appeared to fail and how fire personnel were deployed when the fire broke out on the night of Dec. 4 , 2017 . <p> In the settlement with Bennett , the city said it had 82 documents responsive to Bennett 's public records request from March 25 , 2019 , that had not yet been released . It agreed to release all the records , except for two documents it said were prepared specifically for litigation , an exemption under state public records law . <p> " I 'm glad we 're finally getting the public information that we had a legal right to get two years ago , " Bennett said Monday . " I 'm sorry it has taken us two years . " <p> He filed the lawsuit in Ventura County Superior Court on Aug. 2 , 2019 . While his district includes Ventura , he filed suit as a city resident . <p> Bennett said he was frustrated with a lack of information released by the city about the fire , including why water became unavailable . <h> Posted ! <p> A link has been posted to your Facebook feed . <p> Firefighters run for their lives Saturday at De La Garrigue Road and Highway 150 near Ojai on Dec. 9 . Jay Calderon/USA Today Network <p> The Thomas Fire started just north of Santa Paula around 6:30 p.m. as Santa Ana winds gusted 60 mph . Within hours , flames had reached the city of Ventura . <p> Residents began hosing down their roofs and yards , turning on sprinklers and siphoning water from pools in desperate attempts to save their homes . Generators meant to back up the water system failed to operate when the power went out . Water pressure crashed in some areas . <p> According to an after-action report released by the city more than 15 months after the fire , the system lost pressure as residents and firefighters all rushed to use the water . Leaks also exacerbated the problem . <p> Bennett had requested records regarding water tank levels , how many generators the city had , and where and how they were used in the days before , during and after the fire . <p> He said the public needed to know what happened and what changes may have been made . Without that information , he said residents could not assess whether any changes were adequate . <p> The city initially withheld records , citing exemptions in state public records law that include pending litigation , attorney-client privilege and that the public interest was better served by keeping them private . <p> According to the city , it settled because its other litigation had wrapped up . <p> " Some of the documents he requested were subject to various exemptions under the California Public Records Act because for quite a bit of time we were involved in litigation , " said Andy Viets , senior assistant city attorney . <h> City to pay attorney fees <p> The city 's claim against Southern California Edison related to the fire was resolved by December 2019 , he said Monday . Lawsuits from some homeowners also had been dismissed . <p> " Once that occurred , we believed that the appropriate thing to do would be to try to resolve the case with him , " Viets said . <p> Bennett also had also asked for fire personnel records to look at how and when staff was deployed , according to his lawsuit . The city had released information regarding fire employees last year , but had refused to release most information related to the generators and water tanks . <p> Steve Bennett ( Photo : STAR FILE PHOTO ) <p> Bennett 's attorney had argued the documents requested were created for internal management and operations purposes , not potential litigation . <p> State law exempts documents specifically prepared for use in litigation or prepared in anticipation of litigation , but neither of those applied to records requested by Bennett , his attorney said . The city also failed to provide an adequate justification for why the public 's interest was better served by not releasing the records , according to his attorney . <p> Included in the settlement , the city agreed to pay Bennett $35,000 in attorney fees , a cost Bennett said had grown when the city sought a change of venue for the case . The motion , heard in December , was denied . <p> Bennett signed the settlement in early April and said he had received documents electronically in the past few days . <p> The city hired outside counsel to litigate the lawsuit with Bennett . Viets said Monday he did not have information regarding the cost of those services paid by the city . <p> Cheri Carlson covers the environment for the Ventura County Star .
