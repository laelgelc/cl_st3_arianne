That would be the biggest cut in U.S. energy emissions since at least 1990 , EIA records show . The year after the start of the 2008 recession saw a 7.3% decline . <p> Trump routinely mocks the science of climate change , and his administration has moved to roll back tougher mileage and emissions standards and other climate efforts from the Obama administration . <p> Emissions will fall markedly this year anyway , owing to the slowing economy and restrictions on business and travel related to the coronavirus , the EIA said . <p> Burning of fossil fuels , and the rate of climate damage , typically rebounds as an economy does , after economic downturns . <p> Globally , " we 're seeing radical declines in transportation emissions and drops in other sectors of the economy , " said Stanford University 's Rob Jackson , who heads a group of independent scientists who monitor global carbon pollution . " We have n't seen anything like this since the Great Depression . " <p> The energy agency projects Americans will burn 9% less gasoline and diesel and 10% less jet fuel , and that the electricity sector will generate 3% less power overall , among other declines . Solar and wind power -- which get scant attention from Trump , other than his statements of loathing for wind turbines -- will account for the majority of the country 's new electricity generation , the report says . As marketplace competition reshapes how Americans get their energy , power plants will use 11% more renewables and 20% less coal this year . <p> The 2020 outlook also marks a setback in Trump 's frequently stated mission of helping make the United States the world 's dominant player in energy production . The coronavirus and an unrelated petroleum supply glut caused by ramped-up pumping by Saudi Arabia and Russia will return the United States to being a net importer of petroleum for at least a time , as domestic drilling subsides , the EIA report said . Any global accord to cut back oil production could change that , the agency noted . <p> " These trends are only temporary and they 'll go away as fast as this coronavirus crisis goes away , " said climate scientist Jonathan Overpeck , the University of Michigan 's environment dean . <p> <p> AP Science Writer Seth Borenstein contributed to this report . <p> <p> The story has been corrected to reflect that the University of Michigan 's environment dean , not an Energy Department spokeswoman , said a drop in climate-changing emissions during the coronavirus outbreak would be temporary . <p> Today 's Top Insider StoriesOur journalists provide in-depth analysis and reporting about the people , places and issues that matter most to you .
