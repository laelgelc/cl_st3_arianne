<p> The largest hole ever observed in the ozone layer over the Arctic has closed , says Copernicus ' Atmospheric Monitoring Service . <p> Scientists spotted signs in late March of a rare hole forming and it was thought to be the result of low temperatures at the north pole . <p> The ozone layer shields the Earth from most of the Sun 's ultraviolet radiation , which is a major cause of skin cancer . <p> The record-breaking hole would only have posed a direct threat to humans if it had of moved further south to populated areas . <p> But on Thursday ( April 23 ) , Copernicus - the EU 's earth monitoring programme - announced the hole had now closed . <p> The closing has nothing to do with the reduction in pollution caused by having much of the world on COVID-19 lockdown . <p> Instead , it 's down to the polar vortex , the high-altitude currents that normally bring cold air to the polar regions . This has split in two giving the Arctic region a relative heatwave , with temperatures up to 20 ? C higher than is normal for this time of year . <p> The polar vortex weakened and split in two has diluted the ozone holeCopernicus ECMWF <h> Why has an ozone hole formed in the Arctic ? <p> This year the polar vortex has been extremely powerful and temperatures inside it have been very cold . <p> This generates stratospheric clouds that destroy the ozone layer by reacting with CFC gases , banned by the 1987 Montreal Protocol . <p> In recent days , the polar vortex has broken up and weakened . Copernicus ECMWF ( European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts ) predicts it will form again , but without affecting the ozone layer as much . <p> " It is very unusual for such a strong ozone depletion to occur in the northern hemisphere , but this year 's polar vortex was exceptionally strong and persistent , and temperatures were low enough to allow stratospheric clouds to form for several months , " Copernicus scientist Antje Inness told Euronews . <h> Why is this Arctic ozone hole so rare ? <p> The better-known ozone hole is above the Antarctic at the South Pole and occurs during the austral spring ( July to September ) when the stratosphere is naturally much colder . In general , the conditions for ozone destruction on this scale just does n't happen at the North Pole . <p> This year , the strong and stable polar vortex has caused the concentration of more ozone-depleting chemicals than usual , which added to the extreme cold has created the conditions for this unprecedented hole . <p> The first time an ozone hole was observed at the North Pole was in 2011 , but it was smaller and during the month of January . <h> Is the hole related to climate change and what are the consequences ? <p> It is still too early to attribute this phenomenon to climate change or to assess the consequences , either in the short , medium or long term . <p> " We do n't know at the moment why the dynamics were so unusual this winter , " said Inness . " I 'm sure many scientists will do modelling studies to find out the reasons for this . " <p> The direct consequence is increased UV exposure in the areas through which this ozone hole has passed : during the period Alaska , Canada , Greenland and parts of Russia . <p> Furthermore , they consider that " what happened should not have any effect on the northern summer and neither does it allow us to say much about how the summer will be " . <h> How big is the Antarctic ozone hole ? <p> The Antarctic ozone hole in 2019 was the smallest since this atmospheric disruption caused by CFC gases was discovered in 1985 . The banning of these gases by the Montreal Protocol in 1987 has allowed a reduction in the Antarctic ozone hole and even the gradual restoration of atmospheric currents in the southern hemisphere , according to a recent study . <p> Last year 's reduction in the ozone hole was also due to an extreme episode of stratospheric warming .
