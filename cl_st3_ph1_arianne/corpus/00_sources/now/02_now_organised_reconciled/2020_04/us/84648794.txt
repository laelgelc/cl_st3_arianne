If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <p> OPINION <h> Weir : On Earth Day , no better time to think globally and act locally <p> Fifty years ago , in 1970 , the first Earth Day was celebrated . Wisconsin Sen. Gaylord Nelson had witnessed the destruction from the Santa Barbara oil spill the previous year and was committed to raising public consciousness about air and water pollution . His partner in the bipartisan founding of Earth Day was Congressman Pete McCloskey , a Republican from California . <p> On April 22 , 1970 , 20 million Americans demonstrated at massive rallies for a healthy , sustainable environment . Having seen decades of toxic dumping , pesticides , loss of wilderness , deforestation , extinction of wildlife and polluting factories , they marched and protested across the country . <p> Overwhelming support from Democrat and Republican leaders subsequently led to four years of landmark environmental legislation , including the Environmental Protection Act ; the Clean Air Act , setting the first national air quality standards ; Clean Water Act ; and the Endangered Species Act , which was co-authored by McCloskey . <p> For many individuals , state and local governments and businesses , air pollution had been accepted as a byproduct of prosperity . Then , because of the Clean Air Act in 1970 , regulations enacted over the following decades , vehicle emissions dramatically decreased , and clean , renewable sources have become an economically feasible alternative for power generation . In spite of clean air initiatives over the past 50 years , the burning of fossil fuel ( coal , crude oil and natural gas ) has increased by over 100% in the U.S. , contributing to dangerous greenhouse gases and climate change . <p> Coal-fired power plants , which emit harmful chemicals including mercury , lead and carbon monoxide , continue to foul the air . Ninety-five percent of the world population is exposed to concentrations of particulate matter that exceed the World Health Organization 's recommended limit . Nations with the cleanest air are Canada , Australia and Scandinavian countries . The worst areas are in Asia , Africa , the Middle East and India . Air pollution contributes to millions of deaths each year and is a suspected factor for the severity of COVID-19 in certain affected regions . <p> Deforestation negatively impacts our climate and air quality as well . About half of the forests in the eastern part of the United States were cut down for timber and farming from 1600 to 1900 . Since 1970 , the National Environmental Policy Act has helped to protect our forests , and cities across the nation have instituted tree-planting programs to improve air quality and provide shade and carbon sequestration in urban areas . The biggest current threat is massive forest removal in the Amazon to make way for palm oil plantations and cattle grazing . <p> The Endangered Species Act of 1973 has been instrumental in saving the bald eagle , humpback whale , gray wolf and grizzly bear from extinction , in addition to protecting hundreds of other threatened species . However , biodiversity since 1970 has continued to decrease . Currently the total worldwide biomass of mammals is estimated to be 60% livestock , such as pigs , chickens and cattle ; 36% human ; and only 4% wild animals . Greater species diversity ensures natural sustainability for all life forms , and habitat loss from climate change , mining , pollution and urban sprawl endangers thousands of species on our planet . <p> Since 1970 , single-use plastics have become ubiquitous as a part of our consumer-driven , " convenience " culture . Think about 50 years ago -- no plastic bags , no plastic water bottles or coffee cup lids . The average American now generates about five pounds of trash per day and waste generation rates are rising around the world . Over the decades , cities started recycling programs , and plastic , paper and glass were collected and diverted from landfills , sold to be reused . Much was shipped to China , the biggest market for recyclables . <p> But in 2018 , burdened by too much waste , China essentially shut down acceptance of plastics and most paper , forcing many cities to send all their trash to landfills . E.J. Harrison in Ventura County has successfully adapted to other markets for their recyclables . Plastic waste can take anywhere from 10 to 1,000 years to decompose in our landfills , is toxic when incinerated and comprises 50% to 80% of the litter in our rivers , oceans and beaches . <p> Over the past two months , air pollution has decreased by an estimated 50% worldwide . Children in cities that had been smothered in smog for decades are able to breathe fresh air , and see clear blue skies , far-off mountains and the wonder of a starry night . <p> On this Earth Day 2020 , let 's envision and celebrate a future that is less wasteful , less toxic , more healthy and sustainable , for our environment and our local economy . We can all make a difference by limiting consumption of products that contain palm oil , buying local produce , supporting small businesses , using less plastic , planting trees , walking and riding bikes more and driving less . There has been no better time to think globally and act locally .
