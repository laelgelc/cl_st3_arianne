From Palm Springs , Calif. , I 'm Mark Olalde . ( Many apologies that I abandoned you last week while furloughed . I promise it wo n't happen ... until I 'm furloughed again . ) <p> President Donald Trump 's management of public lands has raised eyebrows since the beginning . He tasked a zealous opponent of federal land ownership with leading the Bureau of Land Management , the country 's largest landholder . So the irony was n't lost on environmental observers when Trump suddenly made access to national parks a political issue during the coronavirus pandemic . <p> A National Park Service poster advertises social distancing . ( Photo : National Park Service ) <h> MUST-READ STORIES <p> The coronavirus will not save the environment . Being able to see far enough on a typical Los Angeles morning to count the cars ahead of you in traffic usually signals a day that 's low in pollution . Angelenos were beginning to get used to such clear days , in part because stay-at-home orders kept cars off the roads , planes on the ground and some pollution out of the air . But the Los Angeles Times ' Tony Barboza has the sad truth for us : Coronavirus is not an environmental savior and air pollution is already back . If we really want to clean up air quality , we 'll need to electrify homes , switch to electric vehicles and cut our addiction to fossil fuels . <p> Paper water is worth some paper . The San Francisco Bay Delta , where the San Joaquin and Sacramento rivers meet , is both the site of great ecological importance as well as huge political fighting over access to its water . The Nation is out with a deep dive on the threats this habitat faces and what this could mean for the multi-billion-dollar agriculture and fishing industries . <h> POLITICAL CLIMATE <p> A tractor kicks up dust in Imperial County on December 5 , 2017 . ( Photo : Richard Lui/The Desert Sun ) <p> The great oil crash of 2020 . Oil is n't dead , but its price did dip below $0 recently . For the next few fiscal quarters , drillers will have a tough time finding buyers willing to pay enough to cover the cost of pumping their crude . The expected string of oil and gas bankruptcies has already begun , and Diamond Offshore Drilling filed for bankruptcy protection on Sunday , S&amp;P Global Market Intelligence writes . That 's far from the last domino expected to fall . Reports of other imminent reorganization , debt-shedding and bankruptcy efforts are already swirling . <p> How do you value a destroyed wetland ? Hakai Magazine is out with a new feature that digs into the permitting process for the Pebble Mine , a massive , proposed copper and gold mine in Alaska that , if approved , would destroy important salmon habitat . In order to earn permits , the mining company behind the operation is offering up a variety of offsets , ranging from beach cleanups to sewage treatment system improvements . But former regulators question this new approach to offsetting environmental impacts and wonder whether this would really trade apples for apples . <h> Food supply chains are falling apart <p> A catastrophic waste . As the economy stumbles in the face of coronavirus shutdowns , millions of people are out of work and going hungry . That does n't mean , however , that the country 's farms are n't still pumping out food . In a new investigation , Politico reveals how the U.S. Department of Agriculture failed to quickly use its authority to purchase and distribute food when supply chains disintegrated . Large amounts of produce are now rotting without ever reaching restaurants or grocery stores . " In California , the industry is projected to lose more than $1 billion per month , " according to the story . <p> The looming plague . East Africa has been hit with with huge food insecurity in recent years , made worse by devastating swarms of locusts . Now -- as a changing climate made fertile breeding grounds and the coronavirus pandemic is hindering a response -- the United Nations predicts locusts could soon devour 2 million square miles in the region , half the size of Western Europe . The Wall Street Journal has the story . <h> AND ANOTHER THING <p> An illustration of the coronavirus ( Photo : USA TODAY Network ) <p> Do you think you 're smarter than a scientist ?
