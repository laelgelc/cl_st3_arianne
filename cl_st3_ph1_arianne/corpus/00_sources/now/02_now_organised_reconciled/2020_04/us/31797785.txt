<p> Jonas Irwin , his family and friends still can not grasp that his parents simply disappeared without a trace . <p> The son of the missing Palo Alto couple last seen at their vacation rental in Inverness on Valentine 's Day said nothing adds up about their mysterious disappearance . <p> " It 's not reasonable given who they were , " Irwin said . " They did not disappear . That 's not in character . It 's not who they were . They 're very connected and responsible people . They had every intention of coming back . " <p> Irwin and his family have been reeling ever since Ian Irwin , 72 , and Carol Kiparsky , 77 , went missing , leaving authorities baffled and a grieving family with few answers . <p> Irwin fears the worst . <p> " There 's no way they 're going to reappear at this point , " Irwin said . " I mean , you do n't know until you have them physically . But the prospect of us never getting that closure is really going to be bad . " <p> His parents were supposed to take a quick Valentine 's Day trip to the area around Point Reyes Station at a cottage on Via de la Vista Road in Inverness , just up the mountain from Shell Beach , where authorities say witnesses last saw them . <p> The couple never checked out of their cottage on Saturday . They did n't show up for an appointment on Sunday and missed a play they bought tickets for that evening . Authorities who went to the cottage found all their belongings still there -- phones , wallets and their vehicle -- and no signs of foul play . <p> The Marin County Sheriff 's Office on Thursday said the extensive search efforts over the past week , with every resource that has been available , " would have located Carol and Ian if they were responsive or in an area accessible by foot on land . " <p> After combing through all leads and areas surrounding the couple 's vacation rental , the sheriff 's office is now calling this a " recovery mission " and plans to search for the pair for at least four more days with help from the US Coast Guard , the National Park Service and the same resources they have been using so far . <p> As for the family , they 've laughed and cried and gone " through every emotion you can think of " together , Irwin said . In some ways , the disappearance of his father and his stepmother have brought the blended family closer together as they try to absorb what happened to their parents . <p> What 's most surprising to Irwin , he said , is that the couple got lost hiking , something they would do together frequently . <p> He recounted fond memories of hiking , camping and singing with his father in the country , and described him as an avid outdoorsman , a huge hiker and a rock climber with lots of gear he no longer used . <p> Irwin said his father -- a scientist with more than 25 years of experience in Parkinson 's research -- always felt comfortable going into the back country , and used to travel dozens of miles off trail . He also said he does n't think it odd that they left all of their belongings , as his dad was " always kind of leaving keys and wallets everywhere " but " had an active mind and focused on solving complex problems . " <p> Irwin said he also wants his parents to be remembered the way so many Palo Alto residents saw them . <p> " They are obviously pretty hippie-dippie , " Irwin said , adding that extended to their politics , something that frequently came up at the dinner table . " They 've been like these activist-type people . They think a lot about social causes and are active in their community . " <p> Trying to keep Palo Alto from " over-gentrifying " with massive developments , Irwin said his parents frequently attended council and community meetings and belonged to a climate change team focused on keeping the city 's character intact . <p> " It 's just who they are , " he said . <p> Irwin said his stepmother -- a prominent linguist and author of books on languages married his father after divorcing her ex-husband Paul Kiparsky -- was " put on this earth to nurture and care and give . " <p> He described her as " smart as hell " with an IQ level likely " off the charts . " <p> She left his father starstruck and in love when they met after picking up their kids at summer camp , Irwin said . <p> Irwin and now stepbrother Michael Kiparsky became best friends at that camp , and when Irwin 's father picked him up " Carol was there looking young and pretty with pretty long hair and it was just fireworks . " <p> " ( Michael ) was my best friend , " Irwin said . " And over time he turned into my stepbrother . " <p> For now , Irwin said the family 's focus is still on finding them . Irwin just wishes there was someone to guide the family through the nightmare they are living . <p> " We wish we had someone to just come in and say ' here 's what we do and here 's the next steps , ' " Irwin said . " To tell us ' do n't worry about this , or that . ' " <p> But through all the tears and worry , Irwin still sees the bright side . <p> " They were together , two peas in a pod , " Irwin said . " And another thing is that we wo n't have to go through the dementia , the deterioration as they get older . ( Ian 's ) health has gone down . It could be a lot worse . My family is also bonding in a way we never would have otherwise , and that 's making us feel good . There 's some silver lining there , I think . " <p> One person was transported to a trauma center and several others received minor injuries Thursday when a truck lost control and slammed into several cars before crashing down atop one car on Holman Highway .
