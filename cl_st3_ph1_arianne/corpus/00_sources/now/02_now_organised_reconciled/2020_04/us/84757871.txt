But lesser known are the more rare causes ; here are six you probably have n't heard about . <h> Study : Humans Are Becoming Dumber Because Of Rising Carbon Emissions <p> Carbon emissions have long been linked to climate change and various health problems . Now , new evidence shows it could also negatively affect how humans think . <p> A new study , published in the journal GeoHealth , found that indoor carbon dioxide could reduce human cognition . It also predicts that by the end of the century , heavily exposed populations may become dumber . <p> Higher carbon dioxide levels in the blood could reduce the amount of oxygen in the brain . Researchers said the decrease causes sleepiness , higher levels of anxiety and impaired cognitive function . <p> The global carbon levels started to significantly increase when the industry started to use more fossil fuels in the 19th century . The carbon dioxide in the atmosphere has already reached more than 410 parts per million ( ppm ) , a figure significantly higher than records in the past 800,000 years , IFLScience reported Monday . <p> Emissions around the world are expected to continue growing . Researchers estimated that outdoor carbon levels could climb to 930 ppm by 2100 . In the same period , indoor concentrations may also increase to 1400 ppm . <p> Exposure to carbon dioxide at such levels may have a significant impact on the human brain . Researchers said humans may reduce basic decision-making skills by nearly 25 percent and complex strategic thinking could drop by 50 percent . <p> " At this level , some studies have demonstrated compelling evidence for significant cognitive impairment , " Anna Schapiro , study co-author and assistant professor of psychology at the University of Pennsylvania , said in a statement . " Though the literature contains some conflicting findings and much more research is needed , it appears that high-level cognitive domains like decision-making and planning are especially susceptible to increasing CO2 concentrations . " <p> The findings come from the analysis of current global emission trends and localized urban emissions . However , the team noted more studies are required to confirm the effects of carbon exposure on how people think . <p> " This is a complex problem , and our study is at the beginning , " Kris Karnauskas , lead author and associate professor at University of Colorado Boulder , said . " We need even broader , interdisciplinary teams of researchers to explore this : investigating each step in our own silos will not be enough .
