<h> A touch of environmental optimism amid COVID-19 <p> Posted Friday , April 24 , 2020 5:39 pm <p> Do n't miss the big stories . Like us on Facebook . <p> By Marina M. McCoy <p> As many people go without jobs , not being able to make ends meet , wondering what the future may hold ; talking about the health of the environment may not seem relevant when you are trying to get food on the table . Which has people wondering , is climate change linked to COVID-19 ? My response ? Absolutely . How ? One of my favorite philosophers , Jared Diamond , speaks to this very subject in his book , " Collapse : How Societies Choose to Fail or Succeed . " The book mainly focuses on how some societies thrive and others well .... collapse . <p> What 's the overarching reasoning on why societies collapse ? They use up all their natural resources that they depend on and fail to realize that they need to change their habits to sustain their community . In the book , he explains how catastrophic events are typically what makes societies realize that they need to change , and most of the time , it was too late . <p> Why do I bring this up ? I fear that we are heading down the same path . Our " business as usual " was not working for us . Our constant need for instant gratification and overconsumption of natural resources has proven to have deep negative externalities in regards to the health of our planet and our communities . If COVID-19 has taught us anything , it has taught us we can all get by with less consumption of goods and services . If we can react and adapt this quickly to a disease , we can make aggressive changes towards fighting climate change . <p> We are habit-forming creatures . We may doubt that at times , but the truth of the matter is our systems get " shocked , " but eventually , they adapt to the new normal , and we make it through . The proof is right in front of us . We have significantly adjusted our lifestyles to fit the mandated orders by our government . We can do the same thing with sustainable habits ; we can adopt them . As with all new habits that you begin to implement , you shock your system , so it feels a little uneasy . But if you work through it , it will start to feel " normal . " <p> I have been living zero-waste for the past six years , and what I have found that intimidates people the most with living a sustainable lifestyle ( besides accessibility ) is they feel like it is " all or nothing . " That if they ca n't live zero-waste " perfectly , " they might as well not do it at all . <p> But that is n't the case . You do n't need to be living zero-waste " perfectly . " You do n't need to be living a vegan lifestyle " perfectly . " And you do n't need to be living a minimalistic lifestyle " perfectly . " What you need to do is try , try every day . Understand that there are going to be slip-ups , but that should n't discourage you and convince you to give up altogether . Work on it every single day and try to improve little by little . <p> One plant-based meal a week is better than none . Slowly work your way up to eating one or two days a week without meat , and eventually , go longer if you can . Do n't try to go all in with living zero-waste , see where you can adjust your lifestyle without it shocking your system to the point of giving up . <p> Article Continues After These Ads <p> The point of this being is , small mindful changes are better than nothing at all . Especially when those small changes are made by millions of people . Think about that collective impact . <p> Let me bring up the point again -- we should n't go back to " normal " because " normal " was n't working . <p> We have proven to ourselves that we can live with less : less consumption , less travel , less buying in general . We can manage to take fewer trips to the store , which saves on gas and ultimately saves on pollution . We can learn to appreciate the community right in front of us , instead of feeling like we need to escape to another town or country to have some fun . <p> I urge you to use this time at home and think about your old habits when everything was up and running " normally . " You know ... a few months back . How much were you consuming ? How much were you traveling ? Now with being ordered to stay home , could you start to adapt fewer trips to the store ? Could you avoid shopping from big retailers and eating at chain restaurants ? Could you instead put your money towards the small local businesses and restaurants ? Which , in many cases , are doing more for their employees than the big corporations . Think about that . <p> I know countless small business owners doing everything they can do to make sure they provide for their employees , even taking personal pay cuts . Yet , big corporations were some of the first to do major layoffs . Saying that it was the only choice they could make , in order to save the company . Meanwhile , their top executives have more than enough money to go around . <p> It 's time that we stand in solidarity with our community for the long haul . Let 's try our hardest to support local small businesses whenever we can . Let 's do our part to slow down consumption , which will directly aid in the conservation of our environment . Let 's remember the companies that stood up for their employees , our essential workers , our community , the most vulnerable , and the environment during this time and support them now and onwards . <p> We must ask ourselves , what type of world do we want to live in after COVID-19 ? And what choices do we have to make to get us there ? Do we want to continue to live in a polluted environment where we ca n't see the mountains because of all the smog ? Or we ca n't swim in the ocean because of all the plastic waste ? Or do we want to see our environment continue to flourish like it is now ? We can do this . Remember , small incremental changes make a huge difference . <p> Marina M.
