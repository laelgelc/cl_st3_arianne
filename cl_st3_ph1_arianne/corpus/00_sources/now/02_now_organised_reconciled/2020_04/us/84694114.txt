If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <p> OPINION <h> Rethinking how close we really are <p> In ancient times we might have felt like we or " they " did something really wrong and this is how we are being punished for our transgressions . Today , as in past times , we have an enemy we can not see , and we are struggling to deal with it . <p> So we toss the issue to our updated oracles , our modern shamans , who are pillars of our age of reason -- scientists and doctors . <p> In a strange way , our virus-driven moment reminds me of when years ago I attended a witchcraft trial reenactment in Colonial Williamsburg , Virginia . As the audience " jury , " we were to determine her guilt under the laws of 1620 . One 1620 test was if she drowned while being forced under water , she was innocent . After our verdict was announced , the actors told us that audiences convicted her about 50% of the time -- as we did . <p> Perhaps an audience watching a dramatization of our 2020 moment hundreds of years from now , will also find our methods of addressing the demon virus primitive at best , and might even provide for them some humor incomprehensible to our current minds . <p> Let 's hope we survive at least that long . <p> With theories advanced over time concerning the dinosaur 's demise in mind , models predicting our demise are inherently flawed . Those long ago creatures now seem likely to have died from huge meteorites , not from the tar pits as we were once taught . Today we do n't know whether nuclear war , global climate change , or now a pandemic , or something now completely unknown , will do us in . <p> At this virus-moment we seem mostly convinced that our scientific modeling is the best test for optimum actions in the face of what is still a shadowy , seemingly unknown menace -- in 1620 parlance , a witch or her brew . As the age of witchcraft trials had its vested religious/political interests driving the prosecution , today we have massive vested political interests aligned one way or the other , stirring up this pre-election disinformation cauldron , ready to drown the other side -- hopefully not as literally as in the case of the poor 1620 woman . <p> Yet , despite our current cauldron , I sense at least two opportunities . <p> First , the immunological scientists and physicians who gave us the polio vaccine when I was young -- and thereafter the life-prolonging wonder drugs we take for granted -- have another heroic victory beckoning -- like the NASA scientists and astronauts victory in July of 1969 in the Sea of Tranquility . In the interim we can recognize the heroes in our midst , such as relatives of mine risking their lives working long hours as part of the team at the University of Washington Medical Center , not just saving lives today but advancing the data modeling invaluable for this crisis , and perhaps the next one . <p> Second , just as President Kennedy challenged us in the early 1960s to reach beyond ourselves by aiming at the moon , today we can reach beyond our pettiness and use our " time out " to learn some new ways -- perhaps starting with maintaining our six-foot distance as our typical greeting between those not yet close friends . <p> Hugging those you barely know seems to me to have become so common as to become nearly meaningless . Maybe we can learn from this moment to develop real , not artificial , closeness . We might start " real " closeness by listening to one another , and develop what I call empathetic conversations -- admittedly a goal of mine in most of my mediation work . <p> For example , let 's tackle a tough one -- the political conversation that rarely happens in a civil way between an ideologically convinced Democrat , and a Republican , equally convinced . While talking -- six feet apart -- try to offer to your ideologically different neighbor two ideological opposite comments . <p> For example , first say , " thanks to our science , and our social actions , the infection and death curves are beginning to flatten , and the sky is getting cleaner and the ocean water clearer . " <p> Second , also say , " we learned a bitter lesson from the 1930s that depression , suicide and violence comes from continued high unemployment , and too much government intervention begins a cycle of dependency robbing our self-esteem and initiative . " <p> Whatever the initial reaction , by advancing such conflicting thoughts you have at least planted the seed for a future empathetic discussion . Today if I were initiating such a conversation , I might risk a perception of bias by mixing in a comment about enjoying seeing the picture of a pride of lions sleeping on a formerly busy road in South Africa . <p> Whether we recognize the medical heroes in our midst or try to bridge political chasms with our neighbor , or both , our challenge is to do a much better job in our " time out " dealing with ignorance , fear , disease and poverty connected with Covid-19 than did our 1620 ancestors with their witches .
