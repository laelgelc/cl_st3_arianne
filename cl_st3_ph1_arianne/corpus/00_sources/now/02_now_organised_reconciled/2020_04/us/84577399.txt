<p> Following the 2008 recession , New York state required our electric and gas utility companies to develop austerity budgets and reduce their rates . Similarly , the state should take aggressive action now in response to the coronavirus pandemic to reduce rates and build towards a renewable energy future . <p> The economic impacts of the pandemic are already manifest . Millions of New Yorkers face immediate , devastating financial hardships . People and businesses have curtailed discretionary spending , delayed plans , and in many instances must reprioritize and make hard choices to meet even basic expenses . Our utilities should be required to do the same . <p> Utility rates are established in a complex process where infrastructure projects are proposed based on maintenance needs and estimated growth . The rates cover all the utility 's projected operating expenses - -- infrastructure projects , personnel expansions , pay raises and benefits - -- and guarantee a rate of return on investments and dividends to shareholders . <p> The economic impacts of the pandemic have turned the assumptions on which utility rates are based on their heads . Interest rates are lower , and estimated growth in customer base and energy use are no longer realistic ; indeed , there was a marked , sustained decrease in energy use following the 2008 recession . <p> The state should require utilities to update their rate plans based on today 's assumptions . Projects should be reprioritized , and where possible , costs should be deferred . Further , discretionary expenses such as pay raises , association membership fees , and dividends to shareholders should be curtailed . Utilities should not be guaranteed a profit while the rest of us suffer . <p> To be clear , utilities must be provided the money needed to ensure safe and reliable service . Further , there are ways the utilities can help with economic recovery by putting people to work on projects that provide clear customer benefits . Indeed , investing in energy efficiency and readying the grid for renewable energy can create jobs and reduce energy use while helping us avoid the next calamity -- climate change . The state should act now to re-evaluate utility energy expenditures to help customers save money while building toward the future . <p> Irene Weiser is a member of the Caroline Town Board and coordinator of Fossil Free Tompkins .
