<p> Emergency services are urging communities not to become complacent despite the state experiencing a brief reprieve from the hot and dry summer , with this time of year one that has historically seen some of the state 's worst fires . Earlier this week , the Country Fire Authority warned that despite the rain and cooler weather in recent days , the risk of fast-running grass and scrub fires still existed , with February a month in which many of the state 's worst fires have occurred . Related coverage : Fire season not over yet , warns Country Fire Authority Just over a year on from the Hepburn fire at Mannings Road , at which flames licked relatively close to the township , emergency services are calling on the Daylesford and Hepburn communities to remain vigilant and to ensure personal fire plans are in place . Earlier in the fire season , facing another hot and windy day , emergency services including the CFA , Victoria Police and Forest Fire Management Victoria gathered for a preparedness meeting , looking at what the plan would be in the instance of a fire , especially in the first hour before back-up resources arrive . Understanding that community concern and confusion about how an emergency situation would be managed in the shire existed , with residents asking how they could prepare for the potential of a disastrous event to unfold on their doorsteps , such as what has occurred in the east of the state and across Australia in recent months , emergency services decided to create an informative video . Related coverage : Buninyong-Mt Helen Fire Brigade urges community members to create detailed fire plans With the area deemed to be a high fire risk due to the townships being nestled amongst bushland , a perceived need to come out of the meeting was to further encourage the community to engage with authorities to help to protect themselves and to remain aware of the surrounding environment . Most of all , they want people living in all parts of the region , even if in the centre of a township , to have a fire plan . While back-up from surrounding fire brigades would be quick to turn out if a fire was to break out near the towns , it is possible a fire could impact quite quickly before other resources such as air support arrive . And if it was a particularly bad day with many fires burning across the state , resources could well stretched . Related coverage : Encouraging female leadership in the CFA Related coverage : They came within metres of disaster but still consider themselves lucky CFA District 15 Commander Luke Kneebone said that during the Hepburn fire in 2019 , conditions had worked in authorities ' favour : there were no other major fires burning and so authorities received all resources they asked for , including air support , and a wind change late in the day had pushed the fire away from the township . Despite this good result - with there not being any injuries or property losses as a result of the blaze - he said authorities did not want this to create a sense of complacency within the community . " We want people to understand that may not always be the case . There are lots of factors that can influence our response , including weather . If the weather is really bad , we may not be able to get aircraft up in the air and if there are other incidents , we may not be able to get as many resources as quickly , " he said . Watch the video here : Commander Kneebone said authorities wanted the community to remain vigilant so that their fire plans could be put in place , if need be . " We want people who live in the Daylesford and Hepburn area to stay alert , " he said . " So knowing the fire danger rating on a particular day , keeping an eye on the weather conditions and VicEmergency app and then applying all that information to your plan . " With its reputation as ' Spa Country ' meaning there are always large groups of tourists wandering around the town and its various attractions , authorities are also trying to engage tourism and accommodation providers , to try to spread the message to visitors . This includes leaving a fire-affected area if safe to do so , as dangerous curiosity was a problem for authorities at the 2019 fire , when a number of people travelled along small tracks to access back areas that had already been evacuated in order to watch the fire and aircraft from a higher vantage point . Related coverage : How authorities work behind the scenes on a high fire danger day Related coverage : Stickybeaks cause frustration for firefighters Sergeant Matt Flood from Daylesford Police Station is the shire 's Municipal Emergency Response Coordinator . A Municipal Emergency Management Plan is required for every local government area and encourages familiarisation and planning across agencies for a number of different incidents that may occur in a particular area - from flooding to fire , road trauma , missing people and even though not an incident , major events . Sergeant Flood said emergency services were working with the community in regards to fire preparedness but faced some challenges such as a lack of phone reception in certain areas and informing visitors to the area of the messaging . If an incident such as fire were to occur on a weekend , when traffic in the towns always increases , it would be a lot more problematic for authorities . The policing role at a fire is to keep the area safe for the control agency - either the CFA and FFMV - and to manage an evacuation process if need be , to door knock houses and to set up traffic management points and road blocks to ensure emergency vehicles can access the impacted area . Both Commander Kneebone and Sergeant Flood urged the community to ensure they have fire plans in place , and if it includes leaving early , to know the trigger point for leaving and not to wait for authorities to knock at the door . Planning where to travel to , such as to a family or friend 's house or to a bigger regional centre such as Ballarat until the fire risk has reduced should also be part of the plan . With both residents and tourists trying to escape the area if a fire were approaching , too many people on the roads in a panic would cause congestion around the townships , with people trying to flee as emergency services try to gain access to fight the fire . While Sergeant Flood acknowledged that there would always be people in a situation in which they would need to access relief centres , if most people have planned and monitored a situation , they should be out of the area . Relief centres - or places of last resort - are determined based on the situation and where the threat is , meaning emergency services are fluid and flexible in determining a location . The towns are too small to cater to thousands of people crowded in the streets or at a relief centre - or places of last resort - so both residents and tourists are encouraged to leave an area before the risk is heightened . Commander Kneebone said people needed to be aware of the risk , including that with dry conditions , it is possible fires could engulf a town . " Plans need to be individualised and that 's why we want people to engage with this , so that they understand the environment they live , work in or are travelling to and think about what the plan is for a number of different possible scenarios , " he said . " You need to be engaged , have a plan , a back up plan and to practice that plan . " Another part of fire , though insidious but which carries a big risk , is heat health . Sergeant Flood said the community could always improve in the ways in which it looks after each other . " More people died as a result of the extremely high temperatures leading up to and during the Black Saturday event , " he said . " It is something , particularly with an older community , that people need to be mindful of looking after older neighbours , friends and family . " With climate change , it is something that will become more prevalent - it will be hot for days at a time . " Stay up to date with Daylesford police on the Moorabool Eyewatch page .
