<h> How we 're going to pay for our changed world , once coronavirus is gone <p> There is a lot more to this time of massive change than the immediate crisis of fighting a pandemic . <p> What many , perhaps most , have n't understood yet is that our economy and society are being upended . <p> " In a week we have shifted from a globalised liberal economy and free society to a command economy with closed borders , regimented movement and a welfare state , " Reform Club chairman and former NSW Treasury secretary Percy Allan recently wrote to friends and colleagues . <p> " And through necessity , all sides of politics support it . <p> " When COVID-19 is over will people demand stronger government ( to cope with crises ) or pine for laissez-faire ( to allow free will ) ? That 's an important debate . " <p> Talk to people who lived through the last world war ( or be someone who had talked to them when more were still around ) and the changes to war-time footing were much more gradual , leisurely even , by comparison . <p> But some of these big changes do need immediate understanding . One of them is : How we pay for it . <p> The Australian Financial Review on Tuesday ran a column by its political editor headlined " The huge stimulus numbers throw up the inevitable question " . That question was " How do we pay for it ? " <p> As it turns out , it 's the government 's intention to pay for the couple of hundred billion dollars of quick extra debt the old-fashioned way : The government will be borrowing the money . <p> There are possible alternatives , extreme unconventional monetary policy whereby the government could issue zero-coupon bonds directly to the Reserve Bank to print money . <p> Instead , the Australian Office of Financial Management will issue bonds to the required value and the market will buy them , boosting our government debt total . <p> In theory , such a sudden flood of extra raising might put some upward pressure on interest rates and , if foreigners fancy our bonds , the Australian dollar . <p> But these are not times of textbook theory , particularly with the QE twist of the Reserve Bank officially targeting a three-year bond rate of 0.25 per cent and buying about $3 billion worth of bonds a day to achieve that target . <p> The RBA has even posted the three-year target on its home page , just to underline the commitment . <p> Given that Monday 's effort was only Stimulus 3.0 , with 4.0 and maybe 5.0 yet to come , the AOFM 's current $558 billion of treasury bonds on issue is likely to end up close to $1 trillion . <p> Remember a decade ago when government debt was a demon figure used to scare the kiddies ? <p> Or June 30 , 2013 -- the year the government changed -- when gross government debt was $257 billion ? <p> It was a nonsense , of course . <p> It was low government debt and affordable , but a decade of political opportunism painted it as evil . <p> The reality of our current debt explosion is that it is quite affordable if it pays for a return to anything like ' normal ' growth . <p> Like the price of reducing climate change , it is cheaper than the alternative . <p> If the interest rate on the bonds is below economic growth , the growth takes care of the government 's ability to service the debt . <p> It would require a mighty bleak world and particularly inept management for Australia 's growth to get stuck below a fraction of a percentage point . <p> So one of the first changes of our post-COVID-19 political world should be a recalibration of political rhetoric . <p> It would be too much to ask for the Prime Minister to come clean with the population , fess up to the decade of debt demonisation being largely political BS , like the attack on pricing carbon and a more rational taxing of resources rent . <p> The debt will have to be dealt with when times suit . <p> It could be used as a force for good , as a cattle prod ( or excuse ) for genuine , overdue holistic tax reform that would help create a healthier economy and society . <p> It would need to be reform that would offend just about every vested interest -- broadening the GST , at least capping the franking credit rebates , an inheritance tax , no-exceptions land tax , a carbon price , lower income tax rates , removing the tax-free status of superannuation in the pension phase , et al <p> " Never waste a good crisis . " <p> Or it could be used to damage the country with the sort of austerity drive and reduction in government services beloved by the hard right -- the small government , low-taxing neoliberal myrmidons exemplified by the Institute of Public Affairs and its alumnae and fellow travellers populating the governments ' ranks . <p> There is a very important debate ahead about our post-COVID-19 nation . <p> In the meantime , be grateful the ideologically straitjacketed did n't hold sway last month or many more of us would be in dire poverty -- or dead . <p> Search real estate for sale or rent anywhere in Australia from licensed estate agents on simply type in any suburb in the search bar below
