<h> It 's OK to finding silver linings in the COVID crisis <p> " Even in our grimmest moments , " said the wonderful Jess Hill when accepting the Stella Prize this week , " we can hold despair in one hand , and hope in the other . " Jess has documented many grim moments in her superb , rightfully lauded book , See What You Made Me Do , examining the disturbing prevalence of domestic abuse in this country . <p> Illustration : Simon LetchCredit : <p> I was struck by this simple truth when she said it . We know we can see beauty in times of ugliness , dream of motion in times of stasis and hope in times of despair . Even though , to be honest , I have wrestled with the fact that many of us raced so quickly , almost instantly , to find " silver linings " during a time of global turmoil , brutal suffering and intolerable pain . I get it , we need it , but #blessed talk sticks in my craw . Much of the " look on the bright side talk " , while well meaning , can smack of privilege ; perhaps I saw one too many trays of hot cross buns , scored sourdough rounds ( and , again , it 's not the fact of this but the volume ) , or videos of celebrities telling us of the pain of being confined in well stocked palaces . When so many are losing people they love , jobs , businesses , apparent futures , it has seemed a little premature . <p> One thing we have seen in the past few weeks is how this pandemic and shutdown has led to an instant , raw exposure of disadvantage ( especially due to race and poverty ) , and how the most vulnerable will suffer the most : the casually employed , the poor , the homeless ( sleeping in painted boxes in parking lots ) , the victims of domestic abuse . We toss and turn with reason : until we look after those in most need , we ca n't sit on our haunches . This pandemic has shot ink into the veins of the deep inequity in our society , making them starker , more obvious , and urgent . <p> Advertisement <p> Loading <p> But then I realised two things . First , the craving for light is innately human and will enable all of us to survive this whole thing , as it has in ages past . That the full daftness , kindness , irrepressible creative energy , laughter and joy of people of all stripes is irrepressible . That delight comforts . That paying attention to the world , to the beauty in it , and to each other is crucial . We need our bards , and poets , our singers and dancers and dreamers and the chortles of children . <p> And second , that perhaps the best , truest silver lining will be if we are forced to focus on the suffering and struggles of other people , and recognise that with sufficient will , we can do a whole lot more to alleviate it . <p> Like the speed with which we hopped into action to find -- yes temporary but still -- housing for the homeless . To raise Newstart , which has not been increased since 1994 . In Australia , apparently thanks to Greg Combet , Christian Porter and Sally McManus , we reached a kind of accord between unions , the government and employers evident in JobKeeper and Job Seeker that we have not seen for decades -- and which took a matter of days . Days ! The bipartisanship has been so refreshing . <p> Some other surprising and deliriously welcome outcomes are also evident . Have you noticed the movement of experts to the forefront of public debate ? Because we actually need them and not the views of ideologically driven pundits ? Because our lives depend on it . Scientists have been scoffed at and dismissed for so long for their work on climate change that it has been excellent to see them take centre stage . I 've never heard the word epidemiologist uttered so much on the airwaves . No one is going to turn to an anti-vaxxer for some kind of false balance . The views of the ignorant have been cast aside for those of the knowledgeable . <p> Loading <p> Then there 's the historically underpaid and undervalued health workers who are saving our lives by risking their own : each night , hundreds of thousands of people have leaned out of windows applauding them . ( Seriously can we pay nurses more please ? Teachers too ? Have n't we seen how much we need them ? ) <p> Nor are we asking celebrities for their opinion on much , partly because -- well , who cares -- but also because so many of their utterances , from plush compounds with pools and thick green grass -- have been tone deaf . Amanda Hess even went so far as to declare in The New York Times that the " cult of celebrity " had been dismantled by this coronavirus . I ca n't remember the last time I clapped eyes on the backside of a Kardashian in my social media feed , for example , and I think I 'm ok . Hess writes : " Celebrities have a captive audience of traumatised people who are glued to the internet , eyes darting towards trending topics for clues to processing the unimaginable horrors looming just outside , and instead are finding Madonna bathing in a rose petal-strewn bath . " <p> A deserted Gran Canal in Venice in early April . The waters of the city are now clear , as boats no longer churn up the mud.Credit:AP <p> Instead of celebrities , entertainers have emerged to delight us , on their violins and cellos , singing from balconies and couches , in harmony with a thousand strangers , as well as just normal , funny people hooting when a marble makes its way through elaborate corridor tunnels . When a ping pong ball bounces off several saucepans and lands on a cup after multiple attempts , we all cheer . <p> There is also reason to hope the earth might have a little time to heal while planes and entire populations are grounded , when we are consuming and discarding less . The Himalayas are emerging from clouds of pollution and there are patches of blue sky above parts of China for the first time in many years . Fish are swimming in clear canals in Venice . Will we return to the climate change debate with a new sense of what might be possible ? <p> In Australia , after the rains , rivers are flowing again and , delightfully , kaleidoscopes of butterflies are swarming the coast . <p> It 's a cliche is n't it : we are all in this together . But we are . We need other countries to work together to stamp this out , or we will all be hammered . We need people to wash hands , to respect distance , to be careful . And we need each other , the comfort and wisdom and humour and company and resilience of each other , to get by . And we need to allow ourselves to delight in the silver linings . <p> Jess finished her speech by saying that in this time hope for her means that , " while everything is cancelled , maybe some of us will start to see the world -- and ourselves -- with a greater sense of clarity . Maybe this clarity will renew our determination to fight for something better than the status quo. "
