<p> AS a new decade dawned , Bendigo was a city gearing up for change . Changes driven both by our community , and the forces beyond it . Here are some we might reasonably expect in the year and decade ahead , given the efforts involved in driving them : One of the biggest Bendigo council decisions of 2019 was opting into the state government 's plan to build a $90-million GovHub in the city 's CBD . Presented with a number of options , the council decided the best way forward was to become a tenant in a 1000-desk building to be built on the site of the existing Lyttleton Terrace offices . Some city staff have already moved out of the main council building , with others to follow this month . Expect news on the sale of the land , the demolition of the existing buildings and the start of construction this year . The GovHub wo n't be the only major construction project in the CBD , with a number of others planned for the decade ahead . They include the new Bendigo Law Courts , as well as the Bendigo TAFE revitalisation . Designs for the new courts , at the corner of Mundy and Hargreaves streets , are expected to be released this year . The Bendigo TAFE city campus revitalisation - consisting of two new buildings and refurbishment of heritage buildings - is supposed to be finished by 2021 . More stories : We welcomed Dai Gum Loong into our midst in 2019 . And what an Easter we had , as all of Bendigo 's processional dragons paraded through the streets . It was the highlight of a packed Easter festival program . The 2019 event was scarcely over as organisers turned their thoughts to the 150th Bendigo Easter Fair in 2020 . This year 's fair is expected to boast the first full four-day schedule in 10 years . The program will feature new attractions , as well as traditional favourites . The Easter fair is n't the only big event planned , with White Night expected to return to Bendigo in 2020 . The city hosted its first White Night in September 2018 . When the Royal Commission into Mental Health handed down its interim report , it recommended urgent action . Actions it said could n't wait until the final report was released in 2020 . So we 'd expect to see some changes in the 12 months ahead , with the promise of more after the full extent of the commission 's findings and recommendations are handed down in October . Central Victorian individuals , organisations and groups were among the thousands that made submissions to the Royal Commission , highlighting everything from a lack of funding for services to a lack of supports for carers . The commission staged its sole rural hearing in Maryborough , with farmers , families , experts and practitioners among those who provided evidence . More stories : These are some of the biggest ongoing issues affecting our community . A horror year on Victorian roads has been felt close to home , with a number of fatal and serious crashes in our region . There have been calls to improve road safety , with people suggesting changes to speed limits and improved infrastructure in a bid to reduce road trauma . The effects of serious and fatal crashes have been felt long after they occurred , and will continue to be . More stories : Stories about our changing climate have been generating discussion for the past year , but the devastating fires raging across our nation have amplified community interest in environmental issues . Young people from our region have been rallying for those in power to minimise the effects of climate change . More stories : Young people have also been taking the lead in conversations about jobs , or a lack thereof . They 're far from the only demographic concerned about employment , but they 've been in the spotlight as city leaders seek to strengthen the economy . Boosting year 12 or equivalent attainment has been identified as key to driving positive outcomes . More stories : People are also struggling to find affordable , appropriate and accessible housing . The Bendigo rental market has been so tight that vulnerable households have been forced into housing stress , and sometimes homelessness . Groups of the region 's residents have been banding together to find solutions to specific housing needs , resulting in projects like the Dunolly District Hospital Auxiliary 's Independent Living Units and Mount Alexander Shire Accommodation and Respite Group 's A Home of My Own . Last year brought hope of a breakthrough for Dunolly 's Independent Living Units . Meanwhile , MASARG sought to progress A Home of My Own . More stories : The response to a former councillor 's concerns about the use of ' ring card girls ' at a high-profile , council-supported boxing event in Bendigo in 2019 exposed just how much progress we , as a community , have yet to make on gender equality . It came at a time when community leaders were drafting a gender equality strategy - a process set to continue this year . Our community already has a gender equity leadership statement - a piece of work launched about the time of International Women 's Day , outlining how 18 organisations that impact almost all Bendigo residents in one way or another ought to work together to achieve gender equality . But much of the discussion generated by the presence of ' ring card girls ' at a boxing event in Bendigo highlighted gaps in people 's knowledge . They included recognition of gender inequality and its consequences . Those gaps in our collective knowledge will be challenged , because while gender inequality flourishes , so too does violence against women . Our courts have been full of stories about family , domestic and sexual violence . And we have n't been alone , with almost 60 women nationwide believed to have had their lives ended by violence in 2019 . More news : How would you like to see our region change in the 2020s ?
