<h> Coronavirus : Air pollution in major cities has dropped by up to 60 per cent during lockdowns <p> Lockdowns restricting travel and industry imposed to halt the spread of coronavirus have resulted in unprecedented reductions in deadly air pollution around the world , new analysis shows . <p> Major cities that suffer from the world 's worst air pollution have seen reductions of deadly particulate matter by up to 60 per cent from the previous year , during a three-week lockdowns period . <p> Researchers from IQAir - a global air quality information and tech company - studied 10 major cities around the world which have relatively high numbers of coronavirus cases and COVID-19 lockdown measures . <p> A woman enjoys the sun on her balcony as the Eiffel Tower is clearly seen in the background during the nationwide confinement to counter the conoravirus in Saint-Cloud , west of Paris . ( AP Photo/Michel Euler ) ( AP/AAP ) <p> The study compared levels of harmful microscopic particulate matter known as PM 2.5 . <p> The pollutant , which is smaller than 2.5 micrometers in diameter , is considered particularly dangerous as it can lodge deep into the lungs and pass into other organs and the bloodstream , causing serious health risks . <p> Seven out of the 10 cities studied , including New Delhi , Seoul , Wuhan and Mumbai , saw significant improvements in air quality . Those with historically higher levels of PM 2.5 pollution witnessed the most substantial drops in pollution . <p> Analysts chose the three-week timeframe to reflect either the period with the strictest lockdowns , or - during longer lockdown periods such as in Wuhan - to coincide with the peak number of daily reported coronavirus cases . <p> The Himachal Pradesh Cricket Association and the distant hills are clearly visible in the clean air due to a statewide shutdown to prevent coronavirus infection in Dharmsala , India . ( AP Photo/Ashwini Bhatia ) ( AP/AAP ) <p> The report was released to coincide with the 50th anniversary of Earth Day , which this year is focused on climate action . <p> Air pollution is already a global public health crisis , as it kills seven million people each year , according to the World Health Organisation . <p> Reducing global heat-trapping emissions is the best way to clean up our skies and prevent avoidable pollution-related deaths , scientists say . <p> The report 's authors say that while they do expect air pollution to rise again when economies restart after coronavirus , " out of these extraordinary circumstances , we can see how changes in our society 's activities can have a momentous impact on our environment and the air we breathe , " said IQAir 's marketing specialist Kelsey Duska . <p> Snowcapped Dhauladhar range of the Himalaya are clearly visible during lockdown to control the spread of the coronavirus in Dharmsala , India . ( AP Photo/Ashwini Bhatia ) ( AP/AAP ) <h> Cleaner air around the world <p> The Indian capital New Delhi -- which frequently tops the world 's most polluted city lists - saw a 60 per cent reduction in PM 2.5 levels from March 23 to April 13 from the same period in 2019 . <p> Both New Delhi and the country 's commercial center Mumbai experienced their best March air quality on record in 2020 . <p> During the initial three-week lockdown period , the number of hours rated as " unhealthy " in New Delhi dropped from 68 per cent in 2019 to 17 per cent in 2020 . <p> On March 25 , India placed its entire 1.3 billion population into lockdown , closing factories , markets , shops , places of worship and suspending most public transport services . The world 's largest lockdown was then extended to May 3 . <p> Global airlines have partially or completely grounded their flights during the coronavirus pandemic in a bid to cut costs and to reduce the transmission of COVID-19 across nations . ( AP/AAP ) <p> India is one of the world 's most-polluted countries and an average resident is exposed to air pollution that exceeds the World Health Organisation 's target for annual PM 2.5 exposure by more than 500 per cent . <p> Meanwhile , the South Korean capital Seoul saw a 54 per cent drop in PM 2.5 levels from February 26 to March 18 from the previous year . <p> South Korea 's air quality ranks among the worst of Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development ( OECD ) countries , with some of the highest levels of particulate matter pollution . Last year in March , the government declared air pollution a " social disaster . " <p> In February , South Korea had one of the largest coronavirus outbreaks outside mainland China but aggressive testing and contact tracing methods have brought cases there under control . <p> A woman crosses an avenue linking southern suburbs of Athens to the centre of the capital , in the Argyroupolis suburb . ( AP/AAP ) <p> And the Chinese city of Wuhan , where the deadly virus was first identified , saw a 44 per cent reduction in air pollution levels from February 26 to March 18 from the same period last year . <p> The city of 11 million people in central China 's Hubei province was the first city to impose a complete shutdown after Chinese authorities struggled to contain the spread of the coronavirus - an unprecedented move at the time . <p> After 75 days those restrictions began to be lifted on April 8 - a milestone in China 's fight against COVID-19 as the country reported nearly zero new local infections . <p> Over the course of Wuhan 's 10-week lockdown , the city experienced its cleanest air quality on record for the months of February and March . <p> A lone commuter waits at a bus stop in an otherwise deserted Regent Street in London . ( AP/AAP ) <p> The average concentration of PM 2.5 plunged from 63.2 and 43.9 micrograms per cubic meter in February and March 2019 respectively , to 36.8 and 32.9 in the same months this year . <p> The World Health Organisation considers anything above 25 to be unsafe . <p> Elsewhere , other major cities experienced cleaner air . Los Angeles saw its longest stretch of clean air on record , over 18 days from March 7 to 28 . <p> PM 2.5 concentration levels were down by 31 per cent from the same time last year , and down 51 per cent from the average of the previous four years . <p> And in Europe , London , Madrid and Rome all experienced reductions in their PM2.5 compared to 2019 during their lockdown periods . <h> Calls to pursue green deals <p> While suddenly closing all factories and banning cars from roads is not a sustainable solution tackle climate change , the IQAir researchers said there are ways to preserve healthier air conditions . <p> These include supporting green deals in government stimulus packages , shifting towards sustainable sources of energy for power generation , limiting individual 's purchases to primarily essential goods , opting for cleaner modes of transportation - including walking and cycling - and encouraging a shared economy of goods , according to Duska . <p> " In our recovery from the pandemic , it 's important that we strive to preserve the cleaner environment , which protects our health from another invisible killer , air pollution , " she said . <p> " We hope that the urgency in tackling this global pandemic may be matched in addressing air pollution .
