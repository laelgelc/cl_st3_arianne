<p> Time 's up for fast fashion , with environmental experts calling for a radical makeover of the fashion industry , which accounts for 10 per cent of global pollution . <p> The fast fashion model -- which relies on cheap manufacturing , frequent consumption , and short-lived garment use , is fuelling environmental destruction and must be discarded , argues Kirsi Niinim ? ki , a professor of design at Finland 's Aalto University , in the journal Nature Reviews Earth &amp; Environment . <p> The fashion industry , the second-largest industrial polluter after aviation , must improve the long-term sustainability of its supply chain , Professor Niinim ? ki and colleagues wrote . <p> It 's crimes against a greener planet include environmental harm , with negative impacts along the entire supply chain from production to consumption , water use , chemical pollution , textile waste , and carbon emissions . <p> Fashion produces more than 92 million tonnes of waste and consumes 1.5 trillion tonnes of water per year , with developing countries often bearing the burden . <p> These impacts highlight the need for substantial changes in the industry , including deceleration of manufacturing and introduction of sustainable practices throughout the supply chain , the authors argue . <p> This environmental devastation continues despite growing awareness of climate change and increasing demand for sustainably produced garments , with fast fashion a major culprit . <p> " Slow fashion is the future " , Professor Niinim ? ki and colleagues wrote . <p> " We need a new system-wide understanding of how to transition towards this model , requiring creativity and collaboration between designers and manufacturers , various stakeholders , and end consumers . " <p> Consumers have a key role to play , the authors say , and must alter their consumption habits and take a hip-pocket hit by paying higher prices for ethical and sustainable products . <p> The solution is for consumers to return their old clothes directly to big fashion chains -- rather than inundating op-shops and charity stores with clothing nobody wants -- University of Technology Sydney fashion and textiles researcher Dr Mark Liu wrote . <p> Some fashion chains such as H&amp;M , Uniqlo and Zara have already introduced collection boxes for old clothes in their stores , with H&amp;M promising to donate three cents for every kilogram of returned clothing to recycling research .
