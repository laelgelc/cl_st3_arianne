<p> We do n't get much time for philosophy and contemplation in the daily grind of our busy lives . Too often we are assaulted by bite size chunks of social media , fake news , rumours and spin ; every day , every hour . Coronavirus : All the latest updates on COVID-19 for Tasmania Suffice to say staying at home for the next month or longer presents opportunities for many of us to read more widely , gaze up at the big picture . This is n't an opportunity we should waste . This is an opportunity to consider life after coronavirus , what it will look like and how we can emerge from this pandemic stronger and safer , in a fairer , more tolerant and kinder society . A column by Crikey 's Political Editor , Bernard Keane , really got me thinking in this regard last week . Keane pointed out that young people have sacrificed much to help keep mostly older Australians safe , to help avert the biggest crisis many elderly Australians have faced in their lifetimes . A recent report from The Grattan Institute also estimates younger workers to be the hardest hit . When this crisis is over , baby boomers will owe a significant debt of gratitude to our younger generations . This debt of gratitude can be repaid by joining younger generations in the fight against the biggest crises of their time - tackling rising intergenerational inequity and the dangers of a warming planet in our climate emergency . While many older Australians are core fighters in Green , conservation and climate movements , the truth is that their generation consistently ranks highest for climate change denialism . This influences the way many boomers vote , and this does undermine real political action on tackling the climate crisis facing our youth and future generations . While this crisis has impacted everyone , and no one is safe , the most vulnerable to the health impacts of coronavirus in our community are statistically the elderly , or baby boomers . It could be argued that those most heavily impacted socially and economically are however , the nation 's young , our millennials . This theme has been covered in several Crikey articles recently , including outlining the massive unemployment and displacement caused to millennials by entirely necessary health restrictions and directions . Many of them are now unemployed , having little to no hope of ever owning a home , looking down the barrel of years of deep recession . Even before the coronavirus pandemic many only had insecure work , working more than one casual job to pay rent , study or survive . Infuriatingly , although The Greens tried to amend the legislation in the Senate earlier this month , many are also missing out on the JobKeeper payments announced in the recent stimulus package . Most young Australians and Tasmanians have heeded the call to follow restrictions , stay at home and do the right thing . Quite simply , they have put it all on the line to protect the most vulnerable in our communities from arguably the biggest crisis faced in their long and productive lives . And so they should . I hope this acute sacrifice by younger generations is acknowledged by our broader communities , including the elderly , and that all generations now come together and join in a solidarity pact to fight and work together to avoid the other great crisis of our time , indeed a crisis that threatens all future generations and life on this planet : climate change . We need to keep the conversation about climate change and rising intergenerational inequality going in the months and years to come . <p> Suffice to say staying at home for the next month or longer presents opportunities for many of us to read more widely , gaze up at the big picture .
