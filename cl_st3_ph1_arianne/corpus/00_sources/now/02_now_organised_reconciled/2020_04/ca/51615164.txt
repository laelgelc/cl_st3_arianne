<p> Saying things like " these are extraordinary times " is increasingly sounding like tired cliches , but every day new policy ideas emerge that truly highlight the unprecedented nature of the coronavirus crisis . <p> CIBC World Markets Inc. ' s economist Benjamin Tal has floated the intriguing idea of the federal government helping many businesses pay their rent -- which is often among the largest expenses for most small enterprises . <p> Tal says one way to alleviate some pressure on small to medium enterprises is to adopt a version of the proposal made by the Federation of Rental-Housing Providers of Ontario . <p> " That proposal aimed to provide a solution to rent issues in the residential market , but it could be adapted to fit the commercial space , " Tal said in a note Wednesday . " The main idea of such a program is to offer a time-limited , interest-free loan to help tenants with rent payments , with some option of debt forgiveness down the road . The approach should be a " pay what you can " system with the shortfall backstopped by the government . " <p> Once a tenant can certify that they are unable to make rent payment or a part of it , the government could step in and pay the unpaid portion . Over time , the government could recover the payments directly from the tenant as the outlook improves . <p> " That cost-sharing approach towards rent payment would go a long way to ease the financial pressure facing many commercial tenants . It would reduce demand for the CEBA program , and might be even financed , in part , by any government savings on that program , " Tal wrote . <p> Speaking of unconventional methods , the Bank of Canada may also announce some unorthodox policy measures today to fight the crisis . Scotiabank thinks the bank could buy longer-dated provincial bonds as it releases its latest interest rate decision and monetary policy report this morning . <p> " Targeting provies may carry knock-on benefits to relative spreads for strained municipalities , agencies and large corporates through portfolio effects , " said Derek Holt , head of capital markets economics at Scotiabank . " Failure to address weakened provincial bond funding conditions and liquidity constraints could induce forced selling in bond portfolios with knock-on effects on other parts of the market .
