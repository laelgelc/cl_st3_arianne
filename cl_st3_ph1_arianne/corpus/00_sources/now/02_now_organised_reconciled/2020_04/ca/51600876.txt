<p> Despite the ongoing " chaos and tragedy " that the ongoing pandemic has caused across Canada and abroad , Rebel Media commander Ezra Levantwas able to come up with a working list of " seven things " that he thinks " are better now than two months ago , " starting with an issue that has been front and centre in the upstart right-of-centre outlet 's coverage for the last few years : <p> " We 're finally enforcing our borders . " <p> He 's quick to offer an overarching caveat : Prime Minister Justin Trudeau has , he notes , " loosened the qualifications to be a temporary foreign worker in Canada , " while " nearly every day , " there are planes landing regularly in Vancouver and Toronto directly from China . But Levant suggests that we " not quibble , " as the border is " 90 per cent more impermeable than it was two months ago , including at Roxham Road " -- and " more important , now Canadians know we can do it . " <p> He 's also pleased to see countries " act in their own interests , " including in supplying crucial products , like face masks and medicines , which he sees as a critical concern given the growing global dependence on China as a source of bottom-dollar manufactured goods . <p> " Maybe this ' Buy American , buy Canadian ' attitude will finally take root in everything ; maybe you 'll spend an extra 5 per cent on a cell phone , but it 's not spying on you , like Huawei does , " he suggests . <p> It 's worth noting that elsewhere on the site , Levant delivers a stirring defence of of U.S. President Donald Trump over what he describes as " fake news " alleging that the White House had " ordered 3M to simply stop sending masks to Canada , and to tear up their contracts with Canadian hospitals , " which , he contended , is " just not true . " <p> Also on the list : " Respecting the work of people we actually rely on , from truck drivers to farmers " and " realizing that many institutions we value are simply coasting on an old reputation , and did n't respond well in the crisis " -- as well as confirming that " crises are n't fun , even if Al Gore , David Suzuki and Greta Thunberg tell us to have one . " <p> On that last point , Alberta correspondent Sheila Gunn Reidpoints out that there 's one other bit of coronavirus-related good news : The crisis has forced the United Nations to postpone its annual climate change conference , which was set to take place in Glasgow this fall . <p> " These people have been squawking about a climate emergency , but the truth is , a real emergency is upon us and now , and only now , the emergency is real enough to make elitist environmentalists change their ways and stop flying , " she notes . <p> " Co2 is n't the deadly concern they said it was . Coronavirus is and cancelling the conference will do more to combat them than actually holding the conference in the first place when all the control freaks and elitists cancel their first class flights to Glasgow . " <p> Even so , she warns that " these ghouls " -- a reference to either the environmental movement or the United Nations , it 's not clear -- " wo n't ever let a crisis go to waste , " as " they see the coronavirus deaths and a means to rewrite the economy to meet their centralized control socialist ends . " <p> She 's also not letting the pandemic stop her from keeping tabs on " Canada 's first family " -- and specifically , the $1,200 tab for " gourmet in-flight food during " during the prime minister 's " extended family vacation in Costa Rica last Christmas , " according to a response to a written question from Conservative MP John Brassard . <p> She also has an update on " Alberta hero " Chase Chomey , who , she notes , was one of " the ' severely normal ' Albertan men who cleared one track off " during the Coastal GasLink rail blockades . <p> " We loved the story so much that we brought a case to beer to each of the men we could identify , " Gunn Reid recalls . <p> " But Maclean 's magazine 's reaction was to publish a defamatory article along with a photograph clearly showing blockade buster Chase Chomey , saying that the group of men were white-supremacist vigilantes . " <p> That article , she reports , is now the subject of a " $50,000 lawsuit against Maclean 's " that the Rebel team has filed " on Chase 's behalf , " with an open invitation to Rebel supporters to " help us be an ' equalizer ' " by contributing to the crowdsourced legal fund . <p> Back in Toronto , Rebel mission specialist David Menzies begins his latest report from the streets of Toronto by highlighting the plight of homeless Canadians amid the COVID-19 crisis -- surely , he suggests , they " have it the worst , be it maintaining social distancing or practicing basic hygiene or just finding a warm place to sleep . " He then shifts his focus to what he sees as the real problem : " refugee applicants and asylum seekers " who , he avers , are currently occupying -- at federal expense -- hundreds of vacant hotel rooms that could be used to temporarily shelter " domestic homeless . " <p> Finally , the campaign to " keep Rebel News strong " during the pandemic has , we 're advised , been a rousing success -- they 've " already raised their initial goal " to $40,000 " to keep David , Keean , Sheila , and our whole team going full-tilt " in their efforts to " hold Trudeau to account during this crisis , " with the reported haul standing at just over $6,000 on Wednesday morning . <p> Elsewhere on the small-c conservative online media circuit , True North News raises an eyebrow over how Ottawa plans to spend over $150 million in COVID-19-related foreign aid , which " includes funding for abortions overseas , " as well as " financial assistance " for the United Nations Relief and Workers Agency , which it describes as " an organization with connections to Hamas .
