<h> Electricity wo n't power new fleet of buses chugging down HRM streets <p> A Halifax Transit bus stops at the bridge terminal in Dartmouth in November . - Ryan Taplin <p> Dozens of new transit buses will be hitting Halifax Regional Municipality streets and roads over the next three years but none of them will be powered by electricity . <p> During a virtual meeting Thursday , Halifax regional council approved the purchase of up to 150 new diesel-fueled buses for Halifax Transit over a three-year period from Nova Bus Ltd. , with an option to renew for up to 40 additional buses over two additional one-year periods . <p> " This year 's budget did include the project manager for fleet transformation , whose first task will be to get going in earnest on the electric bus projects and our plan at this point is that we should be seeing the first of the electric buses hit the road in about a three-year timeframe , " Dave Reage , director of Halifax Transit , told council in response to a question from Coun. Lorelei Nicoll ( Cole Harbour-Westphal ) . <p> " In the meantime , with this RFP ( request for proposal ) , we do have a requirement to keep purchasing diesel buses in order to manage the fleet and maintain a ... degree of repair on the fleet . " <p> Reage said Halifax Transit has flexibility built into the order for buses in that it can buy up to 150 buses over the allotted period but is not obligated to purchase any minimum number . Halifax Transit 's target is to replace buses after 14 years of service . Nova , one of two bidders on the bus contract , will deliver the new diesel buses at a cost of $650,578 per bus for the first three contract years . <p> " The buses we 're buying now are replacing buses that do n't have the same emission controls that newer diesel buses have , " Reage said . " Compared to the buses these are replacing , there will be a reduction in particulate emissions and we expect to see a savings of about 15 per cent in terms of fuel . " <p> Not good enough , says Peggy Cameron , a member of a Halifax citizen advocacy group who maintains that buying diesel buses is committing HRM to more pollution , particulate matter and smog . <p> " Is HRM really set to order 150 diesel buses over the next three years at the same time it has turned down money for beginning a pilot of electric buses , " Cameron wrote in a Thursday morning communication to Mayor Mike Savage and council . " The decision to purchase diesel buses is a capital expenditure that citizens will have to ride for the coming 15 to 20 years . " <p> The upfront cost of an electric bus is about twice that of a conventional diesel bus , but Cameron and many others have pointed out that the lower maintenance and fuel savings associated with an electric bus would save the municipality money in the long run . <p> The environmental advantages of electric buses make the transition a no-brainer , Cameron argues . <p> " Electric buses are zero emissions -- they reduce air pollution and climate change and the effect of these on human health , " Cameron said . " Diesel is a known carcinogen . A recent study recommends that people and bike routes should be no closer than 150 metres to major transportation streets and roads because of associated air pollution and its effect on human health . Diesel was reported as the worst offender . " <p> Reage told council that buying electric buses would be the easy part . <p> " What is more challenging and takes a lot more time is the background infrastructure , the charging infrastructure , the need to expand garages ... to retrofit the technology needed to charge the buses adequately , " Reage said . <p> " That 's the biggest time constraint that we would have in terms of moving to electric buses . We have to extend the infrastructure we have for these diesel buses for an additional 14 or 15 or 20 years and that is going to have a cost . The additional cost is the social and the environmental cost . " <p> Zurawski wanted to know what it would cost to retrofit current buses to make them less environmentally unfriendly instead of replacing them and using the savings to rush through more electric buses . <p> " There is no such thing as clean diesel , " Zurawski said . " Most studies would agree with me on that . " <p> Reage told council that the decrease of particulate matter is a bonus that come with replacing buses that are past their economic lifespan . " But what we do is look at condition assessments , look at buses , how much they are costing us to repair , " Reage said . " As things age , they become less reliable and more costly to repair . " <p> Reage said older transit buses have steel frames and are susceptible to salt corrosion and become more expensive to replace as time goes on . <p> " I am fully confident that the buses that will be replaced under this order , it is the best decision to replace them . Retrofitting them would n't really solve the overall economic lifespan of the vehicle . " <p> Coun. Matt Whitman ( Hammonds Plains-St . Margarets ) wondered if committing to a major bus expenditure in the uncertain financial climate driven by the COVID-19 crisis is prudent . Jane Fraser , chief financial officer of the municipality , said " the key is that the funds are expected when we receive delivery of the buses and what we have here is a fairly long delivery time , between 12 to 18 months so we will have flexibility on the budget to meet the RFP ... when we actually have to spend the cash . " <p> Zurawski and Whitman cast the only dissenting votes against the " up to 150 buses over a three-year period with option to renew " portion of the motion . Coun. Paul Russell ( Lower Sackville ) joined Zurawski and Whitman in voting against the third part of the motion , which authorizes the purchase of up to a total of 87 12.2-metre buses in years two and three of the agreement .
