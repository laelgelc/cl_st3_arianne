<h> Phil Gibson <p> The Harper government 's defence and security policy is adrift -- if not completely defenceless . Two different conferences late last week put the military front and center . A close look at both events drives home the aimless nature of government defence policy . <p> Defence and security are ( with a couple of exceptions ) abstractions far removed from the priorities of most Canadians . Although support for the armed forces has been key to the Conservatives ' hold on their political base , it is n't likely to win them the swing voters they need to hang on to a majority . <p> The start of a new year has always been a time for hope , a pause to make resolutions of self-improvement . As 2014 starts we hear that the end of progress is near -- that global economies ca n't sustain our population and that the planet is groaning under our weight . The experience of the last century shows how many of our expectations were set -- and what we have to do now to keep from running over a cliff . <p> 1955 was the 25th anniversary of one of America 's great journalistic enterprises , Fortune Magazine . A decade after the end of the Second World War , the editors decided to document the ascent of the greatest engine of economic power in history , U.S.A. Inc . To mark the occasion , Fortune 's editors chose to produce an anniversary series of articles on new goals for the next quarter-century . Ten prominent Americans were asked to contribute their views , in articles that appeared once a month throughout the year . <p> Writers predicted that by 1980 , worldwide transportation would become almost as rapid as communications were then ; that nuclear advances would make energy almost as cheap as air ; that climate control would enable scientists to turn the Arctic into a tropical garden or to bring on another ice age at will . <p> Editor-in-Chief Henry R. Luce saved for himself the challenge of summing up the series with what was billed as " ... an inquiry into the measureless future of a measureless force : the human spirit . " It was probably no accident that Luce chose a subject that defied post-assessment . Some of the boldest and most prolific prophets have sought a similar sanctuary in subject matter that can not come back to haunt them . <p> Since the seasonal parties are well under way , here 's a parting thought for the holidays : If the Harper government wins re-election in 2015 , it should put Mike Duffy up on a pedestal . <p> Partisan Ottawa has been so seized with the Senate expense scandal that larger crises are being set aside . Whether it 's climate change , railway safety , jobless youth , the penury of future pensioners , mental health in the military , declining public health in general -- pick a subject and ask yourself if it has gotten the kind of attention we 've lavished on Senate spendthrifts and the PMO . <p> The NDP 's Tom Mulcair has been fixated for weeks on who said what to whom about the $90,000 reimbursement of Duffy 's expenses by operatives in the Prime Minister 's Office . He smells blood . He is n't going to relax his grip on Stephen Harper as long as the prime minister 's political wounds are still seeping . <p> Down at the other end of the opposition benches , Justin Trudeau has made a sartorial assault on the government . Whenever he 's appeared in the House , his tailor 's handiwork has outshone the substance of his questions . Trudeau has paid minimal attention to the Senate issue -- evidently on the advice of handlers who fear he might injure himself , along with his recent surge in the polls . <p> Technological innovation came to jail yards in the U.S. and Canada this week , when remote-controlled drones were seen coming over the wall . This imaginative new application for unmanned aerial vehicles highlights a growing problem with UAVs operated by unlicensed civilians . <p> Guards at the Hull jail reported an orange-coloured flying object hovering over the inner courtyard on the weekend . They were unable to catch the craft . The perpetrators of the stunt got away with it , apparently unsuccessful in delivering the goods . <p> Four people were arrested in Georgia for apparently using a six-rotor heli-craft to drop tobacco -- or some such smokeable contraband -- to the waiting yardbirds . <p> Commercial drone operators are concerned about uses that mischief-makers can make with " rogue " drones , giving the industry a bad name before it can get off the ground . Privacy activists see them as another nail in the hide of our rights to individual space . Air traffic controllers are worried about an invasion of airspace by unlicensed UAVs , threatening the safety of pilots and passengers in more conventional aircraft . <p> Whatever the intent , the RCMP 's legal move to have an 81-page account of its Senate investigation released to the media and the public could mean some welcome redemption for a police force that has been riddled with scandals of its own making . <p> This apparently bold move -- which exposed what could be their entire case -- may have been done to limit the chances of the Mounties ' image being further corroded by getting tangled up in this mess . <p> The document dump made one thing clear : Far from damage control being limited to Nigel Wright , the prime minister 's chief of staff at the time , a large number of operators in the PMO -- bagmen , lawyers , media handlers and message manipulators -- were in on the management of this hot potato . Apart from some factual knowledge , this crew shared something in common : Each was a political fixer of some sort . <p> Faced with this and the government 's record of managing crisis by kicking down , the RCMP has minimized its exposure to any political backlash by being as open as it has been . The force can ill afford another indelible stain on its red serge . <p> An elephant landed on every boardroom and dining table this week with the declaration by the Insurance Bureau of Canada that some disasters affecting millions of people will be simply uninsurable . <p> The statement extinguishes the last , faint hope that insurance failures are aberrations . A major earthquake could take the industry down , a spokesman said . <p> The next major event could leave Canadians in the same fix as the U.S. Eastern Seaboard after Hurricane Sandy tore through it . Given the scale of the damage there , some property owners have found insurers simply unable to compensate them for more than 10 cents on the dollar for their losses after a year of pleading . <p> So if private insurers fail to deliver , what about government , where the money gets printed ? Lest there be any misunderstanding of what 's at stake here , the Alberta government put homeowners whose properties lie in flood plains on notice after this summer 's epic inundation that they will not be bailed out by government again . <p> The extent of cyber-spying among Western allies became undeniable this week -- so the U.S. government did n't bother trying to tell Germany 's Chancellor Angela Merkel that it never happened . <p> To borrow from Donald Rumsfeld , there are ' unknown unknowns ' in the world of international espionage -- not because spying among friends is unheard of , but because it was never openly acknowledged in the polite company of the diplomatic circuit . <p> Another pithy aphorism from the spy world is closer to the point : " Nations do n't have friends ; they only have assets . " <p> The level of hypocrisy displayed this week is underscored by the fact that the French have sought economic advantage by spying on their allies for decades . At mid-week , President Francois Hollande expressed his indignation in a phone call to Barack Obama . Perhaps Hollande 's dismay is sincere , since he is an ing ? nue among European leaders . <p> Signals intelligence officers are discriminating collectors . They have to be , merely to avoid being overloaded with random and irrelevant detail . <p> By law and in practice , intelligence professionals meet the requirements of their consumers . In the case of Communications Security Establishment Canada , their consumers are government policy-makers . With so many ministries and agencies to serve , there has to be some way to set priorities on what to collect . <p> The way that 's done is by assembling a document that lays out the government 's intelligence collection requirements every year . That document becomes a memorandum to cabinet . Ministers get to argue for their respective priorities around the cabinet table . The prime minister brokers any competition among ministers . The result is the cabinet 's annual statement of requirements to be met by the collection agencies . <p> In other words , Canadian intelligence agencies do n't freelance . They do n't go shopping for anything they can vacuum up just to satisfy their own curiosity . <p> A minister in a new portfolio has one chance to make a first impression . Rona Ambrose had her chance last month . The tone she set on drug policy is less than inspiring . <p> " Our policy is to take heroin out of the hands of addicts , not put it into their arms , " the minister primly declared in September when she ordered Health Canada to respect " the spirit and intent " of the Special Access Program . The program was designed to treat exceptional cases and medical emergencies , she said , not as a back door for addiction treatment staff to access to heroin . <p> Although sounding a punitive and judgmental note as she overruled her officials , Ambrose was merely upholding the status quo in a debate over treatment alternatives . <p> After she whipped her bureaucrats into line , Conservative opportunists stained the new minister 's reputation by sending a fund-raising email to party sympathizers asking for money . Connecting these dots , donors might think their contributions would be used to battle drug abuse . That would be na ? ve . <p> While Pauline Marois turns her separatist government into a train wreck , the one that took 47 lives in Lac-M ? gantic is being eclipsed . <p> This week , the federal Transportation Safety Board signaled that somebody mislabeled the volatile crude oil cargo that blew up in the middle of town . That raises the question of motive and the distinct possibility that malfeasance was involved . If so , the evidence so far implies possible criminality . <p> News of the TSB 's latest findings was overrun by the backlash against Madame Marois ' most recent folly . <p> The S ? ret ? du Qu ? bec has laid no charges to date . A spokesperson said that while the investigation is ongoing " ... we can not confirm any charges at the moment . " <p> The TSB has no mandate to find fault . Their advisory this week went as far as possible in describing malfeasance in labelling . <p> Those Lac M ? gantic rail cars were loaded with incendiary fuel ; the debate about rail safety that ensues when the House resumes this fall could be explosive in a different way . <p> A Senate committee has just delivered a timely report on the subject of transportation safety . The report itself was n't all that revealing ; it covers all the main modes of moving hazardous materials , making broad suggestions about reform of the regulatory " culture " . <p> The RCMP was called on the carpet five years ago for an overhaul of its cultural attitudes . While there 's some evidence that the force is beginning to get it , knowledgeable observers say it could take another five years to root out the worst pockets of resistance in the force . <p> Will the Senate report fuel demand for new regulatory measures of questionable value in a vast country where we have always struggled to service our infrastructure ? Or will its more immediate value be to insist that attention be paid to the regulations already in place ? <p> Transportation Safety Board investigators are looking into braking procedures that appear not to have been followed . They are examining the construction of the cars themselves , which are said to be too weak to sustain such an impact . <p> Nothing less than the " political character of our planet ... in the 21st Century " -- that was what Robert D. Kaplan set out to predict in an essay in the February , 1994 issue of The Atlantic Monthly . <p> Using West African criminal anarchy as his starting point , Kaplan forecast that in a few decades the earth would be overrun by hordes displaced by utter environmental collapse , resulting in scarcity and perpetual violence . The first part of Kaplan 's prediction -- his description of the forces at work in West Africa -- was based on some personal observations and an emerging concern among knowledgeable observers . <p> But Kaplan , a widely-published journalist and academic , was not content to leave it there . Mindful of his market , Kaplan went on to predict that " West Africa 's future , eventually , will also be that of most of the rest of the world . " In doing so , he went over a precipice that has become familiar to millennialists of all ages , into a credibility gap that verged on fearmongering . <p> Even so , this bit of prophecy , including Kaplan 's assessment that borders will collapse and disease will isolate the African continent , was widely-circulated and debated in the corridors of western decision-making . Its influence on policy choices in a critical period when governments were ( even then ) stripping their foreign aid and environmental protection budgets is incalculable . <p> Something does n't add up when a woman who died at the hands of rapists in India makes headlines here for weeks , while a silent epidemic claims the lives of thousands across North America whose stories are seldom told . Horrific as the asphyxiation of two young boys by a snake may be , the arithmetic of public attention makes no sense . <p> An prescription opioid abuse claimed more than 16,000 lives in the U.S. in 2012 . Five hundred and thirty-five people died from the same cause in Ontario alone last year . <p> No government agency is tracking the spread of this menace across Canada , say knowledgeable observers . Federal inertia has led to a formalized lobby that is organizing to raise a nationwide alarm . Instead of heading for Ottawa , a pressure group is going to Washington this fall to join a demonstration of continental concern on Capitol Hill . <p> The assessment of Dr. David Juurlink , a toxicologist at Sunnybrook Hospital in Toronto , is blunt : " The federal government has abdicated responsibility for this crisis . " <p> " If you knew that 1,200 to 1,300 Canadians were going to die this year from something that you could prevent , would n't you do anything to stop it ? " he asks . " This needs to be put on the radar of the new health minister ( Rona Ambrose ) . " <p> Public Safety Minister Steven Blaney was just easing into his new chair this week when a nasty rumour gave him a jolt . Kingston Penitentiatry , where they were held , is Canada 's answer to London 's notorious Wormwood Scrubs , or Los Angeles ' inescapable Alcatraz . Not in the Mother Theresa sense of the word , but in the small-town , churchy way of mid-century Ontario where religion still played a large part in peoples ' everyday lives . Although her usually unsmiling , thin-lipped appearance made her seem more severe than she ever was with me , she was seldom quick to anger . " <p> The milkman was n't ' blessed ' in the clerical sense . He would have been damned for his oversight if she had allowed herself to use the word .
