<p> A majority of Canadians are bracing themselves for further economic downturn , as the COVID-19 pandemic is set to enter its second month . <p> According to a new Angus Reid opinion poll released Monday , 87 per cent of Canadians believe the worst is yet to come , while 3 per cent feel the worst is over and 10 per cent are unsure . <p> Just over a quarter of Canadians say they are barely making ends meet financially , while 12 per cent say they 're prepared to weather a recession . The majority ( 61 per cent ) say they are in good shape but worry about the long-term , while 5 per cent were already suffering economic challenges prior to the spread of the virus . <p> Source : Angus Reid Institute <p> Nearly half of Canadian households have experienced job loss due to recent cuts prompted by the virus , while 12 per cent anticipate fewer hours of work . A total of 27 per cent , however , do not expect to lose work . <p> And while both provincial and federal levels of government have announced aid packages for businesses and individuals , thoughts are mixed on whether those will help . <p> About 45 per cent of those who are struggling due to COVID-19 job or wage impacts say the federal Canada Emergency Response Benefit -- which began accepting applications Monday -- will help them , while 39 per cent are skeptical about whether it 's enough . <p> Source : Angus Reid Institute <p> Concern also remains high as Canadians adjust to staying home , physical distancing and video calls to remain connected . <p> A total of 91 per cent of Canadians are worried about friends and family who are not within the same household contracting the virus , while 79 per cent are worried about someone in their own household . <p> The poll also showed that older people are more likely to be concerned about their own personal risk of contracting COVID-19 than those who are younger . <p> Concern levels among those age 55 and up has been growing steadily since February , with 34 per cent of people in the age group saying they were " very concerned " about their own risk in the latest poll , while concern among those between the ages of 18 to 34 has been growing more slowly , with only 16 per cent of people saying the same . <p> In B.C. , the majority of residents ( 93 per cent ) say the information provided by health officials and politicians has been either " totally clear " or " pretty clear " in regards to what people should be doing to help flatten the curve of COVID-19 cases . Some 66 per cent also say officials are providing an adequate amount of information , while 34 per cent say officials need to be sharing more info with the public . <p> Aside from the immediate COVID-19 concerns , British Columbians ranked the environment or climate change ( 28 per cent ) and the economy ( 32 per cent ) as the two other issues most concerning at this time . <p> COVID-19 was declared a pandemic by the World Health Organization on March 11 . <p> The poll was conducted from April 1 through 3 among a representative randomized sample of 2,162 Canadians . A probability sample of this size would carry a margin of error of +/- 2.0 percentage points , 19 times out of 20.
