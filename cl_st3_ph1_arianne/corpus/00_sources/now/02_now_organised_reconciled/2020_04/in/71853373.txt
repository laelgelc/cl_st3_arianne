<h> COVID-19 pandemic puts need for nurses into sharp relief <p> MANILA -- A global shortage of nurses is highlighted in a report from the World Health Organization , released on World Health Day yesterday , April 7 , 2020 , at a time when nursing staff and other health professionals are working around the clock responding to coronavirus disease 2019 , or COVID-19 . <p> The WHO report , called " State of the World 's Nursing 2020 , " projects that , without action , there will be a worldwide shortage of 4.6 million nurses by 2030 unless greater investment in nursing is made now . <p> For 2018 , WHO estimates there was a total shortfall of 350 000 nurses in countries and areas of the Western Pacific , with the most acute shortages in low- and middle-income countries . The CNMI belongs to this region . <p> " The case for investing in the nursing workforce has never been clearer , " said Dr. Takeshi Kasai , WHO regional director for the Western Pacific . " Right now , nurses are on the front lines of the COVID-19 fight , working tirelessly to save lives and protect others in their community . Governments across the Western Pacific ... must invest in strengthening their nursing workforce as an essential part of preparedness for health challenges such as emerging infectious diseases , but also the health challenges brought about by climate change , ageing populations and a growing burden of noncommunicable diseases . " <p> The WHO report paints a picture of an unevenly distributed nursing workforce across the Western Pacific region , composed largely of women aged under 35 years who have left their countries of origin to work in higher-income countries . <p> One in three nurses in this region works outside of the country where they were born or trained . The Western Pacific has the highest proportion of women nurses ( 95% ) in the world , and half ( 50.7% ) are under the age of 35 . The region also has fewer nursing graduates per 100 000 population at 20.6 compared to the global average of 22.6 . <p> According to the report , the number of nursing graduates around the world will need to increase by an average 8% per year . Governments will also need to address inequities in workforce distribution and pay , outdated education models , limited career pathways and weak regulation if they are to train and retain enough nurses to respond to current and future health needs . Investments in quality training and ensuring adequate pay and decent working conditions will improve health outcomes , promote gender equality and support economic growth . <p> For this year 's World Health Day , the " State of the World 's Nursing 2020 " report has been produced by WHO in partnership with the International Council of Nurses and the global Nursing Now campaign , as well as support from governments and other partners . <p> The report provides a global picture of the nursing workforce , using data and standardized indicators from 193 countries and areas , including 31 in the Western Pacific . <p> At the World Health Assembly in May 2019 , ministers of health from across the globe designated 2020 as the Year of the Nurse and Midwife . It marks 200 years since the birth of Florence Nightingale -- the founder of modern nursing and recognizes the critical contribution these professions make to global health . <p> A similar report on the midwifery workforce will be launched in 2021 .
