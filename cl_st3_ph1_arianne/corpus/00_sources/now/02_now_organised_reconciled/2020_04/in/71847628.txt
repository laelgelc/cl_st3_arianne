<h> Last Updated : Friday 03 April 2020 <p> The decision to call off the 26th session of the Conference of the Parties to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change ( COP26 ) is not a surprise . <p> The risks of convening tens of thousands of people this close to flu season are too high . <p> This decision can not be interpreted as one crisis being elevated over the other . Climate action is now counted in years , rather than decades . <p> Climate change poses a collective action problem , so it needs international consultations to generate national or non-state action . <p> A year without a COP thus , comes at a massive cost . This cost must be accepted because holding it in the current circumstances would have been anti-productive for climate priorities . <p> It is not , however , going to be a lost year for climate action . <p> First , there is already a less-than-transparent push to prop up climate-irresponsible industries . <p> Oil company chief executives will convene at the White House this week to seek help amid a sharp dive in global oil prices . <p> The American Petroleum Institute , however , said in mid-March 2020 , that it did not seek government intervention in the industry . <p> This followed a decision by the US Environment Protection Agency to not enforce pollution rules for the oil and gas industry , citing pressure that the novel coronavirus disease ( COVID-19 ) pandemic had put on bottom lines . <p> The EPA doubled down on this approach this week , rolling back Obama-era regulations on the automobile industry , despite overwhelming criticism from environmental groups . <p> This was expected to some extent . An extension of the cynic 's maxim that a good crisis must never be wasted . <p> For the climate movement , however , this pandemic is a tragedy , pure and simple . It is not an excuse to push or accept anti-policies that could cost more lives . <p> This means playing strong defense against highly cynical lobbying -- especially by quantifying costs of regulatory subsidies to fossil fuels . <p> Second , the COP agenda is one part of the climate agenda , not all of it . <p> The priority at the next COP -- whenever it happens -- is quite specific : Rules for carbon emissions trading . <p> The Paris Agreement , however , is designed around national targets , known as nationally determined contributions ( NDCs ) . <p> NDCs are supposed to be submitted every five years and each successive NDC must be more ambitious than the last . <p> The initial round was submitted in 2015 , with the second round expected in 2020 . <p> These are not decided at international conferences and countries can not use the postponement of the COP to postpone their legal obligation to submit new NDCs in 2020 . <p> Enough preparatory work has -- or should have -- been done since 2015 to enable the submission of new NDCs , despite national lockdowns . <p> While not decided at international conferences , NDCs are influenced by them . <p> A big disagreement at COP25 in Madrid last year was whether developing countries were required to submit new NDCs . <p> Certain larger developing countries argued that developed countries had not met previous ( particularly Kyoto-era ) targets and had not transferred the level of finance they had promised to developing countries in Paris . <p> The upshot is that several developing countries now feel that they do not have to upgrade their climate targets in 2020 . <p> There is a simple solution : Developed countries can take the lead on announcing their 2020 NDCs . <p> These must equitably upgrade their own level of ambition in line with the 1.5 degree Celsius target , as well as commit to financing climate ambition in the developing world . <p> This is not impossible . Norway submitted an updated NDC in January and Japan submitted one last week . <p> Both are far from ambitious enough . <p> But if countries can submit unambitious targets during a developing pandemic , they can submit serious ones too , especially once the curve is flattened . <p> We are a voice to you ; you have been a support to us .
