<p> In dismissing the commander of the USS Theodore Roosevelt , the Navy once again punished the messenger , a frontline leader brave enough to tell the unvarnished truth to superiors about a threat to his sailors . <p> Capt . Brett Crozier , fired this week from command of the aircraft carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt , joins a growing list of Navy officers who attempted to raise concerns about the safety of their ships and crew , only to pay with their jobs . <p> Crozier wrote a letter dated March 30 warning that an outbreak of the coronavirus on his ship was a threat to his crew of some 4,000 sailors unless they disembarked and quarantined . <p> " We are not at war , and therefore can not allow a single sailor to perish as a result of this pandemic unnecessarily , " Crozier wrote . " Decisive action is required now . " <p> We do not know all the facts that prompted the letter . But we know that once it was published in the San Francisco Chronicle , the acting secretary of the Navy , Thomas Modly , relieved Crozier of command . Crozier , 50 , had been a rising star in the officer corps . He will remain in the Navy at his current rank , though his career future is uncertain . In viral videos , Crozier 's sailors can be seen cheering him loudly as he disembarks the Roosevelt , alone , before driving away . <p> Navy experts believe that the cumulative effects of the service 's decisions over the past several years to punish those who speak out will result in silencing sailors with legitimate concerns about their health and safety . <p> " This may have the effect of chilling the responses of other commanding officers because it will be perceived , fairly or not , as a shoot the messenger scenario , " said James Stavridis , a retired admiral and former head of the United States Naval Institute , who called for an investigation into the circumstances surrounding the dismissal . <p> The Navy 's top officer , Adm . Mike Gilday , initially praised Crozier 's attempt to warn his superiors . But the next day , Thursday , Modly , the Navy 's civilian boss , reversed course , telling reporters that he fired Crozier because he lost confidence in the officer for not using a secure email network to properly route his complaint . <p> Crozier 's unclassified email wound up with 20 or 30 other individuals and at some point was provided to the Chronicle reporters . Modly said the public airing of the complaint had unnecessarily alarmed sailors and provided enemies with information that exposed weaknesses on one of the country 's most important warships . <p> As part of our 2019 investigation into the incidents in the Navy 's 7th Fleet , its largest overseas presence , ProPublica found repeated instances of frontline commanders warning superiors of risks the fleet was facing -- a lack of training , exhausted crews , deteriorating ships and equipment . Those warnings , all sent through the normal chain of command , were met with indifference . <p> Disaster in the fleet struck in June 2017 , after the USS Fitzgerald , a destroyer , collided with a cargo ship in the Sea of Japan . Two months later , a second destroyer , the USS John S. McCain , collided with an oil tanker in the Singapore Strait . The two accidents cost the Navy 17 sailors -- the biggest loss of life in maritime collisions in more than 40 years . <p> Navy investigations laid blame on nearly the entire chain of command in the 7th Fleet , punishing commanders and sailors for failing to properly train and equip its crews and ships . <p> Adm . Joseph Aucoin , the head of the 7th Fleet , was fired . Vice Adm . Thomas Rowden , who oversaw training , was forced from his job . Cmdr. Bryce Benson , captain of the Fitzgerald , was recommended for court-martial . <p> But ProPublica reported that all three men had repeatedly tried to warn higher-ups of dangerous safety issues in the vaunted fleet , based at Yokosuka , Japan . They argued to their superiors that the Navy was running ships in the 7th Fleet too hard , too fast . Their warnings were dismissed ... Continue Reading <p> Seminal moments are by definition not very common . Interestingly , we have had two in the past 12 years . The collapse of Lehmann Brothers in 2008 leading to the financial crisis changed the global economy and the financial system forever . The coronavirus crisis we are facing currently promises to be another one . <p> We are likely to get over this crisis sooner ( hopefully ) or later ( inevitably ) , with damages that could range from severe to grave . What is interesting to speculate on is how the crisis would impact the world we live in , and specifically , India -- the geopolitics and the economics . The exercise requires a little bit of crystal-gazing , since even the extent of the crisis is as yet unknown . <p> The world has changed radically over the past three decades . For a long time after the World War II , we had a bipolar world with the US and the United States of Soviet Russia ( USSR ) challenging each other for establishing their hegemony over the world . The collapse of the USSR in the early nineties lulled us into thinking that it was the ' end of history ' , as Francis Fukuyama famously wrote . It was widely believed that the liberal values of the Western world had triumphed permanently . <p> Since then , the inexorable rise of China and its attempts to influence global affairs in its own ways , have shattered the myth . China apart , a number of other countries have grown stronger and are not shy of displaying their muscle power . <p> For many thinkers , the financial crisis of 2008 was the beginning of the decline of the US as a predominant superpower and the rejection of unbridled capitalism as the primary means of progress . <p> Today , we face unprecedented problems that defy solution . Climate change , terrorism and religious intolerance , and migration are some of the problems that can be resolved only with global cooperation . <p> Seven decades on , the global governance structure that emerged post World War-II is proving incapable of addressing these issues . The institutions established at that time including the United Nations Organisation ( UNO ) , the World Health Organisation ( WHO ) , the World Bank , the International Monetary Fund ( IMF ) , the World Trade Organisation and the International Court of Justice , amongst others , are being challenged by many countries , which want a greater say in running these institutions , appropriate to their newfound economic clout . In comes Donald Trump , with his philosophy of America first , with scant regard for global organisations and the recipe for disaster is complete . <p> How will the coronavirus crisis impact this rather fluid international world order ? <p> What is likely to happen , in my view , is that the trends over the past few years will get reinforced . What are these trends ? <p> 1 . Nationalism will receive a further boost , with people turning inwards and preferring strong leaders who they believe will be able to take care of their interests and not be unduly involved with global affairs . Over the past decade , this trend has been quite evident and we have seen the likes of Donald Trump , Xi Jinping , Boris Johnson , Narendra Modi , Recep Tayyip Erdogan , Vladimir Putin and many others assuming power and consolidating their authority in their respective countries . <p> 2 . Globalisation is likely to be a major casualty . Given that the high growth rates over the plast few decades have been based on international trade and investments through freer movement of goods , services and money , we are likely to experience slower global economic growth , along with a less free and less open world . <p> 3 . Despite the fact that the coronavirus originated in China , its demonstrated ability to control the menace effectively and fight it successfully has raised its profile in the world community . The US , on the other hand , has largely reacted with ineptness . Unless there is a significant change in the way the US tackles the effect of the virus in the coming days , its credibility is likely to take a beating . <p> This is quite unlike the role it used to play earlier whenever such crisis broke out , where not only was it able to respond effectively internally , it also led and coordinated global efforts , offering its expertise and technology to other countries . Such an effort from the leading global power is missing as of now . Hopefully , we will see that change over the coming days and weeks . <p> Consequently , there is a general feeling that the Coronavirus crisis may shift influence and power from the west to the east , again a trend that has been evident for some time . <p> 4 . The relationship between the two major powers , the US and China , is likely to become more strained . The two will become adversaries rather than partners in growth and the rest of the world is likely to suffer , as a consequence . Are we going to revisit the days of the cold war ? Quite likely , in my opinion . <p> Trump 's mishandling of the coronavirus crisis has certainly diminished his standing amongst people and is likely to adversely impact his chances of reelection in November this year . May be , if the Democrat Joe Biden were to be elected the President of US , some of the trends we have discussed may need to be revisited . <p> As I mentioned , these trends have been evident for some time . The pace of change will be much faster and will significantly alter politics , power structure and the economy on a global scale . <p> What about our own country ? That is always much tougher to evaluate . I am reminded of what the great Cambridge economist Joan Robinson had remarked " whatever you can rightly say about India , the opposite is also equally true . " <p> We have an innate ability to shoot ourselves in the foot when conditions are in our favour ; at the same time , we also have the tenacity to wriggle out of the worst possible crises . <p> Let us not undermine the fact that what we are facing now is a calamity , the kind of which we have never experienced before . Hopefully , we would be able to fight it and come out victorious soon . At the same time , we need to be ready with our own responses to the changed global scenario . <p> Will we be forced to take sides in a US-China cold war ? <p> What are the consequences of such a choice ? <p> As a response to the previous cold war , Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru had opted to be equidistant and had emerged as the founder and leader of the consequent Non-Aligned Movement . <p> Is that a possibility more than six decades on ? Would our choice be dependent on the outcome of the US Presidential elections ? Will Mr Modi 's endorsement of Mr Trump during ' Howdy Modi ' last year be a factor in case of a democrat President ? <p> If global efforts to combat climate change becomes a casualty , how do we respond ? If the world becomes inward looking , do we still adopt an export led strategy ? Or , give real meaning to ' make in India ' ? It is notable that our manufacturing exports have not increased by a single dollar over the past seven years . <p> China may attempt to replace or fundamentally alter the structure of the current US led global governance . It has already moved partially in that direction with the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank ( AIIB ) and the one-belt-one-road ( OBOR ) . It may try and establish other institutions that may compete with the existing ones . <p> Do we keep opposing them without participation or can we ride two boats simultaneously and hedge our bets ? Our foreign policy regime will need to mull all these questions and be proactive in responding to them . Fortunately , we currently have an external affairs minister who has had an illustrious career in foreign affairs and diplomacy . His skills are likely to be tested to the extreme . <p> Geopolitics has never been a staid affair and is not meant for the faint of heart . The next few years are likely to be even more fascinating and consequential than normal . Fasten your seat belts and look forward to some interesting times . <p> ( Sunil Mahajan , a financial consultant and teacher , has over three decades experience in the corporate sector , consultancy and academics . ) <p> Like this story ?
