<h> India has highest level of child wasting in South Asia : Global report <h> Last Updated : Friday 12 October 2018 <p> Credit : Getty Images <p> India may be boasting about its GDP data , but it is second after South Sudan , a conflict-laden country , when it comes to wasting ( low weight for height ) among children . This has come out in Global Hunger Index released on Friday . India has been ranked at 103rd position out of 119 countries . Except Pakistan , all other neighbours are in better position as compared to India . <p> Nepal was ranked 72nd , Sri Lanka 67th and Bangladesh was ranked 86th among all 119 countries . <p> Underlining poor health of children in India , the report points out that wasting has increased in the last two decades . In 2000 , 17.1 per cent children under five years were wasted . Their percentage has now increased to 21 . <p> As mentioned before , India is second ( 21 per cent ) after South Sudan where 28.6 per cent of children are wasted . <p> The report says , " South Asia 's child wasting rate constitutes a critical public health emergency . This is made all the more concerning because it has not decreased but rather has slightly increased since 2000 . The child wasting rate for the region is amplified in part by that of India , which has the region 's largest population and highest level of child wasting . " <p> The findings of the 2018 Global Hunger Index -- published jointly by Concern Worldwide and Welthungerhilfe -- are based on assessment of four criteria : undernourishment ( insufficient calorie intake ) , child stunting ( too short for one 's age ) , child wasting and child mortality . <p> South Asia and Africa south of the Sahara have high prevalence of all four poor health standards , says the report . <p> South Asia has seen a drop in stunting since the year 2000 , however , the rate is still high . Now over a third of all children in the region are stunted . <p> The report claims that undernourishment rate has marginally increased in Africa , especially , south of the Sahara . In 2015 -- 2017 , undernourishment rate was 22 per cent , the highest among all other regions . <p> Though hunger has gone down in terms of percentage , it is still increasing in actual number . As per the report , approximately 124 million people suffer from acute hunger , a steep increase from 80 million , two years ago . <p> At present , the world has 151 million stunted and 51 million wasted children . The report harps on the fact that conflict , climate change and poor governance are threatening hard-won gains . <p> Based on the trend , the report predicts that the world is not going to achieve zero hunger targets by 2030 as it had committed in 2015 .
