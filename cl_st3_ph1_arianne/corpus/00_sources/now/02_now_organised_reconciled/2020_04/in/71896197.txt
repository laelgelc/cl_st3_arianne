This year 's Earth Day , celebrated on April 22 each year , had ' Climate Action ' as its theme . We tend to forget how crucial a healthy climate is for our survival . It is climate that decides good crops and decent rainfall , and it is climate again that regulates temperatures during peak summer and winter seasons . <p> To tackle this , Anant National University has launched a first-of-its-kind 1-year fellowship for people who have already demonstrated a strong commitment to working for climate change solutions . <p> Selected applicants will get to work with some of the top climate pioneers in the world now , and the best part is - 75% of the fellowship classes will be held at locations where solutions for climate change solutions are being implemented on ground level , and at locations that are hard hit by climate change <h> Why do we need to tackle climate change ? <p> In recent years , the world has seen extreme climates with summer temperatures crossing 50 degrees Celcius in some areas in India while winters are harsher than ever . <p> However , the Covid-19 pandemic and the ensuing lockdown almost all over the world had healed the environment to a certain extent . The ozone layer was reported to be healing and pollution has been the lowest in 30 years as reported in big cities in India . <p> There is no better time than right now to gear up our work to heal the planet since it is already taking a few steps on its own now that most humans are locked inside their homes unable to damage the environment further . <p> Nevertheless , experts say that the effects of low pollution due to the Covid-19 lockdown will be short live . As busy life takes over after lockdown , pollution rates will likely spike and go back to ' normal ' with two weeks . <p> Pollution caused by humans in a major factor behind cliamte change . <p> We got talking to Miniya Chatterji , Director of the Centre for Sustainability and of the Fellowship for Climate Action at Anant National University , Ahmedabad , and the CEO of Sustain Labs Paris and adjunct-professor Sciences Po Paris to understand more about the climate fellowship launched by Anant National University and why it is a big step . <h> Why is a climate action fellowship programme from India so important ? <p> " Currently a large part of what we hear about the effects of climate change or the solutions to mitigate it , originate mostly from Europe , " says Miniya Chatterji . <p> We hear very little about how climate change is affecting India and the solutions that are working in the specific context of India , and not western countries . <p> India is an emerging economy with problems that are very different from what developed countries are facing . <p> We are dealing with immediate issues such as poverty , inadequate food distribution , lack of basic shelter -- all besides the effects of climate change . <p> So , when we are talking about effective climate change solutions in India , we also need to think about how it is related to the more crucial problems in our country . <p> India is an emerging economy with problems that are very different from what developed countries are facing . That is the key , " says Chatterji . <p> " And this is because challenges related to poverty , shelter , public health , and the degradation of the environment are inter-related , " she adds . <p> She notes that though we will increasingly be affected by sudden natural disasters , new viruses , and rising temperatures , we need to tackle all of that with context to India 's bigger problems . <p> As an international programme based in India , the Fellowship for Climate Action at Anant National University fills this evident gap of having an adequate voice from emerging economies like India in the current discourse on climate change in the world . <p> " We firmly believe that some of the most effective solutions for the world to adapt and mitigate to climate change will come from India and the emerging economies , " she says . <p> The Fellowship for Climate Action at Anant National University fills this evident gap of having an adequate voice from emerging economies like India in the current discourse on climate change in the world . <h> Is the increased flood and rainfall all due to climate change ? <p> No , says Miniya Chatterji . She explains that we can not scientifically attribute the whole responsibility of increased rainfall and flood to global warming alone . <p> " But climate change does play a role in increasing extreme precipitation events as well as desertification , " she says . <p> " This means that there will be lands with massive rainfall or with no rain at all . We need to identify with all seriousness ecologically vulnerable zones , map them , and see what kind of protection can be given , " says Chatterji . <p> " Being prepared early will avoid panic later , " she adds . <p> The Amazon Rainforest deforestation is being carried out by humans , not climate change . ( Photo : Reuters ) <h> What are the top measures taken in India to battle climate change ? <p> Since 2010 , India has kick-started several national programmes to boost cleaner economic development . These have focused on renewable energy , energy efficiency and clean transport among others . <p> " We have done well in implementing energy efficiency policies , pushing solar energy , and replacing over 700 million street and household lights with energy-saving and long-lasting LEDs , " says Miniya Chatterji . <p> " It is also interesting to note the massively important role that state governments have played in developing regional climate strategies that offer important learnings for scalability and success , " she adds . <p> " In fact , the top 10 performing states on climate action have higher contributions to the national GDP , lower emissions intensity , better energy efficiency , higher utilization of renewable energy potential and a higher percentage growth in forest cover , " she says . <p> Moreover , India is doing its bit to jump on the bandwagon of generating a large amount of renewable energy . <p> It has set a target of achieving 175 GW of renewable energy capacity by 2022 , of which 100 GW is solar capacity addition and 60 GW is wind power capacity . <p> " Over the past decade both the energy and emission intensities of India 's GDP have decreased by more than 20% , but total energy-related CO2 emissions continue to rise in India which indicates that we need to do more , " says Miniya Chatterji . <h> Details about the Climate Action Fellowship Programme <p> Fellowship duration : 1 year <p> Selection criteria : <p> The Fellowship is designed for those who have already achieved a record of accomplishment in mitigating climate change , or have advanced transferable skills , and would like to be propelled into working with the climate pioneers of their field . <p> Applicants must be able to show a demonstrated strong commitment to working towards solutions for climate change . <p> Application deadline : May 15 , 2020 <p> Fellowship commences : August 10 , 2020 <p> Selected applicants will get a chance to work with one of following mentors :
