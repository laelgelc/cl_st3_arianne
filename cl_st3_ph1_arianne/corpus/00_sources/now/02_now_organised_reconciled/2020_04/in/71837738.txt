<p> The world is crumbling , the economy is in a downfall , people are suffering . That 's the reality now , " says Tarun Balani . But for the Delhi-based composer and jazz drummer , who often works from home , " nothing much has changed " . " It is still worrying , " he says . In a strange twist of fate , his latest release , 2 ? , under the moniker Seasonal Affected Beats , coincided with the global outbreak of Covid-19 . And , what 's so strange about that ? Well , he created the songs of the EP based on his experience of living in a " dystopic , apocalyptic world " . <p> " I honestly did n't intend for the EP to be so apt . I conceptualised it around 2017-18 . At the time , the smog in Delhi seemed truly apocalyptic to me , " says Balani , who adds that in order to be a good storyteller , one must be a good listener too and that he often draws from his surroundings . " From mental health to climate change to socio-political changes such as NRC and CAA -- this EP is a sonic representation of all the issues that affect me , " he says . The five-track EP is instrumental , except for one song ( " Let the Light In " ) , featuring vocals by singer Kavya Trehan . <p> The album title , 2 ? , refers to one of the Paris Agreement 's long-term goals , which is to ensure the average global temperature is well below two degrees to avoid catastrophic results . But Balani 's music is n't entirely macabre . " The world ends , and we all die , that 's what we see in popular movies and novels about the apocalypse . But I wanted to string a positive narrative , despite the inevitable challenges , and take responsibility for our actions . " How does he wordlessly achieve this through music ? In the last track of the EP , the jazz-tinged " 2 ? ( For Naima ) " , he leans on uplifting piano notes to render an optimistic outlook . Aptly , the song is dedicated to Balani 's three-year-old niece . <p> " I call it improvised electronic music , " says Balani when asked about how he 'd describe the songs he makes under Seasonal Affected Beats . " It has electro sounds and synths but it is n't dance music , " he says , adding , " It is extremely performative , versatile and open , and I 'm bringing all my expertise to it " . An established jazz drummer for over a decade , Balani says his induction to music was during his formative years . " My parents made me listen to artists such as ABBA , Simon &amp; Garfunkel and Pandit Ravi Shankar . My late grandfather , who unfortunately I never met , was an artist himself and we had his paintings and photographs all over the house , " he says . <p> At first , Balani started by tinkering with a keyboard lying around the house out of curiosity . " MTV was big back then , and I was in the 8th or 9th grade , and was fascinated by the idea of playing an instrument , " he says . Soon , he found the drums to be his weapon of choice . " I joined the school band and saw this senior playing a blue fiberglass drum set , and the power of the beat struck me hard . " Balani was 13 then begged my parents to buy him a drum set , which they eventually did . <p> Keen on becoming a pop-rock fusion drummer , by 18 , Balani was already playing in popular bands such as Advaita , along with his brother and musician Aditya Balani . " I wanted a long-term career as a musician and I also wanted to be a composer , " says the Berklee College of Music graduate . Prior to Berklee , he joined The Drummers Collective in New York in 2005 . " There , I was introduced to jazz by my teachers . New York is a mecca for jazz , and I started frequenting the jazz clubs there . I love the improvisational aspect of it . As a composer , I found it intriguing and was instantly hooked " . <p> Balani is also part of the jazz quintet Dharma , and has been writing and touring with the act for eight years now . Last year , they released their second album on Berthold Records , a German label . A failed European tour with Dharma led Balani to create Seasonal Affected Beats . " It was two years ago , our tour in Europe fell through , and my summer calendar was suddenly empty . My band members were in NY , and I was stuck in India . At this serendipitous moment , I decided to learn a new skill , " he says , alluding to Sensory Percussion , a device that allows one to compose music using a drum kit . " It 's this high tech gear , in which I 'm not pressing buttons or pads . I 'm composing songs by playing everything on the drums . " Balani shares the idea behind the project was to create electro beats over a period of 12 months and see what came out of it . " It was an experimental and low-key project . Basically , to see how the beats affect me . It was very exciting and turned out to be another extension of my artistic expression , " he adds . <p> So how did he land on Seasonal Affected Beats ? " Delhi gets very hot during the summer , it reaches 45 degrees , and I was feeling very low when I started working on this . So I joked with my partner , who is a mental health professional , that I have Seasonal Affective Disorder , like those in the Scandinavian countries where they get very little sunlight , and that 's how the name came about , " he says with a laugh . <p> Sound Bath + Chakra HealingWe are all in need of a bit of healing in these times , right ?
