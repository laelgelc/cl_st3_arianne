<h> Chance to redefine nature of business <p> Acclaimed Indian novelist Arundhati Roy recently wrote : " . . . coronavirus has made the mighty kneel and brought the world to a halt like nothing else could . Our minds are still racing back and forth , longing for a return to ' normality ' , trying to stitch our future to our past and refusing to acknowledge the rupture . But the rupture exists . And in the midst of this terrible despair , it offers us a chance to rethink the doomsday machine we have built for ourselves . Nothing could be worse than a return to normality . " <p> Covid-19 restrictions imposed by the Government seem to be having a positive effect on human and environmental health , but an economic impact that most of us have n't begun to feel . Halting the movement of people presents a major shock to the economy but also presents an opportunity to organise society and the economy differently as we come out of the health crisis . <p> China has seen drastic reductions in nitrogen dioxide levels . Nasa recorded NO2 levels in China 10 to 30 percent lower than normal . Stanford University expert , Marshall Burke , believes between 50,000 and 75,000 premature deaths have been prevented already , simply due to cleaner air across the country . These findings show pre-pandemic economies had massive hidden health costs , and it takes the current crisis to help us see that . <p> Reduced air travel and less industrial pollution are likely to be temporary , returning quickly once the threat of coronavirus passes . So in the absence of new policies and structural measures , these improvements to environmental health will not be sustained . Public servants have a once in a lifetime opportunity to require any industry receiving public support to be better environmental stewards . Without requiring changes , we will miss a major opportunity to combat issues like climate change , erosion , biodiversity loss and ocean dumping . <p> Interventions in the economy to date have rightly focused on protecting employees most affected by loss of income . The private sector ca n't , however , be propped up by taxpayers indefinitely and tough decisions on how best to rebuild the economy lie ahead . <p> At a local level , organisations looking to best position the district can reduce risks from future shocks and environmentally-damaging industries . Local investment should focus on retraining workers and redeployment into higher-value sectors like fruit production , digital technologies and healthcare research , to redefine what has traditionally been a regional economy based on low-value commodities . <p> The worst thing public agencies expected to help prop up the economy can do now is focus support on saving unsustainable industries that in fact need to be wound down . The Government has asked the regions to identify projects worth over $10 million that would be ready to go within six months and contribute to job creation . Approaches like those used after the GFC seem like a terrible waste if the goal is to resuscitate an economic model that should be left behind . Investing public funds in high-tech industries , including new manufacturing , seems like a much better option to shift towards the circular economy of truly sustainable production and consumption . New business models with substantive public ownership and accountability , as we see in Air New Zealand and Eastland Group , should be the new norm . <p> Global pandemic experts like NZ-born virologist Dr Robert Webster warn this pandemic is just a warm-up for the big one that will be even more deadly . The Covid-19 crisis demonstrates that we can and must do things differently . We must not go back to the status quo . We can create better ways of organising society based on rethinking the role of private and public stakeholders , and redefining the nature of business .
