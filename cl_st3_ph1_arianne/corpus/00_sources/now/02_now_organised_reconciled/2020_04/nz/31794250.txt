<h> AIR Index can help lock in cleaner air <p> Words : NZ Autocar <p> 23 Apr 2020 <p> While the automotive industry and global economy have taken a hit as a result of the COVID-19 outbreak , there 's an opportunity to lock in cleaner air gains from reduced burning of fossil fuels . The AIR Index ( airindex.com ) helps buyers identify the cleanest cars when switching vehicles . <p> Its ratings database is the result of rigorous on-road testing , and it details real-world NOx and CO2 emissions , of relevance to air quality and climate change . <p> Vehicles with low ratings on both counts are already available . EVs are the gold standard , but existing hybrid technology can deliver significant reductions in CO2 , while some internal combustion engines now achieve extremely low levels of harmful emissions . However , some older supposedly Euro 6 compliant ' clean ' diesels remain part of the pollution problem . <p> The challenge and opportunity as the world gradually moves out of lockdown is to enable the return to mass mobility without returning to the high levels of pollution evident prior to the pandemic . <p> Already there are calls for ways of stimulating the recovery of the automotive industry including the revival of scrappage schemes . These are expensive , however , whereas the AIR Index provides data to allow a more targeted approach , allowing sensible purchasing choices to be made . <p> Leading scientists have recognised the importance of the AIR Index and are urging car makers to utilise the figures to build cleaner vehicles . <p> Dan Carder , a member of AIR 's Scientific Advisory Committee , commented that the Air Index has the power to inform and shape how vehicles are used , limiting the environmental impact of any economic recovery . <p> NZ Autocar is New Zealand 's leading automotive magazine . Delivering news and authoritative reviews from the car world each month , it includes commentary from NZ 's leading automotive writers and covers the full scope of motoring including new cars , fast cars , classics and motorcycles.
