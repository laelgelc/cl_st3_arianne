<h> Sustainability guardians <p> New Zealand Winegrowers has ramped up its sustainability goals , with a raft of new and improved measures to safeguard our " planet , people and profit " , says General Manager Sustainability Dr Edwin Massey . <p> The initiatives being rolled out by the board include groups of Sustainability Guardians , to work on key areas of water , waste , pest and disease , people , and climate change . <p> The board has introduced environmental health indicators , with objective and measurable benchmarks , and made a commitment to the industry working towards carbon neutrality . <p> There is also to be a staged ban of organophosphates , among other changes to Sustainable Winegrowing New Zealand ( SWNZ ) and the Spray Schedule . <p> Together , the measures signal a new direction for New Zealand Winegrowers ( NZW ) , says Edwin . " Simply put , these changes seek to embed sustainability at the core of our industry for the benefit of all members . " <p> Environmental industry health indicators <p> A recent PwC review of the New Zealand wine industry defined sustainability as environmental , social and financial , says Edwin . <p> Existing health indicators of sustainability are comprised of grape price , the value of exports and compliance with resource management rules . <p> NZW has now adopted four environmental industry health indicators , to ensure care for the planet is also at the core of the industry . <p> They are : <p> * No major new pests established in vineyards . <p> * Percentage of member organisations measuring their carbon production footprint . <p> The NZW board has approved in principle a new financial model that separates SWNZ membership costs and audit costs . <p> The change also includes options to increase the flexibility of audit verification for members . <p> The first of these is the introduction of producer groups , overseen by a specific winery , which will be trialled by a producer group in Northland , then developed , says Edwin . <p> The second option is for " trusted supplier arrangements " , so that members who have passed two consecutive face-to-face audits with no corrective actions can then alternate between a desktop and face-to-face audit every three years . <p> Edwin says audit verification remains vital to ensure the integrity of SWNZ , but the new model recognises an often-raised concern that the one-size-fits-all approach is too blunt . <p> Instead , the Guardian working groups will address difficult issues in each area and promote peer-to-peer learning on sustainability best practice , says Edwin . <p> " There 'll also be a Sustainability Guardians Award to recognise an outstanding commitment to sustainability . " <p> Edwin says the programme will promote sustainability as a core value of the industry and provide members with an opportunity to be recognised for their efforts to go beyond sustainability baselines set by SWNZ . <p> " These efforts to go ' above and beyond ' help to protect the environment and our people , create opportunities for knowledge transfer between members , and enhance the reputation of the industry . " <p> Organophosphates <p> The 2020/21 growing season will be the last where organophosphates can be used on SWNZ certified vineyards . <p> Edwin says using these chemicals comes with significant health and environmental risks , as well as a reputational risk , as they have been banned in the EU . <p> " These risks , and the increasing availability of meaningful alternatives , highlight that the ongoing use of organophosphates is simply not sustainable .
