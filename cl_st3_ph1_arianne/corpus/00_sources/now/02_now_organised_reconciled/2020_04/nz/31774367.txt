<h> Why your next , next vehicle will likely be an EV <p> Words : Mark GIlbert <p> 12 Apr 2020 <p> Opponents to the rise of electric vehicles may be losing key arguments , with EVs silencing many critics in developed markets . The battle for consumers is being won and it 's likely that if not the next car purchase , your next buy thereafter will probably be electric . Opponents have been singing from a well used song sheet , so it might be time to look at how things are shaping up in the Electric Vehicle world . <p> EVs do n't suit my lifestyle <p> That 's true at present , but hold your horsepower , there 's a heap of models coming , from small cars to people-movers and everything in between . Around 40 new EV models are expected in the next year or so . <p> You ca n't get a decent utility EV <p> " How many fenceposts can you fit in a Leaf ? " Not many , but those claiming that only a Ford Ranger can satisfy their vehicle needs should look at the new models proposed by Ford , Great Wall and even Tesla -- vehicles that can definitely pack a punch and have very long ranges . And be prepared for brands you 've never heard of . Rivian , with its R1T , AWD , dual-cab pick-up , answers critics with 560kW of power and a range of about 645km from a 180kWh battery pack . Investors include Amazon and Ford . <p> Batteries are a Waste Problem <p> EV batteries are less of a problem and more of an opportunity with a growing market for second-hand batteries and I will cover this in more detail in a future article . But it 's worth noting that there are a lot of batteries being manufactured globally - not all of them related to cars . If you use lithium mining as an excuse not to buy an EV , then stop using your cellphone and laptop , because the materials for the batteries all come from the same place . <p> The Grid Ca n't Cope <p> New Zealand has plenty of renewable power , enough for electrification of the vehicle fleet . Recently , Mercury Energy expanded its wind farm , saying that its additional 27 turbines could " create enough energy to power 375,000 electric vehicles " . At that rate there 's more than enough renewable power to charge the fleet <p> Our Place in the World <p> The key point is this , Governments and cities are moving to reduce carbon emissions and the world 's leading car makers have to adapt by offering more EVs and fewer conventional models . Some places have signalled total bans on purely carbon-fueled vehicles , some from as early as 2025 , and that includes trucks , buses and ferries . Whether then , 2030 or 2035 , it 's not far away in terms of model life cycles . <p> This change is being driven by climate legislation in Europe - it 's not market-driven . It is the beginning of long term and permanent de-carbonisation of the global vehicle fleet . Most developed countries , including New Zealand , are committed to the Paris Agreement on Climate Change . Also , in New Zealand the " Zero Carbon by 2050 " bill has been passed , and an Interim Climate Change Commission has established reference groups to consider how this may be achieved . <p> In the automotive world , any OEM wishing to sell vehicles in Europe as of 1 January 2020 , has to achieve model range tail pipe emissions of around 95g/km . Hence the rise of the electric vehicle . <p> In short , the change is already coming , the supply chain is being transformed and eventually EVs will take the lead . <p> I encourage you to test drive an EV and see if its performance makes you smile ! <p> NZ Autocar is New Zealand 's leading automotive magazine . Delivering news and authoritative reviews from the car world each month , it includes commentary from NZ 's leading automotive writers and covers the full scope of motoring including new cars , fast cars , classics and motorcycles.
