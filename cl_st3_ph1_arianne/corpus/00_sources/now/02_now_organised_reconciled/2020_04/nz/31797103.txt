<h> Lockdown should cost nothing <p> Life loss , hunger , job loss , business loss , uncertainty of the future : quite a list . Governments around the world have rightly prioritised the first of these . Hence the lockdown . The second priority has not worked too well , here or anywhere else , as no country has the mechanism in place to do the simple thing : give people money . <p> Wage relief , tax relief , mortgage relief , rent controls , extra bank lending , extra welfare benefits . Our government has worked valiantly , brilliantly , to address all these , day after day , yet it remains a safety-net full of holes ; and still the people falling through tend to be those who missed out even when the economy was " normal " . <p> Job losses are a simple result of an economy that is poorly designed , or more accurately , hardly designed at all . It goes under the name of Free Enterprise , which is a laugh , as it works to make a maximum of citizens not free at all , but slaves to debt . Not long ago I was writing that a machine that can not be stopped is a bad design . The problem with lockdown has been that while much production stopped , " unproduction " refused to . Banks , and creditors in general , still want their interest , and even more of it , and landlords their rent . In other words , the winners of unearned profits failed to join the party -- the human race . If monetary parasitism stopped , then taking a break , a holiday , a lockdown , should cost nothing . Delays cost money only because of compound interest . <p> The " massive costs " of locking down are merely economic maldistribution . The real costs of the pandemic , notably the doubling of measurable hunger worldwide , are caused by the old economy . <p> Fortunately the religious commandment , Thou Shalt Not Print Money , has broken down , and there is blasphemous murmuring at all levels of Universal Basic Income ( UBI ) , along with nostalgic memories of the Ministry of Works , and its great instigator , the money-printing First Labour Government . <p> Columnist Gwynne Dyer has long been a gentle advocate of UBI , and last week he pointed out that something like it is happening as various countries react to Covid-19 . Spain is planning an ongoing minimum basic income , which is not quite universal , but is similarly guaranteed by the state . Dyer suggests it could be , apart from all the obvious benefits , an economic boost , as the poor will spend rather than hoard their income , and fiscally neutral , with savings in administration and other social costs . <p> More strongly , I suggest it is essential to a recession , especially a well planned one rather than this emergency , and climate change necessitates a recession . ( Ca n't resist that hissy phrase ! ) <p> Again fortunately , there is widespread recognition of essential industries . At last we can talk openly about the others as , in varying degrees , inessential . A lot of their value lies only in giving people incomes , which , as an emergency shows , is more than a bit inefficient . It leads to a lot of climate-toxic activity , a lot of inequality , and no resilience for the less fortunate . A UBI knocks those problems on the head . As with superannuation , those who do n't need it or appear to deserve it will repay it in taxes . <p> Ever since , decades ago , the Values Party dissolved and the Green Party sprang up in its place , I 've been a fan for the pumpkin model of progress : smash a pumpkin and seeds will germinate all over the place . The economy is a rotten pumpkin full of ripe seeds . Dare we start smiling ? <p> I think you have misquoted your reference to ' the sin of printing money ' Gavin . From my paltry knowledge of the classics , l seem to remember it is more likely to have had its origin from ' Polonius 's ( sp ) advice to his son ' than from any verse in the Bible . But then again , it is probably a mistake to question your authority on such matters .
