<p> " Our aim is to bring back taonga ( treasures ) that we have lost , like titi , kiwi and pekapeka , the native bats . <p> " These and other taonga have disappeared from our areas in a single human lifetime because of introduced animals . <p> " We want huge flocks of kereru and kaka again , and the death of ancient trees to stop . <p> " We want rongoa ( medicinal ) plants to return , we need to address deforestation and sedimentation that tears downstream and ends up in our coastal waters . <p> " This plan is iwi-led , jobs-rich and a ready-to-go proposal that will stimulate the local economy . <p> " In this time of Covid-19 economic downturn we need to look at localising our workforce and looking at projects through a climate change lens , ' she said . <p> " Recovery of life within Te Raukumara would allow the ngahere ( bush ) to thrive once more and turn a sick forest into a healthy carbon sink again . <p> " As trees killed by introduced possums and deer rot away , the forest goes from being a carbon sink to being a carbon emitter contributing to the climate emergency . " <p> Ora Barlow-Tukaki said East Coast iwi were already working with scientists at Niwa to set up research stations to see how much carbon dioxide is being lost as the forest dies . <p> This research will also monitor the carbon dioxide that is locked up as the forest becomes healthy again . <p> " We are ' shovel-ready ' and our aspirations are clear : we want iwi and carbon conscious development . Covid-19 could be something that stays with us for some time . We need projects that can help recover our national and local economy . The time is right for the Government to fully fund the Raukumara Pae Maunga Restoration Project , " she said . <p> The Raukumara encompasses 180,000 hectares of Maori land and is managed by DoC .
