<h> Acting for the collective good <p> Craig Bauld has been busy expressing opinions on how to handle Covid-19 better . He champions getting our country back to work . He said " . . . never let the ' experts ' dictate the narrative " and warned " Let climate change ' experts ' decide public policy and . . . see about 2 billion people die from starvation " . <p> It is obvious he does n't believe in the precautionary approach . <p> It is a shame the same urgency applied to Covid-19 could not have been enacted on the many decades of continuous warnings from the experts on climate change , that still await sufficient action . <p> Unlike me , Craig , now only 74 , would n't have witnessed the precautionary approach being enacted during World War 2 -- with travel restrictions , rationing to deal with shortages , etc . <p> My hopes have been boosted . This pandemic may be the wake-up call for humanity to take climate change seriously , and make the changes and sacrifices needed to face the coming consequences of our fossil-fuelled existence . <p> Last month , prior to the Covid-19 lockdown , I had a letter here directed at council policy : " Other emergencies come and go " ; pandemics , tsunami , earthquakes , severe storms , etc , are short-term events -- against the need to prioritise for worsening long-term climate change effects . I took the chance to again chastise our councillors for rejecting the call in December for a climate emergency declaration . <p> Climate change remains on the backburner here and globally , despite it already taking a tremendous toll on earth 's systems . <p> On April 10 , atmospheric CO2 recorded at the Mauna Loa Observatory in Hawaii reached a staggering 416.67 parts per million . <p> It was known 200 years ago , when CO2 levels were a mere 285ppm , that carbon dioxide was a greenhouse gas and was warming the planet . <p> Before last century humans had never experienced over 300ppm . The safe upper limit is seen to be 350ppm . <p> In my longer-than-expected lifetime , it has climbed from 308ppm to 416ppm . <p> The world 's handling of the coronavirus pandemic -- an imminent threat spreading rapidly across the globe -- offers lessons to turn around the stark lack of global action on the climate crisis . <p> Kim Hill on Saturday , March 28 interviewed UK professor of atmospheric chemistry and Earth observation science Paul Monks on " How the Covid-19 pandemic is massively reducing air pollution " . He described it as having a bonus impact in reducing emissions by cleaning the air . <p> Kim needled Paul on his thoughts around the McPherson paradox of the aerosol effect in reducing air pollution but increasing global warming , through the removal of the atmospheric umbrella ( or reflective particles ) . Paul acknowledged this was true but dismissed it as a bumpy road to recovery , then offered a vague explanation of the possible avoidance of long-term effects through a mixture of policies and twisting of knobs . He made no mention of how long the extra warming might last . I was totally unconvinced . <p> Another hard truth : today 's extremely high CO2/greenhouse gas levels are a result of last century 's excesses . <p> If it were possible to reduce our emissions immediately to net zero , it would take 40 years before our atmosphere reaped the benefits . <p> Finally , let this pandemic be our wake-up call to follow up with emergency measures on climate action . <p> Even though we have waited too long , we must act to show our youngsters we really care -- and ensure them the best future possible . <p> So Jeremy , If you do n't let my view and published facts be shown in reply to Bob 's letter , how is the other side of the argument to be heard ? So I take it you are willing to spread alarmist ideals at this time when we least need it . So be it , I had heard !
