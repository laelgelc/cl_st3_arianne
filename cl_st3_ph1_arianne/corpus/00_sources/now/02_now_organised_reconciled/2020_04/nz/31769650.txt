<h> Let 's learn from this experience <p> A while back I watched a number of MPs make contributions to the " 20 weeks " Abortion Bill 's second reading in Parliament , that has since been passed into law . <p> Being allowed a " free " ( conscience ) vote on this controversial issue was all that was required to release our politicians from the shackles of party discipline and the result was a display of Parliament operating at its very best . <p> I was proud of every member who spoke , while I watched , irrespective of which side of the debate they supported . <p> It is such a shame that all legislation is not the result of such considered opinion based solely on what individuals believe to be a fair assessment of the facts . <p> Our parliamentary and local government systems have proved to be reasonably effective in providing a vehicle that allows the nation to function in a democratic fashion . However , it could be so much better if our elected representatives ' first response was to consider the interests of all the people rather than just those whose special interests they represent . <p> Why is it that , for the most part , our laws are almost always formulated and passed along partisan party lines ? <p> It is unfortunate that humanitarian ideas that had their gestation as the result of injustices in the public system are either watered down to the point of being ineffective when passed into law , or fail to get the votes because they offend the precious feelings of relatively small but influential groups . <p> And it is n't as if the alternative to this charade is n't staring us in the face . <p> This nation is now in lockdown . <p> The government edicts around what we are told is best practice have been accepted by most Kiwis as necessary for the common good . <p> Dramatic measures are required in a national crisis and we expect our leaders to lead . So far , it appears that they are doing a pretty good job of achieving that . <p> But what happens when this coronavirus crisis is over ? Will we have learned how it is possible to do things differently when the country 's inhabitants are under extreme threat ? <p> My concern is that there is likely to be a return to business as usual , with a lapsing back into the same old ways of operating . It does n't have to be that way and there are good reasons why it should n't be . <p> Think about this : When the Government announces that this current threat is over , I suspect the public desire for a return to normalcy will be such that any talk of focusing with equal determination on the remaining ( arguably much more important ) threat to our survival , ie climate change , will be put on the back-burner . <p> We simply ca n't allow that to happen . In fact , it would be totally irresponsible if we did . <p> We need to find ways of dealing with climate change that fit our local environment . <p> A bipartisan approach to reducing emissions without cutting the economic throat of the nation is required immediately . <p> It is perfectly possible for our nation to emerge from this experience with a new resolve to reach political decisions in a climate ( excuse the pun ) that is devoid of the partisan , self-serving manner of the past . Our leaders should be able to look at our local situation in a way that has rarely happened before . <p> If we do so we will prove that , like the hardening of fine steel having gone through the firing process , we are better for the experience .
