<p> They include the possible establishment of a southern hemisphere climatorium in Nelson - a forum for education and development of projects to address climate change . <p> In 2018 , a delegation from Nelson went to Lemvig in Denmark to explore how others were tackling climate change adaptation . <p> A climatorium is currently under construction in Lemvig , and its purpose is to collect information about the climate and water . It will also serve as a shared space where companies , research institutions , educators and the general public can work on finding solutions to climate challenges . <p> The first stage of the Danish climatorium is scheduled to be completed in August this year . <p> The city council and Wakatu Incorporation hosted a return delegation from Denmark last November , to discuss how the different parties could work together on climate change initiatives . <p> During this visit , it was proposed that the parties sign a document that outlined their objectives in continuing to work together . <p> It agreed almost unanimously at a special meeting yesterday to set the terms of collaboration , which will pave the way for access to years of research the Danes have done on adapting to climate change . <p> " What a week for potential of climate action , following on from the launch of our climate forum , O'Neill-Stevens said . <p> " We 're leading with a whole-of-community approach , which is so important to climate action . " <p> He said the principles of the agreement highlighted that climate change challenges had no border , and the fight belonged to the international community . <p> " My hope for this is that we can develop a dynamic relationship , and a relationship that pushes us to do better . " <p> Councillor Tim Skinner was the only opponent to signing the document . He questioned its merits beyond Nelson becoming " penpals with Denmark " , or whether it might commit ratepayers to hefty costs in the future . <p> Skinner said not enough was known about what a climatorium might look like , whether or not it might attract government funding and whether it might cross into territory already covered by the likes of NIWA and Nelson 's science research institute , the Cawthron . <p> Councillor Judene Edgar said it was a valuable opportunity to benefit from years of intellectual knowledge gathered by Danish authorities . <p> " It gives us the access to years of research and investigation that led to Denmark being able to establish their own climatorium . " <p> A council staff report said Denmark consisted of mainly of flat lands with little elevation , apart from the hilly central area on the Jutland peninsula . The average height above sea level was 31m and the highest natural point was 170m . <p> Its lowest point was 7m below sea level , and nowhere in the country was further than 52km from the coast . <p> Senior council staff said the Principles of Collaboration , signed yesterday , did not commit the city to any spending and neither would it add to staff workloads . <p> Chief executive Pat Dougherty said any good ideas that might arise would be channelled through the normal long-term plan budgeting process . <p> A team from Denmark was due to arrive in Nelson next week , but the trip has been postponed due to the Covid-19 outbreak , which is affecting Denmark and New Zealand .
