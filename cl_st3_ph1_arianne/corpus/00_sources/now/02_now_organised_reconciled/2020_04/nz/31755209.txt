The impact of COVID-19 and the lockdown of the country will , ... <p> Auckland Mayor Phil Goff says the city is ready and enthusiastic about promoting infrastructure projects to assist the country to recover quickly from the COVID-19 induced recession . <p> " The impact of COVID-19 and the lockdown of the country will , as elsewhere in the world , have a dramatically negative impact on jobs and business , " he says . <p> " As was the case in the 1930s in New Zealand , infrastructure projects with long-term benefits for the country can be used to stimulate the economy with a multiplier effect for job recovery . With a backlog of infrastructure projects which lagged behind and have constricted growth in Auckland , we can provide Ministers with projects that can get off the ground quickly . <p> " There will need to be a spread of projects across the country and Auckland will be just looking for a fair share of those projects , reflecting the 34 per cent of the country 's population and 38 per cent of GDP that the city represents . <p> " We will provide a smorgasbord of projects that can start quickly and which align with the government 's economic , social and environmental objectives . <p> " Council officers will begin immediately to draw up a range of proposals , with input from Ward Councillors and Local Boards , " says Phil Goff . <p> The proposals could include the following : <p> Developing a rapid transit system to the North West <p> Funding light rail from the City Centre to Mt Roskill and Mangere <p> A fourth main trunk rail line constructed at the same time as the third to save costs <p> Increasing NZTA funding proportions for projects such as the Matakana Link Road , which could start immediately , and the second and third stages of the Eastern Busway to Botany . <p> Sped up construction of cycleways which could start immediately <p> Bringing forward roading renewals currently at the back of the 10-year Auckland Transport Alignment Project ( ATAP ) <p> Creating a charging network and bus depots to accelerate electrification of the city 's bus fleet <p> Funding local roads in new housing developments such as Drury <p> Building infrastructure to allow intensive residential and commercial precincts around the new City Rail Link stations at Mt Eden , Karangahape Rd and Aotea Centre <p> Funding underground fresh , waste and stormwater infrastructure in regeneration programmes such as Tamaki , and new developments such as the Unitec site <p> Bringing forward separation of wastewater from stormwater to clean up Auckland 's harbours <p> Cultural and community amenities such as building a Pasifika and Maori cultural centre at Manukau , Te Papa North . <p> Deputy Mayor Bill Cashmore says , " Planning for beyond COVID-19 and its impact on New Zealand is critical . It is a positive step that the government is looking at projects that will be or are shovel ready . <p> " Auckland is ready and able to present to the government , in short order , a list of key infrastructure projects that are ready to go and whose delivery will add positively to employment and dwelling builds , and act as a primary catalyst to the economic rebuild we are so obviously going to need . " <p> Councillor and Chair of the Planning Committee , Chris Darby , says , " The government 's infrastructure investment strategy presents a unique opportunity to not just reboot the economy but reset and reposition the economy to meet the massive global shift taking place on many fronts . <p> " The arrival of Covid-19 combined with climate change readiness are ingredients for powering big leaps in Auckland 's infrastructure , land use planning and economy , to drive the global flight to quality over quantity . Auckland can lead this for the benefit of the nation . "
