<h> Flood prevention could bring ' huge ' payoff as global risks rise <p> Pakistani farmers head towards the province of Balochistan , as they flee from the floods in Sindh province . The country is one of the world 's most vulnerable to the impacts of climate change . Image : IRIN Photos , CC BY-SA 2.0 via IFPRI Flickr <h> Share <p> Asia faces surging threats from coastal and river flooding as climate change brings more intense rainfall and rising seas - but investing now in dikes and other measures to stem risks could have big financial payoffs , researchers said on Thursday . <p> India , for instance , could save about $250 per $1 spent on protection to lessen the average frequency of floods from every 11 years to every 25 years by 2050 , said Betsy Otto , who directs the global water programme at the World Resources Institute . <p> Bangladesh , similarly , could save nearly $125 in damages for each dollar spent on dikes to reduce the likelihood of flooding from once every three years to once a decade by mid-century . <p> If used around the world , such measures could help cut what charity Christian Aid estimated was a global flood-damage bill of close to $46 billion last year . <p> " It 's a huge return , and it creates jobs and adds to local economies , " said Otto , who led development of Aqueduct , a global online water risk assessment and mapping tool . <p> Since 1980 , flooding has cost the world more than $1 trillion in losses , according to insurer Munich RE , and threats are increasing as population growth , migration and new construction put more people and assets at risk . <p> Aqueduct Floods data shows that by 2030 , 132 million people and urban property valued at $535 billion are likely to be hit annually by river flooding . <p> India , Bangladesh and Pakistan are likely to see the biggest number of people affected , while India , China and Democratic Republic of Congo could see the largest losses to urban infrastructure . <p> On coastlines , 15 million people and $177 billion in urban infrastructure could be affected by floods each year by 2030 . <p> Bangladesh , Vietnam , Indonesia and India are likely to have the most flood-affected coastal residents , while China , Indonesia and the United States could see the biggest property losses in cities , the data indicated . <p> " The numbers are sobering when you look at what the impacts are going to be , " she told the Thomson Reuters Foundation . <p> The United States is far from the only rich country likely to be hit hard , the tool indicated , with the Netherlands and Germany also set to see fast-rising losses from flood damage . <p> " This is n't just about countries that are poorer and have n't been able to make infrastructure investments . Richer countries with really valuable infrastructure also need to be taking action , " Otto said . <p> The new data comes as the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration predicted heavy flooding in parts of the eastern United States this spring , after record-length floods along the Mississippi River last year . <p> Marc Bierkens , a hydrology professor at Utrecht University in the Netherlands , which helped create Aqueduct Floods , said the new tool provided insights into how investments in flood defences could save lives and avert economic disaster . <p> " Economic damages from floods are already formidable under the current climate and will grow tremendously in the future as a result of climate change and socioeconomic development , " he warned . <p> " Flooding adds to the existing challenges we 're already reeling from , " she said . " Flood protection needs to be a priority investment .
