<h> " Environment Minister dismisses analyst " <p> BRASILIA -- Brazil 's Environment Minister Ricardo Salles has dismissed a government analyst who opposed relaxing environmental review of wood exports , an official notice said on Monday , after thousands of shipments from the Amazon bypassed required approvals over the past year . <p> Reuters reported last month that Brazil had exported thousands of shipments of wood from an Amazonian port over the past year without authorisation from the federal environment agency Ibama . <p> After the issue was discovered , amid heightened controversy over deforestation of the Amazon rainforest under far-right President Jair Bolsonaro , Ibama 's chief rescinded a rule requiring the agency authorise all wood cargoes . <p> Lifting the rule flew in the face of a group of analysts led by Andre Socrates de Almeida Teixeira , coordinator-general for monitoring biodiversity use and foreign trade , who had pushed for it to be upheld . <p> According to notices in the official gazette , Salles removed Teixeira from his post and replaced him with Rafael Freire de Mac ? do , who was already working in a related area . <p> Teixeira declined to comment and Mac ? do did not immediately respond to request for comment . <p> Ibama said it was a routine change made within applicable rules , without elaborating . The environment ministry did not respond to request for comment . <p> A source familiar with the matter told Reuters that Teixeira 's firing was in retaliation for his dissent . <p> " It was more of a political change to appoint someone who is more flexible ... to ease controls and facilitate wood exports , " the source said . <p> Ibama had previously stated that wood cargoes must get export clearance from the federal Revenue Service , which is only granted after cross referencing with the country 's system for overseeing wood to verify that it is of legitimate origin . <p> As such , Ibama is still able to make spot inspections of wood cargoes bound for export . <p> The Amazon is the world 's largest rainforest and its preservation is seen as vital to curbing climate change because of the vast amount of greenhouse gas that it absorbs . <p> Destruction of the Amazon surged last year , provoking a global outcry , with some foreign leaders and environmentalists blaming the policies of Bolsonaro for emboldening illegal loggers , ranchers and land speculators . <p> Bolsonaro says poverty in the Amazon is to blame and that he has been unfairly demonised . ( Reuters )
