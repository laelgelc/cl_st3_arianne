<h> Fifa boss Infantino says game will be more inclusive , ex-Bayern chief believes spending will be curbed <p> Football has ground to a halt due to the coronavirus pandemic , and the immediate concern is the simple survival of many clubs because of the financial impact , but there is hope that the global game could ultimately emerge better from this crisis . <p> " We are living through something none of us were used to and which will change us profoundly , " Everton manager Carlo Ancelotti told Corriere dello Sport . <p> Not since World War II has the sport been forced to stop across Europe . The sudden interruption has exposed the deficiencies of a system intoxicated by huge sums of money . <p> Cutbacks are inevitable in the short term . <p> " TV money will go down , players and coaches will earn less . Tickets will cost less because people will have less money . The economy will be different and so will football . Maybe it will be better , " said Ancelotti . <p> " As with most things , crisis is an opportunity , " football historian and academic David Goldblatt , author of recent book The Age of Football , told AFP , before sounding a warning . <h> Related Stories <p> " It could actually get worse . For there to be real change , there has to be a change in the way power and ownership is distributed in the game . " <p> At the moment , the financial power belongs to the lucky few at the top , but even they are being hurt . <p> That is likely to affect the transfer market , and huge spending sprees on players could become a thing of the past . <p> " In two or three years , it will not be possible to spend the sums we have been seeing because every country will be affected . In all likelihood , a new footballing world will emerge from this , " insisted former Bayern Munich president Uli Hoeness . <h> LESSON <p> Fifa president Gianni Infantino also believes that football will be different after the pandemic is over . <p> " Football will come back , and when it does , we 'll celebrate coming out of a nightmare together , " Infantino told Italian news agency Ansa in an interview yesterday . <p> " There is one lesson , however , that both you and me must have understood : The football that will come after the virus will be totally different ... ( more ) inclusive , more social and more supportive , connected to the individual countries and at the same time more global , less arrogant and more welcoming . " <p> He added : " We will be better , more human and more attentive to true values . " <p> Such values were seen over the past week when players at Barcelona - the richest club in the world - agreed to a 70 per cent pay cut . Several clubs across Europe are taking similar measures . <p> It is evidence that clubs , even among the elite , have been living on the edge . It raises the question of whether salary caps could finally be seen as a way forward , despite the difficulties presented by European Union rules . <p> In Germany , the Bundesliga 's four Champions League representatives this season have pledged 20 million euros ( S$31.4m ) to help crisis-hit clubs in an encouraging sign of solidarity . <p> Meanwhile , lessons may also be learnt about how TV revenue is distributed in future . <p> " It is now high time that we find some rules to say OK , let 's get out of this crisis as well as we can , but let 's also put safeguards in that manage player loads successfully moving forward , " warned Jonas Baer-Hoffmann , general secretary of global players ' union Fifpro , as he called for " a much healthier set-up than what we have had lately " . <p> The way forward , as Infantino suggested to La Gazzetta dello Sport last week , would be to reform world football with fewer tournaments . <p> Hence , it may be timely to rework the fixture calendar . <p> The fashion for expanding existing tournaments - like staging a 48-team World Cup and 24-team Club World Cup - is surely not sustainable . <p> Goldblatt believes Fifa needs to review plans to stage the 48-team World Cup in 2026 all across North America . <p> That , and the European Championship that Uefa intend to stage in 12 cities across the continent , are being planned in ways which appear at odds with the need to face up to another imminent threat : climate change . <p> " If we have learnt anything from the last couple of months , it is that we should listen to the scientists , " Goldblatt says . <p> " We need to hit the pause button on all of this and have a massive rethink . " - AFP
