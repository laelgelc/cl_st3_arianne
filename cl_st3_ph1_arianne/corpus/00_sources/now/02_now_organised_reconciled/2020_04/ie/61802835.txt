<h> COVID-19 lifestyle changes can help to repair our climate -- but will they continue once the outbreak is over ? <p> CO2 emissions are down by 40-60% due to the COVID-19 crisis . Picture : Supplied <p> Archant <h> We are in the grip of the COVID-19 crisis . It is restricting us in all areas of life , and causes so much suffering . But we know it will pass and afterwards we will reorient ourselves . Other topics will soon re-emerge , one of which will certainly be climate change . <p> How will we look back and reflect ? Here are a few statistics on our carbon footprints , and what effect the current restrictions have on our emissions . <p> Taking into account UK production and import of goods , the average person in the UK produces 12 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalents per year . To gradually achieve the UK 2050 climate change targets , this year the average should be 10.5 tonnes . <p> The current reduced movement and air travel is reducing humanity 's carbon footprint massively . For example , not holidaying in the Canary Islands saves a massive 2 tonnes of CO2 equivalents per person ! Not driving a car for two months saves over half a tonne ( more for 4-wheel drives ) . <h> You may also want to watch : <p> Many families are now happily walking or cycling together , with many people working from home . When social distancing ends many of us will remember the lovely quiet roads and hopefully choose to travel less . It will be good to be with colleagues again , but we may find we can continue with home working . <p> How about food ? This accounts for up to three tonnes of CO2e per person per year , but can be lowered when food is not wasted , and by eating less meat , fish , processed , or air freighted goods . <p> In the current climate , it is more of an effort to get food , and many of us will find we 're wasting as little food as possible . Life skills are also developing in terms of creative cooking , freezing or sharing food which is close to its sell-buy date . <p> Finally , each of us produces between two and five tonnes of CO2e per year just by buying ' stuff ' . Yes , we are still buying online , but as many shops are closed we are thinking twice about what we need , and we are overall consuming less -- which reduces our carbon footprint . <p> For example , once you have used up your liquid handwash , why not opt for a soap bar , which requires less energy needed for transport and no plastic pollution ? A small change , but clean hands with minimum harm for the planet ! <p> So , how about reflecting on the changes you have had to make in this crisis , and why not explore if you can happily build them into your life when this outbreak is behind us . <p> If you value what this story gives you , please consider supporting the Comet .
