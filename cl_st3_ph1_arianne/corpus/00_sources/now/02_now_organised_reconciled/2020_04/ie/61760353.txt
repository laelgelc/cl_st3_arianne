<p> In Mark O'Connell 's new book , ' Notes from an Apocalypse ' , he explores the fears and aspirations of so-called ' doomsday preppers ' around the world . <p> All the people who spoke to the Irish author are convinced the end is well and truly nigh and they all have various predictions of how it will transpire . <p> From nuclear war to climate change to - appropriately for the times we live in - a pandemic , they run the gamut of apocalyptic wishful thinking . <p> We 're obviously not on the verge of an apocalypse , no matter how bad this current situation appears to be , but one of the interesting aspects of O'Connell 's book is the profound lack of faith in government and politicians . <p> That 's understandable - predictable , in fact - if you 're a doomsday prepper in Utah who thinks that being forced to pay any tax at all is an outrageous example of government tyranny . <p> But from our hopefully more rational perspective , this country simply can not continue to act against Covid-19 without a fully formed and legitimate new government . <p> It 's now a matter of months , not weeks , since the February election and what seemed a pressing issue back in those more innocent times has now become a matter of national urgency . <p> More talks about forming the new administration are continuing this week and while there is certainly a reasonable argument that the pandemic simply wiped everything else off the agenda , we should all cross every digit we have in the hope that the two main parties will finally wake up to the new reality and simply get the business done . <p> Unfortunately for us , it appears that some of the players are still stuck in the mud of the old politics when everyone else in the country has realised that we are now in truly unprecedented times - and it 's a fool 's errand trying to predict when , or even if , things will return to the normality we were used to . <p> It was disappointing , but not surprising , to hear Fianna F ? il 's ? amon ? Discussing that possibility , which still looks like the most inevitable outcome , he argued that : " The options have been artificially narrowed to present us with this choice of this government only , but the reality is that there 's a great doubt if this could be put together ... you would need the approval of the membership of Fianna F ? il . I ca n't tell you which way they would vote . <p> " All I know is that there is serious disquiet within the party in relation to entering into a four- to five-year arrangement with the Fine Gael party . " <p> It 's astonishing , really , that such an utterly outdated and obdurate mentality still exists within our political caste . <p> The world has changed immensely since the grim arrival of Covid-19 . Some countries are faring better than others , but nobody is faring well - and they are countries with functioning governments with the powers to actually pass laws , a constitutional facility now beyond our caretaker Government which , until the next Taoiseach is formally appointed , remains toothless . <p> It should be acknowledged that this sitting Government has done a reasonable job of handling the crisis so far . <p> Certainly , it reacted quicker than either the US or the UK and , for once , it also seemed like we were n't playing catch-up with our EU neighbours . <p> That would n't have been possible without the tacit consent of the people , who deserve far more credit than our dithering TDs . <p> A good example of the current change in the mindset of the population was the recent round of public applause for those healthcare workers who risk their safety every time they simply go into work . <p> What would ordinarily have appeared as a rather cheesy piece of tokenism and virtue signalling was actually a public outpouring of genuine support and gratitude towards people who start every shift knowing it is now quite literally a matter of life or death . <p> We have , by and large , all tried to do our bit - despite the cognitive dissonance this entails . <p> After all , during a ' regular ' crisis , we 're instructed to call into our neighbours , to pop into the elderly for a cup of tea and a chat , to remind them that they 're not alone . <p> We 're also usually instructed to get as much exercise and fresh air as possible . <p> The very things we are normally urged to do are now precisely the worst things we can do . <p> We can suck that up for the moment , but the reality is that next Sunday 's expiry date for the lockdown is going to be extended and we are now looking at another three weeks - minimum - of enforced isolation . <p> This is yet another reason why we so desperately require a proper government . <p> Apart from the catastrophic death toll in Spain and Italy , the social problems caused by their lockdowns have led to unrest and a widening division between the police , the politicians and the people . <p> That fracturing of the always fragile social cohesion is even worse in the UK , where a toxic combination of overzealous policing and people ringing hotlines to inform on their neighbours for breaking the guidelines has given rise to the phrase ' Covid Stasi ' . <p> There 's no doubt that some people will always be idiots who think only of themselves , and the images of large gatherings of people holding ' Covid parties ' are enough to make anyone despair . <p> But while nobody would quibble with such profoundly selfish types having their collars felt by the police , there was fury amongst ordinary Londoners who obeyed the social-distancing rules while sunbathing at the weekend but were still accosted by the authorities . <p> These are the subtle fault lines that can be understandably overlooked in the face of a pandemic . <p> But humans are social animals and they can only spend so long in isolation before they begin to lose both their minds and their respect for the guidelines . <p> We have an opportunity to learn from the mistakes made by our neighbours , but that can only be done with an actual government which can issue actual advice and pass actual laws . <p> With very few exceptions , the ordinary people of this country have stepped up to the plate and done what the politicians asked of them . <p> Now it is time for them to return the favour and do the one thing we are asking of them - put your country before your party , stop squabbling and form a new government .
