<p> FIA Formula E chairman Alejandro Agag has warned that many motorsport series around the world are at risk due to the current coronavirus crisis . <p> Agag added that the industry will have to consolidate around a much smaller number of categories , but he argues that there are some fundamental reasons why ABB FIA Formula E will emerge from the current crisis in good shape . <p> Speaking exclusively to Autosport for our #thinkingforward series with motorsport leaders , the FE chairman struck an optimistic note about the scale of opportunity that faces the industry , despite the severe challenges caused by the temporary cancellation of all events . <p> But it will take some bold decisions , and some pain to make the most of the opportunity - starting with Formula 1 at the pinnacle of the sport . <p> " We are going to see a lot of consolidation in the motorsport industry and I think it will now happen by necessity , because some series are not going to survive on their own , " said Agag . <p> " Already some series were struggling , there were too many series . Motorsport is here to stay , it 's not going anywhere . We need to know that this is not the end of the world . <p> " There is going to be a tomorrow and it is going to be different . We have to prepare for that . <p> " But maybe it does n't need 35 different series around the world . Maybe with .. six or seven series around the world it 's enough to cover the needs of the fans . <p> " Because at the end , motorsport is a service to the fans and a laboratory for technology , but that part can be done with a small number of series . " <p> Agag picked up on the theme raised last week by FIA president Jean Todt that motorsport needs a ' new deal ' to recalibrate not just the cost of competing in the sport , but the whole approach to motorsport 's place in society . <p> " I saw Jean Todt 's interview of course and I agree with him ; I think we need a new deal . <p> " Generally for motorsport this could be an opportunity . And specifically for Formula 1 this could be a massive opportunity ; to restructure the whole model , and I think that is the key for the new deal . <p> " The rest of us will follow and we are smaller . The pinnacle of motorsport is F1 and that 's where the big imbalance exists . We will do our little new deal at the Formula E level , everyone is on board and aligned together with FIA on it . <p> " For the rest of motorsport it depends ; some will do well , some will do less well . Spectator sport is going to suffer . <p> " I think that we are going to see restrictions on mass events , I do n't know for how long they will last but for those sports that are based on ticketing revenue I think that can be a challenge . " <p> While this last point is causing sleepless nights for many series and event promoters , it is one of the reasons why Agag believes that Formula E is able to weather the crisis , while recognising that " if we had seen this problem in our first season it probably would have killed us . " <p> " We acted very fast in a flexible way , we took measures very early , we cancelled races very early and we are taking measures to protect the teams and the ecosystem . We are in very good shape . <p> " We have a business model that is resistant to these kinds of circumstances ; we do n't rely too much on ticketing revenue for example . <p> " Then you have the teams and you need to protect them and cut costs now , because now is when they are having their main problem . <p> " For that we decided to allow only one ( new car ) homologation for seasons 7 &amp; 8 . So a team can choose to use the exact same car they are using for season 6 again for season 7 and then they will make a saving right now . <p> " But also if they had already made part of the spending on the season 7 car they can use that one , but they will have to use it again in season 8 , so they save the money then . <p> " Formula E is going to emerge well ; it is at a level of costs that is manageable , the business model is manageable . <p> " I can send a message to the whole Formula E community that it is going to be in good shape . <p> " It wo n't be exactly the same , we have to cut costs and be smart . " <p> Just as people afflicted with the coronavirus who have underlying health issues have struggled , so it is with across many industries and racing is no exception . <p> Along with consolidation of racing categories , there were a number of other trends that were coming anyway , but which now the current crisis and the reflections on it will accelerate . <p> One of these , Agag argues , will be the drive towards sustainable vehicles and electrification . <p> " I think that this crisis is going to accelerate the trends , " he says . " The big lesson I see from this crisis is the absolute lack of long term planning of our societies . <p> " We do n't plan forward . <p> " I was reading an article about the Bubonic plague of 541AD . And they were in the same situation we are in today ; they had to stay in their home for a lockdown , the economy collapsed . <p> " And that was 1500 years ago ! Exactly the same and we have n't learned any lessons or planned forward . <p> " There are many threats to humanity but climate change is the biggest and most obvious one and this gives a lot more reason to plan ahead against climate change , which is going to provoke something even worse than we are living today if we do n't plan ahead . <p> " And I think this makes the case of electric vehicles more relevant and stronger than ever .
