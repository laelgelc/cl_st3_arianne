<h> " an instant book about how contagion spread in Italy " <p> I liked his take on how , during lockdown , we yearn to get back our normal lives . " All of a sudden , normality is the most sacred thing we have -- even though we had never given it so much importance before , even though we do n't actually know what it is . We just know we want it back . " <p> That insight is an example of how , on a handful of occasions , Giordano 's short book takes concepts that have been dancing away in our minds , just out of reach , and lines them up neatly . <p> He puts an ecological spin on the virus by claiming how devastating events such as last year 's Amazon fire may have set new bugs free . " Micro-organisms that science has never even named might soon be in need of a new home . And we are the perfect breeding ground : there are so many of us -- and there will be so many more . " <h> Reviews <h> " It is modest , lucid , calm , informed , directly helpful in trying to think about where we are now . " <p> He also widens out the argument to tell us that we are ourselves to blame for the contagion because of our aggressive behaviour towards the environment ( climate change , deforestation , urbanisation , extinction of species , intensive farming ) , bringing us into contact with pathogens which were until then confined to their natural niches . " Viruses are among the many refugees of environmental destruction , " he states , and we are the perfect breeding ground for them , so numerous , so susceptible , so connected , travelling so much . There will be other outbreaks .
