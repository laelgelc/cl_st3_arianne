<p> The threat of climate change and biodiversity loss requires " urgent action " akin to the " commitment , cooperation and ambition " that has been undertaken during the Covid-19 pandemic . <p> This message is outlined by Green Party leader Eamon Ryan in his party 's response to the Fianna F ? il and Fine Gael 's joint framework document for a potential government formation . <p> The letter poses 17 questions -- under a number of headings -- that the party is seeking clarity on before it will commit to taking part in any formal government formation negotiations . <p> A number of key points in relation to agriculture are included . <h> Emissions and land use <p> The first pre-condition set out in the letter asks the main party leaders if they will commit to an average annual reduction in greenhouse gas ( GHG ) emissions of at least 7% ? <p> It is stated that any potential government formation involving the Green Party will need to set out " clear and ambitious " targets and timetables in relation to the reduction of carbon emissions -- which , the party contends , will require " enormous changes " in the country 's approach to capital expenditure on transport , energy systems and its agricultural practices . <p> The target set out in January 2020 by the Joint Oireachtas Committee on Climate Action of a minimum targeted reduction of 7%+ per annum , in accordance with the best available scientific advice and our commitments under the Paris Agreement , should be adhered to and exceeded . <p> The letter also outlines how any potential government formation involving the Green Party will need to undertake radical and ambitious action on biodiversity loss and the collapse of ecosystems . <p> It states : " How we manage our agricultural lands , what foods we produce , where and what trees we plant , and which areas we re-wet and re-wild -- these will all determine the future fortunes of our biodiversity . <p> " The integration of our farming , rural and coastal communities into this new landscape will be vital as we embark on an ambitious programme to regenerate rural Ireland . <p> A significant reform of Irish agri-food policy is needed , not only to meeting our emissions targets and to improve our natural environment , but to help build resilient rural communities . <p> Ryan goes on to contend that in order to meet both the country 's climate and biodiversity objectives " a national land use plan " will need to be established . <p> Advertisement <p> He writes : " Such a plan will set out how rural communities can thrive in conjunction with the restoration of biodiversity , a reduction in emissions , improved water quality and flood protection , lower nitrogen and ammonia levels , a switch to closer-to-nature ' continuous cover ' forestry and rebuilding a vibrant family farming sector . <p> " A Citizens ' Assembly on Biodiversity is a welcome initiative and would inform the development of such a land use plan . <p> While we recognise the commitment in the joint framework document to increasing tree planting , we feel this falls short of what is necessary . <p> " In expanding our afforestation ambitions , we must also ensure that we not only support the forestry sector , but at the same time respect the wishes of our communities , and factor in the ecological and amenity value of our woodlands , by planting the right trees in the right places . " <h> Awaiting Response <p> In closing , the Green Party leader states that " we believe the next 10 years are critical " if we are to address the climate and biodiversity crisis " which threatens our safety , security and health " . <p> " The threat of climate change and biodiversity loss requires urgent action with the same commitment , cooperation and ambition as any undertaken during the Covid-19 health crisis , " the letter concludes . <p> The Green Party is currently awaiting a response from the leaders of Fine Gael and Fianna F ? il .
