<p> The world as we know it is melting , and uncovering hidden treasures that were away from our sights for hundreds of years . Recently , retreating ice in Norway , which is melting because of climate change , revealed a lost Viking-era mountain pass with scattered artifacts - and it all started with a 1,800-year-old shirt . <p> Originally , the pass at Lendbreen in Norway 's mountains attracted the attention of local archaeologists in 2011 upon discovery of a woolen tunic that belonged to the third or fourth century AD . <p> Unfortunately , the ice has melted significantly since then , now revealing numerous artifacts including knitted mittens , leather shoes , and arrows with their feathers attached , dating back nearly 2,000 years , National Geographic reports . <h> 1,000 artifacts scattered among the pass <p> Upon finding the items , the researchers used carbon dating to pinpoint when each finding is from . They found that the majority of the items belong to the Viking-era in 1000 AD , where trade in the region was at its highest . <p> The archeologists examined the area and found horse snowshoes , remains of packhorses and their dung piles , a walking stick with a runic inscription , a knife , a wood distaff used to hold wool during hand-spinning , and parts of sleds . <p> A wooden bit for a goat kid or lamb , meant to stop them from suckling their mother , radiocarbon-dated to the 11th century AD , Source : Espen Finstad/secretsoftheice <p> Moreover , they also dug up shoes , knitted mittens , and numerous remains of clothing . This video shows the moment the Viking Age mitten was found . <p> Some items could n't be classified since they did n't have modern equivalents . <p> Their findings are truly stunning , and some of them look almost like they were left there a short time ago instead of centuries . <h> Amid climate change , archaeology is booming <p> While this discovery comes as a dream-come-true for archaeologists , it is also a poignant and evocative reminder of climate change . <p> Lars Pil ? , who leads the Glacier Archaeology Program in Oppland , Norway , says , " Global warming is leading to the melting of mountain ice worldwide , and the finds melting out of the ice are a result of this .
