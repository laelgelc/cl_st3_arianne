To learn more see our Cookies Policy . <h> We should debate Thunberg 's ideas but comments that focus on the symptoms of her condition are out of order , writes Shane Dunphy . <p> MY FIRST JOB in social care was working in a training unit for adults with intellectual disabilities . <p> I loved it . My boss was a social worker named John Carroll who was a bit controversial at the time . He had returned to Ireland in the early 1980s after working in the UK for years , determined to change the way people saw disability . <p> He firmly believed that , regardless of what label you place on a person , we are all inherently the same . <p> Language can become a way of fencing people in , of limiting them . In his unit , he referred to everyone , staff and clients alike , as co-workers . <p> John retired a decade ago , but I wonder what he would make of the recent treatment of environmental activist Greta Thunberg ? <p> Thunberg , who is 16 , is on the autistic spectrum , she has also been diagnosed with Obsessive Compulsive Disorder and selective mutism . <p> Well known for her campaign on climate change , Thunberg has been hugely visible on the world political stage recently and has garnered quite a bit of media attention -- not all of it positive . <p> What surprises me is how much of the commentary has negatively focused on the symptoms of her various conditions . <p> Journalist Brendan O'Neill , writing in the online magazine Spiked , branded her a ' weirdo ' and wrote about her ' monotone voice ' , suggesting her face was always locked in an expression of ' apocalyptic dread ' . <p> Australian writer Helen Dale took to Twitter to call on the BBC to arrange to have Thunberg interviewed by tough journalist Andrew Neil in the hopes she would have a meltdown on air , " Because afterwards , I guarantee we 'll never hear from her again . " <p> To be clear , neither writer was commenting on Thunberg 's ideas . They were targeting the obvious , physical manifestations of her autism . <p> Can I remind you , this is a 16-year-old child ? <p> Neil Markham is a football fan and a proud Dad . Last week he posted a video of his daughter , Ella , who also happens to be 16 , dancing after a Tottenham Hotspur match . Ella lives with Down syndrome . <p> To Markham 's horror , a slew of abusive , derogatory and hugely offensive comments was posted in response to the video . <p> So many , in fact , that it made headlines both here and in the UK . The Markhams received widespread support from the public and many prominent members of the soccer world came out in support of them too . <p> At the time of writing these horrendous posts are still appearing on Neil Markham 's Twitter feed . <p> Once again , to be very clear , these tweets are not comments on Ella Markham 's dance moves or the team she supports . They are attacks and slurs on her because she lives with a genetic condition . And let me once again reiterate that this is a child . <p> Irish politician Michael O'Dowd has commented in the Irish papers this week on the abuse that his son , Conor , who also has Down syndrome , receives on both Facebook and Twitter . <p> What interests me in all of this is that the response to such behaviour seems comparatively muted . Any comment seen as even vaguely oppositional to issues of sexuality , gender or race receives a huge outcry . <p> One example is author John Boyne 's recent decision to shut down his Twitter account as an example of the force of collective opinion . <p> While in the case of the Markham 's there has been a significant backlash - often when it comes to attacks on those with a disability the outcry is not as pronounced as it should be . <p> I do n't want to suggest people with special needs should be wrapped up in cotton wool . I believe Greta Thunberg should be debated -- on her ideas -- and I think Ella Markham and her Dad should be able to hold their own in a discussion on football . <p> I just do n't believe anyone should be attacked for being who they are . <p> John Carroll was right -- those with disabilities should be held to account just the same as everybody else and I 'll provide you with an anecdote to illustrate what I mean . <p> I had probably been working at the unit for about three months when I had a career-changing experience . One of the people who attended the centre was a young man in his late 20s who ( like Ella Markham and Conor O'Dowd ) lived with Down syndrome . For the sake of this article , I 'll call him James . <p> James was usually boisterous and enthused by everything we did , but on this particular day , he was in an uncharacteristically bad mood . He spent the afternoon being obstructive , refusing to engage with any of the activities he would usually participate in and making things difficult for those who did . <p> Down Syndrome , which is also known as trisomy 21 , is a genetic disorder caused by an extra copy of chromosome 21 . It is characterised by physical and developmental delays , distinct facial features and usually carries mild to moderate intellectual disabilities ( although individuals with Down syndrome can have normal intellectual functioning ) . <p> James was very capable but we were aware that he at times struggled to control his emotional responses . <p> When it came time to go home , James stomped out without saying goodbye . He usually cycled to and from the unit , so we all assumed he had made good his escape and hoped he would be in a better frame of mind the following day . <p> I stayed late to do some paperwork and was locking up when I heard a scuffling behind me and turned to find James , his face like thunder . He was now sitting astride his bike , which he had parked right across the porch to our building , meaning I could not leave . <p> " Would you mind moving please , James ? " I asked , trying to sound calm and patient -- I was actually quite annoyed at this stage . He shook his head emphatically . <p> " If you 're trying to be a pain in the arse , James , you 're succeeding , " I said -- I 'd had a long day and was getting really fed up . " Now would you move your bike , please , so I can go home ? " <p> I did n't see the punch coming . It was well executed ( he put a lot of his weight behind it ) and hit me square on the jaw , knocking me back into the door with a heavy thud . <p> I was genuinely stunned , and when the world stopped spinning and I regained my feet , James was nothing but a rapidly pedalling speck on the horizon . <p> The following morning I sat down with John Carroll and told him what had happened . I told him that I felt I had completely mishandled the situation , and suggested that maybe I should be sent on some sort of crisis management training . Maybe I was n't cut out for social care ? <p> He listened patiently , and responded with something that has stayed with me ever since : " Shane , has it occurred to you that James was just being very rude and that he owes you an apology ? " <p> John never swore , but what he was saying was that James had been a bit of an asshole . And I realised that my boss was right -- and that changed how I saw what had happened , and how we should handle it . <p> When James came in later that day looking very guilty , John took him aside and made sure I received that apology , reminding the youngster that I would have been within my rights to press charges . <p> It was clear from the conversation that James was going through his own personal difficulties ( hence his unusually dark mood ) but that did n't excuse his behaviour . <p> The words Down syndrome were never mentioned , and nor should they have been . We discussed the action and the reasonable response to that action . Because what was significant was the act and the thought process behind it . The fact that James lived with a disability was inconsequential . <p> In my career in social care , in my time teaching college and in the world of writing and publishing , I have encountered people of all shapes , sizes , races and creeds , at all levels of physical ability and disability . <p> Some have behaved well , some have behaved badly . Some have been wonderful , inspirational people and some have been assholes . And I would have to admit that I have myself from time to time -- fallen into the latter category . <p> Take issue with the person for what they say or their behaviour but not for having a condition . <p> Shane Dunphy is a child protection expert and author .
