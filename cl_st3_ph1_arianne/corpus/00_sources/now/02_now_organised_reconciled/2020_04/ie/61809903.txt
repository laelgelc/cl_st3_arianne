<p> As global leaders work with medical professionals to control the COVID-19 pandemic , countries have called for self-distancing and in some cases implemented a total lockdown . In the midst of all this , real assets investors , fund managers and advisers have been responding to the unprecedented situation . <p> Industrials not immune from COVID-19 fallout -- " We think that , for the industrial sector , any short-term gain from an increase in online shopping will be outweighed by the plunge in demand and subsequent collapse in global trade , " Capital Economics says . According to the research firm while the industrial sector appears to be more insulated from COVID-19-related fallout , it still faces short-term risks . " For a start , the economic hit is likely to be worse than we had first assumed , which will curtail occupier demand and put pressure on industrial rents . Meanwhile , the research firm says its expectation for global trade volumes to collapse this year will further weigh on demand for industrial space , particularly in port cities . " At around 20% year-on-year , our forecast for the decline in trade volumes in 2020 will exceed the 13% drop recorded during the GFC . " And given that nearly 75% of goods enter and leave Europe by sea , industrial rents in port cities are likely to be hit the hardest , Capital Economics says . Admittedly , the rise in online shopping has been heralded as a boost for industrial assets . " But we are cautious in drawing conclusions from this . " Adds that much of the recent increase in e-commerce has reflected groceries and essential items as consumers have faced strict lockdown measures . But given that food and non-alcoholic beverages make up around only 13% of total consumption at the euro-zone level , the boost is unlikely to compensate from shortfalls elsewhere . <p> Climate change as a public health issue -- Despite the tragic effects of the coronavirus outbreak , COVID-19 displayed the ability of government agencies , businesses and the general public to take drastic actions when risks to public health become urgent and pressing , according to a report by UBS Asset Management . And while the climate emergency is not directly comparable to the ongoing global health pandemic and its resulting disruption and damage , the effects of global warming will eventually have a significant impact on global supply chains , food production and ultimately , human lives . Real estate can not stay business as usual , the UBS real estate and sustainability in APAC report says . Adds that some effects of climate change on selected regions in APAC could include creating conditions that allow vectors such as mosquitoes to thrive . Warmer weather also leads to the rise of vector-borne diseases such as malaria and dengue fever . " All these issues will eventually increase the burden on healthcare systems , either through higher mortality rates or by exacerbating the impact of existing health conditions . " Acting to prevent and protect against global warming will become increasingly imperative in real estate investment management , UBS says . <p> China 's response to COVID-19 : Lessons for landlords and occupiers -- The COVID-19 outbreak has undoubtedly had a severe short-term impact on the Chinese economy and property market , according to CBRE reseacrh . However , headline indicators of business activity had largely returned to pre-outbreak levels by the end of March , while recent weeks have seen a rebound in real estate leasing and investment demand . CBRE says the government 's adoption of a " coordinated approach to containing the pandemic " appears to have shielded the economy from a deep and prolonged downturn , while a range of measures introduced by owners and tenants have provided a solid foundation for a gradual property market revival . " While some of these measures and recommendations may not be relevant or feasible in some markets , we believe they can serve as a valuable guide to our clients and colleagues around the world . " <p> Southern Europe property values to fall the most -- Capital Economics says the uneven economic impact of the coronavirus disruption suggests that property values in Southern European markets will be hit hardest and may take longer to recover than elsewhere in the euro-zone . The research firm says Q1 data trickling in suggest that most of the coronavirus disruption is showing up in higher property yields , with rental values generally holding steady so far . " However , reports continue to highlight that tenants are missing rental payments and we think it is only a matter of time before there is downward pressure on rents . " It is also becoming clearer that the pandemic will have a larger impact on some euro-zone economies , particularly those in Southern Europe . This reflects the greater direct impact of the virus and stricter lockdown measures , along with their larger economic reliance on consumption and tourism and the fact that there is less room to provide fiscal support . For property , this also implies that rental values will take a greater hit in Southern Europe than elsewhere in the euro-zone . Says it now seems likely that yield rises in Southern European will not be fully reversed once the recovery from the virus takes hold , even for office and industrial property . " The upshot is that we think that European all-property capital values will decline by more this year than previously thought . "
