<h> COVID-19 : 10 ways to care for your lungs to reduce infection risk <p> If you 've never cared for your lungs by always smoking or inhaling polluted air , now is the time to seriously take care of your lungs . As the coronavirus pandemic lingers on , health experts have said people with healthy lungs are at lower risk of contracting the virus . <p> The reasons are twofold . First , air pollution can cause or aggravate respiratory illnesses like asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease , according to the American Lung Association . These illnesses can make you more susceptible to the worst effects of lung infections . <p> Second , exposure to air pollution is known to raise the chance of contracting viruses in the first place , regardless of underlying health conditions . <p> A spokesperson for the ALA and an associate professor of pulmonary and critical care at Johns Hopkins University , Dr Meredith McCormack , said in an article in the NewYorkTimes that exposure to air pollution could increase susceptibility to infection . <p> " All things being equal , a person exposed to air pollution would likely have a worse outcome if they were exposed to coronavirus , " she said . <p> Although air pollution is said to have dropped sharply in many parts of the world recently , as businesses are shut down and people stay home to avoid spreading the virus , McCormack noted that there were still areas of high exposure . <p> " Power plants and refineries , for example , are considered essential and unlikely to close . Moreover , even if pollution is down for now , chronic exposure ca n't be mended so quickly . For a person living with daily pollution exposure , the damage to their respiratory and immune system is done , " she said . <p> Furthermore , the ALA said though the body had a natural defence system designed to protect the lungs , there were some important things to do to reduce the risk of lung disease . <p> Hence , the following are some ways to keep your lungs healthy and minimise your risk of contracting coronavirus , which had infected over one million and killed over 54,000 people globally as of Friday , according to the World Health Organisation . <p> Do n't smoke <p> You 've probably heard this advice a million times before and it 's because it 's true -- smoking , chewing tobacco and similar products are not good for your lungs , or overall health . <p> There 's a reason every health professional and organisation says this over and over again : quitting smoking is the most effective way to keep your respiratory system strong and healthy . <p> Cigarette smoking is the major cause of lung cancer and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease , which includes chronic bronchitis and emphysema . Cigarette smoke can narrow the air passages and make breathing more difficult . It causes chronic inflammation , or swelling in the lung , which can lead to chronic bronchitis . Over time , cigarette smoke destroys lung tissue and may trigger changes that grow into cancer . If you smoke , it 's never too late to quit , especially during this period . <p> Discard the air freshener <p> Apart from smoke , regular exposure to other pollutants such as paint , diesel fumes , solvents , wood shavings , even household cleaners can damage lungs . <p> Household cleaners and air fresheners can also hurt your lungs , especially if they are sprays . But if you spray air freshener or insecticide , do n't walk through it immediately . Let the odour circulate properly before staying where you sprayed . <p> According to the ALA , some cleaners release dangerous fumes , such as bleach and ammonia , which should never be mixed because they react to form chlorine gas . Others contain toxic volatile organic compounds or fragrances that combine with ozone to form formaldehyde . These can cause allergic reactions , irritate lungs and worsen asthma , a chronic condition of inflamed airways that narrow when reacting to inhaled substances . <p> The ALA suggested that most cleaning be done with soapy water and urged that air fresheners , even ones labelled as having natural ingredients , not to be used at all . <p> Avoid exposure to indoor/outdoor pollutants <p> Second-hand smoke , chemicals in the home and workplace , and radon ( which can be exposed to by breathing in air that comes through cracks and gaps in buildings and homes ) can all cause or worsen lung disease . Now is the time to make your home and car smoke-free . <p> Test your home for radon . Avoid exercising outdoors on bad air days . Talk to your health care provider if you are worried that something in your home or work may be making you sick . <p> Also , it is advisable to minimise exposure to outdoor air pollution . According to the ALA , the air quality outside can vary from day to day and sometimes unhealthy to breathe . <p> Knowing how outdoor air pollution affects your health and useful strategies to minimise prolonged exposure can keep you and your family well . Climate change and natural disasters can also directly impact on lung health . <p> McCormack said for people living in proximity to major sources of pollution , opening a window might not be a good idea . <p> She said , " That 's where an air purifier might be worth the investment if you can afford it . It 's important to think about allergens , too . If you smell anything musty around you home , you should investigate . Mould aggravates asthma . This might be the time to address damp spot or water damage problems . <p> " This might also be the time to pest-proof your home . Mice and cockroaches can provoke asthma . Fill all cracks around the house that might let in pests and properly store food that might entice them . " <p> Also , add some greenery to your home for improved air quality because adding plants to your home increases the oxygen and air quality thanks to plants ' natural abilities to remove certain toxins from the air . <p> Do n't fry foods if possible <p> Frying foods or cooking without good ventilation can also increase indoor air pollution . Nitrogen oxides from gas stoves are a known respiratory irritant , as it is the fine particulate matter that is most likely wafting off your roasts . You should turn on the ventilation hood above your stove and open windows if you can . <p> In the meantime , there are certain foods and herbs good for the lungs . These are water , broccoli , apple , ginger , walnut , turmeric , garlic , blueberry , cayenne pepper , apricot , and oily fish . Others are grapefruit , poultry products , pomegranate , beans , cruciferous vegetables , and chili pepper . <p> Exercise regularly <p> Whether you are young or old , slender or large , able-bodied or living with a chronic illness or disability , being physically active can help keep your lungs healthy . <p> When you are physically active , your heart and lungs work harder to supply the additional oxygen your muscles demand . Like regular exercise makes your muscles stronger , it also makes your lungs and heart stronger . As your physical fitness improves , your body becomes more efficient at getting oxygen into the bloodstream and transporting it to the working muscles . That 's one of the reasons you are less likely to become short of breath during exercise over time . <p> Both aerobic activities and muscle-strengthening activities can benefit your lungs . Aerobic activities like walking , running or jumping rope give your heart and lungs the kind of workout they need to function efficiently . <p> Meanwhile , always talk to your doctor before you start or modify your exercise routine . This is especially important if you have an underlying health condition . <p> Laugh a lot <p> Laughing is not just good for the soul , it 's also good for the lungs . The action of laughing gives your abdomen and diaphragm a workout . Deep belly laughs help to force out stale air from the lungs . Maybe you should watch more comic shows now more than ever before . <p> Adopt good posture <p> Allow your lungs the space they need to fully inflate and deflate by sitting and standing up straight . Hunching over can push the stomach and other organs into the lungs . Sitting straight with your hands above your head , or leaning back and pushing out your chest from time to time will give your lungs extra room . <p> Remember your medications <p> If you 're taking maintenance medication for asthma , like an inhaler , do n't forget to use it . " Remembering to take it can be challenging , particularly at a time like this when we are n't in the same routines , " McCormack said . " But it 's an important step to protect yourself , particularly now . " <p> She added , " With indoor pollution , we have a lot of control over more aspects of our exposure . <p> " In addition to a source of risk , it 's a source of opportunity . It 's a good time to change habits . " <p> Get regular checkups <p> Regular checkups help prevent diseases even when you are feeling well . This is especially true for lung disease , which sometimes goes undetected until it is serious . During a checkup , your doctor will listen to your breathing to know if you need medical attention . <p> Other tips to prevent infection <p> Wash your hands often with soap and water . Alcohol-based cleaners are a good substitute if you can not wash <p> Avoids crowds during the cold and flu season <p> Good oral hygiene can protect you from the germs in your mouth leading to infections . Brush your teeth at least twice daily and see your dentist at least every six months <p> Get vaccinated every year against influenza . Talk to your healthcare provider to find out if the pneumonia vaccine is right for you <p> If you get sick , keep it to yourself ! Protect the people around you , including your loved ones , by keeping your distance . Stay home from work or school until you feel better . <p> Sources : nytimes.com , lung.com , genesismedical.org , capecodhealth.org , **29;3794;TOOLONG Copyright PUNCH.All rights reserved .
