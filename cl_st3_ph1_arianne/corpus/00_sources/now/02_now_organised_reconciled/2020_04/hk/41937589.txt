<h> Now is the perfect time to fight climate change <h> Share this : <p> Workers stand on the platform of a fracking rig in the Permian Basin oilfield on January 21 , 2016 , in the oil town of Midland , Texas . That industry is suffering from the effects of the Saudi-Russian oil price war . Photo : AFP / Spencer Platt / Getty Images <p> While the whole world is consumed with the disastrous coronavirus pandemic , little attention is being paid to that sector of the economy whose existence had heretofore represented humanity 's greatest threat : the fossil-fuel industry . But now may be the best time to pay heed . Oil and gas prices recently fell so low that they bizarrely plummeted to negative values . This is an industry clearly in crisis , and therefore now is the perfect time for the climate-justice movement to strike . <p> Even before the Covid-19 pandemic , there was a massive glut of oil and gas . Supply was overwhelmingly large , and there was just not enough demand , which led to prices dropping extremely low . Then the coronavirus began spreading , and the declaration of a global pandemic caused a dramatically greater shutdown of the industry . <p> Antonia Juhasz , a leading expert in the oil and gas sector and author of The Tyranny of Oil , explained to me in an interview that the recent drop in prices to negative numbers was " a complete crash of oil . " It meant that " producers are paying buyers to take oil from them . That is just because there was way too much product that the world is trying really hard to get off of . " <p> From the start of his presidency , Trump made the welfare of fossil-fuel companies his business . He embraced the dying coal industry , proudly holding up " Trump Digs Coal " signs at his political rallies , and claimed to save coal miners from what he said were unfair attacks by his predecessor Barack Obama . He filled his administration with oil and gas executives , dramatically deregulated the industry , and pushed for a massive expansion of offshore oil drilling . <p> But Trump is not the only US president to contribute to the frenzy of producing a product for which demand is falling . Obama , in making a bizarre case for natural gas obtained through hydraulic fracturing ( fracking ) as a " transition fuel , " promoted an industry that exacerbated the climate crisis . <p> Thanks to Obama -- and some state-level leaders like former California governor Jerry Brown -- a fracking boom in the United States led to overproduction . Juhasz explained that in fact the US fracking industry was already in debt before the Covid-19 crisis to the tune of $200 billion , and still the industry continued producing even as demand kept dropping . <p> This is clearly an industry that can not and will not see the writing on the wall and enjoys political support to prop it up beyond its lifespan . Just as Frankenstein resurrected a dead man to life in the form of a monster , Trump has attempted desperately to revive fossil fuels . He has tweeted openly that he would " never let the great U.S. Oil &amp; Gas Industry down . " <p> So desperate is Trump to save it that he has instructed the federal government to buy up to 92 million barrels of oil to fill up the Strategic Petroleum Reserve . The reserve is actually meant to help lower oil prices for American consumers when they are high , not as a way to bail out a dying industry . <p> Juhasz also highlighted another way in which the US government appears to be propping it up , saying , " We are forcing airlines to continue to fly as much as they were before Covid-19 even though ridership is down about 90% . " The airline industry is a huge consumer of fossil fuels , and Juhasz says she thinks that one of the reasons they are being told to keep flying is " because they are the lone consumer of jet fuel . " <p> The oil and gas industries in recent years have also attempted to pour their products into the plastics industry and were met with a growing grassroots movement to reduce plastic consumption through widespread bans of plastic bags and single-use plastic . Now , just as the industry is taking advantage of the pandemic to beg aggressively for a taxpayer-funded bailout , it is pushing to overturn those bans in municipalities around the US and profiting off the public 's fear of potential contamination of reusable bags . <p> This is a dying industry , desperate to extract from our soil and our ocean floors every last drop of fuel in order to remain alive long past its prime . It is an industry that has not only wreaked long-term havoc on the climate , but also caused deep and immediate damage through air and water pollution . Ten years ago , the worst marine environmental disaster in US history , the Deepwater Horizon oil spill , caused unimaginable damage to the Gulf of Mexico that is still being felt today . <p> What the coronavirus pandemic has demonstrated to us is that it is possible to lower fossil-fuel use dramatically and pull back from the abyss of rising emissions . There are two ways this pandemic will end -- either by economies remaking themselves to sidestep the need for oil and gas , or for the industry to " roar back " by tapping into the massive supply that has been built up . <p> Already many are warning that our unpreparedness for the pandemic shows how little we are doing to ready ourselves for catastrophic climate change . Even former US secretary of state John Kerry says " the parallels between the two disasters are screaming at us . " <p> If we are to arise from the ashes of the Covid-19 crisis stronger and more resilient to future disasters , now is the time to pull the plug on an industry once and for all that has been on life support for too long . <p> This article was produced byEconomy for All , a project of the Independent Media Institute , which provided it to Asia Times .
