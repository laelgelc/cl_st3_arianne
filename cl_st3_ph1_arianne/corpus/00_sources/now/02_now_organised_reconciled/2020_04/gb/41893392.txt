<h> Follow the author of this article <h> Follow the topics within this article <p> " Every silver lining has a cloud , " was one Labour MP 's rather unkind reaction to the news that Ed Miliband had been given a place in Keir Starmer 's new shadow cabinet . <p> And , of course , there will be those who would prefer not to be reminded of the days , between 2010 and 2015 , when Ed was party leader . <p> They blame him directly for Jeremy Corbyn 's ascent to power , for without Miliband 's decision to scrap the party 's electoral college in favour of a new one-member , one-vote system for electing the leader , the Sage of Islington would have spent the last five years , as he had spent the previous 30 , on the back benches . <p> But what a problem to have ! Miliband 's return to the front line may possibly irk some , but it 's a speck of discontent in a sea of glorious celebration for most Labour MPs . For them , the peculiar blip that allowed the hard Left to gain complete control of the party is history . Their thoughts can turn , at last , to the rather more pleasant task of getting the party into shape so that it can actually present itself as a serious alternative government rather than as a North London collective for squatters ' rights , which is what it more resembled under the last regime . <p> Keir Starmer facing Jeremy CorbynCredit : BEN STANSALL /AFP <p> And a key part of that task -- in fact , the first task that confronted Starmer after his impressive win on Saturday -- was the shaping of the new shadow cabinet . His job was made easier by the pre-announced resignations of John McDonnell and Diane Abbott . It would also have been an easy decision not to appoint Corbyn himself to a high profile position : nothing would have been more guaranteed to hole Starmer 's leadership below the waterline than to retain the " services " of the man who gifted the government its 80-seat majority . <p> After that snub to his former leader , it would have been a matter of minutes to dispense with some of Corbyn 's more enthusiastic cheerleaders : Richard Burgon ( alas ) , Ian Lavery , Dan Carden , Jon Trickett and Barry Gardiner . <p> Because of the dysfunctional nature of the parliamentary Labour Party these past four years , and the mass resignations and boycotting of the front bench that ensued from that dysfunctionality , junior and under-performing MPs were of necessity drafted to serve and were given high profile jobs that , in a normal party and under normal circumstances , they would never have got near . It is the break from that " needs must " approach adopted by Corbyn that has so benefited his successor . Starmer has been able to appoint on merit in a way that was simply not open to Corbyn . <p> So under-used stars of the back benches , like Rachel Reeves , made a well-deserved comeback yesterday , as did Lisa Nandy , Starmer 's former leadership rival who has n't served on the front bench since she resigned in principle over Corbyn 's leadership in 2016 . High-profile appointments for the excellent Jonathan Reynolds and John Healey give the new front bench some much-needed gravitas . <p> However , in a worrying sign that the new leader wo n't be shaking off some of the key policies of the Corbyn era , Rebecca Long-Bailey , another of Starmer 's rivals in the recent contest , gets the education brief . <p> And then there 's Ed . For sure , the former leader has a lot to live down . But he 's also a former cabinet member , a widely-respected individual in the area of climate change who retains a degree of affection among party members , if not his parliamentary colleagues . And although he will go to his grave overshadowed by his ego-driven decision to betray his big brother by denying him the leadership role that David was so obviously more suited to fulfil , what else can he do ? <p> Ed is still only 50 years old . He has deliberately chosen not to leave the Commons and pursue a different career , either in academia or in business , either of which would , no doubt , prove lucrative ( or , at least , more lucrative than the life of an MP ) . Instead he has chosen public service , of his constituents and of his party . If he is not to return to the front bench , what other role is available ? He does n't strike me as the type who would be happy to while away his time on a select committee or even on the Speaker 's panel of MPs willing to chair standing committees . <p> Moreover , if Starmer performs well and the government are perceived as not handling the aftermath of Coronavirus competently , Ed could find himself back around the real cabinet table in three years ' time . <p> Despite the headlines , though , Ed is not the real story here , but a distraction from the narrative that Starmer , unlike his predecessor , has been given a freedom to pick who he wants for whichever posts he judges most suitable . That in itself is a gigantic leap forwards from the dark , dismal days of 2015-2019 . For Labour , there 's more silver lining than cloud in this reshuffle .
