<h> People are encouraged to spend more time engaging with natural world when coronavirus pandemic is over <p> Being cooped up during the coronavirus lockdown has given many of us a renewed appreciation of nature . <p> And as cars vanish from the roads and planes are grounded , reports are emerging of wildlife springing back up as humans head inside . <p> Once the pandemic is over , wildlife and conservation charities say they want people across the country to engage with the natural world even more . <p> Government figures show that , before current restrictions began , Dorset residents were reasonably outdoorsy . <p> Data from Natural England shows that 57% of locals aged 16 or over spent time outdoors at least weekly . <p> The median rate -- the middle value of a range of figures -- for council areas across England was 58% . <p> Local figures are based on 10 years ' worth of surveys from the government body 's Monitor of Engagement with the Natural Environment report , the final of which was completed in 2018-19 . <p> It includes visits to parks , canals and other urban open spaces as well as rural settings . <p> According to the results of the latest survey , 84% of adults across the South West headed outdoors at least once a week -- the highest of the nation 's nine regions . <p> Across England , the rate has risen over the last 10-year period , from 54% in 2009-10 to 65% in 2018-19 . <p> Paul Bunton , from the woodland conservation charity the Woodland Trust , said : " We are pleased that the trend seems to show that people are spending more times in the outdoors , albeit in the current crisis they need to heed government advice and not travel to our woods at this time . <p> " We understand the many positives of visiting woods -- they are wildlife havens , a way to destress , have an adventure and learn about their importance , for example in tackling climate change . " <p> Where people do not have woodland on their doorstep , Mr Bunton suggested going to the nearest park , adding that the charity had " huge aims " to increase tree cover and protect urban trees . <p> He added : " All of this , and our woodlands , will be there to enjoy once the current national crisis abates . " <p> Craig Bennett , chief executive of the Wildlife Trusts , said people were turning to nature more than ever as the world faced health , climate and ecological emergencies . <p> He said : " When the pandemic has passed , there is a battle to resume -- to restore nature and to empower people to take action for the natural world . <p> " At the Wildlife Trusts , we have a pivotal role to play and have a clear understanding of the urgency . " <p> The group -- a movement of 46 charities across the UK -- wants at least a third of land and sea handed back to nature by 2030 . <p> A Department for Environment , Food and Rural Affairs spokeswoman said people should follow the Government 's guidance on using green spaces during the Covid-19 outbreak : <p> Stay local and use open spaces near to your home where possible -- do not travel unnecessarily . <p> Only go outside alone or with members of your own household . <p> Keep at least two metres apart from anyone outside your household at all times . <p> Take hygiene precautions when you are outside , and wash your hands as soon as you are back indoors . <p> If walking your dog in areas used by other people , keep them on a lead to ensure you can safely keep two metres away from others . <p> This website and associated newspapers adhere to the Independent Press Standards Organisation 's Editors ' Code of Practice .
