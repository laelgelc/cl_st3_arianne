<p> What 's driving the day in Westminster . Politics and policymaking in the UK capital . <p> Carrie scoop : <p> Good Friday morning . This is Emilio Casalicchio . I 'll be delivering your Friday dose of Playbook for the next few months , while Annabelle goes on maternity leave . <p> FIRST THING 'S FIRST : Take a look at Brits across the country clapping for heroic NHS and other frontline staff at 8 p.m. last night . Boris Johnson left his sickbed to clap in Downing Street , Jeremy Corbyn and his wife Laura Alvarez were snapped outside their home , and someone paraded the streets of Leith playing bagpipes . See you out there again next week , Britain . <h> DRIVING THE DAY <p> MASOCHISM MATT : Matt Hancock will work to keep the government above water this morning as he opens the biggest hospital in the U.K. The health secretary will cut the ribbon on the new Nightingale Hospital based at the London Docklands ExCeL Centre at 11 a.m . Just days after it was first announced , the medical facility will initially have beds for up to 500 patients , but will soon be able to house thousands . <p> Bed time : New Nightingale hospitals will also be built at the University of the West of England in Bristol and at the Harrogate Convention center , according to the press note released last night . Others are already in development in Manchester and Birmingham . <p> But first : Hancock has the morning broadcast round , during which he will seek to capitalize on a press conference last night that appears to have dug the government out of a hole . His address to the nation from Downing Street , in which he laid out his five-point plan for coronavirus testing , has won plaudits in the press and even cautious praise from critics . <p> Bullseye : Almost all the papers splash on his new target to deliver 100,000 coronavirus tests a day in England by the end of the month , which is a good turnaround for the government after a dismal set of front pages on Thursday morning . In the Telegraph , sketchwriter Michael Deacon argues : " Even while recovering from a deadly viral disease , Mr Hancock is much better at this sort of thing than almost any of his fully fit colleagues . " In his late-night memo , HuffPost 's Paul Waugh wrote that after the " incoherent and evasive Downing Street press conference 24 hours earlier , Matt Hancock had a back-to-work vigor about him . " <p> Indeed : Hancock openly admitted for the first time that the government was not where it wanted to be on testing , and gave clear and confident explanations why . He took more questions from journalists than previous ministers have , and allowed supplementaries . Playbook hears that the health secretary told No. 10 he wanted to take as many questions as possible to show he was across the detail and could take whatever they had to throw at him . He dubbed it his " masochism strategy . " <p> Blast from the past : Tony Blair called his bouts with opponents of the Iraq War a " masochism strategy . " After the Gordon Brown-style five-point plan , Hancock truly has gone full New Labour . <p> Fresh start : Whatever it 's called , the plan appears to have worked . Even the Daily Mail , which yesterday decried the " testing scandal , " has decided to give the government another chance . " Do n't fail this test minister , " its splash headline reads . The leader column says the paper " wholeheartedly welcomes Matt Hancock 's mea culpa last night . " <p> Another fan : Chair of the Commons health committee Jeremy Hunt , who has been critical of the government 's approach to the coronavirus , was also impressed , saying in a series of tweets ( someone teach this guy how to do a thread ) that it was " excellent news . " <p> 5-point plan reminder : 1 ) 25,000 swab tests a day in NHS hospitals and Public Health England labs ; 2 ) New swab testing capacity from private firms like Amazon and Boots ; 3 ) Rolling out antibody tests once approved ; 4 ) Using antibody results to track the spread of the virus ; 5 ) Building the British diagnostic industry ( after Hancock admitted the U.K. could not currently match the German sector ) . <p> Corona festival : With better testing comes a possible exit strategy from lockdown , which Hancock revealed on Question Time last night could be done with wristbands . He said when the science is good enough " we are looking at introducing something like an immunity certificate or maybe a wristband that says ' I 've had it and I 'm immune and I ca n't pass it on and I 'm highly unlikely to catch it . ' " <p> But but but : Not everything is plain sailing for Hancock . In its digital front page , the Independent points out that the 100,000 ambition appears to have been sliced down from 250,000 a day ( promised by Boris Johnson two weeks ago ) . To be fair , Hancock has given a specific date and insisted last night that the 250,000 target is still a future ambition . But there are still questions about how the government will reach its target , after its record on testing so far . <p> More but but but:In the Telegraph , Gordon Rayner notes that Hancock has " staked his -- and the government 's -- reputation on meeting a goal that is four times higher than health chiefs had previously said was possible . " One senior minister gives a word of warning to the paper : " This No. 10 operation and the way it works was always going to be found out . " <p> FAULT LINES : The coronavirus blame has engulfed Downing Street , Whitehall and the health service , according to Seb Payne and George Parker in the Financial Times . One No. 10 insider tells them of " growing anger " at Cabinet Secretary Mark Sedwill 's alleged " inability to execute what needs to be done . " A minister lashes out at " NHS bureaucracy " and says Public Health England is " the reason we do n't have more testing . " Meanwhile , a senior Tory insider describes some Downing Street aides as " inexperienced , unpleasant and naive . " <p> Not happy : Times columnist Iain Martin , who raged about the FT story on Twitter : " Senior SpAds -- some of whom have been completely found out in this crisis and had their arses saved by civil servants working round the clock -- now trashing the civil service . Absolutely contemptible . " Former civil servant and IFG fellow Jill Rutter added : " Stupid , bad , counterproductive , unnecessary -- for everyone 's sake stop childish blame games , and work well together . " <h> THE CORONA ECONOMY <p> MASOCHISM-OFF : Rishi Sunak will launch his own masochism strategy today , fronting up to businesses and workers impacted by the coronavirus in a Twitter Q&amp;A about his measures to protect the economy . <p> Rewrite the rulebook : The chancellor last night overhauled his emergency business loan scheme after the Treasury received more than 130,000 enquiries but less than 1,000 were approved . Previously , government-backed loans for small firms were only available for those businesses which had been rejected for a commercial loan from their bank . The new rules will scrap that requirement , while also banning banks from asking bosses to guarantee loans with their own savings or property when borrowing up to ? 250,000 . ITV 's Robert Peston has an interesting explainer on that here . <p> Meanwhile : Larger firms will also get more help , with government-backed loans of up to ? 25 million to companies with revenues of between ? 45 million and ? 500 million . <p> BUS MONEY : Transport Secretary Grant Shapps announced an extra ? 167 million to keep bus services in England running during the crisis , on top of an existing ? 200 million already announced and ? 30 million which has been reallocated from other funding . <p> SHIPS HIT THE FAN : Shipping representatives warned ministers that without a bailout package for the industry the nation could face food and medicine shortages . In a call with Business Minister Kelly Tolhurst on Thursday , operators said they have been haemorrhaging cash , and that without support , freight ships will be imminently laid up . " If this happens across the sector we could quickly see a shortage of food , goods and medicines , " an industry source told Playbook . " It could be catastrophic for the country . " <p> It 's a good question for : The daily Downing Street press conference -- although it is currently unclear who will be leading it today .
