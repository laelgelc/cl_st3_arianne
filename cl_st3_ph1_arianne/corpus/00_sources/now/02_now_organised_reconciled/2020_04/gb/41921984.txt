<p> What 's driving the day in Westminster . Politics and policymaking in the UK capital . <p> Carrie scoop : <p> Good Friday morning . This is Emilio Casalicchio . <p> While you were sleeping : Two big coronavirus stories from two sides of the world last night . In the U.S. , Donald Trump announced a three-phase guidance plan to reopen state economies in the coming months . Meanwhile , the Chinese economy contracted by 6.8 percent in the first quarter of this year , new official figures revealed -- the first time it has shrunk in decades . <h> DRIVING THE DAY <p> HANCOCK HUNTED : The government will face prolonged scrutiny over its coronavirus strategy for the first time this morning when Matt Hancock submits to a grilling from senior MPs . The health secretary will go head to head with health select committee Chair Jeremy Hunt -- the man who did his job for six years ; sailed past him in the 2019 race to be prime minister ; and has been snapping at the heels of the government over its record on COVID-19 testing -- in a virtual hearing from 10.30 a.m . <p> Sic ' em : Hunt is bringing a gang of bruisers along to help . He has invited fellow committee chairs to form a liaison team alongside the usual health committee membership . The home affairs committee 's Yvette Cooper , foreign affairs committee 's Tom Tugendhat , science and technology committee 's Greg Clark and communities committee 's Clive Betts will all be on the call to press Hancock on a range of issues . The hearing will be a must-watch and available to livestream here , with health experts appearing first from 10 a.m . <p> QUESTION 1 ... Must surely be about the damning survey of care home staff ITV revealed about an hour ago , which paints a grim picture of the coronavirus situation in the community . Some 42 percent of the 2,800 respondents reported a suspected outbreak of COVID-19 in their workplace , with 44 percent saying they knew at least one colleague with the disease . That is quite a break from official figures , which suggest only 15 percent of care homes have been affected by the virus . There are more worrying figures on PPE , testing and mental health support in the survey . Read the whole story here . <p> And there 's more : The Daily Mail also reports on a survey of groups representing 13 percent of care homes , which suggests about 1.4 percent of older residents in social care have died outside hospital due to confirmed or suspected COVID-19 as of April 15 . That would amount to a whopping 5,300 deaths , suggesting 28 percent of coronavirus deaths in the U.K. may have occurred in care homes . Remember : The Office for National Statistics reported 217 care home deaths in England and Wales in the two weeks to April 3 . <p> QUESTION 2 : The committee will also no doubt probe Hancock on the further three weeks of lockdown ( at least ) that First Secretary of State Dominic Raab announced yesterday evening , and how the government hopes to ease the measures and get the economy running again in the future . <p> Homework : Committee members might want to read this piece in the Telegraph by Spectator Editor Fraser Nelson first . He says Cabinet ministers are no more clued up on the exit strategy than the public at large . " They 're not quite sure about the ' five tests ' we keep hearing about , or what firm criteria would be used to judge them , " Nelson writes . " So if you see ministers getting angry when asked about an exit strategy , this is why . They have no answer to give . " <p> QUESTION 3 : Hunt will be eager to grill Hancock on his target to reach 100,000 tests a day by the end of the month -- in particular why the U.K. seems unable to do much better than 15,000 to 20,000 a day . Downing Street yesterday argued the testing issue was one not of capacity but of demand , a claim backed up by this piece in the Guardian by Gianmarco Raddi , a swab tester whose Milton Keynes facility Hancock visited last Thursday . <p> Running commentary : " Our shifts were meant to be excruciating 12-hour marathons , " Raddi writes . " In reality , they are rather more like laid-back morning jogs . Dozens of academics and laboratory personnel from all over the U.K. languish in a hotel with nothing to do . Millions of pounds of equipment borrowed from universities and companies rests silently in the evening hours , when the noise of our collective toil should be deafening . " If the testing capacity is there , the committee will want to know why it 's not being reached . <p> Fight ! The blame game over the ongoing testing issue revs up again in the Times . Whitehall officials blame health service delays in putting doctors and nurses forward , and hint that some are reluctant to be checked and sent back to work , the paper reports , while doctors blame a failure to make testing convenient , plus confusion about eligibility . <p> But but but:In the Telegraph , Institute of Biomedical Science President Allan Wilson insisted there are still not enough supplies to meet the 100,000 a day target . He said NHS lab workers were " increasingly expressing their frustration " because " they are still not able to source the testing kits and reagents they require ... we are clear that it is a global supply shortage holding biomedical scientists back , not a lack of capacity . " <p> Ruh-roh : Justice Secretary Robert Buckland did not do Hancock any favors last night when he told BBC Question Time the German death rate has been lower because " their rate of testing has been much , much higher . " He added : " If we were testing much more widely , then we might have a different set of statistics . " Hunt will be interested to know whether the death rate is one of those statistics . <p> And another thing : The committee might want to ask about this New York Times scoop claiming the government spent $20 million on 2 million antibody test kits from Chinese firms ... which later turned out not to work . <p> QUESTION 4 : Are masks going to be a thing or not ? Chief Medical Officer Chris Whitty made it sound like a U-turn could be coming down the track very soon when he told the Downing Street press conference the debate around masks was " a very live issue . " In the Daily Mail , Political Editor Jason Groves says ministers are discussing whether the use of masks and gloves in the workplace and on public transport could be " the only way to allow a widespread return to normality once the home lockdown ends . " <p> Music to the ears of : London Mayor Sadiq Khan , who last night told BBC London he was " hopeful " the government would advise the use of face coverings such as scarves . Khan has written to ministers urging them to take on board global evidence suggesting face coverings can reduce transmission from asymptomatic carriers of the disease . On Newsnight last night , Shadow Health Minister Justin Madders agreed that masks " might be part of the solution . " Khan will be touring the broadcast studios to press his case this morning . <p> QUESTION 5 : The committee will surely want to ask about the wider health impacts of the lockdown , after the ONS revealed the staggering number of excess deaths compared to past averages . Many of those deaths are not accounted for by COVID-19 , and could be a result of people not getting help for other conditions . In the i newspaper , Professor Karol Sikora , a former head of the World Health Organization 's cancer program , warned that 60,000 cancer patients in the U.K. would die if the lockdown goes on for six months . <p> QUESTION 6 : ITV 's Robert Peston tweeted last night that the new Nightingale hospitals are unable to accept patients with complex health needs on top of COVID-19 , meaning many of the most serious cases have to remain in existing intensive care units . Playbook has heard the same from a source with ties to the London Nightingale : that only patients for straightforward ventilation are admitted , so the majority are staying in other hospitals . There are also rumors the hospital is struggling to get enough staff on stream . <p> QUESTION 7 : Tugendhat , the foreign affairs committee chair , will presumably want to ask what Dominic Raab *really* meant at the Downing Street press conference when he said there would be " no more business as usual " with China once the coronavirus crisis is over . Bloomberg reports that government officials believe legislation allowing Chinese firm Huawei a role in the U.K. 5G network could now be at risk after Tory backbench attitudes hardened against Beijing amid the COVID-19 crisis . <p> Right then , Matt : Have fun ! And there is plenty more to come when parliament starts getting back up to speed next week . <h> LOCKDOWN NATION <p> THREE MORE WEEKS ( OR MORE ) : Aside from the Hancock appearance , Britain is coming to terms this morning with weeks longer at home , despite Raab 's insistence there is a " light at the end of the tunnel " on the U.K. lockdown . Of course , polls make clear Brits are supportive of the measures , so the country got its wish . The public can embrace it fully by reading the new guidance from the National Police Chiefs ' Council on " what constitutes a reasonable excuse to leave the place where you live . " <p> Here 's a flavor : Buying luxury food items and alcohol : Yes . Buying paint to redecorate your kitchen : No . Driving somewhere for a walk that takes longer than the drive there : Yes . Working in a local park : No . In fact , it seems best to avoid extended rests on park benches for the foreseeable . <p> Definitely not allowed : Taking the car out to give it " a run , " which is what 4 percent of drivers owned up to in an RAC study . <p> If in doubt : Transport Minister Grant Shapps is on the broadcast round , so he should be able to clear up the ground rules . Except ... last time Downing Street let him speak to the media it had to quickly pour cold water on his suggestion Brits should only shop once a week . <p> HOW TO LOCKDOWN : Only 8 percent of Brits are having more sex during the lockdown , according to new data released in the last few minutes by pollsters Ipsos MORI . Maybe they are writing 4,000-word emails about Matt Hancock and eating Nutella straight from the jar all night instead ? The survey also found 47 percent of people are spending more time on social media ( SAD ! ) , 39 percent are gardening , 25 percent are playing board games or completing jigsaw puzzles and 12 percent are baking their own bread . <p> Like this Tory councillor in Portsmouth ... Who was suspended after he allegedly baked a swastika into a hot-cross-bun and posted it to Snapchat . Lee Mason , who now faces an investigation over the matter , " strenuously denies baking any bun with a swastika on it , " the News reports . <p> LIFE GOALS : Another poll , commissioned by the Royal Society of Arts , says just 9 percent of Brits hope " everything will go back to how it was " before lockdown began , with almost 55 percent wanting to " change some things about my life . " Playbook would guess more than half the public thought that long before the coronavirus was a thing anyway . Brits are also finding positives among the horrors . Just over 50 percent said they had noticed cleaner air , while 40 percent noted a stronger sense of local community and 39 percent said they were more in touch with friends and family . <p> The bad news : A solid 36 percent of respondents said they were getting less exercise than before . <p> MAKE IT STOP : The lockdown should not end until after parliament and other major institutions have reopened , according to Tom Newton Dunn in the Sun , who says ministers believe there will be a backlash from parents and bosses if kids are sent to school and staff to work beforehand . Business Secretary Alok Sharma might answer the bosses question when he joins CBI boss Carolyn Fairbairn at the daily coronavirus webinar the business group is running from 10 a.m . <p> VIDEO WARS : As for parliament , the two chambers are set for a battle of the videoconference platforms when they get back to work next week . The House of Commons announced it will be using Zoom for question sessions as part of a phased and socially distanced return to business . But the Lords said it had opted for " Microsoft platforms " to ask questions of ministers . This is going to get confusing . <p> VIEW FROM KEIR : Labour leader Keir Starmer , like pretty much everyone in Westminster , does not expect any real movement on the lockdown until Boris Johnson is back at work . " I suspect , although I do n't know , that Dominic Raab is ... probably reluctant to sign it off without the prime minister , " he told the BBC Coronavirus Newscast last night . <p> Talking of Labour : GMB members at Labour HQ yesterday passed their motion of no confidence in General Secretary Jennie Formby . It called on her to " personally apologize to the current staffers " named in the leaked report that has been tearing the party apart . <h> THE CORONA ECONOMY <p> MONEY TALKS : Chancellor Rishi Sunak will meet business bosses today to discuss the progress of his business loans schemes . He announced another tweak last night to ensure the biggest firms get a slice of the action . Previously , support was only available to companies with a turnover of up to ? 500 million , but that cap will be lifted . Meanwhile , those with a turnover of more than ? 250 million will be able to borrow up to ? 50 million -- double the previous amount . The Treasury also noted that it was looking at ways to support start-ups . <p> BACK OF THE NET : Fishers in England will receive cash grants as part of a ? 10 million package to support fishing communities , Environment Secretary George Eustice announced last night . Most of the fish caught is exported , but trade lines have been damaged by the coronavirus . <p> MARK HIS WORDS : In a thoughtful piece for the Economist , former Bank of England Governor Mark Carney looks at the ways the pandemic could shape the future economy . He suggests companies will no longer operate with tight cash flows and stretched supply chains ; that attitudes to debt and insecure work will change ; and that public values , for example in health and fairness , could begin to push back against the values of the market . <p> STATS DUMP : The Office for National Statistics will release figures on the coronavirus and self-employment at 9.30 a.m .
