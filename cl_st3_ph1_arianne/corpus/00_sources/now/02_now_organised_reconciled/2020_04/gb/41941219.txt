<h> The new electric cars on the streets of Oxford as part of a superhub scheme <h> The new electric cars on the streets of Oxford as part of a superhub scheme <p> SIX new electric vehicles have been delivered to Oxford City Council , as it takes steps towards reducing carbon emissions . <p> The vehicles will make up part of the fleet at the council-owned company , Oxford Direct Services , which collects bins and carries out maintenance and building work . <p> The new electric cars and vans are step forward in the council 's agenda to go green , and are linked to the world first Energy Superhub Oxford ( ESO ) , which has an integrated plan for decarbonising power , heat and transport across the city . <p> Tom Hayes , Oxford City Council cabinet member for Zero Carbon Oxford , said : " With this project we 're encouraging the adoption of electric vehicles and the move to zero carbon . <p> Energy Superhub Oxford gives Oxford strengths that no other city currently has : the world 's most powerful charging network and the world 's largest ever hybrid battery , and as a result we can accelerate our electric vehicle charging infrastructure for businesses and residents . " <p> Picture : Ed Nix . <p> ESO is a collaboration between Oxford City Council and private firm Pivot Power which will trial the world 's largest hybrid battery system at 50 megawatts , with the aim of ramping up Oxford 's capacity to charge electric vehicles . <p> There are also plans to heat 300 homes across the city using ground-source heat pumps . <p> The ? 41m superhub project was announced in April 2019 and received ? 10m from the government 's Prospering from the Energy Revolution Challenge . <p> Oxford City Council received ? 1,615,169 for its role in the project . <p> On top of its six new vehicles , the council also has plans for a further 27 EVs including cars , a street sweeper , excavator , and mix of different sized vans , which are due to be delivered over the next four months . <p> The ESO project is also funding a ' Try before you buy ' scheme for Oxford 's taxi drivers . <p> This is aimed at speeding up taxi firms ' switch to zero emission Hackney Carriage vehicles . <p> The scheme enables drivers to trial one of two models , either an all-electric Nissan Dynamo or a London Electric Vehicle Company taxi , for a two or four-week period . <p> From 2025 drivers will only be able to get a licence if they have a zero-emission capable cab . <p> Meanwhile , Pivot Power is installing the world 's most powerful charging network at Blackberry Lane , South Oxford . <p> This network will connect public charging facilities at Redbridge Park and Ride directly to the National Grid . <p> Redbridge Park and Ride . <p> The network will also be able to provide power for local businesses including lorry and bus firms . <p> The Park &amp; Ride EV public Superhub aims to include 20 charge points capable of charging a car in 15-50 minutes , as well as 30 fast charge points which can charge a car over a period of hours while Park &amp; Ride users are at work or shopping in the city centre . <p> Tim Rose , ESO Programme Manager at Pivot Power said : " The smart power network we are installing in Oxford will deliver flexible , reliable power at scale to fast-track EV adoption . " <p> The superhub is also set to help reduce the emissions from Oxford 's buildings , which make up 81 per cent of emissions in the city . <p> In response to the recommendations of the Oxford Citizens Assembly on Climate Change made last summer , the city council committed to moving towards a zero carbon building system . <p> This website and associated newspapers adhere to the Independent Press Standards Organisation 's Editors ' Code of Practice .
