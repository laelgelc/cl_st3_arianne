" <p> The government says that all of its decisions about how to respond to the coronavirus crisis have been based " on a representative range of the most up-to-date science advice " . It says the Scientific Advisory Group for Emergencies ( SAGE ) has been providing ministers and officials with " free and frank advice throughout , based on external scientific evidence and a wide source of essential information " . <p> However , Sridhar has said scientific advice is typically broad and diverse and that decisions such as not banning mass gathering until late March , imposing a lockdown sooner or discontinuing a test , track , and trace campaign were just snippets advice ministers would have received during SAGE briefings . She said a focus on one discipline of science may have led to shortsightedness on coronavirus policy . <h> You may also want to watch : <p> Sridhar added : " World Health Organization ( WHO ) advice , and what we 've learned from lots of previous outbreaks in low- and middle-income countries , is that the faster you move at the start , the better , because it 's exponential growth .
