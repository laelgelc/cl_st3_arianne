<h> Quaranteen : Picturing young lives in isolation <p> Photographers Jean-Marc Caimi and Valentina Piccinni spoke with teens around the world , and made portraits of them using Zoom videoconferencing . The teenagers contributed snapshots of their lives offline . <p> Portraits by Jean-Marc Caimi and Valentina Piccinni for POLITICO <h> Lily , 13 <p> Thon-Samson , Belgium <p> Lily takes circus classes and enjoys drawing , creating collages and horseback riding -- all things that she 's suddenly got much more time for . She lives with her parents and her 11-year-old brother , C ? sar . Living in the countryside gives her the opportunity to take long walks -- or rides -- in wide-open fields . <p> What are you up to , aside from doing some school work online ? <p> I do n't do online courses , but teachers are sending us homework . I 'm lucky because I have a pony to take care of . We went for a lot of rides . C ? sar on his bike , my parents on foot and me on the pony . I also do a lot of baking . Since the lockdown , we 've started baking our own bread without a machine ! <p> How worried are you about the pandemic ? <p> I 'm very worried about the most vulnerable , the elderly ... but also isolated people , those living on the streets , refugees , people who do n't have much money ... I 'm especially nervous about how we recover after confinement is over . <p> What lessons can we learn from all this ? <p> We 've realized that we can live differently : moving around less , making more things at home and buying less as we can live very well without . <h> Laila , 17 <p> West London <p> Laila 's attending the William Morris Sixth Form college in Hammersmith . She wants to become a makeup artist , and the lockdown has allowed her more time to create tutorials for her Instagram followers . <p> at home , which is frustrating as I miss my friends . But I remind myself that going out is n't worth the risk -- to myself or others . On a normal day , I 'd get up early to get ready for school . Now I 'm able to have a lie-in for a bit longer if I want , and I 've got loads of free time . <p> How worried are you about the pandemic ? <p> Quite . When you see the number of people infected , and dying , it 's hard not to be . But if everyone does what 's being asked , hopefully the time will fly by , those numbers will decrease , and eventually it 'll be safe for us all to get back to normal routines . <p> How are you keeping informed ? <p> I get notifications on my phone , and watch the news with my family . <p> Do you think life will be different once this is over ? <p> Mine probably wo n't be much different , but I do n't know what 's going to happen in terms of my education and I really wish I could have more insight into what 's going on there . <h> Jules , 13 <p> Flawinne , Belgium <p> Jules started taking piano lessons a few months ago and now with the quarantine is practicing much more . He 's also a badminton fan . His dad bought a foosball table and they now play together a lot when they 're stuck inside . <p> How are you ? <p> I 'm doing quite well , but I would like to see my friends again . I 've found that some people are very responsible ; they understand the situation well and try to stop the spread as much as possible . But because of others flouting the rules , their efforts are n't being rewarded . <p> What are you up to , aside from doing some school work online ? <p> At least once a day , I 'll run or ride my bike to get some exercise . I use my phone , cook and read comic books . <p> How are you keeping informed ? <p> I watch the news but I do n't want to get too depressed ... so I do n't watch it every day . <p> Do you think life will be different once this is over ? <p> There is good coming out of this confinement : People pollute much less . So I hope that some of these new habits take hold ; that could be beneficial for everyone . <h> Yasser , 18 <p> Marrakech , Morocco <p> Yasser is a first-year student at the Higher School Prepatory , which has been closed for a month now . He 's spending the quarantine period with his mom , sister and " of course my six lovely cats . " <p> What are you up to , aside from doing some school work online ? <p> I 'm focused on staying fit and working out a lot since I have so much time to myself . I play video games with friends and watch movies on Netflix too . <p> How worried are you about the pandemic ? <p> I 'm worried , like everyone else . I do n't want the situation to get even worse than it is right now . Not everyone seems fully aware of the danger , which is why my country and the government are working hard to find solutions . <p> What lessons can we learn from all this ? <p> There 's no way to know what could happen a few months from now , let alone a year , so stop being lazy , enjoy your life and learn how to be content alone . <h> Charlotte , 17 <p> Wiesbaden , Germany <p> Charlotte is an ex-Londoner attending a bilingual school in Germany . " It 's now a cyber school , " she says . " I 've been granted the liberty of adopting a sleep rhythm fit for a teenager : I 'm getting nine hours of sleep a night and have truly never been better . " She and her friends have taken up an old-fashioned pursuit -- letter writing -- as a way to keep in touch more thoughtfully . She sees Germany 's response to the coronavirus as level-headed : " The pandemic is neither dramatized nor belittled . " <p> What are you up to , aside from doing some school work online ? <p> I make sure to find time for exercise . Long-distance running is helping me out immensely . It 's a pillar of my mental health , but I could never keep it up consistently during proper school . I 'm the editor of my school newspaper , so I 've been doing what I can to make sure it comes out continually . Instead of print , students get a PDF issue . Editor 's note : Just like POLITICO 's readers ! I like to listen to loud music and have a dance party every once in a while , to the annoyance of my brother next door . <p> How worried are you about the pandemic ? <p> Thankfully , I live in a town that has n't been hit that hard by the virus . I know that I am in a very privileged position and many , who are in the thick of things , are suffering unimaginably , health-wise , financially . If anything , I 'm worried that world leaders wo n't learn from the spotlight that has been shined on this inequality . <p> How are you keeping informed ? <p> I do n't feel the need to read the news . I know that one day it will all blow over . That 's what I know and it 's all I need to know . <p> Do you think life will be different once this is over ? <p> I hope it will be . I hope the pandemic will prioritize the changes the world needs in health care , equality , global cooperation and climate change . <p> What lessons can we learn from all this ? <p> Humans are n't that bad after all . Everybody is so willing to help . The solidarity with health care workers warms my heart . <h> Julien , 15 <p> Rome <p> Julian is in his second year at C. Cavour , a science high school in Rome . Despite some connectivity issues , he says online schooling is going well . " We have regular lessons every day -- too many , even ! It 's nice that it 's easier to consult books during tests . " He 's been outside a few times to jog ; he 's a high-jump athlete . <p> What are you up to , aside from doing some school work online ? <p> I spend my days mainly on my phone or computer . I 'm very homely , and I do n't even mind the fact that I ca n't physically see my friends -- I 'm happy to see them virtually on video calls . I 'm amazed at how easy it is for me to stay home ; I have no desire to go outside at all . <p> How worried are you about the pandemic ? <p> Not very . <p> How are you keeping informed ? <p> I watch the news from time to time , but I 'm not too up-to-date on how the pandemic is developing . <p> Do you think life will be different once this is over ? <p> Everything will go back to the way it was before . <h> Hasna , 17 <p> Marrakech , Morocco <p> Hasna 's school has been closed since March 16 . Online lessons followed , but only a few teachers are involved . She 's spending time with her family , " especially my older sister Chaimae who helps me to not feel any emptiness , " she says . " And I always do a video call with my rugby girls whom I miss so badly . " <p> What are you up to ? <p> I 'm working out , cooking , reading . The situation is getting worse every day , so I 'm obliged to stay at home . It 's difficult to adapt , but in such circumstances , we must be patient . <p> How worried are you about the pandemic ? <p> Very . What 's happening now affects all of us , as the number of deaths continues to increase . Who knows , tomorrow I could lose people I love as well . <p> How are you keeping informed ? <p> I look at the news on a daily basis , though I try not to be too affected by it . I 'm trying to be optimistic . <p> What lessons can we learn from all this ? <p> Prevention is better than a cure . Doctors and nurses need to be paid more . They 're working with scientists , day and night , to drive the recovery of the world . <h> Edouard , 15 <p> Sorinne-la-Longue , Belgium <p> Edouard loves to cook and play basketball . Being confined to his house allows for more time of both -- he 's cooking meals for his family , refining his kitchen skills and playing basketball in the garden , where he 's set up a hoop . <p> What are you up to , aside from doing some school work online ? <p> I live at home with my parents and three sisters , so I do a little bit of school work , and a lot of sports . Some teachers are sending us some work to do on the school 's platform , but I really do n't have a lot to do . I read a lot of comics and watch a lot of TV . I ride my bike with my dad . I 'm trying to have some fun and do things that I would n't have thought of without the lockdown . <p> How worried are you about the pandemic ? <p> I 'm really worried about the poor and about the developing countries , since they may not have the means to help people infected with the virus . It could be a real disaster . <p> How are you keeping informed ? <p> I was keeping up toward the beginning of the outbreak , but I 'm becoming less and less informed . <p> Do you think life will be different once this is over ? <p> Yes . This has really made me reconsider consumption of certain resources . We 're learning to live without . <h> Anita , 15 <p> Rome <p> Anita 's in her second year at Pilo Albertelli , a high school in Rome . She 's been keeping up with studies online , but distance learning has n't been without its challenges . Anita has only ventured outside a handful of times in the past month , and is trying to see the lockdown as an opportunity to rest and slow the pace of normal life . " But other times , " she says , " not being able to go out drives me crazy . I miss going to school , athletics , and seeing my friends . " But she knows she 's lucky to be healthy . <p> What are you up to , aside from doing some school work ? <p> I read , and watch TV . Sometimes I do video calls with friends , or I draw . Our whole family has lunch together now ; that never happened before . I 'm actually amazed at how much I miss school . <p> How worried are you about the pandemic ? <p> Quite a bit . I 'm worried about the victims and worried that someone I know might get sick . I 'm scared that hospitals are overloaded and there are too few doctors . <p> How are you keeping informed ? <p> I watch the news and other programs that interview experts and politicians . Obviously , every time I open my phone I 'm bombarded with news , but I prefer to watch the news on TV . <p> What lessons can we learn from all this ? <p> Life can change in a second . I 'd never really thought about this before , but in so many other parts of the world this is commonplace . ( I 've also learned to wash my hands very well ! ) <h> Ali , 19 <p> New York <p> Ali was born in Turkey , grew up in Istanbul , then moved to London for high school and landed in New York last August , where he attends NYU . He 's planning a double major , in economics and philosophy -- and though the school is physically closed , classes have moved online , using Zoom . <p> " I do n't particularly enjoy that , " he says . " A lot of my classes are discussion-based , and not being in a physical room together makes people ( including myself ) less argumentative . " Ali says he 's finding it harder to focus at home and his academic motivation has taken a dip . <p> What you up to aside from school work ? <p> I am practicing self-isolation so I stay in as much as possible and do n't interact with people other than cashiers in grocery stores ( and even that is rare , as grocery stores mostly have self-checkout ) . As with everyone else , I find not being able to interact with my friends to be dreadful . We try to FaceTime each other as much as possible to cope , but it obviously does n't replace the real thing . Since the quarantine can potentially go on indefinitely , I wanted to be at least somewhat productive . So I 've reduced the amount of digital entertainment I consume to levels lower than before the quarantine , and have instead been reading books that have been on my list for a while but I never got around to . I 've also taken up cooking , though that has n't been a very successful endeavor . <p> How worried are you about the pandemic ? <p> I 'm really concerned about the impending recession as it will likely still be present as my peers and I enter the job market -- it 's going to be a lot more competitive . Aside from the increased competition , a lot of industries will be transformed significantly , which makes it hard for college students like myself to decide which skill sets will be valued most by employers . <p> How are you keeping informed ? <p> I exclusively use the internet . I read the Economist 's " Morning Briefing " and check in on CNN throughout the day on my phone . I also watch Youtube livestreams of New York Governor Andrew Cuomo 's daily updates , and some Turkish news . <p> Do you think life will be different once this is over ? <p> I think that most aspects of life will return to normal eventually , but there will definitely be changes . It 's hard to predict what they will be and how significant they will be , but I definitely think that we will be more appreciative of everyday things . The quarantine -- and the economic hardships that will follow -- is the grounding event of our generation that will hopefully show us how easily our freedom can disappear , and not even the governments can control it .
