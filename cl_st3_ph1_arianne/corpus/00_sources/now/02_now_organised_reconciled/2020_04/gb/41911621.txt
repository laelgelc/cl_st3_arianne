<p> A 12ft statue symbolising a human figure gazing out to sea has obstacles to overcome before it can become a new permanent feature on the Plymouth waterfront . <p> The cast-iron stack of blocks weighing almost three tonnes is by celebrated sculptor Sir Antony Gormley , who created the iconic Angel of the North at Gateshead , in the north-east of England . <p> But public reaction to the work called LOOK II has been mixed , and the planning process has thrown up questions over the safety of the proposed site on the West Pier at West Hoe . <p> The twice lifesize statue was announced by Plymouth City Council as one of the key artworks to mark the opening of The Box museum and heritage centre , and is set to become part of this year 's Mayflower 400 celebrations . <p> But before the 2,700kg structure can be bolted into place on the stone structure , the project has to go through the planning process . <p> And that means close scrutiny of the details , as well as a chance for the public to have their say . <p> A mock-up of how Sir Antony Gormley 's LOOK II would look in situ at West Hoe <p> Objections to the applications for planning permission and listed building consent to install the work on the pier have been submitted by people unhappy with the scheme . <p> The words they use include " monstrosity " , " hideous " , " out of place " , " ugly " and " waste of money " . <p> The head of contemporary art for The Box , which is buying the statue for an undisclosed sum , accepts that not everyone will like it , but hopes it will become a major attraction for the city . <p> But questions raised about the statue project are not only about its financial and artistic value . <p> The team responsible for the city council 's flood risk management strategy has advised against fixing it to the Victorian structure because of the risk of damage from a severe storm . <p> A report responding to the planning application said : " There has been significant damage to property in the immediate vicinity within the last ten years , and the impact of waves would present an unacceptable risk to the statue , the pier and the surrounding area . " <p> The piers at West Hoe , Plymouth . LOOK II is planned to go on the structure at the left ( Image : Google ) <p> A flood risk assessment carried out by consultants says there is a risk of the sea washing over the top , with a once-in-200-years chance of the waves being a metre higher than the pier . They recommend access should be restricted when severe storms are forecast and the public warned of the danger . <p> The report for the city council from John Grimes Partnership says that with the rise in sea levels and climate change " there is considerable future flood risk to the site . It is planned to fix it to reinforced concrete foundations on piles in holes drilled into the pier structure , with the surface around the base finished with limestone slabs . <p> The site chosen is the listed West Pier , where Sir Francis Chichester landed in 1967 as he became the first person to sail single-handed around the world by the clipper route in his yacht Gypsy Moth . <p> The edges of the two piers which surround an inner basin to moor boats are lined with safety chains hanging between metal posts . <p> An image of LOOK II on West Pier ( Image : Space Design Architecture ) <p> A document from consultants Space Design Architecture submitted with the applications , known as a design and access statement , describes the statue as " a striking and thought-provoking sculpture in the public realm by an artist of international significance , creating immense civic pride , inspiration and wonder . " <p> The iron structure will be allowed to rust naturally . It will be cleaned using water so as not to disturb the natural weathering , and any rust gathering at the base will be washed away . <p> Sir Antony says the work links the ancient stones of the Stonehenge era to modern skyscrapers . He wants it to invoke the yearning to travel to a new world , like the passengers on the Mayflower which left Plymouth for North America 400 years ago . <p> But public criticism submitted online during the consultation period has been fierce . <p> A series of objections include one from David Boon describing the statue as an " ugly monstrosity " and questioning the maintenance cost . <p> Another said the statue was " a complete waste " of taxpayers ' money . <p> Phil Nelson said West Hoe Pier was not a solid enough foundation to hold the statue against the force of the sea . <p> He warned children would use it as a climbing frame or diving board and it would obstruct anglers , pointing out that a plaque about Sir Francis Chichester 's voyage was washed away in a storm . <p> Vision - how ' Look II ' would stand on Plymouth 's West Hoe Pier <p> Jeff Jacks considered the sculpture " totally out of character with the historic waterfront " and said the use of public money would cause an outcry .
