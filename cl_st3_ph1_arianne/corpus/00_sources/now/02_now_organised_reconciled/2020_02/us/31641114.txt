<p> After years of playing third fiddle to solar and wind power , geothermal energy is poised to start growing again in California . <p> Three local energy providers have signed contracts this month for electricity from new geothermal power plants , one in Imperial County near the Salton Sea and the other in Mono County along the Eastern Sierra . The new plants will be the first geothermal facilities built in California in nearly a decade -- potentially marking a long-awaited turning point for a technology that could play a critical role in the state 's transition to cleaner energy sources . <p> Geothermal plants can generate emissions-free , renewable electricity around the clock , unlike solar panels or wind turbines . The technology has been used commercially for decades and involves tapping naturally heated underground reservoirs to create steam and turn turbines . <p> Despite those advantages , development has been bogged down by high costs .
