<p> BEIRUT , Feb 8 ( Reuters ) - Lebanon 's President Michel Aoun said on Saturday that foreign states , particularly France , had expressed a desire to help his country emerge from a severe economic crisis . <p> As Lebanon grapples with its worst economic and financial strains in decades , foreign donors have said they will only help if the government enacts long-stalled reforms . <p> Aoun said he had a phone call with French President Emmanuel Macron and the two would speak again in the next few days . <p> " A number of states have expressed their desire to help Lebanon , with France at their forefront , " Aoun 's office quoted him as saying on Saturday in an interview with French magazine Valeurs Actuelles . It did not elaborate . <p> The new Lebanese government has approved a rescue plan that envisages seeking foreign help , calls for interest rate cuts , and warns some " painful steps " will be necessary , according to a copy seen by Reuters on Thursday . <p> The policy statement will be presented to parliament next week for a vote of confidence . <p> Prime @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the support of the Iran-backed Hezbollah movement and its political allies , which hold a parliamentary majority . <p> The government must contend with a liquidity crunch , shattered confidence in banks , a weakened Lebanese pound and soaring inflation . <p> It took office some three months after Saad al-Hariri 's government resigned under pressure from nationwide protests against a ruling elite accused of corruption .
