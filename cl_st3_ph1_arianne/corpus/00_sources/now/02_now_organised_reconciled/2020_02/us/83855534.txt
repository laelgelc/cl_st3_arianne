<h> Consumption-driven climate change leaves the poor worse off <p> Climate change is disproportionately impacting the poor , elderly and people with a disability , according to an expert from The Australian National University ( ANU ) . <p> Professor Sharon Friel also argues we need a better long term approach to mitigate this problem . <p> She is calling for Australia and the world to move away from what she calls the ' consumptagenic ' system -- the institutions , policies , values and behaviors that drive excess production and consumption , which then act as drivers for climate change and health inequalities . <p> " The world has warmed to dangerous levels and the disruption to the Earth system is profound , " Professor Friel said . <p> " No one will be untouched by climate change , but it is not experienced equally . <p> " Affluent people can afford to live in insulated buildings with air-conditioning and air purifiers . The poor , older people , or those with pre-existing conditions are the least able to adapt to changing conditions , and live in dwellings and environments that amplify its effects .
