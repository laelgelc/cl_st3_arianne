<h> Strength in diversity : Singapore 's intriguing place names <p> A signboard at the Little India MRT station in Singapore points the way to Buffalo Road and Kerbau Road - Photograph : Benedict Lopez <p> Acceptance and respect for race , religion , culture and language -- even in place names -- goes a long way towards uplifting socioeconomic conditions , writes Benedict Lopez . <p> The renaming of historical places has been a common phenomenon in many developing countries , particularly those that were under colonial rule . <p> Often some politicians attempt to gain maximum political mileage by arguing that the best way to remove the residual stains of colonisation would be by removing places named after colonialists . Sometimes the new names were those of well-known local personalities . At other times , places were renamed after undesirable characters . In some countries , roads have even been renamed after murderers , with the justification that they were ' nationalists ' . But would a true nationalist resort to murder ? <p> Some countries like Sri Lanka ( previously Ceylon ) and Zimbabwe ( previously Rhodesia ) changed the names of their countries . In many parts of Malaysia like Penang , street names were given a national flavour by substituting street for lebuh , lane for lorong , road for jalan , drive for persiaran , and -- for the most part -- leaving the personality they were originally named after unaltered . There is even a statute of Raffles in North Bank at the spot where he was supposed to have ' founded ' Singapore in 1819 . <p> Unlike many countries , Singapore has gone a step further by displaying notices and making public announcements in mass rapid transit stations in the four major languages spoken in the country . Singapore is perhaps is one of the few countries in the world -- if not the only -- to have these announcements and notices in all the main languages spoken by its people . <p> On a recent visit to Singapore , I took a keen interest in reading the names of some MRT stations . Names like Little India and Chinatown need no explanation . <p> Stations like Dhoby Ghaut aroused my curiosity @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ found historical reasons for the naming . Dhobi is a Hindi word meaning laundrymen . The word ghaut is also a Hindi word meaning by the river side . In bygone days , Dhoby Ghaut used to be by the river side , where Indian men living in this area in Singapore did their laundry along the river banks . <p> As a Catholic , I was aware of the significance of the naming of another station , Novena , in 1985 . Novena is a popular weekly devotional prayer session held at the nearby Catholic Church of St Alphonsus , more popularly known as the Novena Church . Novena is derived from the Latin word novem , which means nine , and refers to prayers offered for nine consecutive days . Catholics from all over Singapore and Malaysia converge on this church to attend the novena service , especially on Saturdays .
