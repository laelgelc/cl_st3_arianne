<p> The second-largest freshwater lake in Malaysia , Tasik Chini in Pahang , is facing impending " death " owing to logging and mining , and consequent water pollution in its vicinity , as revealed by The Malaysian Insight recently . <p> Tasik Chini , which is second to Tasik Bera in size , was conferred world biosphere reserve status by Unesco in 2009 , given that it is home to 87 species of freshwater fish , 189 species of birds , 51 low-forest species , 25 aquatic plants and 15 fresh-water-swamp forest species . <p> Yet its rich biodiversity and idyllic beauty , which has gained international recognition , does not seem to be able to prevent ecologically destructive human activities in its surroundings . <p> It is as if these perpetrators are intent on making Tasik Chini another Tasik Mentiga ( also in Pahang ) , which has been pronounced " extinct " due to developments in its vicinity , which , in turn , caused it to dry up . <p> Support the struggle to build a New
