<h> SMILE project addresses off-grid islands ' power , dev't needs <p> A Solar ComMunity-Based Island Tourism and Livelihood Energizer Platform ( SMILE Project ) was being piloted in four sites of this province which addresses the islands ' power and development needs . <p> The SMILE project , which is being funded by the European Union ( EU ) , is aimed at enhancing their capacity and resiliency against the impacts of climate change by harnessing the power of technology , such as renewable solar energy that have low-to-zero GHG emissions and connected to a resilient and efficient power network to provide sustainable and adequate 24/7 electricity ; provides environmentally sound livelihood interventions to attain longer term human and environmental resilience ; and developing the islands ' eco-tourism potentials . <p> All these were made possible by the World Wide Fund for Nature-Philippines ( WWF-Philippines ) , together with the University of the Philippines Asian Institute of Tourism ( UPAIT ) , Association of Isolated Electric Cooperatives ( AIEC ) through its subsidiary , AIEC Missionary Green Energy Corporation ( AIEC MGEC ) , Siargao Electric Cooperative ( SIARELCO ) , Dinagat Island Electric Cooperative ( DIELCO ) @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Tourism Council , in cooperation with the local government units of the Province of Surigao del Norte and Dinagat Islands . <p> Small-scale fishing communities often live a vulnerable existence . Physically , fishing is a demanding job . It also solely depends on what nature can offer at a particular time . And fishing is fraught with danger , even at the best of times . <p> In these days where the global community is facing a climate emergency , fishing resources , just like any other environmental resources that sustain households and communities are on a steady decline due to overexploitation in some parts that resulted to a diminishing supply . This put intense pressure on fishing families and communities to make ends meet . <p> A disruption in fishing is also a disruption in the community since fishing is not just a livelihood , especially in the islands , but a way of life . Identified climate change drivers of risks are sea-level rise ( SLR ) , storm surge , tropical storm , increasing air and sea surface temperature and changing rainfall patterns . The risks also include loss of adaptive capacity and ecosystem services on which lives and livelihood of small island inhabitants depend . <p> Tourists are already visiting these islands to surf , dive or snorkel , among others , but due to lack of reliable 24-hour power and accommodation , there is hardly any development going on in the communities . <p> An essential part of sustainable eco-tourism is an eco-friendly accommodation that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ its core . Eco-tourism is hailed as a more sustainable form of tourism since the tourists aim to reduce their impact on the environment while partaking in activities that immerse them in nature and promote the conservation of the natural environment they have come to enjoy . <p> Eco-tourism , according to the World Conservation Union , is an " environmentally responsible travel to natural areas , in order to enjoy and appreciate nature ( and accompanying cultural features , both past and present ) that promote conservation , have a low visitor impact and provide for beneficially active socio-economic involvement of local peoples . " <p> Integral to eco-tourism is the use of renewable energy in the project sites . In the case of the SMILE project , a solar powerhouse will be constructed on each site . Powering these powerhouses is an array of solar panels on the roofs of the micro hotels which will also be constructed in the four barangays to accommodate tourists . <p> With such power plant , the islands become self-sufficient in terms of energy and will no longer rely on GHG-emitting and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ guests , and also the wildlife .
