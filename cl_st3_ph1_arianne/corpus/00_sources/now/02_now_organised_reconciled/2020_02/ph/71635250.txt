( Photo by Kyaw Kyaw Winn ) <p> The ASEAN region is rich with wetlands , and is home to 60 per cent of the tropical peatlands and 42 per cent of mangrove forests in the world ( Fifth ASEAN State of the Environment Report , 2017 ) . While the ASEAN Member States ( AMS ) have been stepping up efforts to conserve and sustainably manage wetlands , these areas are constantly under threat and need greater attention from governments , concerned organisations , and members of the private sector . <p> Wetlands are @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ meets water ; they are among nature 's underrated protectors and providers of ecosystem services . Two out of five of the world 's species either live or breed in these ecosystems , according to the Ramsar Convention , or the Convention on Wetlands , an international treaty to which most AMS are parties . Species living in ASEAN wetlands include the endangered Sumatran tiger and Bornean orangutan . Wetlands in the region are also wintering grounds for 50 million waterbirds migrating through the East Asian-Australasian Flyway ( EAAF ) every year . <p> Fish , shellfish , and seaweed that thrive in wetlands provide food sources to the people of ASEAN and the rest of the world . Also , healthy wetlands clean the soil of heavy metals and other pollutants , thus supplying clean drinking water to communities . Wetlands and their rich biodiversity play an important role in the economy . Their waters provide irrigation for agriculture . Ecotourism activities like birdwatching , trekking , and boating provide income opportunities to people living off wetlands . These ecosystems act as buffers against strong winds from storms , and flooding from heavy rain and sea level rise . Mangroves in particular , protect coastlines and coastal towns . Peatlands , meanwhile , are exceptional carbon sinks , storing 30 per cent of the world 's land-based carbon . <p> But our protectors need further protecting . Wetlands and their biodiversity face threats like logging , pollution , and land conversion for construction , agriculture , among others . Worldwide , over 35 per cent of wetlands have been lost , and 25 per cent of wetland species are globally threatened ( Global Wetland Outlook of the Ramsar Convention , 2018 ) . <p> This World Wetlands Day , the ACB highlights the richness of biodiversity in wetlands and the importance of conserving and restoring these ecosystems . <p> The ACB commends the AMS @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ list of Ramsar sites , or internationally important wetlands being conserved and sustainably used , is growing , with 25 new designated sites over the past decade . At the moment , the ASEAN region has 56 Ramsar sites covering over 2.5 million hectares , and more sites for nomination . <p> All the AMS have been engaged in ecosystem restoration initiatives , among these mangrove reforestation projects . Now that we are on the first year of the United Nations Decade on Ecosystem Restoration , efforts like these are timely and relevant . Restoration draws more species and allows them to flourish once again . For example , the restoration of peatlands in a national park in Indonesia increased the population of key fauna species , including orangutans . Generally , mangroves serve as nurseries to marine species like fish and shrimp , as mangroves ' organic matter provide food , while their roots protect from predators . Restoring mangroves , thus , helps increase fish catch for food and livelihood opportunities . <p> A recent initiative in the region is the ASEAN Flyway Network , a virtual network @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ASEAN region that facilitates regional cooperation needed to ensure the conservation of the migratory waterbirds and the wetlands that support them along the EAAF . The ACB facilitated AFN through the project Improving Biodiversity Conservation of Wetlands and Migratory Waterbirds in ASEAN Region . The project is supported by the Japan-ASEAN Integration Fund and is led by Singapore , through the National Parks Board . From this initiative , we can provide more data from waterbird counts and wetland assessments in selected sites . These are crucial in planning conservation measures in the region . <p> With greater attention on ASEAN 's biodiversity conservation , including wetland-related concerns , the ASEAN and the ACB strive to come up with a unified and stronger voice as the world develops a landmark agreement on biodiversity . The post-2020 global biodiversity framework will guide governments in efforts towards halting biodiversity loss and conserving and sustainably using what is left .
