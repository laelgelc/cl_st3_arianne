<p> Traditionally , trees in a municipal setting are seen as " nice to have " and end up at the bottom of the list of budget priorities . <p> That 's no longer the case in York Region , where " green infrastructure " has gone from " nice to have " to " must have , " says James Lane , manager of natural heritage and forestry for the Regional Municipality of York . <p> Lane was speaking at the recent Forests Ontario 2020 Conference . <p> " There are a lot of competing interests out there " at budget time , he said , and green infrastructure , from boulevard trees to parks , wetlands and regional forests , has not been treated as an asset , in the same way as roads , bridges and watermains . <p> It was only when York Region 's forestry department adopted the principles of asset management that it became possible to develop a long-term sustainable plan for managing green infrastructure , and secure stable annual funding in the budget . <p> The trick was to " put the need for
