<p> There is a solution to the single-use plastic problem , and it 's one that has been right under the noses of policy makers for decades . <p> It 's paper , says Jessica Kaknevicius . <p> Kaknevicius is the vice-president of Sustainable Forestry Initiative ( SFI ) , a partnership of industry , educational institutions and First Nations that uses " forest-focused collaborations " to create sustainability , new programs and green jobs . <p> Forest-based products are renewable , compostable and sustainable , Kaknevicius pointed out , and now is the perfect time , " when we 're trying to look for sustainability , " to find new solutions and educate the public . <p> Plastic was at one time hailed as the perfect solution to packaging challenges : lightweight , inexpensive and flexible . <p> It 's only recently that the scale of the problem created by plastics has become known . <p> According to EarthDay.org , about eight million tonnes of plastic end up in the oceans annually , including microplastics , which are the tiny fragments that can enter the food chain . <p> With growing public concern , Kaknevicius said , " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Responsible packaging is needed . " <p> She was one of the speakers at the recent Forests Ontario 2020 Conference held at the Nottawasaga Inn Resort in Alliston , talking about a new role for forests in finding solutions to environmental challenges . <p> Kaknevicius acknowledged that there is a need for a " culture shift " away from the current " disposable " society . She cited a recent survey in the United States that showed its citizens were as aware of the issue of plastic pollution in the oceans as they were of climate change -- and far more concerned . Sixty-five per cent of respondents said they were worried about single-use plastics , while only 58 per cent were concerned about climate change . <p> And single-use plastics topped the list of major issues that people believe they could impact . <p> Kaknevicius noted that the Canadian government plans to ban single-use plastics by 2021 . <p> " We can help the government get to that point , " she told the audience . " Single-use plastics can be replaced with sustainable forest products .
