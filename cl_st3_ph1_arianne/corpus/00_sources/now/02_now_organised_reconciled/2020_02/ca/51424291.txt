<h> Big city mayors asks feds for permanent public transit funding ahead of budget <p> Mayors of Canada 's largest cities are pitching the federal government on support for public transit and boosting affordable housing ahead of a budget the Liberals say will be environmentally focused . <p> Mayors were in Ottawa this week to push the Liberals to move on an election promise to permanently fund public transit expansion projects and to accelerate the transition towards zero-emission transit vehicles . <p> They also want new funding to top up an oversubscribed federal program providing money for projects on disaster mitigation and climate change adaptation , such as flood-proofing coastlines . <p> As well , cities are calling for Ottawa to launch a program to maintain affordable rental housing stock and to build more homes for Indigenous peoples and those living with mental illness and substance use . <p> Edmonton mayor Don Iveson , chair of the Federation of Canadian Municipalities ' ( FCM ) big city mayors caucus , told reporters on Thursday that the group crafted their budget asks with that lens in mind . <p> " We prioritize the asks that align with the climate imperative @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have declared climate emergencies .
