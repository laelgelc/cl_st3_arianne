The simplest conclusion to draw would be that MacKay , O'Toole and Gladu are simply responding to the Conservative electorate that is front of them . <p> Public opinion on climate change in Canada is sharply divided right now . There are those who view climate change as a major threat and believe reducing greenhouse gas emissions should be a government priority . And there are those who see climate change as a secondary concern and would sooner deal with other issues before making significant changes to reduce emissions . <p> Members of the former group tend to vote Liberal , New Democrat or Green . Members of the latter group tend to vote for the Conservatives . <p> For the moment , at least , the leading contenders do n't seem eager to push the party much beyond the Scheer plan . <p> The candidates could be betting that at least part of the debate will @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ federal election comes around . If the Liberals keep their commitments , they 'll have implemented a clean fuel standard , " just transition " legislation for unemployed energy workers , and climate change accountability legislation by the time of the next election . Would the next Conservative leader promise to uphold , enhance or repeal such measures ? <p> Will the next Conservative leader commit to putting Canada on a path for net-zero by 2050 ?
