<p> Rainbow schools are doing their part this month to reduce energy use and raise awareness regarding the plight of polar bears . <p> With International Polar Bear Day approaching on Feb. 27 , the Rainbow District School Board is urging staff and students to take the Polar Bear Challenge , which includes steps to curb global warming . <p> " Students will explore the connection between greenhouse gas emissions and melting sea ice , and how they each pose a threat to the polar bear species , " said Norm Blaseg , education director with the board , in a release . " Schools can reduce emissions by turning down the thermostat , powering-off electronics , or hosting a Polar Bear Walk to raise awareness . " <p> Polar bears are classified as a species of special concern under the National Species at Risk Act , the board notes , and the iconic white beasts are listed as threatened under provincial legislation . <p> " Habitat loss and reduced access to seal prey due to climate change are the primary concerns , " the board says . " <p> Rapid loss of sea ice is the most significant threat to the polar bear 's long-term survival . <p> " Greenhouse gases in the atmosphere act like a blanket that keeps the earth warm , " the release notes . " This protective blanket helps make our planet habitable . " <p> Over the past 200 years , however , humans have greatly increased the level of greenhouse gases by burning fossil fuels like oil and coal . <p> " This is like throwing on a second blanket , causing temperatures to rise . " <p> The board 's environmental committee issues monthly challenges to staff and students throughout the school year . <p> Last month , students ate pulses ( edible seeds like chickpeas and lentils ) and shared recipes for the hearty , low-cost food source .
