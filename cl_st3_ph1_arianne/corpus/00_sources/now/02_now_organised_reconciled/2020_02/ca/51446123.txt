THE CANADIAN PRESS/Andrew Vaughan <p> Canada appears poised to rack up a climate-change win , says a recent government report submitted to the United Nations . <p> The federal report filed last month says Canada is on track to meet one of its crucial climate-change commitments -- generating at least 90 per cent of non-industrial electricity from emissions-free sources by 2030 . <h> Trending Stories <p> " Yes , it 's good news , " said David Sawyer of the Smart Prosperity Institute , a University of Ottawa research and policy institute . <p> " It shows the provinces and federal government have been doing a lot and they 've somehow managed to do more than we thought they could do . About 44 per cent of that is expected to come from non-emitting sources , but the total amount generated is much smaller . <p> The assessment is based on policies already in place and at least partly implemented . If projections come to pass , it will mean releases from power utilities -- one of Canada 's major emissions sources -- will have declined by 80 per cent since 2005 . <p> As late as last year , Canada was expected to reach 85 per cent emissions-free utility generation -- another in a list of expected shortfalls in the country 's international commitments . <p> Sawyer attributes most of the extra cuts to better hydro connections between provinces and new federal rules that assign carbon costs to natural gas . <p> " That 's the reason we 're on track , " he said . <p> The projected success proves that effective climate change legislation can combine regulations and carbon taxes , Sawyer said @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ taxes made the final push . <p> " This argument either/or , regulation or carbon tax , is not really the way governments are doing regulation , " Sawyer said . " We need both and we are using both . " <p> Overall , Canada will still have a long way to go . <p> Current policies are expected to drive the country 's emissions down to 673 megatonnes by 2030 . The target is 511 megatonnes . <p> The report to the UN promises further climate change measures such as a clean fuel standard that is aimed at getting Canada to within 77 megatonnes of its goal . But the document offers no firm answers on closing that final gap . <p> Canada has committed to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 30 per cent below 2005 levels by 2030 and has promised to be carbon neutral by 2050.
