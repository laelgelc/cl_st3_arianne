<h> Dutch photographer Sarah Gerats spent the past seven years in one of the world 's northernmost towns . <p> Growing up in the northern Dutch city of Alkmaar , photographer Sarah Gerats had always felt a pull to explore farther and farther north . Eventually , she stopped at 78 degrees north latitude , in one of the world 's northernmost towns : tiny Longyearbyen , on Norway 's Spitsbergen island . <p> Seven years on , the 36-year-old 's day-to-day life is spent touring the Arctic and Antarctic by boat , running an artist residency on Spitsbergen and sleeping in her old Volvo . I spoke to her about what it 's like to live in a place where the sun does n't rise between October and February , and where you need to make sure you 're armed every time you leave the house . <p> VICE : How does someone end up in a place like Spitsbergen ? I immediately felt that I had to stay . <p> I used to only consider living in country capitals . But in a small town like this , with 2,000 people , all the practical stuff is so much easier .
