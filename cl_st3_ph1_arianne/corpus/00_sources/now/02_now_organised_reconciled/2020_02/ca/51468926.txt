<p> Ramsey Lake , the drinking water source for some 40,000 Sudburians , is salty , upwards of 30 times higher than the city 's other primary drinking water source , Lake Wanapitei . <p> These are the results of a study released Feb. 19 by the Greater Sudbury Watershed Alliance . In September and October 2019 , volunteers with the Minnow Lake Restoration group collected water samples from a dozen public and private locations fed by drinking water from Ramsey Lake ( eight locations ) and Wanapitei River ( four locations ) . <p> The samples were analyzed by Testmark Laboratories , the alliance said in a news release . <p> As is evidence in the results , depending on where you live in the Nickel City , the amount of sodium and chloride you 're consuming can vary widely . <p> " When water systems contain sodium concentrations higher than 20 mg/L , the Medical Officer of Health must be advised , who in turn notifies community physicians , " the alliance stated . " For persons with chronic diseases requiring a sodium-restricted diet , the intake of sodium from drinking water could be significant . Especially important are zooplankton , a significant group of microscopic organisms that ' graze ' on algae ( including blue green algae ) , " the release stated . " Recent evidence suggests that chloride may be more toxic to zooplankton in lakes on the Cambrian Shield . Therefore , a resilient aquatic ecosystem in all three sampled lakes may be threatened . " <p> As chloride levels rise , zooplankton populations shrink . Lake temperature rise as a result of climate change coupled with a loss of zooplankton could lead to increasing algal blooms , the alliance said .
