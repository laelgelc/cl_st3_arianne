<h> Maori by absorption <p> How realistic is the aspiration to have a million New Zealanders speaking basic te reo by 2040 ? <p> Some say it 's impossible , and the findings released in a Royal Society journal , based on census data , make it look pretty bleak . They say the language is on its way to extinction . <p> But there is hope . <p> Today , Sharon Brettkelly talks to a data cruncher who believes we 're asking the wrong questions in the census -- and the situation is more hopeful than we 're being led to believe . Chris Lane says the question is vague and does n't give us a reliable picture . " You get quite a different picture depending on which statistics you look at , " he says . <p> He 's examined data from four different surveys that put new light on the issue . <p> Lane argues there is evidence of a revival in te reo , but the statistics do n't reflect the differences in proficiency . <p> He also says globally , Maori are in the fore-front of a language revival , and the rest @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Countries such as Wales do a good job of teaching Welsh in schools , but Lane says that has n't translated to a revival of the language at home , and in fact the language appears to still be declining there . <p> " We may have thought that was the case with Maori because a lot of the researchers tended to focus on kohanga reo and kura kaupapa and educational interventions , so we 've almost not known what 's been happening in families , " he says .
