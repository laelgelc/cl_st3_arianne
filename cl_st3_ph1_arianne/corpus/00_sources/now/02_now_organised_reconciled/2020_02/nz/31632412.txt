<p> An investigation into microplastic pollution in Auckland 's waterways has suggested it 's not broken-down litter , but tiny fibres from our washing that are likely causing the biggest problems . <p> So small that they 're typically invisible to the naked eye , microplastics nonetheless pose a goliath threat to our oceans . <p> These particles of broken-down plastic products are now being found within rainwater , sea salt , air and even us , having entered the food chain through species like tuna and mackerel . <p> Just-published findings from a collaborative survey , focused on nearly 40 sites across Auckland , showed the bulk of these particles were fibres that may likely be traced back to the washing of clothing . <p> The study , involving researchers from Scion , University of Canterbury , Auckland Council and Watercare , also suggested the problem was no better -- or worse -- in New Zealand than any other part of the world . <p> " We wanted to establish a sense of the scale of the plastic microparticle issue in New Zealand , and evidential data looking at the sources , " said Dr Florian Graichen , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " These are crucial first steps to design enduring solutions which successfully reduce and remove plastic pollution from New Zealand 's environment .
