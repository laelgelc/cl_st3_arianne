<h> Coronavirus a global ' misinfodemic ' <p> Never before has there been so much true - and false - information exploding through our media channels as an unprecedented flow of information about coronavirus reaches almost every community on earth <p> The emergence of 2019 novel coronavirus is not an exceptional event , after all , infectious diseases have to come from somewhere . What is new is the fact we have the technology to identify it fast , track it , stop it , cure it , and vaccinate against it : We are literally watching the birth of a new human infection in real time . <p> But what is also new is that never before has there been so much true , and false , information exploding through our media channels , of which there are vast numbers , reaching almost every community on earth . <p> This unprecedented flow of information has enabled remarkable progress to be made in both controlling this outbreak as well as potential cures and vaccines , all in a month . <p> However , along with useful facts have come rumours , and misinformation . This is a problem that requires effort at all levels -- from government , to health sector , to communities , key influencers , and the public . <p> While not all of us can find a cure or invent a vaccine , we can be part of the solution to the misinfodemic and not part of the problem by fact-checking before sharing , and sharing good information from a reputable source . Try Snopes or some other fact-checking website . <p> Risk communication <p> This leads nicely into the importance of risk communication , always a vital part of any crisis . Risk communication is defined as an interactive process whereby information that helps people understand their risk is exchanged between experts and affected people . In short , it helps protect people 's health during the crisis . <p> If the key messages do not reach everyone , then people lose trust in the leaders who are there to protect them , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ coordinated team effort . To accurately estimate infectiousness we need to know how many people have been infected and who they got it from . <p> Since quick testing became widely available ( in the last couple of weeks ) more people can be diagnosed . Modelling from the Imperial College in London has estimated around 100,000 ( 30,000 -- 200,000 ) are likely to be infected . Of course the more you test the more you are likely to find . <p> Several research groups have provided estimates of the infectiousness of this virus and they generally land in the vicinity of one infectious person infecting 2-3 other people . That is a little bit more than your average influenza ( about 1.4 -- 4 other people ) , but way less infectious than measles ( 12-18 ) , whooping cough ( 12-17 ) , chickenpox ( 8-10 ) and many other infections . <p> It is not yet established whether the infection can be spread by a person who has no symptoms , but this is a question of burning importance . Infections like flu and the common cold can be spread by people who have no symptoms . In these cases the viruses are carried into the air when they breathe and speak . <p> How deadly ? <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ many people have actually been infected . Of the first 99 cases hospitalised in Wuhan , the average age was 55 , 67 were men , and half had underlying chronic diseases .
