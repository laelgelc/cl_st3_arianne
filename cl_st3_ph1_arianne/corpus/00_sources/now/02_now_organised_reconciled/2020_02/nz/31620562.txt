<h> Can Te Tiriti help solve our big problems ? <p> Te Tiriti is always speaking , but are we listening ? Victoria University of Wellington 's Carwyn Jones says Te Tiriti can help us respond effectively to the urgent challenges of our time . <p> At the beginning of this new decade , we are confronted with a range of complex issues , not least the global challenge of responding to the climate crisis . Does Te Tiriti o Waitangi speak to these urgent issues we face today ? <p> In 1840 , our ancestors responded to some of the challenges of a rapidly-changing world by entering into a treaty relationship where public power in Aotearoa would be shared between Maori and the Crown . But does this relationship provide useful guidance for New Zealand society 180 years later ? <p> The answer is simple : Yes . <p> As has been said before , Te Tiriti is always speaking . <p> We know breaches of the promises made in Te Tiriti has meant the vision of power-sharing has not yet been realised . Our first report , He Waka Roimata ( June 2019 ) , recorded the many problems with the system identified by people we spoke with around the country . <p> The overwhelming message we heard was the system is not working and often adds to harm suffered by individuals and communities . Furthermore , this is an approach that will benefit all of us , driving more inclusive , holistic and restorative responses to harm and strengthening our communities . <p> A framework based on Te Tiriti would also help to address issues of health equity . <p> In 2019 , the Waitangi Tribunal released its Report on Stage One of the Health Services and Outcomes Kaupapa Inquiry . Focused on the primary healthcare system , this report ought to be a significant contribution to developing health policy . <p> The objective of achieving equitable health outcomes is central to the Tribunal 's findings and recommendations . <p> All parties to this inquiry recognised health inequity is influenced by a wide range of factors , including income and poverty , employment , education , housing and the cumulative effects of colonisation . For example , the Tribunal recommended the Crown explore the possibility of establishing a stand-alone Maori primary health authority . <p> Te Tiriti also suggests ways of approaching policy to respond to global issues . And there is currently no bigger or more urgent global issue than climate change . <p> The research suggests that , for various reasons , indigenous peoples are likely to be disproportionately negatively affected by the impacts of climate change . Maori are no exception . And it is becoming increasingly clear the system that created this climate crisis will not provide the radical thinking or develop the processes and actions we need to respond to it . <p> Te Tiriti points to different models , different values on which to base decisions , and different ways of understanding the relationships between people , lands and waters . Fraught and flawed as it is , the process for settling historical claims based on Te Tiriti has shown us glimpses of the potential of reframing environmental relationships .
