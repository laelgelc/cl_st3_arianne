<h> Exporters need more info on novel coronavirus <p> The National Party trade spokesperson Todd McClay is worried the government is not doing enough to avoid an information vacuum about novel coronavirus ' effect on trade and business . <p> McClay said there was a lot of concern , particularly within the tourism and education sectors . <p> Given the jobs and reliance on the Chinese market , officials should be doing everything they can to prise information , he said . <p> " Not all answers will be there , but certainly the government needs to engage more and provide a lot more open information to the export community so they can start to have a bit more certainty and can start planning , " he said . <p> New Zealand log exporters are bracing themselves for supply chain problems due to the outbreak and tourist operators are experiencing a drop in chinese visitor numbers due to travel restrictions . <p> The Forest Owners Association said some companies were already reducing their harvesting rate and that would reduce employment . <p> Forest Owners Association president Peter Weir said problems stemmed from the virus spread in China , extensions @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ from increased softwood supply from Europe . <p> Weir said it was not clear how long harvesting will be paused . <p> It was too early to predict whether lower exports may help the Chinese market recover , he said . <p> " The situation is very fluid , has the potential to become very serious , but it is not armageddon - our resource is not on fire like on Australia . Forest owners large and small who supply the export market have either cut back or are considering cutting back on harvesting so that means we have to ask our contractors to shut down . " <p> Many forest owners had shut down harvesting on Monday and the halt could last three weeks or three months , Weir said . <p> " We 're monitoring log inventories in China .
