<h> Councils in tsunami firing line not doing enough <p> Researchers say councils need to be doing much more to ensure homes and buildings are n't wiped out when a mega-tsunami eventually strikes the east coast of the North Island . <p> It 's predicted an earthquake in the offshore Hikurangi trench will trigger a one-in-1000-year tsunami - swamping up to 300 kilometres of coastline from Te Araroa down to Wellington and around the Cook Strait to Paraparaumu . <p> Civil Defence has the job of mapping its possible impacts and getting people ready to evacuate , while according to local government New Zealand , councils should be ensuring buildings and lifeline infrastructure are built to cope with natural hazards , and their land use does n't put communities at risk . <p> But recent research by Miles Crawford from Massey University 's Joint Centre for Disaster Research suggests councils are putting that job in the too-hard basket . <p> After sifting through council policies from Wellington , Hawke 's Bay and Gisborne -
