If so , maybe it will disappear off the political radar as quickly as it arrived . <p> But from my point of view , the issue being discussed -- exclusive fresh water rights ( ownership ) -- remains as a matter for concern if this type of approach is to become the norm when seeking a resolution to our problems around this vital resource . <p> Think about it for a moment . <p> The proposal to effectively blackmail the Government into agreeing to provide $1 billion for exclusive use by iwi on their private lands to build water storage reservoirs that will @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ deferral of court action , is abhorrent in its concept and breathtaking in its audacity . <p> In writing this column I am forced to temporarily abandon my own series of columns that are designed to discuss this important issue in a more inclusive way . <p> I had hoped that we might be able as a community , dependent as we all are upon this lifeblood for our survival , to sit down together and calmly seek a resolution to the problem of fresh water delivery to all sectors , before the threat of climate change makes the task so much harder . <p> Mr Te Aho 's plans seem designed to railroad those sorts of consultations to the scrap heap . He has chosen instead to take a more direct path in seeking an accommodation for his own people 's needs .
