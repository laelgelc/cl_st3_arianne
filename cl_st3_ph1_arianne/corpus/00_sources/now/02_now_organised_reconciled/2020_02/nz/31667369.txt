<h> It 's more than Weinstein <p> Movie Mogul Harvey Weinstein has been convicted of rape . But the problem is much bigger and more insidious than a few bad men , writes Victoria University 's Jan Jordan . <p> In May 2017 , New York Times journalist Jodi Kantor had a conversation with Hollywood actress Rose McGowan that began the downfall of movie mogul Harvey Weinstein , who was on Monday convicted of sexual assault by a New York jury and faces further charges in Los Angeles . <p> Following Kantor 's article , the producer of multiple successful films from Kill Bill to The English Patient was under watch for sexual predation against women ranging over three decades . <p> What McGowan imparted was clearly bigger than her own experience of sexual assault . All the studios paid out money to silence women , she said , and no actress wanting a future dare speak out . His female assistants were asked to prepare the room , including ensuring the sexual aids he needed to perform were readily available . <p> Weinstein traded on silence . His power , wealth and control within the industry maintained his mastery in a game where others were simply his pawns . Actresses were persuaded into compliance by promises and veiled threats .
