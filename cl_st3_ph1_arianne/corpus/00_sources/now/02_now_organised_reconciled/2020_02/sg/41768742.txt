The vegetation of the high latitudes is moving further north , growing taller , becoming more substantial , more abundant and budding earlier , according to new studies by 40 scientists from 36 European and US institutions . <p> And the whole planet is getting greener too , according to a separate study in a second journal , as more carbon dioxide in the atmosphere -- the chief cause of global heating -- also acts as a fertiliser to stimulate plant growth . <p> It is as if researchers have finally identified a genuine negative feedback effect : as the world warms because of higher levels of greenhouse gases , the plant world responds by absorbing more of the carbon in the atmosphere and modifying the overall impact . <p> But both studies identify problems with what might be a comforting conclusion : it is n't clear why in some Arctic regions the green things are getting greener , while in others the vegetation cover is becoming poorer . <p> Both studies confirm the value of a closer look at the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> In the journal Nature Climate Change , scientists report that they checked the big picture of polar greening based on four decades of data from large-scale satellite observation against more detailed evidence over smaller sample regions collected by sensors mounted on drones and on aircraft , as well as direct examination on the once-frozen ground . <p> The Arctic is the fastest-warming region of the planet : it is warming twice as fast as the globe as a whole .
