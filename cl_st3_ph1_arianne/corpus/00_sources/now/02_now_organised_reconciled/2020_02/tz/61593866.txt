<h> Key to successful agriculture in Africa is more fertilisers <p> A fertiliser is any material of natural or synthetic origin ( other than liming materials ) that is applied to soil or to plant tissues to supply one or more plant nutrients essential to the growth of plants . Many sources of fertiliser exist , both natural and industrially produced . <p> In the later half of the 20th century , increased use of nitrogen fertilizers have been a crucial component of the increased productivity of conventional food systems . <p> Management of soil fertility has been the preoccupation of farmers for thousands of years . The use of commercial fertilizers has increased steadily in the last 50 years , rising almost 20-fold to the current rate of 100 million tonnes of nitrogen per year . Without commercial fertilisers it is estimated that about one-third of the food produced now could not be produced . <p> The use of phosphate fertilisers has also increased from 9 million tonnes per year in 1960 to 40 million tonnes per year in 2000 . For decades , fertilisers were too expensive for African farmers . They had to be imported , and transportation into the continent was expensive . <p> Now , though , Africa is turning a corner toward producing more of it locally . A Moroccan company has signed a nearly $4 billion deal to build a fertiliser plant in Ethiopia . A Danish company is helping the Democratic Republic of Congo build a $2.5 billion fertiliser plant . The African Development Bank Group helped fund a new fertiliser plant in Nigeria that 's already boosting farmers ' yields . <p> And this August in Ghana , the vice president stood proudly before the largest fertiliser plant ever built in the country .
