<h> Renewed General Licence brings greater protection for wild birds <h> Renewed General Licence brings greater protection for wild birds <p> Herring gulls , carrion crows and magpies are among a number of common birds to be given fresh protection following a shake-up of a licensing system that allowed them to be slaughtered as pests . <p> The list of 11 species , which includes rooks , black-backed gulls and collared doves -- regarded by some groups as a risk to livestock , crops and public health -- will receive stronger protection from April 1 . <p> The change of rules means certain birds can no longer be culled without special permission -- a move that may well pitch countryside supporters against conservationists . <p> It also means action to kill birds that may pose a potential health risk in city and town centres , such as wood pigeons , certain gulls and jackdaws , will have to be approved first . <p> Scottish Natural Heritage yesterday confirmed a shake-up to its General Licences process , which permits the hunting and killing of certain birds deemed to cause damage to crops , regarded as a potential risk to at-risk @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a last resort for dealing with problem birds , the system had been heavily criticised as a " licence to kill " , with claims that hundreds of wild birds were being slaughtered with no official oversight , no need for explanation and no control over numbers . <p> The move by SNH to rethink the General Licence system came after a similar system in England was subjected to a legal challenge led by television naturalist Chris Packham and the Wild Justice group .
