<h> F-Gas regulation -- Toughening up on refrigerant gases in 2020 <p> Two months into 2020 and tougher legislation and punishments are coming into play for the refrigerants gases business in the wake of both the F-Gas regulation and the illegal imports associated with it . <p> With the confirmation of the UK 's withdrawal from the EU -- ' Brexit ' -- last month , there are also question marks over how the UK tackles its adherence to the F-Gas regulations . <p> The EU fluorinated greenhouse gases ( F-Gas ) regulations are a central part of the ' European Green Deal ' to limit climate change . F-Gases include refrigerants known as HFCs , which are potent greenhouse gases ( GHGs ) . <p> The regulations aim to stimulate a switch from F-Gases with a high global warming potential ( GWP ) to more modern gases with a lower GWP using a mix of end-use bans and quotas . In the 12-month run up to the EU import quota reduction on 1stJanuary 2018 , the prices for HFCs such as R134a and R404A increased approximately 10-fold . At this price point , the risk-to-reward ratio for smuggling activities was highly favourable and sucked in illegal imports estimated to be as much as 30% of total EU demand . <p> The increased supply led to falling prices and at the end of 2019 , common HFCs were trading at about 30% below levels in 2018 . Furthermore , throughout 2019 there were highly publicised cases of smuggled goods seizures and prosecutions leading to fines .
