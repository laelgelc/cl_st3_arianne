<p> Rising temperatures are killing off flies that birds , bats and fish rely on for food in and around Irish rivers and lakes . <p> Scientists say the two types of stonefly can survive only within a narrow range of temperature and have been reduced to dangerously low numbers , most likely because of climate change . <p> One has been formally declared critically endangered and the other is listed as vulnerable , in a report compiled by the National Parks and Wildlife Service ( NPWS ) . <p> " Other species confined to higher altitudes may become threatened in the near future as temperatures continue to rise , " the report warns . <p> The NPWS yesterday published the 13th All-Ireland Red List covering the stoneflies of Ireland . Red Lists detail information on rare and threatened species . <p> Based on more than 12,000 records for the island of Ireland , the 20 species of stonefly ( Plecoptera ) on the Irish check list were evaluated against the Red List criteria produced by the International Union for the Conservation of Nature . <p> This is the first time the threat status of the Irish stoneflies has @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Agency and University College Dublin . <p> Although there are only 20 species of stonefly in Ireland , the species are an extremely important component of the natural biodiversity of rivers and , to a lesser , lakes . <p> Two species are deemed under threat of extinction on the island of Ireland . Protonemura praecox , was assessed as " critically endangered " - the highest level of threat - and Capnia atra as " vulnerable " . <p> A third species , Perlodes mortoni , was deemed " regionally extinct in Ireland " because it has not been recorded for more than 100 years . The remaining species were assessed as " least concern " .
