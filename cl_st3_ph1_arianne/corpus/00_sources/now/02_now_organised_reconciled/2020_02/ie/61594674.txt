CO2 dissolves in and out of the surface of the oceans , and is sucked out of the atmosphere by photosynthetic plants and algae @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Every year these two carbon sinks exchange around 367 billion tonnes of CO2 each with the atmosphere , according to Britton Stephens , who studies the carbon cycle at the National Center for Atmospheric Research in Boulder , Colorado . ' The system is inherently in balance , ' Stephens explains . ' But that little bit extra matters -- the coal and oil we dug up is screwing up that balance . The process involves using the same land to grow forests over and over again , burning the wood to provide energy while capturing and sequestering the carbon elsewhere . <p> ' It 's a mechanism for transferring CO2 that was originally in the air to underground , ' explains Chris Rayner , an organic chemist at the University of Leeds in the UK .
