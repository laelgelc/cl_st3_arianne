<p> Road freight is one of the largest contributors to carbon emissions in South Africa -- but by making better logistical choices , road freight companies can reduce carbon emissions and save nearly R1.5bn on carbon taxes , research has found . <p> " I have always been passionate about carbon emissions , climate change and the greenhouse effect and how we can make the world a bit better , " Terblanche said , as she recalled how her research supervisor prompted her to find something she was passionate about to make as a subject of her research . <p> Upon investigation , Terblanche said she learnt that road freight had a " terrible carbon footprint " in South Africa and that there was no research on how to effectively manage these carbon emissions in the country . This presented an opportunity for her to explore solutions fit for South Africa . <p> Terblanche defines carbon emissions as the primary gas contributing to the warming of the Earth 's atmosphere , or global warming , and climate change . <p> " We have much more climate disasters because of carbon emissions . That is definitely the most focal point @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " she said . <p> When she began her research , the most recent information available on carbon emissions dated back to 2014 , and was provided by the department of environmental affairs . Terblanche has since calculated that carbon emissions from road freight now stands at 16.8 million tonnes per year - over a third of total road transport emissions , which stands at 43.3 million tonnes per year . The leading carbon emissions producer in the country is electricity generation , at 236.79 million tonnes per year . <p> In her research , Terblanche looked up different ways different countries are trying to reduce carbon emissions . She found a decarbonisation framework developed in the UK , which could be adapted to South Africa . <p> Terblanche interviewed experts and managers in the road freight industry in order to develop her framework . A total of 132 registered road freight companies across the country were sampled and took part in the study by filling out online questionnaires , she explained . <p> Making better choices <p> The framework identified four key decisions a company can make when it @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> These included : <p> Moving from freight from road to rail . <p> Introducing efficiencies in the logistical route or network . <p> Introducing operational and mechanical efficiencies . <p> Developing a culture of compliance with the industry 's self-regulation scheme , the Road Transport Management System ( RTMS ) . <p> The four decisions were broken down to nine further carbon variables which contribute to higher carbon emissions for example missed delivery slots , hijacking which leads to unplanned trips and more carbon emissions , empty loading , unnecessary trips , other efficiencies by drivers i.e. accelerating , using cruise control and making unplanned or unauthorised stops . <p> " The framework was applied on one company during the course of the research as a testing phase and the company reported a considerable decrease in their road freight carbon emissions , " Terblanche said . <p> " If companies in South Africa focus on these core aspects , our carbon emissions in road freight can decrease by a staggering 46% .
