<h> News feed <h> How climate change reduced the flow of the Colorado River <p> The massive Colorado River , which provides water for seven US states , has seen its flow reduced by 20 percent over the course of a century -- and more than half of that loss is due to climate change , according to new research published Thursday . <p> Two scientists at the US Geological Survey developed a mathematical model of the water movements -- snowfall , rainfall , run-off , evaporation -- in the upper Colorado River basin for the period from 1913 to 2017 . <p> To do so , they used historical temperature and precipitation data , along with satellite readings of radiation , in order to understand how climate change had affected those water movements . <p> They observed that global warming had led each year to a reduction in the snow pack , or the amount of snow that accumulates . So if there is less and less snow , it melts earlier and earlier each spring . <p> With less snow covering the ground , the soil in the river basin was absorbing more sunlight -- instead of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a result , there is more evaporation of water in the basin , Paul Milly of the US Geological Survey explained to AFP . That means a decrease in river flow . <p> The study shows that during the 1913-2017 period , the flow decreased by roughly 20 percent . <p> " More than half of this decrease was associated with warming . The rest was related to variations in precipitation , " Milly said . <p> The Colorado River -- which is 1,450 miles ( 2,330 kilometers ) long -- is one of the main rivers in the southwestern United States . <p> It is a vital source of water for 40 million people , including the residents of Denver , Las Vegas and Los Angeles . <p> It flows into Mexico and by the time it ends in the Gulf of California , it is nearly dried out .
