<h> News feed <h> Greta Thunberg puts Africa 's climate activists in media spotlight <p> After a racism debate in Davos on the invisibility of African climate activists , Greta Thunberg held a press conference Friday with fellow eco warriors from Kenya , Uganda and South Africa to stress the importance of their voices . <p> Vanessa Nakate of Uganda was at the heart of a viral debate at the World Economic Forum in Davos after she was cropped out of a news agency photo of young activists , including Thunberg , taken after a press conference . <p> A 23-year-old graduate in business administration , Nakate was the only black person and only African in the photo shoot . <p> She accused the Associated Press of racism in cropping her out . <p> The agency said the photographer had modified the photo for composition purposes and later apologised , calling it a " terrible mistake " . <p> Nakate said she did " not want to talk about " the incident , other than to say that " it was quite a frustrating moment . " <p> " This is the time for the world to listen to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ stories ... This is an opportunity for media to actually do some justice to the climate issues in Africa , " Nakate told reporters via video link from Kampala . <p> She was joined on separate screens by Ayakha Melithafa and Ndoni Mcunu of South Africa and Makenna Muigai of Kenya , who spent an hour answering questions from journalists gathered at Greenpeace Sweden 's offices . <p> -- ' We are the most impacted ' -- <p> So far , Africa is relatively blameless when it comes to climate change . <p> The continent is home to 17 percent of the world 's population and more than a quarter of its nations , but only accounts for about five percent of the greenhouse gas emissions pushing the planet toward runaway warming .
