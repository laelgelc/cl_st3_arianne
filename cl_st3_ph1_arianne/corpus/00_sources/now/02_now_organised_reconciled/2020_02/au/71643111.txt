<h> Opinion <h> On fake news , social giants need to do better or get out of the way <p> As we enter the fourth month of unprecedented bushfires in Australia , it has been disheartening to see misinformation spread on social media , particularly when it comes to the cause of bushfires . Look at any popular post about bushfires on Facebook , YouTube , or Twitter , and you 'll see hundreds of comments and replies arguing that arsonists , not climate change , are to blame . <p> Facebook CEO Mark Zuckerberg has made it clear he does n't think his company needs to take responsibility over the spread of fake claims . <p> I 've spent the last month looking at misinformation on all three platforms , interviewing people on and off the record from the three major social media platforms . Each company responded with a comment that suggests while they take misinformation seriously , they do n't believe it is the job of Twitter , Facebook or YouTube to act as censor or editor of community sentiment .
