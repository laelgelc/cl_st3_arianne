<p> On Jacquelyn Gill 's first day doing field work at the Siberian permafrost caves during the summer of 2018 , a local fossil hunter approached her with a dead bird in his hands . The translator had n't yet arrived , but from the freshly dead look of the bird , Gill assumed it had just recently flown into the cave and died . A modern bird was of little interest to her team , which had flown to this remote region and trekked for miles to study remnants of the last ice age . The man , however , was persistent in offering her the dead bird . <p> Finally , the translator showed up and revealed what the fossil hunter was trying to tell Gill : The bird was ancient , one of the first frozen bird carcasses ever found in a late Pleistocene permafrost deposit . <p> " This is an ice age traveller , and I can touch it , " Gill , an assistant professor in climate science at the University of Maine , told Gizmodo . <p> In the boreal forest 30 kilometres east of the remote town of Belaya Gora in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ban on the ivory trade has driven fossil hunters to blast a network of tunnels into the permafrost using firehoses , looking for preserved mammoth tusks and rhino horns .
