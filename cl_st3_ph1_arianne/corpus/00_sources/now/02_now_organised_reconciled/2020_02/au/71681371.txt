<p> The alarming rate of carbon dioxide flowing into our atmosphere is affecting plant life in interesting ways -- but perhaps not in the way you 'd expect . <p> Despite large losses of vegetation to land clearing , drought and wildfires , carbon dioxide is absorbed and stored in vegetation and soils at a growing rate . <p> This is called the " land carbon sink " , a term describing how vegetation and soils around the world absorb more carbon dioxide from photosynthesis than they release . And over the past 50 years , the sink ( the difference between uptake and release of carbon dioxide by those plants ) has been increasing , absorbing at least a quarter of human emissions in an average year . <p> So , to put it simply , humans are producing more carbon dioxide . This carbon dioxide is causing more plant growth , and a higher capacity to suck up carbon dioxide . Plants and ecosystems use the sugar both as an energy source and as the basic building block for growth . <p> When the concentration of carbon dioxide in the air outside a plant leaf goes up , it can be taken up faster , super-charging the rate of photosynthesis . <p> More carbon dioxide also means water savings for plants .
