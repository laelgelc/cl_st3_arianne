The researchers have used computer simulations to predict how changing aircraft altitudes might reduce the number of contrails and how long they linger , which would reduce their warming impact . <p> This is because contrails only form and persist in thin layers of the atmosphere that have very high humidity , the researchers explained . Because these layers are thin , small changes to flight altitudes would mean that aircraft could avoid these regions , leading to fewer contrails forming , they said .
