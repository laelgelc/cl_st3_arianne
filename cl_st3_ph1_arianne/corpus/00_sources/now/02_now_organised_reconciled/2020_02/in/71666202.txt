<h> The Big Story : Budget tea-leaves <p> This week 's big political story is bound to be the Delhi elections , which took place on Saturday . But we will not know the results till Tuesday , February 11 , and all we have to examine between now and then are the turnout numbers ( which tell us nothing , as I wrote last year ) and exit polls , which have often been wrong . <p> So we 'll look at them next , aside from a short re-cap that you can find below . <h> Tell you how to save ? <p> Maybe the most talked-about Budget announcement in the English press was the option given to income taxpayers to pick lower rates if they give up their exemptions . Leaving aside the reminder of how this makes the most headlines despite impacting less than 5% of the population , the policy approach raises some important questions . <p> For decades , India has offered deductions on its tax rates to channel spending and saving into certain areas . You get tax rebates for items like house rent , home loans , putting money into the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , lower income tax rates come without the option of these exemptions . <p> While individuals will have to see which approach makes more sense for them ( higher rates with deductions or lower ones without ) , for the government it means less control over what people will do with their money . This could unleash consumption , which is what India needs right now , or people might end up saving -- but not in ways that the government can access . <p> For a country where the savings rate has already been falling , this is potentially dangerous . The government has explicitly sold the approach as one that treats taxpayers as mature , trusting them to spend as they see fit , while others worry about the effect it might have in the long run . <h> Borders with windows ? <p> " A little more than two years ago , when Government of India raised some tariffs , as an eternal optimist , I took the view that this was an isolated action driven by a bureaucrat steeped in socialist-era thinking .
