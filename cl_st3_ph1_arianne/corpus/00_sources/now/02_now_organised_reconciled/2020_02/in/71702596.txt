<h> SpecFix : Swept away by Waste Tide <p> Chinese science fiction has been on the rise these past few years , gaining new readers , making new fans with each passing day across the English-speaking ( &amp; reading ) world . Among the fine writers from the newer generation who 've contributed to this is Chen Qiufan , whose short stories have won a shelfful of awards in his native country and published in leading English publications to great acclaim for their deft handling of the spaces at the intersection of technology and tradition , his treatment of social consequences of technology , of a world in change and his realist visions of the not-too-distant future . Waste Tide , his debut novel , is no different . <p> In the near future , Silicon Isle is where all the e-waste of the world goes to die , as the individual pieces of discarded tech await their reincarnation as something else , starting from being picked apart for anything valuable they hold by the ' waste people ' , the lowest class of people in this grim and grimy island shrouded in a leaden miasma . Into this world comes Scott Brandle -- an ' economic hitman ' representing a green recycling company and looking to negotiate a lucrative contract with the clans -- and his assistant and translator , Chen Kaizong , a Chinese-American in search of his roots . Brandle also has another reason to be on Silicon Isle , a covert mission to locate a piece of dangerous and secretive prosthetic tech mistakenly discarded , which also happens to be a biological weapon . But is Mimi , just another ' waste girl ' looking to better her lot in life , who first comes in contact with it thanks to the mysterious and benevolent Brother Wen . Given a VR helmet by Wen , Mimi finds her skull pierced by it and infected with a virus that turns her into something more than human , giving her the power to inhabit and control mecha to become a human-machine hybrid . It is this that brings her to the attention of the powers that be , the American and his assistant , setting in motion a chain of interconnected events , including a worker revolution , as the book barrels forward non-stop in a thriller-like fashion to a satisfying climax . <p> In the hands of Chen , Silicon Isle becomes a character in itself , with his vivid descriptions , the rich details and acrid smoke you can almost taste . But at the heart of Waste Tide are the ' waste people ' and their trials and tribulations ; their hopes and fears . A world that Chen is familiar with , having grown up near Guiyu in China , one of the biggest e-waste dumps in the world . Through the eyes of the workers of Silicon Isle , Chen drives home the dark realities that lie behind our shiny tech , and who the victims of the world economy and capitalism really are , more so when you factor in climate change .
