<h> New study maps how much energy , nutrients and water are locked in world 's wastewater <h> Last Updated : Wednesday 05 February 2020 <p> The world generates about 380 trillion litres ( tl ) wastewater every year . This stores vast amounts of energy , nutrients for fertilisers besides , of course , water , according to recent study by the United Nations Institute for Water Environment and Health ( UNU-INWEH ) . <p> In principle , nutrients such as nitrogen , phosphorous and potassium recovered from wastewater produced annually can offset 13.4 per cent of global demand to produce fertilisers . <p> Energy recovered from such wastewater -- in the form of methane -- can generate electricity for up to 158 million households . That 's roughly the current demand in the United States and Mexico combined . <p> Usable water reclaimed from wastewater can irrigate up to 31 million hectares ( mha ) of land , the study claimed . <p> The volume of wastewater being generated is projected to rise roughly 24 per cent by 2030 to 470 tl and 51 per cent by 2050 to 574 tl . <p> Treating wastewater efficiently can @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ goals ( SDG ) . <p> " Wastewater resource recovery will need to overcome a range of constraints to achieve a high rate of return . But success will significantly advance progress against SDGs and others , including adaptation to climate change , ' net-zero ' energy processes , and a green , circular economy , " Manzoor Qadir , lead author of the study and assistant director at UNU-INWEH in Canada , said . <p> Researchers collected and analysed municipal wastewater data from a variety of sources at the national level in a given year . The data were then grouped together at regional and global scales . <p> The study claimed that global wastewater generation roughly equalled the annual discharge from the Ganga . Asia contributed the most at 42 per cent , followed by Europe and North America ( 18 per cent each ) in 2015 . <p> Per capita , however , rich countries in North America generated almost 140 per cent more than the world average . Europe 's per capita output was half of North America 's .
