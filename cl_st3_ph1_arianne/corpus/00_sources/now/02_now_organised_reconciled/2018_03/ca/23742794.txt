<p> Kathleen Wynne probably never imagined , when she first won her MPP seat in 2003 , that she would one day rehash a memorable Mike Harris moment , but she has : in 1999 , then-premier Harris announced a hasty prorogation , which was followed by a throne speech , a budget , and an election ( that the Tories went on to win handily ) . Wynne is all set to do the same . The speech was delivered Monday , the budget will be presented next Wednesday , and the election will very likely be called in early May . <p> For a party that has spent 15 years railing against the legacy of Harris ( who has been a Liberal bogeyman twice as long as he was actually in power ) , it 's somewhat fitting that it might end this way , as the Liberals look at polling numbers at least as hostile as they 've ever seen since the party formed government . <p> Maybe every government , even one as **34;542;TOOLONG as the Liberals , eventually indulges its most cynical tacticians . But by the end of Lieutenant-Governor Elizabeth Dowdeswell 's recitation on Monday afternoon , it was n't exactly clear why it 'd bothered . <p> After all , the government ( as already noted ) will be presenting a budget next week . The things the government waved at in Monday 's speech -- expanded pharmacare , more spending for hospitals and long-term care staff , better childcare options for parents -- will be nailed down with hard numbers by Wednesday . Some announcements wo n't even wait that long : for the next week , the Liberals will undoubtedly provide glimpses of the things that will formally be presented by Finance Minister Charles Sousa on March 28 . <p> Hence the question : Why prorogue the legislature and give up a week of the legislature 's time , all for a speech that ( sorry , Lieutenant-Governor ) will be forgotten by lunchtime tomorrow ? <p> ( The cynical answer is that the Liberals do n't have much of a legislative agenda left -- unless you count passing a budget promising the moon and stars to Liberal-friendly constituencies -- so the loss of a sessional week costs them nothing . ) <p> There are a few things to note about the throne speech before it recedes into the fog : its title , " A Time for Care and Opportunity , " marks the Liberal pivot from " fairness " ( last year 's Fair Hydro Plan , Fair Housing Plan , and 43 separate uses of the word " fair " in the 2017 budget ) to " caring . " The Liberals care about single mothers who need help paying the rent ; they care about parents struggling to raise kids while caring for their elderly parents ; they care about elderly Ontarians who need long-term care beds . <p> They also care about climate change , which is why there was a line in the speech about carbon pricing . It 's legitimately one of the government 's major environmental accomplishments , so it would 've been bizarre had they not mentioned it ; it was also probably in there to needle the Tories , who 've just emerged from a six-week-long meltdown over the same issue . <p> All in all , the throne speech was just a flowery way for the government to give the public a preview of its re-election platform . Wynne has always been open about her expansive view of the good that government can do in people 's lives ; the throne speech suggests she 's not going to run away from that . Win or lose , she 's going back to the voters as the person she is . <p> Whether that will be enough to overcome her own political unpopularity is an open question . <p> And the Liberals wo n't have the progressive field to themselves . The New Democrats , who were criticized for running a cautious , pocketbook-focused campaign in 2014 , have announced the first of likely several new left-wing policy planks over the weekend . On Saturday , leader Andrea Horwath announced that the party would introduce a public dental-care benefit and comprehensive pharmacare . On Monday ( hours before the throne speech ) , the party offered more details : the so-called Ontario Benefits would be free for people who make less than $30,000 ; they 're intended to be just the first step to creating an expanded social-safety net . The plan would cost $1.2 billion and would be fully implemented by 2020 . <p> Of course , the Liberals have already said that their budget will include expanded dental benefits as well -- if they 're re-elected for a fourth time . <p> The hitch , of course , is that at the moment neither the NDP nor the Liberals is leading in the polls . The Tories are , and while their MPPs were n't impressed with what they heard on Monday , the recent chaos in their party has left a black hole where their alternative proposal ought to be . Rookie leader Doug Ford stated that the Liberals " will say and promise anything to stay in power " and promised to bring " respect for the taxpayer " to Queen 's Park if the Tories form the next government . <p> It was a nearly content-free statement , but when you 're 20 points ahead in the polls , saying anything is basically just an invitation to make somebody somewhere angry , so why bother until you have to ? <p> It wo n't be official for a few more months yet , but we might as well call it : we 're all done with governing and legislating at Queen 's Park -- the only thing left is campaigning .
