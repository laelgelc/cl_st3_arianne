Simply add a form of payment and pay only 27 ? for any article you read . <p> Thank you for supporting the journalism that our community needs ! <h> Moving backwards on environment <p> Re : Province unveils green plan ( March 16 ) <p> My disappointment at the big fail on public transit and education funding in the Manitoba 2018 budget is trumped only by the sad surprise that the recently announced Climate and Green Plan Implementation Act includes the repeal of Manitoba 's venerable Sustainable Development Act . <p> Mature legislation can benefit by refocusing and by clearing out dated underbrush , but to simply efface this connection to the national and international sustainability discourse and the United Nations Millennium Development Goals is misguided . The government 's " made in Manitoba " mantra risks delinking Manitoba from international best practices and reducing environmental efforts to parochial and ad hoc policies and initiatives . <p> The Sustainable Development Act included a suite of policies , guidelines , goals and regulations applicable to government and Crown corporations that will not be easily replaced . <p> Killing the Sustainable Development Act is all the more surprising in that it was the proud achievement of the Filmon government , essentially a bipartisan effort built on the work of the previous NDP environment minister , Gerard Lecuyer . Proclaimed in 1998 , the Sustainable Development Act provided the foundation for former premier Gary Filmon 's support for bringing the International Institute for Sustainable Development to Winnipeg . It was instrumental in saving the Experimental Lakes project and continues its respected international research and advocacy . Why would one knowingly undercut this valuable work ? <p> The $102-million Conservation Trust Fund established in Budget 2018 has received loud hosannas in some sectors of the environment and agriculture communities , glossing over the fact that only the annual proceeds of the fund , perhaps $5 million , may be available in any year and this to both private and public stakeholders . In the context of the annual Sustainable Development Department operating budget of approximately $165 million , the amount is exceedingly modest . <p> Whenever possible , change and " reform " should be for the better . These changes to Manitoba 's climate change and sustainable development practices will provide much fodder for principled investigative journalism . <p> Sig Laser <p> Winnipeg <h> Cutting remarks <p> Re : Cabinet ministers give themselves a break on pay cuts ( March 20 ) <p> So the first order of business , should the deficit be slain , is to put money back in the pockets of Progressive Conservative MLAs ? You have now just incentivized your elected officials to cut more , freeze more and tax more . <p> And when you think of your government focusing on long-term goals , you 'd hope it would be on something that benefits the people and the province , not just a select few . <p> I 've also looked at the budget and listened to the throne speeches and I ca n't point to one solid piece of legislation or any promise that plans for 2024 other than this amendment to the balanced budget legislation and the Fiscal Responsibility and Taxpayer Protection Amendment Act , to make sure the current PC MLAs get what they feel is owed to them . <p> So the current government slays the deficit by 2024 by taking away jobs from Manitobans through layoffs and program closures , passing legislation that freezes the wages of Manitobans so they fall further and further behind because of inflation , freezes or cuts funding to health-care institutions and other services across the province that Manitobans use . And it does so during a time where the overall tax burden on the backs of Manitobans is increasing . And MLAs ' priority will be to reward themselves with a cash bonus . <p> As long as we 're all clear that what they are proposing is a financial benefit to themselves at the expense of you and me . <p> Brian Spencler <p> Winnipeg <p> ? <p> Manitobans have settled for mediocre . We have decided the province is just not going to amount to much . Premier Brian Pallister crowed about how Manitoba should be better and the most improved province . His actions say otherwise . <p> Last week 's budget saw businesses get $70 million in tax cuts , while low-income earners and workers were given $12 per year in tax breaks , about enough to buy a burger and fries . <p> The problem is that on Sept. 1 , the $12 savings will be dwarfed by new carbon tax . <p> Manitobans have always been thrifty , and they believed they would have more money in their pockets with a Progressive Conservative government . <p> The truth is that the one percentage point PST increase that sent the NDP packing was nothing compared to the carbon-tax bomb coming Sept. 1 . The Pallister government is about to hit Manitobans with the equivalent of eight per cent in PST . <p> The part that is forgotten is that PC government saw new federal transfer payments of more than $300 million and a new child-care fund windfall . Not to mention the revenue from legalization of marijuana , which the PCs have n't factored . <p> It 's not surprising that the books look better . If any government of any stripe saw this extra revenue , the books would be looking good . <p> Then the PC government cut roads funding by $152 million , despite promises to keep infrastructure spending at $1 billion . It 's now going to less than half a billion . <p> Pallister has hammered workers with wage freezes and , over four years , a total increase of 1.75 per cent . <p> He went after the sick and cut drug funding for cystic fibrosis meds , Alzheimer 's meds , physiotherapy coverage and CPAP machines . <p> The education minister put forward the lowest funding for education since 1990 . Then , despite a report on how college outcomes are lower than average , funding was slashed to them as well . <p> The one common theme in all this is that workers and low-income people are hit the hardest . <p> The fact that workers ' wages will fall behind by up to 10 per cent over the next four years should set off alarm bells to every business owner because spending will decrease at businesses as people try to stay afloat . <p> The economy in Manitoba was once No. 1 . Now we see it last , with dire predictions of the lowest growth in Canada for the foreseeable future . <p> The message being sent from the Pallister government is Manitoba will be mediocre .
