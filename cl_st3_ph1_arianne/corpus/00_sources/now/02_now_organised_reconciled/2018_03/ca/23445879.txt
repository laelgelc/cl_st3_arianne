And is it good to compare past climate to our daily weather ? <p> article continues below <h> Trending Stories <p> When you hear that today is above normal or below normal we are comparing today 's weather element ( temperature , precipitation , etc. ) to a running average of 30 years of data . In Canada we get this data from the Government of Canada because they have been monitoring the weather much longer than anyone else in this country . Also available are the climate normals from 1971 to 2000 and 1961 to 1990 . Hence the 30-year running average . At the completion of each decade , Environment and Climate Change Canada updates its Climate Normals for as many locations and as many climatic characteristics as possible . The Climate Normals , Averages and Extremes offered here are based on Canadian climate stations with at least 15 years of data between 1981 to 2010 . This means that a station can record half the time and still be a credible source of information to the public . <p> The charts compare temperature and precipitation from this winter to our 1981-2010 climate normals . Looking at temperature we have had a slightly colder than normal winter so far . In terms of how close to normal we are , the 41.20mm at the Brandon airport is about 59% of typical winter precipitation amounts . This jives well with the rest of Southern Manitoba . Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada has the majority of southern Manitoba at 40-60% normal , with areas around Melita having less than 40% of normal winter precipitation . This is significant , in Westman we rely on our snowfall ( November to February ) to provide 15% ( 70.0mm ) of our annual precipitation total ( 474mm ) . <p> Climate normals are an easy way to compare the past to today .
