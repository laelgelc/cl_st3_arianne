<p> It 's just over 70 days to the June 7 provincial election , and Mike Schreiner is riding high . <p> The leader of the Green Party of Ontario has " a full slate " of candidates ready to carry the party 's colours , and he 's at the tail end of a nine-day , 10-stop push around the province to raise the profile of his party . <p> " I think this election will be a breakthrough " for the party , Schreiner said Tuesday . " I am confident we will win our first seat . " <p> The people he has been talking to , he said , want change . <p> " They do n't want the status quo , " he said , pointing at the Liberals , who have formed the government since 2003 , while the Progressive Conservatives " ca n't govern themselves . <p> Nipissing MPP " Vic Fedeli tried to clean up the mess , but he did n't have nearly enough time , " Schreiner said , while the New Democrats are " missing in action on a lot of issues . " <p> The Greens , though , have elected representatives in three provinces , hold the balance of power in British Columbia with three MLAs and are in good position to make inroads in Ontario . <p> " We are the only party that is putting people first , " Schreiner said . <p> In a stop at The Nugget , he introduced the Nipissing candidate for the party , 25-year-old Kris Rivard , a Canadore College student completing a post-graduate certificate in environmental management . <p> Rivard , Schreiner says , is the face of youth the party is building its future on . <p> He hopes to see up to 10 per cent of the party 's candidates in the June election , " young people engaged in the political process . " <p> The Greens , Schreiner says , are the only party that is not relying on " magic money and fairy dust " to cost out its election promises , while focusing on clean economy jobs and support for small business . <p> One indication of how little the Liberals care about the economy , he said , is the cancellation of rail passenger service in Northeastern Ontario . <p> Passenger rail , Schreiner says , is one of the most effective ways of fighting climate change . <p> Funding for passenger rail in the North , he says , will come from levying congestion charges and additional parking levies in Toronto , as well as a land capture program that will be put in place around transit stops . <p> A portion of those charges , he said , would provide in excess of $2 billion a year , and possibly up to $3 billion , for transportation and infrastructure . <p> Both Schreiner and Rivard admit Nipissing will be a tough nut to crack . <p> " I do understand Vic has strong support , " Rivard admitted , saying he can still work hard to " build the Green Party brand and give a voice " to supporters in the region . <p> " Every vote counts , " Rivard says . " It 's something we can build on . " <p> Rivard says his beliefs have always aligned with the Green Party 's platform , which focuses , he says , on people . <p> " We are looking to try to create jobs , " he says . <p> Schreiner also said the party believes strongly in Northern autonomy , with an emphasis on local decision-making .
