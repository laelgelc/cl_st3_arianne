<h> A swordfish and a loggerhead turtle in B.C. coastal waters ? Scientists say warming seas are to blame <p> WATCH : A B.C. researcher has spotted not one , but two species in our coastal waters that are typically only spotted in the south . As Kylie Stanton reports , it 's a troubling sign that points to ocean temperatures rising . <p> It may have been a beautiful sight , but the discovery of two warm water ocean dwellers off B.C. 's coast has researchers worried about climate change . <p> A swordfish and a loggerhead turtle were found swimming off the coast of Vancouver Island in two separate sightings by marine ecologist Luke Halpin , whose findings were recently published in the spring issue of the Northwestern Naturalist journal . <p> " This is , to my knowledge , the first time they 've been seen off Vancouver Island and in coastal waters , " Halpin said in a Skype interview from Melbourne , Australia , where he 's earning his PhD . <p> PHOTOS : See the pictures taken of the loggerhead turtle and the swordfish off the coast of Vancouver Island . <p> Halpin spotted the swordfish , which measured two-to-three metres long , roughly 20 nautical miles from Brooks Peninsula on the west coast of Vancouver Island on Sept. 5 , 2017 . <p> The loggerhead turtle was sighted more than two years earlier , on Feb. 22 , 2015 , roughly 45 nautical miles west of Tofino . It was estimated to be two-thirds of a metre in length and had significant algae growth on its head and shell . <p> Halpin , who works as a surveyor of seabirds and marine mammals , was working under contract to the Canadian government during both sightings . He said the creatures ' appearance in B.C. could be a sign of things to come . <p> " I think it 's potentially indicative of a continued warming trend and we 're likely to see more of these warm species in our waters , " he said , adding that these species could ultimately become acclimatized to northern waters if southern waters become too unbearable . <p> A map showing where the swordfish , near Brooks Peninsula , and the loggerhead turtle , near Tofino , were spotted . <p> Global News <p> That raises concerns about how the potential influx will impact B.C. 's ocean ecosystem , as more predators move in and compete over the same amount of prey . <p> Warmer seas could also make the northern ocean physically unbearable for local mainstays like native salmon and herring , according to marine ecologist Jeffrey Seminoff , who heads the Marine Turtle Ecology and Assessment Program at the Southwest Fisheries Science Centre in California . <p> " It 's amazing how just very , very minute changes in water temperatures can have such massive downstream impacts on primary productivity , on species ranges and on their susceptibility on human impacts , " Seminoff said . <p> Ian McAllister , co-founder and executive director of Pacific Wild , thinks the time has come for an increased conservation effort in order to save these species from getting caught up in the rising temperatures . <p> " These are adding to an already beleaguered ocean and we believe we really should be using a much more precautionary approach to how we 're managing species in the ocean , " he said . <p> Halpin said the sightings are n't necessarily a reason to panic , saying there 's more research needed to see if this is indeed a trend , instead of a one-time coincidence . <p> " These are just two observations ; we do n't know exactly what 's going to happen , " he said . " It may be a more regular occurrence as we see warmer waters off our coast . "
