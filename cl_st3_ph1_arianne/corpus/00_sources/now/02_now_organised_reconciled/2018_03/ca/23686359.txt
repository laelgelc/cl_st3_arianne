<p> Scientists studying a remote and icy stretch of the North Atlantic have found new evidence that fresh water , likely melted from Greenland or Arctic sea ice , may already be altering a key process that helps drives the global circulation of the oceans . <p> In chilly waters on either side of Greenland , the ocean circulation " overturns , " as surface waters traveling northward become colder and more dense and eventually sink , traveling back southward toward Antarctica at extreme depths . This key sinking process is called convection . But too much fresh water at the surface could interfere with it because with less salt , the water loses density and does not sink as easily . <p> In the new research , Marilena Oltmanns and two colleagues at the GEOMAR Helmholtz Center for Ocean Research in Kiel , Germany , found that following particularly warm summers in the remote Irminger Sea , convection tended to be more impaired in winter .
