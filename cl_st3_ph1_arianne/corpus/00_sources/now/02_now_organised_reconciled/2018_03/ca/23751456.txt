<p> For the past three years , the Ontario Progressive Conservative party has crafted a message that is perceived to be rare among conservative parties in the Western world : one that is diverse , pragmatic and environmentally friendly all the while maintaining a strict adherence to fiscal conservatism . The party took a leadership role on LGBTQ issues , stood firmly against Islamophobia , and , yes , took a strong and unequivocal position on climate change . It was a wildly successful formula for the political landscape in Ontario and for its messenger , PC leader Patrick Brown . It was a strategy that uniquely recognized that the people of Ontario are sick and tired of Kathleen Wynne but want change that recognizes certain core values . The party recruited star candidates , raised record amounts of money and its membership swelled . The strategy was memorialized in The People 's Guarantee platform , which added significant tax cuts , a child-care plan and the largest investment in mental health in Canadian provincial history . <p> History will treat Patrick Brown much better than the past few months have <p> But as the old Yiddish adage has it , " mann tracht , und Gott lacht " : Man plans and God laughs . The People 's Guarantee will never come to be . History will treat Patrick Brown much better than the past few months have . I am proud to have worked for him and hope to again . His positive legacy will live on . But he is a born winner , and he will be fine . Just watch him . <p> To those who have been so excited about the future of the PC party over the past three years -- the caucus members , candidates , party members , union leaders and workers , ethnic leaders and their communities , and others -- I have a message . Doug Ford is a different leader with a different style and different policy priorities and , speaking as one of the architects of the tone of the past three years , he has my unwavering support . I am proud to have him as my leader and my next premier . If you have been a PC supporter or have been interested in what the party has achieved in recent years , you should be , too . <p> I have heard pundits compare Doug to Donald Trump . They say he is a climate-change denier and he 's intolerant . As a proud centrist in the conservative movement , I can tell you these pundits are categorically unfair and wrong . <p> I know this first hand . Doug played a leading role in our historic victory in Scarborough -- Rouge River in 2016 , possibly the most ethnically diverse riding in Ontario . He has for the past three years helped the party across the province by door-knocking , including flying up to Sault Ste. Marie for our historic win there in last year 's byelection . He remained a party adviser and we encouraged him to run under the PC banner in Toronto . While he was aligned with us on climate change , he quietly -- never publicly -- expressed his displeasure with the carbon tax . We disagreed based on the realities of the provincial landscape but he nonetheless continued to selflessly support the party . <p> Many party members and other stakeholders do n't know about all of this . The truth is that Doug did not seek any media attention or publicity while helping out or expressing his views . <p> Our party held a unity rally Monday night in Etobicoke . Over 2,000 people were reported to have attended . Candidates and caucus members from across Ontario stood firmly together . It was a beautiful display of a united , diverse party looking forward . The party will offer a new platform over the coming weeks . I expect that it will make fiscal conservatives and those worried about the economic health of our province proud . I expect the " media party " to keep criticizing him . But -- and I never thought I 'd be saying this -- they will criticize any conservative leader , no matter his or her tone or policy priorities . Trust me . I have lived it . <p> Doug will appeal to voters in Toronto , where I now live , and in Mississauga , where I grew up . He will appeal to voters in temples and mosques in Brampton and Scarborough and he will appeal to voters in union halls in Windsor and Sault Ste. Marie . He will appeal to mothers in Vaughan and families in Ottawa . People want a decent life , not political platitudes . Doug , uniquely , gets this . <p> Running a political party in our system is not easy . Doug will have many difficult decisions to make over the coming weeks leading up to the election . But I fully expect Ontario will have its premier-elect in Doug Ford on June 7 and anyone who found themselves drawn to the PC party over the past three years under Patrick Brown should join me in celebrating that evening with our new leader . <p> Walied Soliman served as chair of the Ontario PC party election campaign until earlier this year .
