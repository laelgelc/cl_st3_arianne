<h> The Federal Government , says population growth and effects of climate change are some the factors causing shortage of supply of potable water . <p> The Minister of Water Resources , Mr Suleiman Adamu , said this at a symposium to commemorate the 2018 World Water Day in Abuja . <p> According to him , in spite of the accomplishments and enormous opportunities such as abundant freshwater , favourable weather , substantial human capacity available in the water sector , a lot of work still needed to be done . <p> He said it was saddening to note that the current national access to water supply stood at 57 per cent , millions of Nigerians , especially in rural areas are still without access to improved drinking water sources . <p> ' ' The provision of water infrastructure is capital intensive , hence the need for a concerted effort by all stakeholders to overcome the challenges in the water sector . <p> ' ' Statistics have shown over the years that with the rapid increase in population growth , access to clean drinking water and sanitation steadily declines . <p> ' ' A lot of factors contribute to the water crises , such as climate change , poor management and a lot more , the most incriminating is the neglect of our ecosystem . <p> ' ' This has resulted in environmental damages such as floods , droughts , water pollution among others . <p> ' ' A worrying challenge is the declining percentage of Nigerians that are getting that water supply through piped networks , from 31 percent in 1990 to less than 7 percent in 2017 . ' ' <p> Adamu , represented by the Permanent Secretary , Dr Musa Ibrahim , said the result had shown that the remaining 93 per cent of Nigerians got water from other sources . <p> He said it was evident in the drilling of boreholes with resultant consequences on the environment in urban areas and fetching of unclean water from streams and rivers in rural areas . <p> The minister restated the Federal Government 's commitment towards formulating and implementing policies and programmes that will enable sustainable access to safe and sufficient water for all Nigerians . <p> Mr Simone Grego , UN Educational , Scientific and Cultural Organisation ( UNESCO ) Representative , said that drinking water resources was one of the major issues of the century globally . <p> Grego , quoting a UN World Water Development Report , said globally , 3.6 billion people live in potentially water-scarce areas , saying this figure could rise to 5 billion by 2050 , if nothing was done . <p> According to him , 80 per cent of waste water returns to the ecosystem without being treated , saying this development was leading to degradation of the ecosystems causing ecological imbalances and water scarcity . <p> Grego called for urgent solutions that would protect , manage and restore natural ecosystems that would respond to the human and ecological challenges being faced . <p> ' ' Planting new forests , reconnecting rivers to flood plains and restoring wetlands are solutions that will address contemporary water management issues , ' ' he said . <p> He pledged the commitment of UNESCO to support government 's transition to green economies and in programmes to implement better integrated water policies . <p> Dr Chichi Okoye , WaterAid Country Director , said the SDG 6 commits the world to ensure that everyone has safe water by 2030 , saying protecting the natural environment and reducing water pollution was imperative . <p> She urged the Federal Government to prioritise water and sanitation by improving budget allocations to the sector . <p> ' ' The dire situation needs the highest level of government 's attention , especially in delivering water for the poorest and most marginalised people . <p> ' ' In addition to financing , we need an integrated approach to solving the water crises where water , sanitation and hygiene needs are integrated into health , education , nutrition and gender equality . <p> ' ' Without water , none of the UN Global Goals can be met , ' ' she said . <p> News Agency of Nigeria reports that World Water Day is celebrated on March 22 annually to focus attention on the importance of water to mankind . <p> The theme for World Water Day 2018 is : ' Nature for Water ' -- exploring nature-based solutions to the water challenges we face in the 21st century .
