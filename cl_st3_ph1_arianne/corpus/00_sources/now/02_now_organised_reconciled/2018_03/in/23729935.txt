<h> Government of Nigeria to provide universities with solar energy : AfDB to provide USD 410 million <h> Vice President noted that the Buhari administration , through various projects , is making more efforts to ensure there is improved power supply nationwide . <p> Government of NigeriaViews : 48 <p> The first phase of the project will include nine federal universities and one teaching hospital . ( Image credit : Flickr ) <p> Despite the sectoral challenges , the Buhari administration will complete major projects that will improve the power sector in the country and also implement a plan that will provide solar energy in a number of universities in the country , according to Vice President Yemi Osinbajo , SAN . <p> Prof. Osinbajo stated this today at the Presidential Villa , Abuja , when he received a delegation from the African Development Bank ( AfDB ) led by the bank 's Vice President for Power , Energy , Climate Change &amp; Green Growth Complex , Mr. Amadou Hott . <p> The Vice President noted that the Buhari administration , through various projects , is making more efforts to ensure there is improved power supply nationwide . <p> One of such projects , Prof. Osinbajo noted , was the federal government 's Energising Education Programme ( EEP ) , which is a key component of its Economic Recovery and Growth Plan ( ERGP ) and Power Sector Reform Programme ( PSRP ) . <p> The project , which is being carried out through the Rural Electrification Agency ( REA ) , is expected to power federal universities and teaching hospitals with solar energy . <p> The first phase of the project will include nine federal universities and one teaching hospital . <p> The Vice President said it was an important project for the government as it would " improve general satisfaction and create an atmosphere where academic activities can thrive and go undisturbed . " <p> While noting that the federal government has made budgetary allocation to fund the project in some of the federal universities , the Vice President said the bank 's support would also be welcome . <p> " As ( the President of AfDB ) Adesina would always say , " Light up and power Africa " , I would say it is good to light up Nigeria also , " he said . <p> Prof. Osinbajo thanked the AfDB for the great support it is giving the TCN transmission project and in the training of its engineers , adding that the federal government would always be ready to support the bank in carrying out its initiatives in the country . <p> The AfDB is supporting the training of 300 engineers from the Transmission Company of Nigeria ( TCN ) through a programme with the National Power Training Institute of Nigeria ( NAPTIN ) . <p> In the same vein , the Vice President said the federal government welcomed the AfDB 's support for solar Independent Power Projects ( IPP ) in the country . <p> The Bank noted that it would support solar IPPs in the country through different projects . <p> In his remarks , the AfDB Vice President said the bank has a programme to support the solar project in the country with USD 477 million , and another USD 150 million for other projects . <p> He said , " We are on the verge of approving grant money of USD 1.5 million to support the Jigawa solar project , a new initiative of up to 1,000 MW ; the first phase will be 100MW . We have agreed to support the Transmission Company of Nigeria ( TCN ) with a total of USD 410 million for the critical transmission project , which was initially USD 200 million . We are expecting to have this approval by September depending on the studies and other work that needs to be done . " <p> Hott further said the AfDB plans to provide USD 300 million to support the federal government 's power sector recovery programme ; or the federal government 's planned investment in power distribution companies to enable them to unlock 2,000MW of stranded power . <p> The Minister of Finance , Mrs. Kemi Adeosun , and other senior government officials were in attendance at the meeting . <p> ( This is a reproduced press release from the government of Nigeria as is .
