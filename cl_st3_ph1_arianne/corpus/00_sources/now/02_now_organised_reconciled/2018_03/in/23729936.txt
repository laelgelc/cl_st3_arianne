( Image credit:Pexels ) <p> Amazon is the home to half of the world 's plant and animal species due to its naturally rich areas . These species could face extinction due to climate change if carbon emission continues to rise unchecked . <p> Paris climate Agreement 's target is to meet two degrees celsius , even this much can not stop these places to lose their species , as per WWF . <p> Researchers examined the effect of climate change on nearly 75,000 plant and animal species in world 's naturally wildlife-rich and diverse areas , each area was chosen for its variety of plants and animals found there . <p> According to the WWF report , found that Miombo Woodlands , which is home to African wild dogs , Amazon Guianas , south-west Australia , are some of the most affected regions by climate change . In these regions up to 90 percent of amphibians , 80 percent of mammals and 86 percent of birds could potentially become extinct and Amazon could lose about 65 per cent of its plant species . <p> South west Australia has become dangerously effected by climate change as per research 89 per cent of amphibian will extinct in the coming years . The Fynbos in the western cape region of South Africa which is facing drought could face another problem of extinction of some of its unique species . <p> According to WWF , climate change will not only impact on plants and animals but also increase average temperature and more erratic rainfall in other regions . There has been a constant pressure on elephants in Africa due to the scarcity of water as they need to drink 150-300 liters of water on a daily basis . <p> If these species can move freely to new locations than they can escape from the risk of extinction . The overall research shows the best way to protect wildlife is to keep the global temperature as low as possible . Paris Agreement says to reduce global warming from 4.5 degree celsius to 3 degrees celsius but to see greater improvements , two degree Celsius should be achieved , as per WWF . <p> This is the reason , on March 24 , millions of people across the world will gather for Earth Hour to commit for protecting biodiversity and becoming the part of conservation to build a healthy and sustainable future which will send a strong message to government and business that there is global will to change these figures of extinctions.
