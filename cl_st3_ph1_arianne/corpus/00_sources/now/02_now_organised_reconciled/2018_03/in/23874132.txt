<p> To teach your children to be custodians of the environment , start by showing them how to cut their carbon footprints . <p> Teaching children the importance of environment conservation may seem like a lot of work to time-starved parents , especially when combined with the mounds of homework and all the extracurricular activities that today 's children already have on their plates . Many parents also struggle with breaking down complicated concepts , such as eco-protectionism and carbon footprints in a manner that is suitable for young minds to digest . However , as children 's writer and environmental advocate Katie Bagli emphasises , these are necessary lessons . " Children are the future keepers of the planet , and sensitising them about wildlife and nature today , can go a long way in shaping them into responsible , mature adults of tomorrow -- adults who will have the willingness and ability to tackle pertinent issues such as global warning and climate change , " she says . <p> She talks about the ' biophilia hypothesis ' , proposed by American biologist and naturalist Edward O Wilson , which postulates that human beings have an innate urge to affiliate with other forms of life . " The role of parents is to nurture this inborn instinct in their children , and ensure that it is n't dampened by external agents , " Bagli adds . Here are some easy tips for parents who want to raise environmentally conscious children . <p> Make learning about ecology fun <p> Lecturing your children about being responsible in their use of natural resources and in their treatment of animals will invariably lead them to ' switch off ' , Bagli says . Instead , she recommends appealing to your child 's innate curiosity by using tools like storytelling and innovative games . " Many children 's authors such as Ruskin Bond , Deepak Dalal and Ranjit Lal weave plots around nature and wildlife . Reading such stories can leave a wonderful and lasting impression on children growing up in concrete jungles -- urban areas that have limited green spaces and where they rarely deal with plants and other wildlife in their day-today lives , " she says . She also recommends craft activities such as making collages and drawings , and creative games . " Ask children to create their own creatures by combining two or more animals -- a lion and a red deer , for instance -- and encourage them to make up a story about this animal . This will get their creative juices flowing , and help them to understand the behaviour of different species and also the various ways in which species are interrelated . " <p> Practice what you preach <p> Children model their own behaviour on their parents ' habits , Bagli says , and all the teaching in the world will not help if you do n't set a good example . " Lead the way by doing simple things such as using cloth bags instead of plastic bags , cutting down on how often you use your washing machine , and switching electrical appliances off after use , " she says . <p> Get them to plant trees <p> Exposing children to the miracle of life will encourage them to be compassionate and considerate as adults . " I conduct a Nature Exploration class for three to fiveyear-old . On our field trips to the garden , we say ' hello ' to the banyan trees , the bamboos and other flora . I also give them seeds that they can plant in small pots . Muddying their hands helps them to feel one with Nature , and the pride and joy they feel when they see their plants growing is incomparable . These children , upon growing up , will never want to cut down trees for trivial reasons , " says Bagli . <p> She recommends taking your children for Nature walks on weekends , " so they can listen to birdsong and play with animals in a non-structured setting . Parents can help them to explore various trees , help them to identify the calls of different birds , and point out the squirrels and snails around them . " <p> Be there for the birds <p> Teach your children to perform simple acts of kindness . They can , for instance , place bird feeders on your balcony or position water bowls in your building compound for stray dogs and cats . " They can be taught to avoid loud firecrackers during festivals , and to volunteer with organisations that rescue injured or sick animals . <p> " There are plenty of vet clinics and where your child can volunteer . Older children can even spend time at the Bai Sakarbai Dinshaw Petit Hospital for Animals , " Bagli says . <p> Spread the word <p> Children are far more aware than most adults would believe , especially in matters concerning flora and fauna . " I 've found that many children who travel abroad for their vacations come back with insights about the plants and animals they have encountered during their trips , while children from rural families have much to offer in terms of their knowledge about indigenous plants and animals .
