<h> Restoring the glory of Lake Chad <p> A map of Nigeria today without a depiction of Lake Chad as a prominent part of the physical features , to many , would be incomplete . But that may soon be the fate of the great lake if the current drive spearheaded by President Muhammadu Buhari and other stakeholders to reverse its dwindling fortunes falls short of expectations . <p> This , indeed , is one of the classic cases of pitting the intellect and resolve of man against the seemingly indomitable might of nature . But man has been able to come this far , not by resigning himself to fate in the face of daunting challenges , but by continuously striving to conquer , and adapt to , his harsh environment as represented by nature . The restoration of Lake Chad should , therefore , not be a mission impossible if there is the political will to accomplish it . <p> Over the centuries , the lake had sustained empires , industries , commerce and agriculture . Its fresh water and aquatic contents have been a source of livelihood to millions in Nigeria and countries such as Cameroon , Niger and Chad , which are all directly connected together by its sprawling water . But due to the effects of climate change -- and the debilitating activities of man himself -- the water started a slow but deliberate process of unchecked shrinkage . <p> At a recent three-day international conference on Lake Chad held in Abuja , it was revealed that the lake , which used to cover an area of 25,000 square kilometres , making it the fourth largest in Africa , had shrunk to just 2,500 km2 ; between 1960 and today , 90 per cent of the water has gone . Gone with the receding shoreline are the fauna and flora that it used to sustain . <p> Besides human activities , other factors that have contributed to the shrinkage of the lake include the dwindling and unpredictable rainfall , indirectly linked to climate change and encroaching desertification , evaporation and underground leakage , where water seeps into the Soro and Bodele depressions . As a matter of fact , 95 per cent of the water disappears through evaporation . <p> The main rivers that empty into the lake -- the Yedseram , Ngadda , Hadejia , Jama'are-Komadougou , Yobe , and Chari-Logone , from which it derives 90 per cent of its water -- are either seasonal or have had their flow disrupted by the careless construction of dams along their routes or indiscriminate abstraction of the water for irrigation . <p> Some of these developments are unfortunate because the Lake Chad Basin Commission was actually established to check them . The commission was established in 1964 , with initial membership of Nigeria , Chad , Niger and Cameroon , " to regulate and control the use of water and other natural resources in the basin , and to initiate , promote and coordinate natural resources development projects and research . " The membership of the basin was later expanded with the cooption of the Central African Republic in 1994 and Libya in 2008 . <p> From what remains of the lake , it is obvious that the LCBC has not lived up to expectations . Apart from the shrinkage , the depth has also been badly affected . The lake that had its deepest part measuring at 11 metres is now credited with an average depth of 1.5 metres . <p> Buhari is particularly concerned about the effect of the shrinking lake on human population . While noting that over 45 million people are affected by the current condition of the lake , he also connected the Boko Haram insurgency experienced in the North-East to joblessness and harsh economic conditions spawned by the shrinking lake . His lamentation , " Fish varieties long gone , leaving the farmers jobless ; herdsmen struggle over the little water left ; herdsmen migrate in search of greener pastures , resulting in conflicts , " captures the current essence of the lake . <p> It is obvious that the Lake Chad calamity did not just creep up on everybody . Both the LCBC and the international community watched , perhaps unconcernedly , as the place described by Buhari as an oasis in the desert gradually deteriorated to its present state . Even if they had acted at all , it is obvious that what they did miserably failed the test of adequacy . <p> That is why the last conference in Abuja , with the theme , " Saving Lake Chad to Revitalise the Basin 's Ecosystem for Sustainable Livelihood , Security and Development , " is very apt , even if it is belated . Restoring the lake will give a new lease of life to the basin countries described as some of the poorest in the world . It will also make the resettlement of the more than two million internally displaced people in that region much easier . <p> Critical to recharging the lake will be reopening new channels of water supply . This is so because of the unpredictability of the current sources of water . Definitely , the lake can no longer be left to the vagaries of irregular rainfall . This will involve diversion of rivers to channel their water to the lake currently being threatened by sand dunes . Attempts were said to have been made in 1929 and 1960 to divert River Ubangi into the lake , through River Chari in CAR . It has to be renewed . <p> But , above all , Libya seems to offer an important clue to recharging the lake . One of the legacies of the country 's late maximum ruler , Muammar Gaddafi , was the concept of the Great Man Made River , through which about 2.5 million cubic metres of fresh water is transported daily from under the Libyan Desert through pipelines over a distance of up to 1,600km to the coastal areas of Tripoli and Benghazi for mass irrigation , industrial use , drinking and other domestic uses . <p> A similar project could be embarked upon , through which large volumes of water could be piped to Lake Chad , to save it from becoming a relic of what used to be a lake . With the current interest generated among some international agencies such as the African Development Bank and the United Nations , this is the time for the LCBC members to push for assistance to be able to recharge the lake . The cost of recharging the lake has been put at between $15 billion and $20 billion . While the whole amount might not be available at once , whatever is available should be put to use .
