<p> Recent reports indicate utilities and states have begun to take more seriously an expected exponential increase in the number of electric vehicles in the U.S. , with 227 EV-related actions nationwide in 2017 . <p> So far , though , it 's not enough . <p> A report released Wednesday from the Smart Electric Power Alliance ( SEPA ) found that among 486 American utilities surveyed , 74 percent remain in the early stages of planning for growth in electric vehicle ownership . That 's troublesome , according to EV advocates , because a utility 's responsibility to manage the grid and serve customers means they 'll be in the middle of the EV revolution . And as the report notes , " based on numerous and continuously revised EV forecasts , time is not on the utilities ' side . That 's not even a fifth of the 5.5 million charging units the Edison Electric Institute and the Institute for Electric Innovations estimates the country could need by 2025 . SEPA cites Bloomberg New Energy Finance and RethinkX estimates that energy consumption associated with EVs could rise to between 118 and 733 terawatt-hours by 2030 . <p> According to SEPA 's survey results , large investor-owned utilities have done the best to hurdle those infrastructural and demand challenges .
