That proposal comes with a catch -- it requires 1.6 million gallons of water a day , or the contents of more than two Olympic-sized swimming pools . <p> But instead of pumping the freshwater up from aquifers , the company might be able to reuse water from the Empire Wastewater Treatment Plant near Farmington . <p> That plant treats an average of 10 million gallons a day , then flushes the water 12 miles to the Mississippi River . <p> Instead , the treated water could be pumped north to a satellite facility , cleaned a second time and piped another five miles to the Enerkem plant . <p> City administrator Joe Lynch likes the idea . <p> The city could not now handle an increase of 1.6 million gallons per day . " It would be a tremendous draw for our system , " Lynch said . <p> The alternative -- digging new wells for the thirsty facility -- would be difficult and expensive . But Lynch said recycling water would make the Enerkem plant more viable , bringing in jobs and tax revenue . <p> Lynch said that the pipe route from Farmington would be flanked with developable land -- perfect for other plants that could also use the recycled water . <p> But for now , there is no statewide policy regarding water reuse . Officials say better rules are needed , including rules about paying for the reuse projects . <p> " Water reuse in Minnesota has been hampered by a lack of a clear approval process , " said Minnesota Health Commissioner Jan Malcolm in a news release . <p> Reuse can be good for the environment and good for public relations , said Dave Kazmierczak , superintendent of the Prestwick Golf Club in Woodbury . <p> Four years ago , the course started storing rain and snow runoff to irrigate .
