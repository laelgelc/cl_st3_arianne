We are discovering how interconnected the economy and society we inhabit is , and how difficult it is to disentangle the territory of the UK from the institutions and rules of the EU . Leaving the single market and the customs union could bring drastic economic consequences , as well as the possible breakup of the UK and the renewal of conflict in Northern Ireland . Yet a soft Brexit merely means that we will have no political say on how these institutions develop . And as our politicians spend most of their time tediously reminding us , there are many equally problematic variations between these two options . <p> These dilemmas are reflected in the contradictory position of the Labour party . The customs union , by contrast , is arguably the central plank of the neoliberal vision for Europe . Many of its founders envisaged a united socialist Europe . It was successive British governments , starting with Margaret Thatcher , that contributed to creeping neoliberalism and acted as a brake on more progressive developments . <p> We need to reimagine the EU as a new kind of political institution capable of managing today 's interconnected economies and societies . We could think of it as a sort of model of global governance : closing tax havens for multinational companies , regulating global financial flows , or controlling carbon emissions -- and overcoming inequality , bringing peace to conflict zones , managing migration and refugee flows , and constructing resource-saving infrastructure .
