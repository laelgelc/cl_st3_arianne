<h> Exciting year ahead for Lakeland paradise of art and nature <p> FEW historic houses can offer wonderful vistas , ancient semi-natural woodlands , peaceful sounds of wildlife and at the same time be a vibrant centre for contemporary art . <p> A paradise of art and nature , Brantwood has all the above and more . <p> Each year the former Coniston home of one the great visionaries of the 19th Century , John Ruskin , hosts artists and craftmakers who develop special exhibitions and new displays inspired by the very spirit of Brantwood . <p> The first of 2018 is Fair and Foul , Jill Rock 's interactive exhibition of ' vegetative grotesques ' which takes its name from Ruskin 's book Fiction Fair and Foul . <p> Writer , artist and prominent social thinker , a true polymath , Ruskin - who was born in 1819 - was one of the first people to investigate the role of the grotesque in depth , concluding that in its purest form it was a sign of what he described as a " vigorous " society . <p> In his @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the ways that grotesques are portrayed , relating them to society . <p> Artist Jill sees the grotesque as a source of delight , her exhibition reflecting on society 's liking for it in vegetation and in art , if not always in human form . <p> In Fair and Foul on show in Brantwood 's Blue Room , Jill through the medium of painted fragments of wood , many collected from the Brantwood estate , has set up an exhibition which could be seen as a dialogue with Ruskin 's analysis of the grotesque . <p> He coined the prophetic phrase The Divinity of Decomposition as he watched the urbanisation of Victorian London , reflecting on its affect on the imagination of people and artists in particular . <p> " In my mind Ruskin is a man of ideas who has become increasingly relevant as society draws closer to many of the concerns he expressed in his writings <p> some 150 years ago , " explains Jill . <p> " I do believe that my realisation of the importance of the grotesque in art began in 2003/4 when @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ .
