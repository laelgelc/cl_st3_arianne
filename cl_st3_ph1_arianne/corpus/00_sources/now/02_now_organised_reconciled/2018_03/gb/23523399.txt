<h> Major solar farm plans on Gwent Levels south of former Llanwern steelworks site submitted to Welsh Government <p> PLANS to build one of the largest solar farms in Wales which could power more than 15,000 homes a year have been lodged . <p> An application submitted by the Gwent Farmers Community Solar Scheme seeks to erect up to 245,000 panels on 345 acres of land south of the former Llanwern Steelworks in Newport . <p> Over the course of its proposed 30-year lifespan , the farm would generate up to 50 megawatts of electricity and save more than 21,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide per annum . <p> Planners from estate agents Savills say the scheme has the potential to power a 428 hectare ( 1,057 acre ) housing development - larger than the 4,000-home Glan Llyn development . <p> A report reads : " The whole of the former Llanwern steelworks site covers an area of 415 hectares , demonstrating the sheer capacity of the proposed scheme to provide a substantial amount of renewable energy . " <p> Due the size of the proposals , the application has been @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ national significance . <p> The application was validated by the Planning Inspectorate on March 5 , having been submitted in January . <p> Rows of panels would be built on six parcels of land between Goldcliff and Whitson , with the nine land landowners behind the scheme hoping it will secure their farms for " future generations " . <p> Peter Grubb , director of rural planning at Savills said : " If approved , the scheme would be one of the largest renewable energy schemes in Wales and provide an important contribution to meeting the stringent renewable energy targets set by the Welsh Government . <p> " Crucially , the scheme will make use of existing infrastructure previously used to service heavy industry at the Llanwern Steelworks site . " <p> " The application is accompanied by a full Environmental Impact Assessment and data gathered over a period of three years prior to submission of the application . <p> " As such , the biodiversity of the site has been fully assessed through a suite of ecology surveys . The panels themselves will be distanced from the hedgerows and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ be incorporated into the plans , and the site would be subject to an ecological management plan for the duration of the scheme . " <p> But the plans have been subject to criticism in the past , with the Royal Society for the Protection of Birds ( RSPB ) saying last May : " The UK must switch to greater use of renewable energy as we look to meet our climate change targets for reducing greenhouse gas emission . <p> " However , this must be in harmony with nature to avoid damaging the very things we are trying to save .
