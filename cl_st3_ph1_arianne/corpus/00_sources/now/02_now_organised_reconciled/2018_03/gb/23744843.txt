Photo : Institution of Civil Engineers <p> Felixstowe Docks : <p> From 1886 Felixstowe Docks has grown to become one of the country 's busiest commercial ports handling 42% of the UK container trade as it accommodates increasingly bigger ships . <p> The port employs around 3,000 workers and contributes to the local economy . <p> Willis building : <p> The Willis building in Ipswich is one of the earliest buildings designed by architect Sir Norman Foster . <p> Now seen as a landmark of the ' high tech ' style of architecture , the building uses elements of modern industry and technology as part of its design . <p> In 1991 it became the newest in the UK to be given a Grade 1 listing . <p> London Array : <p> The London Array offshore windfarm spans the Thames Estuary from Margate to Clacton on Sea . <p> The ? 1.8bn project is one of the world 's largest windfarms . <p> 175 turbines have an output of 630MW into the London grid . <p> It is seen as a landmark scheme for the world 's renewable energy industry and credited with paving the way for a new generation of bigger windfarms . <p> It supplies enough power for nearly 500,000 homes - enough to supply two thirds of homes in Kent . <p> It reduces harmful CO2 emissions by more than 925,000 tonnes a year . <p> Mulberry harbours : <p> You might not expect the World War II Mulberry harbours to feature amongst East of England projects but remnants of it can be found at Shoebury Ness near Southend on Sea . <p> These floating artificial harbours were designed and constructed by British military engineers for the D-Day landings of June 6 , 1944 . <p> At the end of the war some were left in situ and can still be seen on the Normandy coastline , others were transported for other uses such as breakwaters . <p> Whilst being towed from Immingham to Southsea , one of them began to leak and was intentionally beached on a sandbank in the Thames Estuary at Shoebury Ness . <p> Nathan Baker , engineering knowledge director at ICE , said : " Our research has shown that the majority of both adults and young people do n't know what a civil engineer does and most ca n't identify a single UK civil engineering project . <p> " We aim to change these perceptions with 200 People and Projects , explaining not just the importance of civil engineering but how it has directly transformed people 's lives . <p> " The chosen projects showcase how civil engineering paved the way to modern life and how it continues to tackle the problems of today , safeguarding the future for generations to come . <p> " With the world facing unprecedented challenges , such as climate change and the pressures from a rapidly growing population , there has never been a greater need for civil engineers and the vital work they do .
