<p> Despite the bleak findings , the study also pointed to some successes in reversing declines in wildlife . <p> The study by the Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services ( IPBES ) showed forest cover in China and other parts of north-east Asia had risen by more than 20% between 1990 and 2015 . <p> It also found that animals , such as the amur leopard , which were once on the verge of the extinction had grown in population . <p> Speaking at the 2018 Biodiversity Summit in Colombia , leading British scientist Sir Robert Watson said : " We must act to halt and reverse the unsustainable use of nature or risk the future we want and have . <p> " Fortunately , the evidence also shows that we know how to protect and partially restore our vital natural assets . " <p> Academic volunteers looked through some 10,000 scientific publications for what is said to be the most extensive biodiversity survey since 2005 . <p> Among the list of the biggest threats to food and water security were pollution , climate change , and deforestation . <p> Scientists say governments , businesses , and individuals must consider the impact on biodiversity when taking decisions on farming , fishing , forestry , mining , or infrastructure development .
