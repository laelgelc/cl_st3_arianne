<p> Image caption The divers found the eggs ( darker sections ) covering the sheltered ridges of the seabed <p> A huge spawning ground for herring has been discovered near Gairloch in Wester Ross . <p> The ground , thought to be around three square kilometres , was discovered by scallop divers who operate in the area . <p> Scientists from Marine Scotland are examining egg samples from the site to try to identify their genetics . <p> It is hoped the find is a positive step towards the restoration of herring stocks which were wiped out by overfishing in the 1960s and 70s . <p> The site is south west of Gairloch village at a point where the Inner Sound meets the Minch . <p> It straddles a marine area which is closed to dredging boats and there are now calls for that protection to be extended across the whole spawning ground . <p> Image caption The eggs were discovered by scallop divers <p> The concern is that gear dragged along the sea bed would damage the eggs when they are at their most vulnerable . <p> Diver Alex Cameron was one of the first to discover the site . <p> He told BBC Scotland News : " It 's just as if there 's been a hail storm through it and there is loads of hail on the sea bed floor , obviously with the hail being the herring eggs . The whole place is absolutely covered . <p> " It was pretty amazing , yeah . I have never come across anything like that . " <p> Efforts have been under way for several years to " rediscover " wild herring around Gairloch . <p> A local project was aiming to document the history of the industry which dominated north west coastal communities for centuries . <p> But the Wester Ross Fisheries Trust , which was behind the project , now hopes the new discovery could elevate herring out of the history books . <p> Sue Pomeroy from the trust said : " I 'm surprised and excited because herring stocks have fallen since the 1970s . The odd herring in spawn had been found but nothing this size . <p> " Both Ullapool and Gairloch were founded on the herring industry - it was the keystone subsistence for the people who lived here . Then in the 1920s to the 1950s the fishermen were raking it in , absolutely raking it in . Families and the whole community relied very heavily on the herring industry . <p> " Basically , they went out and fished and fished and fished and caught more and more and more . " <p> It takes about 10 days for the eggs to hatch in water temperatures experienced in the Minch . <p> The process appears to have already begun with signs that egg numbers are diminishing . <p> There are other spawning grounds off Scotland with a large one near Cape Wrath on the north coast . <p> But that one is for Autumn Herring which spawns later in the year . <h> ' Fantastic opportunity ' <p> Local skipper Alasdair Hughson said : " It 's a fantastic thing to have found . Not only have we proved now that this area is for certain a herring spawning ground . We 've also seen ... how important the sea bed is . <p> " It would be a fantastic opportunity for the future if , and I do say if , there are signs of a recovery here . It 's just hard to describe how important it could be for the future of fisheries in an area like this . <p> " Gairloch probably would n't be here if it was n't for the herring , like many other ports along the west , north and east coast of Scotland . " <p> Overfishing has long been considered the main reason herring numbers fell sharply . <p> But more recent research suggests climate change affecting plankton , which the young herring need to survive , could also have played a part . <p> A Scottish government spokeswoman said : " This report of large concentrations of herring spawn off Gairloch is welcome news . <p> " The area where eggs have been found recently was first identified as being important for herring spawning nearly 100 years ago , and herring populations off the west coast have fluctuated significantly over the intervening period . <p> " Marine Scotland has been working with the fishing industry to try and understand the factors at play . We encourage the different sectors of the fishing industry to continue working together to help improve the stock . " <p> She added : " In addition , there are wider management measures in place to protect the marine environment in this area , including a year round fisheries closure to mobile bottom-contacting fishing gears in Loch Gairloch , and protection for harbour porpoise . "
