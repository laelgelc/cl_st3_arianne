<h> Early spring greets polar regions than anywhere else <p> Due to climate change , the Earth is experiencing earlier springs , but nowhere so much and so fast as at high latitudes , new research suggests . <p> " Yes , spring is arriving earlier , and the Arctic is experiencing greater advances of spring than lower latitudes , " said lead author Eric Post from University of California , Davis . <p> " What our study adds is that we connect such differences to more rapid springtime warming at higher latitudes , " Post said . <p> The study , published in Nature 's online journal Scientific Reports , showed that for every 10 degrees north from the equator you move , spring arrives about four days earlier than it did a decade ago . <p> This northward increase in the rate of springtime advance is roughly three times greater than what previous studies have indicated . <p> For example , at southern to mid latitudes such as Los Angeles , New Orleans , or Dallas , the study suggests spring might be arriving a mere one day @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ north , in Seattle , Chicago , or Washington DC , it might be arriving four days earlier . And if you live in the Arctic , it might be arriving as much as 16 days earlier . <p> " This study verifies observations that have been circulating in the scientific community and popular reports for years , " Post said . <p> For the study , the researchers analysed 743 previously published estimates of the rate of springtime advance from studies spanning 86 years across the Northern Hemisphere , as well as rates of springtime warming over the same range of years and latitude . <p> Springtime provides important biological cues for many plant and animal species , and it is unclear how an accelerated spring could play out for these species across the planet . <p> The study noted that impacts to migratory birds are a potential concern .
