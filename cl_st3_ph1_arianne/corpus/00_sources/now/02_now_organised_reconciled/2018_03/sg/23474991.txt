<p> Where can 1.5 million penguins hide ? In Antarctica , of course . But scientists tracked them down after they spotted scads of guano -- penguin poop -- on satellite images of an area where they had no idea such hordes existed . <p> The previously unknown mega-colonies of Ad ? lie penguins were tracked to the Danger Islands , an archipelago of nine islands off the northern tip of the Antarctic Peninsula , according to a study published Friday . <p> The islands host 751,527 nesting pairs of Ad ? lie penguins -- more than the entire rest of the peninsula -- and include the third and fourth largest Ad ? lie penguin colonies in the world , according to the researchers . <p> The numbers were toted up by humans counting the birds on land , and via automated tallies of images taken by unmanned aerial vehicles . <p> Ad ? lie penguins only live in Antarctica , and their population generally has been declining due to climate change and melting sea ice . Based on aerial survey comparisons , the penguin population of the Danger Islands appears to have remained stable or even slightly increased over the last 60 years , according to the study . <p> The islands could continue to be a " hotspot for avian abundance , " according to the research paper , and therefore " deserve special consideration in the negotiation and design of Marine Protected Areas " in the region . <p> Counts of Ad ? lie penguins in the area are critical because they 're " sentinel species " that help point the way to other climate change effects that are less easily measured , the study notes .
