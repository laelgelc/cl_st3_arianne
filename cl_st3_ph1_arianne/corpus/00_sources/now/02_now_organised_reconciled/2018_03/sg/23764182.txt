<h> You are here <h> Asia-Pacific 's biggest ports face steep climate bill : study <p> Wed , Mar 21 , 2018 - 11:16 PM <p> KUALA LUMPUR Upgrading Asia-Pacific 's biggest ports to cope with the effects of climate change will cost up to US$49 billion , but the bill could be even higher if no action is taken , researchers said . <p> Sustainability consultancy Asia Research and Engagement ( ARE ) analysed the risks to 53 of the region 's largest ports and estimated costs for them to adapt to rising sea levels and storm surges . <p> Benjamin McCarron , managing director at ARE , predicted " increasing damage to ports " from storms and related stoppages in operations if measures are not taken to prepare for global warming impacts , including more extreme weather . <p> " That kind of disruption would grow and continue , " he told the Thomson Reuters Foundation . <p> It put the costs for those ports to adapt to climate change at between US$31 billion and US$49 billion . <p> Japan 's Kitakyushu port faces the highest cost at US$4.9 billion , the report said , while five of the region 's 10 largest ports by capacity could see bills of more than US$1 billion each . <p> Indonesia 's Cilacap could take steps to deal with climate change most cheaply at US$65 million , the report said . <p> It is more expensive to fix up ports with large areas covered by warehouses than those made up mainly of yards , which can be elevated relatively easily using concrete , said Mr McCarron . <p> Port owners should factor the report 's findings into their long-term plans and upgrades , while banks , insurance companies and other investors should question port operators about the resilience of their assets , he added . <p> Incorporating climate protection at the design stage is cheaper than doing so once a port is built , Mr McCarron said . <p> The report also urged financial backers of new infrastructure , like China 's " Belt and Road " initiative , to ensure they include climate projections in construction plans to avoid higher costs in the future . <p> " This report is designed to start conversations amongst those who use and finance sea-ports in Asia by asking the multi-billion dollar question : Are you ready for climate change ? " Wai-Shin Chan , head of climate change at HSBC , said in a statement .
