<h> A group of scientists have proposed geo-engineering glaciers in Greenland and Antarctica as a way to prevent sea level rise caused by climate change . <p> Scientists have proposed a series of radical engineering projects in Antarctica and Greenland to help stop glaciers melting and entering the oceans . <p> The suggested ideas include underwater walls , artificial islands and giant pumps that push cold water onto glaciers to assist in stopping them melting . <p> The group of researcher led by John C. Moore who is the chief scientist at the College of Global Change and Earth System Science , Beijing Normal University , China ; and professor of climate change at the Arctic Centre , University of Lapland , Rovaniemi , Finland has outlined their plans in the current issue of Nature . <p> " We think that geoengineering of glaciers could delay much of Greenland and Antarctic grounded ice from reaching the sea for centuries , buying time to address global warming , " the article reads . <h> Huge costs justified <p> The researchers justify the huge costs involved in the project by outlining the alternatives . " If nothing is done , 0.5 -- 5% of the world 's population will be flooded each year after 21002 . For example , a 0.5-metre rise in Guangzhou , China , would displace more than 1 million people ; a 2-meter rise would affect more than 2 million . <p> Without coastal protection , the global cost of damages could reach US$50 trillion a year . Seawalls and flood defenses cost tens of billions of dollars a year to construct and maintain . " <p> The article lists other recent large-scale water based engineering projects that have come in under the potential costs the damages caused by the climate change may occur . They list Hong Kong 's artificial island built to host the city 's new international airport costing $20 billion and China 's Three Gorges Dam , which spans the Yangtze River to control floods and generate power , costing around $33 billion . <p> " If the world does nothing , ice sheets will keep shrinking and the losses will accelerate . " <p> " Geoengineering of glaciers has received little attention in journals . Most people assume that it is unfeasible and environmentally undesirable . We disagree , " the scientists state . " If the world does nothing , ice sheets will keep shrinking and the losses will accelerate . <p> Even if greenhouse-gas emissions are slashed , which looks unlikely , it would take decades for the climate to stabilize . " The group proposed three ideas to stimulate discussion on the subject . <p> The scientists suggest a 100-meter-high wall with sloping sides be built in front of the Jakobshavn glacier in Greenland . The Jakobshavn glacier in western Greenland is currently one of the fastest-moving ice masses on Earth . <h> Support ice shelves <p> Ice shelves are formed where Antarctic ice meets the sea . These formations play a crucial role in holding back the glaciers behind . Due to the warming of the air and sea around the Antarctic ice shelves are thinning . <p> In 2002 , 3,200 square kilometers of the Larsen B ice shelf collapsed . It is now just 30% of the size that it was in the 1980 's . In order to stop the further collapse of ice shelves , the authors of the article propose to support them by artificially pinning the ice shelves to the front of the glaciers . <p> This would be achieved by building berms and islands that extend from the shelves or are built into the sea floor . <h> Dry subglacial streams <p> The final idea proposed in the article is to freeze subglacial streams that contribute to water runoff and increase the speed of glacial melt . This complex process that involves drilling through deep layers of ice has a projected cost of $500 million USD . <p> The article ends with a warning that the proposed measure will not halt global warming if greenhouse gas emissions keep rising .
