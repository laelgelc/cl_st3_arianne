<h> Operators should be ready to ' rip up the brochure ' <p> High-end travel operators should not be afraid to " rip up the brochure " and be spontaneous in order to deliver true " money ca n't buy " experiences . <p> Sharelines <p> " Guests have travelled the world over and stayed in the best hotels , some have even been to Antarctica before , so we have a challenge , and it 's up to us in our role to give them something different . " <p> Yann Rashid , an expedition leader for Ponant in the polar regions , explained to delegates at the Abta Luxury Travel Conference on how employees of the French cruise line are tasked with delivering " a seamless five-star experience " in some of the most inhospitable areas on Earth . <p> " Guests have travelled the world over and stayed in the best hotels , some have even been to Antarctica before , " he said . " So we have a challenge , and it 's up to us in our role to give them something different . " <p> According to Rashid , being flexible during extreme weather , in particular the variable nature of sea ice stopping Ponant 's vessels sailing certain routes , had enabled the line to provide experiences others could not . <p> He described how on a number of occasions changing the scheduled itinerary had allowed calls at local settlements on the coast of Greenland , where ships rarely visit . <p> " You just need to keep guests informed and allow them to be part of the adventure and they will understand that , " Rashid said . <p> Delegates also heard from Simon Lynch , director of sales and business development for Abercrombie &amp; Kent , who said the luxury operator was seeing a rise in the popularity of " transformational travel " . <p> Lynch explained how A&amp;K was reaping the benefits of guests looking for more educational holidays centred on issues such as wildlife conservation and climate change through its Abercrombie &amp; Kent philanthropy programme .
