However , if you would like to , you can change your cookie settings at any time . <h> Climate risk -- business as usual impossible <p> Business as usual will be impossible as climate change continues its current trajectory , a leading climate scientist told a City audience in London this week . <p> The risks posed by climate change were the topic of discussion at an event held by law firm Norton Rose Fulbright at Lloyd 's of London . <p> Professor Sir Brian Hoskins addressed the audience on the topic of " Climate change : are we up for the challenge ? " . <p> " There is no such thing as business as usual in the future , " said Hoskins . " We ca n't expect to go on releasing greenhouse gases and live the life we have lived in the past . " <p> Hoskins is professor of meteorology at Reading University and director of Imperial College London 's Grantham Institute for Climate Change . <p> Recent cold weather patterns in the UK and US in 2017 and 2018 can , anecdotally , give the impression that temperatures are not warming ; Hoskins noted that taking a global average during those spells , the world was still warming to set new records in overall terms . <p> It is a misconception that climate change just means higher average temperatures ; it will also change weather patterns , he highlighted . " Rainfall records are expected " , noted Hoskins , " because a warmer atmosphere can hold more water . " <p> Droughts can also lead to broader consequential risks , he suggested , using the example of a 2006-2010 drought in Syria , which badly affected farmers in the Middle Eastern country . <p> " Climate change is often mistaken for one problem on its own , " said Hoskins , referencing connected factors of urbanisation , population growth , geopolitics , and sustainable development goals . <p> Climate models began life as weather forecasting tools , he noted , adding in other data over time . Furthermore , Hoskins stressed , modern computational climate models are tested against historical climate data to check their validity for predicting the future . <p> While the world is certainly hotting up -- by some 0.8 Celsius since 1880 already -- the land is warming more than the oceans . <p> Coping with today 's extreme temperatures is already tough for developing countries , he noted . <p> The Paris agreement ( COP-21 ) agreed in 2015 " only takes us a quarter or a third of the way " towards curbing temperatures before a " crucial threshold " is likely to be crossed , he suggested . <p> The seas are rising by around 3.3mm per year , " which does n't seem like much " , but can be closely watched from space by satellites , and translates into 3cm per decade and 33cm within a century . <p> Hoskins linked this to Hurricane Sandy , which struck the US in 2012 , causing great damage along the US east coast , including New York . <p> " The sea level for Sandy was about 20cm higher than it used to be , " said Hoskins . <p> By 2050 , temperature rises mean there might be no ice left in the Arctic except in winter time , he explained . <p> Within a couple of centuries -- depending on mitigation efforts -- there may not even be any ice in the Arctic in winter time . <p> It is hard to undo the level of carbon dioxide that has been allowed to build up already . For every 100 tonnes of carbon dioxide in the air now , Hoskins noted most it will still be there for hundreds of years to come . <p> On a more optimistic note , he suggested mitigation efforts are improving thanks to technology -- particularly by harnessing renewable energy and developing electric vehicles to the point where they become desirable among consumers . <p> Climate risk is decreasingly a matter for " future generations " , he noted , allowing greater urgency in mitigation efforts . " We ca n't put off the problem into the future -- we have to deal with it now , " he added . <p> A panel discussion followed his speech , which highlighted some of the industry risks already coming to the fore because of climate risk . <p> Neel Lane , a partner at Norton Rose Fulbright , highlighted new risks for architects and engineers , as they face new professional standards and legal risks should , for example , floods exceed previous models used for construction projects . <p> Higher standards of knowledge and care will likely be balanced by competitive costs , creating climate-related risks for the construction sector , Lane suggested . <p> Tricia Hobson , global chair of Norton Rose Fulbright , highlighted that after Queensland floods in Australia in 2011 , dam engineers are already being blamed in a class action lawsuit . <p> Climate risk could be the next cause celebre for plaintiff law firms and activist shareholders , she suggested . <p> Aldo Argento , a Canada-based partner at the law firm , suggested it was " only a matter of time " before business interruption claims hit insurers because of protesters disrupting oil pipelines . <p> Drought risk was highlighted by Maria Philippides , a South African partner at the firm , noting a risk that in 2019 " Day Zero " will occur in Capetown , when taps are expected to run dry . <p> Political risk , protests and civil commotion are expected to ensue , she suggested , as residents blame the municipality and others for failure to supply water . <p> She also highlighted heightened war and political risks from migration , noting the huge refugee camp set up in Kenya to deal with people displaced by recent drought in East Africa . <p> Previous , prehistoric warmings of the earth have not taken place in an era with political borders to stem migration , pointed out Hoskins , while developing countries will struggle most to mitigate the effects of climate change on society . <p> He also had a word for those who remain sceptical in the face of a weight of scientific evidence . <p> " When asked do I ' believe ' in climate change I say ' no , but the evidence for it is very strong ' -- this is not a credo , " Hoskins concluded .
