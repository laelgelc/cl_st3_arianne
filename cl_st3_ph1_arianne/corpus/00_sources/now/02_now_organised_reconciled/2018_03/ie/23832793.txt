<h> It looks like the extreme weather we 've been experiencing is here to stay <h> Worrying news ... <p> Ireland has been badly affected by extreme weather , particularly in recent months , where status red warnings had to be issued on the back of Storm Ophelia and also for the Beast from the East . <p> And it looks like we 'll have to get used to the extreme weather according to the a new report from the European Academies ' Science Advisory Council ( EASAC ) . <p> The group is made up of 27 national science academies in the European Union , Norway , and Switzerland . <p> New data published this week shows that man-made climate change has resulted in increasingly frequent extreme weather events . These include events like excess rainfall leading to flooding , coastal flooding , heatwaves , and increased risks of wildfires . <p> According to the study published called , Extreme weather events in Europe : Preparing for climate change adaptation : an update on EASAC 's 2013 study , the new data shows that extreme weather events have increased dramatically over the last 36 years . <h> Advertisement <p> The study observes that " climate-related extreme events are rising , with particularly sharp rises in hydrological events . " <p> The entire findings are available to read here , and show the number of floods and other hydrological events have quadrupled since 1980 and have doubled since 2004 . <p> Climatological events , such as extreme temperatures , droughts , and forest fires , have more than doubled since 1980 . Meteorological events , such as storms , have doubled since 1980 . <p> " Some of the underlying drivers of extreme weather which were speculative four years ago are looking less speculative , " said Michael Norton , environmental programme director for the EASAC . <p> The prospect of the Gulf Stream -- also known as the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation ( AMOC ) -- slowing , or even shutting down entirely , " must be taken as a serious possibility , " he added .
