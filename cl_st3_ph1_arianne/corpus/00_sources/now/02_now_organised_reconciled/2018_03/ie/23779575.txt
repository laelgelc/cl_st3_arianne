<h> World Water Day 2018 : Nature for Water <p> This year is the 15th anniversary of World Water Day , which is designed to highlight the vital importance of something many in the developed world take for granted : freshwater . <p> The UN estimates that 2.1 billion people in a world of 7 billion lack access to safe drinking water . And this figure could grow by another 2 billion out to 2050 . <p> The major water crisis that is unfolding in Cape Town where the city has taken to drastic measures to respond to the worst drought it has faced in history . <p> If so-called ' Day Zero ' takes place , the city runs out of water in nearby reservoirs will force millions of taps to be shut off in homes and businesses . <p> This year 's theme is ' Nature for Water ' , which encourages people to look at solutions to maintaining and sourcing clean drinking water throughout nature . <p> An annual report released by the UN 's special water division this week states that utilising natural processes , such as soil moisture retention , or building new wetlands , can help take the pressure off areas suffering from water scarcity . <p> " We need to deal with the water paradox , " said Erik Solheim , head of UN Environment , while discussing nature-based solutions . " Water is the essence of life , but we do n't save it enough . It 's time to change mindsets , it 's not about development versus the environment . " <p> It 's hoped that taking tips from nature can be cost-effective , efficient and restore degraded ecosystems . <p> And , of course , climate change has a major role to play in the water crises around the world through increasing the likelihood of extreme weather events , such as drought and storms , which destroy infrastructure and cause large-scale crop failure . <p> Take a look at the events taking place around the world to celebrate the day ; from walks and dances , to lectures and film screenings . <p> And if you 're on social media , share stories and thoughts using the hashtags : #WorldWaterDay , or #EveryDrop.
