<h> Plan to generate and store renewable energy using groundwater in Bendigo 's disused mine shafts more realistic after feasibility study <p> GOING DOWN : A pumped hydro electricity storage scheme using Bendigo 's mines is one step closer following positive results from a feasibility study . <p> An innovative plan to generate and store renewable energy using groundwater in Bendigo 's disused mine shafts is closer to being realised following positive results from a feasibility study . <p> The study , which began in June , investigated whether the historic shafts have the capacity to power and store pumped hydro electricity , and concluded there was a strong prospect for cost-effective energy storage . <p> It concluded the project has a generation capacity of 30 megawatts and could store six hours , or 180 MWh , of energy for the local power grid , but would cost $50 million to create . <p> The state government is calling for expressions of interest from industry and other parties over the next eight weeks to progress the work . <p> The initial $150,000 , six-month study was funded by the state government and the City of Greater Bendigo . <p> It is unclear who will fund the second stage of the study . <p> Minister for energy , environment and climate change Lily D'Ambrosio said the study results were an exciting next step for the region 's renewable energy capacity . <p> Bendigo has seven major mine workings running alongside the old quartz reefs . The Sheepshead , Deborah and Hustlers reefs are the deepest . Hustlers reef is the source of the rotten egg smell in central Bendigo . <p> " There is enormous potential for Bendigo 's empty mine shafts to store dispatchable , renewable energy and support generation into the grid , " she said . <p> " New energy technology is delivering jobs and a more affordable and reliable energy system to regional Victoria . " <p> Information sessions will be held in Melbourne and Bendigo to provide interested parties with an opportunity to seek further details on the next stage of the study . <p> The Bendigo goldfields had the largest concentration of deep shafts in the world , reaching over 1400 metres at its deepest point . <p> The plan is believed to be the first of its kind -- certainly the first to be totally underground using man-made mines . <p> The pre-feasibility study made a number of conclusions , some of which are listed below . <p> The capital cost of this project is $50 million , with $1.5 million required for a further , in-depth study . <p> Given pumped hydro installations generally operate much longer than 30 years , the idea is considered cost effective . <p> There are several ancillary benefits of the proposed concept , including ; dewatering of Central Deborah Tourist Mine , dewatering of Swan Decline below Northern Exploration Drive , reducing peak transmission network demand and easing constraints on Bendigo Terminal Station transformers , potential to contribute to long-term management of groundwater , project development process may assist with development of other sustainable energy projects around Bendigo , project would create approximately 50-60 jobs during construction and 5 jobs during operation
