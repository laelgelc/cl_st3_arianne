<p> The Tasman Sea experienced a " marine heatwave " over summer that pushed the surface temperature to a record high , climate scientists say . <p> Following a particularly hot summer on both sides of the Tasman and in between , the Bureau of Meteorology and New Zealand 's National Institute of Water and Atmospheric Research teamed up to release a " special climate statement " . <p> New Zealand 's summer was the hottest ever recorded , while Tasmania had its hottest November-January on record . <p> " Tasmania had its warmest November on record and its second-warmest December and second-warmest January , " senior BOM climatologist Dr Blair Trewin said . <p> " In New Zealand , they had their hottest summer on record and January was their hottest month on record , so it was exceptionally warm on both sides of the Tasman . " <p> Dr Trewin said the water surface temperature in the southern Tasman Sea was also exceptionally high . <p> " They were more than two degrees above average in December and part of January , " he said . <p> " And for the November to January period , they were easily the highest on record -- 0.6 of a degree on any other year . " <p> Dr Trewin said the record temperatures were caused by a " very persistent " belt of high pressure from Tasmania to New Zealand in November last year that remained stationary for three weeks . <p> " That allowed the ocean waters to heat up under very constant sunshine without being disturbed , " he said . <p> " Ocean waters take a long time to warm up or cool down , so they stayed persistently warm right through until the end of January before coming back a bit closer to average in February , " Dr Trewin said . <p> Tasmania 's waters have experienced a number of marine heatwaves in recent years , taking its toll on abalone stocks and kelp forests . <h> Urge to document ' major climate event ' <p> The collaboration was a first between the two organisations , who said the purpose of the report was to " document major events and act as a historical record " . <p> " Sea surface temperatures in the southern Tasman Sea rose to exceptionally high levels in late 2017 and early 2018 , " the report begins . <p> " These temperatures were far above any others previously observed at that time of year in the region , and extended west from New Zealand to Tasmania and mainland southeast Australia . " <p> Dr Trewin said scientists had identified a long-term warming trend in the world 's oceans . <p> " You 're seeing these extremes emerge in different places in individual years but when you look at the overall number of significant marine heatwaves , it 's only going in one direction and that 's upwards , " he said . <h> Alarm over extreme weather events <p> Acting Climate Council CEO Dr Martin Rice said the weather patterns were alarming . <p> " Worsening climate change , driven by the burning of coal , oil and gas , is causing temperatures to rise at unprecedented rates and is making extreme weather events across Australia and elsewhere more intense , damaging and costly . <p> " As 2018 gets underway , we 've already seen the country hit with a series of extreme weather events , including tropical cyclones , severe heatwaves , intense rainfall and bushfires. "
