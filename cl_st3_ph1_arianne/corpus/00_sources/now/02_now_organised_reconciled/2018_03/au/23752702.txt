<p> QBE Insurance Group chief executive Pat Regan has admitted that sceptical investors are looking for " proof points " before passing judgment on the success of a new strategy following eight successive profit downgrades . <p> In an interview in Hong Kong on the sidelines of the Credit Suisse Asian Investment Conference , Mr Regan said QBE had shown " too much volatility in our results " and " too many surprises have come along the way " . <p> " So our program is really about how do we simplify the group and how do we strengthen performance management and create a bit more consistency in what we do , " he said . <p> " As we have gone around doing our meetings there is a general acceptance that the plan of work is sensible , " he said . <p> Mr Regan said QBE was close to appointing a new chief underwriting officer as part of his plan to bring consistency to the pricing of policies and boost returns to long suffering shareholders . <h> Related Quotes <p> " This role of chief underwriting officer is quite important for making sure that we have consistent excellence across the group in underwriting , pricing and claims , " Mr Regan said . <p> " It sounds simple but actually when you have got a global business it 's difficult . There 's a very strong correlation between doing that well and getting good underwriting results . " <p> Mr Regan said investors he had spoken to during meetings in Hong Kong had been positive about his strategy which includes dividing the 80 separate insurance portfolios in QBE 's four main divisions into cells which can be the subject of " forensic performance management " . <p> But he admitted the investment community wanted to see results . <p> " They are waiting to see some proof points and , to be fair , that 's understandable , " he said . <p> Mr Regan is working hard on trying to improve QBE 's culture with an emphasis on delivering consistency of performance across all divisions and improved customer service . <p> Also , he is implementing new underwriting techniques including machine learning through a partnership with Cytora , a London based start-up that uses artificial intelligence and data from social media to protect against insurance losses . <p> " I think in 10 years time that 's how insurance will be done -- it 's a real game-changing sort of thing , " he said . <p> Mr Regan rejected a suggestion that insurance companies were becoming uninvestable because of the increased frequency and intensity of natural perils caused by climate change . <p> He said QBE was able to address the threat to its business from extreme weather and changing climate through its annual policy pricing reviews . <p> " As climate change clearly does change and alter weather patterns and risk exposures you do get to address that each year , " Mr Regan said .
