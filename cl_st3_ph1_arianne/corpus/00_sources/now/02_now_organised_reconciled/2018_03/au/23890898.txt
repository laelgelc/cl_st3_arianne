<h> How Willis Island weather observers survive life working at the remote outpost off Queensland <p> There is a crisis of sorts unfolding on a small island in the Coral Sea . <p> Four weather observers who have been living on tiny Willis Island -- only 7.7 hectares in size and some 450 kilometres off Cairns in Queensland 's far north -- for almost six months , are about to run out of cheese . <p> Janet Shelley , 39 , who is the officer-in-charge of the Willis Island Meteorological Office said it was the little things , like cheese supply , that can make or break a remote posting . <p> Cheesy jokes aside , these four self-described " nerds " are doing vital work in a difficult environment to protect Queensland . <p> The reason why Queensland gets such accurate information about cyclones powering through the Coral Sea towards the coast is due to the teams who have lived and worked on Willis Island for almost 100 years , who collect vital weather information to keep people informed and safe . <h> One of the world 's most remote weather stations <p> Ms Shelley , a Melbourne-based environmental officer with the Bureau of Meteorology ( BOM ) , left her comfortable inner-city existence and barista-made coffee about three months ago , to live on Willis Island . <h> A desolate place with lots of bird poo <p> Every year since 1921 , four weather observers have been stationed on the island for six-month periods , sending important data back to the bureau . <p> If you are imagining in your head a beautiful tropical island , with swaying coconut palms and sunset cocktails , think again . <p> This is a fairly desolate place , with few trees and a massive bird population which , in itself , presents some messy challenges . <p> " As with all birds , they leave a lot of bird poo around and that can play havoc with our instrumentations performance and also just the cleanliness of the place , so we spend a lot of time just cleaning up after them , " Ms Shelley said . <p> Three years later on October 1 , 1921 , a party of 15 sailed out of Melbourne on a steamer for Townsville and then onto Willis Island , with 150 tons of cargo onboard , mainly construction materials . <p> Since then , Willis Island is the only site in the Coral Sea providing real-time weather observations , and is also an important site for monitoring climate change because of its remoteness and pristine environment . <h> ' You 've got to be self-sufficient out here ' <p> After seven years with BOM and a previous three-month stint in Antarctica , she was selected to lead the team on Willis Island . <p> " It is a really unique feeling to be on your own out here , " she said . <p> " If things get hard , you ca n't just call in some help , or if something breaks you ca n't call in a tradie to fix it , you 've got to be self-sufficient out here . <p> " You really are at the mercy of the weather and the elements and it give you a healthy respect and awe for the surrounding environment and the weather that you do n't normally get in downtown Melbourne . " <p> " I like to get up early , just as it 's getting light , which is about 5:30am , " she said . <p> " It 's a very short commute to the office -- a whole 30 seconds from my bedroom down to the met office section -- and that is when we do our first weather reports of the day . <p> " After that I like to take advantage of the early morning to walk around the island to check that nothing has broken overnight and say hello to my favourite birds and if you 're lucky , maybe even see a turtle returning to water . " <p> The first of three weather balloons are released mid-morning and the day is spent observing and recording the weather and maintaining equipment . <p> " Buildings and equipment generally do n't like seawater and there 's plenty of that here , so we spend a lot of time looking after the infrastructure that 's keeps us alive , " she said . <p> The Willis Island crew have nicknamed their tour " Nerd Island " and Ms Shelley describes it as the ultimate share-house experience . <p> " We live together , work together , and rely on each other if things go wrong , " she said . <p> " I 'm lucky in that my crew are all hard-working and good-tempered , and who find the same things entertaining as I do . <p> " We have recreated scenes from Game of Thrones with our station mascot the inflatable dinosaur Bernie , built a life-size model of Mario Kart with tractors and coloured shells , and we are currently trying to have every jigsaw on station completed and on display in the office -- we have n't gotten bored yet . " <p> The island has a small gym and a pool table . A lot of down time is spent watching movies and making home-brewed beer . <p> " There 's nothing better than sitting down and watching the sunset and having a beer with your teammates , " she said . <p> " The last batch did n't turn out too well , unfortunately the quality is purely dependent on the skill of the manufacturer , so I am avoiding it because of the risk of food poisoning .
