<h> By Eryk Bagshaw &amp; Peter Hannam <p> Tathra : The Rural Fire Service has defended its handling of Sunday 's Tathra bushfire , following claims it rejected offers of help from Fire and Rescue NSW before the town was engulfed in fire . <p> Meanwhile , locals have pointed to fallen powerlines as the source of the fire that burned 69 homes to the ground , saying they saw " fires under the powerlines " which were moving in " hideous " winds . <p> The Rural Fire Service was on scene within 15 minutes of the first triple zero calls , but declined offers for help from NSW Fire and Rescue until the fire was bearing down on Tathra 's streets . <p> Photo : AAP <p> On Tuesday it was revealed the RFS was on scene within 15 minutes after the first triple zero calls , but declined multiple offers for help from NSW Fire and Rescue as it raced through the bush until the fire was bearing down on the town 's suburban streets . <p> " The competition between the state 's two fire services is dysfunctional and dangerous . In this case it has contributed to the loss of scores of homes that may have been saved had FRNSW urban crews been in Tathra . It has to stop . " <p> A spokeswoman for FRNSW downplayed any tension between the two organisations . <p> " Due to the initial location of the fire in remote and rugged bushland , the nearest FRNSW resources , being a city ( urban ) fire truck and hazardous materials van , were not suitable , " she said . <p> " FRNSW provided co-operative and effective assistance to the RFS when the fire escalated and life and property protection was required , and is still doing so today . " <h> What caused the fire ? <p> Jo Dodds , a councillor with the Bega Valley Shire , whose house was under threat from the blaze , said powerlines may have been the ignition source . <p> A friend of her stepson had run out of his house at Tarraganda and had seen the powerlines all moving because the winds were " hideous " and saw " fires under the powerlines " . <p> An electricity worker inspects properties on Wildlife Drive in Tathra after the bushfire . <p> Photo : Alex Ellinghausen <p> " It will all come out in the investigation I 'm sure , " Ms Dodds said . <p> Noah Dean , Ms Dodd 's stepson , said the location of the ignition appears to have been a clearing of powerlines that run down the hill from his friend 's house on Reedy Swamp Rd. " it was just crazy windy , " he said . " The power went out about 12.30pm and that was the first sign . " <p> Essential Energy 's Chief Executive Officer , John Cleland , said : " Given the complex nature of the post fire environment and ongoing investigations it is inappropriate at this point to comment on the likely cause of the fire . " <p> Loading <p> Local MP Mike Kelly said the weather conditions for the fire were bizarre , with temperatures reaching record March highs on Sunday . <p> " I 'm not sure what else could have been done to deal with that sort of situation , frankly , " he said . <p> Scores of residents were bussed through the town on Tuesday to see if their homes were still standing but were unable to leave the bus due to fears that asbestos dust has contaminated the bushfire-stricken streets . <p> Labor leader Bill Shorten visited the area on Tuesday morning after meeting with residents still locked out of the area and said he was taken aback by the scale of the fire 's destruction . <p> Mr Shorten said there was a " legitimate debate about the effect of climate change , " a day after Greens leader Richard Di Natale blamed global warming for the Tathra bushfire . <p> Loading <p> He warned insurers they " would be on display " over their response to the disaster . <p> " Sort out the claims quickly , do n't stuff people around , " he said , hinting at the public backlash that resulted in the nation 's largest insurers being hauled before the banking royal commission . <p> " This is the kind of disaster that people pay their premiums for and expect to get the service they paid for . " <p> Insurers have already begun fencing off areas and preparing to send in teams to do assessments before residents are expected to be allowed back into the town on Wednesday .
