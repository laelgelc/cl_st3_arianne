<p> A disease caused by a highly contagious fungus has wiped out as many as 200 species of frog worldwide since the 1970s , and pushed many more to the brink of extinction . <p> But researchers now believe that some frogs may be developing a resistance to the deadly chytrid fungus . <p> When chytridiomycosis wiped through a biodiversity hotspot called El Cope in Panama in 2004 , scientists said the spread was so rapid that , in places , dead frogs littered the forest floor . <p> In the study published today in Science , researchers have documented the recovery of nine frog species in three regions of Panama , including El Cope , and have observed infected frogs showing no ill effects from the fungus . <p> " We know that immediately after the outbreak , the frogs seemed to be completely gone , " lead author Jamie Voyles from the University of Nevada said . <p> " But in some locations they have recovered remarkably well and they 're infected with the pathogen . " <p> As part of the study , the researchers also exposed captive frogs to chytrid fungus . Those frogs were from a breeding stock taken into captivity before the outbreak of chytridiomycosis , and therefore had no chance to develop immunity . <p> Scientists at the time of the Panama pandemic said dead frogs littered the forest floor . <p> Supplied : Jamie Voyles <p> Six Australian frog species have n't been observed in the wild since the disease was detected here in 1993 , and the CSIRO say several Australian species are currently needing " urgent conservation intervention " to halt their decline . <p> Infected frogs can typically be found sitting in the open , emaciated , with peeling skin and showing signs of lethargy . <p> The fungus is able to use the frogs ' natural process of skin shedding to carry out its lifecycle , according to scientist Dr Lee Berger , who was not involved with the study . <p> " It gets inside the cells of the epidermis and as the cells turn over and are shed ... the mature fungus gets carried to the surface where it releases the infected spores to the water , " Dr Berger said . <p> " Sodium and potassium in the frogs ' blood goes down really low and they have a heart attack . " <p> She first positively identified chytridiomycosis in Australia in 1997 . <p> " People were looking at pollution and climate change and other things , " she said . <p> " But frogs were disappearing within months , people were seeing populations crash within months , so it made sense that it was a fatal disease . " <p> The fungus is mostly limited to moist and cooler environments like rainforests , and has n't yet been detected in areas like Kakadu . However a number of populations have been hit in North Queensland 's wet tropics . <p> Scientists have successfully translocated species to regions where the fungus does n't occur , such as the armoured mistfrog , which was reduced to a single population along a 4-kilometre stretch of stream in north-east Queensland .
