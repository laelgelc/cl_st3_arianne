But Ference helped the NHL Players ' Association create the Carbon Neutral Challenge , a program that sought to offset the carbon footprint of players traveling to games . He worked for years on the NHL Green initiative that spearheaded a dozen environmentally friendly projects adopted by teams . <p> He believes in hockey , because he believes the sport can be used as he has used it : as a way to build communities and , via the NHL , as a platform to effect change . <p> " From the NHL 's point of view , in the last year we put out our declaration of principles that spells out what we know hockey culture to be . It puts it on paper that we are welcoming of all forms of the game , and everybody who loves the game . No matter where you come from , no matter what your sexual orientation is , " Ference said . " It 's great , but those are words . It 's up to us to turn that into action . " <p> So he has taken action : Ference , who last played in 2015 for the Edmonton Oilers , has joined the NHL as its first director of social impact , growth and fan development . His focus will be on grass-roots growth , community efforts and better facilitating the relationship between players and the league . <p> " During his playing career , Andrew Ference demonstrated his commitment to excellence both on and off the ice serving as an innovative ambassador for the sport and the league in building vibrant communities through social impact , " said Kim Davis , NHL executive vice president of social impact , growth initiatives and legislative affairs . <p> Ference believes in hockey , because he also believes he 's not alone . <p> " There are a ton of guys that would love to be a little bit more open , be a little more P.K. " <p> So how do they get there ? <h> Getting political <p> Ference was outspoken on social issues during his playing career , and he is a leading voice now that he is employed by the NHL . Jeff Vinnick/NHLI via Getty Images <p> Ference 's environmentalism is , among NHL players , a populist initiative . Peers like Jonathan Toews of the Chicago Blackhawks have been vocal about climate change . Ference believes green initiatives resonate with NHL players because they understand them on a personal level . <p> " So much of it can be broken down to the local level . When you hear ' green initiatives , ' you think globally . I think sometimes that 's the hiccup : When you make it a global issue , which it is , but locally we can have such an impact , " he said . " If I 'm a player , and you start talking about the big issues about global warming , I might not be able to relate to that as much as I can relate to my local rink and some environmental designs for it , so it can stay open . Or that the waterway in our hometown is n't polluted . " <p> But step away from an issue like " let 's save local rinks " and you hear silence from NHL players who would rather tap dance on a landmine than wade into anything close to a political issue . That 's what would take the handcuffs off of guys . " <p> A big part of that equation , then , is trust between the players and their employers . Which , as we 've seen between the NHL and the NHLPA , does n't always exist . <h> Building a partnership <p> Article 32.1 of the NHL collective bargaining agreement with the NHLPA references a " Joint Broadcasting/Marketing Committee " between the league and its players . It 's supposed to explore sponsorships and licensing , media access and the relationship between players and their teams . It has been around for a while , and Ference and Brendan Shanahan both served as representatives on the committee . But it 's not exactly a priority in 2018 . <p> " I think it 's important . The better the relationships , and the more you have players understanding the relationship between the league and the union , the better . But it 's equally as important to understand your business partner . " <p> The players and the league point fingers at each other when it comes to problems in marketing the game , and in their overall partnership . The NHL claims players wo n't participate in promotional initiatives that may take them out of their comfort zones ; for example , the NHLPA shut down the All-Star Game fantasy draft . The players claim that the NHL does n't have a partnership with them to grow the game ; for example , the hypocrisy in not participating in the Winter Olympics while continuing to play NHL games internationally . <p> Part of Ference 's new gig is better facilitating that partnership , and he believes the key is to have both sides dive into the pool at the same time . <p> " You have to bring people in early . If you do a big initiative in isolation -- you do the work , the planning , but it 's all inside your own giant bubble -- there 's a good chance that when you reveal that with your business partner , it wo n't go over so well , " he said . " They need to cook up the ideas together . Get them involved at the start rather than finding out , after months of work , that there 's a hiccup . Things that involve players require that . " <p> Ference also believes this is n't a one-size-fits-all situation when it comes to partnering with the players . Things change with each new crop of players . Heck , when he started his career , players were on MySpace . <p> " I 'm of one generation . We have an entirely different generation of players that need to be heard , and I have to understand my own limitations when it comes to connections to the locker room , " he said . <p> In other words , the makeup of the NHL is changing .
