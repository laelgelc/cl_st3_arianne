<p> More of one of Antarctica 's largest and most important glaciers is floating on top of the ocean than previously thought , researchers say , which could have a significant impact on the rise of global sea levels . <p> The new research from the Australian Antarctic Program has found the Totten Glacier , located in the southern continent 's east , could be affected more by warming ocean temperatures because more of the glacier is directly in contact with the ocean , and much less is sitting on rock than originally thought . <p> Glaciologist Dr Ben Galton-Fenzi said the new research had caused a dramatic change in thinking . <p> " The Totten is actually one of the most important glaciers in Antarctica , and you might even argue the world , because it drains a region of the Antarctic ice sheet that could lead to several metres of potential sea level rise in the future " , he said . <p> " The amount of ice sitting behind the Totten is comparable to that which is contained within the whole West Antarctic ice sheet . " <p> Dr Galton-Fenzi 's team of researchers included scientists from Australia and overseas , with Professor Paul Winberry joining from Central Washington University in the United States . <p> Professor Winberry said the new information was obtained by using seismic technology . <p> " We use seismic methods . They 're very similar to how the oil companies might explore for oil underneath the earth , " he said . <p> " So we try to generate soundwaves , they go down , and we hear echoes coming from big discontinuities inside the Earth . " <p> These methods allow researchers to determine how thick the ice is , as well as how thick the ocean column is underneath the ice . <p> Antarctic researchers estimate the glacier itself holds enough ice to raise global ocean levels by around three metres if it were melted in its entirety . <p> " The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change IPCC estimates of future sea levels by 2100 suggests that there 's going to be about a metre of sea level rise through thermal expansion of the oceans , that 's atmospheric heat going into the oceans causing them to warm where they expand , " Dr Galton-Fenzi said . <p> " But also there 's a contribution of the Antarctic ice sheet -- and the Greenland ice sheets actually -- so as they melt that ice mass flows into the oceans and causes a further increase in sea levels . <p> " So when you think about that one metre that the IPCC have suggested by 2100 , which is significant , next to this idea that the Totten Glacier catchment contains the potential for three metres of sea level rise . <p> " ... then that 's an incredibly high number , and it 's a region that we really need to understand how soon its going to change to warming climate and how soon its going to cause sea levels to rise . " <p> Dr Galton-Fenzi says it was too early to understand what the consequences of these findings would be , but he said that any increase in ocean temperature meant that the glacier would likely be affected . <p> The researchers flew down to Casey Station in Antarctica in early December last year , and spent about eight weeks on the southern continent while investigating the glacier . <p> Despite the seriousness of the research , Professor Winberry said Antarctica was not a bad place to spend Summer . <p> " It was brilliant ... most days there was a high above freezing ! " he said .
