<h> Global bottled-water boom sparks local fight <p> Water extraction had divided the local community in the Tweed Valley ( Credit : ABC ) <p> It 's the new battle in the bush -- the bottled water wars . <p> On one side is Australia 's $800-million-a-year bottled water industry and its suppliers , on the other , rural residents who fear their most precious resource , groundwater , is being squandered . <p> " It 's dividing the local community , " said Larry Karlos , one of half a dozen water extractors in the Tweed Valley in northern New South Wales . <p> He 's been pumping water from an aquifer beneath his property for 16 years . <p> But his recent bid to increase the amount he sells to bottling companies has ignited local opposition . <p> Fourth-generation farmer Patrick O'Brien fears his children 's future is being jeopardised for the profit of the water industry . <p> " If they do n't stop this type of thing then , you know , what 's going to be left ? " he told 7.30 . <p> " What 's going to left for future generations ? No-one was really worried when they were trucking the water out in small amounts , but then they want more , they want more trips , they want bigger trucks . " <h> Does extraction affect groundwater ? <p> Without access to a town water supply , locals in the Bilambil Valley rely on water from the creek . <p> Mr O'Brien is concerned water extraction is affecting that supply . <p> " We never used to see any problems with it , " he said . <p> " Lately , in the last few years , it 's stopping more and more constantly throughout the year . " <p> Mr Karlos rejects that link . <p> " The water we 're taking out has no bearing on localised water , groundwater or creek water , which is what the farmers are using , " he said . <p> A hydrology report he commissioned in 2016 found his water extraction was " not considered likely " to have an impact on local creeks . <p> But Ian Acworth , an Emeritus Professor with NSW University who has researched ground water for the past three decades , said on the scant data available it was quite possible the two water systems were interconnected . <p> " It requires more investigation , " he told 7.30 . <h> Water mining v licenced extraction <p> The Tweed Water Alliance ( TWA ) , a group of concerned residents , is campaigning against what it calls " water mining " . <p> " It 's not just the extraction of water , but concerns that this has become a lawless industry , an industry which pays no attention to their conditions of approval , " TWA member Jeremy Tager said . <p> " They breach hours of operation and size of truck . " It appears they also take water they 're not entitled to -- it 's water theft . " <p> Mr Karlos denied such claims . <p> " That is an untrue comment . I 've never stolen water in my life , " he said . <p> " The water that we 're selling does belong to me because I have a licence to sell 60 megalitres a year , " he said . <p> But it 's complicated . <p> The Karlos operation does have a licence from NSW Water to extract a maximum of 60 million litres of water a year -- enough to fill 24 Olympic swimming pools . <p> However , to transport the water it needs development approval from the Tweed Shire Council . <p> By limiting the number , size and hours of operation of water tankers , the Greens-led council has effectively halved the amount of water the Karlos operation is allowed to sell . <p> " It 's a political thing and there 's no sense as to why we have the restrictions that we do on our business , " said Mr Karlos 's son , Matthew , who joined the family business three years ago to ensure it was complying with its licencing and council conditions . <p> Larry Karlos is taking the council to the Land and Environment Court this week after it rejected his bid to run bigger tankers , which would allow him to sell his full 60-megalitre allocation . <h> ' Water quality one of the biggest issues in the world today ' <p> Mr Tager said it was the Karlos operation which had a case to answer for what he alleged were breaches of its council approval . <p> He showed 7.30 photographs which allegedly showed tanker movements around the water filling station on the Karlos property on February 19 and 20 . <p> He said they showed nine tankers on the February 19 and 10 tankers the following day , both more than the six tankers allowed under the council development approval . <p> " He 's potentially making hundreds of thousands of dollars a year he 's not entitled to , " Mr Tager said . <p> Larry Karlos said he would not comment on the photographs , on legal advice , before this week 's court case . <p> But in an earlier interview , Matthew Karlos insisted the business operated within its council and NSW licence conditions . <p> The Tweed Shire Council last year narrowly voted to prohibit any expansion in the water-extraction industry , although it needs state government approval . <p> Faced with a burgeoning population , climate change and potential water shortages , Greens Mayor Katie Milne said a precautionary approach was needed . <p> " The problem is really that we do n't know what 's going on ... underground , " she said . <p> She said there had been a recent " rash of applications " for water extraction and that she was worried the industry could " expand exponentially " . <h> World partners : <p> This
