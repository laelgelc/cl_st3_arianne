<h> UK emissions fall as coal use plummets <p> A decline in coal-fired power generation helped reduce greenhouse gas emissions in the UK by 3% . <p> Britain 's greenhouse gas emissions fell by 3 per cent last year from 2016 levels , largely due to a decline in coal-fired power generation and marking the fifth straight yearly drop , preliminary government data shows . <p> Output of the heat-trapping gases in Europe 's second-largest emitter behind Germany fell to 456 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent ( CO2e ) , the Department for Business , Energy and Industrial Strategy ( BEIS ) said on Thursday . <p> The data shows Britain 's emissions have fallen 43 per cent since 1990 , meaning it is more than halfway towards meeting a legally binding target to cut its emissions by 2050 to 80 per cent below 1990 levels . <p> A breakdown of the 2017 figures showed emissions of carbon dioxide , the main greenhouse gas blamed for climate change , fell 3 per cent to 367 million tonnes . <p> Energy-sector CO2 emissions fell by 8 per cent as coal-fired power production dropped , and was replaced by record output from renewables such as wind and solar . <p> Separate provisional data , released by BEIS on Thursday , showed power generation from coal plants fell 26 per cent in 2017 to 21.36 terawatt hours ( TWh ) , making up less than 7 per cent of Britain 's total electricity supply . <p> Britain plans to close all coal-fired power stations by 2025 unless they are fitted with technology to capture and store carbon emissions . <p> Earlier this month , it also rejected plans for a new open cast coal mine in northeastern England on climate grounds . <p> Gas-fired power generation fell almost 6 per cent in 2017 , while renewable power generation from wind and solar soared , the data showed . <p> Wind power rose 33 percent to a record 40.9 TWh while solar generation was up 43 per cent to a record 2.9 TWh .
