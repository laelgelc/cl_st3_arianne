<h> Unease over government 's decision to ' take on ' agriculture <p> Lyn Webster says she is too busy doing farm work to deal with any new bureaucracy the Government might bring as it looks to bring in new policies to deal with agricultural emissions . <p> OPINION : In Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern 's Vogue magazine article , she points to New Zealand 's long-standing nuclear-free policy as an example and wants to apply that same moral leadership to action on climate change . <p> " We 're surrounded by island nations who will feel the brunt of climate change . So I see us as having a responsibility , " she said . <p> Of course New Zealand is a tiny contributor , overall , to the warming planet -- and yet carbon-heavy industries like farming , horticulture , and forestry are the country 's biggest businesses . Ardern is ready to take those sectors on , " Vogue said . <p> " The most difficult thing for us to do is to mitigate and offset our agricultural emissions , " Arden went on . <p> As someone who has invested a lifetime of money and energy into a struggling dairy farming business those words from our new leader make me feel very uneasy . <p> I can tell Environment Minister David Parker wants to weigh in on farming . The tools on his belt seem to be all about technology and artificial intelligence , he wants to see transformational land use change . <p> Sorry David , farming is not actually about AI and technology . It can help , but a lot of technology is unproven , expensive and unaffordable to many . <p> If the " transformational land use changes " are subjected to the same intense scrutiny dairy farming ( for example ) has been , they may be revealed as just as bad or worse than what we already have in terms of environmental impact and contribution to climate change . <p> Farming can be seen as a carbon sink , especially pastoral systems which New Zealand farmers excel in . <p> Ideals are great , implementing them in real life can be difficult . Currently I believe there is a group of scientists from the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) meeting in Christchurch that are discussing the future of farming in New Zealand . <p> I hazard a guess there will not be a farmer in the room . <p> I hope I can trust the New Zealand government not to throw agriculture under the bus . <p> I hope the meeting of scientists comes up with some achievable goals and clear pathways to reaching them . <p> However I fear that this is all just smoke and mirrors disguised as a money grab that will achieve nothing environmentally and put New Zealand agri-businesses under , which will quickly be followed by the rest of the economy . <p> All they need to do are the sums and realise that for this country to maintain or improve its standard of living , agriculture in New Zealand needs to be treated with respect . <p> Yet I see the words of our new prime minister and read that she " wants to take us on " . <p> Agriculture is food production . Surely there are other better places to start focusing on to mitigate environmental impacts . Food production should be the last thing to go . <p> So if " taking on " agriculture means good ideas and sensible policy that is easily implemented then good .
