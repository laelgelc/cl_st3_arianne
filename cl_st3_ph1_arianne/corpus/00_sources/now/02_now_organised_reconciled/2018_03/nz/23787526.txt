<p> Long term monitoring at Goat Is Marine Reserve shows water along the Auckland coastline is not warming up yet , despite climate change . <p> We all know warming ocean temperatures go hand-in-hand with climate change . But temperatures along the northern coastline of New Zealand , and in the Hauraki Gulf , look likely to stay unchanged for some time , according to new research . <p> Marine researcher at the University of Auckland , Dr Nick Shears , said any changes for Auckland in the near future are likely to be summer water temperatures staying a bit longer into autumn , rather than ramping up . <p> That 's good news for marine life that might struggle with warming temperatures . <p> Autumn and winter water temperatures at the southern end of the country have increased by 1.3 degrees Celsius in the last 50 years , while the ocean off the coast of the upper north island , at Leigh , has stayed the same . <p> Part of the East Australian current that meanders across the Tasman Sea and brings warm water to our northern east coast is weakening . <p> Shears and ecology expert at the University of Auckland , Dr Melissa Bowen , made the surprising find after looking at some of the longest continuous coastal water temperature records in the southern hemisphere . <p> These have been taken at Tasmania since 1946 , near Dunedin at Portobello , since 1953 , and for 50 years at Leigh . <p> Coastal currents flowing along the Australian coast are behind the odd differences in our northern and southern temperatures . <p> Nick Shears <p> Map of warming trends in the South West Pacific from satellite data ( 1982-2016 ) <p> Anyone who has seen the movie Finding Nemo will remember turtles and fish hitching a ride on the warm East Australian current sweeping down the Australian coastline .
