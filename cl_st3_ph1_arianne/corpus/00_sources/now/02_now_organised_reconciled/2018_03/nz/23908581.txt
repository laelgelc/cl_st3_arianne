<h> Auckland scientist climbs Kauri to study impact of climate change on giants of the forest <p> share <p> Source : <p> An Auckland scientist is climbing high into the canopy of kauri trees gain a better understanding of what keeps the giants of the forest alive , as climate change and extreme weather events pose a risk to their survival .. <p> Not just dieback , but climate change and extreme weather events are posing a risk to the giant trees ' survival . Source : 1 NEWS <p> Auckland University scientists are gauging how much water kauri trees in west Auckland are using . <p> " It uses water during the day . It shrinks a bit and overnight it refills again , so we get the shrinking and growing , " said Cate Macinnis-Ng , Auckland University ecophysiologist . <p> She says special instruments put into the trees measure the sap flow - how fast the water is moving up the tree . <p> " And then we can sort of sum it all together to work out how much water a tree uses in a day , or in a season , or in a whole year . " <p> Ms Macinnis-Ng has been studying how kauri trees adapt to climate change , for seven years . <p> While kauri dieback is the primary threat to the trees , its shallow root system makes it susceptible to extreme weather events too . <p> A tarpaulin at the foot of a tree catches the rainfall to dry out the soil and simulate drought-like conditions . <p> " When it comes to climate change here in New Zealand , it 's really the extreme events that are a big problem for forests . <p> " So the floods are an issue . Storms are a problem because they can knock over the trees , but droughts are also a really extreme event that impact our forests , " Ms Macinnis-Ng said . <p> The scientists can also measure how much carbon the tree is taking up . <p> " And that 's really important because those things change as the climate changes , " she said . <p> The scientists hope the influences of climate change on kauri can be reduced .
