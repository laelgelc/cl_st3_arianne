<h> Greenpeace says Ardern has ' the benefit of the doubt ' over end to oil <p> Greenpeace has said it is giving the Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern " the benefit of the doubt " over her position on ending oil exploration but has continued to mount pressure . <p> Environmental group Greenpeace says a failure to end oil exploration would put Labour in the same camp as National , but for now Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern has " the benefit of the doubt " . <p> The future of oil exploration in New Zealand was placed in the spotlight when Ardern made a highly symbolic move to receive a petition calling for the end of oil exploration on Monday , delaying attending a state visit to tell the rally that the Government was " actively considering " the issue . <p> Hours later Ardern suggested consideration being given to the issue was one which Government 's considered annually : how the annual block offer process under which exploration areas are marketed to the oil and gas industry , was administered . <p> HAMISH RUTHERFORD/STUFF <p> Flanked by Energy Minister Megan Woods and Climate Change Minister James Shaw , Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern takes the unusual step of personally receiving a petition calling for an end of oil exploration in New Zealand . <p> " What is the difference between considering the future of oil and gas exploration and considering oil and gas permits for the next year ? It 's exactly the same thing ... Considering block offers , you 're considering the future of oil and gas exploration . That is how people access oil and gas exploration . " <p> Ardern at one point appeared to rule out any change to existing contracts , but later said that " everything " was being considered . <p> " For now we 're giving her the benefit of the doubt and we 'll continue to send a strong message that New Zealanders want to end oil exploration . " <p> The organisation is maintaining pressure , running advertisements in newspapers on Wednesday to publish an open letter from notable New Zealanders calling for an end to exploration . <p> Simcock said ending oil exploration would represent " major success " for the climate movement , but there was only one option to avoid being on the wrong side of the argument . <p> Simcock said if Ardern 's Government awarded new exploration permits " she 's probably in the same boat as National leader Simon Bridges when he put all of this into action . Our position on that was we 've got a Government that 's not listening to the people and not taking climate change seriously . " <p> Bridges has speculated that the Labour-led Government is more likely to be considering changing how it offers new permits for oil exploration . <p> The National leader said Ardern was using the oil and gas issue as a means to distract from Labour 's other problems . <p> " Words really matter . This is a multi-billion dollar sector that contributes hugely in terms of jobs in our regions , not a political play thing for the Prime Minister to get her out of a hole . "
