<h> Bay woman on trip of a lifetime <p> Bay of Plenty engineer Amanda Kirk is one of 78 women involved in STEMM fields on a trip to Antarctica this month . Photos : Oli Sanson/Homeward Bound . <p> A Bay of Plenty woman is one of 78 female leaders taking part in a unique gender forum in Antarctica this month . <p> Environmental engineer Amanda Kirk is currently participating in ' Homeward Bound ' -- a global initiative for women in STEMM ( science , technology , engineering , mathematics and medicine ) fields addressing the global deficit of women and scientists in leadership roles . <p> Amanda , who grew up in Tauranga and now works in Whakatane for WSP Opus , says she applied for the programme after reading an article about ' Homeward Bound ' in February of last year . <p> " The programme brings together so many important issues into one programme : science , leadership , gender equity and global challenges such as climate change , " she says . <p> The application process consisted of submitting a two-minute video and answering a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ' Homeward Bound ' programme . <p> Applications are open to all women with a science background around the world . <p> The women spend half their day on a ship learning and developing skills in the four key areas of the programme : science , strategy , visibility and leadership . <p> " The content is delivered by experts in their fields through lectures , workshops and open discussions , " says Amanda . <p> The other half of the day is spent on land , exploring Antarctica and experiencing the wildlife . <p> " We have walked amongst colonies of thousands of penguins , seen seals on icebergs and watched humpback whales feeding in the ocean nearby . <p> " We have been fortunate enough to set foot on land most days of our trip .
