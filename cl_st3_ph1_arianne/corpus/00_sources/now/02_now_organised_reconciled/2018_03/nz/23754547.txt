<p> Few seriously doubt that oil and gas is a sunset industry . But there still is genuine debate about whether New Zealand has the gas reserves in operational fields to manage the transition away from fossil fuels . <p> ROSA WOODS/STUFF <p> Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern has put herself an a difficult position on whether to end oil exploration , whether the economy is yet ready to transition away from fossil fuels or not . <p> Much of New Zealand 's industry relies on gas , as does the electricity sector . Most of our electricity generation is renewable , but it is gas that gives the system security . <p> Few would rather see their granny sitting in the cold than burn gas if parts of the transmission network fell over . <p> But Ardern has created problems of precedent ( how to decide which other groups deserve such a greeting ) as well as expectation . <p> The symbolism of the Greenpeace rally was the type of theatre many groups try to manufacture at Parliament , but few manage . <p> Standing in front of posters of Norman Kirk , David Lange and Helen Clark , who had all made historic environmental moves , as well as a poster of herself , Ardern volunteered that she had delayed attending a state lunch to deliver such an important message . <p> HAMISH RUTHERFORD/STUFF <p> Flanked by Energy Minister Megan Woods and Climate Change Minister James Shaw , Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern took the unusual step of personally receiving a petition calling for an end of oil exploration in New Zealand . <p> " We 're working hard on this issue and we know that it 's one that we ca n't afford to spend much time on . " <p> Ardern gave an air of immediacy , but the Government is clearly not in a position to comment . <p> Hours after Ardern accepted the petition she gave a much more ' business as usual ' message to Parliament 's Press Gallery , even distancing herself from what may have been seen as a snub to the Indonesian president . <p> RNZ <p> Regional Economic Development Minister Shane Jones is claiming success over the storm he whipped up attacking Air New Zealand and calling for the chairman to resign . <p> Unlike the other controversies of recent weeks , where Ardern has been justifying the positions of her colleagues , this was a storm entirely of her own making . <p> The prime minister , who labelled climate change as her generation 's nuclear free moment , is now in an almost impossible position , for little political gain . <p> She has to either call a permanent end to oil exploration in New Zealand , whether the economy is ready or not , or face accusations of hypocrisy . <p> Pressure from Greenpeace will only mount . Cancelling the 2018 block offer , under which exploration space is offered to oil companies , will not be enough if the door is opened to future exploration . <p> Instead , we may not know the Government 's plans , for weeks or months . <p> For the industry this creates major uncertainty , the significance of which Labour seems to struggle to appreciate . <p> Businesses , by and large , are better at coping with bad news than they are at coping with uncertainty . You can not plan for it or adapt to it . <p> Cameron Madgwick , the chief executive of lobby group Pepanz , seemed more concerned about the lack of clarity , pointing out that whatever one took from the Greenpeace meeting , Labour 's stated policy suggested an ongoing role for exploration . <p> It was not even the only time this week that a major part of the economy was put on notice that the operating conditions are much less certain now than they were under National . <p> Perhaps Air New Zealand should be pressured to improve its regional connections . Perhaps such pressure could be partly subsidised by cash from the $1 billion a year provincial development fund which Jones manages . If regional connectivity is really as important as many people think , it may not be the worst use of the money . <p> Instead , though , the Government gives symbolic gestures and name calling . <p> Ardern ruled that Jones had gone a " step too far " in calling for the sacking of Air New Zealand 's chairman , but that is hardly a full rebuke . <p> Other industries may be getting nervous , at a time when business confidence is already low . <p> The electricity sector , where the Government has a controlling share in three of the four biggest players , may wonder what influence ministers will wield . How long before the hugely profitable banking sector gets a serve from a Cabinet minister ? <p> The Government has every right to take action against any part of the economy it chooses . But in a series of recent episodes it has delivered nothing . <p> If Ardern 's Government is going to be more interventionist , as it has promised it will , then it should spend a little more time working on its plans and warning the industry , rather than making political statement with no back-up .
