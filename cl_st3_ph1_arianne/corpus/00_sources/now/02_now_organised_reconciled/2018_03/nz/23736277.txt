<h> Simon Bridges accuses Jacinda Ardern of being ' reckless ' over comments calling for end to oil exploration <p> share <p> Source : <p> NZN <p> The prime minister 's message to activists calling for an end to oil drilling was a " stunt " and she had changed her tune later in the day , National leader Simon Bridges says . <p> Jacinda Ardern and Simon Bridges . <p> Source : 1 NEWS <p> During a surprise appearance at the presentation of a Greenpeace petition on parliament 's lawn on Monday , Jacinda Ardern said the government was " actively considering " the issue and that it was " not long " until a decision would be made . <p> The 45,000-signature petition called for the end to future oil and gas exploration in New Zealand waters . <p> Asked to clarify what she meant later in the day , Ms Ardern told reporters the government was currently mulling the annual offer of exploration permit requests and the future annual tender process . <p> Mr Bridges says those two things are miles apart and the speech to climate change protesters was a " political stunt " and " big talk " . <p> " There 's a world of difference between changes to block offer ... and ending all new oil and gas exploration , " he said . <p> " What matters is the words you use . Prime ministers ca n't be loose with these things . It has a chilling effect . There are thousands of people with jobs here , do n't be reckless like that . " <p> But Ms Ardern on Monday told reporters the party was thinking hard about the future of permits and that she believed it 's what the climate change campaigners were asking for . <p> " I think what you 'll find the people outside were asking us to consider was what we 're doing in the future . " <p> She also told the activists the government had to balance the need for action on climate issues with a " just transition " that considered economic impacts as well . <p> Environmentalists have widely praised Ms Ardern for stating before the election that climate change was the " nuclear-free moment " of her generation and have since put pressure on her to move on stopping new exploration . <p> But supporters of oil drilling say an end to the industry would put at risk about 10,000 jobs - many in the regions - and that it adds $2.5 billion a year to the economy . <p> Mr Bridges said while he believed an eventual transition away from gas was needed , he did n't think it could take place for possibly two decades without forcing New Zealand to import energy from dirtier sources - such as coal - to meet energy demands .
