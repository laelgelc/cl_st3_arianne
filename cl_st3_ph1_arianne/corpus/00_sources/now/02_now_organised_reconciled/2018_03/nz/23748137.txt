Science reporter Jamie Morton asked Adrienne van der Sar , the visiting deputy director of Dutch Delta Programme Commissioner , which is tasked with protecting the country against high water and securing freshwater supplies . <p> The Netherlands has put in place impressive mitigation efforts - and that 's perhaps not surprising given its ingenuity and history in this area . Can the Netherlands ' efforts be partly explained by a greater awareness , appreciation and culture of preparedness and pragmatism around the threat of climate change ? And is this perhaps lacking elsewhere ? <p> The Netherlands has been managing its waters for centuries and that has resulted in a quite well organised system , also financially and institutionally . <p> Due to the fact that the government there has taken adequate measures over the last decade , there is great trust in it to keep the Netherlands flood proof . <p> But as a result of that , Dutch citizens nowadays are not always very aware of the risks of water . <p> So the Dutch government is also currently working on increasing awareness on flood and fresh water problems in the future . <p> Living in a flood-prone country - about 60 per cent of the Netherlands is prone to flooding - means that we have to continuously keep on working on flood-risk management . <p> In The Netherlands , we have made the decision to prevent future disasters instead of responding to them as we used to do . <p> That 's why we have set up the Delta Programme in 2010 . <p> During my visit to New Zealand , I learned that in areas where floods recently occurred , the awareness of the challenges related to climate change is increasing <p> The work of the Climate Change Adaptation Technical Working Group also contributes to this awareness . <p> I am curious about the next steps New Zealand is going to take on climate adaptation . <p> Despite our different geographies - although two-thirds of Kiwis live in potentially exposed areas to storm surge , flooding and sea-level rise - what lessons might the Netherlands be able to teach New Zealand and why ? <p> Indeed , both countries are different and require different approaches . <p> On the other hand , there are certain lessons that we have learned that might provide interesting insights for New Zealand . <p> In my opinion , it starts with developing an adaptive or step-by-step strategy with a long-time horizon . <p> Climate change goes slowly , so it gives time to make decisions and think of the long-term challenges ahead . <p> Most countries take action after a disaster and that is too late and expensive . <p> Better to invest ahead and avoiding disasters happening . <p> Amsterdam canal . <p> Yes , it is certainly required to take upstream measures to ensure flood protection in the downstream areas . <p> Investments are necessary . <p> Still , it is much cheaper than the costs after a disaster has hit and repair works need to be initiated on top of protection measures . <p> Infrastructure is not always a good solution , for example , a successful approach in the Netherlands is the " Room for the River " projects . <p> These projects allow controlled floods in rural areas - or living with water instead of fighting against water .
