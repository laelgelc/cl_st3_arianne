<h> Students visit research ship <p> COMPETITION WINNERS : Gisborne Boys ' High School students Matthew Proffit ( left ) and Travis Mitchell in front of the JOIDES Resolution at the Timaru Port . The ship is a research vessel that drills into the ocean floor to collect and study core samples . Scientists use data from the JR to better understand climate change , geology and Earth 's history . Picture supplied <p> Two Gisborne Boys ' High School students , accompanied by their teacher , toured the research vessel JOIDES Resolution last week before it embarked on its two-month research voyage . <p> The students were winners of the Name a Deep Sea Observatory competition , which asked students to name the observatories that would be lowered 500m below the seafloor during the research voyage . <p> They travelled to Timaru , where the ship was in port , and were given a tour and shown the science equipment on board by co-chief scientist Laura Wallace and educator Aliki Weststrate . <p> " The ship tour was a highlight of the trip -- how high-tech and huge it was , " said year 12 student Matthew Proffit . <p> It was exciting to see it up close and go on board . <p> Matthew had selected the winning name Te Matakite , which means to see into the future . <p> The sea observatories will collect information to learn more about the future earthquake and tsunami risk the Hikurangi subduction zone might pose to the East Coast . <p> " I was also blown away with seeing the device that I named and knowing that I had a small part in the whole project that has taken so long to be put in place . " <p> The two observatories will measure and record how the Hikurangi subduction zone is behaving over the next decade . <p> This is a type of subduction zone where the Pacific plate ' dives ' underneath the Australian Plate , and is New Zealand 's most rapidly-moving fault line . <h> Poll <p> Voting please wait ... <p> Your vote has been cast .
