<h> Vanishing sea ice could alter Antarctic marine ecosystems <p> Separately , scientists found a " catastrophic " absence of snow and ice on a glacier in Arthur 's Pass National Park . <p> Scientists returning from Antarctica say an " astonishing " lack of sea ice could herald major changes in the marine ecosystems around the icy continent . <p> An international research team aboard the Niwa vessel Tangaroa spent six weeks installing instruments and carrying out experiments in the Ross Sea to help their understanding of the climate , atmospheric and oceanographic processes in the region and how they influence the marine ecosystem . <p> But they were shocked at the scarcity of sea ice -- with the coverage on the oceans around Antarctica this summer the second lowest since satellite records began back in the 1970s . <p> IAIN MCGREGOR/STUFF <p> Scores of species , including penguins , could be at risk from vanishing sea ice in Antarctica . <p> The lowest level was recorded last year and , while the pattern does not indicate a trend , scientists say the expected long-term predictions of less ice could trigger huge changes to the marine ecosystem . <p> Speaking from the Tangaroa after it docked in Wellington on Wednesday , voyage leader and Niwa marine ecologist Dr David Bowden said : " We planned this voyage knowing it was late in the season and that we might not be able to get where we wanted to go because of ice , but it has been almost completely ice-free . <p> SUPPLIED/NIWA <p> Niwa scientist Dr David Bowden , on the research vessel Tangaroa , says his team was astonished at the lack of sea ice in the Southern Ocean . <p> " A lot of the ecology of the Ross Sea and the whole of the Antarctic marine system is tied up with ice . <p> " The animals and plant life in the sea to a greater or lesser extent is all dependent on sea ice , so if we end up with no sea ice it 's going to be a very different ecological system than we have now . " <p> One species that is highly dependent on sea ice for survival is krill , a crustacean that forms a key part of the food chain for everything from Adelie penguins and seabirds to whales . <p> DAVE ALLEN/NIWA <p> A team of 23 scientists spent six weeks carrying out experiments to understand the influence of the changing climate on the marine ecosystem . <p> " Less ice , less krill -- it will have a knock-on for the rest of the system there , " Bowden said . <p> " There are also animals that are dependent on sea ice for resting . Crab eater seals we know very little about , but in a normal year we would expect to sea a lot of them holed up on ice floes . <p> " With no sea ice there is less food for them because they feed on krill as well , and there is less habitat generally . <p> DAVID BOWDEN/NIWA <p> Researchers want to determine whether the objectives of the new Ross Sea marine marine protection area are being achieved . <p> " So there will be changes certainly , but from a single voyage like this all we can do is gather data , which becomes one piece of the jigsaw puzzle . " <p> While he could not categorically blame climate change for the loss of coverage , Bowden said more work is needed to understand the impact it has in the polar regions . <p> During the expedition , the 23-strong team of scientists examined everything from microbial production in plankton to the distributions of seabed fauna , krill and whales , and collected data to help determine whether the objectives of the newly-established Ross Sea marine marine protection area are being achieved . <p> They also established an experiment to examine the movement of extremely cold water from the Ross Sea towards the tropics that acts as a regulating system on the global circulation of the oceans . <p> Meanwhile , University of Canterbury researchers have found a catastrophic loss of snow and ice on the Rolleston Glacier in Arthur 's Pass National Park from the extremely hot summer . <p> Dr Heather Purdie said the glacier had lost nearly all the snow accumulated over the previous year , as well as snow from earlier years . <p> " This is likely the most negative mass balance year ever recorded on this glacier . <p> " The only snow left on the Rolleston Glacier was snow that had avalanched down onto the glacier from the surrounding mountain peaks . Without this avalanche input , the glacier would have had no snow left at all . "
