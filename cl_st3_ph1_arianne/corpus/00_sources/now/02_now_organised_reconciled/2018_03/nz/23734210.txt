<h> Maths illiteracy has led to Trump and Brexit - Australian teacher Eddie Woo <p> PALLAVI SINGHAL <p> Last updated 09:16 , March 20 2018 <p> JANIE BARRETT/FAIRFAX AUSTRALIA <p> Cherrybrook Technology High School maths teacher Eddie Woo says the way maths is taught in Australia and around the world needs to be changed radically . <p> For Eddie Woo , there 's a direct link between the trouble many adults have with maths and the election of Donald Trump , Brexit and climate change denial . <p> Woo , the head maths teacher from Cherrybrook Technology High School northwest of Sydney , who has become known around the world for his maths YouTube videos , said the inability of many people to understand data and statistics has become a major social problem . <p> " We 've just had major election results that stem directly from mathematical illiteracy , because people do n't have the mathematical wherewithal to understand the statistics they 're being fed , " Woo said . <p> " They look at them and say , ' Well OK , sure , I do n't know any better . ' The fact that climate change denial can survive has a direct causal relationship with mathematical illiteracy . " <p> Woo was a top-10 finalist for this year 's US$1 million Global Teacher Prize , which was awarded to British arts and textiles teacher Andria Zafirakou in Dubai on Sunday . <p> He said things would get worse unless the way maths is taught was changed radically . <p> " My big thesis is that people hate maths because they do n't know what maths is , " Woo said . <p> " Growing up , I hated music because I was forced to play scales . I lasted 1 1/2 years and and went , ' Thank god this torture is over ' and then , when I was 17 years -old , I picked up an acoustic guitar because I wanted to play a song I heard on the radio and realised music is a joy . <p> " Most people never transition in their understanding of mathematics , so they 're still like seven-year-old me . " <p> Woo said he was working with the NSW Department of Education to help primary school teachers become more confident with maths . <p> " Many primary teachers I speak to are afraid and anxious about mathematics and they ca n't help but communicate that to their students , so I think targeting that is really important , " he said . <p> " Another part of the problem is the way the subject is taught . We move forward in lockstep fashion where , if you have n't gotten algebra in the two weeks I spend on it in year 7 , you 'll never learn it again . Next year , you 'll still be trying to wrap your head around it but I 'll have moved on . <p> " You can contrast that to a mastery-based system . If you 're learning a musical instrument , how old is an eighth-grade piano student ? And the answer is , however old they need to be to get to eighth grade . " <p> Woo , whose videos have been watched in nearly every country in the world , said being nominated for the global prize was " pretty ' pinch me ' " . <p> " I ca n't even get from one end of the ballroom to the other without getting stopped 10 times , it 's bizarre , " he said . <p> " It 's also been eye-opening seeing the challenges that people around the world go through . I was speaking to a principal in Kenya and the government only pays for half of the teachers his school requires so , for the other half , he as a principal sometimes pays their salaries out of his pocket . <p> " It really puts into perspective the issues that we face in Australia . " <p> Zafirakou , who teaches in Brent , a low-income area in Britain , said some of the challenges facing her students " sound like they come from the pages of a Dickens novel rather than 21st century Britain " . <p> " Some have been forced to play truant because they share a kitchen with other families and can not miss their allocated time slot to prepare the family dinner , " Zafirakou said . <p> " The most important thing we can do as teachers is to ensure our schools are safe havens . " <p> The reporter travelled to the Global Education and Skills Forum as a guest of the conference .
