<p> The Government is being urged to get cross-party support for its zero-carbon goals , in a new report by the Parliamentary Commissioner for the Environment . <p> Simon Upton wants to see climate policies developed that will remain consistent over multiple electoral cycles : " A long-term reorientation of the economy away from fossil fuel dependency requires policy stability decoupled from the short term ebb and flow of politics . " <p> " This new Government has a once-in-a-generation opportunity and a clear mandate and now needs to set out a timetable for action and implement it , " says Canterbury University political science expert Bronwyn Hayward . <p> The report , released on Wednesday , details the commissioner 's recommendations for establishing an Independent Climate Commission and a UK-style Zero Carbon Act . <p> " The report is clear , thorough and compelling , and in my view all nine recommendations make sense and should be supported by the Government and Parliament , " says Professor Jonathan Boston , of Victoria University 's School of Government . <p> Mr Upton says the UK 's 2008 Climate Act provides @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ but that there are important differences in our economies and emissions profiles that must be taken into account . <p> He also says all sectors need to play their part to reduce greenhouse gas emissions - including agriculture . <p> According to the commissioner , a new Climate Commission should consider setting separate targets for the different greenhouse gases . <p> He says the Zero Carbon Act should put in place a process for meeting carbon budgets and targets , including the use of policy tools like the Emissions Trading Scheme and Electric Vehicle Policy . <p> Mr Upton says he supports the intent of the UK 's approach , but notes its long-term effectiveness has yet to be measured .
