Why , then , is there an increasing call to " connect with nature " ? Does it mean we 've become disconnected with our surroundings or have we failed to respond to the requirements of nature ? That question still hangs on a delicate balance that is dictated mostly by individual perspective . <p> Urbanisation <p> One might say with good reason that living in an increasingly urban world has disconnected us from nature : How can we connect when we are not living in lush green forests but within a city constructed from concrete and glass , often designed to keep nature out ? <p> Urbanisation began well after the agricultural revolution , turning small riverside settlements and trading posts into townships and , eventually , with the advancement of technology and science , into complex cities with millions of inhabitants . <p> As cities became more complex , so did the lives of its citizens . With the onset of the industrial revolution , cities became mega-cities , centres of art , culture , innovation and education . <p> But many cities , plagued by random development and overpopulation have now become undesirable places for human habitation . <p> There is every reason to believe that many of the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and water pollution are externalities of technology-dependent urban life . <p> No matter to what extent we become disconnected with nature , nature reconnects with us , often violently , represented by prolonged heatwaves , increasing frequencies of cyclones and hurricanes , irregular rainfall patterns , increased epidemics and so on . <p> Over time , we have learned that cities are actually heavily reliant on the surrounding natural landscape , from the input of energy and resources to the disposal of waste , to freshwater resources for drinking and sanitation . <p> A city is thus not an isolated system , but very much a part of nature . One does n't need to go live in the jungles to connect with nature . Simple acts like planting a roof garden , recycling waste , conserving water , going on excursions or picnics to the country-side , helping clean litter from local parks , participating in local cleanup of degraded water bodies such as lakes and canals , taking public transport instead of personal vehicles are all activities the helps us connect to nature . <p> As urban citizens , we have better access to information technologies which we take full advantage of to maintain virtual social connectivity and even businesses .
