<h> It 's too soon to strip grizzlies of federal protection <p> In just a few short months , trophy hunters could start lining their walls with the heads of famous animals that have been killed for pure sport : Yellowstone 's grizzly bears . <p> The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service has stripped Yellowstone grizzlies of federal endangered species protections , turning over management to state governments eager to declare open season on these American icons . But grizzlies have regained barely a foothold in the lower 48 states , so any delisting is premature and could undermine 40 years of complex work to recover the species . <p> Grizzlies currently occupy less than 5 percent of their historic range in the contiguous United States . Before Europeans settled the West , roughly 50,000 bears roamed the western half of the country . Once those new settlers arrived , the bears were shot , poisoned and trapped nearly to extinction . <p> Now fewer than 1,800 grizzlies can be found in five identified recovery areas in the northern Rocky Mountains , with three of these populations barely hanging on @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ area , the Selway-Bitterroot ecosystem in Idaho and Montana , even though it harbors one of the largest wild areas in the lower 48 . <p> This means it 's still too soon to strip federal Endangered Species Act protections from any grizzlies in the lower 48 -- including bears in and around Yellowstone , whose numbers have improved since federal protections were first implemented . <p> Despite population gains since 1975 , Yellowstone grizzlies still face significant threats . Federal biologists documented a record-high 61 grizzly deaths in 2015 and 58 in 2016 , with the majority of those caused by people . These deaths are likely being fueled by changes in the bear 's primary food sources that are forcing them farther into areas where they 're more likely to come into conflict with people . <p> In recent years Yellowstone bears have suffered the loss of some of their most important food sources , including seeds from whitebark pine , which has seen massive declines related to disease and climate change and is now itself a candidate for endangered species protections . But they 're not fully recovered yet . That 's why the organization I work for , the Center for Biological Diversity , opposes the removal of Yellowstone 's -- or any other -- grizzlies from the federal endangered species list . <p> Grizzlies are a vital keystone species , regulating prey populations and dispersing untold numbers of seeds .
