<p> Let me just say this at the outset : Mildred Dresselhaus , who died in February at the age of 86 , was an amazing women . An accomplished physicist who won just about every scientific award short of the Nobel Prize , Dresselhaus was by all accounts a pioneer , patriot and great American . <p> Nonetheless , I still find GE 's commercial in her honor to be profoundly creepy . <p> If you have n't seen the ad , which debuted at the Oscars and is omnipresent on MSNBC , you can find it on YouTube . <p> It opens with a little girl unwrapping her birthday present . " A Millie Dresselhaus doll ! " she exclaims with glee . The camera then cuts to a video of the same scene on a Facebook page , with a proud parent captioning it " Found her a Mille Dresselhaus doll ! #BestMomEver . " The doll shows the elderly Dresselhaus in her apparently trademark red sweater and black slacks . <p> The montage speeds up after that . Little girls are all dressed like Millie for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ her talk show . We see a maternity ward where all the little girls are named " Millie . " Then , a girl leaving her physics exam texts her friend . " Aced physics ! ! " she types , followed by two Dresselhaus emojis . <p> Finally , the narrator chimes in , during a scene with two young women snapping pictures of themselves posing in front of a giant mural of Dresselhaus , wearing a crown , on the side of a building . " What if we treated great female scientists like they were stars ? " the narrator asks . <p> More scenes of young people freaking out over the octogenarian scientist unfold , including a young woman pointing to the Dresselhaus image on her T-shirt and saying , " Yasss , queen ! " <p> The narrator goes on : " What if Millie Dresselhaus , the first woman to win the National Medal of Science in Engineering , were as famous as any celebrity ? " <p> Paparazzi fight to snap a picture of her having lunch . Crowds fill public squares as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Glassy-eyed women wait in anticipation for her to walk on stage . <p> " What if we lived in a world like that ? " the narrator asks . <p> Then the screen goes black . And in simple white text we learn that " GE is helping create that world . " <p> The commercial goes on to explain that GE wants to get more women interested in science and engineering , which is fine by me . <p> But the company , which depends heavily on government contracts , subsidies and regulations , has a long history of tapping into trendy political fads and causes that just so happen to help its bottom line . <p> The most famous was GE 's " Green Week " propaganda campaign back when it owned NBC . GE ordered all of NBC 's shows -- news , sitcoms , dramas , sports -- to incorporate messages about the importance of fighting climate change and using renewable energy . Only rarely would anyone disclose that the corporate behemoth made billions of dollars selling products -- light bulbs , wind turbines , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ government subsidies and regulations . <p> The crowd that normally demonizes big business as evil and " right-wing " did n't care . Neither did the usual media critics and decriers of conflicts of interest . Why ? Because they agreed with the political agenda that GE was parasitically exploiting . When big businesses do what the left likes , words like " greed , " " propaganda , " " crony capitalism , " " corruption , " etc. , never come to mind . They call it " corporate responsibility . " <p> At the time I liked to ask , " What if Rupert Murdoch ordered all of Fox 's shows , including its prime-time sitcoms , to commit to ' Pro-Life Week ' ? What would be the response then ? " To my mind , that would have been better than Green Week , because at least Murdoch has no financial interest in federal abortion policy . <p> I have no idea what , if any , profit motive informs the Dresselhaus ad other than the desire for GE to be seen as a loyally @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ various regulators , bureaucrats and feminist groups . <p> But it 's still creepy and awfully condescending . It 's creepy because any society based on deranged cults of personality is scary and weird . Making science itself into some kind of fanatical faith is almost a dystopian cliche . <p> It 's condescending because it makes it sound like the notion that women can be scientists and engineers is the stuff of science fiction or fantasy .
