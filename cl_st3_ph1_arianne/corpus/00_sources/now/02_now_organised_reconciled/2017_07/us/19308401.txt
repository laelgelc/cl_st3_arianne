<p> M'HAMID EL GHIZLANE , Morocco -- For generations , they were known as " rain nomads , " herders who moved constantly along the western rim of the Sahara Desert in search of a patch of green where their goats and camels could graze . <p> Then the rain , never plentiful , became even more sporadic . Temperatures got hotter . A dam choked another source of precious water , the Draa River . Not even the camels could endure . <p> Families whose lives revolved around the seasons and the needs of their livestock , gave up and became villagers . Over the years , many settled in this oasis town whose one main street merges into the edge of the desert . <p> About two-thirds of Morocco 's roughly 25,000 remaining nomads live in this region about 200 miles south of Casablanca , according to a 2014 government survey . The number of nomads had fallen by 63% from the previous decade , the same survey by the Moroccan High Commission for Planning found .
