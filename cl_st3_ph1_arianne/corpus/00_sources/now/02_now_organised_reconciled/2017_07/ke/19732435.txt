<p> INCREASING FARM incomes , addressing the food security needs of the country and mitigating climate change are the three main issues any policy maker in Africa should focus on . <p> But what happens when policies are based on limited information and intense lobbying by agribusiness and agrochemical institutions who hold the key to donor funds ? <p> You face a situation like the one in Kenya where billions of shillings are sunk into projects and models which are just not producing results . <p> Let 's take the example of the Galana/Kulalu Food Security Scheme in Tana River . This project is expected to make Kenya self-sufficient in maize production . <p> With about 2,500 acres of land under maize cultivation until late last year , the figures were extremely bewildering . Here 's a project where most of its Sh7 billion budget has already been allocated and spent . <p> According to the Water ministry , this figure could reach Sh9 billion . At the end of it all the farm is only able to produce a yield of around 2,790kg/acre from its third harvest . <h> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ terms of production ? To put it into perspective , let 's look at figures documented by Greenpeace Africa in 2014 among farming communities adopting innovative ecological practices like push-pull technology . <p> We found yields averaging 2,347 kg/acre , but what was even more interesting was that this was in Trans Nzoia where the farmers using chemical inputs that we documented were struggling with an average yield of less than half of this per acre . <p> Farmers practising ecological farming were making three times more when compared to those using chemicals . <p> In Ukambani , we discovered that organic farmers had much lower crop losses compared to conventional farmers due to the drought and effects of the el nino and la nina . <p> This then raises a number of questions : What is the purpose of big projects like Galana- Kulalu ? Why are billions being poured in supporting and subsidising the use of chemicals ? <p> It is important that policymakers rethink and take a multidimensional approach to agriculture . They need to ensure that farmers are empowered and our farming systems are strengthened .
