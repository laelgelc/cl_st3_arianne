<h> Impulsive spider just as accurate as a docile one <p> Associate Professor Li Daiqin and PhD student Chang Chia-chen are studying whether personality affects areas such as the ability of a species to survive , as management of animal personalities could help significantly in environmental initiatives.ST PHOTO : SEAH KWANG PENG <h> Spiders have personalities , and individual traits determine how they hunt and make choices : NUS study <p> Spiders have surprisingly human traits , with personalities that determine if they make decisions hastily or cautiously . <p> But while a person who rushes into a decision might be more likely to make a mistake , an impulsive spider is just as accurate as a docile one , a study by researchers at the National University of Singapore ( NUS ) has found . <p> The study was conducted on Portia labiata , a species of jumping spider - an invertebrate known for its high cognitive ability . <p> The study found that aggressive individuals made decisions three to four times faster than their docile counterparts , but were just as accurate in hunting down their prey . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ The researchers distinguished the spiders ' personalities by stroking them with a brush . Those that attacked the brush were categorised as aggressive , while those that avoided the brush were categorised as docile . <p> The findings of the study were published recently in the scientific journal Behavioral Ecology . <p> Said Associate Professor Li Daiqin from the university 's department of biological sciences , who led the study : " The outcome is rather surprising , as our team had initially thought that spiders that made quick decisions were more likely to make the wrong choices , similar to humans . <p> " This new knowledge provides us with a better understanding of ecological processes such as foraging and predator-prey interactions in the animal kingdom . " <p> Prof Li said that animal behaviour studies in the past had focused on the average behaviour of the species studied , with individual variations in behaviour dismissed as " noise " during the research process . <p> DARE TO EXPLORE <p> Species that are bolder and keener to explore their surroundings might be more likely to invade new environments . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> Only in the past decade has interest in the behaviours of individual animals picked up . <p> " What we want to understand is whether a personality type influences fitness , including whether they survive better or have healthier offspring , " said Prof Li . <p> In another study on Portia labiata led by Prof Li , aggressive and docile spiders had varying success in catching prey that displayed predictable and unpredictable traits . <p> The prey used in the study was Cosmophasis umbratica , another species of jumping spider . <p> " The results showed that aggressive predators fared better when catching prey with unpredictable behaviour , while docile predators performed much better when hunting prey with predictable behaviour , " said Ms Chang Chia-chen , a PhD student from the same department at NUS , who is the first author of both studies . <p> Prof Li said that the findings and the management of animal personalities could play a crucial role in areas such as conservation , invasion biology and climate change . <p> " For instance , species that are bolder and keener @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ new environments , " he pointed out . <p> The team is planning to conduct further studies to test the relationship between the personality and the decision-making style of spiders by giving them tasks with various levels of difficulty . <p> The researchers will also study the gene profiles of spiders
