<h> Watershed studies underbudget , so city will do more of them <p> Greater Sudbury 's approach to watershed studies has left a $500,000 surplus , money the province says the city can use to conduct another three studies . ( File ) <p> Greater Sudbury 's approach to its watershed studies has left a $500,000 surplus , money the province says the city can use to conduct another three studies . <p> The surplus is from a $2.3 million grant from the Ministry of Environment and Climate Change to pay for nine studies in the city . Approved in February 2016 , it funded studies on Ramsey Lake , seven areas along Junction Creek , as well as White Water Lake in Azilda . <p> A staff report said there were savings in combining the Junction Creek studies , creating the $500,000 surplus . <p> " In discussions with the MOECC , it was agreed the remainder of the provincial grant can be used to complete additional subwatershed studies in keeping with the intent of the funding agreement , " the report says . <p> That means the money @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ list : the Whitson River , where a subwatershed study and stormwater master plan will be done . <p> " This study includes three studies that were identified in the 2006 Stormwater Background Study , the Whitson Lake , Whitson River ( Blezard Valley , Val Caron and Hanmer ) and Chelmsford , " the report says . " Similar to the Junction Creek Study currently underway , combining the above studies into a single study will allow the city to gain a holistic assessment of the Whitson River Subwatershed . " <p> As with the other studies , the goal is to determine the best stormwater management practices to ensure water quality is protected during major storms . <p> " The quality of the urban storm drainage can impact the quality of downstream water systems which can directly affect municipal and private drinking water supplies , " the report said . <p> The city has also committed $250,000 a year to the studies . The studies will lead to recommendations to develop management practices to reduce erosion , prevent flooding and maintain or improve water quality . Recommendations will also help determine rules for residential and other developments near local watersheds . <p> The impact of climate change will also be factored into the results of the studies . The studies will collect data on surface water resources ; surface water quality ; groundwater resources ; stream morphology ; terrestrial resources ; aquatic resources ; land use ; and , demographics . <p> They will also look at what happens during extreme events -- floods and big melts , for example -- and the impact the events have on the lake . Control measures will be focused on minimizing the impacts of these big events .
