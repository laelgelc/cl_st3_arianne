<h> Prof. Gordon Hughes of Edinburgh , U.K. , writes : <p> In his letter of July 14 , Scott Moe , Saskatchewan 's Minister of the Environment , makes inaccurate aspersions and misconstrues the point of my research . I co-authored one of the earliest international assessments of climate change for the World Bank in 1992 . For me the critical issue was , and still is , how to find the correct balance between prevention and adaptation . To get this balance correct it is essential to spend money on measures that yield the highest benefit per dollar spent . Carbon capture is nowhere near the top of the list when it comes to prevention . Adaptation is even cheaper . <p> In early May , Mr. Moe told the Canadian Press that Saskatchewan can not afford a carbon tax of C$50 per tonne of CO2 . That figure is less than one-third of the level required to justify deploying carbon capture in 2040 under the most optimistic assumptions about cost reductions over the next 25 years .
