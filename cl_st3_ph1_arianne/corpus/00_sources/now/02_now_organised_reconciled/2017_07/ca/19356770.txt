An assessment report recently delivered to council is helping the district prioritize how it will manage a number of debris and flood-prone creeks . photo supplied <p> The District of North Vancouver is heeding staff 's advice when it comes to prioritizing how it will manage its debris and flood-prone creeks . <p> Fiona Dercole , the district 's section manager for public safety , noted the priorities in a report recently delivered to council . <p> These include : managing creeks with the highest likelihood of posing a risk to life , direct damage to buildings or infrastructure , or having the potential to affect a high number of properties . <p> With the help of BGC Engineering Inc. , staff recently completed a debris and geohazard risk assessment on 35 creeks across the district in order to provide these recommendations . <p> " Most of them are steep mountain creeks and are prone to what 's called debris flooding , which means there is a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ sediment . <p> These debris flood events occur primarily during the wet winter months and can be highly destructive to anything in their path , " Dercole explained . <p> The report specifically identified Thames , Kilmer and Gallant creeks as being the district 's highest priorities . Dercole said work was already underway to reduce risk . <p> " For example , at Kilmer Creek we have built a debris basin and some debris retention structures , " she said . <p> She added that Thames and Gallant creeks will have sediment basins installed in August . <p> Although risk to public safety and property were identified as top priorities , updating municipal infrastructure that may be impeding certain creeks , as well as overall feasibility , were also listed as factors to consider . <p> The district has been analyzing debris flood risks over the past 20 years and its last comprehensive assessment was more than 10 years ago . <p> According to Dercole , the district 's latest assessment incorporates new information on climate change 's impact on flooding and a more thorough analysis of storm drainage @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ system , " she said .
