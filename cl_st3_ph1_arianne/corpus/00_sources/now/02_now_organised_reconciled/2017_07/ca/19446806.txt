<p> Canada needs to stop draining its swamps in order to reduce flooding linked to climate change , warns a new report . <p> Wetlands , including swamps , marshes and bogs , are natural catch-basins that can quickly absorb much of the excess water dumped by increasingly fierce storms . <p> And research from Ontario 's University of Waterloo has found that leaving wetlands intact can reduce the financial costs of floods by up to 38 per cent . <p> " The wetlands are n't there for decoration -- they actually serve a purpose , " Blair Feltmate , one of the researchers , said in an interview . <p> Flood waters this spring near Vernon Creek in Kelowna , B.C. The authors drew on the experience of massive floods in southern Ontario triggered by Hurricane Hazel in 1954 , noting that such severe events are becoming more common with climate change . <h> Wetland absorber <p> Computer modeling , historic insurance data and recent damage estimates from floods in Ontario and Alberta showed the cost to repair major flood damage at the rural site at $8.9 million with wetlands absorbing the impact , compared to $12.4 million without . <p> At the Waterloo site , projected costs were $84.5 million with a wetland absorber compared to $135.6 million without . <p> The role of wetlands as catch-basins has long been recognized , but the study -- from the university 's Intact Centre on Climate Adaption , founded by a large insurance firm -- is among the first to quantify the financial benefits . <p> The research was paid for by Ontario 's Ministry of Natural Resources , which already supports wetland conservation , and Ducks Unlimited , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ waterfowl habitats . <p> ' People have to understand that the actual dynamics of the system have changed ...
