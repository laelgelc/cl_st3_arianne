<h> G20 summit must look beyond Trump <p> Canada 's Justin Trudeau and 19 other top political leaders will gather in Hamburg , Germany , on Friday and Saturday and try to figure out what to do about U.S. President Donald Trump . They should aim to keep the conversation going with the United States , but they should not let the president 's disruptive methods block co-ordination of economic policy among the leading nations . <p> This Group of 20 industrial nations , functioning with a shared agenda since 1999 , brings together public authorities whose economies account for about 85 per cent of the world 's economic output . Australia is in , New Zealand is not . South Africa is in , Nigeria is not . The European Union is in , the Association of Southeast Asian Nations is not . It does not pretend to be democratic or even especially nice . The gathering will be pointless if it turns into sterile finger-pointing and recrimination . It may be useful if it shows these decision-makers can speak hard truths to each other without rancour . At best , it may show the rest of the world can move trade and development forward , even when the U.S. president is preaching the gospel of " America First . " <p> Mr. Trump loves to be seen talking tough to foreigners and refusing to sacrifice American interests to the interests of other nations . But the leaders of U.S. industry know perfectly well that consumers around the world need the chance to enrich themselves through trade in order to buy the products of U.S. industry . <p> Mr. Trump imagines that Germany is a bad or misbehaving country because U.S. consumers ' purchases of German products greatly exceed German consumers ' purchases of U.S. products . The 19 who seek rational and humane management of mass migration should create the organizations they need to achieve that goal . <p> Outfits such as the G20 are most effective when they can achieve unanimous agreement .
