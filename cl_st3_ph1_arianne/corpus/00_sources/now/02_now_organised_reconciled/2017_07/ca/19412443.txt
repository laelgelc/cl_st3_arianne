<p> CALGARY -- Canadian lender Desjardins is considering no longer funding energy pipelines , a spokesman said on Saturday , citing concerns about the impact such projects may have on the environment . <p> Desjardins , the largest association of credit unions in North America , on Friday temporarily suspended lending for such projects and may make the decision permanent , spokesman Jacques Bouchard told Reuters by telephone . <p> He said the lender would make a final decision in September . <p> Dejardins , a backer of Kinder Morgan Canada Ltd 's high-profile expansion of its Trans Mountain pipeline , has been evaluating its policy for such lending for months , Bouchard said . <p> If it makes the decision permanent , that would likely mean Desjardins would not help finance other major Canadian pipelines projects , including TransCanada Corp 's Keystone XL and Energy East and Enbridge Inc 's Line 3 . <p> Such a move would follow that of Dutch lender ING Groep NV , which has a long-standing policy of not funding projects directly related to oil sands , and is the latest sign that pipelines @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ increasing pressure to back away . <p> Patrick Bonin , a campaigner with the environmental group Greenpeace , praised Desjardins for temporarily halting pipeline funding , but called on the lender to make it permanent and reconsider its $145 million commitment to Trans Mountain . <p> Desjardins is among 24 financial institutions that agreed to lend money to a subsidiary of Kinder Morgan Canada , majority owned by Kinder Morgan Inc of Houston , according to regulatory filings . <p> A coalition of more than 20 indigenous and environmental groups , including Greenpeace , in June called on 28 major banks to pull funding for Trans Mountain , citing the risk of pipeline spills and their potential contribution to climate change . <p> ING , which was targeted by the coalition , clarified it will not fund any of the major Canadian pipelines . <h> Almost Done !
