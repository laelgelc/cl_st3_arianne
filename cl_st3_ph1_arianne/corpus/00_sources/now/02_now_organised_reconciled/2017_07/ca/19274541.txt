<p> On a recent spring day , I climbed with my husband and two children down the steep side of a valley in an Ontario provincial park so we could reach the creek at the bottom . The children moaned , you can be sure . Mud , gross . Mosquitoes , blech . <p> Until we reached the bottom , that is . And there was the mundane magic of a Canadian landscape : a gorgeous , fast-flowing body of water , surrounded by trees and flowers and birds . A group of men sat on rocks in the middle , speaking a language I did n't recognize . A young couple had hauled their infant in its stroller down the trail and dipped the smiling baby into the water , a Canadian baptism . My children stopped complaining . We all sat with our feet in the creek , strangers united by this water . <p> This is one way to think of Canada : We are strangers united by water .
