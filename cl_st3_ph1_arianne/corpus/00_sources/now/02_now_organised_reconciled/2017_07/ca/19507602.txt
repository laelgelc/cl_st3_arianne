<h> Natural foreshore continues to give way to development <p> In light of the 2017 flood in the Central Okanagan , an existing report on shorelines of the area has taken on additional significance . <p> In 2011 , the Okanagan Lake Foreshore Inventory and Mapping ( FIM ) report revealed data of the Okanagan 's foreshore and a percentage of how much of the natural shoreline had been disturbed by development . <p> In the fall of 2016 , a second round of data was used to update the report . <p> All 290 kilometers of shoreline along Okanagan Lake was mapped and catalogued . <p> Following this year 's flood , this data will come in handy for governments and property owners to both recover from the flood and plan for the future . It also gives an ongoing measure of the environmental impacts of development over time . <p> The 2016 FIM data shows that 59% of the shoreline of Okanagan Lake has already been developed while 41% of the lakefront remains in its natural state . <p> Since the initial report in 2011 , 4.1 km @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ or permanently altered . <p> The following numbers show the change in percentages of disturbed shorelines between 2011 and 2016 . <p> City of Kelowna <p> Approximately 30% of the foreshore remains in its natural state , compared to 32.6% in the 2011 report . Last year , 43.1% of the shoreline was recorded as being altered , while the 2011 report found 41.8% had been disturbed . <p> Key changes to the shoreline included the removal of native vegetation ; construction of 165 retaining walls that altered 1.45 km of shoreline ; 164 new docks ; 9 new marinas ; more road access and general landscaping . Lakebed materials were also disturbed along 4.1 km of the shoreline . <p> " Each small change to the natural areas around the lake has a negative cumulative effect on the natural ecosystem to function and provide services such as clean drinking water , water infiltration , flood control , and critical habitat for fish and wildlife . " said Jason Schleppe , Senior Biologist with the FIM project . <p> " At the current rate of land development , the natural areas around the lake that are not located in parks or protected areas could be completely lost to development in 40 to 160 years .
