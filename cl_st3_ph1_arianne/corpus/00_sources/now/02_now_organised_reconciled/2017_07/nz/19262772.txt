<h> Book review : The Ice by Laline Paull <p> SUE GREEN <p> Last updated 05:00 02/07/2017 <p> Supplied <p> Author Laline Paull , <h> Relevant offers <p> The Ice <p> Laline Paull <p> 4th Estate , $33 <p> This second novel from the critically acclaimed author of The Bees hits the shelves as climate change 's dangerous impact on Arctic ice has caused the cancellation of a Canadian climate change study . As president Donald Trump pulls the United States out of the Paris climate treaty , The Ice is set in a future in which the sea ice in the resource-rich Arctic has melted and government and business power plays threaten its remaining wildlife . It 's not so much science fiction as prophecy , a time described by its marketers as " the day after tomorrow " . <p> The Bees , in which one worker bee was at odds with her colony , had a strong environmental message . Here , the message threatens to overwhelm the narrative , but Paull manages to pull it back in this tense and chilling -- literally and figuratively -- @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Paull skilfully moves her eco-thriller narrative back and forth in time . It opens with the discovery of a body , shot out of a calving Arctic glacier in front of cruise ship passengers hungry for sightings of the last remaining polar bears . It 's environmentalist Tom Harding , who died three years earlier when an ice cave collapsed . Sean Cawson , his business partner and best friend of 30 years , survived the collapse and the story is told from his point of view . <p> It moves between the " present " , with the recovery of Harding 's body and the repercussions of its discovery and the past , in which the pair of Arctic explorers come together in Sean 's Arctic business venture which is not all that it seems . Their friendship had been tested to breaking point by the incompatibility of Cawson 's pragmatism -- accepting climate change as a given and negotiating the best possible outcome with business -- and Harding 's hardline conviction that it is not too late to halt the ravages . <p> Cancelling their study because of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ realisation that the climate change 's impacts are happening now . Paull , well aware of this , has trouble resisting didacticism . Joe Kingsmith , Cawson 's sinister backer and mentor , tells Harding : " The ice is going going gone ... Time 's passed , there is no political will ... Much easier to make all you bleeding hearts feel guilty for flying on vacation than change a trade deal . " <p> Paull 's research is impressive -- for further reading there is a bibliography -- and The Ice is a timely wake-up call . It asks the big questions : can environmental and business ethics really be reconciled , or is Sean Cawson and those sharing this belief naive ? But it 's also a gritty and involving story of friendship and betrayal , political machinations and a tense buildup to a gripping conclusion . Some questions are answered , not least , was Tom Harding 's death really an accident ? But the bigger questions about the impact of climate change and how we will cope with it remain .
