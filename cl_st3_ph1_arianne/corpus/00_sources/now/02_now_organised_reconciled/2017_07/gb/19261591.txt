<h> YP Letters : Vivid memory of my D-Day school day <h> D Day veteran Ken Cooke . <p> I AM humbled by Ken Cooke 's memories of D-Day ( The Yorkshire Post , June 20 ) Mine are also vivid , but trivial by comparison . I was just 15 , and hurrying to school happily as my boyfriend had given me his Scout knife with the rabbit 's paw handle as a love token . <p> On arrival at school the head girl rang the bell earlier than usual and told us to go to the Assembly Hall , whatever our religion , as the headmistress had something important to say to us . So off we went , " single file and no talking " . <p> Miss Macrae took the podium , looking more than usually distant and preoccupied . " Girls , it is my duty to tell you that our troops , and those of our Allies , are at this moment landing on the shores of Normandy to free the peoples of Occupied Europe . I would ask you all to pray that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that June 6 , 1944 , would be forever remembered in the annals of our history along with 1066 , Agincourt and Trafalgar . In silence we returned to our classrooms and resumed our studies . <p> I pondered over Miss Macrae 's words , and spent every spare moment of that day carving the date on my desktop with the Scout knife hidden in my satchel , in huge letters and numbers . Then I inked it in carefully so it stood out proudly . I thought it looked wonderful ; but Miss Holdsworth , the ogre of the Upper IVths , did not when she discovered it at 4pm . " You are a disgrace to the school and will stay behind and clean it up as best you can , " she said . <p> So it was that on D-Day at 5.30pm I was still at school with a packet of " Monkey Brand " provided by the school cleaners .
