<p> New images show an enormous iceberg bigger than Norfolk and weighing a trillion-tons has broken off from an ice shelf in Antarctica . <p> The continent is currently in darkness during the Southern Hemisphere winter , allowing the Thermal Infrared Sensor ( TIRS ) on a NASA satellite to capture the 2,240 square mile iceberg that split off from the Antarctic Peninsula earlier this month . <p> The giant iceberg , called A-68 , broke away from Larsen C ice shelf -- the fourth largest in the world -- some time between July 10 and July 12 . <p> Larsen C is almost 19,000 square miles and the massive iceberg 's split from the continent had been predicted for years due to the rapid movement of the ice shelf . <p> But the gargantuan hunk of ice fell off reducing the ice shelf 's total mass by between nine and 12 per cent . <p> On July 12 an iceberg has ' fully detached ' from the Larsen C ice shelf ( Picture : EPA/NASA/USGS ) <p> The false-colour view shows the relative warmth or coolness across the region @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cryospheric scientist at NASA 's Goddard Space Flight Center and the University of Maryland , said : ' The back-and-forth movement of A-68 looks akin to manoeuvring a parallel-parked car out of a tight parking space -- like an Austin Powers three-point turn .
