<p> The scale of contemporary forced human displacement is unprecedented and difficult to comprehend . The United Nations High Commission for Refugees ( UNHCR ) has just reported staggering statistics . As of 2016 , 65.6 million people are displaced globally . This includes : <p> 40.3 million internally displaced persons ; <p> 22.5 million refugees ; and <p> 2.8 million people seeking asylum in industrial countries . <p> The number of people displaced by conflict or persecution has increased by 5.8 million since 2014 . <p> Beyond these categories are those forced to move by environmental factors . The International Displacement Monitoring Centre reports that an average of 22.5 million people have been displaced annually , since 2008 , by climate- and weather-related disasters . <p> These numbers will rise exponentially as the severity of climate change impacts increase . The continued rise of forced displacement points to the increasing difficulty the multilateral forum faces in tackling crisis situations in due time . <h> Maintaining international peace and security <p> The maintenance of international peace and security is the prime multilateral promise of the UN Charter . The continued relevance of this function remains undoubted given conflicts and wars remain a reality . <p> In its 2016 report on the work of the organisation , the secretary-general reminded the international community that : <p> ... the world will not be able to reach the Sustainable Development Goals in 2030 without greater efforts at working on conflict ... and an unambiguous lesson of the reporting period is that conflict prevention and mediation need to be brought back to the centre of all United Nations engagements . <p> UN member nations need to devote themselves to a renewal of this central goal , in the knowledge that conflict mitigation and prevention is key to stemming the dramatic escalation in forced human displacement . <h> Protecting and promoting @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ central multilateral promise of the UN . Through UN channels the international community has established a wide-ranging human rights framework and made undoubted progress in many areas . <p> However , people who experience forced displacement often fall into protection gaps , whether in refugee law or human rights law . Some of these gaps exist in the legal framework itself , for example in the lack of specialist human rights protection for climate migrants . <p> Arguably more significant , though , is the incapacity or unwillingness of the international community to prioritise the human rights of those forcibly displaced . Human rights violations continue to drive displacement , and those displaced remain more vulnerable to further human rights abuses when forced from their homes . <p> Countries must work together to tackle human rights violations as a driver of forced displacement . Through multilateral co-operation , countries can assist in building human capacity to manage conditions that drive displacement . <p> This would include co-operation to improve socioeconomic conditions for vulnerable people , making them less vulnerable to displacement . Yet around the world we are witnessing retreats towards nationalism and the rejection of the notion of the " common good " . <p> When the UN was established , we did not have the economic strength , the technological infrastructure , or the many regional arrangements we have today .
