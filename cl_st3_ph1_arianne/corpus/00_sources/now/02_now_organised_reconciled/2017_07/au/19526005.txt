<h> More Tas marine heatwaves likely : study <p> Marine heatwaves in Tasmanian waters are likely to become more commonplace in the coming decades , scientists warm . <p> Temperatures in waters off the state 's east coast peaked at nearly three degrees above average in the summer of 2015/16 . <p> The record spike in temperature was linked to the Tasmania 's first Pacific Oyster Mortality Syndrome ( POMS ) outbreak , which killed millions of oysters and caused a reported industry loss of nearly $6 million . <p> A paper published in the international journal Nature Communications says the heatwave was " almost certainly " due to climate change . <p> Researchers found it was caused by an growing flow of warm water travelling south on the East Australian Current . <p> " It 's the strongest we 've seen and we can expected them to be more frequent and more intense , " co-author and University of Tasmania associate professor Neil Holbrook said . <p> " The evidence shows that the frequency of extreme warming events in the ocean is increasing globally . <p> " In 2015 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ experienced a marine heatwave that was either the longest or most intense recorded since global satellite records began in 1982 . " <p> The heatwave in the Tasman Sea affected an area more than seven times the size of Tasmania for 251 days , the study found . <p> Near-surface waters are warming at nearly four-times the global average , making ocean off south-eastern Australia a global warming " hotspot " . <p> The study , titled the unprecedented 2015/16 Tasman Sea marine heatwave , looked closely at temperature data from September 2015 to May 2016 . <p> Abalone , lobster and salmon industries were also under stress during that period , the report noted . <p> " There were reports salmon were not as healthy or growing as much as normal , " Prof Hollbrook said . <p> In a bid to combat POMS , scientists in May released selectively bred POMS-resistant oysters into a waterway near Hobart .
