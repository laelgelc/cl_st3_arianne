<p> 20 million people are currently on the threshold of famine in seven countries in East Africa and Yemen . This major crisis has been driven by droughts , climate change , inflation and ongoing conflict in some countries . There are millions of people without water and sustainable livelihoods and the situation facing all the countries are critical . <p> In an interview with The eRecord , Caritas Australia CEO Paul O'Callaghan who has recently returned from Kenya , speaks about the Africa Emergency Appeal , the urgency to donate and raise life-saving funds and why this appeal is considered a major humanitarian disaster that requires urgent action . <p> The Africa Emergency Appeal , Mr O'Callaghan said , started in April when Chairman of Caritas Australia , Bishop Greg O'Kelly SJ AM DD wrote to all bishops in Australia and asked for their support in their diocese . <p> " That was when we were becoming aware of the 20 million people who were on the brink of starvation in the seven countries in East Africa and Yemen . <p> " We were conscious that there was very @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ United Nations has regarded this as the largest humanitarian disaster for the last four or five decades . <p> " So essentially what we have tried to do is let our diocese and schools around Australia know about the dire situations for so many people in this part of Africa , " Mr O'Callaghan said . <p> An emergency food distribution near the centre of Lowdar town in Northern Kenya .
