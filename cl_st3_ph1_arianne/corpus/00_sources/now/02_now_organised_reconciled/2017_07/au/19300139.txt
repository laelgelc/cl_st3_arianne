<h> Frogs jumped forward after dinosaur death <p> Frogs leaped to take advantage of the global catastrophe that killed off the dinosaurs , scientists have discovered . <p> New research shows that 88 per cent of frog species alive today owe their existence to the meteor impact that wiped the planet clean of most terrestrial life 66 million years ago . <p> Nearly nine out of 10 of the amphibian species are descended from just three lineages that survived the mass extinction . <p> Each of them jumped forward precisely at the junction of the Cretaceous and Paleogene periods - formerly known as the KT boundary - when the disaster happened . <p> The first survivors may have ridden out the meteor strike by burrowing underground , the scientists believe . " <p> US colleague Professor David Wake , from the University of California at Berkeley , said a key factor in early frog success was the way the creatures adapted to living in trees as flowering plants spread across the planet . <p> " Frogs started becoming arboreal , " he said . " It was the arboreality that led to the great radiation in South America in particular .
