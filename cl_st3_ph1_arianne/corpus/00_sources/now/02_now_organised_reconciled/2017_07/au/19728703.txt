<h> Trees at the centre of neighbours ' disputes have hidden benefits <h> Robin Powell <p> That old proverb about good fences making good neighbours needs an update to include trees . Trees are a major area of dispute between neighbours . When the Law Reform Commission looked at what sent neighbours into a fury with each other , trees were right up there with fences and barking dogs . In response , the NSW government introduced the Trees ( disputes between neighbours ) Act in 2007 , with an amendment in 2010 to deal with hedges that severely obstruct sunlight and views . <p> The Act deals with damage and imminent damage from trees , but what gets people riled about their neighbours ' trees most often are irritations not covered by the Act . She says she can understand why people might have fears about falling trees . " Whenever there is a storm , television cameras will find a tree fallen across a house or car , giving the idea that falling trees are a common occurrence , when they are anything but . " <p> Fakes explains that many neighbourly complaints about falling leaves and fears of falling trees stem from a lack of understanding about how trees grow and function . Trees moving in a storm are dissipating energy , not showing they are at risk of falling . Trees improve air quality , provide wildlife habitat , and increase the value of the neighbourhood : houses in leafy streets routinely fetch higher prices than those around the corner where all is bitumen and buildings . <p> Knowledge and appreciation of trees would reduce neighbourly disputes , says Fakes . People who buy a property that neighbours a big tree should remember its benefits , rather than fixating on its falling leaves . On the other side of the fence , she suggests tree owners have their trees inspected by an arborist every so often and have dead wood removed . Thinking about where trees are planted and anticipating their mature size may also minimise future conflicts with neighbours . Good fences make good neighbours , but so do well-loved trees .
