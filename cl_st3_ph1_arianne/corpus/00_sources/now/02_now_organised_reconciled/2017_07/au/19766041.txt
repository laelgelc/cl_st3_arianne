Their analysis reveals that if 9% of the ocean were to be covered in seaweed farms , the farmed seaweed could produce 12 gigatonnes per year of biodigested methane which could be burned as a substitute for natural gas . The seaweed growth involved would capture 19 gigatonnes of CO2 . A further 34 gigatonnes per year of CO2 could be taken from the atmosphere if the methane is burned to generate electricity and the CO2 generated captured and stored .
