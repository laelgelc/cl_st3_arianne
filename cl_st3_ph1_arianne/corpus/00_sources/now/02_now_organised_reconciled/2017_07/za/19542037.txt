<h> Toads will croak at this overuse <p> As a Western leopard toad volunteer for some years , I was distressed to learn about the low water level at Die Oog in Bergvliet , which is an area of critical importance to the toads for breeding . <p> A plea to the council to request that residents in the adjacent areas minimise their borehole water usage yielded this reply : " In terms of the leopard toad , not much can be done and it is likely that they might not breed for a year of two in certain areas where there is little or no water . This unfortunately is part of a natural process which is made worse by borehole water use . Hopefully this will right itself once we have good rains . " <p> The possibility of the critically endangered amphibians not breeding for a year or two is disastrous to the species as a whole . It is most certainly not a natural process -- climate change , excessive building , alien vegetation , busy roads , polluted and drying wetlands are the causes of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and more , are due to human pressure . <p> In this case , overuse of boreholes plays a significant role -- and one which can be monitored and controlled by the council . <p> Simply because people have well-points or boreholes installed at their homes or places of work does not give them licence to abuse the privilege to the detriment of their neighbours , the surrounding landscape and our ecosystems . At least we have the toads to warn us of the impending disaster . What about all the other wetland species : The birds , fish , otters , terrapins , insects and water plants ? Do they also not deserve some consideration in this drought ? <p> Our toads are much-loved by all the volunteers in the area , as well as residents and even some members of BKM who move them to safety while on patrol during breeding season . <p> People who have the privilege of living in the vicinity of our wetlands need to help keep them going and not simply think of their own gardens .
