<h> Agricultural emissions pose challenge for economic competitiveness <p> Agriculture makes the greatest contribution to Ireland 's emissions , despite the industry managing to cut releases to below the peak levels seen in 1990 , according to a report . <p> This in turn constrains the potential to meet climate change commitments , posing a direct challenge to the country 's economic competitiveness , according to ' Ireland 's Competitiveness Scorecard 2017 ' from the National Competitiveness Council ( NCC ) . <p> Between 1990 and 2015 , total emissions increased by 6.7% to 59,878 kilotons ( kt ) , with the agri-sector accounting for 33% of this , compared to the second-biggest contributors -- transport and energy -- which were both at 20% . Transport emissions have soared by 130% over the 25-year period . <p> Launched today , July 20 , the report identified climate change as a " very significant " challenge for Ireland -- both in terms of mitigating emissions and achieving national and international bindings targets , as well as in adapting to its effects . <p> Climate change was singled out alongside slower than @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ partners -- the UK and the US -- as a downside threat to competitiveness . <p> The NCC raised concerns that the country 's recent , strong economic performance was overstating competitiveness and " masking " weaknesses in the underlying drivers of future competitiveness -- particularly in terms of costs and productivity . <p> Highlighting the importance for Ireland to diversify its export opportunities , NCC Chairman , Prof. Peter Clinch , said : " The scale of the challenges which confront us have magnified over the past year since the Brexit referendum result . <p> It brings into sharp focus the need for Ireland to maintain and improve our competitiveness performance across a range of areas such as infrastructure , ease of starting a business , talent , tax and innovation . <p> " Only a renewed focus on competitiveness will enable us to achieve sustainable improvements in living standards that help us withstand external shocks and factors beyond our control " . <p> This comes after figures from the Central Statistics Office ( CSO ) showed Ireland 's growth in agri-food exports to the EU was 8.4% for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , growth in agri-related shipments to the UK was 2.2% . <p> In terms of overall exports for the month of May , the value of exports to Belgium alone was ? 1,298 million -- compared to ? 1,242 million for the UK.
