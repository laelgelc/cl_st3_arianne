<h> Paul Kershaw : B.C. budget will be unhealthy if medical care gets most of the new spending <p> In last year 's B.C. election , the NDP campaigned on a platform that promised to increase spending on medical care more than any other social policy . <p> But new research published in the Canadian Medical Association Journal cautions Premier John Horgan against this strategy . Do n't grow medical-care spending more than other social spending , the research warns , because health does n't start with health care . <p> That 's the finding from decades of science . It shows that health starts where we are born , grow , play , work and age . These conditions are shaped by the distribution of wealth , income , education , employment , housing , food , human impact on the environment and the many government policies that influence these factors . <p> While it may seem surprising , this means that health promotion is actually the domain of social , economic and environmental ministries , whereas medical ministries treat illness more than they prevent it . Accordingly , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " health-in-all-policies " perspective . <p> As a result , it 's worrisome that per-capita medical spending in Canada has increased 10 times faster since 1981 than social spending . That spending pattern is connected to lost opportunities to improve life expectancy and prevent avoidable mortality compared with a more even distribution between medical and social spending . This is the finding reported by Daniel Dutton and colleagues in a new study about the health impact of government spending . <p> When it comes to evaluating the 2018 B.C. budget , the research reveals we should judge it to be healthy only if new spending on programs like child care , housing and poverty reduction outpace medical spending . <p> A healthy budget is n't just about spending . A commitment to health in all policies also invites review of government decisions that shape major costs of living like housing . Average home prices used to cost four times median earnings for typical young British Columbians in 1976 , but now cost 14 times . Since many medical scientists consider climate change as the greatest risk to human health , it 's time to revisit why carbon pricing only generates 2.4 per cent of B.C. government revenue . <p> Tax more what we do n't want , so we can tax less what we do want . Find a better balance between investing in programs like child care and medical care . And modernize housing policies that limit suitable supply and increase harmful demand to drive up our major cost of living . That 's what population health research recommends for government budgets .
