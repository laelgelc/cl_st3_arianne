<p> Hundreds of residents in Cape Town , one of Africa 's largest economic and tourism hubs , are lining up nightly to stock up on water from natural springs ahead of a planned government shutdown of the city 's pipes following three years of crippling drought , Reuters reported . <p> Levels in the dams that supply the city 's drinking water , as well as those of communities nearby , fell to 25 percent of capacity this week , down from 38 percent a year ago and 100 percent four years ago . Its largest reservoir , the Theewaterskloof Dam , is down to 12.4 percent . <p> Cape Town began limiting water usage in early February to 13 gallons a day per person -- seven times less than the average U.S. citizen uses . The dams that supply Cape Town 's water resources are entirely fed by rainfall , which climate models project will become increasingly unpredictable in the coming decades . Cape Town 's water crisis also has been exacerbated by the region 's burgeoning population . <p> " I think there is going to be chaos , " resident Saleigh van der Schyff told Reuters as he waited in line to collect water at 11 PM .
