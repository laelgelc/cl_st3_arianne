Dotted across the grassy plain , as far as the eye could see , were the corpses of thousands upon thousands of saiga antelopes . All appeared to have fallen where they were feeding . <p> Some were mothers that had travelled to this remote wilderness for the annual calving season , while others were their offspring , just a few days old . Each had died in just a few hours from blood poisoning . In the 30C heat of a May day , the air around each of the rotting hulks was thick with flies . <p> The same grisly story has been replayed throughout Kazakhstan . MMEs are among the most extreme events of nature . They affect starfish , bats , coral reefs and sardines . They can push species to the brink of extinction , or throw a spanner into the complex web of life in an ecosystem . And according to some scientists , MMEs are on the rise and likely to become more common because of climate change . <p> Ochre sea stars . After 32 postmortems , they concluded the culprit was the bacterium Pasteurella multocida , which they believe normally lives harmlessly in the tonsils of some , if not all , of the antelopes .
