<h> Caribou hunting quotas make scapegoats out of northern First Nations : study <p> Newly published research argues Indigenous hunting did n't cause the collapse of once-mighty caribou herds in Canada 's North and harvest bans only force First Nations to shoulder the blame for problems they did n't create . <p> The paper in Science Advances concludes the more likely cause for the disastrous declines is the cumulative effects of mineral exploration . <p> " The scapegoat is the Indigenous person who depends on caribou for subsistence , " says the paper 's author Brenda Parlee , a biologist at the University of Alberta . " It 's easier to point the finger at somebody with a gun than it is to look at the more complicated problem of habitat disturbance . " <p> A biologist with the Northwest Territories says restrictions on a crucial northern food are necessary to prevent the Bathurst caribou from disappearing . Jan Adamczewski said some quotas were requested by First Nations governments . <p> The Bathurst herd ranges over a vast region of the central Arctic -- from Great Slave Lake to the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ mid-1980s , it has fallen to 20,000 today . <p> In response , the N.W.T. and some Indigenous co-management boards banned non-Indigenous hunting in 2010 . All harvesting was suspended in 2014 -- a heavy blow for Dene communities where store-bought food is expensive and caribou was on the dinner table several times weekly . <p> Caribou populations fluctuate naturally . Climate change and habitat damage are also considered to play a role . <p> But territorial biologists argue uncontrolled hunts using high-powered rifles , snowmobiles and GPS were , at the very least , preventing the herd from recovering . They have estimated Indigenous hunters were taking between 4,000 and 6,000 caribou , mostly cows , every year . <p> Parlee says those numbers are speculative . <p> Using figures for 1985-2000 , she found harvests closely tracked population levels of the four major herds . When caribou were abundant , people ate more . When they were n't , other resources were used . <p> In 1987 , consumption was 0.8 caribou per capita . By 2000 , that figure was down to 0.1 per person . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ rebound , hunting pressure was moderate . For every increase of 10,000 animals , consumption increased half a caribou per person . <p> " There 's been a lot of conjecture about how people must be over-hunting , but the data shows the absolute opposite , " says Parlee . " The amount of caribou being harvested today is a fraction of what it would have been 20 , 30 years ago . <p> " We 're distracting people from what is a more central or problematic issue . " <p> That issue , Parlee says , is habitat disturbance , mostly from mineral exploration . She and her co-authors found spikes in mineral staking in the 1990s and 2000s corresponded with falling caribou numbers . <p> Noise , dust , roads and seismic lines from exploration camps all linger and add up , she says . <p> " You have a very fragmented landscape as a result of exploration .
