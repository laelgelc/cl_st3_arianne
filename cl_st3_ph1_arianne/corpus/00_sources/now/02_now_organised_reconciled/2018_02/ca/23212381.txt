<p> The concept of a road connection to isolated communities on Labrador 's north coast is once again a source of conversation , and even those who support such an ambitious undertaking realize it would be a tough sell . <p> ' You 've got to have a dream . ' - Randy Edmunds <p> But for some , it 's not beyond the realm of possibilitity . <p> " You 've got to have a dream for a dream to come true , " said Torngat Mountains MHA Randy Edmunds , whose district encompasses all of northern Labrador and its roughly 3,200 residents . <p> Both from a cost and engineering standpoint , it 's a daunting thing to imagine . <p> Picture this : a main trunk from Happy Valley-Goose Bay in central Labrador to Nain in the north , with branch roads into Rigolet , Makkovik , Postville , Hopedale and Natuashish . <p> That 's about 860 kilometres of road in all , with an estimated cost of nearly $900 million for construction and pavement -- likely much more -- plus the cost of winter snowclearing @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Here is one scenario for a road network to Labrador 's north coast .
