<h> City aims to make Robin Hood Bay less stinky <p> A City of St. John 's garbage truck enters the Robin Hood Bay landfill site on Friday morning . - Joe Gibbons <h> A $3-million landfill project should reduce smells affecting residents in the east end <p> East end resident Peter Whittle is hopeful a new measure to reduce the vomit-inducing odours at the Robin Hood Bay dump will be successful . <p> The City of St. John 's is spending $3 million to try to deal with the stink emanating from the Robin Hood Bay dump . <p> Whittle says the smell can be horrid if the conditions are right . He told a recent story of the smell being too much for one resident . <p> " Thursday evening was probably the worst odour event I 've smelled in a long time , " said Whittle . <p> " I was at the Ultramar on Newfoundland Drive at 5:30 and there was a lady on the other side who actually threw up . The fill will prevent gases from seeping out into the air and allow them to be collected and burnt off , converting harmful methane into less harmful carbon dioxide . <p> Public Works lead Coun. Ian Froude says on top of addressing some of the more pungent concerns , the changes will lessen the greenhouse gas emissions wafting from the area . <p> " A lot of methane is produced through decomposition . Methane is among the most potent greenhouse gases , 21 times more potent than carbon dioxide , which is often more talked about when it comes to climate change , " he said . <p> Froude says it 's hard to quantify how much gas will be prevented from escaping , but 34 hectares of cover added over two years should be substantial . <p> Whittle says he 's cautiously optimistic about the plan . <p> " I 'm glad to see that they 're working on it .
