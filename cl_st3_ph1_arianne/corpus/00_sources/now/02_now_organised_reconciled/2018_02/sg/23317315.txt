<h> Singaporean wins Bafta award <p> Producer Low Ser En and British director Paloma Baeza ( both above ) with their awards for the film Poles Apart at Royal Albert Hall in London on Sunday.PHOTOS : AGENCE FRANCE-PRESSE , CARTOONS UNDERGROUND <p> Producer Low Ser En and British director Paloma Baeza with their awards for the film Poles Apart ( above ) at Royal Albert Hall in London on Sunday.PHOTOS : AGENCE FRANCE-PRESSE , CARTOONS UNDERGROUND <p> Singaporean producer Low Ser En has won a Bafta award for Best British Short Animation for her stop-motion short Poles Apart . <p> The 12-minute film , about a polar bear and a grizzly bear who are brought together by their changing habitats , was the graduation project of a team of about 10 students from Britain 's National Film and Television School ( NFTS ) , which included people " from four continents " , including an American and a Briton , she said . <p> Bafta is an independent charity that supports , develops and promotes film , television and games in Britain . <p> Bafta awards are given every year @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ widely seen as the British equivalent of the Oscars in Hollywood . <p> Poles Apart beat two other films : Have Heart by Will Anderson , about a looping gif that has an existential crisis , and Mamoon by Ben Steer , which tells the story of a mother forced to leave her home with her child using projected animation on polystyrene film sets . <p> Low , 27 , who returned home to Singapore on Tuesday night after her win at Royal Albert Hall in London on Sunday , told The Straits Times that the team of about 10 was " very surprised " by the win . <p> " It was very incredible , " said the graduate of the NFTS . <p> " We were very surprised and very happy about it . It was quite a huge moment for us , it came after a year of festival runs . " <p> She received a statuette alongside her British director Paloma Baeza . It now sits in her Housing Board flat in Kallang . <p> She had both a creative role and a business role as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the script , as well as coming up with the budget and schedule for the production and looking through the edits . <p> " We got the inspiration from an article we read online about a phenomenon where the polar bears are moving south and grizzly bears are moving north because of climate change , " she said . <p> " It 's a climate refugee story . In a way , it 's related to Singapore because with global warming , our weather is becoming more and more unpredictable . " <p> They took 15 months to complete the film as stop-motion is a painstaking process involving capturing each scene with animated figures . <p> The film , which won Best British Animation at the Edinburgh International Film Festival last year , as well as an Annie Award for Best Student Film earlier this month , was shown at two festival screenings in Singapore last year . <p> It is currently not available in Singapore , but there are plans to make it available on video platform Vimeo . <p> Low , who has done production work since 2009 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Entertainment and the Info-communications Media Development Authority of Singapore to study at the NFTS . <p> She graduated in February last year with a Master of Arts degree in producing and now works with mm2 Entertainment . <p> Low , who is not married , shared that she has just completed production for an upcoming local movie called Zombiepura , a live-action zombie apocalypse movie set in an army camp in Singapore that stars Alaric Tay and Benjamin Heng . <p> It is set to air in cinemas in the third or fourth quarter of this year , she said . <p> A version of this article appeared in the print edition of The Straits Times on February 22 , 2018 , with the headline ' Singaporean wins Bafta award ' .
