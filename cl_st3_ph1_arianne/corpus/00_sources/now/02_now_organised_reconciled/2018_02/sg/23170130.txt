<h> The surplus electricity from solar and wind power gives hydrogen the chance to replace oil and gas . <h> Share this story <p> Hydrogen is the least talked-about renewable energy but has the greatest potential to replace fossil fuels , both to heat homes and to provide fuel for road transport . <p> The possibility of using hydrogen has been known about for generations , but only in the last two years has it become both practical and financially viable to see it as a large-scale competitor to both gas and oil . Networks of hydrogen filling stations are now being opened in Europe and parts of the US . <p> Batteries for road transport have attracted most of the recent publicity around renewables and have become the focus of many governments with targets for switching away from petrol and diesel , particularly in cities with air pollution problems . <p> But hydrogen has even greater potential because its only emissions are pure water and warm air . <h> Storage potential <p> What has made hydrogen so attractive is that it can use surplus renewable electricity from wind and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is a process of passing an electric current through water and converting it into oxygen and hydrogen . The large-scale introduction of both wind and solar power has meant that increasingly , when the wind blows and the sun shines and demand for electricity is low , there is no use for the power . <p> However , if this surplus power is diverted to produce hydrogen that can be stored , then the cost of the valuable gas produced is low .
