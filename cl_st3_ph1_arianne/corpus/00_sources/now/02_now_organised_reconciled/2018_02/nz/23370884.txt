<p> What 's thought to have been one of the landing sites of New Zealand 's first settlers , about 800 years ago , has since been hit by at least four major earthquakes and three tsunamis . <p> The Wairau Bar , a windswept wedge of grass and gravel off the Marlborough coastline , has been one of New Zealand 's most important archaeological sites since relics uncovered there were found to have come from what were likely our first human inhabitants . <p> Successive studies have put their arrival at the bar , which at that point would have been a scrub-covered island , at some point between 1288 and 1300 . <p> Now , two Victoria University archaeologists have uncovered evidence of earthquakes and tsunamis at the site over past centuries - some which may have killed some of the people once living there . <p> Dr Bruce McFadgen and Associate Professor Peter Adds ' study , just published in the Journal of the Royal Society of New Zealand , also highlighted concerns that sea level rise from climate change could threaten secrets yet to be uncovered @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to monitor current and future site degradation from natural processes . <p> The authors said there had been at least four earthquakes at the bar large enough to have changed the landscape -- transforming the site from an island to part of a boulder bank -- as well as the surrounding area . <p> The site had also been ravaged by three tsunamis -- including one 1855 event that was large enough to have knocked down several buildings , stranded fish and lowered the land and nearby lagoons by one to two metres . <p> Roger Duff ( left ) and Jim Eyles dig at the Wairau Bar site in the 1940s .
