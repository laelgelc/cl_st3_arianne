<h> Are Jellyfish Chips the Future of Snack Foods ? <p> It sounds as unlikely as can be , but researchers in Denmark have created a crispy new snack food based off jellyfish - and the key chemical used to turn the otherwise slimy jellyfish crunchy is ethanol . <p> Jellyfish have been a staple in Asian cuisine for centuries , but remain an oddity to the western palate . Traditionally , the bell or body of a jellyfish is marinated in salt and potassium alum for several weeks to produce a crunchy , almost pickle texture . <p> Now , the Danish research team has developed a new technique that produces the same results in only a few days . <p> " Using ethanol , we have created jellyfish chips that have a crispy texture and could be of potential gastronomic interest , " said Mathias P. Clausen , a postdoctoral fellow at University of Southern Denmark in Odense , Denmark . <p> The research team further investigated how the long fibrous filaments in the gelatinous jellyfish bell are transformed during the curing process to produce the crunchy texture @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the jellyfish , " Clausen said . " We are still not completely sure which structures we are visualizing . " <p> With overfishing and climate change having a devastating effect on traditional fish stocks , jellyfish have exploded in numbers in recent years . Turning them into crispy snacks could be the way forward for Western consumers . Additionally , jellyfish are rich in vitamin B12 , magnesium , phosphorus , iron and selenium , making them potentially quite a low-calorie , healthy snack .
