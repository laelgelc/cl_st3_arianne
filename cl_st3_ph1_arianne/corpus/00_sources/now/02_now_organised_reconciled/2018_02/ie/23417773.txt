<h> How activist investors use the power of their shareholdings <p> In the buyout boom of the 1980s activist investors were known as corporate raiders . Now , these individuals or groups of shareholders have undergone a dramatic rebranding of late . Rising in number in recent years , it seems that no sector or region is safe from the activist campaign . <p> The aim of an activist shareholder is to purchase large numbers of a public company 's shares and attempt to disrupt the makeup of the board to counterbalance poor management in firms whose balance sheets have become skewed . <p> According to a Lazard report , activist shareholders deployed over $45bn in new campaigns in 2017 , nearly double the amount for all of 2016 . Another report by McKinsey &amp; Company states that working together , ' active activists ' could mobilise trillions of dollars to challenge the strategies and performance of publicly traded companies . <p> More sophisticated approach <p> A steady increase in public proxy fights added to the volatile political environment means that activists are targeting more and bigger companies .
