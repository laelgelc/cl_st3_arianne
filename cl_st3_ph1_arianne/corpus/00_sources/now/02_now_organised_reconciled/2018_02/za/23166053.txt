<h> Two Congos set joint approach for peatland help <p> KINSHASA - The Democratic Republic of Congo and the Republic of Congo are to jointly seek funds to help them protect their tropical peatlands , a major factor in the issue of climate change . <p> In a joint statement seen by AFP on Monday , the two central African neighbours said they hoped to gain " guarantees , in the form of funding ... with the aim of sufficiently offsetting " sacrifices made by their populations if the peatlands are left untouched . <p> The two countries ' environment ministers , meeting in the DRC capital of Kinshasa last weekend , agreed to set up a " joint peatland management mechanism , " the statement added . <p> The swampy peatlands in the Congo Basin hold a massive stock of carbon -- the result of billions of years of vegetal growth and decay . <p> Draining these peatlands for agriculture would release huge amounts of planet-warming greenhouse gases , scientists have warned . <p> Last month , scientists co-led by Simon Lewis , a professor at the University of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ covered 145,500 square kilometres ( 56,000 square miles ) , an area the size of England . <p> In addition to being a biodiversity hotspot , the peatlands contain previously unknown carbon stocks equivalent to three years ' worth of global carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) emissions , they found . <p> Most climate change is caused by burning oil , gas and coal to power our economies , but a tenth of global emissions come from land use , mainly deforestation and agriculture . <p> In Southeast Asia , vast expanses of peatland have been drained and stripped of their vegetation to make way for commercial crops , especially palm oil . <p> That process releases CO2 and nitrous oxide -- another potent greenhouse gas -- and creates health-threatening pollution when the land is burned . <p> International funding to prevent carbon emissions through tropical deforestation is already well-established . However , there is no scheme yet on a similar scale that is specifically framed for tropical peatlands.
