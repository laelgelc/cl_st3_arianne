Picture : Shutterstock <p> In the past decade , the banking industry has been a central focus of attention for regulators , academics and the general public . The 2007-08 financial crisis led to new regulations and institutions to keep things in check . <p> In contrast , the issues of the pension industry have tended to be swept deep under the carpet . Much the same way that people think about retirement saving -- that it can wait for another day -- we are now beginning to see how much of a mistake this is . <p> The pension industry is already in a deep financial crisis and could well be the trigger for another global financial and economic meltdown . This has largely been overlooked . Many pension funds moved away from equity investments , increased the share of bonds in their portfolios and missed out on high returns . For example UK pension funds decreased their portfolios ' equity share from 61% in 2006 to 29% in 2017 and increased the bond share from 28% to 56% over the same period . <p> The net effect is that the pension industries in many countries are in a bad way . According to a Citibank report from 2016 , the 20 largest OECD countries alone have a US$78 trillion shortfall in funding pay-as-you-go and defined benefit public pensions ' obligations . This shortfall is far from trivial . It is equivalent to about 1.8 times the value of these countries ' collective national debt . <p> Private pensions are not any more sound .
