<p> Six young scientists from around Australia impressed delegates at the Australian Dairy Conference as they presented the results of their research . <p> The six were taking part in the DOW Agrosciences Young Dairy Scientists ' Award , which aims to encourage excellence in communication among young researchers . <p> Reflecting the increasingly international nature of scientific research , four of the finalists were completing PhDs in Australia after having completed undergraduate studies in other countries , while another had completed a Masters in the Netherlands , after having done undergraduate studies in Australia . <p> A panel of judges assessed finalists on their verbal presentation at the conference , an article they had prepared for publication in the Australian Dairyfarmer magazine and a visual display at the conference . <p> The overall winner was Tasmanian scientist Alison Hall , who presented her research about farmers using pasture measurement and monitoring technology . <p> Other finalists were : <p> Min Wang - who presented her research on decrypting the bovine genome . <p> Pablo Alvarez - who presented his research into the cost-effectiveness of using NOP supplement to reduce @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ presented his research about the effect of climate change on pasture yields . <p> Beth Scott - who presented her research about the cause of stillbirths in cows . <p> Paula Giraldo - who presented her research assessing the safety of genetically modified ryegrass . <p> The young scientists ' presentations were introduced by meteorologist and weather presenter Jane Bunn , who talked about her passion for science and communication .
