<h> NZ scientists to study Antarctic whale poo <p> A New Zealand-led expedition is headed to Antarctica to carry out research in the world 's largest marine reserve . <p> Updated Updated 7 hours ago <p> How whale poo affects the Antarctic eco-system will be one of many studies New Zealand scientists hope to undertake during an expedition to the world 's largest marine reserve . <p> The multidisciplinary science expedition by the National Institute of Water and Atmospheric Research was set to leave New Zealand on Thursday for a week-long journey to the Ross Sea shelf break , about 3400km south of Wellington . <p> A focus will be mapping the ocean floor of the Ross Sea Marine Protected Area as it is affected by global climate change , NIWA Oceanographer Dr Mike Williams says . <p> " The highest priority is the physical oceanography work where we 'll be looking at the outflow of the cold dense water from the Ross Sea into the global ocean , " he said . <p> This will include travelling into new areas to collect data , Dr Wiliams said . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ where we basically do n't have any observations about - the more northern areas - we do n't even know what the temperature is , " he said . <p> " That kind of information is really really useful in understanding how it will all change . " <p> The 23 scientists onboard will also look at the atmosphere , biology and marine ecosystems of the area . <p> The job of seeing how whale poo affects eco systems has fallen to Auckland University whale biologist Rochelle Constantine , with preliminary research suggesting plankton rely on it for their iron source . <p> This week 's expedition is the Tangaroa 's 12 Antarctic voyage . <p> Voyage leader Dr David Bowden says sea ice conditions look good but they are expecting some choppiness over the 7500km six-week journey , which may limit data collection . <p> The Ross Sea Marine Protected area came into effect in December last year and is subject to three-yearly data collection to ensure it meets a number of aims . <p> These include protecting sea life , measuring the effect of climate change and protecting the area from toothfish fishing .
