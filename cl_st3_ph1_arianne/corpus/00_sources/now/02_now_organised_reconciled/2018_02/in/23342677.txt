<p> Climate change could hurt farmers ' income by up to 20-25% in the medium term , according to the Indian government 's latest annual economic survey . Extreme weather events , temperature rise and lower rainfall all threaten to derail the Indian government 's agenda of doubling farmers ' income across the country . <p> There are ways to counter this , however . New technology and better farm management can be deployed to improve irrigration systems . And , to ensure long-term impact , it is also important that the Indian government invests in agricultural research . <p> Agriculture is central to the Indian economy . It accounts for 50% of the country 's employment and 18% of its GDP . And in India , about 80% of farmers are smallholders -- which is generally considered to mean they have two hectares or less of land . The main crops are wheat , maize , rice , millets , pulses , sugarcane and oil-seeds . <p> Indian farmers must contend with a number of risks , including adverse weather , competition from larger landowners or food corporations and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ social , market and economic pressures are huge , often leading to considerable distress . India is home to nearly 25% of the world 's hungry population and has an alarming rate of farmer suicide ( around 60,000 cases in the past three decades ) . <p> So the effects of fluctuations in weather , temperature and rainfall on agricultural productivity will have a significant impact on the wider population . <p> Climate change <p> Extensive meteorological data in the recent government report shows that average temperatures are rising across the country and annual rainfall is declining . It also shows a rise in the number of days with extremely high temperatures and a corresponding decline in the number of days with low temperatures . <p> Extreme temperature shocks , when a district is significantly hotter than usual , results in a 4.7% decline in agricultural yields . Similarly , when it rains significantly less than usual there is a 12.8% decline . <p> Areas lacking in irrigation are worst affected by these extreme weather conditions . The report estimated that extreme temperature shocks will reduce farmers ' incomes by 4.3% and extreme rainfall shocks will reduce incomes by 13.7% . In a year where temperatures are 1 degree Celsius higher , farmers ' incomes would fall by 6.2% in unirrigated districts . Similarly , in a year when rainfall levels were 100mm less than average , farmers ' incomes would fall by 15% . <p> Temperatures in India are likely to rise by 3 to 4 degree Celsius by the end of the 21st century . It follows that in the absence of any adaptation by farmers , farm incomes will be lower by 20 to 25% on average in the coming years , especially in the unirrigated areas . <p> There are five important ways to mitigate against this that the Indian government could start investing in now . <p> 1 .
