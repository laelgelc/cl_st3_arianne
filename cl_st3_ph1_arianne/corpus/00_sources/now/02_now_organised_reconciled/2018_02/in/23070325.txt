<h> The Undoable Targets Of A Positive Budget <p> THE general expectation from the Union budget was about addressing crisis in agriculture , education , health , infrastructure and jobs . Union Finance Minister Arun Jaitley also mentioned right in the beginning that the budget aimed at providing " ease of living " to the masses including the poor . <p> It seems that the budget has covered more or less all these areas , and appears to be positive . There is no doubt that the budget has come out with several new ideas . However , there are a few questions that need clear answers ( 1 ) are the targets given in the budget doable ? ; ( 2 ) how will these be implemented ? ; and ( 3 ) are the funds allocated to different heads enough to meet their implementation ? <p> Doubling farm incomes <p> To start with some of the major targets are clearly not doable . For example , doubling farm incomes in five years is unlikely to be achieved . Prices of inputs are rising and the selling prices frequently fall due to gluts . Crop insurance is spread very thinly . <p> Again , covering 500 million people under healthcare ( little less than half the total population in India ) also does not seem to be doable . Studies have shown that even the present social security programmes need a lot of improvement in its implementation . The budget has not addressed this aspect . Uplift of SC/ST population has been a goal frequently repeated by all governments in India in the past . However , this section of the Indian population has remained more or less excluded from the gains of development through generations . It is now realised that SC/ST population is different from the poor in general . The government has not recognised this adequately in the past and in this budget . They need a different strategy . <p> Finally , as regard jobs , which is an urgent priority of the economy , the government assumes that all beneficiaries of MUDRA scheme are employed which is far from the truth .
