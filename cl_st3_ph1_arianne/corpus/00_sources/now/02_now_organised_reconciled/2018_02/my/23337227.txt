This was brought upon by the rise in sea temperatures from global warming and the coinciding El Nino effect . <p> Prompted by the realisation that we could lose all our reefs , and that the loss would be permanent , IYOR 1997 was created . <p> By IYOR 2008 , global warming did not show any signs of slowing down . As a result , coral bleaching events became more frequent . <p> The Global Coral Reef Monitoring Network ( GCRMN ) reported as much as 19 per cent of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cent in " critical state " in 2008 . <p> At the same time , new threats like ocean acidification , human induced coastal pollution and storm intensification due to global warming were thrust into the limelight . <p> That brings us to today : 2018 . The decade since 2008 has seen global warming , ocean acidification and coastal pollution further intensify . 2016 was the warmest year ever to be recorded ; followed closely by 2017 , 2015 and 2014 . <p> And this does n't come without consequences . Two thirds of the Great Barrier Reef , the largest coral reef system in the world , was severely bleached last year . It is still unclear whether these affected areas will ever recover . <p> Does this then make worldwide reef ecosystem loss imminent ? Or will reefs rise again in an age where global temperature records fall every year ? <p> Wishlist <p> I refuse to be crippled by news of doom and gloom , instead have prepared a list of five suggestions that can better protect reef ecosystems in Malaysia . A government that fulfils international commitments <p> The Malaysian government is party to several international conventions related to the environment . While this piece does not attempt to explain every single one of them , I intend to highlight the importance of fulfilling these commitments . <p> Often times , conventions are formed to tackle a common global problem like climate change ( which includes global warming ) and decline in species diversity . <p> However , since countries have respective sovereignties , conventions allow for leaders to act locally while remain consistent with the rest of the world . <p> For example , in response to climate change , a concerted effort to reduce carbon dioxide emissions can lower global sea temperatures and lead to healthy reefs . <p> 2 . Perhaps the Department of Marine Parks Malaysia ( DMPM ) should look into other equally threatened but lesser known marine ecosystems like sea grass beds , tidal pools and open sea as potential marine parks . I welcome the proposed Sibu-Tinggi Dugong Sanctuary as a perfect example of this . <p> 3 . The creation of a special federal agency for marine conservation <p> At the federal level , all agencies that carry out conservation efforts are under the purview of the Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment ( NRE ) ; save for one . <p> The Department of Fisheries , which is empowered by the Fisheries Act 1985 to safeguard our marine resources , is under the Ministry of Agriculture and Agro-based Industries ( MoA ) . <p> I argue that there is a misalignment in priorities when the agency roles are structured in this manner . <p> On one hand we have NRE which strives to manage and conserve , while on the other , we have MoA , tasked to improve yield . <p> Additionally @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ marine parks , the same level of protection is not afforded outside these boundaries . <p> This is also true for large reef fish species like sharks and bumphead parrotfish . Such a situation is disconcerting since coral reefs are known to be nurseries of open sea fish while marine predators regulate the health of a marine ecosystem . <p> The creation of a centralised agency for marine conservation , empowered by a new act is able to solve both these problems . <p> This agency can follow the mould of Perhilitan where critically endangered marine species could be placed under federal jurisdiction while the habitats remain under the state . <p> 4 . The fishing industry to play its role in reef conservation <p> In 2015 , I interviewed about 100 artisanal fishermen from Penang island as part of my university honours thesis . They lamented the reducing catch and cited the increased presence of trawlers as the main threat to their livelihoods . <p> Awareness of this needs to be improved alongside investment on habitat restoration if the industry wants to be sustainable . <p> 5 .
