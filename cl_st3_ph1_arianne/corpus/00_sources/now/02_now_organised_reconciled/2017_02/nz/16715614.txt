<h> Fossilised pollen helps GNS scientists study climate change <p> Joe Prebble of GNS Science has used fossilised pollen to look at climate change over the last 34 million years . <p> Fossilised tree pollen is giving us a better understanding of climate change over the last 34 million years . <p> More than 2000 samples of fossilised tree pollen held at GNS Science in Lower Hutt have been looked at to get a picture of the climate over the past 34 million years , covering a period when New Zealand was six to eight degrees Celsius warmer than today . <p> The research , published this month in Earth and Planetary Science Letters , is based on fossilised tree pollen that scientists have extracted from rocks around New Zealand over the last 60 years . <p> By comparing the pollen found in the rocks to the modern distribution of closely-related trees , the scientists were able to reconstruct changes in air temperature and rainfall . <p> The scientists used pollen from about 75 plant species -- mostly trees -- and their distribution provides data on temperature and rainfall @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ trees are today only found in New Caledonia , New Guinea and Australia . <p> The pollen grains are microscopic and generally need to be magnified several hundred times for meaningful study by scientists . <p> " Other than computer climate models , these types of fossil climate records are the only way we have to understand how climate responded to elevated carbon dioxide concentrations in the past , and might respond in the future , " said palynologist Joe Prebble of GNS Science , the lead author of the study .
