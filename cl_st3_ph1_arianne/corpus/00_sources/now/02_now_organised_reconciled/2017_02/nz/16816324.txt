<h> Higher temperatures mean more worm risks <p> Parasites in sheep will become more of a problem for New Zealand farmers as temperatures rise with climate change , says AgResearch principal scientist John McEwan . <p> And market demand is rising for products which do not contain anthelmintic , he says . This is likely to increase the importance of breeding sheep for parasite resistance , the science for which has been well proven over 20-30 years . <p> " Pressure is rising in our overseas markets to reduce antibiotic use in farmed animals , because antibiotic resistance is affecting the use of antibiotics in humans . This is also having a spin-off on anthelmintic use . Some European countries have restricted the use of anthelmintics -- to be by prescription only -- as a response to anthelmintic resistance in worms . <p> " We sell into those markets . There is a lot of pressure in recording the use of antibiotics and minimising their use .
