<h> Columnists <h> So Aunty , So What ? <h> How I became a clever girl <p> A certain yellow-bordered magazine played a big part in this writer 's education . <p> THE notice came in December . And tears threatened because it was yet another reminder of Dad and his continuing influence on my life , even after his death last August . <p> The scrap of paper that could play havoc on my emotions was the notice to renew his subscription to the National Geographic magazine . <p> This was Dad 's favourite reading material , along with Reader 's Digest and the newspapers . <p> Looking back , it 's easy to see how our family 's reading habit was formed : Dad made sure he filled our home with books , magazines , encyclopaedias and special editions like Time-Life 's Great Ages of Man and its Science and Nature Libraries , which I devoured . <p> Our first issue of National Geographic was the January 1971 edition . Dad kept every copy of the magazine and the collection is now in my house . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ my siblings and I realised it as kids . <p> We took it for granted that every month , we could look forward to reading the latest discoveries and findings in science , archaeology , palaeontology , medicine , wildlife conservation , space exploration , climate change -- you name it , National Geographic had it . <p> Every story came beautifully illustrated with graphics , maps and those amazing photographs which National Geographic is famous for . Through text and pictures , we vicariously visited many countries and far-flung places . And that 's how my mind was opened up and I became a clever girl . <p> By clever , I mean my interest in and curiosity about a lot of things was piqued and my general knowledge expanded . <p> I trusted what National Geographic had to teach me so much , it led me to challenge my history teacher in secondary school . <p> That was the year we studied the voyages of Captain James Cook . <p> My school history book and teacher stated that 67 ? 15 'S was the most extreme southern @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ it circumnavigated the Antarctica . <p> But I knew differently : in National Geographic 's September 1971 issue , an article titled , Captain Cook : the Man who mapped the Pacific , showed he reached 71 ? 10 'S on his second voyage in 1774 . <p> And I confidently stated as much in my homework and got marked wrong ! <p> I was so incensed and convinced I was right because National Geographic said so , I wrote to the magazine at its Washington D.C. address explaining my predicament . <p> It took a while -- it was snail mail in those days -- and to my delight , a letter dated June 12 , 1975 , arrived from Lesley B. Rogers of the Research Department . <p> The typewritten letter assured me I was right , citing the most authoritative source , Cook himself , from his journal entries . <p> I showed the letter to my teacher but she had never heard of the National Geographic Society or the magazine !
