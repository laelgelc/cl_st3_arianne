natural habitats , like coral reefs and sandy beaches , are generally well studied . <p> But the same can not be said for rocky shores , which can be found in places such as the Labrador Nature Reserve and Pulau Ubin . <p> But last month , Professor Stephen Hawkins , an expert on rocky shores from the University of Southampton in Britain , was in Singapore to speak about these habitats , in hopes of spurring greater scientific interest in them . <p> Formed when waves erode rocks along the shore , rocky shores are a landscape of bedrock @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ such as crabs like to hide . in town under the National University of Singapore ( NUS ) Society Professorship programme . <p> However , though rocky shores are widely studied in temperate regions , there is scant data on them and how they function in the tropics , he added . <p> This is not surprising , considering the multitude of other marine habitats in this part of the world , said Prof Hawkins . <p> The great diversity and " nice and warm " waters here make the study of other habitats such as coral reefs and mangroves attractive options , he said . <p> But up north , where waters are cold , researchers like Prof composition of barnacle species on the rocky shores .
