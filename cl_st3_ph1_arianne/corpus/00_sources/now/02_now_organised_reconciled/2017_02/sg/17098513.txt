<h> Agricultural output must go up by 50% to feed growing population <p> WASHINGTON * The world will have trouble feeding itself in decades to come unless countries undertake " major transformations " in the way they grow and distribute food , the United Nations said in a report that paints a bleak and hungry future . <p> Due to a growing global population , agricultural output will need to increase by 50 per cent , the UN Food and Agriculture Organisation ( FAO ) warned in The Future of Food and Agriculture : Trends and Challenges report released on Wednesday . <p> Experts estimate that the world will have 10 billion mouths to feed in 2050 , against 7.3 billion today . <p> Beyond just a growing population , diets are also changing from a cereal-heavy one to one that includes more meat , which requires significantly more grains and water . <p> For example , the average Chinese person went from consuming 13kg of meat per year in 1982 to 63kg of meat in 2016 . <p> Groundwater sources are also being depleted rapidly . Likewise , climate change is altering weather and precipitation patterns , and in decades to come , is expected to shrink agricultural yields , especially in already-vulnerable regions such as the Sahel in Africa . <p> The UN estimates that 600 million people will be undernourished in 2030 , and overall global food security will be " in jeopardy " unless there is a concerted effort to change eating habits and mitigate the worst effects of climate change . <p> Avoiding the report 's dire forecasts is possible , if pricey , the report 's lead author told Foreign Policy . Dr Lorenzo Bellu , senior economist at the FAO , said additional annual investments of US$265 billion ( S$372 billion ) would be needed till 2030 to keep the hungry fed . <p> That could include investments in new agricultural technology to improve crop yields , researching genetically modified organisms , new distribution methods , and more investments in humanitarian aid @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ habits , such as intensive agriculture , over-consumption of meats , and little practical action to tackle the causes of climate change .
