<h> ACT is nation leader in progressing energy efficiency <h> Shane Rattenbury <p> In recent months the federal Coalition government has been going to great -- and often laughable -- lengths to try and denigrate renewable energy , and to champion coal ( or ' clean ' coal ) as the energy source of the future . In case their fiction has n't already been debunked enough -- by the mountains of scientific and economic evidence -- the recent experience of the ACT provides the final nail in the coffin . <p> Far from being to blame for electricity supply problems during the recent NSW/ACT heatwave , renewable electricity generation was actually the quiet achiever . It reduced demand spikes that threatened the grid , and it maintained supply while big fossil fuel plants failed in the heat . <p> During the heatwave last month , three of the ACT 's solar farms paid $840,000 to the ACT . Photo : Lukas Coch <p> And far from being a high cost to consumers , the ACT 's renewable energy policies are actually protecting consumers from electricity price spikes . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ farms see these generators actually paying the ACT during periods of high wholesale electricity prices . These payments translate into savings on the electricity bills of ACT residents . <p> The payments can be substantial . During the heatwave last month , three of the ACT 's solar farms paid $840,000 to the ACT . That benefits every household , business and community group in Canberra . By 2020 , not only will the ACT be producing 100 per cent of its electricity from renewable sources , but its financial arrangements will help to keep our electricity costs stable . <p> ACT residents should be pleased that our electricity purchasing agreements with 10 wind and solar projects across Australia have locked-in a set price for the renewable electricity they produce for the next 20 years . If the generators make more money than the set price for the electricity they sell into the national electricity market , they pay the difference back to the ACT . <p> This is a way of providing long term certainty to ACT electricity consumers and helping insulate them from further wholesale price rises @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ renewable electricity will peak at about $5.50 a week for households in 2020 . This cost will be largely offset by the ACT 's Energy Efficiency Improvement Scheme , which will deliver average savings of $3.20 a week across all ACT households and $5 a week for households participating in the EEIS by 2020 . A quarter of Canberrans recently surveyed were even willing to pay more than this amount to cover additional costs of infrastructure and technologies to reduce emissions . <p> Advertisement <p> This is all happening right under the noses of federal politicians as they sit in Parliament House . But instead of addressing the true issue , the federal government looks the other way and announces they want to build new coal fired power generation , subsidised by taxpayers . Ironically , at the same time as these announcements , the east coast of Australia was suffering through a record breaking heatwave . The heat caused people to become sick and infrastructure to fail -- including coal fired power plants , whose cooling systems can fail in extreme heat . <p> Not only is this @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ but it 's economically irresponsible . Recent University of Melbourne research shows that new coal plants aimed at reducing emissions would cost $62 billion , while using renewables would be about half the cost . <p> Residents in the ACT and elsewhere should know that renewable energy generation , including solar and wind , is absolutely compatible with energy security . It is not to blame for supply issues and blackouts that arose during the heatwave . Freedom of Information documents recently revealed that the federal government was informed about this fact . Yet outrageously , it continued to blame renewable energy . <p> There are several real reasons for recent problems with electricity supply . Our electricity grid is out of date and governments have failed to modernise it . More coal wo n't help ; but new technologies like solar and battery storage are actually very effective at responding to the demand spikes that are overloading the system . <p> The National Energy Market also operates under a series of market and regulatory frameworks that , perversely , can allow blackouts to occur while private companies hold @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> And our electricity network is suffering due to extreme weather events like heatwaves . In these scenarios infrastructure is increasingly fallible and electricity demand spikes . Climate change is making these events more frequent and severe , and burning more coal will only make it worse . <p> As the icing on the cake , last week Canberra started to receive electricity from Hornsdale wind farm -- boosting the ACT renewable energy supply from 20 to 35 per cent . We are on track to meet our targets of 100 per cent renewable electricity generation and 40 per cent reduction on 1990 levels of greenhouse gas emissions by 2020 . The next step is to develop and implement a plan to reach zero net emissions by 2050 at the latest . This is what the science tells us we need to do . We must hold fast when the federal government is failing us . <p> Shane Rattenbury is the ACT Minister for Climate Change and Sustainability and leader of the ACT Greens .
