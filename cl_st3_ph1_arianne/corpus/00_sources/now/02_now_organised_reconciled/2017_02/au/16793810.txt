<h> Great Barrier Reef ' may be dead in 20 years ' <p> Published : 5:21 am , Wednesday , 8 February 2017 <p> The Great Barrier Reef could be dead within 20 years if greenhouse gas emissions are not curbed , scientists say . <p> A report by Australia 's Climate Council reveals the mass bleaching event in 2016 , which killed two-thirds of all coral in the northern third of the reef , will occur every second or third year by the mid-2030s if greenhouse gas emissions continue to rise at their present rate . <p> ' Which basically will kill the reef , ' Will Steffen , the lead author of Cranking up the Intensity : Climate Change and Extreme Weather Events , told AAP . <p> This is just one of the warnings from the report , which shows how extreme weather events in Australia are shaped by climate change . <p> Another example is coastal flooding . Sea level rises of about 20cm have been recorded in Sydney and Fremantle during the past century . <p> ' That does n't sound like a lot @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ increase in flooding in both Sydney and Fremantle , ' Prof Steffen said . <p> Under current projections , sea levels will rise between 50cm and a metre by 2100 . <p> ' That means if an event is now expected to happen once in 100 years , it would occur every year , ' he said . <p> As long as coal , oil and gas burns as it does now , heatwaves , droughts , floods and fires will become more frequent and fierce , the report says . <p> ' All extreme weather events are now occurring in an atmosphere that is hotter and wetter than it was 60 years ago , ' Prof Steffen said . <p> ' There is more energy in the system to drive more extreme weather . ' <p> For example , 2013 was Australia 's hottest year on record , and Prof Steffen said there was only a one-in-12,000 or 13,000 chance the record could have been reached under pre-industrial greenhouse gas emission levels .
