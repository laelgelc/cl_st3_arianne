<h> Jeff Angel on the art and craft of environmental campaigns <h> Tina Perinotto 28 February 2017 <p> Ask Total Environment Centre 's Jeff Angel what advice he gives to young environmental campaigners who look with dismay at the antics of our federal government on coal , and worse , at the rise of Donald Trump in the US , and he does n't beat around the bush . <p> It 's all about staying power and a refusal to give up , Angel says . <p> " I often tell younger campaigners , ' Do n't expect it to be easy or quick . Do n't expect the people who are opposing you to be nice . ' " <p> Angel ought to know . He 's probably one of the longest serving environmental campaigners in Australia . He joined TEC in 1973 and has been its executive director since 1995 . <p> And he 's led some of the toughest campaigns in environmental history -- such as the ban against lead petrol and the madness of felling giant old trees for woodchipping . <p> Most recently , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ been in the fight for a deposit container scheme , so that the millions of bottles clogging up our environment can be returned and recycled . <p> It 's taken 13 years and has involved taking on fierce resistance by the giant Coca Cola company , but now Angel is waiting on just two more signatures to make the scheme national . <p> The TEC has also worked with the property industry , most recently with the Property Council of Australia , in a bid to reform the National Electricity Market , another battle that will take the willpower of giants to win , but on which he 's hopeful . <p> For an unassuming , softly spoken man , it 's not a bad legacy to look back on after more than 40 years . <p> Angel , a notoriously hard worker , took time out on a recent Friday afternoon in his friendly rabbit-warren offices in Surry Hills to talk about the art and craft of political campaigns . <p> But he had to be prised from his computer , even as The Fifth Estate stood in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , before finally swinging his chair around to talk big picture . <p> After the emotional anxiety engendered the world over by Trump 's win in the US , Angel 's laconic style is a welcome relief . He 's even sympathetic in some ways . <p> " People voted for Trump because he was a real person , unlike well-rehearsed politicians we 've had before , " he says . <p> Globalisation advocates have not paid sufficient attention to the consequences of their policies . <p> " It 's one of the things I find irksome . On his estimates the environmental movement and the regulations they spurred created " millions " more jobs than were lost -- in pollution control programs , land care , environment management sectors , tourism and renewable energy . <p> Angel is also more patient with Trump 's rise than many others in his patch .
