<h> Human callousness crosses the threshold of nature 's tolerance <h> Share Via Email <p> A few days ago , a fire broke out at Bellandur Lake in Bengaluru . Heavy smoke spewing from the waters of the 892-acre lake cast a giant pall over the entire area around it . <p> This was not the first time : the lake had burst into flames in May 2015 , and again in August 2016 , apparently due to the effects of chemical effluents and untreated sewage routinely dumped in the lake by surrounding factories . <p> This time , the authorities say that the dry hyacinth weeds on the fringe of the lake probably caught fire due to rise in temperature caused by methane , a combustible gas produced by the toxic chemicals flowing into the lake . Residents , on the other hand , insist that the fire was triggered by unchecked burning of illegally dumped garbage near the lake . <p> The Environment Ministry has ordered an inquiry . Add to that the apathy of the civic authorities , and we have a recipe for disaster . <p> The flames and smoke are only the outward symptoms of a larger malaise . The lake is not an isolated water body but part of a chain that sustains the storm water infrastructure of the entire valley area . Rapid and unplanned urbanisation , absence of infrastructure , rampant encroachments and a negligent attitude towards waste management have caused untold harm to the whole ecosystem to which the lake is connected . To stem the decline calls for a massive collective effort .
