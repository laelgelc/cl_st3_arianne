It is of unique cultural and historical significance . Practically every civilisation and all the great religions have employed it for both secular and devotional objects . <p> Until the colonial era ( the 19th &amp; early 20th centuries ) it was a highly prized material used with a care bordering almost on reverence by some of the most highly skilled artists , or else by indigenous peoples who also afforded it the highest status . <p> The problem began , in my view , in the second half of the 19th century . Britain especially , but also other countries particularly China and Japan imported colossal amounts of ivory , in the case of the former both raw tusks and worked , in the case of South East Asia mostly just raw tusks . <p> By the 20th century this once very special material had become so ubiquitous that it was used for inexpensive jewellery and trinkets , piano keys and handles .
