<p> Three years after becoming the first British person to complete seven of the hardest oceans swims in the world , Adam Walker has set his sights on the new challenge of ice swimming . <p> Following a successful spell out of the water managing his own swimming business , called Ocean Walker , the 38-year-old record-holder from Newark has decided to take on this fresh challenge to help raise awareness about climate change . <p> And after being invited to a Global Commons Conference in Washington DC last October , Adam felt moved to alert people to the dangers of melting ice caps and ocean pollution in the only way he knew how - open water swimming . <p> Submerging himself in waters of five degrees or less , the former Radcliffe-on-Trent Junior School pupil has been training for a series of icy challenges that most recently involved swimming a mile in the four degree waters of Lough Dan in Ireland . <p> " I 'm looking to complete a series of challenges that will push my body to the limit , " said Adam . " Last @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ complete a mile-long swim at Lough Dan at a 3.8 degree water temperature . " <p> " It 's all done with a view to raise awareness for the issues regarding climate change and hopefully what I 'm putting my body through will help achieve that . " <p> With three ambulances on hand to help any of the 15 swimmers at the event , Adam said his sport is by no means for the faint-hearted and requires serious mental preparation . <p> " You can be the best , fastest swimmer in the world but once you 're in there and start panicking from the cold you 're in trouble . It 's a massive mental game and you have to have a strong mindset and be able to switch off the negatives , " he said . <p> " Swimming in freezing temperatures for any small amount of time is like swimming through lard . It numbs your legs , it numbs your arms and you have to be very experienced to deal with it . "
