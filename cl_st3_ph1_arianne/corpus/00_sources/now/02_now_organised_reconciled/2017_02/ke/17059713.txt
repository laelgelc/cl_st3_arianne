<h> Kenya 's middle class abetting national decay <h> share <p> Are you broke ? Are you among Kenyans who do not consider themselves in the class of the poor , but are still finding it increasingly difficult to make ends meet , even without luxuries ? Of course , you may not admit . <p> It is like a preacher asking those who want to die and go to heaven to step out of the pews . It all boils down to both individual and collective denial and hypocrisy . <p> And this is what has become the defining characteristic of the archetypal Kenyan . Unknowingly , we are now living in a post-truth society where lies and deception have become the common currency of doing business in all spheres of life . <p> Morals and basic human decency have been thrown to the dogs in our bid to get ahead of everyone else , or simply to survive . The post-truth society is a relatively new phenomenon that is gaining credence around the world , particularly in nations that worship the god of neo-capitalism . <p> Today @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is what it seems . People will say one thing and privately do the opposite , just to get ahead in the rat race . Lies have become internalised and truth has actually become stranger than fiction . <p> Thus , no one wants to deal with inconvenient truths anymore . There is nowhere this is more pronounced than in leadership circles . Those in positions of authority in various areas have perfected the art of exploiting the gullibility of humanity . <p> That is why we adore the wealthy and corrupt among us , while we give no credit or ignore the honest and struggling people . There is a distinct disconnect and alienation of the values that used to create social stability and progress . <p> Now , by default , the middle class is supposed to be the lighthouse and conscience of modern society . This is because the wealthy are too engrossed in protecting and perpetuating affluence , while the poor are gripped by the struggle for the most basic of livelihoods , living from hand-to-mouth . <p> In the evolved developed world , the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ first to pour on the streets to protest against misrule . Indeed , most revolutions in these countries were led by the middle class , who mobilised the proletariat to throw out insensitive and inhuman monarchs . However , the Kenyan middle class is a curious lot ! <p> We pay the highest taxes , but are given the rawest of deals in return . For instance , we have let the government get away with non-performance in many sectors through sheer corruption . <p> Instead of demanding for better roads , we unnecessarily spend more to buy " four by four " vehicles to tackle the terrain , even within urban areas . <p> Rather than demand that our taxes be invested in ensuring constant supply of clean tap water , we surrender to digging boreholes and buying massive water storage tanks . <p> In place of demanding uninterrupted electricity , we buy generators or spend hours sheepishly complaining how corruption is destroying the economy . Indeed , the middle class in this country is lost in a make-believe world . <p> We have redefined the concept of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ securing expensive mortgages , purchasing second hand top range vehicles , shopping in malls and taking our children to posh schools -- with money we really do n't have . Suffice to say that the country is , in fact has been , on sinking sand for long , institutionally speaking . <p> The ruling class know the buttons to touch , and the amount of crumbs to give in order to make the middle-class complacent , or look the other way as they milk us dry . <p> In order to avoid a bloody revolution by the masses like what almost happened about ten years ago , the middle class must rise up , roll up their sleeves and get the country out of the muddle of misgovernance .
