<h> More than 100 protesters come out for Woodfibre LNG meeting <p> ANTI-LNG Protestors gathered in front of Squamish municipal hall on Friday , Jan. 27 as government officials met with Woodfibre LNG to discuss the company 's planned LNG project on Howe Sound . <p> More than 100 people protested at Squamish Municipal Hall last week as government and Woodfibre LNG officials met to discuss a controversial natural gas facility . <p> Organized by environmental activist group My Sea to Sky , demonstrators from across the region gathered on Friday , Jan. 27 to make their opposition known to Woodfibre 's planned LNG project . <p> Whistler 's John Wood , 65 , made the trip down Highway 99 with his wife , and explained his reasoning for attending . <p> " I just see the danger in us getting onboard with a sunset industry , " he said . " Everybody seems to be pretty much agreed that fossil fuel use is contributing to this coming catastrophe of climate change , and we 've got to do something about it . " <p> The focus of the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ governments , including Sea to Sky MLA Jordan Sturdy and MP Pamela Goldsmith-Jones , centred on communication with the public and improving transparency , and also provided Woodfibre with a chance to present an update on the project . <p> " One of the ( province 's ) conditions , condition 25 , which is how the project is monitored -- what is the status of the conditions and the monitoring ? " said Sturdy , discussing the goal of the meeting outside municipal hall . " This is about trying to better understand the impact of this and better monitor the impacts . " <p> Members of the public and media were barred from attending the meeting , an irony that was not lost on Tracey Saxby , co-founder of My Sea to Sky . <p> " It 's really concerning when they 're inviting all of the mayors , Indigenous leaders , and representatives from the province and federal government to enhance transparency around a project when the media and public is not invited , " she said . " They are listening to us and acknowledging @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ working ... but it calls into question all the decisions that have been made to date . " <p> Woodfibre LNG 's VP of corporate affairs Byng Giraud said his company did not set the terms of the meeting , which was organized by North Vancouver MP Jonathan Wilkinson , and was happy to share the project update publicly . ( Woodfibre forwarded a copy of its presentation to local media . ) <p> Woodfibre also set up a beta version of an online portal last week where the public can view updates to the project , as well as the remaining permits and conditions the company has to meet . <p> " How do we keep people informed in a more live manner as to whether we 're carrying out those conditions ? I think it 's something governments are struggling with ; you have these technical processes , but how do people understand them , how do they trust them ? " Giraud said of the portal . " We agreed to come and participate and talk about that and put up ( the portal ) as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in progress . " <p> Last week Woodfibre filed an amendment to its Environmental Assessment in order to meet one of the Squamish Nation 's conditions to switch the LNG plant 's previously planned seawater-cooling system to air-cooling . A decision on the amendment is anticipated for early spring . <p> With a provincial election looming , Saxby is worried that B.C. Liberals -- who 've long touted the LNG sector as a boon for the economy and the logical alternative to coal -- will simply " rubberstamp " the project in spite of considerable local opposition . <p> " I think they 're trying to get as many permits ticked off as they possibly can before the election because there 's a real risk ( Premier ) Christy Clark 's Liberals are n't going to get elected again , " she said . <p> " We 're going to continue to organize and we 're going to continue to stand up and fight for the rights of the environment . "
