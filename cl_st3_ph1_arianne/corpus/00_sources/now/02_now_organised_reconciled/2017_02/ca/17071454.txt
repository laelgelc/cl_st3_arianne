<p> New research suggests that climate change has mysteriously caused lakes in a northern protected area to nearly double in size , forcing a herd of at-risk bison off some of their best habitat . <p> Lakes in the Mackenzie Bison Sanctuary off the northwest shore of Great Slave Lake are now bigger than any time in at least the last 200 years , said Josh Thienpont , a University of Ottawa scientist and a lead author on the paper , published Thursday in the journal Nature . <p> Bison reintroduced to Banff National Park ( Reuters ) <p> " The whole landscape does appear to be getting wetter , " he said . <p> Thienpont and his colleagues grew intrigued with the 10,000-square-kilometre sanctuary after people from Fort Providence , N.W.T. , pointed out things were changing . <p> " Some of the local community had noticed that it was more difficult to travel on the landscape because it was wetter , " Thienpont said . <p> Thienpont and his colleagues examined satellite imagery of the area between 1986 and 2011 .
