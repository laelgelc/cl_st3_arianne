<h> Sticky , insect-sized drones could act as pollinators : study <p> Small drones coated with horsehair and a sticky gel could one day help pollinate crops and help offset the costly loss of bee populations worldwide , researchers in Japan said Thursday . <p> The miniature robots described in the journal Chem are a long way from being deployed in the field , but researchers say they may offer a partial solution to the loss of bees due to disease and climate change . <p> " The findings , which will have applications for agriculture and robotics , among others , could lead to the development of artificial pollinators and help counter the problems caused by declining honeybee populations , " said lead author Eijiro Miyako , a chemist at the National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology ( AIST ) Nanomaterials Research Institute . <p> In 2007 , Miyako began experimenting with liquids that could be used as electrical conductors . <p> One failed attempt produced a sticky gel , like hair wax , which he relegated to a storage cabinet for almost a decade . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ its unchanged nature gave Miyako an idea . <p> He began experimenting with houseflies and ants , applying the gel to them to see if it could help pick up pollen from tulips in a box . It worked . <p> Then he moved to drones , coating them with horsehair to mimic a bee 's fuzzy coating . <p> He added the gel to their undersides and flew them over pink-leaved Japanese lilies ( Lilium japonicum ) . <p> " The robots absorbed the pollen and then could be flown to a second flower , where the grains were deposited , artificially pollinating the plants and causing them to begin the process of producing seeds , " said the study . <p> The United States recently listed the rusty patched bumblebee ( Bombus affinis ) as an endangered species .
