<p> The trouble with politics lately is that it has become a bit too political . In the 17th century John Locke defined political power as the " right of making laws , with penalties of death , and consequently all less penalties ... and all this only for the public good " . Political power , properly exercised , serves the public ( though whether the death penalty is appropriate is a question we might do better not to raise in the current climate ) . Sometimes , though , power becomes an end in itself -- whether to run the nation , party , even the local branch . That 's when people of goodwill lose heart , even more so when power is used to pursue private interest . <p> It is , therefore , an interesting moment to move , as I have just done , from a department of philosophy to a school of government , focused on the education of future leaders from around the world , and to engage on national and international policy issues .
