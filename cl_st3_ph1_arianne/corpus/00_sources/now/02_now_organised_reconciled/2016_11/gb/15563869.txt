<p> W ater scarcity and climate change have long been political issues in Bolivia . At the turn of the century , Bolivia experienced a " water war " , as huge numbers of people protested and rioted over the privatisation of a dwindling water supply in the mountain city Cochabamba . <p> Since then , the problems have only intensified . The Chacaltaya glacier , whose seasonal melt waters had traditionally supplied La Paz with water during the dry season , disappeared in 2009 . And then in 2015 came the news that Lake Poopo , formerly the country 's second largest lake , had completely dried up . Temperatures around the lake had risen by 0.9 degrees in just two decades , leading to accelerated levels of evaporation , which is part of a wider pattern of warming across the Andes . <p> Now , faced with the worst drought in 25 years , President Evo Morales has been forced to declare a national state of emergency , following 172 local authorities requesting help .
