Do a few table scraps thrown into the garbage really make that big of a difference ? The answer is yes . When organics end up in landfills , they often get sealed in pockets beneath other layers of garbage . When this happens , oxygen is unable to reach the organics and it can then take an additional 25 years for the materials to break down . <p> Additionally , because organics in landfills break down without the aid of oxygen , they end up releasing methane gas into the atmosphere , an emission that is approximately 25 times more harmful than regular carbon dioxide emissions . According to documents from the Manitoba government , Manitoba could divert 100,000 tonnes -- composed of approximately 60 per cent food waste and 40 per cent of leaf and yard waste -- of total organic waste annually from landfills by 2020 . This diversion would be the equivalent of reducing GHGs by 59,000 tonnes annually . Truthfully , this is a small step . Manitoba has been releasing approximately 20 megatonnes of GHG emissions every year for the last decade .
