<p> I am one of four finalists in the Lifetime Achievement ( gasp ! ) category of the third annual national Nature Inspiration Awards . These awards are sponsored by the National Museum of Nature in Ottawa , The Globe and Mail , The Walrus magazine , Bruce Power and Enbridge ( yes , the pipeline company ) . A gala event was held at the Museum of Nature on November 9 to celebrate all the finalists in seven different categories and , of course , to announce the winners . Neal Jotham , an 82 year-old from Ottawa won this particular award for his decades of work successfully developing and promoting more humane traps for the fur industry . I am happy for him , but I still want to share my acceptance speech , particularly in light of the election results south of the border . So , here goes : <p> Thank you very much . I feel honoured and humbled . <p> I seek to engage learners of all ages with their natural surroundings . I bring children and adults into the experiential midst of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in a freshwater stream ; or holding sunflower seeds in an open hand so that hungry chickadees can touch down for a winter feed ; or , this past September , using an iPhone and a blue tooth speaker to call in a family of curious and talkative barred owls on a northern Ontario canoe trip . I invite participants to experience first hand the intricate interconnections of our marvellous natural world , to be in awe and wonder , and to feel empowered to act on behalf of our increasingly threatened planet . <p> As I spent much of this afternoon wandering through its exhibits , it struck me that such relationships underlie what the Museum of Nature does . I want to commend the staff for their world-class collaborations , research and exhibits . <p> These awards are a wonderful idea -- to nationally celebrate Canadians who are dedicated to nature in an impressive variety of ways and , through this , to inspire others to make similar connections and commitments .
