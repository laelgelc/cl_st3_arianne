<p> Gordon Hoekstra 's series on earthquake preparedness has been eye-opening . I think most people had hoped our state of earthquake preparedness was more advanced than it is . <p> The major problem seems to be the billions of dollars it will cost to retrofit or rebuild old buildings to meet modern engineering standards . That kind of financial obligation is enough to make most governments procrastinate . <p> Fortunately , there are interim solutions that are effective and wo n't take billions of dollars and decades to carry out . The biggest one is earthquake warning systems of the type mentioned in the Nov. 3 article . <p> Catholic schools in the Archdiocese of Vancouver have been installing early warning alarm systems for the last few years . Our immediate priority was our 46 schools because they are occupied by more people , particularly children , more of the time . All the schools now have the system installed and we 're working on our churches . <p> For a few thousand dollars , a school can be equipped with a system that provides students and staff @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ time for them to get under desks or tables , reducing the risk of injury or death . The system has already worked successfully , giving advance notice of a minor earthquake . <p> I hope anxiety about financing seismic upgrades wo n't dissuade public officials from implementing this effective and low-cost solution .
