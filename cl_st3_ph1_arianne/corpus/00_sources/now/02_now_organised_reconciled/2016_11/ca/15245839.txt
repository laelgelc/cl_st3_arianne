<h> Canadian fights for tropical dry forests at United Nations meeting <p> University of Alberta scientist Arturo Sanchez poses at an environmental monitoring super site in the Santa Rosa National Park in Santa Rosa , Costa Rica , in this undated handout photo . University of Alberta scientist Arturo Sanchez is studying the effects of climate change on the dry tropical forests of Central America . He says rising temperatures are largely behind the drought that is causing major social and environmental problems in the area , a problem the world can no longer afford to ignore . But Sanchez says the dry forests -- where torrential rainy seasons alternate with a long arid season -- are the most endangered forest ecosystem in the tropics . <p> " Right now , only 40 per cent of the original extent remains , " Sanchez said . <p> Much of the problem stems from centuries of exploitation . But the following year , the rain simply dried up . <p> " We had one of the worst droughts ever in Central America . " <p> About 100,000 families were forced to pick up and move .
