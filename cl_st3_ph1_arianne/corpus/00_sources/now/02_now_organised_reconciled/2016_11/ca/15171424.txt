But one of his favorite tasks at work is sitting on a tractor , moving the used grain and malt onto farmland for compost . <p> " It feels good , " said Smith , who says he likes watching the compost steam . A simple pleasure . <p> Unusual work for the CEO , perhaps , but the Persephone Brewing Company is n't your usual craft brewery . The company does everything farm-to-bottle -- almost @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> Gibsons on the Sunshine Coast is home to Persephone 's Beer Farm . It 's an apt name : brewing operations and tasting-room are housed inside a red , barn-like building , and the 11 acres of land are used for its own hop yards , bee farm , apple trees -- and even chickens , providing eggs for pizza and breads made on-site . <p> But there 's a lot more brewing here than just fresh beer . Thanks to its business model , Persephone recently won the Real Estate Foundation of BC 's 2016 Land Award in the private sector category . In addition to composting , Persephone reuses wastewater ; it leases surplus land to local farmers ; hires local people with developmental disabilities ; and is a social enterprise owned by 132 individuals and organizations . <p> And Persephone had already earned B Corp certification for its commitment to social responsibility and sustainability , joining companies like Patagonia and Ben &amp; Jerry 's ice cream . <p> Smith said Persephone is all about keeping agriculture close to home -- " reducing food miles @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a responsible way . " <p> The company name is a nod to two Persephones that represent its values : Persephone the log salvage boat from CBC 's The Beachcombers , as a tribute to life on the coast ; and Persephone the Greek goddess of vegetation . <p> Smith is no stranger to the world of land use or social enterprises . He has a master 's degree from UBC 's planning school and worked at the non-profit Building Opportunities with Business , which was active in Vancouver neighbourhoods like the Downtown Eastside and Strathcona . That was how he met Save On Meats proprietor Mark Brand . <p> For a while , Smith and Brand flirted with the idea of a craft brewery in the Downtown Eastside . After Smith moved to Gibsons , the idea transformed into the Beer Farm . <p> One important task : growing hops , which require a special array of giant supports . <p> That 's where Dion Whyte came in . <p> " I 've been home brewing for years , " said Whyte . He set up the first hop yard with the help of staff , volunteers and clients from the Sunshine Coast Association for Community Living , which is one of Persephone 's owners and provides services for people with developmental disabilities . <p> " It was very neat for the community , " said Whyte . " Our property is right up against North Road , and there were vehicles coming off the ferry watching this crazy installation . We had stuck a sign on the side of the road that said ' Beer Farm ' and they were driving by honking their horns . " <p> It was a small shift for Whyte , who worked on water conservation , zero-waste , and climate change mitigation for the regional district . <p> " All of that I 've been able to put into practice here , which is great , because I was very much at a policy and planning level there , " he said . The company may expand to another orchard and industrial property . <p> And it 's not just Persephone that 's growing . So is the brewery scene in B.C. There are about 130 in the province today , but unlike Persephone , they 're located mostly on light industrial land . <p> " What I like most about this approach is the way I see our customers and members of our community connecting the dots about the importance of local agriculture , " said Smith . " You can bring the kids to check out the chickens , look at the hop yards , see the gardens , watch the bees , check out the ducks eating the spent grains in the compost pile . It brings people back to a simpler place . It 's kind of like removing the curtain that covers the black box of how all the stuff we eat and drink is made .
