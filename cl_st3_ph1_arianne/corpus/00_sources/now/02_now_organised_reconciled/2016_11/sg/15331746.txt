<h> In Conversation With Joel Adriano , Regional Coordinator of SciDev.Net <p> AsianScientist ( Nov. 10 , 2016 ) - Good science communications in the Philippines is crucial , as the country is constantly on the receiving end of natural disasters such as typhoons . <p> Joel Adriano , regional coordinator at international science and technology news outfit SciDev.Net , tells Asian Scientist Magazine about what it 's like to be a science communicator in the Philippines , where interest in science is steadily growing , and where everyone still hates the slow internet . <p> Could you briefly describe your role as regional coordinator at SciDev.Net ? <p> As regional coordinator , I 'm basically like an assistant director in charge of a specific region , in my case , Southeast Asia and the Pacific . We are now expanding to an Asia Pacific desk by mid-November -- we are merging my region with the South Asia desk . <p> I handle the day-to-day operations of the regional desk , from news pitches and giving out assignments , to editing and posting of articles . This makes recruiting freelance writers and team members easier than when we first started . <p> Moreover , science officials in the country are generally free of politics , making it easy to approach them without going through the bureaucratic hassle , and making the coverage of local topics easier . <p> However , while the country has lots of science writers and officials who are easy to approach , there are actually very few science stories that are worthy of news space at the international level . <p> An even bigger disadvantage is the very slow internet in the Philippines , which is often described as one of the worst in the world . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ -- more expensive than in our UK office if you consider the same speed . <p> How has your previous expertise in environmental science shaped the way you approach writing about it ? <p> It provided me a deeper understanding of the issues , which is quite useful when handing out assignments , deciding on pitches , and editing articles . My technical background has made me appreciate and comprehend science topics and issues from a scientific and technical perspective as well , enabling me to avoid the emotional traps associated with certain thorny issues , like those involving genetically modified organisms and climate change . <p> Adriano at an editorial meeting with colleagues at the Science and Development Network ( SciDev.net ) . <p> What are some challenges that you face when communicating science to the public ? <p> The main challenge is how to make science interesting for the public , so as to capture their interest , stimulate discussions and build support . There is a general perception that science topics can be very technical or limited to interest groups . You need to help them @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to make informed decisions and choices . A perfect example is making an informed decision on vaccination when presented with good science . <p> Moreover , science can be both exciting and entertaining given all the uncertainties , wonderful discoveries and innovations , which , hopefully , would stir enough interest among the younger generation to take up science courses . <p> In your opinion , is the awareness of environmental issues growing in the Philippines and the region , and if not , how can we do our part to raise awareness ? <p> There is certainly an increasing awareness of environmental issues in the region . There are now plenty of laws and research geared towards green ideas , improving the environment , and the efficient use of resources . Fishermen have become more aware of the need for sustainable fishing methods to preserve their livelihood . There are more stringent measures against pollution from motor vehicles and factories . Governments have become more vigilant of mining activities , and that segregation is becoming a norm in most parts . These are just but a few examples @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ these developments . <p> However , despite the awareness , concrete actions have materialized very slowly in many cases . For instance , the haze issue still persists despite so many studies , laws , discussions and decisions . <p> What is one piece of advice you would like to give to aspiring science journalists in Asia and beyond ? <p> It 's one thing to hone your journalistic skills , but it 's another to learn and write about science .
