<p> Marine biologist Robin Aiello is seeking to bridge the gap between researchers and the tourist industry . <p> My knees hit the seabed as I come face to face with two moray eels , their trim , long heads bobbing out from the crevice of a rock . The slithery scene seemed innocent to the untrained eye . However , marine biologist Robin Aiello knew otherwise . <p> " Those eels were mating , " Aiello says , climbing out of the Indian Ocean and onto the boat . Without her diving regulator , the scientific expedition leader had no qualms describing in explicit detail on what we had stumbled upon . <p> Arranged by luxury hotel Cheval Blanc Randheli in the Maldives , our sub aqua excursion was set in the secluded Noonu Atoll . We were guided by Aiello and Asraf , the hotel 's local dive master , who patiently calmed my underwater unease while pointing out the stunning oceanic life . <p> " The fact is everyone loves a good story , " says Aiello , a Harvard graduate in evolutionary biology .
