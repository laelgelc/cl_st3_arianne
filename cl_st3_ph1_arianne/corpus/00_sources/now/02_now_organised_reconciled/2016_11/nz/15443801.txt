<h> Deals prompt global warming optimism <p> A former University of Otago dean is optimistic international agreements on greenhouse gas emissions will help avert some of the worst fears about global sea level rises . <p> " The reason for my optimism is because I 'm seeing commitment to dealing with global warming , that I do n't believe I 've seen previously , " Emeritus Prof John Hannah said yesterday . <p> " There 's greater world recognition that we have a problem that needs to be dealt with . " <p> He believed New Zealand would face sea level rises of between 0.5m and 1m by the year 2115 . <p> This would pose a significant challenge for some parts of the country , including South Dunedin . <p> But Prof Hannah ( 66 ) , a former dean of the surveying school , said he was not despairing about South Dunedin 's future and acknowledged technological solutions had been found to protect low-lying areas in other countries , including in the Netherlands . <p> He now lives in Nelson , having retired from the university five @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Future sea level rise in New Zealand , an updated perspective " , at the New Zealand Coastal Society 's annual conference in Dunedin , which ends today . <p> He was concerned some New Zealand local authorities could be " tempted " to put in place coastal restrictions based on postulating significantly higher future sea level rises than his own estimates.He urged planners to continue to adopt creative approaches to the challenges , and to work closely with the community . <p> US president-elect Donald Trump has stated several times he will withdraw the US from international climate change agreements . <p> Asked after his talk about this , Prof Hannah said any action by the new US administration would only delay matters but would not thwart the international momentum on the need to limit greenhouse gas emissions and to restrict future sea level rises . <p> Conference organiser Dr Mick Strack said about 100 people , mostly from the North Island , were attending the conference .
