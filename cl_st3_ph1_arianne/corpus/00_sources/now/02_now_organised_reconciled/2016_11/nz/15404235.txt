<h> Set for another year of record heat <p> The United Nations expects this year to be the hottest on record . Photo / AP <p> This year is on track to be the hottest on record , the United Nations forecast yesterday . <p> Scientists , meanwhile , reported that greenhouse-gas emissions from burning fossil fuels , the main driver of global warming , remained constant for the third year running last year , while the World Bank calculated that 26 million people slip into poverty annually due to natural disasters , reflecting the risks posed by climate change . <p> All three reports were issued on the sidelines of high-level United Nations climate talks in Marrakesh still reeling from news that Donald Trump , who denies climate change , had captured the White House . <p> The fact that economic growth and CO2 pollution no longer move in lock-step is an encouraging sign , the scientists said . <p> But a steady pace of emissions still puts Earth on a collision course with deadly superstorms , droughts , heatwaves and other extreme weather events . <p> Unless @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ humanity will exhaust its " carbon budget " - the total amount of CO2 that can be emitted without crossing the redline of dangerous warming - within a few decades . <p> At the same time , the UN forecast that average global temperatures this year will " very likely " be 1.2C above the pre-industrial era , the benchmark for global warming . <p> Sixteen of the 17 hottest years on record will have occurred this century , according to the World Meteorological Organisation ( WMO ) . <p> The world is already more than halfway to the upper limit of " well under " 2C agreed , the limit set in the 196-nation Paris Agreement that went into force earlier this month . <p> In parts of Arctic Russia , temperatures were 6C to 7C higher than the longterm average , the WMO reported .
