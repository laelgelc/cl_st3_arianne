<h> Arable farming to be big winner from water scheme <p> Cropping using irrigation could become a lot more prevalent in Wairarapa . <p> A report on future land uses in Wairarapa brought about by a region-wide water storage scheme , points to growth across a variety of agricultural industries . <p> Farm advisory consultancy BakerAg has investigated the changing land uses in the Ruamahanga valley , both with and without water storage in its report entitled " Water Wairarapa Future Land Use Scenarios " . <p> The report summary said with the proposed Water Wairarapa scheme the primary sector in the region will flourish , but without the scheme the future was less rosy , especially with climate change impacts . <p> Criticism of the scheme has focused on the possibility of driving unsustainable growth in dairy farming and the Green Party says any intensification is bad for waterways . <p> Wairarapa Water project director Michael Bassett-Foss said the scheme was not about dairying , but instead , creating certainty around water availability , new land use opportunities , and promoting environmentally sustainable land uses and practices . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a section that said with the scheme , pastoral and cropping farming would be the first to expand and even though there may be an initial increase in dairy as a faster adopter of irrigation , it will soon shrink compared with current land uses . <p> " Wairarapa is not conducive to widespread dairy increase because of its relatively small blocks of land and land being more expensive . The report shows there will be much more productive uses for land than dairy , " Bassett-Foss said . <p> Factoring in climate change projections , BakerAg forecasts less livestock farming , including dairying , and more plant-based food production in the valley . <p> That will lead to increasing export of edible crops , vegetables , fruit and growth in boutique value-added businesses associated with high value locally grown produce . <p> Green Party spokeswoman Catherine Delahunty said she was pleased to see the report acknowledged that climate change was going to fundamentally affect future farming practice <p> " But I was disappointed to see that basically despite that acknowledgement it is still going to basically be business @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ solution , which it 's not , " she said . <p> Delahunty noted that the report showed dairying would increase with a water scheme and any intensification brought environmental problems . <p> " If you intensify agriculture any more in your area you are going to intensify nutrients into the waterways .
