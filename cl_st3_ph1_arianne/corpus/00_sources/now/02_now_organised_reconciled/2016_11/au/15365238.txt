<h> Donald Trump has fans in the Liberal Party , but Australia wo n't follow <p> Pauline Hanson has sometimes given this country a scare , notably in 1996 when she was elected to Parliament and this year when her One Nation Party won four Senate seats . But her American equivalent , Donald Trump , last week delivered a still sharper shock , inflicting the biggest election upset in living memory by winning the White House . <p> Like Hanson , Trump is a symptom of a wider political malaise : fear of globalisation and radical socio-economic change . Perhaps that 's why Hanson herself cracked open a bottle of bubbles to celebrate last week 's victory . <h> How to prove a Shakespearean play <h> Shorten on Trump : Australia could go same way <p> The twist is that many Australians on the centre-right spectrum also cheered on Trump . Cory Bernardi is hardly alone . I have many local mates who lionise The Donald : last Wednesday , while I was wiping the egg off my face at Channel 7 in Martin Place -- like most @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ -- several friends celebrated the election result at a rugby club down the road . Some , such as former Liberal MP Ross Cameron , Spectator Australia editor Rowan Dean and News Limited columnist Miranda Devine , proudly predicted Trump 's triumph . So , too , did my friend and former Labor leader Mark Latham , who admires Trump for defying the Washington cabal of media elites , defence hawks and identity politics zealots . <p> I fully understand why Latham , not to mention Hansonites and Palmerites , root for Trump . But why would people who barracked for mainstream centre-right politicians John Howard and Tony Abbott support a rude and lewd demagogue who 's peddled conspiracy theories ? A crass opportunist who from 1999 to 2011 changed political parties five times . <p> The issue is all the more perplexing when you bear in mind that most Australian conservatives are unashamed advocates of US global leadership that culminated in the Iraq war , which the more dovish Trump says he opposed . Liberals champion free trade whereas Trump supports high tariffs on China and Mexico and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the Abbott and Turnbull governments have backed . And most Liberals espouse free-market reforms whereas Trump rejects Tea-Party style cuts to welfare entitlements . <p> Moreover , Trump 's election , as the left-wing documentary maker Michael Moore recognised early on , was largely thanks to the white working class in those rust-belt states ( Ohio , Pennsylvania , Michigan , Wisconsin ) that usually vote Democrat . These blue-collar battlers had suffered wage stagnation and economic dislocation in recent decades . Far from panting for Reaganite capitalism and a Pax Americana , they were attracted to Trump 's economic nationalist and less interventionist foreign-policy pitch . <p> Advertisement <p> So why do so many Liberals find Trump such an attractive political figure ? <p> I suspect part of the answer lies in understanding how Trump has struck a hammer-blow against political correctness . For years , a left-liberal media and a political establishment stood at odds with the instincts of the general public . Many serious subjects could not be debated openly without inspiring immediate hysteria . <p> Illustration : Jim Pavlidis . <p> Climate change was one @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to raise concerns about porous borders or decarbonising the economy they were vilified in the mainstream media . As a result of the refusal of the political establishment to discuss illegal immigration or carbon regulations , and other issues of concern to ordinary voters , a giant vacuum had emerged in American public discourse . <p> Trump dared to state in public what many American people -- and many others all over the western world -- feel : that uncontrolled immigration makes ordinary people feel their national identity is being undermined and that multiculturalism seems only to intensify intolerance . When the American media entered into the spirit of denunciation in epic fashion , the result was the Trump backlash . <p> All this has led to talk that Trumpism -- an angry populist revolt against the political elites -- is destined for Australia . Do n't bet on it . <p> To be sure , populism is on the rise across Europe . From Scandinavia to France to the former Warsaw Pact states , support for nativist movements is increasing in response to sluggish economic growth , lax @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ into western society . Such trends have been exacerbated by the decline of the mainstream media and the proliferation of obnoxious social media and Internet outlets that allow populists , such as Marine Le Pen , to propose simplistic solutions to fantastically complicated problems . <p> In Australia , the circumstances are very different . Sure , Brexit and now Trump have emboldened Malcolm Turnbull 's internal critics . But remember we have not experienced a recession in a quarter century . Nor have we softened our border protection controls . <p> With continuous economic growth and public confidence in our immigration system , it is no wonder that Hanson 's support is confined to the margins of the Australian body politic . Compulsory voting also plays a role . At the federal election , One Nation won only 4 per cent of the national vote -- a far cry from Trump 's 47 -- or Marine Le Pen 's 30 per cent support . <p> The lesson here is that orderly and controlled immigration and a robust economy helps guard against nativism and protectionism .
