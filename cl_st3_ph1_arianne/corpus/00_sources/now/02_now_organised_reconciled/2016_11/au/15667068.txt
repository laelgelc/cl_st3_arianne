<p> Special interest groups have had much more influence than the wider community on the new regional plan for Southeast Queensland . A draft of the plan , ShapingSEQ , was recently released for comment . Prior input from the wider community was limited to submitting " thought bubbles " about the region without having the benefit of any report card on how the previous plan had performed . <p> This process did not accurately gauge community concerns and submitters were not a representative sample . Perhaps it gave people the feeling they 'd " had a say " . The process just as likely reinforced cynicism about government consultation . <p> The initial focus was very much on getting southeast Queensland councils to accept the need for regional planning . At that time , the role of the wider community was relatively minor . <p> A 1990 meeting of representatives from government , business , trade unions , professional groups and community organisations was an important impetus for starting the regional planning process .
