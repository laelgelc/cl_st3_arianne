<h> Trump win must not put pause on Paris accord <p> While Donald Trump 's administration will only clarify their global climate change agenda early next year , early indications are worrying , writes Edna Molewa . <p> It 's three weeks since the Paris Agreement to combat climate change came into force and the 22nd Conference of the Parties ( COP22 ) to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change ( UNFCCC ) has concluded in the Moroccan city of Marrakesh . <p> COP22 focused on operationalising the Paris Agreement by 2020 and work has progressed in a number of areas . <p> The parties emphasised the need to increase their collective efforts on the basis of science and equity . <p> With the recent presidential elections in the US , the " elephant in the room " at the Marrakesh climate change negotiations was around the US 's future participation in driving global climate action . <p> After China , the US is the second-largest contributor to global greenhouse gas emissions , mainly from burning fossil fuels . <p> The US 's Nationally Determined Contribution submitted to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ secretariat last year sets emission reduction targets of 17 percent below 2005 levels by 2020 climate change negotiations , culminating in the early entry into force of the Paris Agreement . <p> It is of concern to some now that the president-elect Donald Trump has previously made statements indicating that if he was elected he would withhold his country 's support for the Paris Agreement , and possibly even withdraw from it altogether . <p> We view these early pronouncements made by the US president-elect as serious . <p> A further possible matter of concern is that , should the US 's commitment to the Paris Agreement be annulled , both in terms of its Nationally Determined Contribution and financial support to developing countries , it may further reduce the global effort to reduce emissions . <p> First , in our analysis and our view , the way in which the Paris
