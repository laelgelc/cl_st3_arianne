Photo/Courtesy <p> Today marks end of the Second High Level Meeting ( HLM2 ) of the Global Partnership for Effective Development Cooperation ( GPEDC ) . <p> The meeting , which kicked off on Monday in Nairobi , was meant to enhance positive impact of development cooperation by taking stock of the implementation of internationally agreed principles of development cooperation . <p> In his press release before the event launch , National Treasury Cabinet secretary Henry Rotich said the meeting would provide a learning space to showcase successful examples and lessons learnt , as well as identify innovative approaches to sustainable development goals that can be scaled up . <p> The theme of the HLM2 is , " Towards inclusive and accelerated implementation of the 2030 agenda " . The GPEDC is an inclusive forum that takes place every two years and brings together development actors from governments , parliaments , international organisations , trade unions , private sector , civil society and development foundations . <p> It was created in 2011 at the fourth @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and brings together development actors around a set of four internationally agreed principles that form the foundation of effective development cooperation . <p> These include ownership by developing countries , a focus on results , inclusive partnerships and transparency and accountability between development partners . <p> As the meeting closes , it is high time that beneficiaries of foreign development assistance , particularly in Africa , honestly interrogated the effectiveness of aid received over the years . It is a case of mixed results , with some sectors having progressed , while others have not had any benefits from the billions invested in them . <p> For example Kenya has received copious amounts of financial and material support from the US and the European Union since independence . Most of the aid has been injected in programmes while the rest has been wasted through grand corruption and sheer ineptitude in matters of governance . <p> Kenya has been in almost every other buzzword in the development cooperation sphere .
