<h> 80,000 reindeer starving to death due to climate change <p> The effects of global warming are starting to take their toll on the wildlife of Yamal Peninsula in northwestern Siberia . <p> According to a recent study from the journal Biology Letters , the mortality rate of reindeer around the region has alarmingly increased in recent years due to abnormally thick layers of snow and ice in their habitat , which made it impossible to gain access to vegetation . <p> The region , which was once considered a nomadic paradise for reindeer , has seen at least 80,000 deaths in the past decade alone . <p> ADVERTISEMENT <p> With the ice getting thicker due to the worsening climate , scientists are projecting an even higher death toll in the coming years . <p> Research also showed that the icy terrain started becoming thicker and harder around 2006 , which became impenetrable even for the reindeer 's sturdy hooves . <p> For instance , the Peninsula 's temperatures plunged to -40 degrees Fahrenheit after a 24-hour rainstorm in 2013 . <p> At the same time , the region @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ or one-quarter of the local population , perished due to harsh living conditions . <p> Scientist described the phenomenon as " rain-on-snow " events ( ROS ) which is caused by fluctuating temperatures . <p> The melting ice produced high levels of evaporation and humidity which , in turn , prompted heavy bouts of rain that soaked the snowy ground below . The ROS events were then followed by a sudden dip in temperature that caused the snow to freeze . <p> Unfortunately for Siberia , scientist have also predicted that rains will become more frequent and intense in the Yama Peninsula , as Siberia continues to warm . <p> The unlikely weather changes could further worsen the ROS effects , which could threaten not just local reindeer populations but also the nearby human communities as well . <p> Due to their recent decline , the International Union for Conservation of Nature ( IUCN ) has classified the reindeer as a " vulnerable " species . <p> The IUCN has also noted a 20-percent decrease in reindeer population in Russia from 1990 .
