But hydroelectric power , in which electricity is generated by moving water , accounts for about 16 percent of the world 's electricity supply , and it 's touted as one of the most promising sources of clean energy . But the man-made bodies of water emit more methane than natural ones because the water level changes more often , and the change in pressure during draw-downs of the water stimulates bubbling . <p> " These emissions appear to be linked to the biological productivity of the system , " Bridget Deemer , a doctoral candidate at the Washington State University and the study 's lead author , said in an email . <p> These findings suggest that reservoirs sited in locations downstream of " nutrient " ( i.e. nitrogen and phosphorus ) inputs will produce more methane than those receiving fewer nutrient inputs .
