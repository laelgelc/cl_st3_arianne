<h> By <p> Lauren Carasik is the Director of the International Human Rights Clinic at Western New England University School of Law . <p> As a Native American-led opposition to a $3.7bn North Dakota pipeline that galvanised an international movement takes on increased urgency with the election of Donald Trump , repression by law enforcement response continues to escalate . <p> If completed , the 1,886km Dakota Access Pipeline ( DAPL ) would transport a half million barrels of fracked crude oil from the Bakken oil fields in North Dakota to Illinois , crossing multiple waterways including the Missouri River , that serves as the drinking water source for millions of people . <p> The Standing Rock Sioux Tribe 's organised resistance to the pipeline , which began in April as a small prayer camp , has swelled to thousands and drawn support from some 300 tribes , representing the largest Native American gathering in a generation . <p> The uprising has been joined by climate and social justice activists . Most recently , the United Nations Special Rapporteur on the rights to freedom of peaceful assembly and association denounced the excessive force deployed by law enforcement and private security , and said those detained had been kept in " inhuman and degrading conditions " . <p> International opprobrium has not tempered the law enforcement response .
