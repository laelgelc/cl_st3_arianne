<h> Examining ' responsible development ' of ANWR <p> FAIRBANKS -- Next time you hear the words " responsible development , " ask some questions . Ask to whom or what the development will be responsible . Ask how far that responsibility will extend . Ask what about this particular project will make it more responsible than other comparable projects . <p> There has been a lot of use of the word responsible in connection with development in the news recently . The Pebble Partnerhship is assuring us they will develop a " responsible " plan for the mine . Alaska 's delegation to Congress repeatedly use the mantra that oil and gas development in the coastal plain of the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge will be " responsible development . " The U.S. Secretary of the Interior just announced the " next step for responsibly developing the National Outer Continental Shelf Oil and Gas Leasing Program for 2019-2024 . " <p> Insisting that development will be responsible allows everyone to walk away feeling good about the future . People who would never have supported that development before can now feel comfortable . People who live far away will sleep easier knowing that only responsible development will take place in those remote pristine locations they value . Native communities will not be impacted . But , should we be so complacent ? Should anyone actually believe that if responsible is hyphenated with development everything will be OK ? <p> I worked in heavy industry for 25 years , and I will call bovine excrement when I see it . We believe this nonsensical rhetoric about " responsible development " at our peril . The question is not whether development is done responsibly ; it already is . The question is whether it should take place at all . In the case of ANWR , for many years that was the focus of the argument , and those who wanted to preserve the refuge for its intended purpose were the winners because they had the better position . The refuge is one of those places ; the addition of responsibility has not changed that . <p> Citizens behaving responsibly do so in response to something : law , custom , social norms , a duty to family or community . We have a moral or legal obligation to behave correctly toward something or in respect of it . Often , we behave responsibly when nothing actually requires us to do so ; we may return lost items or assist strangers because we believe it is the right thing to do , and not doing so would be irresponsible . How does this translate into responsibility for an industrial developer ; to whom or what are they held responsible and who is doing the holding ? Developers are held responsible to laws and regulations of the jurisdiction where they are developing . How strictly they will be called upon to comply will depend on the strength of the rule of law in that jurisdiction . Corporations may be involved in corporate social responsibility , but when @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is required for legal compliance and no more . That is simply good business . <p> Corporations are responsible to laws and to their shareholders . Responsibility equals liability , which equals expense . When there is an accident , the first thing they do is try to limit their liability . Responsibility is decided in the courts , and there are plenty of examples of corporations fighting back to reduce their responsibility including here in Alaska with the Exxon Valdez . <p> Being responsible does not mean there will be no accidents and no pollution ; it simply means that companies abide by the rules which we must hope they are already doing . Before we do away with industry regulations , remember that regulations are holding companies responsible . Reduce regulations , and you lower the bar for responsibility . <p> Responsible does not equal small ; large developments can still be conducted responsibly . ANWR development wo n't reduce the impacts of climate change . It will add to pollution resulting from the use of fossil fuels .
