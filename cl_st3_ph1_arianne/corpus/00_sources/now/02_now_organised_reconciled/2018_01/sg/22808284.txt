<p> SINGAPORE - Last week 's cool spell was the longest in the Republic for at least a decade , weather experts said on Thursday ( Jan 18 ) . <p> " In the last 10 years , there was no cold spell of five days or more with minimum temperatures between 21 deg C and 22 deg C , " the Meteorological Service Singapore ( MSS ) told The Straits Times . <p> " Over this period , most monsoon surges that affect Singapore are of short duration lasting two to three days . " <p> The five days of cool weather , which lasted from Wednesday to Sunday , delighted many locals and was a result of a monsoon surge . <p> Monsoon surges , which are common from December to March , are sudden increases in wind speed , which bring in cool air from the winter chill in the northern hemisphere . <p> As this cold air moves south , it warms and gathers moisture , resulting in rain over the equatorial region , including in Singapore . <p> Last week 's surge led to five @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to break out their winter wear and spurring heart-warming acts of kindness . Restaurant owner Francis Ng , 44 , for example , bought blankets to distribute to seniors who sleep on the streets . <p> Satellite images showed that wind speeds picked up significantly last Wednesday as temperatures in Singapore fell to 22.8 deg C. The surge continued until Sunday , when temperatures in Jurong West and Admiralty dropped to a low of 21.2 deg C - the lowest temperature recorded in Singapore since 2016 . <p> The mean daily temperature range for January is between 24 deg C and 30 deg C , according to the Met Station 's long-term climate records dating from 1982 . <p> Such cool spells could become more frequent due to climate change , the MSS spokesman said . Projections for 2100 made by MSS ' Centre for Climate Research Singapore show that there could be more rain from cold surges during the north-east monsoon season . <p> " This indicates a possibility of more intense surges with higher risk of extreme weather , " the spokesman added . <p> Experts have warned @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as the world warms . This made 2017 the warmest year on record that was not influenced by El Nino - a weather phenomenon associated with hot and dry weather in this part of the world . <p> While the mean annual temperature in 2017 was lower than the 28.4 deg C in 2016 , and 28.3 deg C in 2015 , those two years were influenced by El Nino . <p> Very warm days were also experienced in Singapore last year despite it not being an El Nino year , with the normally cool months of January and December also seeing warmer than usual temperatures on some days , the MSS said . <p> Heavy rain from intense thunderstorms caused flash floods and fallen trees .
