<h> Concern over climate change linked to depression , anxiety : Study <p> NEW YORK ( REUTERS ) - Depression and anxiety afflict Americans who are concerned with the fate of the environment , according to a study of the mental health effects of climate change . <p> Most hard-hit are women and people with low incomes who worry about the planet 's long-term health , said the study published this week in the journal Global Environmental Change . <p> Symptoms include restless nights , feelings of loneliness and lethargy . <p> " Climate change is a persistent global stressor , " said Sabrina Helm , lead author of the paper and professor of family and consumer sciences at the University of Arizona . <p> Risks to mental health from climate change are a " creeping development " , she told the Thomson Reuters Foundation . <p> Due to climate change , scientists predict sea levels are on track to surge as temperatures rise , posing threats such as deadly heat , extreme weather and land swallowed by rising water . <p> World leaders mobilised to curb man-made greenhouse gas @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ although the United States has since said it would withdraw from the landmark deal . <p> Signs of depression do not appear in people concerned about climate change 's risks to humanity but do appear in people worried about its impact on other species , plants and nature overall , the research said . <p> The study pulled from 342 online surveys of respondents whose views broadly reflect the wider US population , it said . <p> Experts have looked at ways extreme weather such as hurricanes and floods , whose intensity has increased due to climate change , can cause mental health issues such as post-traumatic stress disorder , it said . <p> But little research has looked into anxiety arising from climate change as an everyday concern , the study said . <h> The Straits Times <p> We have been experiencing some problems with subscriber log-ins and apologise for the inconvenience caused .
