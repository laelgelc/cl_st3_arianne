<h> With good quality bikes , dedicated lanes , and efficient system , bike sharing is seen as a viable option for Indian cities . <h> Share this story <p> A handful of Indian cities are gearing up to launch bike-sharing systems to ease traffic congestion and deadly air pollution , as urban populations surge and vehicles clog the streets . <p> Bike sharing systems in the southern city of Mysuru and the central city of Bhopal , both launched last June , have met with a tremendous response , officials say . <p> About half a dozen more cities , including Bengaluru , Pune and Bhubaneswar , are now drawing up similar plans , according to the World Resources Institute ( WRI ) , a Washington D.C.-based research organisation . <p> " Bike sharing is a viable option for Indian cities - we just need good quality bikes , dedicated lanes and a system that is efficient , " said Chandramauli Shukla , chief executive of Bhopal Smart City Corporation . <p> The corporation implements the federally-funded Smart Cities programme to improve services like internet connectivity and public transport @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and a large young population , passenger vehicle ownership in India has nearly tripled in the last decade , government data shows . Gridlock has become common and transit systems struggle to keep pace . <p> Vehicle emissions account for almost a third of air pollution in India , which has nearly half the world 's 20 most polluted cities , according to the World Health Organization . <p> Some 2.5 million people in the country die every year due to pollution . <p> Mysuru , close to the technology hub of Bengaluru , was the first to introduce a bike-sharing programme , with the state 's chief minister riding a yellow Trin Trin bike at the launch . <p> Registered users can borrow any of 450 bikes from 48 docking stations for a nominal fee . <p> In Bhopal , users can register on their smartphones and unlock one of 500 bikes from 60 docking stations . <p> The city now has 11 kilometres ( 6.8 miles ) of dedicated bike lanes , according to WRI , which provided technical expertise to the Bhopal Municipal Corporation on the bike-sharing @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ first few months , half of them women , said Amit Bhatt , director of transport at WRI India . <p> " In India , people look down on those who bike .
