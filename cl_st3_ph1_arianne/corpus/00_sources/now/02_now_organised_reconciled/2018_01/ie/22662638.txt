<h> We should place food and nutrition at the heart of our foreign policy <p> Irish Ambassadors from around the globe will meet in Dublin next week to strategise on the direction of our foreign policy post-Brexit . They will plan for changing Irish relationships with the UK , EU-26 and the wider international community , and identify opportunities for Irish influence at European and international level . <p> It is difficult for a small country to carve out a distinctive policy position and to achieve influence internationally . But it can be done through combining credible domestic policies , high-level political advocacy and partnering with like-minded countries and institutions . <p> For example , Ireland has played an internationally recognised leadership role on policy for global food and nutrition security within the past decade . It was based on the government implementing the recommendations of the Hunger Task Force , and engaging in high-level political advocacy . Policy consistency was illustrated when Irish policy remained the same through a change of government in 2011 . <p> The political benefits accruing from Irish leadership were seen in a series of partnerships with the UN , the EU , the UK and US governments . <h> Lead role <p> In looking to the future Ireland should seek to continue its lead role in fighting global undernutrition . But the focus should broaden through placing food and nutrition at the heart of Irish foreign policy in a more strategic , long-term and whole-of-government approach . <p> The changing nature of malnutrition should also be recognised .
