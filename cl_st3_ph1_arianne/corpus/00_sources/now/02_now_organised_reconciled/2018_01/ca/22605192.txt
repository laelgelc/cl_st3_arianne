CLIFFORD SKARSTEDT/PETERBOROUGH EXAMINER <p> A change to Douro-Dummer Township 's garbage pickup has some residents questioning what 's considered trash . <p> As of Tuesday , the township changed its rules on the type of garbage bags residents can use . <p> Trash will only be picked up if it 's in a regular-sized , clear plastic garbage bag , as opposed to the green or black bags previously accepted . <p> The switch is meant to deter residents from sending recycling and compostable items to the Bensfort Rd. landfill . <p> Garbage regulations remain mostly the same otherwise , save for a decrease in bag weight from 50 to 40 pounds . Residents can still put out one " free bag " and purchase stickers for up to two additional bags for $3 a piece . <p> Residents are also allowed to toss out one opaque privacy bag - the size of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ questions asked . Anyone who might need more than one privacy bag because of health-related needs is asked to contact the township . <p> Dave Clifford , Douro-Dummer Township 's CAO , said there were a number of reasons for the change , all with the environment in mind . <p> An audit of garbage bags done in the township by the county revealed 70 per cent of bag contents were filled with recyclables or compost . <p> " The results of those audits have been a little bit disturbing , " Clifford said . <p> The township also adopted a climate change action plan , committed to reducing green house gas emissions . <p> " One way of doing that is reducing waste , especially organics that go to land fills . Organics create methane gas , which is a main contributor to green house gases , " said Clifford . <p> The weight of waste collected in the township increased by 100 tonnes in 2016 , despite a consecutive decline in previous years . <p> The township held a public information meeting in November to fill residents in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the mail , used social media and its website to share the news and posted notices in municipal buildings . <p> Many residents are all for a change to help the environment , but seem to be a bit baffled by some of the logistics . <p> Sue Guest , who lives on two acres with her husband , said the change seems extreme and abrupt . <p> " I do n't think we 've been prepared properly for this , " Guest said . <p> She also said she does n't know exactly what she is and is n't allowed to throw out . <p> " My biggest concern is that I do n't know what I can put in the garbage and if they do n't pick it up what do we do with it ?
