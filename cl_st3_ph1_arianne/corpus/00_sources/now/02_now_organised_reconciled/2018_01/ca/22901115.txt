This year , the World Economic Forum 's luck seems to have run @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ down for the past six days , burying the town in 182 centimetres of snow , 91 centimetres of it in the last two days alone . Snow was still falling fast Monday night , and the steep , pine-dotted slopes were so heavily laden that some neighbourhoods here in Davos had to be evacuated for fear of avalanches . <p> Head-high snow drifts quickly piled up along the roads , leaving no place for street plows to push more snow . Sidewalks completely disappeared . Pedestrians slipped and slid in traffic between huge trucks and luxury minivans on streets carpeted with compacted ice several inches thick . <p> Davos is not alone in getting clobbered with snow in Switzerland this winter . Just two weeks ago , 13,000 tourists were stranded at the foot of the Matterhorn by heavy snow and rain . But the timing of the snowfall -- on the eve of the World Economic Forum 's annual conference -- has had an outsize impact . <p> By Sunday night , heavy snow had already blocked the rail line through the Alps from Zurich , and villages @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ alert . Swiss Rail began unloading Davos-bound passengers from their express trains , taking them on a half-hour bus trip on back roads around the blockage and then loading them onto a crowded red commuter train that ran the rest of the way into Davos . <p> " What shall we do , " the mayor of Davos , Tarzisius Caviezel , said at a news conference Monday , admitting he was at a loss for how to respond . " It 's impossible . " <p> There was too much snow to find places to put it in the narrow , steep-sloped valley , he explained , and no easy way to take it anywhere else . That throngs of uber-wealthy conference goers and their entourages were trying to push their way toward Davos did not help .
