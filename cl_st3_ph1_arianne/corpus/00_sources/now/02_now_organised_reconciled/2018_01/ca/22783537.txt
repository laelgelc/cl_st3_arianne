<p> In town for a public talk on growth and intensification , Vancouver-based urban planner Brent Toderian was quick to point out what he does n't like about the city 's downtown . <p> On a social media account , Toderian posted photographs of the buildings on Block D along Ontario Street and the portion of Confederation Harbour at the foot of Johnson Street . <p> " Previous generations did n't make good design decisions for #KingstonOn 's waterfront , " he wrote . " Slab buildings boxing it in , blank walls , fences &amp; parking along the building edge at-grade . My hotel view does n't make a good first impression , but lots to see &amp; learn today . #urbandiagnosis . " <p> With 25 years of urban planning and design , Toderian 's opinions about such things carry more weight than most . <p> An award-winning planning expert , Toderian has worked with city governments around the world to solve their planning problems , including Buenos Aires , Medellin and New York . <p> He was the chief planner for Vancouver for six years and in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ design . <p> While working for the City of Vancouver , Toderian worked on initiatives including new waterfront and skyline-shaping strategies , multi-modal mobility and active transportation , and next generation transit-oriented development -- policies designed to create a " complete city , " not just a livable one , as his website explains . <p> On Wednesday night , he is to speak at a special town hall meeting about growth and intensification at Memorial Hall in City Hall at 6 p.m . <p> On Friday , Toderian will be among the guest speakers at the Sustainable Kingston Climate Change Symposium at the Grand Theatre , from noon to 8 p.m.
