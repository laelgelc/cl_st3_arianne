( Marine Animal Response Society ) <p> By Star Editorial Board <p> Tues. , Jan. 2 , 2018 <p> The catastrophic die-off of right whales in the Gulf of St. Lawrence in 2017 has presented Canadian policymakers with a stark choice : take decisive steps now to save this spectacular species or be complicit in its likely extinction . <p> Between April and November of last year , 16 right whales were found dead , 12 of them in Canadian waters . Only about 450 of the mammals remain . The startling death @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in a recent feature , represents more than 3.5 per cent of the North Atlantic right whale 's total population , " the equivalent of 1.25 million Canadians suddenly dying in the span of seven months . " <p> The problem , it seems , is that the whales are migrating to the highly trafficked Gulf of St. Lawrence at an unprecedented rate , their range expanding probably due in part to climate change . In the Gulf , right whales are more likely to encounter mortal threats : collisions with commercial ships and entanglements in fishing gear . <p> In August , in response to the spate of deaths , Ottawa asked ship operators to reduce the speed of vessels in parts of the Gulf where whales are frequently sighted . Fisheries Minister Dominic Leblanc also suggested he would consider rerouting shipping lanes away from feeding grounds . <p> These are sensible measures . Similar programs to save right whales in the Bay of Fundy and the Roseway Basin successfully reduced deaths there a number of years ago . But for these to work , and for Leblanc to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ protection to bear " to safeguard this rare and precious species , more must be done . <p> Article Continued Below <p> For instance , as many researchers have argued , investments must be made now to study the whales ' shifting migratory patterns . Without more precise information about where right whales go and when , new shipping regulations will inevitably fall short . <p> Then there 's the tricky issue of whale-ensnaring fishing gear . Entanglements are not only occasionally deadly for right whales ; they have also been shown to increase stress and thus decrease mating . The lucrative lobster , crab and other fisheries in the Gulf should not be shut down lightly , but short of such extreme measures , Ottawa must invest aggressively in the development and testing of new fishing technologies researchers say may be an important part of the solution . <p> Finally , the government should put an end once and for all to misguided plans for oil and gas exploration in the Gulf of St. Lawrence . <p> The Harper government opened the door for possible offshore drilling in the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that underlined the area 's energy potential and made it easier for oil companies to explore for petroleum resources . <p> For their part , the Trudeau Liberals have allowed the process to continue by permitting exploration protected areas and allowing for the extension of exploration licences . <p> This is a terrible idea . The Gulf is a semi-enclosed estuary , meaning the damage from any spill would not easily dissipate . It would instead reach five coastlines , affecting land and livelihoods in all provinces touching on the Gulf . <p> And even short of a spill , the seismic method used to explore for oil and gas carries its own risks . It would involve sending sound waves into layers of underwater rock to determine the best spot for drilling . Scientists warn that can seriously disrupt fish and marine mammals . <p> Ottawa should follow the example of Quebec , which recently cancelled plans for oil and gas exploration on Anticosti Island in the Gulf . <p> In Canada , by virtue of our vast wilderness , three oceans and enormous holdings of fresh water , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ reports suggest we are failing in this regard . It is not an exaggeration to say that , in this way , we are endangering our food and water , the health of our economy and the sustainability of our planet . <p> The case of the right whale presents a clear and consequential test of our commitment to conserve .
