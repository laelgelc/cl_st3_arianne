<h> Cumbrian farmer attends trailblazing conference for launch of farming with nature group <p> A CUMBRIAN farmer is attending a national conference which aims to lead the way in encouraging conservation through farming . <p> David Corrie-Close , of The Horned Beef Company based near Ulverston , is part of a group of more than 100 farmers launching the Nature Friendly Farming Network ( NFFN ) . <p> The launch is taking place in Oxford , at the Real Farming Conference . <p> Mr Corrie-Close said : " I joined this conference because I wanted there to be a group of farmers with a strong voice to debate as we approach the discussion post-Brexit agricultural policy . <p> " The farmers in Cumbria need to be represented . It is important to get involved at an early stage , and I hope to encourage other farmers to get involved as well . " <p> The NFFN is calling on the UK and devolved governments to create a post-Brexit framework that will help farmers restore British wildlife , reverse declines in soil quality and help manage the impacts of climate change @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Mr Corrie-Close said : " I have been farming in Cumbria for a while now , and I know a lot of other farmers , as well as environmentalists . <p> " People often think that these two groups are at odds with one another , but that really is n't the case . <p> " Farmers grow up in these amazing landscapes , and want to help look after the places where they live . <p> " But this is not done by rewilding the area . We do n't want farmers out on the street because they ca n't farm anymore . <p> " There are farming practices which can help with biodiversity , provide homes for wildlife and improve water quality . It is not just about food production . " <p> Mr Corrie-Close stressed that the group was not claiming to have a list of specific actions that farmers must take . He acknowledged that ' nature friendly farming ' can mean different things on different farms , as the land varies . <p> He also highlighted the potential for new farming policy implemented post-Brexit @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> " The way farming is at the moment is not the fault of the individual , but the fault of the system . Farmers need to come together to work on a new system that works better for us . <p> " This is therefore not just about farming to promote wildlife . It is also about a future where farming businesses thrive .
