<p> Plastic is one of the biggest threats to the future of coral reefs after ocean warming , say scientists . <p> More than 11 billion items of plastic were found on a third of coral reefs surveyed in the Asia-Pacific region . <p> This figure is predicted to increase to more than 15 billion by 2025 . <p> Plastic raises by 20-fold the risk of disease outbreaks on coral reefs , according to research . Plastic bags , bottles and rice sacks were among the items found . <p> " Plastic is one of the biggest threats in the ocean at the moment , I would say , apart from climate change , " said Dr Joleah Lamb of Cornell University in Ithaca , US . <p> " It 's sad how many pieces of plastic there are in the coral reefs ... if we can start targeting those big polluters of plastic , hopefully we can start reducing the amount that is going on to these reefs . Branching or finger-like forms of corals are most likely to get entangled in plastic debris . <p> These are important habitats for fish and fisheries , the scientists say . <p> " A lot of times we come across big rice sacks or draping plastic bags , " said Dr Lamb , who led the study . <p> " What we do find is these corals with a lot of complexity like branches and finger-like corals will become eight times more likely to be entangled in these types of plastics . " <p> In the study , published in the journal Science , international researchers surveyed more than 150 reefs from four countries in the Asia-Pacific region between 2011 and 2014 . <p> Plastic was found on one-third of the coral reefs surveyed . Reefs near Indonesia were loaded with most plastic , while Australian reefs showed the lowest concentration . Coral bleaching is caused by unusually warm water . Coral polyps loose algae from their tissues , which drains them of their colour . They may recover if temperature changes are reversed in a reasonably short time , but this process can take many years . <p> In the case of diseases , organisms attack coral , leading to likely death . Previous research has found that plastic debris can stress coral through blocking out light and oxygen , thereby giving pathogens a chance to take hold . <p> Based on projections of plastic waste going into the ocean , the researchers suggest that the number of plastic items snagged on Asia-Pacific corals may increase from 11.1 billion to 15.7 billion plastic items by 2025 . <p> An estimated 4.8 to 12.7 million tonnes of plastic waste enter the ocean in a single year . <p> More than three-quarters of this plastic is thought to originate on land .
