<h> Experts blame lack of winter rain for prolonged cold spell <p> PATNA : Weather experts have attributed the prolonged spell of severe cold in Bihar to lack of winter rainfall in the region . " The amount of winter rainfall ( November- February ) in the state has gradually declined over the last few years , " said Bihar State Action Plan on Climate Change member Pradhan Parth Sarthi who has done a study on the rainfall in the state in December from 2000 to 2016 . <p> The study noted the rainfall was 21mm in 2002 , 36mm in 2003 and 58mm in 2009 . The remaining years saw no rainfall , while there has been no rainfall in the current winter season as well . <p> The weather scientist ascribed the lack of showers to reduced frequency of western disturbances in the Indo- Gangetic plains due to the impact of climate change . <p> " Western disturbances originate in the Caspian Sea or Mediterranean Sea and gradually travel across the middle-east from Iran , Afghanistan and Pakistan to enter the Indian sub-continent . They lead to rise in temperatures and fall in surface pressure as well as high , medium and low clouds , " Parth Sarthi said . <p> According to him , western disturbances produce snowfall in high-altitude regions like J&amp;K , Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand . They also cause winter rainfall in Punjab , Haryana , UP and Delhi as well as the Gangetic plains of Bihar and northeast India . <p> " The frequency of western disturbances is the highest in January when 5-7 such systems affect the weather in this region . However , not a single western disturbance has passed through the region this season as a result of which the weather conditions comprised only ' cold day ' and fog , " Parth Sarthi said . <p> A Patna Meteorological Centre scientist nodded . " Absence of western disturbances is one of the reasons for the prolonged spell of severe cold conditions , " he said . <p> Winter rainfall is also important for Rabi crops , especially wheat .
