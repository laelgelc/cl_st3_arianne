<p> The extreme heat at the open has been a hotly debated topic all week as temperatures have soared close to 40degC at Melbourne Park . <p> Dr Liz Hanna of the Climate Change Institute at the Australian National University and the Climate and Health Alliance told news.com.au " someone could die " if players pushed their bodies too far in extreme conditions . <p> " The temperatures players face are a lot hotter than recorded and we know with tennis players - particularly when they 're playing at their full capacity - their muscles are generating an enormous amount of additional heat , " she told news.com.au . <p> " They 're at very high risk of overheating and that is potentially lethal . The worst case scenario is that somebody would succumb to severe injury . " <p> The Australian Open has been no stranger to extreme heat throughout the years . <p> In 2016 , players were taken off the courts during the opening round of qualifiers , while Canadian Frank Dancevic complained about the open 's " inhuman " conditions in 2014.
