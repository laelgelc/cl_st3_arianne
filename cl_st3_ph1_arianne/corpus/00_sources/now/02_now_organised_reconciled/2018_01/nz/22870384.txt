<h> Statistics House built on known hazard area <p> Wellington 's doomed Statistics House sat amid swathes of specifically designated " hazard " areas across the city , prone to high shaking in earthquakes . <p> While the failure of Stats House in the 2016 Kaikoura quake was blamed on various factors , an official information response from Greater Wellington Regional Council shows that it had been a known hazard area for about a decade before ground was broken at the CentrePort site . <p> A 2004 Wellington City Council land use consent for construction of the building shows the site was then designated a hazard -- ground shaking -- area . <p> SUPPLIED <p> Greater Wellington Regional Council 's maps show where in Wellington - marked in red - has the highest ground-shaking risk . <p> It appears this description came from a 1992 investigation by Greater Wellington Regional Council . <p> The investigation gave the same designation to large tracts of the city -- including the shoreline from Queens Wharf to Oriental Bay , and parts of Te Aro , Kilbirnie , Lower Hutt , Wainuiomata , and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ after the Kaikoura quake . ( file photo ) <p> These were identified as " zone five " areas -- characterised by " high to very high amplification of earthquake ground motion , relative to bedrock , and therefore subject to the greatest ground shaking hazard " . <p> Greater Wellington environment manager Nigel Corry said the Stats House land had " high ground shaking potential " . <p> " What this means is that the ground there has a higher risk from amplified ground motions due to the underlying soils and sub-surface geology . <p> JOHN NICHOLSON/STUFF <p> The eastern Wellington suburb of Kilbirnie is also a high ground shake area . <p> " In this situation , loose unconsolidated marine sediments , gravels and other fill was used in the reclamation of the port land from the late 19th to the mid-20th century . " <p> The hazard ratings of the Stats House area , and others around Wellington with the same designation , had not changed since the 1990s . <p> " It is not recommended that the information be used for engineering and construction purposes . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ further investigation in any proposed development . " <p> The underlying geology and the potential seismic hazards at Statistics House were recognised and foundations were designed accordingly . <p> A Ministry of Business , Innovation , and Employment ( MBIE ) report into the failure of the building found four causes -- flexible frames , floor construction , up to two minutes of significant shaking , and localised amplification of the shaking . <p> " The site conditions were investigated and the geotechnical advice provided was generally in accordance with the standard of practice for this type of building at the time , " the report said . <p> The report shows the land was reclaimed between 1893 and 1901 . That included three to four metres of fill -- mostly sand and gravel , on top of up to 6m of natural sand and silt , below which was " natural colluvium " of loose gravel , sand and silt mixture . <p> According to the regional council , other areas around Wellington with high potential for " amplified ground shaking " include the coast from Queens Wharf to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Aro , the Kilbirnie isthmus from Evans to Lyall Bay , central Miramar , and Seatoun . <p> " Outside of Wellington City , much of Lower Hutt , Wainuiomata and the Porirua CBD are built on loose , unconsolidated sediments at risk from amplified ground shaking and liquefaction . " <p> Wellington City councillor Iona Pannett , who has the infrastructure portfolio , said there was no going back on where Wellington had been developed . <p> But information now available on quake risk and climate change meant more care needed to be taken with where was developed in the future , she said .
