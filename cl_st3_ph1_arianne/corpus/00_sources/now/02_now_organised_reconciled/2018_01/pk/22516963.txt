<h> Mushahidullah asks Sindh gov to increase forest cover to 10-12% <p> ISLAMABAD : Federal Minister for Climate Change , Senator Mushahidullah Khan has asked the Sindh government to make efforts on the urgent basis to increase forest cover to at least 10-12 per cent to mitigate the impacts of climate change-induced disasters , particularly floods and heat wave , which show increasing trends in the province . <p> " Sindh government must wake up to the current state of forest cover of 2 per cent and make efforts on the urgent basis to increase it to at least 10-12 per cent to mitigate the impacts of climate change-induced disasters , particularly floods and heat wave , which show increasing trends in the province " he said in a statement . <p> He said , " Without such forest restoration , conservation and protection efforts , the province 's climate vulnerability is destined to aggravate in full gear and increase the pace of recurring floods , coastal cyclones , dusty storms , heat waves , jack up public health costs , further increase environmental degradation and deepen poverty , " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ rising poverty , out-migration of a huge number of rural people to cities , unchecked ecological destruction , vanishing wildlife has been further exacerbated by the paced systematic deforestation over last 30 years now , which now must be brought to an end with strong political and policy interventions to overcome these environmental challenges for the provincial government to boost environmental and health developments , " . <p> According to various reports , Sindh province 's total area of 14.091 million hectares , the province 's reverie forests have declined to 0.05 million hectares ( 0.35 per cent ) , irrigated forests to 0.082 million hectares ( 0.14 per cent ) , and mangrove forests to 0.2 million hectares ( 1.41 per cent ) till October 2017 . However , the forest cover of Sindh has reduced to 1.90 per cent from 5.11 per cent . <p> According to the government data , massive deforestation in Sindh took place from 1979 to 2010 .
