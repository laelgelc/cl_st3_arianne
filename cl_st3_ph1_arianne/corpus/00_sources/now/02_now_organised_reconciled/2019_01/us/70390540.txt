<h> Missed opportunity at the State of the City <h> Sunday <p> Jan 6 , 2019 at 12:01 AM <p> Eugene Mayor Lucy Vinis delivered her State of the City address on Thursday , and it reminded us of grandma 's whipped cream and fruit dessert . There were a few substantive nuggets , but they were buried in too many sweet platitudes and airy talking points . <p> First , however , credit where it 's due . We applaud Vinis for holding her address at an event free and open to the public . The same goes for Springfield Mayor Christine Lundberg who will give her state of the city this week . Some Oregon mayors -- in Portland , Corvallis and Bend , for example -- opt to give their addresses before private clubs with paid admission . All residents deserve an opportunity to hear the annual update from the top elected official , so kudos to Eugene and Springfield for being better . <p> Credit also to the council and mayor for approving an additional $8.6 million for more police , a day center for the homeless and outreach to at-risk youth , which Vinis @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ public safety and some of Eugene 's most vulnerable residents . <p> But that was last year . What about 2019 ? <p> Vinis identified four priorities for the year ahead : homelessness and housing , climate resiliency , public engagement and government accountability , and inclusiveness . <p> Housing and homelessness <p> Vinis was disappointingly vague on what she would do about homelessness , the community 's most pressing issue . She pointed to a soon-to-be-released report that " will help us build a shelter that integrates and strengthens our complete system of services for the unhoused . " But how specifically she plans to pull that off went unsaid . <p> The consultants who wrote the preliminary report recommended building more low-barrier shelter beds . The report did n't explain where the money should come from , how to choose a location , who will run it nor any other details that make the act of building a shelter much harder than just talking about building a shelter . The city held a few workshops and is talking about increased density , but actual action remains elusive . <p> " The continued suffering in our midst is unconscionable . Failure to act is not a choice , " the mayor said , but that is the choice the city repeatedly has made , and there was no clear sign that Vinis is ready to choose something else . <p> Climate resiliency <p> As with housing , the mayor has goals but not substantive plans .
