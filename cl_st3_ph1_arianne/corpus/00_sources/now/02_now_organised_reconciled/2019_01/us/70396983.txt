The federal Liberal government claims that its plan to impose a carbon tax on provinces that do not price carbon is the effective way to fight climate change . It chastises non-complying provinces for being unconcerned about the environment and having no plan to reduce emissions . <p> This is a false dichotomy . We believe the federal approach is far too inflexible . Ottawa should recognize that other potential schemes can achieve emission reductions . However , a carbon tax controls prices but not emissions directly -- it therefore lacks the ability to ensure that a target can be reached . Cap-and-trade systems , meanwhile , are in essence regulations . They 're imposed on large emitters to achieve a target , enabling those who can not achieve targeted reductions to buy credits from those who can . While carbon pricing is looked upon as a substitute for regulations and subsidies , it has instead been laid atop existing and planned regulations and subsidies . <p> Climate-energy economist Mark Jaccard has provided hard data that show that existing carbon pricing " is doing little to decarbonize the economy . In British Columbia , for example , flex-regs were three times more effective than the carbon tax in reducing emissions -- and produced no opposition . <p> At moderate levels carbon taxes produce limited emission reductions , necessitating the imposition of regulations as well . However , increasing carbon taxes means that people must pay substantially more for gasoline and power , virtual necessities in a vast , cold country like Canada . Yes , supporters argue that carbon taxes can be revenue neutral if the increased costs are offset by equivalent tax reductions . But while governments may initially reduce business and personal taxes to offset higher carbon costs , they can subsequently abandon the tax-reduction part of the plan , as happened in B.C. A better approach is to develop an alternative federal-provincial climate plan based on the spirit of co-operative federalism , where both levels of government work together to achieve meaningful change . Co-operative federalism has resulted in many important achievements , like the 1990s Canada Pension Plan ( CPP ) reform .
