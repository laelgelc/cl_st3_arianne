Social Security was designed as a program to support those unable to work , but over time its spending came to be dominated by payments to retirees . Medicaid was intended for the poor , but now mostly pays for medical care for lower-income older Americans . In 2018 , the uninsured rate for Americans overall was 8.8 percent , but for Americans aged 26 to 34 , it was 16.2 percent . Medicare for all would represent a much larger expansion of health insurance among young adults than among older adults - with a corresponding increase in costs . My favorite theory - and that 's all it is - is that they are payback for decades of pro-elderly monetary policy . <p> If you bought a house or started investing in stocks and bonds in the late 1970s or early ' 80s , you probably did pretty well . At the time interest rates were high ; at their peak in October 1981 , the average home mortgage rate was 18.5 percent . High interest rates tend to push down the value of the underlying asset . So while interest payments on homes may have been exorbitant , the principle was relatively small . <p> These high interest rates were designed to reduce inflation , and they worked . As inflation fell , so did interest rates . In response , asset prices @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ stocks , bonds and other assets that Americans of that generation bought - went up in value . And , with new lower interest rates , many homeowners could refinance and save even more . <p> Meanwhile , those low interest rates made student debt look less daunting , so many states allowed their university systems to raise tuition . ( In many cases , the rationale was that full-paying students would subsidize aid for lower-income students . ) All those paying customers radically changed incentives for universities . Instead of focusing on academics , they spent lavishly on administrative staff , campus renovations and amenities , largely in an effort to improve the student experience .
