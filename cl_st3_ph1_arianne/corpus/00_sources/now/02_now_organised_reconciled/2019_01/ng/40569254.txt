<h> The Struggle For Water Is A Battle For Life <p> The human right to water and sanitation was formally recognised by the United Nations General Assembly on July 20 2010 , through Resolution 64/292 . The resolution explicitly stated , " The human right to water entitles everyone to sufficient , safe , acceptable , physically accessible and affordable water for personal and domestic uses . " <p> With that in mind , we can not overemphasise the fact that access to potable water remains a common challenge to all humans on this Blue Planet , irrespective of race or locational space . Just as some persons , governments and corporations deny that climate change is caused mostly by human activities , so is the case with the water crisis . Rather than seeing the crisis as a mark of abdication of responsibility by governments , crisis entrepreneurs see this as an opportunity to further block public access to water through privatisation . Privatised water is only accessible to those that can afford it . It eliminates the critical need to provide water as a right . <p> The campaign against privatisation of water has recorded notable milestones @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ A key highpoint is that the ' Our Water , Our Right ' movement has grown and continues to grow , including through the growing synergy between labour and civil society organisations in resisting water privatisation . In addition , the invigoration of grassroots power has been instrumental to the stunting of moves towards privatisation of water in Lagos . The ' Our Water , Our Right ' coalition stood firm in opposing anti-people sections of the Lagos Environmental Law . The media has remained a strong ally of the movement and this is very much appreciated . <p> The arguments for privatisation of social services , public entities and the commons have remained basically the same over the past decades . The mantra is that public institutions can not function efficiently or effectively . For some of us who have been around from that time , we can attest that the arguments are fatuous and that the transfer of public utilities from public to private pockets ( PPP ) is just a means of dispossession of the public to feed the insatiable appetite of controllers of capital . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that the institutions and services to be privatised are dwindling , the drive is now to grab the very basis of live , the privatisation of water and even whole ecosystems through land and sky grabbing . <p> The pro-privatisation forces and their allies , especially the World Bank and its International Financial Corporation ( IFC ) , have become more daring in the face of rejection of peddled mythic , " PPP success stories . " In their desperate moves in this direction , a huge delegation of World Bank Execs visited Governor Ambode of Lagos in May 2018 expressing readiness to do business , including " water business , " which literally translates to trading with our human rights and blocking access to life for millions of our compatriots . <p> We note that The IFC has announced a Memorandum of Understanding ( MOU ) to provide Lagos State " advisory services " that asphyxiate all the sectors including power , transportation , municipal waste , health , education , and energy efficiency . " Responding to the grand offer , the outgoing governor of Lagos declared , " We welcome IFC 's support and are confident that signing this memorandum will translate into viable infrastructure projects , expertise building and better access to financing for the benefit of all Lagosians . " If this partnership works as well as the World Bank 's Structural Adjustment Programmes ( SAPs ) did , then the " benefit " referred to by the governor will in reality be a huge deficit . <p> At the national level , the Nigeria Integrated Water Resources Management Commission Bill before the National Assembly tilts heavily towards experimenting with privatisation and does not see water as a human right . The bill has been touted by some lawmakers as a plank needed by the World Bank to supply finance for water-related projects . The bill takes rivers and other water bodies from their place as commons and secures only the rights of licensees ( read businesses ) to these resources .
