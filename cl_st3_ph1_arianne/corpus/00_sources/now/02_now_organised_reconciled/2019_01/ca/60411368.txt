<h> ' People feel the sense of urgency and want to take action , ' says Peepeekisis First Nation 's Michelle Brass <p> Michelle Brass says her climate change workshops on Saskatchewan First Nations are important because they can empower communities to come up with solutions that work specifically for them . ( Submitted ) <p> Michelle Brass believes everything is connected . That 's apparent when she talks about the climate change workshops she has been doing with a couple of First Nations in Saskatchewan . <p> " If you look at our traditional teachings , we live in relationship with all other life , with all the other animals , with the plants , with water , with the air , " she said . " All of these elements have spirit and they are our relatives . " <p> Brass , a recent CBC Future 40 winner and a member of Yellow Quill First Nation , is also a member of the steering committee for Indigenous Climate Action . She said she had been disconnected from traditional practices and ceremonies while living in the city . <p> When she reconnected with those practices , she said she started to notice the land changing firsthand . That 's another reason why she is doing this work now , she said . <p> " I used to be really focused on healing the relationship between Indigenous and non-Indigenous peoples , and focusing on the past and how we could heal from past events , " she said . <p> " And for me now , what it 's about is healing that relationship with eyes to the future . What kind of a future are we going to create ?
