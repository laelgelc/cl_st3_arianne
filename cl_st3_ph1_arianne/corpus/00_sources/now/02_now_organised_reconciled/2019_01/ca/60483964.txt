<p> The role of greenhouse gases in climate change is often misunderstood by the public . Most people know that climate change is caused by increased emissions of greenhouse gases . However , many do n't understand how -- for example -- carbon dioxide traps heat in the atmosphere . <p> U of T chemistry professors Jessica D'eon , Jennifer Faust , Kristine Quinlan , and Scott Browning are acutely aware of this knowledge gap and have developed a lab to address it . Their findings were published in the Journal of Chemical Education . <p> The researchers designed a first-year chemistry laboratory on the greenhouse effect that provides a topical and engaging introduction to the undergraduate student laboratory . <p> The relatively simple experimental design allows students to focus on grasping complex , big picture concepts without feeling anxious about measurements or dangerous chemicals . <p> Despite climate science being taught in primary and secondary schools , researchers from Purdue University found that most students enter post-secondary education with a fragmented understanding of the climate system . <p> D'eon agrees with the findings of this study , writing that many of her students have " put the mental @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " and that generally " the greenhouse gas effect has been identified as a poorly understood concept in climate science . " <p> Now , these tangible experiments are giving students the ' aha ' moment that they rarely experience when untangling complex and abstract concepts . As students move on from this course to pursue careers in science and non-science disciplines , they will do so with a fundamental understanding of greenhouse gases . <p> In the first experiment , students are asked to recognize phase changes using dry ice -- solid carbon dioxide . Here , they develop a sense of scale while improving their qualitative observation skills . <p> In the second experiment , students compare types of radiation and energy , discussing their relative importance for the greenhouse effect . <p> They then apply this knowledge by comparing the heating rates of two ' beaker Earths ' -- one containing a normal atmosphere and another enriched with carbon dioxide . The students observe firsthand the faster rate of warming in the latter beaker , which they can relate back to their studies . <p> Reflections before @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ lab , 87 per cent of students significantly improved their mechanistic understanding of the greenhouse effect . <p> Prior to the experiments , most students gave an unscientific description of greenhouse gases or were too vague in their explanations .
