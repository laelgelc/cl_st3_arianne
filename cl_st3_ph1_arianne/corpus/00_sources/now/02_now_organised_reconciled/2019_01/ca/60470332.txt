Since 1963 , the Liberals have been in power nationally 36 years , compared to 19 years for the Conservatives -- nearly a two-to-one ratio -- which partially explains why most populist movements in Canada over the past half-century have come from the political right . <p> As a democratic phenomenon , populism is clearly enabling , but in its portrayal of the masses as like-minded , like-interested and like-betrayed , it is also misrepresentative and troubling . Several commentators have suggested that the most common , core sentiment being expressed in the public square today is the French concept of ressentiment ( derived from the English word resentment ) , " a deep-seated resentment , frustration and hostility accompanied by a sense of being powerless to express those feelings " ( Merriam-Webster ) . <p> The populist weaponizing of ressentiment , a term capturing the deep grievance produced by feelings of both envy and humiliation , is possibly the defining theme in global politics today . <p> But ressentiment is not merely a product of the current historical moment . At the most macro , globalized , historical level , it is a manifestation of the Age of Anger ( Pankaj Mishra , 2017 ) , wrought by the costs of liberal capitalist modernity in general , reaching back to the 18th century . Yet socio-political conditions in the early 21st century have produced an alarming increase in a somewhat universal irritability of everybody against everybody else that borders on mutual hatred . <p> As it @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ undermines political liberty . Currently it is leading a global turn toward authoritarianism and toxic chauvinism . <p> The current resurgence of populism in Canadian political culture , as in other western societies , is driven by distrust of politicians , political parties and governments , insecurity concerning the future and decreasing citizen deference to all traditional institutional authorities . Therefore most politicians now increasingly borrow the style and rhetoric of populism . But in a complex globalized political economy , grassroots control by citizens over the major institutions that in turn control their lives -- the goal of traditional populist politics -- is ever more difficult to achieve . <p> Nevertheless , populism may well burst into flames in 2019 . <p> Dennis Hiebert is a professor of sociology at Providence University College in Manitoba . <p> You can comment on most stories on The Winnipeg Free Press website .
