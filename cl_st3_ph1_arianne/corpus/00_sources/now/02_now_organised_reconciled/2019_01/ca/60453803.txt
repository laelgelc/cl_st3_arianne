<p> An ancient river channel that cuts through downtown Calgary is now plugged with concrete . Edmonton 's city-owned utility is ranking drainage basins by risk . Key roads in Fredericton have been elevated to guard against the next deluge . <p> Cities and utility providers across Canada are girding for a wetter future as rising global temperatures supercharge storms and lead to more frequent and severe flooding . <p> The preparation , while not entirely new , has gained urgency amid a push by major insurers , bond-rating agencies and governments to reduce the mounting financial and social costs tied to extreme weather . It aligns with an emerging consensus that worldwide efforts to curb emissions of planet-warming greenhouse gases are likely to fall short of targets , necessitating costly retrofits of infrastructure to cope with the worsening effects of climate change . <p> Story continues below advertisement <p> " We have a growing adaptation deficit in Canada , and we 've got to reverse that trend , " said Blair Feltmate , head of the Intact Centre on Climate Adaptation at the University of Waterloo . <p> A new report from the think tank pegs flooding in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ challenge , with implications for everything from residential insurance premiums to municipal credit ratings . <p> It cites data from the Insurance Bureau of Canada that show payouts for catastrophic losses jumped from an average $405-million a year between 1983 and 2008 to a yearly average of $1.8-billion since 2009 , with flooding accounting for more than half of the increase . <p> The report is the latest in a series sponsored by major insurer Intact Financial Corp . and is also backed by the National Research Council of Canada and the Standards Council of Canada . <p> It makes the case for a national framework to limit flood risks in communities by taking into account factors such as local design standards , aging infrastructure and whether a region has a history of extensive flooding . <p> Some jurisdictions face threats from ice-jamming in rivers , while others are exposed to increased rainfall or coastal flooding .
