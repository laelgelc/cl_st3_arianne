Jane Goodall 's inspirational messages about peace , wildlife conservation and human and animal welfare are undeniably connected to her lifetime of travel . <p> If we want to keep travelling across continents and oceans , we have to immediately reduce the climate change impacts of air travel . A carbon tax -- a government-mandated surcharge on anything that produces greenhouse gases -- is the only way to spur the drastic change needed . <p> A carbon tax would take into account all the environmental costs of air travel . A recent United Nations report showed anywhere from US$135 ( in 2010 dollars ) to US$6,050 per metric tonne to keep global temperature increases below the 1.5 C goal . This would drive up the price of my Sydney-to-LAX ticket by a modest US$177 or a whopping US$7,925 . Clearly , airline tickets would cost more , potentially much more , for a while . <p> A carbon tax needs to be high enough to drive airlines to become greener -- and quickly -- but not so high that it kills the industry . Airlines might start by directly purchasing CO2 offsets from companies that have created technologies to remove carbon from the atmosphere for US$100 to US$200 a ton . Production of renewable fuels , such as algae-based diesel , might become viable and readily available if carbon taxes are higher . At least one company is working to produce jet fuel from carbon removed from the air . Indeed , I wrote the first draft of this essay on a plane . I want to be able to explore -- and work to save -- some of the Earth 's biodiversity . I want to meet colleagues around the world . We live in a golden age of interconnectedness and I genuinely believe many good things emerge from this . <p> But I want future generations to have those opportunities , too . So I know I need to start paying a carbon tax on my plane tickets now . <p> Daniel T.
