I also attended the commemorative for Patti at Pier 21 . Three things became clear : <p> Each service was a welcome pause from being relentlessly digitally connected . It was a chance for mindfulness . The services were intense and there was no opportunity , or reason , to be thinking about anything else but the present . <p> Although many of us were strangers to one another , we were intimately joined by a common purpose and emotional vulnerability . It was community in pure form . It was safe . <p> I felt a growing despair for the people who are not as well served @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ last week reminded me not to give up . So here goes .... <p> Health care is a " wicked problem . " Like terrorism and climate change , it is complex , constantly changing and adapting , and predictably unpredictable . It seems simple on the surface : if people are sick , provide the resources to make them better . But that simple linear relationship gets twisted into a knot by social pressures , big and small ' p ' politics , economics , belief systems , competing hierarchies , priorities , etc . <p> My first suggestion is for the premier , the minister of health , and CEOs of the Nova Scotia Health Authority and IWK : please publicly admit there are problems ; you do not have all the answers , and you need help . <p> I was told years ago by a senior administrator and communications person that I could not " tell it like it is " because " it will scare the public . The dictionary defines capitalism as " an economic system characterized by private or corporate ownership of capital goods , by investments that are determined by private decision , and by prices , production , and the distribution of goods that are determined mainly by competition in a free market . " <p> We can look to the U.S. to see the results of lowering taxes , deregulating industries , and the increasing divide between haves and have-nots : health care and social security for those outside the " elite " class are taking a beating . We do n't want this to happen in Canada . <p> Health care needs the creativity , innovation , risk tolerance , and nimbleness of private companies , but these criteria need to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ with public oversight . Public and private delivery of health services can work , but require strong public leadership , a new philosophy , and corporations that accept the balance of a healthy , productive workforce , a good place to live , and are invested in the long game ( rather than quick profits at any cost ) . <p> We welcome the recently publicized federal airline passenger bill of rights , with its clear standards and penalties . We need a similar " patient bill of rights " in Nova Scotia , to establish clear standards , outcome measurements and reporting requirements to hold providers accountable . We can lead this . <p> Health care needs the creativity , innovation , risk tolerance , and nimbleness of private companies , but these criteria need to operate within a strong set of rules , regulations and with public oversight . <p> My last suggestion is to break the current administrative logjam . The premier , Department of Health and Wellness , DoctorsNS , NSHA , IWK , and Colleges of Physicians , nurses and other health professions are all @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Their leaders , all well-meaning , have little " skin in the game , " and no great incentive to change . Each hoping that someone else will take the lead . <p> Stephen McNeil , I think that is your job . You gave nurse Patti Melanson her Order of Nova Scotia in November . You then went to her home , a month before she died , to talk about her goals for MOSH , and your ideas for Nova Scotia . I think you " get it . " <p> But the recent Canadian premier popularity poll , which puts you near the bottom , should be a trigger to make some big changes and take risks . Nova Scotians can not wait another two years , endure promises of stupid five- , seven- , or 10-point plans to " fix " health care from party leaders , only to see nothing change . <p> We need a clear call , right now , that the organizations listed above must collaborate horizontally in addressing what is absolutely a health-care crisis in Nova Scotia . <p> Furthermore , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ goal : create and support a healthy and productive population and workforce . Acknowledge and deeply understand the social determinants of health , and how their failures end up in the health-care line . Work directly with companies to help support workers , drive innovation and create meaningful , engaging jobs . If our personal and corporate taxes are higher but our quality of life is too , then it is worth it . <p> Our small population and geography makes this the province that should be agile and leading innovation -- because all other provinces are in deep trouble , too . The current game plan has us in last place . That is appalling . <p> Change happens when it starts at the top . Mr. Premier , you need to take a risk , and change the game and the plan . You have nothing to lose , and we the people of Nova Scotia have everything to gain .
