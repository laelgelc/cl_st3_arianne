<p> Glaciers in British Columbia , Yukon and Alberta are retreating faster now than at any time in history , threatening to increase water levels and create deserts . <p> The region is a hotspot for warming related to climate change , and the impacts are easier to see in glaciers than in other natural features , says Simon Fraser University 's David Hik . The Peyto glacier in Banff National Park , for instance , has lost 70 per cent of its mass in the last 50 years . <p> " Probably 80 per cent of the mountain glaciers in Alberta and B.C. will disappear in the next 50 years , " Hik says in a report he co-authored with the University of Alberta 's Zac Robinson titled " State of the Mountains . " <p> The melting is directly linked to rising sea levels , coastal erosion and flooding , as well as dry areas and dust bowls . " Mountains give an important glimpse into the future , and can show us what 's coming down the line , " Robinson writes .
