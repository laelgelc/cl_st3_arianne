<h> Canada 's new Food Guide is a good upgrade , but skirts around issues of inequality <p> The centrepiece of Canada 's Food Guide used to be a largely incomprehensible rainbow graphic telling us how many portions to eat from each of the four major food groups . It 's been replaced with a much easier-to-digest image of a plate of food that is half fruits and veggies , and almost half nuts , grains and legumes . <p> The new , improved guide has even dispensed with the meaningless notion of " portion " altogether , opting to urge people instead to pay attention to the proportion of foods they eat -- again , heavy on the fruits and veggies , light on the meat , cheese . <p> The document also tells us our " beverage of choice " should be water -- not milk , juice or beer . In fact , it has some pretty strong words about avoiding sugary drinks ( including pop , sports drinks and chocolate milk ) and alcohol . <p> Story continues below advertisement <p> And Health Canada has grown a backbone and distanced itself from industry by excising @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , such as the recommendation to drink two glasses of milk a day and consume at least two tablespoons of canola oil every day . <p> All in all , it 's an excellent upgrade to the iconic Food Guide . <p> But the Food Guide does n't much matter to the average consumer , and it never has -- but that 's okay . It 's not a diet or a weight-loss plan . <p> What it 's supposed to do -- and does relatively well now -- is describe a pattern of healthy eating for individuals and set a standard for institutions and policy-makers . <p> Most of the dietary advice people really need can be summed up in the seven words of author Michael Pollan 's manifesto : " Eat food , not too much , mostly plants . But that 's not going to happen simply by publishing glossy brochures and creating an interactive website . <p> Healthy eating , as it is proposed in Canada 's Food Guide , is a privilege of wealth . The symbolic fruity/nutty/grainy plate is actually out of reach for many who struggle with poverty , food insecurity and health illiteracy . <p> A Food Guide is an essential part of but not a substitute for a food strategy . <p> It does nothing to address the fact that one in six children in Canada are affected by food insecurity , or that this country ranks 37th of 41 in access to nutritious food , according to UNICEF . <p> The reason Canada got a Food Guide in the first place ( in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that it was difficult to recruit soldiers to fight in the Second World War . <p> The original Food Guide ( actually called the Official Food Rules ) was designed to get people to eat more and to respect wartime rationing . We got food groups because those categories of food were rationed , not because they were essential for good nutrition . <p> Story continues below advertisement <p> Today , our fancy new Food Guide skirts around some important political and social realities . We have as much malnutrition today as 70 years ago , but today it is as much about eating too much as too little . <p> The rationing of food that exists today is largely the result of economic inequality : People on low incomes can only afford or access the highly processed foods that do n't appear on the pretty plate . <p> Initially , the global issue driving food policy was feeding the war machine . Today , climate change is the most urgent and overwhelming policy challenge in the world .
