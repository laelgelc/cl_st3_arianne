An ability to connect with disenchanted centre-left voters in urban centres allowed the party to win a majority , one that included their best performance in British Columbia since 1968 . <p> It is too early to tell how these voters will react this year . They may be dissatisfied with specific decisions taken under Prime Minister Justin Trudeau 's watch , including the approval ( and later , purchase ) of the Trans Mountain pipeline . The results point to a voting public that has already taken sides and one that may not be swayed by policy proposals . <p> Across the country , three in five Canadians ( 60 per cent ) think global warming is a fact and is mostly caused by emissions from vehicles and industrial facilities . <p> A significantly smaller proportion of Canadians ( 15 per cent ) think global warming is a fact and is mostly caused by natural changes . Almost one-in-five ( 18 per cent ) say global warming is a theory that has not yet been proven . <p> Canadians aged 18 to 34 ( 68 per cent ) , Quebecers ( also 68 per cent ) and British Columbians ( 64 per cent ) are significantly more likely to say that climate change is caused by man . <p> These demographics are essential for the success of the Liberal Party in 2019 . Campaigning with Kathleen Wynne will be very different from campaigning against Doug Ford . <p> Canadians , as a whole , are feeling positive about the economy , in spite of the usual threats ( such as oil prices ) and some unusual ones ( Donald Trump in the White House ) .
