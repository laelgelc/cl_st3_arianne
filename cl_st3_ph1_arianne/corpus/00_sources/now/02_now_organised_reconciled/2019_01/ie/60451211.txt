<h> Eating habits need to radically change <h> Health and planet at major risk <p> Eating habits and food production need to radically change in order to improve people 's health and avoid potentially catastrophic damage to the planet , major new research has found . <p> The findings come from the EAT-Lancet Commission , a three-year project which brought together almost 40 worldwide experts in a number of fields including human health , agriculture and environmental sustainability . <p> The commission found that the world 's food system needs urgent transformation because currently , more than three billion people are malnourished - this includes people who are undernourished and overnourished - and food production is driving climate change , pollution , the loss of biodiversity and unsustainable changes in land and water use . <p> It emphasises that feeding an increasing population of 10 billion people by 2050 with a healthy and sustainable diet will simply not be possible if eating habits are not transformed and food production is not improved . <p> The commission recommends a diet high in plant-based foods , such as fruit , vegetables , nuts and legumes and low in animal-based foods , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Based on a diet of 2,500 k/cal per day , people should be consuming around 500g of fruit and vegetables every day , and no more than 14g of red meat , which is equivalent to a half a rasher of bacon . A typical pork chop or sirloin steak usually weight at least 200g . <p> It recommends 232g of whole grains per day , such as rice and wheat , 50g of starchy vegetables such as potatoes , and 250g of dairy , such as whole milk and cheese . <p> It also recommends just 29g per day of poultry , 28g of fish and 13g of eggs , which is equivalent to one-and-a-half eggs per week . A typical chicken fillet weighs approximately 160g . <p> The commission warns that the way people eat is inextricably linked to health and environmental sustainability , and both are now at major risk . <p> It points out that unhealthy diets are the leading cause of ill health globally and following this healthier diet could lead to 11 million fewer premature deaths every year .
