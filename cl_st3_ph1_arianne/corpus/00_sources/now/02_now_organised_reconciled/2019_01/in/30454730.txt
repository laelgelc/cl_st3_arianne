<p> With documentary screenings and debates across the world , the Women SenseTour tells the story of female changemakers in Morocco , Tunisia , Turkey , Iran and Indonesia through five one-hour films , said French-Moroccan filmmaker and activist Zouak . <p> " I researched the women on the internet , by reading books or through word of mouth , " said 29-year-old Zouak , who organised three screenings in Indonesia late last year and hopes to stage similar events throughout Asia in the future . <p> " They had to be feminists , Muslim and have founded a project for women 's empowerment , " she told the Thomson Reuters Foundation by phone from Paris . <p> Muslim women are seen in many countries , particularly the West , as oppressed and having limited freedoms and the films aim to reshape these cliches with stories of strong Muslim women . <p> Crowdfunded and shot during a five-month trip by Zouak three years ago , the films include women of all ages and backgrounds , working in health , education and with refugees , as well as those helping drug users and domestic abuse victims . <p> Having never @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ subjects , filming the women going about their daily routines . <p> " In Indonesia I met a woman who founded a feminist organisation for fisherwomen - even in the fishing industry there are feminist women working , " said Zouak , <p> " It was interesting to see that sexism is everywhere - even industries you would n't think .
