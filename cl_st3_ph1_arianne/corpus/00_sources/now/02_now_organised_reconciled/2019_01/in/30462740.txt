<p> New Delhi , Jan 17 ( PTI ) Air quality tests done in Delhi and Gurgaon in the last two months showed presence of alarming levels of toxic heavy metals , according to a new report released Thursday . The report , titled ' Death in Every Breath ' , released by NGO Lung Care Foundation , analysed results of seven air quality tests taken in New Delhi and Gurgaon . The PM2.5 levels - fine particulate matters in the air with a diameter of less than 2.5 micrometers - in all the seven tests were above statutory limits , the report said . " The PM2.5 levels ranged from 90.3 ug/m3 to 563.5 ug/m3 and were between 1.5 and 9.4 times higher than standards prescribed by the Ministry of Environment , Forests and Climate Change ( MoEFCC ) , " it said . The levels of manganese in five of the seven tests exceed the US EPA Reference Concentration for exposure to manganese ( 0.05 ug/m3 ) and the World Health Organisation annual health-based guidelines value of 0.15 ug/m3 . There are no standards in India for manganese in ambient air , the report said . Nickel levels in all the tests exceed the WHO annual health-based guidelines value of 0.0025 ug/m3 , which is based on the risk of cancer associated with long-term exposure to nickel . " Manganese , lead and nickel are neurotoxins that damage the brain . Children are particularly vulnerable to the effects of lead . Exposures to even low levels of lead early in life have been linked to effects on IQ , learning , memory and behaviour . It is a matter of very serious concern that such high levels of these toxic metals are found in the air that our children breathe , " said Arvind Kumar of Lung Care Foundation .
