<h> ' Universal Basic Income wo n't make people lazy , but afford them more choice ' <h> Last Updated : Monday 28 January 2019 <p> Universal Basic Income ( UBI ) has been a talking point , off and on , in India ever since the 2017 Economic Survey batted for its potential to reduce poverty . From time to time , there have been discussions and debates about how existing social welfare schemes can be replaced with direct cash . <p> This aspect has also led to opposition from several quarters on various grounds . Questions have been raised on whether such a scheme , if and when it is rolled out , will eat into the rights-based approach of the Constitution of India . <p> The moot point of a UBI is that every citizen , irrespective of their social position , would be entitled to a cash payment -- a direct transfer to their account . Supporters say such a scheme can weed out fund leakages and loopholes in existing welfare measures . <p> Opponents caution that it could not replace schemes like the rural-employment guarantee and public distribution system . India will also do good to learn from the experience of other countries . <p> Of late , with the General Elections round the corner , there have been speculations about whether the Narendra Modi-led government would announce a form of UBI . Some sections feel the push towards universal banking via the Jan Dhan Yojana was a precursor . Congress President Rahul Gandhi on January 28 , 2019 already ensured minimum income guarantee to every poor in the country if he comes to power . <p> But before that , Sikkim Chief Minister Pawan Kr Chamling announced that if his Sikkim Democratic Party ( SDP ) is voted back to power , it would roll out UBI in the next three years . <p> Down to Earth spoke to SDP leader Prem Das Rai , the lone Lok Sabha member from the state , on his party 's plans regarding UBI .
