( Reuters ) <p> The year 2018 was full of declarations . In the country , some major policies and programmes were launched to tackle issues ranging from air pollution to plastic pollution . At the international level , too , two major agreements got underway -- the rulebook to implement the Paris Agreement was adopted and the Kigali Amendment to the Montreal Protocol came into effect from January 1 , 2019 . <p> The agenda for 2019 , therefore , is clear-cut : we should setup the institutional and regulatory framework to implement the major programmes announced in 2018 and fulfil our international obligations . Here is my list of the top 10 environmental priorities for 2019 . <p> * Implement the National Clear Air Programme : The National Clean Air Programme ( NCAP ) , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for air quality management with a target of 20-30% reduction of PM2.5 and PM10 concentration by 2024 . But , NCAP has weak legal mandate and is not supported with adequate financial and institutional resources . Without these , NCAP will remain one of the many top-down programmes destined for failure . We must not allow this to happen . So , we must work to strengthen NCAP and institutionalise it for effective enforcement . Without strict enforcement , all other measures will fail . <p> * Ban single-use plastics : The pledge to ban all single-use plastics by 2022 is the single-most important environmental agenda put forth by the government in 2018 . In 2019 , this agenda must be put into action . At present , different states have differently interpreted the term " single-use plastics " . A national definition along with a comprehensive action plan , including the promotion of alternatives , should be put in place in 2019 . <p> * Strengthen Swachh Bharat Mission : Governments come and governments go , but certain successful programmes must continue . It has been a decade since India adopted its National Action Plan on Climate Change ( NAPCC ) and State Action Plan on Climate Change ( SAPCC ) . The outcome has been mixed . While the National Solar Mission and the National Mission for Enhanced Energy Efficiency have done very well , other missions under NAPCC have not progressed as expected . The SAPCC has not progressed beyond documentation . It is time we revisit NAPCC and SAPCC and develop a comprehensive framework to decarbonise our economy and build resilience to adapt to the changing climate . <p> * Implement National Action Plan on AMR : Antibiotic resistance is emerging as a major health threat .
