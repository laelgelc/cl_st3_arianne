How can we accelerate the rate of change ? <p> In China , coal pollution is reportedly responsible for over 600,000 premature deaths per year . Furthermore , the UN body IPCC recently released a new report on the pathway to limiting the rise in global temperatures to 1.5 ? C. The report paints a stark picture : 2 ? C we can avoid the most devastating consequences . However , reaching this target requires urgent action . In the US and Europe in particular , the economics of coal power generation do not stack up . The US today produces enough gas to largely meet domestic demand , according to the US Energy Information Association . Commodity prices , together with the significant environmental and health impact , means there is little incentive for investors to fund new coal capacity in this market . <p> Similarly , Europe benefits from reasonably cheap and readily available gas from the North Sea and Russian pipelines . In combination with recent policy intervention in the form of carbon prices , which Carbon Tracker suggests are seemingly on a steep upward trajectory , investments in new coal generation on the continent makes little financial sense in our view . <p> Additionally , the cost of wind and solar power has fallen to the point where in many parts of the world , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . In India , coal plants already represent 15 per cent of stressed assets on banks ' balance sheets , according to the Economist . <h> Power politics <p> While we believe that the economic case is clear-cut , the political rationale is less so . In the name of job protection and creation , policymakers across developed and developing markets continue to prop up investment in coal . In the US , President Trump has famously made reviving the industry a campaign pledge , and the trend extends to Western Europe . Last year , the Spanish government issued a royal decree in an attempt to stop utility company Iberdrola from closing down its last remaining coal assets in the country , before being rejected by the national market and competition regulator .
