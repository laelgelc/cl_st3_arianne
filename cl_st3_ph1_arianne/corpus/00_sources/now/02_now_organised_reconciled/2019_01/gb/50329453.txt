<h> Concern over ' large packs ' of dogs being exercised at Levenhall Links <h> Concern over ' large packs ' of dogs being exercised at Levenhall Links <p> PROBLEMS with commercial dog walkers exercising " large packs " of dogs at Musselburgh 's ash lagoons were highlighted at the annual general meeting of the Friends of Levenhall Links . <p> Jason Rose , who was re-elected the group 's chairman , said : " This can be disruptive to birdlife and intimidating for other site users . " <p> He added : " I understand the countryside rangers are considering what options there might be to try to regulate this kind of business . <p> " We need to balance access to the site with the fact that it is an important area for wildlife . " <p> Mr Rose said the AGM also heard about anti-social behaviour , mainly over the summer , which resulted in some fires being set and rubbish being left behind in the bird viewing hides . <p> He added : " This seems to have calmed down but we would encourage anyone noticing such behaviour to report it to the police , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ they are to respond . " <p> An East Lothian Council spokesperson said : " The council continues to promote responsible dog ownership , which includes a voluntary licensing scheme for commercial dog walkers which restricts the number of dogs exercised by one person to six . <p> " We are aware of occasional problems and both the countryside rangers and community wardens regularly visit the site and will advise when witnessing possible concerns with dogs . " <p> At the AGM , Nick Aitken , countryside ranger , gave an update on progress towards restoring and reshaping the last two ash lagoons , near the mouth of the River Esk and opposite the entrance to Prestongrange Museum .
