<h> Breadcrumb <h> The definition of happiness <p> Overall , how happy did you feel yesterday ? This question has been posed to the British public each year since 2011 , after David Cameron decided to construct a National Happiness Index , mooted to replace GDP as a measure of the nation 's wellbeing . <p> This attempt to quantify the experience of happiness in the individual was the impetus of Lynne Segal 's wonderfully affirming book Radical Happiness . <p> " I began by looking into the promotion and measuring of happiness with the idea that it 's some quantifiable inner event , " Segal tells me . " I say , actually , no emotions are like that . All emotions are far more complex and mixed , far more contradictory . And what 's more , they have a public dynamic as well as a personal dynamic . " <p> Collective joy <p> This insight is central to the book 's subtitle , Moments of Collective Joy . For Segal , the idea that happiness can be individuated is a particularly pernicious aspect of neoliberalism . <p> On the contrary , she believes that joy is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ only come from recognition of our dependency on one another and on Nature . <p> " Everything I write is an argument for our need to recognise our ties to others , and that 's the only way to escape the gloomy ties to the self we 're pushed ever more into , " she tells me . <p> " Joy , it seems to me , is almost quintessentially collective , " she continues . " Even if it 's coming from something like the view from a mountaintop , we want to be able to share it . The more we can share something , the more it will stay and linger . " <p> This outlook explains Segal 's suspicion of what we might term as the ' wellbeing industry ' , in which unhappiness is to be treated as a personal defect that can be remedied through some combination of marketised medi ? cine and mindfulness . In such an approach , structural issues that might have given rise to legitimate unhappiness are obscured . Segal sees things differently . <p> Socialist feminist <p> Out now @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have to look at it in a social context , " she says . For Segal , however , even resistance in the face of despair can provide the conditions for radical acts of collective joy .
