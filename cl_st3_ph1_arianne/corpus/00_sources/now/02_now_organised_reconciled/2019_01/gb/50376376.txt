<p> Despite a series of reforms following deadly factory fires and protests , many of Pakistan 's 15 million garment workers struggle with poor working conditions and face threats if they try to unionise , Human Rights Watch ( HRW ) said in a report . <p> " Pakistan 's government has long neglected its obligations to protect the rights of the country 's garment workers , " said HRW 's Asia director Brad Adams in a statement . <p> The new study shines a spotlight on a sector that faced heavy criticism in 2012 after a fire swept through one factory and killed nearly 300 people . <p> HRW interviewed more than 140 people , including garment workers , and found that most were forced to work overtime , denied wages , paid and maternity leave , and given short-term , oral contracts . <p> Pakistan 's top two textile and garment industry associations rejected the allegations and said foreign companies would have stopped sourcing from factories if they were not meeting labour standards . <p> " Our members cater to some of the top brands in the world and are compliant with both labour and environmental regulations @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Hamid Tufail Khan , head of the All Pakistan Textile Mills Association . <p> On bathroom breaks , Mubashir Naseer Butt , main chief of the Pakistan Readymade Garments Manufacturers and Exporters Association , said : " If they are going to spend an hour there , naturally , that is not on " . <p> A top labour official in Sindh province , the largest textile hub in Pakistan , said there was no evidence of widespread malpractice . <p> " If they ( workers ) were unhappy , they would not be going to work , " Abdul Rashid Solangi told the Thomson Reuters Foundation . <p> HRW found factories regularly flouted labour laws , which they said did not meet global standards .
