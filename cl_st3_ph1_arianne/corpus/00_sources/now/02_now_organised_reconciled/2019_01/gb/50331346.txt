Either cling to your genuine pork product sausage rolls or get on board the plant-sourced movement and go with the new Greggs vegan sausage roll . For this has been the week in which Greggs lobbed its new vegan offering into the stramash that is contemporary dietary politics and watched the food fight commence . <p> Of course , the food world has far more to offer than these two pastry products , but if you were to watch social media , you would think that this key issue -- where you stood on the sausage roll -- said everything about a person . Show sympathy for the vegan option , and you would quickly be marked as a moraliser who failed to grasp that we built , to paraphrase a now well-known pop song , this city/society/masculinity on sausage rolls . At the same time food has become a tribal thing , linked frequently to politics , so mired in suspicion . <p> We are assaulted with often contradictory messages . MP Caroline Lucas last week proposed creating a tax on meat , as one means of avoiding the worst impact of climate change . Even the word moderation feels like a bit of a drag . But , nevertheless , it seems to me that 's what we need . <p> No , you do n't have to stop eating meat all together , but would it really hurt if you cut it by half ?
