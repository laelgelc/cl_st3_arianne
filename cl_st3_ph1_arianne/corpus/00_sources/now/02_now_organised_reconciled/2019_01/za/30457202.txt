climate change . <p> But despite fonio 's benefits , Senegalese farmers struggle to make money from the grain . <p> Fonio is less popular with local consumers than rice or millet , and processing it suited to hot climates with unpredictable weather patterns . Its highly developed root system makes it resistant to drought , and the plant matures quickly , making it a reliable crop for farmers unsure of when the next rains will come . <p> But before fonio can catch on in the West , farmers in Senegal need to overcome the obstacles that are stifling the domestic market , Thiam said . <p> While farmers have little trouble growing fonio , producing enough of it at export quality is a challenge , he said . <p> And removing the grain from the husk is labour-intensive .
