They also found that , based on the data , 21% more storms form for every 1 degree Celsius that ocean surface temperatures rise . <p> " It is somewhat common sense that severe storms will increase in a warmer environment . Thunderstorms typically occur in the warmest season of the year , " Aumann explains . " But our data provide the first quantitative estimate of how much they are likely to increase , at least for the tropical oceans . " <p> Currently accepted climate models project that with a steady increase of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere ( 1 percent per year ) , tropical ocean surface temperatures may rise by as much as 2,7 degrees Celsius by the end of the century . The study team concludes that if this were to happen , we could expect the frequency of extreme storms to increase by as much as 60% by that time . <p> Although climate models are n't perfect , results like these can serve as a guideline for those looking to prepare for the potential effects a changing climate may have . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to the consequences of the predicted warming of the oceans , " Aumann says .
