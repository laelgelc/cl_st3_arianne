<h> Uh oh , wild coffee species at risk of extinction due to climate change <p> 21 January 2019 - 06:40 By Odwa Mjo <p> Sixty percent of coffee species are at risk of extinction , according to a recent study . Image : iStock / Grandriver <p> A recent study published in Science Advances found that 60% of wild coffee species are at risk of becoming extinct . <p> Forbes reported that this includes wild species of arabica coffee , the most cultivated coffee species , which requires favourable weather conditions and is difficult to cultivate . <p> According to the report , global coffee trade mainly relies on two coffee species , arabica , which contributes 60% , and robusta , the remaining 40% . <p> Global coffee trade now faces new risks that threaten the survival of these two species . <p> Arabica naturally occurs in parts of South Sudan and Ethiopia , while robusta grows in wet and tropical parts of Africa . <p> These threats include drastic climate change effects , such as lengthy droughts , the spread and escalation of fungal pathogens , diseases and pests , as well as social , economic @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ despite arabica and robusta being the most produced coffees for global trade , there are 124 coffee species known to science that grow naturally in tropical parts of Africa and the Indian Ocean islands including , Comoros Islands , Mascarene Islands and Madagascar . <p> Seventy five coffee species ( 60% ) have been regarded as at risk of extinction , with 13 being critically endangered , 40 endangered and 22 labelled vulnerable , based on the International Union for the Conservation of Nature 's Red List Categories and Criteria . <p> Madagascar has the highest number of threatened coffee species , with 43.
