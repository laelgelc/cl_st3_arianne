In a number of publications over the last five years , Joubert has argued that our understanding of concepts like hunger , food security and nutrition is often a bit , well , nutty . From smug metropolitans who think obesity is a sign that people are too well fed , to impoverished mothers feeding their infants Stoney 's ginger beer to make them strong , Joubert prescribes a diet of cold , hard facts . <p> Do you think the government should be able to influence what people eat ? " When people make poor food choices , we blame the individuals . " <p> In reality , though , issues of infrastructure and basic services -- the stuff the government is supposed to be taking care of -- play a major role in the decisions people make about their diets , says Joubert . <p> " A lack of good service delivery impacts on people 's food choices , " she says . <p> What drives people to buy high calorie , low nutrition food like processed maize products is not just the cheapness of the goods but also the fact that they may lack any way of preserving perishable foods . <p> Joubert 's latest book , Tomatoes &amp; Taxi Ranks , is an accessible presentation of research into food systems in local urban settings conducted by UCT 's African Centre for Cities . " <p> Big business is also a major source of misinformation about diet in South Africa , says Joubert . <p> One particularly alarming incident cited in Tomatoes &amp; Taxi Ranks saw a nurse in Khayelitsha advising patients to wean infants on Stoney 's ginger beer because advertising for the soft drink had informed her that it would make consumers big and strong . <p> In Zambia , meanwhile , one woman @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ baby cheese curls -- because the reference to " cheese " in the name led her to believe it was a dairy product . <p> But if educated middle-class people are tempted to scoff at such misunderstandings , Joubert says that one of the most widespread and frustrating social myths about food in South Africa is that obesity among poor people is the result of gluttony , laziness or apathy . <p> " People are actually making quite rational economic decisions under the circumstances , " she says . <p> " When someone is dealing with a very tight budget , one of the easiest things to do is cut back on spending on food by filling your belly with cheap , starchy food : lots of calories , but low on nutrients .
