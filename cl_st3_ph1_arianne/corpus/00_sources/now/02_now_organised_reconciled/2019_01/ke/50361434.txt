<h> Death of coffee ? More than half of wild species threatened with extinction <p> Jan. 17 , 2019 , 9:00 am <p> By DAILY MAIL <p> ' Arabica beans Arabica beans make up almost two-thirds of the coffee we drink . ' <p> It is news which may have over-caffeinated office workers choking on their lattes . <p> At least 60 per cent of wild coffee species are under threat of extinction , mainly due to deforestation and climate change . <p> Arabica beans make up almost two-thirds of the coffee we drink , as the premium species used in ' real ' rather than instant coffee . <p> But its wild relatives have now been declared an endangered species , raising fears for the future supply . <p> While coffee shops in Britain do not serve wild Arabica coffee and it is not usually drunk here , it is a vital future resource for coffee farmers . <p> Some wild coffee plants are hardier than farmed species , better able to withstand climate change and devastating disease such as coffee leaf rust disease . <p> There may be dozens of wild coffee species which are perfectly drinkable @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ - Arabica and the Robusta species used in instant coffee . <p> However , researchers at the Royal Botanic Gardens , Kew , have found that 75 of the world 's 124 wild coffee species are under threat . <p> These include wild Arabica and Coffea stenophylla , otherwise known as the ' Highland coffee of Sierra Leone ' , which had only just been rediscovered after being lost for more than 60 years . <p> They are vital because wild coffee could also be cross-pollinated with the farmed types to make them stronger . <p> Dr Davis , head of coffee research at Kew , said : ' What we 're saying is 60 per cent is just really high , that 's a real wake-up call . For a major global commodity , that starts ringing alarm bells . <p> ' It 's a tragedy losing any wild species - whether it 's a bird or plant or animal , that 's bad enough . <p> ' But when you 've got a crop that supports the livelihoods of 100 million people just in production in coffee farming @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ chains and supermarket coffee , it 's enormous . ' <p> The risk for coffee is surprisingly high , as only an estimated 22 per cent of plants in general are threatened with extinction . <p> This is the first time a threat assessment has been carried out for all 124 coffee species , which grow wild in tropical Africa , Asia , Australasia and the Indian Ocean islands including Madagascar . <p> Researchers , who published in the journal Science Advances , scientists assessed coffee species against the extinction risk criteria of the International Union for Conservation of Nature ( IUCN ) Red List of Threatened Species . <p> Among the 75 species considered to be threatened with extinction , 13 are critically endangered , with 40 endangered and 22 vulnerable to extinction . <p> The wild relative of Arabica has become endangered largely due to climate change projections , according to a separate study published in the journal Global Change Biology . <p> Its natural population is estimated to fall by 50 per cent or more by 2088 due to climate change alone . <p> Fewer than half @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ or living plant collections and more than a quarter are not known to occur in any protected areas , the scientists warn . <p> Dr Eimear Nic Lughadha , senior research leader in Kew 's conservation department , said : ' Some of the coffee species assessed have not been seen in the wild for more than 100 years , and it is possible that some may already be extinct . <p> ' We hope this new data will highlight species to be prioritised for the sustainability of the coffee production sector so that appropriate action can be taken to safeguard their future .
