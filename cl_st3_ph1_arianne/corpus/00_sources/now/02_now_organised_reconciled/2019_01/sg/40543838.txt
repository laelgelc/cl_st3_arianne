<p> SINGAPORE - The clouds gathering over the global economy will probably grow darker in the years to come , but the situation for Singapore is not bleak , Deputy Prime Minister Tharman Shanmugaratnam said on Friday ( Jan 25 ) . <p> He said in a Facebook post that Singapore can still do well by educating the young for a new world , developing deeper skills in every job and paying special attention to helping those who lose their jobs . <p> Another important area is to strengthen the innovative capabilities of Singapore businesses and build on what Mr Tharman considers Singapore 's biggest international asset : " our credibility among countries and businesses from around the world " . <p> His post on the final day of the four-day World Economic Forum ( WEF ) in Davos , Switzerland , paints a picture of a very divided world , made worse by fragmented politics in most democracies and that things will probably get worse before a return to a strong centre in the politics of advanced democracies . <p> At the WEF , which he attended with Finance Minister Heng Swee Keat and Communications and Information Minister @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ dozen meetings over three days with political , business and civil society leaders from around the world <p> He gave his basic take from these meetings . <p> One , the world 's major powers are more divided than they have been since the end of the Cold War 30 years ago . <p> He added that the United States-China trade conflict is a symptom of deeper and more enduring problems . <p> Two , technological rivalry is a reality and the prospect of a world divided into technological blocs will have major consequences for innovation and growth . <p> Three , the deeply divided people within advanced nations which are the " biggest difficulties " and is the heart of the problem , he said .
