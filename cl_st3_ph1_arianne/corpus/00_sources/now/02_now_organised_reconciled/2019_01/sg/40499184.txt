<h> Too much brine ? Study highlights growing toxic brine problem <p> FILE - The Sept. 4 , 2015 file photo shows the Carlsbad , Calif. desalination plant which borders Interstate 5 on one side and the Pacific Ocean on the other in Carlsbad , Calif . UN Warns of Rising Levels of Toxic Brine as Desalination Plants Meet Growing Water Needs . ( AP Photo/Lenny Ignelzi , file ) <p> BERLIN ( AP ) -- The world 's thirst for fresh water is causing a salty problem . <p> Desalination plants around the world are producing enough brine waste to swamp an area the size of Florida with a foot of salty water every year , according to a U.N.-backed report released Monday . <p> The study by researchers from Canada , the Netherlands and South Korea warned that much of the brine is being dumped untreated into the sea , and some is laden with toxic chemicals , causing harm to sea life . <p> The authors called for better brine management , particularly in countries that rely heavily on desalination for their water needs , such as Saudi Arabia , the United Arab Emirates , Kuwait @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is increasing in many regions across the world due to increased water demands , which are associated with population increase and economic growth , " said one of the authors , Manzoor Qadir , assistant director of the United Nations University 's Canada-based Institute for Water , Environment and Health . <p> At the same , climate change is making the availability of freshwater less predictable , such as by changing the amount of runoff snow in some regions , he said . <p> The authors examined 16,000 desalination plants worldwide and found they produce 142 million cubic meters ( 5,014 million cubic feet ) of brine each day , or 51.8 billion cubic meters a year . That 's about half more than previous studies had estimated , said Qadir . <p> The researchers called for better brine management , noting that studies have shown it can be used in aquacultures to boost yields of salt-tolerant species of fish , and the metals and salts contained in it -- such as magnesium and lithium -- could be mined.
