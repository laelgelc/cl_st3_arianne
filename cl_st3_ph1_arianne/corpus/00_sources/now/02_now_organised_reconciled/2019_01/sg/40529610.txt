<p> The world has been pursuing some sort of globalisation - the integration of economies through the exchange of goods , people and ideas - since the dawn of time . But economic historians tend to posit we 've seen three phases of globalisation so far . <p> The first , dating from 1820-1914 , came alongside the advent of steam power and the industrial revolution while the second lasted from the end of World War II to roughly 1990 . That 's when we entered the latest era - a period of what some have dubbed " hyperglobalisation " coinciding with the advent of the Internet , the fall of the Berlin Wall and the rise of emerging economies such as China and India . <p> Which brings us to the fourth wave of globalisation , which some argue we are now entering . <p> The simplest way to think about it : If all previous waves were about the trade in goods then the next one is about digitally-enabled services .
