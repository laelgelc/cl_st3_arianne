<p> DAVOS , Switzerland ( AFP ) - Touting its green credentials , the World Economic Forum has pushed back against research that said company bosses are flying to Davos in record numbers on polluting private jets . <p> The study this week from the leasing company Air Charter Service ( ACS ) forecast that nearly 1,500 such flights would occur over the week of the forum , to and from four airports near Davos in the Swiss Alps . <p> That would be higher than previous Davos meetings , it said , triggering accusations from activists that CEOs are failing to back up their talk on fighting climate change , stamping an outsized carbon footprint with their luxury aircraft . <p> " From an environmental perspective , taking a private jet is the worst way to travel to Davos , " the WEF said in a blog post . <p> But it said one of the airports examined by ACS was not affiliated with the forum , and another was a military base used only for " public figures " such as heads of state , and that its flight operations were classified . <p> On its own @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ airports , Zurich and St Gallen , the WEF estimated there would be 14 per cent fewer private jet flights this year compared to 2018 , or about 270 in total . <p> The WEF calculated private jet flights in and out of the airports over the course of three days around the forum , while the ACS study looked at six days . <p> But the ACS study was " based on very unclear methodology and forward-looking statements " , it said , attacking " a PR release from a commercial operator " . <p> The ACS issued a new statement in response , defending its calculations as based on all the airports likely to be used -
