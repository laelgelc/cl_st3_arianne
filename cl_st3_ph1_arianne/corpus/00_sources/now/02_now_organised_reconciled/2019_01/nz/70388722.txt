<h> Danielle McLaughlin : Why this new diverse Congress matters <p> Danielle McLaughlin says the new diverse make-up of the US Congress is striking for Americans more used to being governed by " a bunch of old white guys " . <p> OPINION : This week , the 116th United States Congress was sworn in . Largely due to the Democratic caucus , it is the most diverse group of senators and House members in the country 's history . <p> While it might not seem like a big deal to Kiwis accustomed to more than a modicum of diversity in their politics , the change for Americans -- more used to a bunch of old white guys running the country -- is striking . Particularly in the House of Representatives , where nearly a quarter of the lower chamber are women . <p> The freshman class of House members includes two Native Americans , two Muslims , twenty-four people of colour , a number of first-generation immigrants , and the first openly gay and bisexual Representatives . One way to avoid it , according to Grant , is to create diverse organisations , and ensure that the leaders of those organisations appreciate the power of that diversity . <p> Grant writes that authentic dissent increases the likelihood that a group will re-examine its assumptions and reach better or more creative solutions . He also warns against building an established organisation around cultural " fit " because it only serves to reinforce the existing groupthink . Instead , cultural contribution -- including new and opposing points of view -- is a means of building more creative and innovative organisations . <p> Grant 's findings are also borne out in private sector research . The most recent study on the issue from global management consultants McKinsey &amp; Company ( from 2017 ) shows that ethnic and gender diversity in company leadership correlates with stronger profitability . <p> Pelosi acknowledged in her acceptance speech this week that the challenges facing the House are " as daunting and demanding as any that previous generations of leadership have faced " . <p> She 's right .
