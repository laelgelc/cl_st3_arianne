<h> Plant pines , not natives to make money from carbon farming , says consultant <p> Landowners planting forests for carbon credits should plant pine trees rather than natives to achieve the best returns , a carbon consultant says . <p> Ollie Belton , a partner of Permanent Forests NZ a Christchurch-based carbon consultancy , said that the rate that natives absorb carbon dioxide was much lower than for pinus radiata . <p> Sequestration calculations used by the Emissions Trading Scheme for forests under 100 hectares showed that pinus radiata absorbed almost 1000 tonnes of carbon over 25 years , while native forests absorbed less than 300 tonnes . <p> Belton said measurements he had done on native forests of more than 100ha showed most performed less than the ETS calculations , some only achieving a half to a third of this .
