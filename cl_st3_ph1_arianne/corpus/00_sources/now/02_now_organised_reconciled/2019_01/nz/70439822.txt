<h> Kiwi farmers likely to weather financial storms - Lincoln report <p> The goal of most farmers is to improve their properties , the Lincoln University survey shows . <p> Most Kiwi farmers are well placed to weather financial storms , although heavily indebted dairy farmers will struggle , a Lincoln University report says . <p> Crises such as trade uncertainty and climate change will challenge farmers but they are likely to survive . <p> The report authors said they set out to do something which had rarely been attempted before - to show the situation on individual farms and the human side of the use , benefits , abuse , and stress caused by farm debt . <p> It said in real terms capital gains in farming were virtually non-existent , with the return on capital hovering around 3 per cent . If good financial performance was the main objective , many farmers would sell up , the report said . <p> Average debt on dairy farms is about $2.8 million , much higher than other forms of farming . <p> A total of 414 farmers was surveyed , showing a wide gulf in debt and equity levels @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ debt on dairy farms is about $2.8 million , on sheep and beef farms it is $662,000 . <p> Farm equity is , on average , nearly 82 per cent , ranging from 62 per cent of horticulturists who have 100 per cent equity , down to just 10 per cent of dairy farmers with 100 per cent . <p> Report co-author , associate Professor Peter Nuthall , said most farmers would be able to operate and absorb financial shocks in at least the short term . <p> " Profit levels are not high relative to the investment , but this has been the case for decades and has not caused problems due to farmer and farm family resilience , " he said . <p> " This does not mean some farmers have not struggled financially , particularly over periods of low payouts , including low wool prices , and periods of severe drought . New farmers with high initial debt will have found it difficult to meet their commitments in these periods .
