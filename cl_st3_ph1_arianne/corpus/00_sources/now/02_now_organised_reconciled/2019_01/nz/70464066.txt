<h> Waltzing to nowhere <p> In a speech last year , the former Secretary to the Treasury Ken Henry , now Chairman of the National Australia Bank , summed up the distressing state of policy making in Australia -- " Not so many years ago , an optimistic nation of Australians could be proud of a country that pioneered world 's best policy ... But nobody any longer looks to Australia to see how it should be done . " <p> How can he make such a claim when Australia continues to enjoy a world-beating period of uninterrupted economic growth now exceeding a quarter of a century ? Very easily . It is a classic case of the old adage -- good times make bad policy . In the economic sphere , perhaps more than in any other , Australia is resting on its laurels . It languishes in what economist Ross Garnaut calls " the great complacency " . <p> Every major policy responsible for Australia 's economic success was in place by 2001 . The last piece of substantive economic reform was the introduction of GST in 2001 . As in New Zealand , the GDP growth picture is much less impressive on a per capita basis , particularly over the last ten years . This is currently being experienced in the form of an extended period of stagnating wages . Journalist David Uren highlighted the reality in early 2018 -- " Australians have endured their longest period of falling living standards in more than a quarter of a century as growth in costs outstripped earnings for the fifth consecutive quarter , leaving households worse off than they were six years ago . As think tank executive John Roskam pointed out in 2017 , " in 2005 only seven countries had a lower level of government debt than Australia . Now 50 countries do " . <p> Australia 's rising government debt reflects an unbroken run of federal budget deficits for the last decade . Year after year various federal Treasurers have produced a current deficit but earnestly promised surpluses just around the corner -- an eternal treadmill of disappointment . The recurring refrain from these latter-day St Augustines has been give me deficit chastity and fiscal continence , but just not yet . While light is finally appearing at the end of the tunnel , it may yet be extinguished by politicians seeking to buy the 2019 federal election with promises of tax cuts and increased government spending . <p> A decade of deficits seems both remarkable and disheartening during a period in which resource-rich Australia has experienced the greatest mining boom in its history . By comparison , Australia has the Future Fund of just US $100 billion ( to meet US $150 billion of public sector employee superannuation liabilities ) and record government debt . <p> Policy making beyond the economy is often equally lamentable . The most crucial area for the country 's future is education . The best measure of the relative performance of Australia 's education system is the Programme for International Student Assessment ( PISA ) . Its most recent report from 2015 makes depressing reading for Australians . The average performance for 15-year olds has declined steadily over a ten year period in every subject measured -- science , mathematics , and reading . In the same subjects there has been a constant decline in the share of top performers and a constant increase in the share of low performers . <p> Indigenous affairs is another stunning example of policy failure , whether @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ gap ' or the on-going debacle of constitutional recognition . The government also seems incapable of resolving the problem of the offshore detention of refugees , a problem that does more to tarnish Australia 's international reputation than any other . The National Broadband Network is a source of national embarrassment . Other countries , including New Zealand , appear able to create much faster and more reliable broadband networks at a fraction of the cost . Few things unite Australians quite like their shared hostility to the seemingly doomed NBN . <p> For pure political malfunction , nothing approaches the fiasco of Australia 's energy policy . Journalist Paul Kelly calls it " the catastrophic Australian legacy of the past decade " . With vast reserves of coal , oil , natural gas , and uranium , and limitless supplies of sun , wind , and waves , Australia ranks as one of the most energy blessed nations on earth . Wherever the policy makers stand on climate change , they should be able to ensure that Australians enjoy a reliable and affordable supply of energy .
