<p> Canterbury medical officer of health Dr Alistair Humphrey and Environment Canterbury ( ECan ) chief scientist Tim Davie spoke to media at the Ashley River in Rangiora this week after two rivers in North Canterbury were affected by benthic ( river bottom ) cyanobacteria . <p> Last week , ECan and the Canterbury District Health Board ( CDHB ) issued health warnings for the Ashley/Rakahuri River , near the Rangiora-Loburn bridge and for a short stretch of the Waimakariri River where the Otukaikino water mixes into the Waimakariri , near the State Highway 1 and Main North Rd bridges . <p> SUPPLIED <p> Toxic algae ( cyanobacteria ) can be toxic to people and fatal to dogs . <p> Other access points along the river may have cyanobacteria present and people are advised to check for signs before accessing the water . <p> Some factors contributing to the contamination were outside ECan 's control , such as climate change , but it was working with farmers to improve farming practices and plans to reduce nitrate levels in rivers . <p> Davie said cyanobacteria appeared as dark brown or black mats attached to rocks along the riverbed . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a rock covered in cyanobacteria from the Ashley/Rakahuri River in North Canterbury . <p> It often had a strong musty smell that was particularly attractive to dogs , but it was highly toxic to them and should not be touched . <p> " Dogs and children should be kept well away from areas with cyanobacteria .
