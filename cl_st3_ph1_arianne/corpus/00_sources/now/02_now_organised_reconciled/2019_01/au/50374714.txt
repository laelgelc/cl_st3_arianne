<h> MP chauffeur-driven cars should be electric <h> By Greg Partridge <p> 23 January 2019 -- 12:00am <p> The NSW government has just announced its policy on electric vehicles . It includes $5 million dedicated to delivering new charging infrastructure and a 10 per cent target for electric vehicles in the NSW Government 's passenger vehicle fleet by 2020-21 . <p> All well and good as far as it goes , although a 10 per cent target is n't very ambitious . However it may well be an improvement on what is happening at Federal level . Just before Christmas , when minds were festive-full , the federal government chose to issue a notice that it would probably rather went un-noticed . News trickled out that politicians may soon be chauffeured to work in brand new BMWs or Mercedes , as the Commonwealth looks to upgrade its fleet of official vehicles known as COMCAR . <p> There are reasons why the government would n't want to draw attention to the fact that is is replacing the ageing fleet . The government 's own figures on all transport emissions show a projected increase of 3 per cent by 2020 -- with the biggest contributor being road transport . <p> But what about the cost to the taxpayer of these COMCAR vehicles ? <p> Advertisement <p> Early this year , the finance department will begin trialling new cars and BMW is considered by industry insiders to be the preferred manufacturer to replace the ageing Australian-built Holden Caprice sedans . <p> The luxury vehicles that the government is looking at retail for about $100,000 including the BMW 5 series ( 520d and 530d ) , as well as the Mercedes E220 both of which are likely to be among the models strongly favoured by the Commonwealth . <p> A BMW 520d to be considered for chauffeur-driven MPs . <p> COMCAR is used to transport federal politicians , judges and other dignitaries to official @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ would be to see the people we have elected into high office using vehicles that are n't contributing to the pollution of our cities and ill-health of citizens . <p> How much more impressive would it be if they were being charged using solar energy , to underline the elected leaders ' commitment to a sustainable future for us and future generations . <p> At a state level , the ACT is ahead of the game . As part of the territory 's target to become carbon-neutral by 2025 , the Barr government announced last year that by 2021 , it will stop buying cars with combustion engines . It 's a move designed to encourage its 400,000 residents to follow suit and switch to electric vehicles . <p> As a host of affordable mid-range EVs come onto the Australian market in 2019 , that makes the ACT 's move timely . Hyundai , Nissan , Audi , Mercedes-Benz and Jaguar are all introducing new models with batteries included . In Australia , unlike 80 per cent of the world , we have no mandatory standards for our cars fuel efficiency . As a result , our cars on average need more petrol to run than most other countries . If the federal government wants to act on climate change it has to start putting its money where its mouth is .
