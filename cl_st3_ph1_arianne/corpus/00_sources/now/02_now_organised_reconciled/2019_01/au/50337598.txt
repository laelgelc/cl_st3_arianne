<h> Choosing to live in the present has consequences for the future <p> In the middle of last week 's heat-wave I woke , at two in the morning , to remember a villa I 'd stayed at in Mosul . The original owners had placed beds there so you could lie on the roof at night , cooled by gentle wisps of wind floating up from the Tigris after the sweltering , 42 degree days . <p> That 's what 8000-odd years of continuous human habitation gets you -- architecture that actually works . ( The ruins of biblical Ninevah , in 650BC the biggest city in the world , lie on Mosul 's outskirts ) . <p> Sleeping out , on a roof , related far better to that environment than the square boxes and tiled roofs in which we live today . Now we 're so dependent on air-conditioning and cheap , prefabricated designs that we 've even lost the art of adding a simple thing , like a sleep-out for a verandah .
