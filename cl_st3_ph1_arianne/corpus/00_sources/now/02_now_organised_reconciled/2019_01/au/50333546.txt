Such is the conclusion of the latest report by the World Health Organization ( WHO ) , released last month to coincide with the COP24 climate conference in Katowice , Poland . The report makes clear that immediate action on climate change is necessary to mitigate the extensive health impacts of climate change and that meeting the goals of the Paris Agreement would save one million lives from reductions in air pollution alone . <p> The direct impacts of increasing global temperatures include death , injury , and illness as a result of temperature extremes . <p> Each year , air pollution kills an estimated 7 million people . The burning of fossil fuels results in emissions that are the main drivers of climate change . In the European Union and in the United States , while varied , reductions in such costs could be substantial ( covering between 7-84% and 10-41% respectively ) . Shifting rainfall patterns are likely to affect the spread of the Aedes aegypti mosquito : the vector for diseases including dengue fever , zika , yellow fever , and chikungunya . <p> Climate change also impacts human systems , including agriculture and the quality , nutrition , and availability of food .
