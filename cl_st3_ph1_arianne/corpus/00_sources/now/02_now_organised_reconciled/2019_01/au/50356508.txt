<h> There are big economic risks in Labor 's imputation plan <p> By Adrian Blundell-Wignall <p> 15 Jan 2019 -- 11:00 PM <p> In the Trumpian tradition that if you repeat something often enough it will become an alternative truth , Labor keep repeating a false morality tale : imputation credit refunds for retired people who do n't pay tax is " so unfair " , it is " such a loophole " , " millionaires are getting thousands of dollars of taxpayers ' money " , and so on . Politics in Australia needs improvement . Promoting bad policy through fake news is not a good start . <p> Imputation is one of the most important of Australia 's economic policies . It solves the problem in classical tax arrangements whereby corporate interest costs are deductible while dividends are not . The double tax of dividends creates an enormous incentive to prefer debt over equity . Countries under such regimes see more share buybacks , lower investment , increased financial innovation ; and periodic debt crises . Labor 's proposal punishes a subclass of savers , but not industry and other funds that will get the full benefit of their credits . It punishes those who looked after themselves for retirement . Politicians have no idea of the future world retirees may face : low returns , climate change , the reconfiguration of world trade , or military conflict . <p> But I want to focus on the serious macro issues raised by this proposal . <p> Company investment is funded from shareholders ' equity -- retained earnings and new equity issuance . If the investment fails , this equity is wiped out . Debt , on the other hand , is not wiped out , pushing up a company 's non-survival risk . <p> Financial fragility risk is associated with excessive debt . It is already an acute risk with low interest rates . For some years credit has been an issuers ' market . This is because investors are scrambling for yield , and riskier companies are more able to issue lower-coupon , covenant-lite securities . <p> Denying imputation refunds for tax-exempts will cause the tax bias to shift in favour of debt and away from equity .
