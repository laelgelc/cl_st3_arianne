<h> Andrews government rules out re-routing Great Ocean Road at risk of erosion <h> By Royce Millar &amp; Sumeyya Ilanbey <p> 11 January 2019 -- 11:16am <p> The Andrews government has ruled out re-routing key sections of the Great Ocean Road that experts say are at risk of being washed away within years . <p> Expert reports commissioned by the government have warned that the heritage-listed road is " at risk " and could be " compromised " within five years at locations around Apollo Bay . <p> In a recent report , engineers GHD raised re-routing of the road as a long-term option . The report followed dramatic erosion at Apollo Bay and neighbouring Mounts Bay in 2017 and 2018 . <p> But Environment Minister Lily D'Ambrosio has ruled out the move , flagging that other options - including groynes and other structures - were more likely instead . <p> Advertisement <p> Loading <p> " The report also puts actions for consideration that can mitigate the need to re-route that road , " Ms D'Ambrosio told 3AW on Friday morning . <p> " Those options are being considered , will continue to be considered , and all the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ensure that road , that route remains in place and there 's no reason to re-route that road , " said Ms D'Ambrosio . <p> " The road has been there for 100 years and will continue to be there for many many years , decades to come .
