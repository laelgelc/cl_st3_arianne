<h> From Skyrail to sewer , Victoria embraces a new state of play <h> New public spaces are helping to combat the effects of increased density and climate change . <h> By Ray Edgar <p> 31 December 2018 -- 5:11pm <p> The Underline , it 's been dubbed . A 17-kilometre stretch of bike paths and green space created in the shadow of the nine-metre-high Skyrail that extends along the Pakenham line from Caulfield to Dandenong . As trains trundle into sleek stations overhead , locals play basketball , clamber on artificial rock walls and do tricks on scooters . Children enjoy bright new playgrounds . Picnic areas and dog parks dot the reclaimed space . The Underline reflects -- if not underlines -- the changing shape of public spaces and particularly parks in the 21st century . <p> Victoria has long thought of itself as the Garden State . But the Underline is a long way from the 19th century gardens -- Treasury , Flagstaff , Carlton , Kings Domain -- that ring the CBD . Lord Mayor Sally Capp wants to create a " Greenline " along the Yarra 's north side extending to Docklands . Unlike the parks of old , these linear parks are both destination and journey . <p> " The typology of the park is changing , " says Claire Martin , director of the Australian Institute of Landscape Architects ( AILA ) and associate director with Oculus . " In the 19th century our idea of public space was the street and the garden . The garden was the destination . It was discrete . It had a particular character . In the 20th century public spaces became more around events and programming and more plaza and urban conditions . We 're coming to another stage in the 21st century where you are ending up with hybridised spaces . There 's the pleasure of discovering hidden gems , such as the redesigned Parliament House garden . Two of the most successful public spaces -- Federation Square and Bryant Park in New York -- rely on full-time staff to program events . Architectural pavilions enliven the NGV 's " backyard " , emphasising the gallery 's role as a public space . And of course , there is that most sacred Australian space , the beach . <p> " Public space seems to be the zeitgeist at the moment , " says Kirsten Bauer , a director of Aspect studios , which designed the Underline . In 2017 , a 100-metre pilot project was constructed at Williams Landing . Like the High Line it involves the transformation of old industrial infrastructure . It also incorporates a stormwater harvesting system to ensure the sustainable irrigation of the green space . <p> Meanwhile Melbourne Water 's Reimagining Your Creek program relies on community involvement to remove concrete channels and restore creeks to their natural state . Both projects are part of the Victorian government 's $150 million election commitment to create 6500 hectares of parkland and new walking and bike trails across Melbourne . <p> These are encouraging signs . Who pays for it ? Who looks after it ? For whose benefit ? <p> As population pressures intensify , public spaces have to work harder . But so do people -- planners , designers and the public .
