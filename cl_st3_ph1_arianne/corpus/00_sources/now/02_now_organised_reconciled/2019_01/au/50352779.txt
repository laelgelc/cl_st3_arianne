<h> Scott Morrison wants to be friends with Pacific , and Pacific has a shopping list <p> By Stephen Howes <p> 14 Jan 2019 -- 6:00 PM <p> A visit by an Australian prime minister to a Pacific Island country is a rare event . Such a visit , other than to attend a regional meeting or to Papua New Guinea ( regarding offshore detention ) is almost unheard of . <p> Scott Morrison 's visit to Vanuatu and Fiji starting on Wednesday is a sign of just how much has changed in Australia 's official thinking with regards to the Pacific , and how much more priority the region now receives in official thinking . <p> Australia 's " step up " , as it is called , has been gathering pace for a while , but the shift in momentum even in the last few months is tangible . The annual Pacific Islands Forum was held in September , just after Morrison replaced Turnbull , and the new PM withdrew his attendance . Under Howard and Downer , the Coalition was staunchly against providing any special labour market access to the Pacific . That started to change under Labor and the shift in policy was accelerated by Julie Bishop . <p> Vanuatu now has access to both the well-established Seasonal Worker Program ( SWP , for short-term agricultural work ) and the new Pacific Labour Scheme ( PLS , for longer-term work of any sort outside the capital cities ) . But Fiji has access only to the SWP , and that only recently , owing to the long after-effects of the 2006 coup .
