<h> Coastal areas reel under climate change impacts <p> The south-western coastal belt of the country is one of the most disaster prone zones where the natural disasters are now a regular phenomenon <p> Gazi Moniruzzaman from Khulna : Experts have underscored the need for improving disaster management system in the south-western costal area with a view to tackling storm , cyclone , tidal surge , drought , river erosion and water-logging . Strong disaster management system can reduce the affect from the natural disaster in the south-wester region in in the country , they said . The south-western coastal belt of the country is one of the most disaster prone zones in the world where the natural disasters are now a regular phenomenon . Due to climate change the frequency and intensity of disasters are increasing , threaten lives and livelihoods . A large number of people are become ultra poor due to disaster and climate change effect every year . Referring that cyclonic storm Sidr on November 15 , 2007 and Aila on May 25 , 2009 battered south-western districts . According to unofficial sources , Sidr claimed over thousand lives in Southkhali union under Sharankhola upazila @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ near the Baleshwar River were rendered homeless by tidal surges . Besides , at least 190 people were killed in Gabura and Padmopukur union of Satkhira district and Dacope and Koyra upazila of Khulna , 4 lakh people became homeless , 237 km embankments were completely and 1,557 km partially were damaged , crops on 3.23 lakh acres of land were damaged when the Aila hit the coastal areas in the country on May 25 , 2009 . Dr Ahsan Uddin an environmentalist in his research recently in Khulna said the number of seasonal or temporarily migrated population will be increased from 10 percent to 20 percent in the country . Bangladesh hardly has ability to manage the climate-induced displaced population because of high density of population . Research shows that climate change will have a massive impact on the habitations , livelihoods , environment , irrigation , navigation , ecology , biodiversity , weather and under ground water levels . Climate change will also influence the availability of water for human consumption and for food production . Prof Dr M Rakib Uddin , head of Environmental Science Discipline of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the regulation of river flows by a series of dams , barrages and embankments for diverting upstream water for various human needs and for flood control has caused large reduction in freshwater inflow which has seriously affected the biodiversity due to an increase in salinity and changes in sedimentation . Further threats arise from global climate change , especially sea level rise along with its salinity , which has already caused death to many big trees of the largest mangrove forest Sundarbans , he added . Cholera attacks may significantly rise in the country due to extreme climate changes like drought or flooding that influence the pattern of rainfall . Md Amirul Azad , Officer-in-Charge of Khulna Met office said ' Daily Industry ' that the amount of rainfall in the Khulna region was going down . He said the total rainfall in Khulna was 2,137 milimetres in 2006 and 2,130 milimetres in 2007 . The total rainfall reduced to 1,595 milimetres in 2008 while the region experienced the record low rainfall of 1,358 milimetres in 2010 , 1,948 milimetres in 2011 , 1,646 milimetres in 2012 , 2,070 milimetres @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 2015 , 2,221 milimetres in 2016 , 2,283 milimetres in 2017 and only 1,050 milimetres in 2018 . Deputy Director of Department of Agriculture Extension ( DAE ) , Khulna Md Abdul Latif said , " The coastal rivers get swelled with tidewater due to the effect of climate change and the water over flowing the river banks enter the arable land with adverse effects causing loss of fertility . " He added farming pattern in Khulna has undergone a transformation owing to climate change , jeopardizing the livelihood of local farmers . Besides , increasing salinity in coastal land is making the region prone to flash floods . It also severely affecting aqua life , bio-diversity and threatening food security in the coastal belt , Md Latif further said . Bashudeb Chandra Ghosh , a farmer of Dasbaria village under Koyra upazila said , climate change had prevented them from planting different crops . Because of while there was heavy rain flooding croplands sometimes , there was also the problem of scanty rainfall . We are witnessing a poor harvest now as the dry spells are longer these days @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ two decades , Bashudeb added . Affected fishermen and farmers are now searching for alternative livelihood and urging the authorities to find out salinity resistant varieties of crops to save them from crop losses and the burden of loans .
