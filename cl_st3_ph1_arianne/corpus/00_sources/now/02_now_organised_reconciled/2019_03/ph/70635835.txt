<h> Earth Hour focuses on plastics <p> PULLING the plug when devices are not in use , avoiding the use of disposable utensils , and recycling and upcycling materials are only a few of the many ways to practice sustainable living . <p> The World Wide Fund ( WWF ) Philippines continues to celebrate Earth Hour this year with the theme #Connect2Earth which was first launched in 2018 and brings to focus the issue of single-use plastics . <p> In a report released by Greenpeace in 2017 , the Philippines was named as the third-biggest plastic polluter of oceans after China and Indonesia . <p> According to the WWF-Philippines website , " Single-use plastics ( SUPs ) , which are used on an average of seven minutes , take more than 100 years to decompose . Worse , they break up into smaller pieces called microplastic . These can be ingested by fish , and make it up the food chain to humans . <p> " The culture of disposables is difficult . There is no value . It is cheap and so easy to throw away , " WWF-Philippines President and CEO Joel Palma told BusinessWorld shortly after @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Ortigas . <p> WWF-Philippines hopes to create further awareness on the dangers of single-use plastics with the #AyokoNgPlastik movement which is adapted from WWF 's " No Plastics in Nature " initiative , and WWF-Philippines believes that Earth Hour 2019 can help send the message for others to act upon plastic pollution and mobilizing future legislations on plastic waste management . <p> As part of the movement , Gia Ibay , Earth Hour Philippines national director , noted that WWF-Philippines is currently working with different institutions , organizations , and LGUs in their efforts to educate the public about the reduction of plastic waste and resource conservation . <p> THE SWITCH OFF Introduced in Australia in 2007 , Earth Hour is the world 's largest grassroots movement for the environment . <p> Now on its 12th year , Earth Hour 2019 will hold its main switch-off event on March 30 at the Globe Circuit Event Grounds in Makati from 5 to 10 p.m. , with the actual switch-off at 8:30 p.m .
