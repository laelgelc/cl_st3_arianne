<p> Researchers harnessed datasets spanning two decades to examine how two species of Arctic wildlife -- beluga whales , also known as white whales , and ringed seals -- are adapting to their changing homes . <p> Both species traditionally hunt for food in areas with sea ice and particularly at so-called tidal glacier fronts , where glaciers meet the ocean . <p> But with climate change melting sea ice and prompting glaciers to retreat , researchers in Norway decided to look at whether and how animals in the affected areas were adapting . <p> " The Arctic is the bellwether of climate change , " the researchers wrote . <p> " With the rapid pace of change rendering genetic adaptation unfeasible , " they reasoned that behavioural and dietary changes " will likely be the first observable responses within ecosystems " . <p> They compared datasets produced by trackers attached to seals and whales over two sets of time periods . <p> For the seals , they compared tracker data from 28
