<h> " <p> Jo Darkwa , professor of energy storage technologies , University of Nottingham <h> Filling the gap <p> " In the past , this waste material was under-utilised . However , feasibility studies indicate that cocoa pod husks could be converted into valuable biofuels and become an important energy supply for rural areas that have only 15 per cent electricity coverage at present . " <p> The plan is to design , build and put into operation small-scale bio-power electricity generation units that would burn cocoa pod husks in a gasification system . Each unit , which would include a gasifier , a small generator and a solar drier and pelletiser , would cost an estimated US$50,000 . <p> Ghana 's population , now 30 million , is growing fast ; about 80 per cent of households in the country use wood as the main source of fuel for cooking and heating water . <p> As a result , Ghana 's forests are under considerable pressure , with severe consequences not only for wildlife and ecosystems but also for the climate . <p> Forests are an essential element in the fight against climate change ; trees absorb @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ prevent global warming . <p> " Undoubtedly , provision of sustainable energy services through cocoa pod husks would go a long way towards improving the quality of lives and thus alleviate poverty in rural communities as well as fight against climate change " , Professor Darkwa told Climate News Network . <p> The aim is not only to build sources of sustainable energy ; collection , treatment and processing of the pod husks would also create jobs and provide much-needed incomes in rural communities . <p> The specialists at Nottingham are collaborating on the project with the Ghana Cocoa Board and various other organisations in Ghana . <h> Thanks for reading to the end of this story ! <p> We would be grateful if you would consider joining as a member of The EB Circle .
