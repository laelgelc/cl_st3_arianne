<h> Duty on minor produce will violate forest rights : Experts <h> Last Updated : Wednesday 20 March 2019 <p> Photo : Getty Images <p> The Ministry of Environment , Forest &amp; Climate Change ( MoEF&amp;CC ) in the first draft to amend the Indian Forest Act , 1927 ( IFA ) has proposed imposing duty on not just timber but also other forest produce . But , experts say , this is a violation of the forest dwellers ' rights . <p> " The central government and state government in their respective jurisdiction may levy a duty in such manner , at such places and at such rates as it may declare by notification in the official gazette on all timber or other forest produce , " according to the draft . <p> However , experts say these provisions violate the rights given to forest dwelling communities under the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers ( Recognition of Forest Rights ) Act , 2006 ( FRA ) . <p> The Act gives the forest dwelling communities rights over these MFPs , and gives gram sabhas the authority to issue transit permit for transporting them outside the forest @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ violation of the power vested with the gram sabha under FRA and The Provisions of the Panchayats ( Extension to Scheduled Areas ) Act , 1996 . This clearly is an attempt to redefine the IFA cunningly by giving supreme power to the forest department , " says Chitta Ranjan Pani , an Odisha-based researcher working on livelihood issues of tribal communities . <p> Moreover , the amendments also propose a forest development cess , over and above the tax imposed on the forest produce . <p> " Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act or the provisions of any other Act , the state government may levy a cess , hereinafter called forest development cess , at a rate not exceeding 10 per cent of the value assessed of the mining products removed from the forests and water used for irrigation or in industries , " reads the draft . <p> This cess will be kept in a special fund which will be used exclusively for reforestation , forest protection and other ancillary purposes . <p> Tribal communities depend on MFP for food , fodder , shelter , medicines and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ seasons , particularly for primitive tribal groups such as hunter , gatherers , and the landless . In fact , according to Planning Commission data , tribals derive 20-40 per cent of their annual income from MFP . <p> " This draft points towards an autocratic management of forest rather than making forest governance more democratic , as envisioned by FRA to undo the historical injustice meted out to the forest dwelling communities , " says Pani . <p> We are a voice to you ; you have been a support to us .
