<p> The past may be a surprisingly useful guide for predicting responses to future climate change . This is especially important for places where extreme weather has been the norm for a long time , such as the Indian subcontinent . Being able to reliably predict summer monsoon rainfall is critical to plan for the devastating impact it can have on the 1.7 billion people who live in the region . <p> The onset of India 's summer monsoon is linked to heat differences between the warmer land and cooler ocean , which causes a shift in prevailing wind direction . Winds blow over the Indian Ocean , picking up moisture , which falls as rain over the subcontinent from June to September . <p> The monsoon season can bring drought and food shortages or severe flooding , depending on how much rain falls and in what duration . Understanding how the monsoon responded to an abrupt climate transition in the past can therefore help scientists better understand its behaviour in the future . <p> When we researched this weather system 's ancient past , we found it was highly sensitive to climate warming 130,000 years ago .
