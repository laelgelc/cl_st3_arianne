<h> Why cities are focal areas to address climate change <h> Last Updated : Tuesday 12 March 2019 <p> The impacts of climate change are growing and projected to reach dangerous levels in absence of concerted actions . The Paris Agreement and resultant Paris Rule Book for its implementation has paved the way for largely decentralised climate action . The focus is , therefore , on national and sub-national actions with domestic stakeholders to address climate change . In this backdrop , cities acquire greater role in driving climate outcomes . <p> The world is urbanising in an incremental manner and this trend is set to continue . United Nations ' data suggests that cities are responsible for 70 per cent of the total greenhouse gas ( GHG ) emissions , though they occupy just 3 per cent of the total land space . UN Habitat further projects that 60 per cent of the world population would live in cities by 2030 . <p> Hence , much of the population growth in coming times would happen in cities . How cities would develop in future would significantly impact emission profiles . Such strategies involve low carbon development , energy efficiency measures in transport and buildings as well as use of renewable energy sources . For instance , in Cyprus , 93 per cent of the dwellings use solar water heating resulting in major reduction in environmental pollution every year . <p> In Nuevo Leon in Mexico , ECOCASA project is being successfully run by the Mexican government and International Development Banks , which incentivises housing developers to build homes for low income category groups , designed to minimise GHG emissions demonstrating that sustainability is compatible with affordability . <p> In another such example , as per the UN Habitat global report on Human Settlements 2012 , the green lighting programme in Beijing has been able to save 14.4 megawatts ( MW ) of energy and reduced annual energy @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the replacement of normal bulbs with energy efficient lights , since the programme started in 2004 . <p> Likewise , the super congested city of Medell ? n in Columbia has a cable car transport network which carries 50,000 persons per day . In the city of Sihanoukville in Cambodia , approaches of mainstreaming climate change into already existing plans , with involvement of local stakeholders at every stage of planning and implementation , have proved to be successful in making the tourist city resilient and resource efficient . <p> Challenges involved <p> To assess GHG emissions arising from a city and determining adaptive capacity is necessary to derive specific solutions to climate change .
