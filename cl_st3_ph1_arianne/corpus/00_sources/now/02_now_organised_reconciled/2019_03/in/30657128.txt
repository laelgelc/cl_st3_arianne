<h> Americans in cool states misjudge threat from rising heat waves <p> NEW YORK , March 12 ( Thomson Reuters Foundation ) - Americans most at risk from more frequent and intense heat waves tend to misjudge the deadly dangers hot spells can pose to their health , scientists said on Tuesday . <p> People 's vulnerability to heat waves is growing as cooler states become hotter , in part because air conditioning and other ways to cool down are less common there , according to a study in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America . <p> Right now , people in cooler areas " do n't experience hotter weather as frequently , " said co-author Peter Howe from Utah State University . <p> That means " they have less of that experience ... to handle those hot days " . <p> Global temperatures are on course for a 3 to 5 degrees Celsius ( 5 to 9 degrees Fahrenheit ) rise this century , overshooting a global target of limiting the increase to 2C ( 3.6F ) or less , the U.N . World Meteorological Organization has said . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ soon become more common as annual average temperatures - already a degree higher than in 1901 - continue to rise , according to the National Climate Assessment , a U.S. government report . <p> After surveying more than 9,000 people in all 50 states , researchers carrying out the heat wave study discovered that people in northern states , including in the northern Midwest , had fewer health concerns about extreme heat than those living in the country 's south . <p> Residents of the normally temperate Rocky Mountains and Appalachians were among the least concerned by extreme heat , the study said , while those in sweltering Hawaii , Texas and Louisiana were most worried . <p> But previous research has shown that as climate change pushes temperatures up , residents of the northeastern United States and those living in high elevations are at particularly high risk of heat-related complications , the researchers said . <p> That 's because they often lack air conditioning and other means of combatting extreme heat , and are less acclimatized to potentially dangerous temperatures . <p> Elderly people also were often oblivious to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ though they constitute one of the highest risk groups , the findings showed . <p> " People generally , if they are older , they do n't consider themselves to be elderly , " he said - and so do n't see themselves as particularly at risk . <p> Heat in 2017 killed more people than any other weather-related disaster except floods , according to the National Weather Service .
