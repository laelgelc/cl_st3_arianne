<h> Hey politics , wake up to sustainability <p> The pace at which the world is extracting and consuming materials -- metals , mineral , fuels and biomass for food , livestock and energy -- is not just unsustainable , given that these are finite resources , but harmful to human well-being and of the planet . The 2019 Global Resources Outlook , the most recent assessment by the United Nations Environment-sponsored International Resource Panel , finds that the extraction and processing of materials , fuels and food is responsible for more than 90% of biodiversity loss and water stress , and about half of the total greenhouse gas emissions . If the world is to achieve the goals of the Paris Agreement on climate and the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development , then countries must decouple economic growth from material consumption by moving to sustainable consumption and production pathways . <p> As a lower-middle income country , India 's material footprint is smaller than its high and upper-middle income counterparts ' . However , as India grows rapidly , so would its material footprint , and all the faster , as more of its people are pulled out @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of a materials-intensive growth model : polluted air , water stress and climate change-induced weather events . The shift to sustainable consumption and production pathways through the adoption of a circular economy is , therefore , imperative if India is to deliver on its promise of eradicating poverty and inclusive development . For this , the country has to move beyond resource efficiency to an economy in which products and materials are designed in a manner that they can be reused , remanufactured , recycled or recovered . For a resource-constrained country with huge developmental deficits , the circular economy provides the best way forward . <p> This election season , as political parties across the spectrum make promises of a better future to the people , they would do well to integrate sustainability into their platforms . Anything else will make the promise of inclusive growth for all a distant dream . <p> This piece appeared as an editorial opinion in the print edition of The Economic Times .
