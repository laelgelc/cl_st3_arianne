<h> Westmorland Gazette launches The Big Clean campaign 2019 <h> George Romney pupils who care about the environment kick of The Big Clean campaign 2019 <p> THE Westmorland Gazette this week launches a campaign aimed at keeping our streets free of rubbish and litter . <p> The Big Clean campaign , run in collaboration with our sister paper The Mail , aims to encourage our local communities to take pride in the areas where they live . <p> Sponsored by Kimberly Clark and Spirit Energy , it calls upon schools , community groups , businesses , residents associations and individuals to do what they can to clear up any litter-strewn patches of land in the south Cumbria , Eden and north Lancashire areas . <p> It might be a park or green that is beginning to look a little scruffy . It might just be checking pavements and hedgerows and picking up items that have been carelessly discarded . <p> Andrew Thomas , Editor of The Westmorland Gazette , said : " It is really important everyone takes pride in the community in which they live , work and play . This campaign will hopefully help to improve not @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ parks , playgrounds and the streets in which we live . " <p> Helping to launch the new campaign were pupils at a Furness school . <p> Youngsters from George Romney Junior School in Dalton donned high vis jackets and carried litter pickers as they carried out the first Big Clean of 2019 , collecting litter in the school grounds . <p> They also showed they really care about the environment by warning people about climate change . The pupils carried placards as they ventured into the streets to talk to the community about protecting the environment . <p> It came on the same day as thousands of other schools across the world protested as part of Climate Action Day . <p> Headteacher David Reddy said : " We first discussed doing something for this day with our school council . <p> " The pupils wanted to go on strike from lessons like a lot of others have but instead we decided to go out and speak to people in Dalton .
