The heating energy could , in principle , be replaced by renewable energy but the decomposition emissions are unavoidable . Some improvements are possible ( for example , depending whether the cement kiln dust is added back to the kiln or sent to landfill ) . However , there are limits to recycling concrete while the building stock is growing -- even if we recycle 100 per cent of demolished concrete . Could timber replace a significant proportion of concrete ? What sort of volumes are required ? <p> Global concrete production is approximately 11,000 million m3/year . Current timber production is around 3,800 millionm3/year -- and our use of wood is causing deforestation even at present levels . Significant replacement of concrete with timber might reduce CO2 emissions , but it would dramatically increase deforestation . <p> If we wish to reduce our impact we need to reduce our consumption , our production , and live within the planet 's means <p> So far , this may seem bleak -- and rightly so . Concrete has an enormous impact on the environment not because it 's intrinsically bad , but because we use so much of it . Possible replacements also have huge impacts when used at scale . There are no technological fixes to make its impact sustainable ; no magic bullets . If we wish to reduce our impact we need to reduce our consumption , our production , and live within the planet 's means . <p> Efficiency improvements alone will not @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and in the context of a rising population , this means a dramatic fall in per-capita consumption . <p> Any use of a non-renewable resource is fundamentally unsustainable . Concrete uses fossil fuels to make cement , and sand and gravel for the aggregate . CO2 is emitted and limited resources are consumed , so any use of concrete can not be sustained on long timescales ; it is therefore incumbent on people to use as little as possible , and to make the very best use we can of the quantities that we use . <p> It is important that architects understand , and advise on , the wider impacts of their projects -- which means sometimes placing such issues above the commercial interests of their clients .
