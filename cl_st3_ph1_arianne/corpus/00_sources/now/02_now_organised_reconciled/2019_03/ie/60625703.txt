<p> A number of environmental groups have challenged research by the Economic and Social Research Institute that Irish consumers would pay significantly less for their gas if liquefied natural gas , or LNG , facilities were given the go-ahead . <p> The research showed that combining imports of LNG at a proposed terminal on the Shannon estuary with a storage facility at Islandmagee , in Co Antrim , would lower household gas bills . <p> The projects , which have the backing of the EU to integrate European energy markets , would lower prices , as Ireland could tap into world market prices , which are significantly lower , and help the country to transfer from oil and coal . <p> But the green groups , which include Safety Before LNG , Friends of the Irish Environment , and Friends of the Earth , said the ESRI bulletin did n't take account of the costs of climate change involved in LNG gas " and the economic consequences related to the need to fully decarbonise our economy by 2050 " . <p> In an open letter to the ESRI , the groups claim that there are hidden costs with LNG @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ industry . There is also an operational expense and a shipping expense . <p> " On top of that , the process is complicated and requires up to 25% of the energy content of the gas , making it , therefore , a risky bet for a country that counts on gas , at a time when we need to get completely off fossil fuels , " the letter said . <p> " Wind and solar are now cheaper than coal and fossil gas in many regions . This means new fossil gas capacity often displaces new wind and solar , rather than old coal , " it said .
