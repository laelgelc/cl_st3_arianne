<h> Students have ' dung ' a big favour for climate change <h> Story by Deirdre Kelly <h> Wednesday , 13th March , 2019 1:18pm <h> Share <h> Share this with : <h> Copy the link <p> SHOVELLING cow muck is all in a day 's research for a group of Mayo students . <p> And what has started with a small load of manure could be a big game changer in tackling climate change . <p> A group of transition years from St. Louis Community School in Kiltimagh have found that by switching to the Aberdeen Angus breed a farmer could cut their greenhouse emissions almost in half . That 's because the Angus produces just half the amount of dung as a Charolais -- the preferred breed of many Irish farmers -- does . <p> ' Moo Says the Angus ' is the group name for the students -- John Connaughton , Nathan Hession , Orlaith Gilmartin , Ava Charlton and James Cunnane -- who are taking part in the Irish Angus Beef Producers schools competition this year . They will showcase their findings in Croke Park at the finals this Thursday . <p> As well as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ consumers and visited the Connacht Rugby team where they did a blind tasting . The verdict : 70% preferred an Angus steak . <p> When working on their project the group noticed a gap in research into the Aberdeen Angus and waste production . <p> Nathan and John did the dirty work . On both their farms they each had two 550kg bullocks -- one Aberdeen Angus and one Charolais . The slats in their pens were taped over and the bullocks were fed the same food each day -- 10kg of buffalo beef nuts and straw . <p> Fourteen hours after being fed they collected the manure produced in bin bags , took it away and weighed it . <p> The Angus , they found , produced 2.3kg of manure each day while the output from the Charolais was 4.2kg . That 's a significant 56% less waste , a finding which , even at this early stage of research , is ' very exciting ' they say . <p> The trip to Galway to the home of Connacht Rugby was another highlight where they met with dietician Laura @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and staff , including Tiernan O'Halloran , Davie Heffernan and Shane Donohue and head coach Andy Friend . <p> For farmers on the ground , the Aberdeen Angus breed have a lot going for them , they say , being hardier than other breeds . They also do n't tear up land and are extremely docile , with a calm temperament . <p> Aberdeen Angus is associated with quality , making it a go-to on the shelves for shoppers . <p> Follow the Moo Says the Angus group on Twitter , Facebook or Instagram #farmtofood
