<h> Chimpanzees losing traits in areas of high human impact , study claims <p> Chimpanzees are losing their distinctive behaviours in areas of high human activity , new research suggests . <p> A study of 144 different wild groups in 15 countries found the range of behaviours shown by the animals was reduced in areas where humans had made the biggest mark . <p> The international team of researchers warned that some traits , which have yet to be noticed in the animals , could disappear without intervention . <p> The behaviours of chimpanzees , for example the tools they use and gestures they communicate with , vary between groups . <p> Existing research suggests they may learn from members of their community , meaning each group is culturally different , much like humans.Male chimpanzees groom one another ( Tobias Deschner/Loango Chimpanzee Project/ PA ) <p> Data on 31 chimpanzee behaviours across the groups was compiled for the study . <p> The study found that chimpanzee communities in areas with a high degree of human impact were on average 88% less likely to exhibit the behaviours than groups in areas with the least human impact . <p> These included @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , nuts and honey , as well as the use of tools for hunting and digging . <p> The use of stones , pools and caves was also considered . <p> Human impact was measured using factors such as human population density , roads and rivers . <p> " This pattern was consistent , independent of the grouping or categorisation of behaviours . <p> " On average , chimpanzee behavioural diversity was reduced by 88% when human impact was highest compared to locations with the least human impact . " <p> The researchers suggest that chimpanzee populations in areas with high human activity may be smaller , making it hard to pass on certain traits between generations . <p> The animals may stop displaying some behaviours to avoid attracting human attention and climate change could also be impacting behaviours , they said .
