<h> Great spot : Mediterranean ladybird found in Cork <p> The bryony , unlike many other ladybirds , is a plant-eater and is considered large , measuring 5mm-7mm . <p> By Linda McGrory <p> Monday , March 11 , 2019 - 05:40 AM <p> A ladybird originating in the Mediterranean has been recorded for the first time in Ireland . <p> The bryony ladybird , with its distinctive orange colour and 11 spots , was discovered when it flew into a house in Bandon , Co Cork , last month . <p> It was released but seen again a week later on the exterior of the same home . <p> The sighting was made by Lydia Rose who managed to photograph the insect and submit it for identification . <p> Her find has since been verified by Gill Weyman , a researcher on a joint ladybird conservation project between Fota Wildlife Park and University College Cork , funded by the Irish Research Council . <p> " This is the first record for Ireland and the origin of this individual is not known . It may have arrived by a number of routes , for example , on a plant @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , or by air currents , " said Ms Weyman . <p> " The bryony ladybird , unlike many other ladybirds , is a plant-eater and prefers members of the cucumber family . <p> " It has a preference for the plant white bryony from which it gets its common name , " she added . <p> The herbivore bryony is considered large at 5mm-7mm and fairly benign in terms of its impact on native wildlife . <p> Ms Weyman said it was first recorded in the UK in the 1990s and its movement north " may be as a result of a range of factors including climate change " . <p> Some 20 species of ladybird have been recorded in Ireland . The insect favourite comes with as few as two spots and as many as 24 . <p> Anyone with an interest in ladybirds is asked to submit their findings at biology.ie or via social media @irishladybirds.
