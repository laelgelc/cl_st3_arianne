<h> 40% drop in number of waterbirds spending winter in Ireland <p> The number of waterbirds spending the winter in Ireland has collapsed by 40% in just 20 years . <p> That 's according to a new study by BirdWatch Ireland which found that over the past five years , the number of wintering waterbirds like ducks , geese , swans and wading birds had fallen by 15% . <p> However , since the mid-1990s , this number has fallen by almost 500,000 individual birds - a fall of 40% . <p> Ireland is an important wintering area for these birds which breed at Arctic latitudes and migrate southwards to spend the winter on estuaries , coastal bays , rivers and lakes . <p> The study found that wading bird species , including Knot , Dunlin , Golden Plover and Redshank , have been the worst hit , suffering a combined loss of more than 100,000 individuals ( 19% ) over the past five years . <p> Wildfowl , including 14 species of duck , three species of swan and four species of geese , declined by 28,000 individuals ( 9% ) .
