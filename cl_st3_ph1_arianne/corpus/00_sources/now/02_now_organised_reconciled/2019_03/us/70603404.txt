<p> The most intensive drought ever recorded in Syria lasted from 2006 to 2011 . Water scarcity hit households , businesses and infrastructure , while in the countryside crops failed , livestock died , and entire families moved to the country 's cities . The subsequent eruption of civil war in 2011 led to as many as half a million deaths , as well as massive migration flows to neighbouring countries and beyond , and untold misery . Syria 's war has been a tragic illustration of the central , driving role that water insecurity can play in instability and conflict . <p> This is no surprise . In 2017 alone , water was a major factor in conflict in at least 45 countries , including Syria . Its importance as a resource means that water-related insecurity can easily exacerbate tensions and friction within and between countries . It can be weaponized ; nefarious actors can gain control of , destroy , or redirect access to water to meet their objectives by targeting infrastructure and supplies .
