<p> Our Constitution specifically gives Congress , not the president , the power to authorize federal spending . Congress ' " power of the purse " limits the executive branch from spending the people 's money without the consent of their representatives . This feature reflects a key pillar of our constitutional government : Responsibilities are to be separated between the different branches of government so as to prevent any single branch from centralizing power . <p> Were the president to successfully circumvent Congress using an emergency declaration , not only would our Constitution 's separation of powers be weakened , but a dangerous precedent would be set . Future presidents , frustrated by Congress , could declare national emergencies to unilaterally advance whatever controversial policy they might favor .
