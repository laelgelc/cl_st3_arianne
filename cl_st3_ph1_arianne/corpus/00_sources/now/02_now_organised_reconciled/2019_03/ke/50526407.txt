<p> Lions in Kenya may become extinct in the next 15-20 years if nothing is done to save them , conservationists have said . <p> The Kenya Wildlife Service ( KWS ) says the country is losing 100 lions per year while the current population is estimated to be less than 2,000 . <p> " Loss of habitat and biodiversity due to corruption , climate change , poverty and increased human population pose the greatest threat to conservation of wildlife , protected areas and the country 's ecosystem , " Dr John Waithaka , the director of Africa Protected Areas Congress ( APAC ) , said during its launch last month . <p> In west Africa , lions disappeared 60 years ago while in north Africa they became extinct 150 years ago . <p> CONSERVE <p> As it is , they may also disappear in East Africa if governments do not take stern action to stop the decimation of wildlife and protected areas , Dr Waithaka , who is also co-chair of the International Union for Conservation of Nature ( IUCN ) , observes . <p> The decline has been unabated in the past two decades . In 1975 , the continent had 200,000 lions , but in 1990 there were just 100,000 . <p> ENDANGERED <p> More disturbing is that about 10 years later , the population had declined drastically to only 35,000 . <p> Dr Waithaka , who is also the chair of the KWS board , said in the last 100 years , the lion population in Africa had declined by 96.5 percent . <p> In the last 21 years , it had dropped by 43 percent . The trend may be irreversible , and that is why the lion species was put on the 2013 list of endangered species through the Wildlife Conservation Management Act .
