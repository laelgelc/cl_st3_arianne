<p> The Stanley Soil Management Association ( SSMA ) has been working with the Pembina Valley Conservation District ( PVCD ) to promote shelterbelts . <p> At the SSMA 's annual meeting this past Wednesday , the two organizations gave reports on their initiatives in the community , presentations on renovating shelterbelts instead of removing them , and how shelterbelts can mitigate the effects of climate change . <p> The not-for-profit organization 's mandate is sustainable agriculture ; a technician with SSMA Richard Warkentin , says there was a push 30 years ago to plant shelterbelts , but the understanding of why they 're beneficial seems to have diminished . <p> " Because of the value of land , and other factors , people are feeling shelterbelts are taking away from them . We want to promote the whole concept that shelterbelts are n't taking away from productive croplands , they 're helping out . The thing is once they 're taken out , then they 're gone . " <p> Warkentin notes , not only do shelterbelts help protect topsoil , it sequesters carbon from the air , aiding in small part to the mitigation of climate change @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the region is green ash , which is where the renovations come in says Warkentin . <p> " Ash has been the species of choice , and the ash borer has been found in Manitoba , so we 're looking at diversifying . " <p> He adds one of the potential options is to have several species within a single shelterbelt , reducing the risk of disease or parasites damaging the trees . <p> Shelterbelts are n't the only project the SSMA is working on .
