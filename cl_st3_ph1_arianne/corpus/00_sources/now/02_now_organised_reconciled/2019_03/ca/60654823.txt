<p> A prescribed burn will occur this month on Crater Mountain in B.C. 's Similkameen region . The Okanagan Nation Alliance says it supports the important role of re-establishing prescribed fires on the region 's landscape . <p> The Okanagan Nation Alliance says a planned burn near Keremeos this month will restore forest and grassland health while also reducing wildfire risks . <p> The burn will take place on Crater Mountain , which is due west of Keremeos , and will target 192 hectares . The Okanagan Nation Alliance ( ONA ) says the burn is part of a multi-year land management plan that will target a total of 680 hectares along the eastern slopes of the mountain . <p> According to the ONA , the planned burns will protect nearby Lower Similkameen Indian Band ( LSIB ) members and the community of Keremeos from potential wildfires moving up from the south . <p> " After the devastating wildfires that we experienced in 2018 , it is vital that we implement these practices to enhance wildlife habitat and adapt to the effects of climate change , " said Chief Keith Crow of the LSIB . <p> WATCH : Forestry @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ risk of wildfire <h> Related <p> Crow said prescribed burns are a long-standing traditional method of enhancing wildlife habitat while also reducing wildfire risks . <p> The burn is described as phase one of a comprehensive , multi-year project aimed at improving bighorn sheep habitat , reducing wildfire threats , protecting cultural values , and providing an opportunity for collaboration to support the re-establishment of fire as part of the natural disturbance regime in the Okanagan . <p> The planned burnis a partnership project between the LSIB , ONA , the Ministry of Forest , Lands , Natural Resource Operations and Rural Development and B.C.
