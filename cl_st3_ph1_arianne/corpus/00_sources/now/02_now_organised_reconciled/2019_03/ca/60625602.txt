Monday when Canada was finally mentioned during a panel discussion at the annual CERAWeek energy conference in Houston -- and even then , it was lumped together with a list of other countries . <p> The context was liquefied natural gas ( LNG ) . Not the oilsands , or the need for a pipeline to get Canada 's oil to market . <p> That 's a marked contrast from last year 's event , when Canada was mentioned in the opening press conference held by the International Energy Agency ( IEA ) as being an important contributor in the growth of oil production by non-OPEC producers . <p> The reason for Canada 's absence this year surfaced during the question and answer session at Monday 's IEA session where it released its five-year outlook ; it goes back to the lack of certainty on pipeline infrastructure , which has compromised investment . <p> The fact that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ world , will rank behind countries including Brazil , Guyana , Iraq and Libya in terms of oil production growth is more than troubling . <p> And the picture is not more encouraging on the LNG front . <p> While LNG Canada -- a $40 billion project in northern B.C. led by Royal Dutch Shell -- is going ahead . there are signs that Canada 's role in the global LNG market is unlikely to be significant as LNG exports grow south of the border and from Australia and Qatar . <p> As Jack Fusco of Houston-based Cheniere pointed out on Monday , growth in LNG has surpassed all expectations .
