In everyday conversations , we use the terms " leader " and " leadership " loosely to describe a position at the head of an organization , a set of personal attributes or traits , a process by which decisions are made and implemented and , finally , the results achieved by individuals performing in formal and informal leadership roles . <p> Most of the vast literature on leadership is derived from the experiences of private-sector corporations . That literature tends to celebrate the heroic accomplishments of the charismatic and bold CEO who single-handedly turns around a failing corporation . <p> Leading in the political world involves different and , I would argue , greater challenges than leading in the corporate world . <p> The essence of politics is a clash of philosophical values and related conflicts among competing interests . In politics and government , compared to business , there is less agreement on goals and the best means to pursue them . <p> Politics has become highly personalized as parties have used attractive leadership personalities @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have focused more on leaders than on policy issues . This is happening in an era of permanent campaigning , in which the approaches and techniques used in election contests are applied to the activities of governing . <p> Professionals trained in the arts of political management , such as polling , focus groups , the generation of psycho-demographic data and the development of sophisticated communications strategies , are relied upon to generate positive images and headlines on a daily basis . Reflective , longer-term thinking is displaced by short-term political calculations . <p> The novelist Robert Louis Stevenson once declared , " Politics is the only occupation for which no preparation is thought to be necessary . " That may be the perception , but it is not true . There are business schools , but there is no " school of government " for aspiring political leaders . Most political leaders need time in public office to develop their knowledge , skills and capacities for informed , balanced judgments that correspond with what is politically feasible . <p> Business leaders serve several stakeholders who can influence the fate @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a much wider range of competing interests . In politics , it is not good enough to make the right decisions -- leaders must also explain and defend those decisions before skeptical , even hostile , audiences in legislatures , in the media and among interest groups whose demands have been denied . <p> Corporate leaders are answerable to boards of directors , shareholders , regulatory bodies , the media and to public opinion . However , the accountability demands they face are far fewer and less strict than in government . In business there is nothing equivalent to the " official opposition " paid with public funds to attack the performance of leaders in government . There is no daily question period in which leaders are grilled and the exchanges are amplified and sensationalized in the media . <p> There is also an increasingly large and influential set of parliamentary watchdogs/auditors general , child advocates , election officials and commissioners for such purposes as conflicts of interest , ethics , freedom of information and protection of privacy . These " accountability enforcers " usually do not have the legal @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ negative reports from them can cause serious political damage . <p> Voters claim they want bold and decisive leaders . However , visionary leadership is rare and probably overrated . Visions work best when problems are well understood , the means to solve them are known and there is a consensus on what constitutes success . Fundamental problems such as climate change and income inequality do not have these characteristics . Most political leadership is adaptive , involving smaller rather than big changes within society because that corresponds to what is politically feasible . <p> A few individuals are naturally gifted politicians , but most must develop their leadership capacities on the job . The best leaders develop contextual intelligence that enables them to judge situations and people . They become adept at managing multiple agendas and recognizing windows of opportunity to move issues forward . They learn to cope with uncertainty and accept the need for reversibility of policies should they go wrong . <p> Finally , but most importantly , they understand the potential and limits of power and the ethics involved with its use .
