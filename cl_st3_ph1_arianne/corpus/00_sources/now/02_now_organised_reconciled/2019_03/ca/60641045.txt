<h> Earltown maple syrup farmer worried about climate change <p> Climate change may mean less maple syrup on your breakfast pancakes if current trends continue . <p> Farmer Alex MacNutt said that cool nights and warm days are needed to produce a high volume of maple products , but operations this year have been hampered by a seemingly endless winter . <p> " It does not look like it 's going to be good this year , because we 've not had the weather and it 's predicted it 's going to be hot for about three or four days and it could be over very quickly , " said MacNutt on March 16 . <p> Maple harvesting requires several cool nights and warm days in a row with steady temperatures , but a changing climate is bringing more extreme weather to Nova Scotia . This includes more severe winter storms as well as flooding , droughts and hotter temperatures in summer , which is already impacting farmers both in Colchester County and across the province . <p> MacNutt said that climate change " may not be a good thing for maple syrup .
