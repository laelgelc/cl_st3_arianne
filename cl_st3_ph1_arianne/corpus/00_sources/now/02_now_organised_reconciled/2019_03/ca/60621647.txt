All of the largest U.S. insurance companies , including AIG and Berkshire Hathaway , continue to invest in and underwrite the coal industry . <p> The intersection of insurance and climate <p> A recent report from Cambridge University has underlined just how necessary it is to have conversations about the intersection of insurance and climate in the context of the Green New Deal . The Cambridge report was produced in partnership with top global insurance and reinsurance firms . <p> Alarmingly , the report highlights that increasingly severe losses for insurers due to climate change could result in a global financial crisis . Given the historical precedent for economic crises caused by insurance losses , the industry is justifiably concerned . <p> The history of insurance is in fact the history of crisis . Since its inception , the insurance industry has had to grapple with its @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ so is by transferring catastrophic risk to reinsurance companies -- firms that specialize in providing insurance coverage to insurers and spreading the risk globally so as to dilute its impacts . <p> However , these efforts are not always successful , and massive catastrophes continue to result in the bankruptcy of insurers . <p> As I discussed in a previous article written in the aftermath of Hurricanes Harvey , Irma and Maria , significant changes have occurred in the insurance industry in an attempt to better insulate primary insurance companies from catastrophic risk . <p> These changes have largely been focused on increasing the amount of what 's known as reinsurance capital available to cover insurers ' exposure to catastrophe . <p> New strategies involve the introduction of alternative sources of reinsurance capital provided by bringing capital market investors into the insurance sector . This process has been accomplished through the packaging of risk into insurance-linked securities , and then selling those securities to institutional investors like sovereign wealth funds , pension funds and dedicated hedge funds specializing in catastrophic risk . <p> Pattern repeating <p> The scenarios raised in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ by the collision of climate change and insurance fit the historical pattern of the industry . <p> Changes to the insurance industry since the mid-1990s , along with the proliferation of alternative reinsurance sources through the integration of capital markets and institutional investors , are significant . That 's why initiatives like the Green New Deal must take into account the changes occurring in the insurance industry . <p> The primary source of systemic risk outlined in the Cambridge report stems from rising global temperatures and untenable losses to insurers as a result . For example , the authors warn that if climate change is left unchecked , the world will witness the tripling of catastrophic losses on property investments over the next 30 years . <p> Eerily reminiscent of 2008 <p> While this is a shocking and extremely disturbing finding , there are other equally troubling ways that the intersection of insurance and climate change could produce global financial systemic risk . <p> That 's due to the transformation of risk into securities which are then sold to capital market investors . <p> The creation of insurance-linked securities to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and better protect them from catastrophic risk -- creates at the same time a perverse incentive structure .
