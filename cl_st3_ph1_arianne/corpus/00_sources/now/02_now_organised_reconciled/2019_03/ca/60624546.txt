<h> Rubin : NCC 's new plan for LeBreton Flats is both piecemeal and uninspiring <p> The just-announced new NCC plan for LeBreton Flats sounds stale , mundane and familiar : LeBreton anchored by an events centre of some sort , and the rest of the 21-hectare site broken up ? -- whenever - ? for flexible developer bids . How very exciting ! <p> Not only is there no inspiring public vision for LeBreton , but some worrisome issues arise from this hasty relaunch . <p> First , it looks like City of Ottawa taxpayers are being dragged in for more costs to get the zoning , secondary plans and infrastructure requirements in place . Then there 's the " Library District " -- ? a proposed private development sell-off zone next to the tax-funded LRT station . The new central library would still be separate , off to the side and not tied directly to the Pimisi LRT station . The Library District , like the rest of LeBreton , will be chopped up , rezoned and sold off to a host of competing private developers under NCC plans . What you 'll get is a hodge-podge @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the opposite of a classy destination and a livable place to go in Ottawa . <p> On the other hand , maybe developers and investors are plain tired about their LeBreton prospects and will not jump in ; Claridge in 2004 had to be begged to stay as the only bidder for the eastern part of LeBreton . <p> And let 's look at some of the developers . Senators ' owner Eugene Melnyk and Trinity Development Group 's John Ruddy , who busted up the last four-and-a-half-year attempt to develop the Flats , are still eligible to waltz back in with a bid . On the sidelines , Claridge can continue its uninspiring development of the already-sold-off eastern part of LeBreton . And the nearby planned 900 Albert St. towers , which contributed to the explosion of the Melnyk-Ruddy proposal , will , sadly , fit right in to the expected patchy high-rise condo development now being enabled by the NCC at the Flats . <p> So far , funding needs are not broken out , and reliance and risk management seem to be dependent on developers ' shaky @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ driven , meaning Ottawa 's last prime downtown site will become a marketplace to make profits and private decisions . <p> And there are more costs . Contaminated soils will keep getting hauled away , costing more millions on top of the multi-millions already spent on LeBreton 's cleanup . Millions have also been spent by the NCC " administering " LeBreton and more will be . The NCC potentially gets any revenue it can negotiate out of any private development sales and leases it helps approve . <p> Overlooked so far in these sketchy plans are possible climate change impacts , specifically flooding that could mean part of the site , especially near Nepean Inlet , is in jeopardy . And Indigenous land title claims could throw a wrench into the whole giveaway plan for these unceded lands , with Indigenous Peoples wanting more than token " accommodations " in private sector developments at LeBreton . <p> The public itself gets a little input during this 2019 election year , but then there will be more secretive " Requests for Proposals " from developers and behind-closed-doors haggling while hidden @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ could just shut down the whole LeBreton planning exercise or hand it to Canada Lands Development Corporation or someone else ? -- or keep the area vacant . <p> Maybe all that happens at LeBreton is that an isolated library gets built , even if the NCC wants the area to become a place full of runaway boutique developments . We just saw how stage 2 LRT 's future was decided . Now stage-two NCC efforts are thrust upon us at LeBreton .
