<h> Dead bodies are turning up on Mount Everest , here 's why <p> Isabella O'MalleyDigital Writer/ Climate Change Reporter <p> Friday , March 22nd 2019 , 2:03 pm - Removing a dead body can cost up to $80,000 <p> Mount Everest expedition operators are discovering an increasing number of dead bodies of climbers that were previously frozen in ice , and the warming temperatures point to the impacts of climate change . <p> The spring climbing season has begun , and the melting and thinning of the glaciers in the Everest region is apparent . According to a report by CNN , bodies from British expeditions in the 1970s were recently found , and in 2017 the hand of a dead climber appeared above the ground . <p> Operators at Mount Everest have faced a number of issues with modern expeditions , including the increasing amount of litter and human waste left behind by decades of mountaineering , as well as concerns of overcrowding . Some estimate that the amount of human waste on the mountain could weigh up to 14 tons , and some have even dubbed it a ' fecal time bomb . Removing a dead body can cost up to $80,000 , and most have been surfacing at the Khumbu icefall , which a glacier that loops around the mountain and is known as one of the most dangerous parts of the climb . <p> A climber crossing Khumbu icefall .
