<h> Dene stress the Sacredness of water on World Water Day <p> March 22nd is generally celebrated by First Nations and environmental organizations as World Water Day . <p> But elders from across the Northwest Territories spoke out about their concerns over the cleanliness of fresh water , and the impacts industrial activities such as strip-mining for bitumen rich oilsands in Fort McMurray , and pipelines are having on the Athabasca , Peace and Mackenzie Rivers which all eventually flow to the Arctic Ocean . <p> " Water is life . It feeds farmers fields , we relied on it for generations for transportation and the fishery . That has all changed now . Our whitefish are turning pink , the pickerel have blisters and deformed spines . The commercial fishery is done , and will likely not return in my lifetime , " Fort Chipweyan elder Alice Rigney told CKLB radio on Friday . <p> A River destroyed , is how Rigney sees it . She says the science being touted by the Alberta Government and Oil companies is laughable . <p> " Next time they come to our community and claim the water is clean , I @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ them to dip a cup and drink it .
