<p> A handful of nearly extirpated Lake Superior woodland caribou wandering the sandy shores of Caribou Island and an almost equal number of wolves roaming the forests of neighbouring Michipicoten Island are awaiting a decision . The iconic Northern Ontario animals are under the microscope and a lot of interested people are taking a look . That is why an upcoming Ministry of Natural Resources and Forestry workshop , Seeking Advice on the Future of Caribou in the Lake Superior Coast Range , in Thunder Bay on Thursday , could very well provide an excellent opportunity to arrive at some kind of solution . <p> Among the workshop participants will be Aaron Bumstead , who is director of Lands and Economic Development for Michipicoten First Nation . Michipicoten First Nation , a community located about 20 kilometres west of Wawa on the north shore of Lake Superior , is keeping an extra keen eye on the wolves and the threatened caribou herd since the recent Ontario Ministry of Natural Resources and Forestry translocation of wolves to the U.S. Isle Royale National Park . <p> The work Bumstead refers to is the development of a long-term management plan for the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that MFN can once more partner with MNRF to develop a plan to return the herd to a healthy status . Bumstead states that MFN 's goal is to " have a true , close , honest , professional partnership , " with the MNRF . <p> Michipicoten Island once was an excellent breeding ground for caribou . From a small group of transplanted animals in the 1980s to a large herd of hundreds by 2014 , the caribou thrived on the seclusion and lush foliage of the Michipcoten Island wilderness , until a pack of predators tested the fate of the caribou . During the winter of 2014/2015 three or four wolves crossed to the island from the mainland and altered the dynamics of Michipicoten Island 's caribou sanctuary . The wolf pack grew and basically wiped out the herd . For decades , the wolves there had kept the resident moose population in check but due to disease and inbreeding only two wolves remained , a non-breeding pair of father and daughter . The moose numbers have risen to a critical level of about 1500 , too many for the health of Isle Royale . Since Isle Royale needed wolves and Michipicoten Island needs to eliminate theirs , the fit was perfect . Hence , due to the combined work of U.S. Park Service and the Ontario Ministry of Natural Resources and Forestry , biologists and veterinarians , two black wolves from the mainland of Superior coast and two others from Michipicoten Island now call Isle Royale home . <p> Bumstead is concerned about the rest of the wolves on Michipicoten Island . MNRF states that climate change is a problem with warmer , drier forests and more frequent severe weather could shift the boreal forest , so important to caribou , northwards . With such a forest change , more moose and white-tailed deer could move northward too , which brings another set of problems . Deer carry a parasitic nematode worm that is harmful to moose and caribou . Also more severe winters could increase the probablity of ice bridges to the islands .
