<p> A small flicker of " this is horrible " , grew to sickening feeling in the stomach that the shooter was still at large , to a realisation that this was an act of terrorism , and that a schism had forever been struck across the rhetoric of New Zealand . <p> In many of my travels overseas I have bragged about what a safe haven New Zealand was , sharing jokes with fellow backpackers that my greatest fear was being run down by a crazed sheep . <p> Unfortunately , in that one moment , New Zealand had joined the ever growing club of countries whose openness and willingness to accept outsiders is being punished by those with extremist views . <p> For the Prime Minister , not only would she and her staff have to process this realisation themselves but they also have to do so on behalf of the country . <p> What do they need to do now , to ensure the country felt safe ? What level of information is appropriate to give when the whole country is reeling with shock and horror ? <p> You could see that realisation and pressure in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in New Plymouth , where she knew that the scant information was just the beginning of something much bigger . <p> By her Beehive press conference later that afternoon , the true extent of what happened was realised . The Prime Minister started providing the reassurances the country needed . <p> Ms Ardern gives her press conference after a full briefing at Parliament on Friday . Her words , that they are us , will forever be stitched into our history . <p> It is no coincidence that in the 24 hours surrounding this attack , there were so many events scheduled that celebrated our diversity and ability to peacefully gather . <p> The thousands of young people able to protest peacefully against climate change ; the celebration of our Polynesian heritage at Polyfest , and the highlighting of diversity at the Wellington Pride Parade are just a few . <p> The Prime Minister @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ joined the community in Christchurch on Saturday to express their condolences , their sorrow and join in the bewilderment that this event happened here . <p> The empathy was etched in to the faces of everyone in that gathering - reassuring us that we are united in our collective grief . <p> Jacinda Ardern at a local community meeting in Christchurch the day after the attacks . Photo : RNZ / SIMON ROGERS <p> MPs have attended vigils around the country , with more to come . <p> While we look to the leaders in our community to reassure us in times like this , it is also comforting to know that they too held their families a little tighter on Friday evening . <p> This is what will make what happens next for the government be the most difficult to navigate . <p> Already they have met the expectations of the community in promising to ban semi-automatic weapons . For most , it is confusing that these were n't already banned . <p> How they deal with the security questions , how the shooter avoided any watchlists , and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ be difficult . <p> The role of social media will also be examined , but the organic spread of the video is largely out of the government 's control . Tighter security is likely around our politicians , and at large community events . <p> There will be a high expectation put on our parliamentarians to ensure that this never happens again . When we never expected it to happen in the first place . <p> These are important and worthy questions , but none of these policy questions will change what happened . <p> They can not repair the damage to our community .
