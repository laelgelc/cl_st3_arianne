<p> Forest owners believe the just announced reform of the Emissions Trading Scheme will encourage farm foresters and hill country pastoral farmers to plant more trees . <p> Farm Forestry Association Chair , Neil Cullen , says carbon average accounting , where a forester is not penalised for harvesting so long as they replant , will encourage farmers to plant out land in forests , without a concern that this will result in a cash-flow problem for them . The reform reduces ETS compliance costs . <p> " This reform reflects the reality of how forest land locks up atmospheric carbon - which is after all the whole point of the ETS . " <p> Neil Cullen says making the system simpler to understand is also crucial . <p> " I know it 's a complex system , but it needs to be understood by a forester or a farmer without having to go on a course . If it 's too complicated , a landowner will do something else , or nothing . The end result is regional investment opportunities are missed and rural communities are less wealthy . We simply ca n't have politicians shifting goal posts every three years . In the big picture New Zealand needs equity between the land-based sectors , and long-term stability . Without these factors behind it no reform of the ETS will make it work . "
