Soon it will eat everything <p> OPINION : She called it the nuclear-free moment of her generation , but Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern was nowhere to be seen as more than 1000 children streamed into Parliament on Friday to protest inaction on climate change . <p> Ardern 's office instead sent out an information-free release on the Zero Carbon Bill headlined " Climate legislation progressing " . This law was supposed to be introduced last October and in select committee by now . We still do n't even have a draft .. <p> But her absence was understandable , politically . Climate change is still something of a second-tier issue , its effects too murky and disparate to clearly be blamed on any government-of-the-day . <p> ROSA WOODS/STUFF <p> Climate change will swallow politics in these kids ' lives . <p> The strike has mostly been an opportunity for politicians to talk about whether they would let their kids " wag " and a brief distraction from the chief political battle of our time : the capital gains tax.This wo n't last . But that is just one of the many ways a warming planet will change how we live . <p> We can start with the economy . Yes , climate action will probably hurt growth and that pain will not be even- our economy is built on fossil fuels far more than we like to admit . But warming will also hurt the economy , a lot . <p> One study estimates under a ' business-as-usual ' scenario that fails to lower emissions the world economy would be 20 per cent smaller in 2100 than it would be had temperatures not risen . That 's about 25 recessions on the scale of the one in 2008 . <p> Then there 's the refugee crisis . That 's before you even consider the massive increase in wildfires , devastating impacts on agriculture , increase in storms , literal heat deaths , and the sea-level rise . <p> There will be plenty of anger about the inaction of our current politicians , especially as cutting carbon out of the economy gets harder the longer the wait .
