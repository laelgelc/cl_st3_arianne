<h> Scientists consider earthquake warning system to help Kiwis prepare for tremor <p> Wellington 's central library closed on Tuesday night following advice received from engineers that the building may not be safe in the event of an earthquake . <p> Would New Zealanders benefit from a early warning system for earthquakes ? <p> Researchers from around the country are conducting a survey to identify whether a warning system should be implemented in New Zealand . <p> The Earthquake Early Warning Survey ( EEW ) , funded by the Earthquake Commission , is asking Kiwis what they would do if were warned about a strong earthquake approaching , and what type of early warning system would work best . <p> " The survey is being carried out to understand how New Zealanders would respond to an early warning without incoming earthquake shaking , " said lead researcher , Dr Julia Becker , from Massey University in Wellington . <p> Earthquake scientists , engineers and various social scientists from Massey University , GNS Science , and the University of Auckland are working to understand what Kiwis expect from such system . <p> " We also want to find out how @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ what is the minimum strength of shaking would they want to be warned about and how would they like to receive the warning , " Becker said . <p> The detection system works off the two waves sent by the earthquake -- P-waves and S-waves -- before a tremor is felt . Becker likens these waves to how we usually see lightning before we hear thunder . <p> " With an earthquake , the P-waves travel very fast and are picked up by sensors first , automatically sending an alert to locations further away warning them to expect strong earthquake shaking , " Becker said . <p> " The S-waves that cause the shaking and damage travel more slowly , so unless you 're at the centre of the earthquake , you can get a warning of what is coming .
