<h> Large numbers turnout across Taranaki for annual seaweek event <p> A large group were at the Hawera Aquatic Centre for the new snorkel trail as part of Seaweek . <p> Hundreds of Taranaki people helped celebrate Seaweek by attending a range of events . <p> For the first time , Experiencing Marine Reserves brought their snorkel programme to South Taranaki with a pool snorkel trail held at the Hawera Aquatic Centre where over 30 participants of all ages attended . <p> The programme had students research marine reserves , implement action projects to raise awareness about marine conservation and experience snorkelling both in and outside a marine reserve to compare the difference . <p> Karen Pratt , a volunteer who assisted with the event , said the feedback on last week 's programme had been overwhelming . <p> " There were two five-year-olds , a girl and a boy , who thrived on the experience and made incredible progress , " she said . <p> " A number of kids were delighted when they were able to have a second go at the marine trail . " <p> Seaweek is held annually with events held right across the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Moana , or Care for our Seas , which aimed to highlight the impact of plastic , litter and other pollutants . <p> Elvisa Van Der Leden , EMR Taranaki Coordinator , was pleased with the positive feedback on the event 's Facebook page and said she hoped to bring the event down to Hawera again in the near future . <p> The week began in New Plymouth with a Ngamotu Beach Community Snorkel Event . More than 50 people attended the third and final community snorkel day . They were among more than 300 people who attended Seaweek Events . <p> EMR also worked with Whanuakura School in Patea , Puketapu School , Mimi school and Highlands Intermediate and many of the schools that took part also staged their own beach cleans and other conservation-related activities . <p> Other Seaweek celebrations included a beach clean hosted by Taranaki Conservationists and a climate change author reading session by Neville Peat .
