<h> New form of carbon payments for forest owners <p> Forest owners are to be paid by a different method for the carbon their trees absorb , the government has announced . <p> Such payments have been part of the way Governments fight climate change for years , since trees take CO2 from the atmosphere as they grow . <p> CO2 is a powerful greenhouse gas . <p> At present , forest owners receive ascending carbon credits as their trees grow , and pay most of that back when the trees are cut down for timber . <p> They then start earning again when they replant the next crop of trees . <p> But the Government has ruled that this so-called saw-tooth model will become a flat line . <p> The Forestry Minister Shane Jones said growers would in future get paid the same amount of money all the time , based on an average of their earnings . <p> " Today 's announcement includes the introduction of averaging accounting for all forests registered from January 1 2021 and the option to use the new accounting method for all forests registered in 2019 and 2020 , " Shane Jones @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ RNZ / Richard Tindiller <p> " By taking a long-term view of the amount of carbon in a production forest , forest owners will be able to trade more carbon at lower risk , and not have to worry about finding units to repay when they harvest . <p> " It 's essential the Emissions Trading Scheme provides the right incentives for forestry over the long term so we can deliver on our One Billion Trees programme , as well as our commitment to taking action on climate change and supporting the transition to a low emissions future . " <p> Mr Jones said he believed the changes would encourage foresters to plant more trees , and he picked 89 million as the likely number . <p> He aimed to introduce the changes to Parliament around the middle of this year . <p> NZ Forest Owners Association president Peter Weir said he was pleased with this change , as his organisation had asked for it and had got it . <p> " Carbon averaging gives all the credits up front , until aged 19 or 20 of the trees with @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ least cost , and no harvest liability . <p> " It de-risks the whole thing . " <p> He added this change could only do good , but he would not concur with Shane Jones ' forecasts on tree numbers - " My crystal ball 's not that good . "
