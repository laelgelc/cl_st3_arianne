In 1900 , the firm 's first known female staff member was a Miss Baird , a shorthand writer and typiste employed in Auckland . She was possibly the only one employed in a law firm in New Zealand at the time . Soon after , other women were taken on as support staff in Auckland , and in 1905 a " lady clerk " was appointed Assistant Bookkeeper . <p> Luckily it got better . The first two women to be admitted to the legal profession in Wellington , Lyra Taylor and Crissy Corry , were employed in our Wellington office . Both were admitted in 1918 . <p> The first Maori woman known to have worked for the firm , Ria Tahiwi , was recruited in 1919 . From early in her career Lyra Taylor had an interest in social justice issues , particularly as they affected women and children . She was involved with the National Council of Women from 1918 and was a pro bono lawyer for the Society for the Protection of Women and Children from 1921 . She went on to work , lecture and study overseas , became renowned for her social work , and was awarded an OBE. <p> The Auckland office employed Gertrude Marton , an Austrian war refugee of Jewish descent , who graduated in law in Vienna in the 1930s .
