<h> Trees slow climate change by absorbing gases and water vapour <p> OPINION : In our efforts to avoid the worst impacts of climate change , trees are our allies . <p> They absorb carbon dioxide and other gases which would otherwise be circulating in the atmosphere contributing to the greenhouse effect that is rapidly warming our planet . <p> The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change recommends planting forests and restoring ecosystems as a way of lessening the impact . So our Government 's billion tree planting project , for all its flaws , is likely to help us in the long run . <p> But trees serve another purpose , the value of which scientists are just starting to quantify . They absorb and transpire rainwater as water vapour . The forest canopy also stops the soil from drying . This has a cooling effect . <p> At the same time , the dark forest foliage absorbs more of the sun 's energy than bare ground , which has a warming effect . <p> Brazilian scientists have modelled the effects of these two forces , known as transpiration and albedo respectively and just published their results in the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ current level of illegal deforestation in the Brazilian rainforests continues , it could result in a 1.45 degrees Celsius increase in the local temperature by 2050 . <p> They looked elsewhere and found similar warming and cooling patterns , though the effect is n't as pronounced in temperate forests like we have in New Zealand . <p> But the message is clear .
