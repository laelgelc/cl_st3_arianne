<h> Book reviews : Fragment and Rage Becomes Her <h> Fragment by Craig Russell ( Thistledown Press , $22 ) <p> Climate change and its likely impact is the key focus of our age . US President Donald Trump 's ostrich-like denial only serves to increase the attention . So , it was only predictable that the subject would enter the domain of fiction . <p> Canadian author Craig Russell has a fertile imagination . His take on our changing world features a gigantic slab of ice that breaks off the Antarctic sheet . It is so enormous that it is unstoppable , crushing everything it encounters , even establishing its own micro-climate . So far , so plausible . We have even seen stray icebergs float past the South Island . <p> Russell 's eco-thriller , however , is not big on plausibility . Aimed at a late teenage market , it is alarmist in its tone and sensationalist in its narrative . First published in Canada in 2016 , it has just been released here , presumably because one of its many protagonists is a " Maori scientist " , although you would never guess that . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cast of thousands to track the progress of this giant ice cube . These characters range from glaciologists , scientists , submarine crew , TV reporters , marketing executives , carpet salesmen , admirals , bomber pilots , boat charterers , Pentagon officials and so on . Even the President gets a scene , in which he reveals he is possibly ( but only possibly ) more ignorant and bigoted than the present incumbent . <p> In a cataclysmic rush , the ice fragment charges up the South Atlantic , heading past the tropics and into the Gulf of Mexico , where it wreaks immense damage . Russell 's prose is breathless , as he records people shot out of a submarine 's tubes in the Antarctic Ocean , surfacing right next to a yacht which has just patched a hole in a sinking cruiseliner . Credibility and plausibility are junked early on in a mad scramble for the lifeboats ! It is as if Titanic has met Independence Day in a competition for the end of the world . The whole book ends in a riot of uncontrolled imagination @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Kiwi scientist who helps save the day . And it is all because he has learnt how to speak Whale ! Yes , our non-te reo speaker has decoded cetacean-speak and communicates with one handily surfing before the ice-cube . Neat , eh ! <p> Russell 's own focus is extremely diffuse . He darts from one situation to the next , often devoting just a page to each . Likewise , characterisation is also sketchy , at best . <p> In a competitive market for young adult fiction , it is a real pity that teenagers are not fed more substantial material . It does them no service to load them with saccharine cliches from sub-Hollywood plot lines . The final insult is to end with a sermon , in case they missed the point behind this torrent of tosh . -- Steve Walker <p> Fifty-six per cent of men , according to a 2016 American study , believe sexism has been eradicated from daily life . Read that again . More than a quarterof the population genuinely believes sexism is a thing of the past . <p> Does @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ would you feel comfortable standing up and saying , loud and clear , that it makes you angry ? <p> Probably not , says Soraya Chemaly . In Rage Becomes Her , the activist and writer sets out to prove that sexism is very real and that we have every reason to be enraged by it . She demonstrates how women 's anger is systematically silenced as a way to maintain the status quo . And she encourages us to reclaim our anger and use it as an instrument for change . <p> The statistic I led with is one of hundreds that Chemaly cites , relating to all aspects of modern life . ( Most of the studies are American ; depressingly , we can assume attitudes are more or less the same throughout the Western world . ) Parents pay boys 13 per cent more pocket money than girls . Men hold over 70 per cent of the roles in top US films . More than half of Americans think women should be legally required to take their husbands ' last names . Men 's emergency room wait @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ women 's . Male Supreme Court justices interrupt their female colleagues three times as often as they do each other . <p> Just one of those figures should be enough to make your blood boil ; reading them all , page after page , is maddening in the extreme . But , as Chemaly says , we 've been conditioned to suppress this feeling , causing severe harm to ourselves in the process : " The unfairnesses that we intuit and experience but can not ' prove ' , as we are asked to do so often , are more likely to become internalised anger rather than externalised action . " <p> Indeed . Being asked to repeatedly prove that your daily reality is , well , real is one of the most exhausting , disheartening parts of being a woman . So Chemaly does the job for us -- first of providing evidence , such as the stats above , and then of showing us how we can take action effectively , fuelled and informed by our anger . There 's even a 10-point plan for harnessing anger productively @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ text , not a self-help book designed to be easily digested and then promptly forgotten . Chemaly does occasionally slip into bad academic-writing habits , resulting in clunky sentences like this : " This socialisation makes the internalisation of objectification second nature in processes known as self-objectification and self-surveillance . " <p> Most of the time , though , Rage Becomes Her is an absorbing read and one that will stay with you . You 'll think of it every time you describe yourself as frustrated , stressed or disappointed instead of angry ; every time you hear a high-profile woman being described as crazy , bitchy or unlikeable for behaviour that would be praised in a man ; and every time you cry when you really want to yell. -- India Lopez
