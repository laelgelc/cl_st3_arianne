<h> Sub makes rapid ascent in Indian Ocean <p> The Nekton Mission 's submersible had to make an emergency ascent after smoke filled the cockpit . ( AAP ) <p> The crew of a sub filming the bottom of the Indian Ocean are safe after making an emergency ascent from 100 metres down when the cockpit filled with smoke . <p> UpdatedUpdated 20 March <p> Share <p> Technicians investigating the cause of a subsea emergency in which a British scientist and her American pilot made a rapid ascent from 250 metres beneath the Indian Ocean now think it 's unlikely it was caused by an electrical fire . <p> The pair with the British-led Nekton Mission investigating climate change off the Seychelles initiated the emergency procedure on Tuesday after an acrid smell of smoke seeped into the cockpit of their submersible . Neither was injured in the incident . <p> Nekton Mission director Oliver Steeds said he hoped it would be back in the water on Wednesday , adding that emergency procedures had worked well . <p> The sub was off Aldabra Island when pilot Robert Carmichael smelled smoke and immediately began to ascend .
