<h> I feel duty bound to support striking students <h> By Craig Challen <p> March 12 , 2019 -- 12.00am <p> Recently it was reported that the mosaic-tailed rat , Melomys rubicola , which lived only on a tiny island in the Torres Strait , has been officially declared extinct . It is believed that its habitat was temporarily submerged due to rising sea levels . As a result , it may well be the first mammal to become extinct as a direct result of anthropogenic climate change . <p> This Friday I will be attending the School Strike for Climate in Sydney . I feel both honoured and duty bound to offer my support to the students striking around Australia , in all capital cities and many regional centres , as they call for action to address climate change . The Australian students are inspired by the efforts of the Swedish teenager Greta Thunberg , who has campaigned since August last year for the Swedish Government to meet its commitments under the Paris Agreement . Nor can the Australian government legitimately claim a lack of necessary economic and technical capability for action . <p> I do have some words of advice to the strikers . As admirable as their actions are , meaningful and effective policy change will only be achieved by moving beyond confrontation . The sending of a message is only the first step , and success will ensue only through understanding other points of view and persuading those that hold them . Considerable social change will be required to solve the problem of climate change and it is inevitable that some people and communities will be disadvantaged by that change . If people feel threatened , then it is legitimate in their eyes for them to oppose that change .
