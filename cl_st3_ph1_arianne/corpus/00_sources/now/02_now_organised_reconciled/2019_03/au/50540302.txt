<p> This is what we saw at the climate change march : hundreds of worried children , to the supposed adults in the room : " Please help ! Our futures are at stake , and it 's urgent ! " Aspiring Federal MP : " We 'll be back after lunch . " I write to say bravo to the hundreds of students who stood up for themselves and their generation . As reported in the Port News article reporting the local students ' strike for climate on Friday ( " Students across the Hastings marched for climate action as part of the Schools Strike 4 Action in Port Macquarie " , Port News online , March 15 ) , Nationals ' candidate for Cowper Patrick Conaghan 's comments were two-pronged . The first prong was weak , defensive and fallacious party line : " ... sensible plan ... works towards renewables without ... " followed by a string of baseless economic scaremongering . This is precisely the kind of dithering that has gotten us to the point of an emergency , instead of taking effective action long ago , when it would have been so much easier @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in the second prong of Mr Conaghan 's comments he indulged in an appalling piece of victim-blaming : " ... it starts at home .... " Attempting to turn responsibility for a crisis that has been stoked by our generation ( mine and yours , Mr Conaghan ) onto a bunch of children is cowardly and cruel as well as factually incorrect . Firstly , he 's assuming that these students are not already living as sustainably as reasonably possible in the current system ( which he has no basis to assume ) . Secondly , anyone who thinks for a moment will recognise that climate catastrophe is bearing down on us not because of a teenager 's mobile phone - or even hundreds of teenagers ! - it 's caused by extractive industry on a scale difficult for any normal person to imagine . So , Mr Conaghan can credibly tell kids to turn off the aircon when the government he seeks to represent legislates and regulates industry appropriately instead of supporting their polluting friends as they have been doing .
