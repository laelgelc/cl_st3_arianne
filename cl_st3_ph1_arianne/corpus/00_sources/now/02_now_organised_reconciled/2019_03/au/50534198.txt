<p> There are many people who might lay claim to being pivotal figures of the 1960s but Augustus Owsley Stanley III is right up there . <p> Who , you ask ? And it 's a good question because Owsley , as he was known , did n't talk a lot . <p> " I do n't want my life exposed publicly , " he told Rolling Stone magazine when they tracked him down in Queensland shortly before his death . " I 'm interested in the work I 've done and the things I 've discovered ... I 'm not into being a celebrity , because I think celebrityhood has no value to anyone , least of all the celebrity . " <p> Take note and weep as we find ourselves in a world full of wannabe celebrities . This was a guy who spoke little in public but did a lot to change the way we viewed the world : firstly through his sound engineering skills and later through his expertise as a chemist . <p> As his daughter Redbird Ferguson told me : <p> " He was highly intellectual , voraciously smart and focused tightly on @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ needed to understand how everything fits together . It also led him to perfect the process of making the purest form of LSD outside the famed Sandoz labs in Switzerland . More than that , he saw to it that some of those tabs would be ingested by artists on the frontlines of the ' 60s ' revolution . <p> If you 've watched the Beatles ' Magical Mystery Tour you should know the " Fabs " were flying high on Owsley acid . When it came time to synthesise acid he simply read the chemistry books before he set to work perfecting the process of LSD purification . <h> A legal high <p> When he first made LSD in 1965 it was not illegal . Working with the band , he created a sound system of remarkable quality . <p> Having revolutionised live music sound he did something else that changed rock music . <p> He found a way to record in stereo a band playing live , by plugging his tape recorder into the sound desk . <p> Having done this he then set about making an archive of the Grateful Dead and many other artists . <p> As a result Owsley gathered 1300 reel-to-reel tapes documenting Jefferson Airplane , Hot Tuna , Doc and Merle Watson as well as the Allman Brothers Band ; all at the peak of their powers . <p> The tapes are now the property of the Owsley Stanley Foundation @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ its archive release program . <p> However , there is a problem looming . <p> The recordings are held on reel-to-reel oxide coated tapes and the tapes are now 50 years old . <h> A race to protect history <p> As Redbird , who is the foundation 's chief financial officer , puts it : " we are up against the ticking clock " . <p> Not all the tapes are in great condition and there 's an added complication , she says : " Each time a tape is opened and placed on a machine the oxide can disintegrate .
