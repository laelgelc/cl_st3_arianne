<h> 60 Million Tonnes Of Toxic Coal Ash At Lake Macquarie <p> Accumulation of coal ash and water discharge from New South Wales ' Eraring and Vales Point power stations is creating a toxic aquatic environment in Lake Macquarie states a new report . <p> The Hunter Community Environment Centre ( HCEC ) says its water sampling indicates both power stations have been discharging aluminium , iron , and manganese above the recommended concentrations for marine waters used for recreation , along with arsenic , lead , copper , nickel , and zinc above the Australian and New Zealand Environment and Conservation Council ( ANZECC ) Guidelines . <p> Added to this , sediment in the creek receiving the Eraring coal ash dam overflow was found to have a selenium concentration of 110 parts per million , more than 55 times the level recommended in order to protect sensitive fish and birds . There are also concerns over cadmium levels in crabs . <p> People who regularly consume fish , crabs and other seafood sourced from the Lake are also at risk from the contamination as heavy metals are bioaccumulative . <p> " The discharge of these heavy metals @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the coal-ash from the shores of the lake , " said HCEC Coordinator , Jo Lynch . <p> Ash from Vales Point and Eraring power stations is disposed of in two huge dams constructed over wetlands on the shores of Lake Macquarie . But reuse of the ash has been sitting at around just 20 per cent for quite some time .
