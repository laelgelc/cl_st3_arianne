<p> With the Venice Biennale kicking off in less than two months , Australian artist Angelica Mesiti and curator Juliana Engberg have revealed details of the artwork taking over the Australian Pavilion . <p> Assembly , a three-channel video work , features an ensemble of performers reflecting the diverse cultural heritage and ancestries of contemporary Australia , enacting a cycle that runs from gathering through dispersing to re-gathering . <p> The sequences were filmed in Australia 's Old Parliament House , in Canberra , and the Italian Senate in Palazzo Madama , Rome . <p> Mesiti describes it as a testament to the " human necessity to come together , whether it 's to memorialise , to act in solidarity , or to come together and say ' that is not us , this is us ' . " <p> " In Christchurch and in France we 're seeing people coming together in solidarity ... we see it in the women 's marches and in our students taking to the street to voice their concerns about climate change . " <p> To make the work , Mesiti drew together her own assembly of about 40 artists , performers , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ dancer Deborah Brown , composer Max Lyandvert , film producer Bridget Ikin and cinematographer Bonnie Elliott . <p> Assembly was developed by the Sydney-born , Paris-based artist in collaboration with Engberg , who she has worked with several times previously . <p> Their proposal was one of more than 70 submissions made in response to an open callout by the Australia Council for the Arts -- the first year that Australia 's contribution to Venice Biennale has been selected by this process . <p> Her companion , a lawyer , enlightened her : it was a stenography machine used by the Italian senate to transcribe proceedings . <p> " I was fascinated that I was looking at a typewriter as well as a musical instrument , " Mesiti recalls . <p> " My first thought was ' you could translate text into music with this -- you just have to decode the musical notes that were being depressed while it was writing text ' . " <p> The idea sat idle in her mental bottom drawer for a couple of years , until she came to consider a proposal for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 2017 . The world seemed to be in crisis mode , " Mesiti says . <p> " I thought about this little machine , and it sort of being witness to the democratic process for over a century . So many historical events had passed over the keys ... as it recorded every utterance in the Parliamentary process . <p> " What a timely device to be thinking about , as we 're considering the state democracy is in right now . " <h> Translating text to music <p> Mesiti 's video work has often been concerned with " translation " , as both a theme and a strategy : in The Calling , she explored traditional " whistling " languages ; in The Colour of Saying , she demonstrated how sign language and gesture could be used to express musical scores . <p> For Assembly , she went looking for a text that she could use the Michela to " translate " ( it uses a syllabic abbreviated language ) . <p> The logical place to start was legislation : the bread and butter for a machine such as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " dogmatic or prescriptive " for her tastes . <p> " The issue I kept coming up against was that it using a piece of legislation would include but then also disclude certain arguments or ideas , or groups . " <p> She and Engberg looked to poetry instead , and found their way to David Malouf 's Poem to be Written in Another Tongue , in which he grapples with the language of his Lebanese grandfather . <p> " It immediately struck a personal chord with me ... there 's an imaginary conversation going on with an ancestor with whom the writer no longer has a linguistic link , " Mesiti says . <p> " It 's about the impossibility of translation and the distance between one language and another . " <p> As the Michela transposed the poem via keystrokes , MIDI software recorded a musical notation . <p> Mesiti then invited Australian composer Max Lyandvert to extrapolate this set of notes into a musical score . <p> This score was then given to musicians from different backgrounds and training to play and perform . <p> The result @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ European tradition and 20th century avant-garde music , and then through the inflections that people from different backgrounds bring into it through their musical styles and traditions , it is transformed into this other , new sound . That 's the experiment . " <p> Mesiti says " polyphony " is a key concept within Assembly . <p> " Polyphony is made up of individual voices or instruments that all have their own inherent sound , but when brought together can create a new music . " <h> Gestures to save democracy <p> Having found the first piece of her artwork 's puzzle in the streets of Rome , Mesiti found the final piece on the streets of Paris , during the Nuit debout demonstrations in 2016 and 2017 . <p> There she discovered the so-called " gestures to save democracy " , adapted from the Occupy movement and designed for communication and consensus-building without words .
