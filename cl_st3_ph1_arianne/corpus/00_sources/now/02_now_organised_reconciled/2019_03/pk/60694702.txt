<h> The last drop <h> Editorial <p> Pakistan , a country which is unable to provide the majority of its people with safe drinking water , is set to become water scarce by 2025 . The country is already rated as being water stressed , with insufficient water to meet the needs of its rapidly growing population both in urban centres and in other locations . The rapid depletion of aquifers is a key factor in the risks we run of running dry in the very near future . The year 2025 is not far away . It will fall within the lifetime of most of us alive today . It is therefore imperative that we draw the attention of people towards the water crisis , the need to conserve water and to put in place policies to clean up water resources which have become poisoned as a consequence of industrial waste , pesticides and other toxins leaching into them . The high arsenic and fluoride content in some parts of the country add to the risk factors for large sections of the population . Last year , Cape Town warded off what was known as ' Day Zero ' for the city , or the day when it would run out of water , by launching a mass campaign to reconsider how we use water and to avoid even a drop going waste . <p> We will need to follow suit . Climate change is making our task harder . Much of Pakistan 's water needs are met by rivers which are filled as glaciers melt . If these glaciers are depleted as a result of global warming , there will be less fresh water available to all of us .
