<p> Some of the world 's largest airlines have yet to set long-term targets to reduce their climate-changing emissions , climate and economic researchers warned Tuesday . <p> Top publicly listed airlines have cut their " emissions intensity " -- how much pollution they produce for the same amount of activity -- significantly in recent years , said researchers from the London School of Economics ' Grantham Research Institute . <p> But they are not making clear plans for the much larger emissions reductions needed to meet internationally agreed climate goals , the researchers said . <p> Beyond 2020 and particularly in the long-term " the targets these airlines have set to reduce their emissions are not clearly consistent with the Paris Agreement goals , " said Simon Dietz , co-author of a study released Tuesday . <p> The study , which looked at 20 of the world 's largest publicly listed airlines , noted that air travel currently accounts for about 2 percent of global carbon dioxide emissions , and 12 percent of transport-related emissions . <p> Cutting those emissions -- and emissions from shipping -- is particularly challenging because their mobile nature makes it harder for companies @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ power . <p> Alternative fuels , such as hydrogen , may offer long-term solutions but are still being developed . <p> The study , backed by investor groups , analysed the public disclosures of airlines as a way of assessing their performance on combatting climate change , Dietz said . <p> The airlines were evaluated based on their carbon management practices and emissions performance . Airlines with lower emissions often had younger fleets , more passengers per flight and a focus on longer versus shorter flights , Dietz said . <p> Helen Vi ? es Fiestas , the deputy global head of sustainability at BNP Paribas Asset Management , said the report raises key questions about what the aviation industry is prepared to do to contribute to climate change action in the long-run .
