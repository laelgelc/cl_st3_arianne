<h> Climate change forces Arctic animals to shift feeding habits : study <p> Seals and whales in the Arctic are shifting their feeding patterns as climate change alters their habitats , and the way they do so may determine whether they survive , a new study has found . <p> Researchers harnessed datasets spanning two decades to examine how two species of Arctic wildlife -- beluga whales , also known as white whales , and ringed seals -- are adapting to their changing homes . <p> Both species traditionally hunt for food in areas with sea ice and particularly at so-called tidal glacier fronts , where glaciers meet the ocean . But with climate change melting sea ice and prompting glaciers to retreat , researchers in Norway decided to look at whether and how animals in the affected areas were adapting . " The Arctic is the bellwether of climate change , " the researchers wrote . <p> " With the rapid pace of change rendering genetic adaptation unfeasible , " they reasoned that behavioural and dietary changes " will likely be the first observable responses within ecosystems " .
