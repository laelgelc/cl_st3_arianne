<h> AfDB Earmarks $25bn On Climate Change Initiative <p> The African Development Bank ( AfDB ) has said that it was significantly achieving its target of allocating 40 per cent of its funding to climate finance by 2020 , a year ahead of schedule . <p> This is according to the bank 's president , Akinwumi Adesina , who highlighted this at the recent One Planet Summit , which took place in Kenya . Adesina said the bank plans to commit at least $25 billion towards climate finance . <p> The bank 's commitment on the target , the highest among all multilateral development banks , has progressed steadily from nine per cent in 2016 to 28 per cent in 2017 and 32 per cent in 2018 . <p> " The required level of financing is only feasible with the direct involvement of the entire financial sector , " said Adesina . <p> He continued , " Consequently , the bank launched the African Financial Alliance for Climate Change ( AFAC ) to link all stock exchanges , pension and sovereign wealth funds , central banks and other financial institutions of Africa to mobilise and incentivise the shift of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " <p> The AfDB made another milestone announcement . " It is not good enough to simply ask countries to stay away from polluting technologies . <p> " We have to be proactive in exploring alternatives . We will therefore be launching the ' green baseload ' facility under the Sustainable Energy Fund for Africa ( SEFA 2.0 ) to provide concessional finance and technical assistance to support the penetration and scale-up of renewable energy , to provide affordable and reliable renewable energy baseload , " he said . <p> Several donors , including Canada , Denmark , Germany , Norway , Italy , the UK , and USAID have indicated their interest in this transformative instrument , which would also help to replace coal . The AfDB has played a critical role in building Africa 's clean energy capacities . The bank 's last investment in a coal project was 10 years ago . <p> Additionally , and in line with its ambitious New Deal on Energy for Africa , 95 per cent of all bank investments in power generation over the 2016-18 period have been in renewables.
