<p> The Food and Agriculture Organisation ( FAO ) has reiterated its commitment to strengthen the agricultural response to climate change in North East through promoting the Climate Smart Agriculture ( CSA ) programme . <p> Mr Nourou Macki , the Deputy FAO Representative in Nigeria , said this in his statement at the just concluded validation workshop on the programme in Yola . <p> Macki said CSA approach in North East targeted the conflict-affected states : Adamawa , Borno and Yobe and was being handled by FAO in collaboration with regional Ministries of Agriculture and Environment in the affected states . <p> According to him , the International Crops Research Institute for the Semi-Arid Tropics ( ICRISAT ) is also among the organsations in such collaborative effort . <p> He noted that climate change had adversely affected food production in the Sahel and promoting conflicts in the area , adding that FAO was committed to CSA , to address conflicts related to climate change . <p> " The development of the CSA profiles for the three states is a step in up scaling FAO 's CSA activities in North East Nigeria . <p> " The ultimate aim is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ livelihood more sustainable in the face of climate change , " Macki said . <p> Iyabo Mustapha , the Chief Land Resource Officer , who represented the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development at the workshop , described the programme as timely , adding that it would enjoy the full support of government to succeed . <p> " The continued dialogue between the government and FAO is therefore of high importance and will maximise the outcome of CSA interventions , " Mustapha said . <p> Prof. Daniel Gwary , the CSA Consultant for Yobe and his counterpart for Adamawa , Dr Adam Lawan , said the programme would among others , increase agricultural productivity of climate-change and conflict-affected households . <p> They also said that it would support adaptation to climate change and mitigate the emission of Greenhouse Gases .
