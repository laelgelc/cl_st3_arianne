<h> Small town , one big dumpsite for plastic waste <h> Share this article <p> SINCE China banned the import of plastic waste in 2017 , Malaysia has become one of the world 's biggest plastic importers , receiving hundreds of thousands of tonnes of rubbish each year . And a small town called Jenjarom is paying the price . <p> Home to about 30,000 people , about 20km southeast of Port Klang , Jenjarom is the main dumping site for the plastic waste arriving through the country 's main port . <p> In January to July last year , Malaysia received 754,000 tonnes of plastic waste , mostly from the UK , Germany , Japan and Australia , according to Greenpeace . <p> Not all plastics can be recycled . But instead of handing over unrecyclable scraps and parts to waste centres for proper disposal , the illegal factories save costs by burning the plastics , releasing noxious fumes that threaten public health . What 's still there are about 17,000 tonnes of waste piled high in heaps , giving the town the look of a giant landfill . <p> Energy , Technology , Science , Climate Change and Environment Minister Yeo Bee Yin said there were several ways to manage the waste , the most feasible being to send the plastics to a cement plant to be burnt as a heat source for the boiler . She added that the transport costs were nearly RM2.5 million . Not surprisingly , no one has taken up the offer . <p> Yeo said a 139 illegal plastic waste recycling factories had been shut down across Malaysia since January . <p> Malaysia only allows the import of clean plastic scrap that could be recycled into resin , and only 19 out of 114 approved permit holders currently meet the requirements to import plastic scrap , said Housing and Local Government Minister Zuraida Kamaruddin. -- March 21 , 2019 . <p> Resident turned activist Pua Lay Peng looks at piles of plastic waste at an illegal recycling factory @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and others like it are profiting at the expense of the health of the people and the environment . -- The Malaysian Insight pic by Najjua Zulkefli , March 21 , 2019 .
