<h> A failure of imagination <p> One reason why people find it difficult to think about climate change and the future may be their understanding of human history . <p> The present day is believed to be the product of centuries of development . These developments have led to a globalized world of complex states , in which daily life for most people is highly urbanized , consumerist , and competitive . <p> By this account , humanity has triumphed over the dangers and uncertainties of the natural world , and this triumph will continue to unfold in the future . Anything else would seem to be going " backwards , " in a world where " backwardness " is pitied or despised . <p> But it is now clear that we have n't triumphed . The future has become very uncertain and our way of thinking needs to change . Could new historical narratives help ? How might they look ? <p> Progress towards oblivion <p> The current view of the past , present , and future as a trajectory of progress is constantly reiterated by politicians and taught to children in schools . The direction of history itself is towards the general good . <p> It is very hard , then , for anyone thinking in this framework to imagine a future in which societies adapt to the challenges of climate change . <p> This is especially the case where adaptions might have to take the form of significantly reduced consumption , unfamiliar forms of social organization , and harder work to produce food or manage local environments . <p> These ideas about the future look very different from the technologically advanced and globalized tomorrow that the progress narrative seemed to promise . At present , ideas in popular culture about the impact of climate change are often apocalyptic and dystopian . Ideas about mitigating climate change seem limited to fantasies of last-minute salvation by scientific genius or alien intervention . <p> In this respect @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ are more rooted in a cultural understanding of history . This poses both a challenge and an opportunity to the study of history . <p> Fields such as climate , environmental , or global history help to think about the past in planetary rather than national terms . Some of that questions the Western interpretation of history and the exploitation of people and nature which punctuates it . <p> Recovering the stories of people marginalized from these narratives helps people think about life in a different light .
