<h> Latest Video <h> OPINION : The state of the world 's water <h> Friday March 22 2019 <p> By Ibrahim Byatike Kabole <p> Today , Friday 22 March , is World Water Day -- a day to raise awareness of the global water crisis and urge stakeholders to take action . <p> Statistics from the JMP/WHO show that 884 million people worldwide still do not have access to clean water near their home . Around 4 billion people live in physically water-scarce areas , where demand exceeds supply . This number is expected to go up to 5 billion by 2050 . <p> In Tanzania , the 10th Economic Update by the World Bank reported that the renewable per capita freshwater resources dropped over the past 25 years from more than 3,000 cubic meters per person to around 1,600 . The decline , which is driven by increasing demand for a finite resource , will likely continue and reach around 1,400 cubic meters per person by 2025 , well below the 1,700-cubic meter per person threshold that defines water stressed countries . <p> Lacking access to this human right means people are deprived of an equal chance to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ by a lack of access to clean water are women and girls , who often bear the responsibility of walking long distances to fetch water . <p> The amount of time spent collecting water impacts the quality of life for women and girls , and limits their development opportunities . <p> A new report released today from WaterAid -- ' Beneath the surface ' -- looks at the issue of water scarcity -- both in physical and socio-economic terms . Water scarcity exists for two reasons . Physical scarcity means there is n't enough water to go around . Socio-economic scarcity means there is water present , but it is n't available to all because of lack of investment and political will , often excluding poor and vulnerable communities , and more so women and girls . <p> The availability of water can also be affected by wealth , creating a vicious circle for the poorest and most marginalised people . Spending hours collecting water from an unsafe source that will make you and your family sick makes it much harder to earn a good living . When Cape Town 's drought last year threatened to make it the first modern city to run out of water , wealthier residents fitted high-tech filters to the 30,000 private boreholes in suburban gardens and bought bottled water , while poorer residents queued at standpipes . Similarly , in Dar es Salaam , some of the poorest communities in Mbagala , Kibondemanji in particular , are paying more for unsafe water than those in wealthier communities in Mikocheni and Masaki . This is has to change . <p> The Ministry of Water currently reports that 64.8 per cent people have access to water in rural areas , 63 per cent in smalls towns and 80 per cent in urban areas .
