Photo : Flickr.com / United Nations Photo <p> ACCRA -- Ghana economy is losing more than $2-billion a year due to the impact of child malnutrition , which has driven up healthcare costs , strained the education system and hindered the productivity of the workforce , a study said on Tuesday . <p> Malnutrition comes in many forms , such as poor child growth and development or vulnerability to infection among those who do not get enough food , which is known as undernutrition . <p> Undernutrition among children costs Ghana $2,6bn ( R36,56bn ) a year -- 6,4 percent of its GDP -- according to a study led by the AU and backed by UN aid agencies and the African Development Bank . <p> Stunted growth , which occurs when children miss out on vital nutrients in the womb and their first two years of life , is the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ( COHA ) study , which was carried out in 12 other African nations . <p> " The goal of eliminating stunting is key to achieving zero hunger , Sustainable Development Goal 2 , " said Thomas Yanga , director of the World Food Programme 's Africa office . <p> UN member states agreed last year to 17 ambitious goals -- the Sustainable Development Goals -- to tackle the world 's most troubling problems ranging from hunger to climate change . <p> " The losses to the economy can be averted through strategic interventions which ensure adequate nutrition for mothers and young children , " Yanga said in a statement . <p> Ghana has made progress in improving child nutrition , as stunting rates have almost halved , to 19 percent from 36 percent -- in just over a decade , the 2016 Global Nutrition Report showed .
