<h> Quality debut novel for young adults <p> Australian author Mark Smith 's debut novel tells a beautiful and intimate story that belies the bleak future in which it is set . <p> It is is told through the eyes of 16-year-old Finn Morrison , the only person from his isolated coastal community to have survived waves of illness and violence that have killed most of Australia 's population . Thanks to his father 's foresight , he has enough food and fuel stockpiled to satisfy his basic needs for the meantime , and his life has settled into a familiar routine ; hunting , fishing and surfing , an activity which keeps him in touch with his old life and , by his own admission , sane . <p> But the only human contact he has had in two years is with an elderly man in the neighbouring valley , so when a terrified Afghani girl appears in the town , closely followed by a violent gang of men who control the land to the north of the settlement , he responds instinctively to protect her . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ his home that Finn has time to fully consider the ramifications of his actions ; although the girl , Rose , who is ill and pregnant , is not herself a threat , by rescuing her he is in real danger of being discovered himself . <p> Her presence takes the edge off his loneliness , and after she describes her life growing up as a refugee and her treatment by the men now chasing her , he feels compelled to make up for all the bad things that have happened to her . So when she begs him to find her sister , from whom she became separated during their escape , he embarks on a second rescue mission , even though it forces him into direct conflict with her pursuers . <p> Although the basic plot elements of The Road to Winter are by no means new , what makes this novel stand out from the multitude of other dystopian near-futures that crowd bookshop shelves is Finn 's narrative voice . Although young , he is neither naive nor cynical , and , despite what he has lived through @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ immensely appealing . We get glimpses of the plague and its aftermath but no more than necessary to set the scene . <p> Smith also draws attention to important contemporary issues - Finn mentions how winters are growing briefer and milder due to climate change , and learns through Rose how asylum-seekers are forced into indentured labour , a policy euphemistically termed " refugee reassignment ' ' - but he does so with a similarly light touch . <p> Because of this focus on the characters at the story 's heart we become deeply invested in their welfare , and the main tension in the novel comes not from horrific details but from our desire to see them succeed . Like the best YA fiction , The Road to Winter is sure to appeal just as much to an adult audience , and I can easily see it being discussed as passionately by a local book club as a high-school English class . It is already being compared to John Marsden 's best-selling " Tomorrow ' ' series , in my opinion justly so .
