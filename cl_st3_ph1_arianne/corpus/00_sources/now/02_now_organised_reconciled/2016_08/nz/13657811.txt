<p> Above all , David Robie 's Do n't Spoil My Beautiful Face : Media , Mayhem and Human Rights in the Pacific is a damning indictment of the parlous state of affairs in parts of this region . <p> The book is also a telling account of the continuous failure of leadership on a fairly grand scale , with ordinary people bearing the brunt of it . <p> Dr Robie , professor of journalism at the Auckland University of Technology and director of the Pacific Media Centre , deals with the vital issues of environmental degradation , media censorship , social chaos and human suffering ( largely caused by bad governance ) , various types of violent and nonviolent conflicts , and colonialism and neocolonialism . <p> Allegedly apathetic international and local media also attract some flak . Reflecting on his two-and-a-half decades of Asia-Pacific coverage , Robie intones , " Sadly , not a lot has changed " ( page 6 ) . <p> He adds , " Political independence has not necessarily rid the Pacific of the problems that it faces , and in many cases , Pacific political leaders are themselves part of the problem " ( 27 ) . But it is not only rogue Pacific Island leaders who are causing problems . <p> Robie also faults leaders from developed countries for their inaction in the face of what he describes as a litany of tortures , murders , exploitation , rapes , military raids , and arbitrary arrests . Most affected is West Papua , where the brutal repression of the native Melanesians by the Indonesian security services is well documented . <p> The book reminds us why the Pacific is still struggling despite copious amounts of bilateral aid over the decades . However , new ideas often face resistance . <p> As a result of challenging the orthodoxy , Robie has attracted criticism from some traditionalists , who believe that concepts such as " peace journalism " contravene media objectivity , what with Bainimarama 's military-backed government in Fiji touting its version of " journalism of hope , " fueling suspicions about government control of the media under the guises of stability and development . <p> Robie is adamant @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ neither would it pander to political slogans such as " cultural sensitivity , " which he sees as a cover-up for abuse of power . <p> To the contrary , Robie envisions an approach based on a greater level of intensive journalism focused on exposing the truth , reporting on alternatives , and offering solutions . Robie 's central thesis is that the Pacific is caught in a vicious cycle of conflicts and underdevelopment . <p> Traditional subsistence lifestyles have been under sustained pressure from globalisation and other forces . The media are duty-bound to keep track of these trends and draw attention to them , but their response has largely been inadequate . <p> Robie is calling for a new media strategy , based on greater journalistic effort , commitment , and foresight . Some may question whether such a new direction is even possible , given media 's entrenched , deadline-driven , profit-focused economic model . <p> These ideological arguments aside , the post -- Cold War trend of mayhem in the Pacific demands investigation into the media-politics-conflict nexus in the Pacific context .
