<h> Neil Walker promotes carbon farming in Taranaki <p> Carbon farming advocate Neil Walker says many people have been surprised by the rise in the price of carbon . <p> A Taranaki political leader is urging the region 's landowners and investors to grab the opportunity created by rising carbon prices . <p> Neil Walker says selling accumulated carbon could not only be a boost for Taranaki back country farmers but could also help dairy farmers seeking a bridge between the sustained downturn in farmgate milk prices and the projected upswing . <p> " People with carbon hidden away could make money that will provide a bridge to better times , " he said . <p> Although Taranaki 's " front shops " showcasing the black gold of the energy industry and the dairy industry 's white gold were historically prosperous and successful , the back country had struggled . " So we need to do something to boost those areas where in fact a great opportunity exists , " he said . <p> He 's also suggesting many Taranaki dairy farms have areas that could be developed @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ he says dairy farmers could diversify their investments by turning to carbon farming rather than buying another dairy farm . <p> As a believer in climate change , he 's already made moves to manage the risk it presents - and has reaped the benefits . " The climate is moving , so we 're going to have do a few things . The pressure is on . " <p> He 's just received more than $110,000 for more than 6000 carbon units grown in forest blocks he planted in South Taranaki . " I registered with the carbon exchange , sold my units and the money was in the bank four days later , " he said . By 2019 he will have for sale 4500 carbon units a year . <p> He has 114 hectares of carbon forest in the Waverley-Waitotara area - on 200ha at Nukuhau and on 320ha at Mangaone - developed with his own funds and money from the Afforestation Grant Scheme ( AGS ) , a $22 million Ministry for Primary Industries programme designed to help establish 15,000 hectares of new forest between 2015 and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in the value of carbon in New Zealand from $10.80 on March 31 2016 to more than $18 a unit now had surprised many observers .
