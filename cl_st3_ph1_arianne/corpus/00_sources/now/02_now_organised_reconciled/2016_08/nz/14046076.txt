<h> Fight for local democracy <p> The battle lines are being drawn as a fight for the future of local democracy heats up across New Zealand . But , as some mayors vow to take to the street , others say change is overdue . Chris Morris reports . <p> Reaction has been swift , as an local government sector in uproar declares the proposals " amalgamation by stealth " and an attack on local democracy . <p> The opposition was apparent at last month 's Local Government New Zealand conference in Dunedin , when 97% of councils voted to vigorously resist any loss of local input or control . <p> It was the kind of concern that prompted Invercargill Mayor Tim Shadbolt and his councillors to take out out a full-page newspaper advertisement , vowing to fight the changes and urging the rest of Southland to do the same . <p> Other southern mayors were also prepared to join the struggle , including Central Otago Mayor Tony Lepper . <p> He told the ODT the reforms threatened local democracy , but predicted " people would be out @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Otago Regional Council chairman Stephen Woodhead agreed , saying the reforms attacked " some of the core principles of local government " and were " a recipe for outrage " . <p> Dunedin Mayor Dave Cull said the changes could strip communities of control of their assets without giving people a say . <p> " It 's an extraordinary breach of democratic process , " he said . <p> It was all a far cry from the upbeat message delivered by Local Government Minister Peseta Sam Lotu-Iiga when he announced the reforms in March . <p> The changes , he said at the time , were designed to give councils new ways to manage their finances , improve their efficiency and " create value for ratepayers " . <p> Councils were collectively responsible for $134 billion in assets , but had a host of challenges to tackle in the future -- from rising rates and service costs , to infrastructure renewal and the demographic and climate change hurdles that loomed . <p> " Local government must respond and adapt to an increasing range of challenges if it is to deliver @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ expectations of New Zealanders .
