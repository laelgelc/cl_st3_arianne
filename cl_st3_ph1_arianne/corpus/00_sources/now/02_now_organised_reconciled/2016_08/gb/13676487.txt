Number of cities disclosing climate action passes 500 globally <p> Carbon Disclosure Project reveals 70 per cent increase in number of cities around the world reporting climate actions since 2015 <p> A record number of cities around the world are now measuring and disclosing environmental data on an annual basis in order to better manage emissions and build resilience against climate change impacts , according to the CDP . <p> The non-profit organisation , formerly known as the Carbon Disclosure Project , revealed today that 553 cities globally representing 621 million citizens have this year reported on the climate actions they are taking , representing a rise of 70 per cent since 2015 . <p> Europe saw an 83 per cent increase in the number of cities reporting on their climate actions since the start of the year to 126 across 32 countries , with @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ first time . <p> Moreover , of the 83 global ' megacities ' in the C40 Cities Climate Leadership Group , which includes London , Paris , New York City , Dubai , Lagos , Tokyo and Rio de Janeiro , 90 per cent have disclosed environmental data so far this year . <p> In Africa , there was a near four-fold increase in the number of cities reporting climate information to CDP from 12 to 46 since the Paris climate talks last December , with many of the cities now reporting on their progress located in some of the least developed countries in the continent , such as the Democratic Republic of Congo , Ethiopia and Uganda . <p> Significant increases in the number of cities reporting to CDP have also been seen in North America where now 131 cities are disclosing information , and Latin America where 136 cities have provided data , of which half are in Brazil . <p> " We are thrilled to have so many new cities , in particular from the developing world , share their climate strategies through CDP for the first time @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . " Disclosing environmental information fuels awareness that in turn helps city leaders plan , finance and build low-carbon resilient cities . You can not manage what you do not measure , and this year city leaders around the world are sending a clear message that they are ready and able to take on the global climate challenge . " <p> According to CDP , increasing awareness of climate risks has also led to more cities undertaking greenhouse gas emissions inventories in order to better understand their climate impact . In 2011 , 10 per cent of cities reported undertaking a citywide emissions inventory , but this has now risen to 40 per cent . <p> " When cities measure their climate footprint and seek a sustainable path to green growth powered by clean energy , they take us all further towards the global transition to low emissions and resilient development , " said Patricia Espinosa , the new executive secretary of the UNFCCC , who welcomed the news . " I congratulate cities taking action and encourage everyone to use NAZCA to showcase their climate commitments . "
