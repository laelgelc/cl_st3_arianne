<h> Diesel spill feared after oil rig runs aground on Isle of Lewis <p> Thousands of litres of diesel are feared to have spilled into the ocean from an oil rig that ran aground on the Western Isles in severe weather . <p> Checks found that two of the fuel tanks on the Transocean Winner rig have been breached but the Maritime and Coastguard Agency ( MCA ) said it is " unclear at this time " how much oil from the tanks has escaped . <p> The rig was carrying 280 tonnes of diesel - more than 300,000 litres - when it was blown ashore in severe weather conditions on the western side of the Isle of Lewis early on Monday . <p> The semi-submersible rig detached from its tug during towing and the weather prevented the line being reconnected . <p> A " low level " of pollution was detected by tests on Monday , Western Isles Council said , but no obvious signs of a spill remain at the site . <p> Hugh Shaw , the UK Government 's representative for maritime and salvage , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ signs of a slick further out in the Atlantic . <p> No-one was on board the rig when it grounded at Dalmore beach near Carloway . <p> Salvage teams were winched on board by helicopter on Tuesday to assess the damage but have been unable to return due to the weather . <p> The diesel was being stored in four tanks on the rig , two of which have been damaged . <p> People are being urged to stay away from the beach as teams continue to work and a temporary 300-metre ( 980ft ) exclusion zone has been set up at sea . <p> Environmental groups have raised concerns , while it is expected to take " some time " to re-float the rig as it came ashore at high tide with a storm surge . <p> An MCA spokeswoman said : " During the inspection the salvors discovered that two of the fuel tanks appear to have been breached ;
