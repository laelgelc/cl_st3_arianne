<h> Services <h> Time to address the issue of nutrition <p> IN the run-up to the Olympic Games we heard a great deal about performance-enhancing substances . Now that we have watched the feats of the clean Olympians we appreciate the results of healthy diets . <p> It seems very appropriate that alongside the Games the World Health Assembly met to discuss the problem of the effects of poor nutrition on the world 's poor and vulnerable , under the banner Nutrition for Growth ( N4G ) . <p> The world-wide nature of the problem and the extent of malnutrition in individual Nations make collecting data extraordinarily difficult . <p> Loading article content <p> Poor nutrition harms almost every aspect of a person 's life . It impairs the immune system of the under-fives , exposing them to the deadly effects of killer diseases , such as pneumonia and diarrhoea . <p> It causes stunting and wasting , which affect the capacity to learn at school , which goes on to have a real effect on the person 's earning capacity in later life . Under this last heading , undernourishment in the 1,000 days after the start of a woman 's pregnancy is critical for the child 's wellbeing and even calls for protection of adolescent girls . <p> Analysis shows that 24 of low and middle-income governments allocate just two per cent of their spending on under-nutrition . <p> Much more must be done to address this whole problem . The financial incentives are enormous - an estimated return on investment of ? 16 for every ? 1 spent . <p> But it is not only the poor who are affected . Bad nutrition among the rich is causing obesity , diabetes and certain cancers among the more prosperous nations .
