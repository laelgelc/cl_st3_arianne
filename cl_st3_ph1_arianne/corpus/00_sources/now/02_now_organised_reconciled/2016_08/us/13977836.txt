<p> No lightning and thunder . No way to see it . But with smartphones awakening sleeping residents , Concord and surrounding communities were made aware of the possibility of an approaching tornado . <p> Tornadoes are not an every day - or even every week - occurrence around our area . Tornadoes that strike at night are even more of a rarity . The last nocturnal tornado to strike Massachusetts was in 1970 . The frequency of such severe weather leads many to utter that phrase : " We do n't get weather like that around here . " <p> Regardless of one 's stance on climate change , the fact is that we do get that kind of weather . The challenge lies in the fact that the frequency of such weather is not very high , thus possibly leading to complacency . <p> Monday morning 's tornado in Concord underscores the importance of having the emergency alerts setting on our smartphones turned on , or having an app or weather radio programmed to alert for approaching severe weather . <p> " All of us in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ o'clock this morning that there was a potential for a tornado in our area that at least put people on early alert , " Concord town manager Chris Whelan said in a press conference late Monday morning . <p> Emergency alerts are location-based , meaning the smartphone will only alert if it detects that it is within the impact area . In other words , the phone wo n't wake you up in the middle of the night for a storm several miles away . <p> In a case like Monday morning , the only things that awakened residents in Concord were the chaos of snapping trees at 3:20 a.m. or a smartphone or weather radio chirping 20 minutes before the tornado struck .
