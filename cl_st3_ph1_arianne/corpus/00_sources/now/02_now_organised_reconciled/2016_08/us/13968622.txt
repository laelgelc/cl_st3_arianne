the solar eclipse of 20 March 2015 from the UK , Faroe Islands and Iceland , published today ( Monday 22 August 2016 ) , sheds new light on the phenomenon . <p> The research , led by Professor Edward Hanna , of the University of Sheffield 's Department of Geography , used data from 76 UK Met Of ? ce weather stations , 30 weather stations in the Faroe Islands and 148 stations in Iceland . <p> It the eclipse by about ten minutes . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ stations , the mean temperature drop was 0.91 ? 0.78 ( 0.31 ? 0.40 ) degC , but the mean temperature drops for relatively calm and windy 9 per cent on average during the ? rst half of the eclipse , in line with previous studies . <p> There was no detectable effect of the eclipse on the wind-direction or barometric pressure time series
