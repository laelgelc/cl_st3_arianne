<h> Ecologists urged to embrace the power of ' natural experiments ' <h> August 31 , 2016 by David Stacey <p> Researchers at The University of Western Australia are urging ecologists to increase the use of large-scale natural and manmade disasters as experiments to help solve the greatest environmental challenges facing the planet . <p> Earthquakes , tsunamis and oil spills such as Deepwater Horizon have created experiments , known as Large-scale Unreplicated Natural Experiments ( LUNEs ) , that scientists have used to understand how the world works . <p> A paper by UWA researchers Dr Shanta Barley and Professor Jessica Meeuwig , published today in the journal Ecosystems , says using the results of LUNEs is critical , especially at a time when investment in science is increasingly constrained . <p> Lead author on the paper , Dr Barley , said humans have caused the rapid demise of wildlife on Earth by altering the climate , cutting down forests and overfishing the oceans . <p> " Scientists recently named humans as a ' super predator ' in recognition of our voracious consumption of the planet 's @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ more efficient than any other predator , " Dr Barley said . <p> " Yet our understanding of how human activity influences ecological processes remains poor . Shark fisheries , for example , have largely removed the predators on some coral reefs , allowing researchers to test hypotheses about their ecological role in marine systems . <p> " Large-scale natural experiments have many advantages in the current financial climate where investment in science is constrained -- they 're low budget , they 're more ecologically realistic than lab experiments and they 're often the only way to ethically and practically study some subjects , " Dr Barley said . <p> However , LUNEs come with a catch : they are impossible to exactly replicate .
