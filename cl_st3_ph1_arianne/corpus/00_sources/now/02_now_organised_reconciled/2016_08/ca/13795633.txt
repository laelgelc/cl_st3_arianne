<h> Instead of the new Quebec , Ontario should become the new Michigan Add to ... <p> When Ontario Premier Kathleen Wynne and Michigan Governor Rick Snyder promised this month to work together to reverse the flow of auto-industry investments to warmer climes , there was little doubt about which leader of a former industrial powerhouse had more economic good news to brag to about . <p> Since the last recession , Ontario and Michigan have experienced a reversal of fortunes . The Rust Belt state has overtaken Ontario in auto production amid a manufacturing renaissance that has bypassed its northern neighbour despite a decline of nearly 25 per cent in the loonie 's value . <p> There is no overstating how low Michigan went during the first decade of this century . The state lost more than 200,000 manufacturing jobs from 2006 to 2010 , wiping out 31 per cent of all factory employment . The Michigan economy contracted by 15 per cent during the same period and the unemployment rate surged above 14 per cent during the Great Recession . Michigan 's unemployment rate has declined , spectacularly , to 4.6 per cent . Ontario 's stands at 6.4 per cent . <p> The two jurisdictions have also moved in different political directions . While Liberal-led Ontario has embraced bigger government and antagonized business with evermore regulation and expenses , Republican-led Michigan cut spending and adopted a pro-business policy agenda . <p> Is it any wonder their economies diverged too ? <p> Two new studies , by admittedly right-leaning think tanks , place the responsibility for Ontario 's relative economic decline at the doorstep of the politicians ( and their union backers ) , who have made the province a less attractive place for businesses to invest . Ontario 's per-capita net debt has doubled since 2003 while its program spending as a percentage of gross domestic product has climbed a full three percentage points . <p> Even if Ms. Wynne 's government meets its deficit-reduction targets , Ontario 's net debt per capita is set to rise to $22,947 by 2018 , surpassing Quebec 's $22,340 .
