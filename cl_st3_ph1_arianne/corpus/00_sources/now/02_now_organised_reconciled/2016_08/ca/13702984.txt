<h> Melting Greenland ice sheet will soon unearth waste from Cold War-era military base : York University study <p> When a secret U.S. military base built in Greenland during the Cold War was decommissioned in 1967 , hazardous wastes were abandoned at the site , and scientists believed that the toxic sludge would be forever trapped in layers of built-up ice . <p> But new research published today in the journal Geophysical Research Letters suggests that melting of the Greenland ice sheet could cause the trapped wastes to re-enter the environment and interfere with nearby ecosystems . <p> According to lead study author William Colgan , assistant professor at the Lassonde School of Engineering at York University , the " bury and forget it " attitude at the site was based on false assumptions about the ice sheets in Greenland . <p> " Two generations ago , people were interring waste in different areas of the world , and now climate change is modifying those sites , " said Colgan . By the end of the decade , the United States had built several bases in Greenland , including an underground installation called Camp Century , dubbed the " city under the ice . " <p> The city spanned three kilometres , and housed roughly 200 people . <p> From Camp Century , and without the knowledge of the Danish government , the United States carried out a nuclear testing program called " Project Iceworm , " named for the tunnels scheduled to house nuclear missile silos under the ice . Camp Century -- itself powered by a nuclear reactor -- was the blueprint for developing a launch system in northwestern Greenland that could put 600 nuclear warheads within striking distance of the Soviet Union . <p> Because of physical changes in the ice sheet that made tunnels impossible in the long-run , the U.S. Department of Defense eventually suspended Project Iceworm in 1964 . Camp Century was never finished , and was shut down , while hazardous waste produced at the base was dumped under the ice .
