<p> The World Economic Forum 's Global Risks report has continued since 2012 to rank water scarcity1 as one of the top three threats facing the global economy . Nearly two thirds of respondents to CDP 's water questionnaire reported that water scarcity poses a substantial risk to their business , with financial impacts exceeding USD 2.5 billion in 2015 . According to Sustainalytics , a leading global provider of ESG research and ratings , climate change is likely to worsen the situation and to " exacerbate water challenges for business in meaningful but unpredictable ways . " The findings are based on Sustainalytics ' April 2016 report , Water scarcity : Will investors be left high and dry ? <p> The situation in Asia is particularly acute . This means that key sectors , such as India 's agricultural sector and China 's industrial sector , are likely to experience operational disruptions . These disruptions are already occurring with regularity in the utilities sector . In March 2016 , for example , NTPC was forced to shut down its 1,600 MW Farakka thermal power plant due to a water shortage , which also led to an increase in power prices on the India energy exchange.4 Sustainalytics ' report also notes that a growing number of Asian countries -- including China , India and South Korea -- are depleting non-replenishable aquifers to meet water demand , which is likely to worsen water scarcity issues in these regions . <p> Despite overarching evidence supporting the intensification of impacts on businesses due to water scarcity , Sustainalytics ' research demonstrates that strong water management programmes globally are the exception rather than the norm in most water-intensive industries .
