<h> Action must follow waste management strategy <h> share <h> Waste management . Photo/Courtesy <p> Lack of proper garbage disposal in Kenya has reached alarming levels . Nearly every other major urban centre is facing a crisis of mounting waste due to neglect of collection from relevant authorities . Indeed , Kenyans have become accustomed to living with trash everywhere . <p> Smartly dressed ladies and gentlemen walking on overflowing slimy gutters without any qualms is a common sight in our urban centres . In Nairobi , much of uncollected waste is found in the Eastlands area and in informal settlements where majority of the residents live . <p> Most of the garbage here consists of organic materials from foodstuffs , polythene paper and used plastic containers . In the affluent areas , most of the waste consists of the foregoing with a fair share of solids and metals ( e-waste ) from malfunctional information and communication technology gadgets . <p> Basically , the main types of waste in the country include domestic waste , waste tyres , construction and demolition waste , asbestos waste and , industrial @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ from fluorescent lamps , pesticides , used oil and sludge , and sewage . Another major urban area experiencing dire waste management crisis is Mombasa , whose county government has let garbage to pile up at the city 's main entry along the Nairobi-Mombasa highway . <p> Nakuru town , formerly a model of a modern and civilised living due to its cleanliness , is today another garbage dump , as is Kisumu 's central business district . Basically , this is a countrywide problem . Now , collecting waste is one thing , but managing it through proper treatment and disposal is another . <p> Dumpsites in urban centres are today filled to capacity and are , in fact , a serious health hazard . Residents in some of these areas have been suffering from respiratory infections and other conditions due to contamination of both water and air . <p> The launch this week of the National Solid Waste Management Strategy ( NSWMS ) is an attempt to salvage a clearly desperate situation . Some of the relevant legal , regulatory and policy principles include Article 42 of the Constitution ; Environmental Management and Coordination Act , 1999 ; Vision 2030 ; Environmental Management and Coordination ( Waste Management ) Regulations of 2006 ; Occupational Safety and Health Act , 2007 and ; Public Health Act , 2012 . <p> Kenya is also a signatory to international agreements such as the Basel Convention on the control of transboundary movements of hazardous wastes and their disposal . <p> Like many other well-meaning blueprints , the NSWMS risks ending up as a waste of resources if the comprehensive strategy is not followed on . <p> The National Environment Management Authority needs to ensure there is sufficient sensitisation of the various roles we all need to perform in order to reduce pollution and environmental degradation as far as waste management is concerned . <p> Indeed , I would propose the formation of a separate entity to address this menace . We do not need to reinvent the wheel . <p> There are good examples of cost-effective waste management technologies from South Africa , Germany , Japan and China . The government should zero-rate recycling machinery to encourage private sector involvement in waste management . <p> We do not need to tax Kenyans more to meet the cost of waste management . All we need is to encourage innovation and offer incentives to organisations willing to turn the waste into money . <p> County governments should also encourage private sector participation in waste disposal and pay for these services from current revenues .
