<h> Popular Stories <h> Local Real Estate <h> Subscribe Now <p> THE call to ban net fishing from Noosa beaches makes sense , deserves support and should be a policy objective for all of the Sunshine Coast . <p> The arguments to sustain a practice that has been outgrown by the development of the coastal strip are thin . Our inshore fishery is struggling under the pressures of reduced spawning habitat through the destruction of mangroves , increased pollutant run-off into our rivers and streams and environmental changes through climate change . <p> The commercial benefits of beach netting are few and despite claims to the contrary from the industry do little to improve the availability of fresh fish locally . <p> Mooloolaba already processes the largest volume of seafood from trawler and line fishing of any port on the Australian east coast . <p> Much of the catch seasonally netted from our beaches is poorly handled and destined for the bait and pet food markets . There are strong arguments in taking pressure off the habitat to allow stocks to rebuild and to further boost the tourism @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ netting has historically been an element of life on the Sunshine Coast and critical to the financial support for the families that pursued it . They should without doubt be properly compensated . <p> The Queensland Government is already taking that approach in regions further to our north . <p> Noosa councillor Frank Pardon deserves support in his bid to see it do the same here even if that requires a contribution from local authorities who would benefit .
