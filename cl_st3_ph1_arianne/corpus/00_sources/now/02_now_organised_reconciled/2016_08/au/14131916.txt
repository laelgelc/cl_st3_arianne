<p> Inspire 's influence has been linked to a number of lone wolf terrorist attacks including the 2013 Boston Marathon bombings and the 2015 San Bernadino shootings . <p> In both cases , investigations discovered the attackers were consumers of Inspire . The magazine has also been implicated in a number of failed terrorist plots in the UK and the US . <p> " We fight terrorists , but we do n't understand them as well as we could , " says Dr Julian Droogan of the university 's department of security studies and criminology . <p> " We are trying to better understand what this group is saying in its magazine , and how and why it appeals to people in the West who then become lone wolf terrorists . " <p> Droogan and colleague Shane Peattie found that al-Qaeda and its magazine appear to show concern about a range of issues unrelated to the business of terrorism . <p> " The insights we found in this study will allow governments to create more tailored responses to the propaganda of al-Qaeda , and will be useful in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ terrorist groups , and hopefully prevent people for adopting their cause , " says Dr Droogan . <p> The issues of the magazine studied upheld " Islam is at war " as the main theme but Inspire also covers topics more commonly found in a general news publication . <p> These include climate change , media coverage of terrorist activities and financial corruption in banking , in addition to pieces on fighting the West and calling people to arms . <p> " We found al-Qaeda and Inspire proved easily distracted by local issues and the martyrdom of al-Qaeda leaders , which fluctuated through time in response to local events such as the Arab Spring , " says Dr Droogan . <p> " Their concerns were divided between religious issues , local issues and the expected terrorist ideological concerns of engaging in a war with the Western world . <p> " Understanding Inspire 's thematic landscape and its shifting character proves important in understanding and responding effectively to its jihadi discourse . "
