<p> Camp Century was built in north-west Greenland in 1959 as part of US research into the feasibility of nuclear missile launch sites in the Arctic , the University of Zurich said in a statement . <p> Staff left fuel and an unknown amount of low-level radioactive coolant there when the base shut down in 1967 on the assumption it would be entombed forever , according to the university . <p> While the waste is currently about 35 metres underneath the ice , the part of the ice sheet covering the camp could start to melt by the end of the century , based on current trends , the scientists said . <p> " Climate change could remobilise the abandoned hazardous waste believed to be buried forever beneath the Greenland ice sheet , " the university said , of findings published this week in the journal Geophysical Research Letters . <p> The study , led by York University in Canada in collaboration with the University of Zurich , estimated pollutants in the camp included 200,000 litres of diesel fuel and the coolant from a nuclear generator used to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of political challenge we have to think about , " lead author William Colgan , a climate and glacier scientist at York University , said in a statement . <p> " If the ice melts , the camp 's infrastructure , including any remaining biological , chemical , and radioactive wastes , could re-enter the environment and potentially disrupt nearby ecosystems , " the University of Zurich said . <p> The study said it would be extremely costly to try to remove any waste now . It recommended waiting " until the ice sheet has melted down to almost expose the wastes before beginning site remediation " .
