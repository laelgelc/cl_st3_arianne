<h> COAG Energy Council has no time to waste in modernising our system <p> There needs to be a shake-up in the energy industry with a greater focus on renewables . Luke Sharrett <p> by Kane Thornton <p> The COAG Energy Council on Friday will bring together state and federal Energy Ministers for the first time since Josh Frydenberg was named as Energy and Environment Minister . The anticipation across the entire sector ahead of the meeting is high , particularly when combined with the cordial relationship between Frydenberg and Labor 's Mark Butler . Planning for the future is not simple or straightforward . It will require big changes and it will require courage , but we now have a window of opportunity to make important bipartisan changes for the future . We ca n't afford to waste it . <p> Australia 's electricity system has worked well to date . But it was designed at a time when electricity meant huge power stations built next to a coal pit or gas pipeline in the middle of nowhere , transporting electrons in one direction across long distances @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Almost 20 years on from the beginning of Australia 's National Electricity Market , the energy landscape looks very different . <p> More than 1.5 million homes and businesses produce their own power from solar panels , meaning electricity now flows both ways -- from the grid into these homes and from these homes into the grid when they produce more solar energy than they need . Additional flexibility is just the beginning of why we need to change our electricity system for the future . <p> Energy storage is getting cheaper rapidly and will be an undeniable part of everyday life five or 10 years from now . The future growth of electric vehicles is unclear , but tech giants such as Apple and Google have made big bets in developing autonomous cars that could change the way we all think about both driving and vehicle ownership . The energy industry 's vast assortment of players all want something different , based on whether they are an electricity retailer or whether they own coal or gas power stations , wind farms or parts of the grid . Add to this the sheer complexity of the electricity system , and it is not surprising that the energy industry has struggled to find a united voice for so many years . <p> With the agreement reached at the UN climate conference in Paris last year and Australia 's own emissions reduction targets over the next 15 years , a dramatic change in our energy system is required . Climate change or no climate change , our existing energy generation is old and getting older . It will need to be replaced and it is now clear that renewable energy is the least-cost form of any new generation . <p> Opponents of renewable energy are quick to point out that the wind does n't blow at a consistent speed for 24 hours a day , and that the sun sets every evening as well . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ not a reason to halt the increase in Australia 's use of renewable energy . It does mean we must carefully design a flexible future energy system that enables more and more renewable energy , combined with battery storage and stronger networks . <p> Australia 's National Electricity Market ( NEM ) has reliability standards that require greater than 99.998 per cent of all demand for power to be met , and all states have achieved this benchmark for many years . Those who claim the lights are about to go out are scaremongering to try and get people to listen to them , yet they often do n't have much to say when it comes to developing solutions . But there are clearly solutions to these challenges and these problems are solvable . Australians can do anything we set our mind to . <p> And with the ministerial portfolios of Energy and Environment finally brought together under Frydenberg and a renewed spirit of bipartisanship , we need to seize this opportunity for meaningful change in the interests of all Australians.
