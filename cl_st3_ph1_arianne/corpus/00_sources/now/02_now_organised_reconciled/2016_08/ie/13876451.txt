And it is sometimes a case of ' two steps forward , one step back . ' <p> I feel that at the moment , as I see what is happening in Malawi , ' The Warm Heart of Africa ' that at one point not long ago was reporting economic growth at a rate of more than 15% per annum , yet in 2016 is again like parts of Ethiopia and Yemen - on the cusp of a global food crisis like nothing it has ever seen before . <p> The reasons for this are complex , but put simply , a combination of devastating floods and unseasonal drought has left rural poor farming communities looking at barren fields and empty grain stores . <p> In April , the country 's President declared a national state of emergency , with reports that as many as 6.5 million @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to need food aid this year . Both local commentators and international observers blame the current crisis on global warming , and an inability of rural poor and vulnerable households to adapt to the impact of changing climate on their ability to produce the crops that they need to put food on the table , from one month to the next . <p> This is where the organisation I work for comes in . Across Malawi and Ethiopia , Gorta-Self Help Africa is implementing programmes that are focused on improving agricultural production , helping households to adapt to changing climate , and supporting farmers so they can grow and earn more -- and thus improve the food that they can provide , the education and healthcare they can pay for , and the support they can give to their families . <p> In short , the aim is to break the cycle of hunger and poverty that is a reality for many millions of people across sub-Saharan Africa , Malawi and Ethiopia included . <p> As anyone who is involved in farming in Ireland will tell you , there are no @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Yields fluctuate all of the time , depending on the weather , while prices for farm commodities also go up and down , depending on the seasons , and numerous other factors .
