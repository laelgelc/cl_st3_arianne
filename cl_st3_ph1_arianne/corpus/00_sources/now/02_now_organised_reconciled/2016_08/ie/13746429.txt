<h> Extent of coral reef bleaching in the Maldives is alarming , scientists say <p> More than 60% of coral in reefs in the Maldives has been hit by " bleaching " as the world is gripped by record temperatures in 2016 , a scientific survey suggests . <p> Bleaching happens when algae that lives in the coral is expelled due to stress caused by extreme and sustained changes in temperatures , turning the coral white and putting it at risk of dying if conditions do not return to normal . <p> Unusually warm ocean temperatures due to climate change and a strong " El Nino " phenomenon that pushes up temperatures further have led to coral reefs worldwide being affected in a global bleaching event over the past two years . <p> It took place on Alifu Alifu Atholhu -- North Ari Atoll -- chosen as a representative atoll of the Maldives . <p> Dr Ameer Abdulla , research team leader and senior adviser to the IUCN on marine biodiversity and conservation science , said : " Bleaching events are becoming more frequent and more severe due @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ undertaken at the height of the 2016 event and preliminary findings of the extent of the bleaching are alarming , with initial coral mortality already observed . <p> Scientists are worried coral bleaches could be permanent ( Tadashi Okubo/AP ) <p> " We are expecting this mortality to increase if bleached corals are unable to recover . " <p> The Maldives contains around 3% of the world 's coral reefs and the islands are considered particularly at risk of climate change because they are low-lying and threatened by sea level rises . <p> In Australia , more than a fifth of the Great Barrier Reef is estimated to have died as a result of the worst mass bleaching event in history . In Kiribati in the Pacific , as much as 80% of the coral is dead .
