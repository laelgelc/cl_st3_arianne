<h> Labor 's Medi-scare could seriously hurt Turnbull <p> Labor does n't care whether its claims about Medicare privatisation are true or not , nor does it need to . All that matters is voters get behind them . This has wedged both major parties and hurts all of us , writes Terry Barnes . <p> I first voted in the 1980 federal election , when then-Liberal Malcolm Fraser was prime minister and Bill Hayden the Labor leader . <p> For most of that campaign , Labor was ahead . Fraser 's second-term government was smugly complacent , drifting and dithering , out of ideas and puff . On the other hand , Hayden was campaigning strongly , Labor was comfortably ahead in the polls and its great messiah , Bob Hawke , was entering parliament as the member for Wills . <p> Then , a week or so from polling day , Labor backbencher Peter Walsh - later to be a distinguished finance minister under Hawke - made an off-the-cuff reported remark in favour of a capital gains tax . For Fraser , the Liberals and astute @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ coupling it with reminders of the Whitlam government 's spending orgy , Fraser spearheaded a relentless , ruthless scare campaign on " Labor 's secret wealth tax " targeting the family home and hard-earned personal assets . <p> On polling day , Labor just fell short , picking up 49.6 per cent of the two-party-preferred vote and 13 extra seats , halving Fraser 's majority . But when the dust settled Fraser was still in The Lodge . <p> Fast forward to 2016 and it 's the same tactic . <p> Less than a fortnight from polling day , with national polls stuck around 50-50 , Bill Shorten and Labor are paying back the Liberals for 1980 , with interest . <p> While Labor 's claim of a Coalition Medicare privatisation agenda has been running since the story broke in February , and featured in the early part of the election campaign , it came back with a vengeance on Sunday , when Shorten made it the centrepiece of his policy pitch . <p> Ever since , current Liberal PM Malcolm Turnbull has been on the defensive . Under pressure last @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ shut down . But even that did n't shut Labor up . At every opportunity Shorten has repeated the claim , over and over again , and Labor now is running advertisements repeating it . <p> Even in Turnbull 's strongest performance by far in the campaign to date , his venturing into hostile Q&amp;A territory on Monday , questions about the so-called privatisation and claimed cuts to Medicare and hospital funding dominated his night . The issue is dogging Turnbull like Wilderness Society members dressed as koalas dogged Bob Hawke 's every step in the 1990 election campaign . <p> Clearly the issue is biting with voters , even if commentators and journalists are cynical about Labor 's claims and its lavishing billions on new health spending while promising to cut nothing . <p> That Turnbull immediately tapped the mat - effectively declaring the exploration of private sector involvement in the operational management of Medicare back-office payments systems a dead letter - was not just a cave-in to Labor 's blatant Big Lie . It gave it legitimacy . Not wise tactics , as it indicates that Medicare , and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a danger in Coalition polling . <p> Labor does n't care whether its claims are true or not , nor does it need to . All that matters is voters are more likely to trust it on Medicare . The privatisation scare , and reminding people about the Coalition Government 's budget in 2014 proposing less than previously-projected spending on public hospitals and , of course , the $7 mandatory GP co-payment , is playing to this received political wisdom . <p> That it also distracts the media conversation from the Coalition 's strong suit , economic management , not to mention scrutiny of how Labor 's own profligate spending promises are to be paid for , is a bonus . <p> Liberals may fume but ca n't complain .
