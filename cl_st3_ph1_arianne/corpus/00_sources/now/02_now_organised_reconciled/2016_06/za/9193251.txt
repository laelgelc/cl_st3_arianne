<p> NAIROBI - As they struggle to deal with more extreme weather , a range of food crops are generating more of chemical compounds that can cause health problems for people and livestock who eat them , scientists have warned . <p> A new report by the United Nations Environment Programme ( UNEP ) says that crops such as wheat and maize are generating more potential toxins as a reaction to protect themselves from extreme weather . <p> Image : UNEP report <p> But these chemical compounds are harmful to people and animals if consumed for a prolonged period of time , according to a report released during a United Nations Environment Assembly meeting in Nairobi . <p> " Crops are responding to drought conditions and increases in temperature just like humans do when faced with a stressful situation , " explained Jacqueline McGlade , chief scientist and director of the Division of Early Warning and Assessment at UNEP . <p> Under normal conditions , for instance , plants convert nitrates they absorb into nutritious amino acids and proteins .
