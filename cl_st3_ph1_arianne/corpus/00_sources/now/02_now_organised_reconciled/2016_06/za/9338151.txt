<h> Climate change is making food toxic <p> Poisonous crops are just one of the problems caused by extreme climate change , say scientists <p> As South Africa battles the worst drought in its history , scientists warn that extreme weather is causing hazardous changes in crops . <p> The report entitled UNEP FRONTIERS 2016 REPORT : Emerging Issues of Environmental Concern , found that extreme weather patterns are causing crops to release toxic chemical compounds that are dangerous to animals and human beings . Prolonged drought can cause nitrate to collect in crops such as wheat , barley , maize and millet . When consumed , this can lead to nitrate poisoning which can cause miscarriages , insufficient oxygen circulation and death . <p> Last week Minister of Co-oparative Governance and Traditional Affairs Des Van Rooyen announced that eight out of the nine provinces had been declared drought emergency areas . In a media statement released by his department , the minister said : " The drought situation is still gloomy . Mycotoxins are the toxic by-products of fungal growth . <p> According to another study published in the British Medical Bulletin called Toxigenic fungi and mycotoxins , there are four types of toxicity levels to mycotoxins which affect human beings and animals in different ways . <p> They range from acute , chronic , mutagenic and teratogenic . Acute mycotoxin poisoning commonly causes the deterioration of liver or kidney function , which in extreme cases may lead to death . <p> " Some mycotoxins act primarily by interfering with protein synthesis , and produce effects ranging from skin sensitivity or can lead to the death of skin tissue and even extreme immunodeficiency . The study says that other mycotoxins act as neurotoxins , which " in low doses may cause sustained trembling in animals , but at only slightly higher doses cause brain damage or death . " <p> The UNEP says the solution to the problem is the development of crops that are drought-resistant and can handle the stress of extreme weather patterns .
