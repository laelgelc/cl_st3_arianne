<p> June 16 , now called Youth Day , is one day in our annual calendar of 13 public holidays . <p> But , 40 years down the track what are we trying to remember ? <p> A spontaneous uprising of students against Afrikaans as a teaching medium ? <p> A general state of discontent with the discriminatory Apartheid Bantu education system ? <p> An overreaction by the authorities -- Marikana style -- to peaceful protest ? <p> The immutable loss of ' youth ' for that generation of protestors ? <p> The global exposure , after a long period of quiet , to the callousness of Apartheid rule ? <p> A PR disaster for South Africa , that was to change our public image indelibly ? <p> The beginnings of sanctions , isolation , disinvestment , kragdadigheid and ' being the skunk of the world ' ? <p> Or , just plainly , what could and should have been after 22 years of our constitutional democracy ? <p> Over the past two days I have listened to SAfm ' Forum at 8 ' talk shows where both @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of that day recalled their horror , their loss of youth , their time on Robben Island , their loss of opportunity , and their then - hopes for the future . <p> It was sad listening to their stories of 40 years past . <p> It was sadder listening to their disappointment of to-day 's present . <p> On June 16 1994 Nelson Mandela , in commemoration of the students who gave their lives said , " We commemorate this day , not with the song of defiance on our lips . This is no longer a day of protest by an excluded majority . The brave young people of that generation are today eminent premiers , ministers and members of national and provincial parliaments . They are taking their rightful place in the ranks of the new South African National Defence Force and other institutions of state and civil society . " <p> As we enter the new and glorious epoch that June 16th helped to usher in , we will do well to emulate that heroism and turn it into an asset for the tasks that lie ahead @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ lamented that these ' eminent premiers , ministers and members ' lack heroism and " are feeding at the trough , treading on one another and pushing away those who are more deserving . " <p> In ' 94 Madiba continued " In order to address the root causes of the education crisis , we intend to table a Bill in parliament soon , to eradicate discrimination and take the first major steps towards ten years of free quality education . <p> For these changes to be effective , there has to be close co-operation between government and organisations of teachers , students , parents , workers and the business community . The task to make South Africa a learning and learned nation belongs to all of us . <p> The culture of teaching and learning means also that there should be a culture of rights in the schools . Teachers deserve to be treated with respect by students , society and government . In turn , they will enjoy such respect if they act responsibly , appreciating the central position that their fraternity occupies in society . " <p> Again @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . 22 years down the track 80% of our 30 000 schools remain dysfunctional as places of learning . <p> True , infrastructural functionality has improved immensely ; more schools have electricity , have sanitation , have water , and have feeding schemes ; <p> BUT , the instructional functionality , the quality of teaching , the delivery of teaching material and the time spent on extramural activities has declined ( in the 150 rural schools I have evaluated only a handful have any activity after 14h00 -- no sport , no music , no drama , no art , nothing ) . <p> AND , the regulatory functionality has much to be desired . The scandals of SADTU 's ' pay for positions ' , the shocking matric results , the drop-out rate , the strikes , the conferences during school terms , the teacher-student pregnancies , the stories of headmaster indifference etc speak volumes . <p> So again , what are we remembering , what did the students of ' 76 sacrifice their lives for ? <p> Is there anything to commemorate as we recall the cataclysmic events of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> NGO 's ( Non-government organisations ) <p> We have approximately 100 000 registered NGO 's in South Africa , roughly 10 000 are focussed solely on education . <p> On Thursday we must celebrate their contribution to our ailing education system . Many of those who were involved in the events of ' 76 have set up , or are involved with , NGO 's that are making a significant contribution where the Education Department has not . <p> Their contribution is massive . There is hardly a disadvantaged school in the country where there are n't NGO 's at work , except maybe in deep rural areas ( a huge shame I acknowledge ) . <p> Employer assisted volunteering sees literally 10 's of 1000 's of ' staff ' being given time off during work hours to assist in schools , ECD centres and cr ? ches . If time-off is not given then some sort of time ' matching ' is encouraged . <p> Many corporates have adopted schools , evaluated their instructional , infrastructural and regulatory functionality and set about developing a set of short , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ learning functionality of ' their ' schools . <p> Financial sponsorship of the Education NGO sector is significant with corporates setting clear benchmarks for improvement . <p> Again , another untold South African story of commitment . <p> ADVANTAGED SCHOOLS/UNIVERSITIES <p> Virtually without exception every ' advantaged ' , and some not , school/university I have visited has partnered via an ' Outreach ' programme with less advantaged or disadvantaged schools in their neighbourhood . It is heart-warming to see young kids of all colours giving up time to read , explain , coach kids of their own age . <p> During the holidays workshops are organised for teachers and headmasters to assist with both curriculum and teaching ability . <p> Often when the kids from the advantaged school are playing sport in the afternoons kids from disadvantaged schools are invited onto their premises to use the library , the science lab , the media centre and the computer facilities . <p> Conclusion <p> The students and teachers of ' 76 sacrificed much to draw the attention of the world to the exclusion , discrimination and inequity promoted by Apartheid doctrine @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ world has moved on ; the spectre of terrorism , conflict , war , refugees , climate change and puerile political battles is now front of mind . <p> But we South Africans have n't forgotten . <p> We need to remember and commemorate the events of ' 76 . <p> We need to remember those who ' in the past ' made the ultimate sacrifice of death , the sacrifice of prison , the sacrifice of exile , the sacrifice of losing out on their youth , the sacrifice of revolution before education . <p> We need to remember those who ' in the present ' give selflessly in the NGO , corporate , school and university sector to improving the education of those less fortunate . <p> We need to remember those teachers and headmasters who remain dedicated and who achieve extraordinary results against the odds . <p> COPYRIGHT South Africa - The Good News .
