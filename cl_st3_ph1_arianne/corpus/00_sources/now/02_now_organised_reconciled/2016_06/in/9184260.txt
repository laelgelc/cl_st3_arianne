<h> Nepal seeks to drain giant glacial lake near Everest due to flood fears <p> The move to partially drain the lake comes one year after a major international study warned that glaciers in the Everest region could shrink by 70 percent or disappear entirely by the end of the century , owing to climate change . ( Representational Image ) <p> Kathmandu : Nepali soldiers have kicked off efforts to partially drain a giant glacial lake near Mount Everest , fearing possible flooding that could threaten the lives of thousands , an army official said on Friday . <p> Imja Tsho , located at an altitude of 5,010 metres ( 16,437 feet ) , just 10 kilometres ( 6.2 miles ) south of the world 's highest peak , is the fastest-growing glacial lake in Nepal . <p> The surface area covered by the lake expanded from 0.4 to 1.01 square kilometres between 1984 and 2009 , triggering concerns that it may breach its banks and flood villages downstream . <p> " With the aim of minimising the risk of a possible outburst ... the Nepal Army @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Imja lake by opening a channel , " said army spokesperson Tara Bahadur Karki . <p> " A mechanical gate will be installed and will be manually operated to control the water flow through the channel , " Karki told AFP . <p> The work began in April and the army expects to complete the project by the end of the year , he said . <p> A flood would have a catastrophic impact on the lives of more than 56,000 people living in villages located as far away as Nepal 's densely populated Terai plains , one scientist said . <p> " Dozens of villages located nearby and in the downstream Terai districts up to 50km away from the lake are vulnerable , " Rishi Ram Sharma , Director General at the national Department of Hydrology and Meteorology told AFP . <p> " The lake had been gaining in volume over the last 30 to 40 years due to climate change .
