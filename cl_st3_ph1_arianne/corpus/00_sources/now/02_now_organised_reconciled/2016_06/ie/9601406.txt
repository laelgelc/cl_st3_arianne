<h> Wild Atlantic Way could be wiped out by climate change -- Keating <h> Share This <p> Edwin McGreal <p> A failure to address climate change could spell the end of the Wild Atlantic Way according to a leading Mayo County Council official . Martin Keating was speaking at last Thursday 's Planning , Environment and Agriculture Strategic Policy Committee . He said a failure to enforce the Paris Agreement , which binds 195 countries to limit global warming to ' well below 2 degrees centigrade ' , could be cataclysmic for the west of Ireland . " Those two degrees could be the difference between us having a Wild Atlantic Way and not having a Wild Atlantic Way , " Mr Keating told the meeting . He was speaking on the subject of the Council 's Climate Change Strategy , a policy the council conceded is simply ' getting started ' . As part of its strategy the council is seeking to create a ' climate change co-ordinator ' post while an inter-departmental team will also be put together to address the council 's obligations . " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to effect change , " Mr Keating said . " We need three things to achieve it : the commitment to do it ; integration and collaboration ; and it requires innovation , " he said . Director of Services Tom Gilligan stressed the importance of ' thinking locally , acting globally ' . He said Mayo County Council is seeking to play a ' lead role ' in addressing climate change problems -- and not just its Environment section but ' every section in the council ' .
