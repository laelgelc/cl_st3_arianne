<p> WASHINGTON : US President Barack Obama is taking his family to two national parks this weekend on a working vacation where he will spend some time with his teenaged daughters while making the case for more spending on conservation and curbing climate change . <p> The Obamas will venture into the ancient labyrinth of caverns beneath the desert at Carlsbad , New Mexico , on Friday before flying west to the Sierra Nevada mountains and Yosemite , the country 's oldest national park and one of its most popular land marks . <p> The president will help celebrate the centennial of the National Park Service , and he is also expected to talk about his plan to reduce climate-changing carbon emissions . <p> He sees this as part of the legacy of his time in office . <p> " Stronger storms , worsening droughts , increased flooding , and longer wildfire seasons are putting our national parks and natural treasures at risk , " said Christy Goldfuss , a White House adviser on environmental issues . <p> Warming temperatures in Yosemite have pushed the pika , " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a rabbit and chipmunk , " to the brink of extinction , she said . <p> Obama 's trip comes as visits to national parks surge due in part to lower gasoline prices . <p> During his presidency , which comes to an end on Jan. 20 , 2017 , Obama has added 20 sites to the national park system , 10 of which were approved by Congress . <p> He used his own authority for the others . <p> Interior Secretary Sally Jewell said national park visits in 2016 were on pace to beat last year 's record of 307 million . <p> All those tourists contributed an estimated $300 billion to the economy , supporting about 2 million jobs , she told reporters . <p> Those are numbers she wants Congress to remember as it considers investments in public lands and addresses an $11.5 billion backlog in parks maintenance projects .
