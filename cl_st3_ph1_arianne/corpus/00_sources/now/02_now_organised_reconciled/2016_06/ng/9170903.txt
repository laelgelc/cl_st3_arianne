<h> Why Nigerian Science Professor bagged US biggest honour <p> A Nigerian professor in the United States , Prof. Deji Akinwande has received the 2016 Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers ( PECASE ) from President Barack Obama , the United States government 's highest honor for scientists and engineers in the early stages of research . <p> Prof. Akinwande , according to a report by africanleadership.co.uk , is an associate professor in electrical and computer engineering and the Jack Kilby/Texas Instruments Endowed Faculty Fellow in Computer Engineering in the Cockrell School of Engineering at The University of Texas at Austin . <p> Earlier in the year , precisely in February Akinwande was named by President Barack Obama , as one of the recipients of the 105 recipients of PECASE awards . <p> He is currently an Associate Professor with the University of Texas at Austin . The current focus of his research explores materials and electronic systems based on 2D atomic layers . He is a co-inventor of a high-frequency chip-to-chip interconnect and an electrically small antenna for bio-electronics . <p> Speaking to the newsmen @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ on May 5 , 2016 , at the White House , where the 105 researchers were hosted . <p> According to the White House , Akinwande was awarded for his " outstanding research accomplishments in nanomaterials , graphene device physics , and opto-electronics , and for dedication to the education of future scientists and engineers . " <p> Now in its 20th year , the Presidential Early Career Awards are coordinated through the President 's Office of Science and Technology Policy , which selects winners " for their pursuit of innovative research at the frontiers of science and technology and their commitment to community service as demonstrated through scientific leadership , public education , or community outreach . " <p> Prof. Akinwande is known for his groundbreaking research on nanomaterials , sensors , devices and flexible technology . He is considered one of the top researchers in the world in the areas of graphene , silicon electronics and 2-D nanomaterials for use in flexible electronics .
