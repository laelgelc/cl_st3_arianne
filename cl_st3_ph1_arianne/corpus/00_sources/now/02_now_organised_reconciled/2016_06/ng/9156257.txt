<p> Although it 's problematic to generalise about a large , diverse place it 's fair to say that Africa is fast emerging as a consumer continent . It is populated by " Afropolitans " , who are sometimes also referred to as Pan-Africanists . These are young , urban , well educated people . They are willing to spend money on luxury goods . <p> However , these people are a relatively small middle class in a sea of poverty . They are unlikely to drive a classical " trickle down " improvement to Africa 's general economic picture . <p> It 's also worth noting that not much of what these young consumers covet is actually being produced on the continent . I would argue that Africa should be called the " borrowed continent " rather than a consumer continent . Its raw materials are exported , refined elsewhere and generate big profits for other countries . Researchers come , conduct their work and run their experiments . Can Africa discard the mantle of " borrowed continent " ? <h> A history of control <p> To understand the situation that Africa finds itself in now , it 's worth looking back into the continent 's history . <p> The Monrovia group envisaged a continent whose controlling power remained with Western governments . Development would come through the exchange of resources with the western world , trading Africa 's vast mineral resources and agricultural produce . <p> The Casablanca group , meanwhile , envisaged a self-reliant continent that would be capable of governing itself through intra-continental trade . This continent would integrate socio-economic cultural values among all countries . <p> Although the two had different missions , they upheld a singular vision -- the idea of a united Africa . Over time these aspirations were swept under the carpet and in May 1963 a partially unified body emerged : the Organisation of African Unity . This was relaunched as the African Union in 2001 , at which time it adopted grandiose plans to improve governance in its member states through a process of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ seriously implemented . Instead , the relative pluralism and openness witnessed during the late 1990s and early 2000s is being eroded by a new wave of authoritarianism . <p> Ordinary Africans have not enjoyed much of the oneness imagined by this " union " . Much of the blame for this lies with western powers that remain embedded on the continent . African leaders have also compromised notions of unity through grievous maladministration and systemic oppression of their own people . And so Africa remains little more than a test site for new scientific experiments and a place from which raw materials can be extracted . This is known as neo-colonialism . <p> Today the neo-colonialists are more likely to come from the East than the West . Against this backdrop , how can Africa shift its role from borrower to creator and producer ? <p> The answers lie with integrating the continent 's people , tapping into existing indigenous knowledge and focusing on capacity development .
