<h> Editorial : Save Wellington 's signature cable car <p> Ordinarily , the council-owned service covers its costs without subsidies . But hefty upcoming capital costs , especially the purchase of new cable cars , will put it deep into the red . <p> It needs external funding or it will cease to run within 10 years , the cable car company says . <p> Of course the cable car must be saved , and of course Wellington 's politicians will do it . A report on the cable car 's future acknowledges as much : " There is no appetite among stakeholders to see the cable car cease operations . " <p> That 's good , because the case is clear . The cable car is a historic , romantic piece of Wellington furniture , a much-visited tourist attraction , and a modestly useful piece of public transport to boot . <p> Even with relatively high fares ( $4 one way for an adult trip ) , it attracts decent patronage and covers most of its costs . <p> The question is who exactly should pay the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Should it be purely Wellington City Council , which owns the service and has previously stepped in with grants when upgrades have been required ? <p> Or should , as the company suggests , central government pitch in via the New Zealand Transport Agency 's public transport subsidies ? <p> The cable car really is a form of public transport , even if the company 's suggestion that closing it would meaningfully increase road congestion seems a stretch . Still , it does ferry some 230 passengers per day from Kelburn to the city and back during peak commuter periods -- certainly as many as some Wellington bus routes . <p> In a city that values public transport unusually highly , in an age of climate change , there 's every reason to encourage even the more gentle and eccentric forms of mass transit . <p> Moreover , the company makes the valid point that NZTA funds other , more unusual forms of public transport . Whanganui 's Durie Hill elevator , for instance , may be charming , but it is an elevator -- and still it receives subsidies @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ costs too , if it wants to retain the service and the symbol . It is Wellington city , after all , that gets the takings from the cruise boat visitors and other tourists , and Wellington 's heritage that is tied up in the cable car . <p> The investment is effectively a 40-year one , perhaps longer . It will seem a large cost to the council that approves it , but it is not so great when spread over the life of the vehicles . <p> There 's no need to muck around with a decision . The company usefully points out that it took a brake failure and a serious injury in the 1970s before the council coughed up last time the cable cars needed replacing . It should not dither half as long this time . <p> Even if public subsidies are not forthcoming , Wellington should be prepared to wear all the replacement costs . The cable car was installed in 1898 . It 's the city 's second most popular tourist attraction . It is n't just a link to Kelburn . It 's @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ .
