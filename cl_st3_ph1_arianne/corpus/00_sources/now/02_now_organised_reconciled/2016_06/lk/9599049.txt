<h> Maldivian Supreme Court upheld a prison sentence of former President Mohamed Nasheed <p> The Maldives supreme court has upheld a 13-year prison sentence passed last year on the country 's first democratically elected president , who is living in exile in Britain . <p> Mohamed Nasheed was sentenced in March 2015 after he was convicted on a terror-related charge that the United Nations said was politically motivated . <p> Facing intense international criticism , the government of the Maldives took the unusual step of appealing against the conviction based on Nasheed 's claim that he did not have adequate time to prepare for the trial . <p> But a judge ruled on Monday that Nasheed , now leader of the opposition , had been given sufficient time to present his defence . <p> High Court also ruled that sufficient time had been given by Criminal Court for Nasheed to hire lawyers in his defense over the terror charge , despite claims to the otherwise . <p> High Court addressed concerns over integrity of the judges that presided over the case at Criminal Court , ruling that there @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ hard evidence was provided to prove that the judges were unjust . <p> There was no immediate comment from Nasheed . He recently won political asylum in Britain , where he went for urgent medical treatment this year . <p> Nasheed , a climate change activist who was also imprisoned during the three-decade rule of Maumoon Abdul Gayoom , was elected president in 2008 . <p> He rose to international prominence when he hosted a cabinet meeting underwater to draw attention to the threat global warming posed to the atoll country 's existence . <p> He was toppled in February 2012 after a mutiny by police and troops , which followed weeks of protests over the arrest of a judge for alleged corruption as well as for politically motivated rulings . <p> Nasheed 's decision to order the arrest was the centrepiece of the terrorism case against him . <p> President Abdulla Yameen , a half-brother of Gayoom , has been criticism over the jailing of Nasheed , whom he beat in a presidential election in late 2013 .
