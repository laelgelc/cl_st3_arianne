<h> Cohen : The imaginative legacy of Barack Obama <p> President Barack Obama pauses while speaking at the Treasury Department in Washington , Tuesday , June 14 , 2016 , following a meeting with his National Security Council to get updates on the investigation into the attack in Orlando , Florida and review efforts to degrade and destroy ISIL.Susan Walsh / The Associated Press <p> From the day of his inauguration , the biggest challenge for Barack Obama was to create a record beyond his race . <p> He knew that every assessment of his presidency -- and every biography of his life -- would begin the same way : " Barack Hussein Obama , the first black president of the United States ... " <p> That he would be seen that way first would be inevitable . A generation after lily-white America ended its apartheid , Obama 's election was not only unprecedented , it was epochal . <p> But that alone was not enough of a legacy , any more than it was enough for John F. Kennedy to be the first Catholic president in 1960 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ speak , Obama would have to change other things . That 's what good presidents do . <p> As he alights in Canada and prepares to address Parliament Wednesday , we can say Obama has succeeded . He has a legacy , imperfect in places , but empathetic , imaginative and progressive . <p> He is no longer simply the first black president ; he is the first green president , gay president and Hispanic president . He has made it safe , after a generation in exile , to be a liberal in America . Indeed , his presidency marks a new liberal ascendancy . <p> Obama inherited a staggering economy when he took office on Jan. 20 , 2009 . Unemployment hit 10 per cent that year . The automotive industry was failing . <p> The recovery is uneven ; national family income has fallen and the national debt has ballooned . But the deficit is down , GNP is up and Americans are working . It is one reason , in the sun-lit evening of his stewardship , that he enjoys a 56-per-cent favourability rating . <p> Like @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Obama followed the disastrous George W. Bush . This helps Obama , as it helped his hero , Abraham Lincoln , who succeeded the hapless James Buchanan . <p> It gives a president space to run -- and Obama did . <p> The Affordable Care Act , his greatest domestic achievement , is a landmark in making the United States a more equitable society . On the environment , he raised the alarm on climate change , promoted clean energy , cancelled the Keystone XL pipeline and protected some 265 million acres of federal lands and water from development , more than any other president . <p> On gay rights , he endorsed gay marriage , repealed " do n't ask , do n't tell , " protected gay federal employees and named a record number of gays as judges and diplomats . On Hispanic rights , he championed ( stalled ) immigration reform and appointed Hispanics to high office ( the first Latina to the Supreme Court and six Latino cabinet secretaries ) . <p> In the world , Obama recognized Cuba , abandoning a half-century of isolation . He @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ He killed Osama bin Laden and in a rain of drone strikes , took out several terrorist leaders . <p> What is arresting about Obama -- and why we will miss him -- is his temperament as much as his intellect . It is his dignity , restraint , humility and equanimity . He has governed in a climate of viciousness , presumably facing death threats . His legitimacy has been questioned . Critics doubt his faith , denigrate his colour and deny his place of birth . <p> Throughout all this , he has maintained a sense of dignity . His administration -- and his personal behaviour -- have been beyond reproach ; there has not been a hint of scandal around him . <p> He has been an attentive father who chose to dine with his young daughters rather than with Republican Senate majority leader Mitch McConnell . Who could blame him ? He has been a mourner-in-chief , comforting too many victims of violence , even as he " apologized " to them for failing to contain guns . He has always looked presidential , conveying a singular @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ wonder if he enjoyed the job in the way FDR , JFK and Bill Clinton did . No matter . He showed up . <p> Ultimately history will offer a judgment on his place -- probably among the near great -- in the pantheon of presidents . For now , here 's a more immediate one : we are blessed to have lived in the Age of Obama . <p> Andrew Cohen is author of Two Days in June : John F. Kennedy and the 48 Hours That Made History .
