<p> Engineers Canada presented the first cohort of professional engineers with the new certification of Infrastructure Resilience Professional ( IRP ) at a launch event in Ottawa on Thursday . <p> The six engineers certified have each completed a series of professional development workshops that enhances their competency in planning , designing and managing resilient infrastructure in the face of a changing climate and extreme weather -- as well as practical application of their knowledge . <p> " I am proud to present these accomplished engineers with our first-ever Infrastructure Resilience Professional certifications , " said Engineers Canada CEO Kim Allen , MBA , FEC , FCAE , P.Eng . " Infrastructure owners and operators , governments and the public can have increased confidence that the recommendations and approvals made by these engineers are supported by advanced training and experience in climate vulnerability assessment , risk management , and climate adaptation . " <p> Legal responsibilities <p> Licensed engineers across Canada have a professional duty to serve the public and protect health , safety and the natural environment . " <p> Four training workshops <p> To be certified as an Infrastructure Resilience Professional , engineers must complete four training workshops on topics related to engineering and climate change , including climate law , climate science and asset management , and a workshop on the application of the Public Infrastructure Engineering Vulnerability Committee ( PIEVC ) Protocol .
