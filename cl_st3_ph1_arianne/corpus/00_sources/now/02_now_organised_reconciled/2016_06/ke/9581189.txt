<p> Climate change has affected the citrus sector in Tamaulipas . Production has been low in the last four crop cycles and it has fallen by more than 50 percent . <p> According to Javier Ibarra Echartea , president of the Emiliano Zapata Regional Union of Citrus Producers , which is part of the National Peasant Confederation ( CNC ) , 5 years ago they managed to produce more than 600,000 tons of oranges per year and currently they only achieve harvesting less than 400,000 tons per year . <p> He said that the effects of climate change could be seen in the citrus activity as the young trees , which previously would yield a lot of products , now had a low production . <p> Ibarra Echartea estimated that the next harvest would not surpass 400,000 tons , which he considered unfortunate for his fellow citrus producers . <p> In addition to this natural phenomenon , the sector faces the threat of the yellow dragon plague , which would completely end this activity if it 's presence were confirmed in the state . <p> Ibarra acknowledged the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that inspects all the plants and fruits that pass through the roads of Tamaulipas so as to detect the presence of the bacteria . <p> One of the steps that have been taken is the destruction of lemongrass , an ornamental plant widely used in homes , as it can host the bacterium.
