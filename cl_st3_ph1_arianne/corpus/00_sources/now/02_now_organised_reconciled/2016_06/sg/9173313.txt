<p> RIAU : A mammoth task is underway in Indonesia to repair degraded peatland forest in Sumatra , an area twice the size of Singapore . <p> Located in the Riau province , the 150,000 hectares of peatland on Kampar Peninsula peek out from oil palm and Acacia tree plantations that turn out palm oil and paper products . <p> The peatland stores several gigatons of carbon , which if released in the atmosphere could have a catastrophic impact on the climate . <p> Singapore and Malaysia -- just across the Straits of Malacca -- would potentially bear the brunt of the environmental catastrophe , which could be many times more than the haze that enveloped the region last year . <p> Said Mr Brad Sanders , deputy head of Conservation at the April Group - Indonesia 's second largest pulp and paper company : " This area of the Kampar Peninsula is very critical to maintaining the integrity of the hydrology for the rest of the peninsula . It includes the painstaking tasks of planting specific species of trees in the degraded forests . <p> Since 2015 , more than 5,000 trees have been planted -- covering over eight hectares . <p> " But it is still far from done , " said April Group 's Forest Ecology and Rehabilitation manager Muhammad Iqbal .
