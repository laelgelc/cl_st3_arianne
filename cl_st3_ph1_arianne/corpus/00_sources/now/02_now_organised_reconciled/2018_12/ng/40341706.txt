<p> The Nigerian Conservation Foundation ( NCF ) has tasked stakeholders in the sector to work together to recover the nation 's forest potential . <p> According to the foundation , in the last six years , Nigeria is estimated to have lost about 95per cent of its forest cover , remaining just five per cent in places like Cross River , Mangroves in the Niger/Delta and very few in creeks which are located all around the country . <p> NCF Director General , Dr. Muhtari Aminu-Kano made the appeal at its 2018 Green Ball/Award Night held in Lagos with the theme , " Green Recovery Nigeria-restoring mangroves and reclaiming the desert " . He said the Food and Agricultural Organisation ( FAO ) group , from 2005 till now , had observed that Nigeria has the highest rate of deforestation in the whole world , more than any other countries . <p> Aminu-Kano also said , the nation is also losing 351,000 hectares of land to desertification every year , " With that concern , we have to make sure that there is a initiative like the green Ball for Nigeria to be green again . He further observed that non-release of fund to address ecological problems and non-adherence to the five-year plan to return the lost forest are great impediments to efforts spearheaded by stakeholders . <p> At the ceremony , the widow of late General Murtala Muhammed , Mrs. Hafsat Ajoke Muhammed and wife of the Governor of Ogun State , Mrs. Olufusho Amosun received the environmental stewardship awards .
