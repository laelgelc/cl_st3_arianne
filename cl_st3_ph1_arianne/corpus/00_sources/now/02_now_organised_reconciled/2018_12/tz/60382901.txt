<h> Thursday December 27 2018 <p> Karl Lyimo <p> Advertisement <p> By Karl Lyimo <p> Not only are Kenya and Tanzania cheek by jowl in geographic location . <p> They also more or less have a common History , dating back to the end of the Great/First World War ( WW-I : 1914-18 ) when Germany lost the war to the ' Allies ' led by Britain . Germany had ' owned ' Tanganyika as ' Deutsch Ost-Afrika ' post-the 1884/85 Berlin Conference . <p> The newly-cobbled together League of Nations ( 1920-46 ) tossed Tanganyika into Britain 's lap as a mandated territory , from July 20 , 1922 until April 18 , 1946 . That was when the League 's successor , the United Nations Organisation ( UN ) , took over -- and re-tossed Tanganyika into Britain 's lap , this time as a Trustee Territory ... <p> From 1920 , Kenya had been a British Crown Colony ( the country 's interior lands ) and Protectorate ( the 10-mile coastal strip leased from the Zanzibar Sultanate ) . <p> So , in a sense , Kenya and Tanganyika were serving the same ' colonial master @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Tanzania was simply ' entrusted ' to Britain by the UN ! <p> Kenya and Tanganyika were members of the East African High Commission ( 1948-61 ) ; the EA Common Services Organisation ( 1961-67 ) ; the first EA Community ( 1967-77 ) ; EA Co-operation ( 1993-2000 ) , and the second EA Community ( 2000 -- to-date ) . <p> In addition to that , Tanganyika had as early as in 1927 joined the Customs Union between Kenya and Uganda that was formed in 1917 . <p> Judging by all the foregoing , one is sorely tempted to commend the two nations for being together in times of need , sharing common interests and challenges . <p> But , good things never seem to last long -- let alone last forever ! <p> Let 's look at one example which is developing along trajectories that are as alike as cheese is to chalk : electricity generation . <p> But , where Kenya and Tanzania differ here is in power generation methodologies . While Kenya has plumped for thermal power , Tanzania is sticking to hydropower ! <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ power plants -- but each in their chosen field . <p> Generally acknowledged as Africa 's largest geothermal energy producer , Kenya went on to increase its geothermal capacity . President Uhuru Kenyatta presided over the ground-breaking ceremony for the construction of its 83MW Olkaria-1 unit-6 geothermal power plant , to become operational in 2021 . <p> Construction of the 165MW Olkaria-V commences in July 2019 ... Etc. , etc . <p> Kenya ( 676MG in January 2018 ) is ninth out of the world 's ten top thermal power generating nations , led by the US ( 3,591MW ) -- and beating Japan , at 542MW ! <p> Perhaps not to be outdone , Tanzania President John Magufuli also witnessed the inking of agreements for the construction of East Africa 's largest hydroelectric project , located at the controversial River Rufiji 's Stiegler 's Gorge in the UNESCO World Heritage Site Selous Game Reserve . <p> Apart from myriad vehement objections to the project by environmental activists and some development partners , the Magufuli government is implementing the project come Hell or High Water ! <p> Talking of ' @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Climate Change developments and possible prolonged droughts that could dry up River Rufiji ... <p> Besides , Tanzania boasts 57+ trillion cubic feet of natural gas , including rare helium .
