<h> ' Sustainable intensification remains the most desirable way forward ' <p> While the alleviation and reversal of climate change is critically important -- and all sectors must play their part -- a strategy of ' sustainable intensification ' remains the most desirable way forward , a co-op representative has pointed out . <p> President of the Irish Co-operative Organisation Society ( ICOS ) , Michael Spellman , took this stance while addressing the Oireachtas Joint Committee on Climate Action today , Wednesday , December 12 . <p> Food production systems throughout the world will have to provide 70% more food to feed a global population , which is expected to exceed 11 billion people by the end of this century . <p> " We must produce more food , while conserving available land , water and energy resources . <p> Advertisement <p> " However , Ireland offers key advantages in this regard where , for example , the majority of output by Irish agriculture comes from milk and beef produced in a grassland environment . " <p> Spellman outlined that the possibilities for carbon mitigation are generally limited in agriculture compared to other economic sectors . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the economic contribution which agriculture makes to the Irish economy where there is simply no other industry generating wealth , investment and jobs to such a significant scale in rural Ireland . " <p> The ICOS president quenched the suggestions of levying carbon taxes on Irish agriculture , as made earlier this year by the Citizens Assembly , saying " it is most certainly not a solution and should not form part of Government consideration " . <p> Any such proposal , if implemented , would directly damage the competitiveness of Irish agri-food exports and it would hurt rural Ireland , while benefiting little to the environment . <p> Concluding , Spellman stressed : " In reality , a carbon tax on agriculture would be counterproductive , resulting in food production in carbon efficient locations -- such as Ireland -- being negatively affected , while highly inefficient regions would be free to increase output unhindered . "
