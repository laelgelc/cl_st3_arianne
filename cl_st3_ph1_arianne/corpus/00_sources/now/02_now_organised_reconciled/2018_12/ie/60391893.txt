<p> The suckler sector faces many challenges . The central issue has always been price and profitability . <p> In recent years , the focus on climate change and the expansion of the dairy herd have combined to accentuate the sense of a sector under siege . <p> Meanwhile , the ongoing structural realities of an ageing farmer base and the fact that many suckler farms are small scale , often part-time and typically are located on poorer land with high rainfall means that solutions are hard to find . <p> ICSA believes that the obsession with how many suckler cows ' Ireland Inc ' has , is the wrong starting point . It might matter to Government ministers or meat factory bosses to measure success by the total tonnage of suckler beef available for export . <p> However , it does n't matter to the individual suckler farmer how many cows there are in Ireland ; what matters is how much profit that farmer can make from suckling . And , as is painfully obvious this year , scarcity is better than over supply for the individual farmer . <p> For that reason , trying to maximise the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ coupled payment is not a smart policy for farmers . <p> We should be wary of policies to encourage farmers to keep much more suckler cows than they otherwise would . <p> The only hope for the suckler herd is to focus on viable markets .
