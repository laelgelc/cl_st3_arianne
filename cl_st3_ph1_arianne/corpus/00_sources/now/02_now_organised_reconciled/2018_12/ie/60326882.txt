<h> Erosion threat at 30% of coastal sites surveyed , Coastwatch reveals <p> A threat of erosion has been identified at 30 per cent of all coastal sites surveyed in 2017 by Coastwatch during its annual coastal survey . <p> Presenting the preliminary findings of the survey at Trinity College Dublin this morning , Coastwatch coordinator Karin Dubsky said that erosion is a " really serious thing in terms of climate change " . <p> While state-led policy on tackling erosion is coming down the line , Ms Dubsky said that there is no erosion management policy at present <p> As such , she said , every council , individual and business such as a golf club is in charge of coming up with their own mitigation plan . <p> This can lead to a " ricochet effect " where hard erosion control at one site impacts negatively on other coastal areas close-by due to a lack of joined-up thinking on tackling the problem . <p> The survey is undertaken by Coastwatch volunteers around the island of Ireland every autumn , with over 600 coastal site audits submitted . <p> There was a 20 per cent increase in the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Dubsky said . <p> Kelp on beach Photo : bluebudgieSeagrass <p> Many survey results pointed to special features such as coral sands , seaweed forests , mussel beds , and important seagrass habitats . <p> Seagrass is very valuable for Irish biodiversity , Ms Dubsky said , and are an important home to winkles -- a small , black , snail-like shellfish -- and various species of fish , and is also " filet speak " for brent geese . <p> She said that volunteers in Bantry Bay , Co Cork found an " absolutely habitat mosaic " of seagrass and a " sting of seagrass beds " in Lough Foyle , Co Donegal that measured several meters tall . <p> Aquaculture practices , however , were found to be encroaching on seagrass in the border areas , she said , with none of the sites currently subject to licensing requirements .
