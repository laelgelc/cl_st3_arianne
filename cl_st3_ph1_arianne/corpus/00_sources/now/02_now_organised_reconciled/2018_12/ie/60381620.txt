<p> Large swathes of coastal Ireland - including roadways and family homes - are at risk of crumbling into the sea as climate change threatens serious coastal erosion . <p> Violent winter storms and a warming climate mean that hundreds of properties are already in danger of being washed into the sea . Ireland has a high concentration of coastal communities - some 1.9 million people or 40pc of the population live within 5km of the coast . <p> Of these , 40,000 live less than 100 metres from the sea and there have been repeated warnings about the risk of erosion . But it has emerged that many local authorities have zoned land in at-risk areas . <p> And now a report , commissioned by the local authorities themselves , warns of the devastating impact that will be wrought on the landscape , transport and homes . <p> It says more than 800 properties and 300km of roads are potentially at risk of being washed away , and emphasises the threat faced by coastal communities in counties such as Galway , Louth and Wexford . <p> Office of Public Works ( OPW ) Minister Kevin ' Boxer ' @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of climate change , but the issue fell between two Government departments . <p> While the OPW was responsible for flooding , including on the coast , coastal erosion was a matter for the Department of Housing , Planning and Local Government .
