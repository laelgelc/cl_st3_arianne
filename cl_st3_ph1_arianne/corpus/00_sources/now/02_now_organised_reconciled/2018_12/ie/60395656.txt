Picture : Delil Souleiman/AFP/Getty Images <p> No Middle East conflict is as complex as the one raging in Syria . The fight involves a government that is antithetical to Western values and a Sunni extremist insurgency that at one point captured the borderlands between Syria and Iraq and fought all the way to the gates of Baghdad . <p> The stakes of the war are so high that a varied cast of foreign actors , including Russia , Turkey , Iran , and Hezbollah , have all been drawn in . <p> But there are actually numerous wars being fought in Syria . <p> One struggle , waged against the Islamic State ( ISIS ) , is well known to the public . <p> Less understood is the war to succeed the House of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ dynasty for almost 50 years . <p> A third conflict involves northern Syria 's Kurds , who joined with the United States to fight ISIS but whose efforts have stoked fear among Turkish leaders that the aspirations of Syria 's Kurdish population could embolden the Kurds in Turkey . <p> Now add to this many-sided conflict a US president who is uncomfortable with nuance or detail . Donald Trump neither possesses an internationalist mindset nor grasps the message that American power conveys . <p> But while Mr Trump could have been forgiven for arguing that America 's only interest in Syria was the defeat of ISIS , his recent decision to withdraw all US forces -- which he justified with an erroneous declaration of victory -- is inexcusable . <p> Mr Trump 's decision will embolden President Bashar al-Assad , whose rule has been catastrophic for Syria . <p> Mr Assad has consistently shown an inability to navigate the complexities of the crisis , including the rapid urbanisation , and subsequent radicalisation , of rural Sunnis displaced by climate change ; the incubation of radicalised Sunnis in neighbouring Iraq amid @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the growth of Kurdish nationalist sentiment in the region . <p> But Mr Assad did know whom to call to maintain his grip on power , and the infusion of Russian , Iranian , and regional Shia groups was enough to turn the tide in his favour . <p> That has given his regime a new lease of life in a region not accustomed to giving leaders second chances . <p> And yet , Mr Assad , like the French Bourbons , seems to have learned nothing and forgotten nothing during his 18 years in power , and is unlikely to consolidate his victory by introducing the federalised , decentralised structures needed to govern Syria effectively . <p> US policy in Syria , the policy that Mr Trump campaigned against and has now officially abandoned , has long rested on two pillars : stability in Iraq and the defeat of ISIS . <p> Under President Barack Obama , and then briefly under Mr Trump , US forces worked with local fighters to bring ISIS to heel .
