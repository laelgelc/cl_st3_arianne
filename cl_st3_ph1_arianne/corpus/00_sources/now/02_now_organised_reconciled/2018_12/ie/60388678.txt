I think the key with garlic is early sowing and next year 's crop is already popping out of the ground having been sown in late October . <p> Leeks were fantastic -- I have about 40 in the ground and we 've started dipping into them already . <p> Tomatoes and Fruiting Things <p> It was a great year for tomatoes for me , though I think I started a wee bit early in February and so the crop was finished almost entirely by mid-October . <p> Still , I would prefer them to start cropping early rather than last into November . In nearly a decade of growing , I 've only ever grown them successfully once and that one triumph keeps the insanity going year on year . <p> Salads and Greens <p> Richard finally convinced me to abandon the idea of sowing oriental leaves in module trays for later transplanting , in favour of sowing direct . I have to confess it worked great , sowing two or three x 1m rows of oriental greens every 3 weeks or so . I also had a nice crop of perpetual spinach , chard and kale in the tunnel . <p> Brassicas <p> We 've been enjoying brussels sprouts for the last few weeks -- so much so that I might have to buy some for Christmas . <p> I grew the delicious Cavolo Nero kale in the tunnel which has @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ broccoli plants I sowed in June should provide some great crops during the hungry gap months in spring next year . We 've been enjoying carrots and parsnips for about 6 weeks now and there is plenty yet to enjoy . <p> There 's definitely some carrot root fly damage on the carrots . My usual plan with beetroot is to have a year-round supply from three sowings in February , March and July but the final sowing in July did n't get going quick enough and is only good for leaves . <p> I include celery and celeriac in the roots rotation -- the former I only grew a small amount of , while the latter I grew over 30 .
