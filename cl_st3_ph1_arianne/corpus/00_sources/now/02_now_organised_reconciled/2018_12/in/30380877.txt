<h> A meeting with a reclusive genius <p> Earlier this week , I attended The Penguin Annual Lecture 2018 and came away with my brain scrambled . Scrambled , not fried . In its 12th edition , a more inspiring world personality would have been hard to identify for this prestigious event . Getting the reclusive Yuval Noah Harari , 42 , to Mumbai is a coup in itself . But persuading him to take questions from the audience is nothing short of a triumph . Aaaah ... the audience ! <p> When I made it to the lawns of the Taj Land 's End Hotel in Bandra , I was worried I would n't find a seat . Fans of the Israeli writer , thinker , and historian had started to grab places from 4.30 pm . Many were turned away , given the tight security arrangements in place . I looked over my shoulder and saw people seated right till the very last row close to the boundary wall . Most of them were college kids and young professionals . They had come clutching copies of his books , which have been translated into 45 languages and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ nimbly stepped onto the stage , adjusted the mike and began his 60-minute address , a hush descended on the crowd . Nobody wanted to miss a single word or thought . With a PhD in history from Oxford , Harari is one of the world 's most successful motivational speakers -- after listening to him for just the first five short minutes , one can understand why . <p> He speaks with utmost clarity , avoids bombastic words and takes his time to expand on an idea . Most importantly , he does not talk down to the audience . These are the hallmarks of an excellent orator . In India , we tend to get carried away by grand flourishes and a pompous manner . Our self-styled ' public intellectuals ' preen and strut on stage . They appear full of themselves as they puff up their chests , toss their carefully coiffed hair , and articulate their speech in an exaggerated fruity accent . We do n't , need that , boss ! Be direct . Keep it simple . And focus on the content . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ needs to identify as crisis points -- nuclear war , climate change , technological disruptions . He was candid enough to admit he did n't have the answers to ' everything ' , and what he did n't know ... well , he did n't know ! Most public speakers in India take their cues from political leaders ... and make the same mistakes as them . Rarely have I heard a speaker say s/he did n't know something . How easy the best-selling author of books like Sapiens and Homo Deus makes it all appear ! And yet , his work is staggeringly complex and has startled readers with its original thinking and radical perspectives . <p> His take on artificial intelligence and how it is going to transform our universe totally blew me away . It was at once fascinating and deeply unsettling . While discussing his address with some friends later , I was surprised to hear them describe his vision as ' dark and dystopian ' .
