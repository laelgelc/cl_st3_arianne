<h> Last Updated : Wednesday 05 December 2018 <p> Ministry of Environment , Forest and Climate Change ( MOEFCC ) said projects seeking to divert forest lands do not need to comply with forest rights act ( FRA ) for initial clearance . This sidelines concerns raised by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs ( MoTA ) and raises concerns that FRA provisions will be diluted . <p> " ... as per the provision under Forest ( Conservation ) Amendment rules , 2016 the compliance under FRA is not required for consideration of in-principle approval , " Deputy Inspector General of Forest Shrawan Kumar Verma wrote to the Maharashtra government . <p> The December 3 letter was regarding several coal mining projects of Western Coalfields Ltd ( WCL ) " being stalled at various levels " of the state 's forest department . WCL , a subsidiary of the Union Ministry of Coal , sought to divert forest land under for the projects . <p> Apart from sidelining concerns of the Ministry of Tribal Affairs ( MoTA ) , Verma 's letter has also made experts uncomfortable . It clarifies that compliance with the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ( FRA ) is not required for initial stage of forest conservation ( FC ) approval . <p> The FC process , according to the Forest ( Conservation ) Act ( FCA ) , 1980 , is carried out in two stages -- Stage I , the in-principle approval , and Stage II , the final approval . The rules amended in 2016 made FRA-compliance a prerequisite for diverting forest land . <p> But the stage in which it is to be done is it required has became a bone of contention . At a January 12 meeting between the two ministries , the MoTA raised the concern that a later-stage requirement for FRA would be a " fait accompli " .
