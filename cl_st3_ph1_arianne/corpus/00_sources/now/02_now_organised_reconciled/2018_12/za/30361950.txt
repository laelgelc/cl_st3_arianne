<h> Residents of Wakkerstroom call for municipal water pipeline project to be put on hold <p> One of South Africa 's largest reed marshes that supports a treasure trove of rare bird and animal species is under threat from a planned 30km water pipeline project that locals worry could imperil water security in the Wakkerstroom region . <p> The Dr Pixley Ka Isaka Seme local municipality in Mpumalanga plans to construct a R13m pipeline in a three-year project but Wakkerstroom residents want the project to be put on hold . <p> " The residents are extremely concerned about the proposed water pipeline from Martin 's Dam , above the Wakkerstroom wetland , to Vukuzakhe in Volksrust , Mpumalanga , which may threaten the water supply to residents in the area " , says the Wakkerstroom Interest Group . " This could have a major negative impact on the environment , tourism and job opportunities and ultimately impact on the sustainability of Wakkerstroom itself . " <p> The interest group includes BirdLife SA , the Wakkerstroom Tourism Association and people living in Wakkerstroom and eSizameleni , and the Wakkerstroom Natural Heritage Association , which manages the 700ha Wakkerstroom wetland . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ provincially-recognised ecosystem of top conservation priority and a recognised Important Bird Area . <p> The interest group believes the processes " leading to this stage of the project are fatally flawed " on many counts . " <p> The group says it had come to light that no EIA had been performed , nor was any geo-hydrological study undertaken .
