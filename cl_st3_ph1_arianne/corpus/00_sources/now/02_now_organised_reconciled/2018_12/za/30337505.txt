of its struggle for better conditions for workers . <p> The national minimum wage should also be seen as an achievement for business , for it demonstrates the commitment of employers to fairer wages and better working conditions . <p> It is a great achievement for a young democracy that is striving to overcome a legacy of poverty and severe inequality . <p> Today 's signing ceremony is the culmination of several years of intense deliberations among the social partners . <p> There were many areas of divergence , several disagreements , many setbacks . <p> Yet , even at the most difficult of moments , the parties were united by a shared desire for a more equal , more prosperous South Africa . <p> In this sense , the national minimum wage represents the triumph of cooperation over South Africa . <p> It is a decisive step towards the achievement of a living wage and a more equal distribution of income and wealth . <p> We have heard the voices of those who say the starting minimum wage level of R20 an hour is too low . <p> We agree . It is far below what we would want workers to earn . <p> But we must understand that in setting the starting level , the social partners sought to strike a balance -- between the need to measurably improve the income of the lowest paid workers and the need to sustain and increase levels of employment . <p> The social partners agreed on this starting level because the available evidence showed that it would not lead to widespread layoffs , but at the same time would increase the earnings of as many as six million working South Africans . <p> It is structured to make a real difference in the lives @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> We should expect that this additional income will contribute to greater consumption and higher demand , contributing in turn to greater economic growth and more jobs . <p> If implemented comprehensively , with appropriate use of exemptions and other safeguards , we see the national minimum wage as an instrument of economic stimulus . <p> The national minimum wage takes effect in the 25th year of our democracy -- a quarter century in which the rights of workers have been progressively enhanced and entrenched . <p> In giving effect to the Freedom Charter , we have put in place legislation to provide for trade union workplace organisation , collective bargaining , the principle of equal pay for work of equal value , improved health and safety , affirmative action , skills development , the right to strike , and the right to peaceful protest . <p> These achievements fulfil a global commitment to the achievement of fundamental rights and freedoms . <p> Earlier today , at Constitution Hill , we celebrated the 70th anniversary of the adoption of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights . <p> This Declaration , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , holds that : <p> " Everyone has the right to work , to free choice of employment , to just and favourable conditions of work and to protection against unemployment . " <p> And , importantly for us today : <p> " Everyone who works has the right to just and favourable remuneration ensuring for themselves and their family an existence worthy of human dignity ... " <p> As we gather here in Kliptown , let us reaffirm our shared commitment to ensure that all the people of this land may achieve an existence worthy of human dignity . <p> Let us do so mindful of the past that we are working to correct and the future that we are working to build . <p> For as we celebrate the great achievement of a national minimum wage , we need to acknowledge that the world could deepen the inequality in our society at the very moment we are introducing measures like the national minimum wage to reduce it . <p> It could deepen unemployment and leave those without appropriate skills languishing on the margins of the economy . <p> Yet , the 4th Industrial Revolution also presents great prospects for progress and inclusive prosperity . <p> It holds the promise of new and better jobs -- jobs that are more fulfilling , better paid , safer and healthier . <p> It presents countless opportunities to deploy technology to address social problems and solve developmental challenges . <p> It could reshape the relationship between work and life , between learning and earning . <p> If , as South Africa , we are to avoid the risks of the changing world of work , and instead seize the opportunities @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for these changes . <p> We need to approach this challenge as we have approached so many others -- through collaboration and partnership . <p> In the same way that we worked together to introduce a national minimum wage , we need to organise ourselves as social partners to prepare for the new world of work . <p> We should not allow technology to determine our future . <p> Instead , we should use technology to shape the inclusive , prosperous and free society that we seek . <p> This means that we must adopt policies and build institutions that can direct , regulate and mediate the uncertainties of a rapidly changing economy . <p> It means that we must invest in the capabilities of our people , providing them with the means to acquire skills , to reskill and to upskill . <p> We must give real effect to the notion of lifelong learning , because the skills you acquire in your 20s may be different from those that you will establishing a firm platform for a social pact on the future of work . <p> We must sustain the momentum of this moment , not only to address the very pressing economic challenges of the present , but
