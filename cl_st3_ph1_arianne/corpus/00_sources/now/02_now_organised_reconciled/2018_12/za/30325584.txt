<h> KZN vows to conserve migratory waterbirds <p> South Africa and KwaZulu-Natal are committed to the conservation of migratory waterbirds , Economic Development MEC Sihle Zikalala said . Picture : Facebook <p> Durban -- South Africa and its KwaZulu-Natal province are committed to the conservation of migratory waterbirds to preserve global biological diversity for future generations , the Economic Development MEC Sihle Zikalala told delegates at an international conference on Tuesday . <p> Zikalala was speaking at the seventh session of the meeting of parties ( MOP7 ) to the agreement on the conservation of African-Eurasian migratory waterbirds , being held at the Olive Convention Centre , in Durban . <p> The conference runs until 8 December and will see more than 300 delegates in attendance from member countries including Africa , Europe , Central Asia and the international conservation community . <p> The meeting aims to strengthen coordinated conservation and management efforts for the protection of migratory waterbirds throughout their routes . <p> " The Africa-Eurasian Migratory Waterbirds Agreement ( AEWA ) is an important intergovernmental Agreement . They serve as a key contributor to the provision and the sustainability of essential goods and services such as eco-tourism . <p> Migratory species could also act as crucial indicators of global climate change and could reveal unsustainable land use practices such as overgrazing of livestock or overfishing . <p> It was also well documented , said Zikalala , that during migration , the birds face a wide range of threats that impact their chances of surviving the journey . Some of the dangers include electrocution due to collisions with high-voltage power lines and pollution . <p> " Hazards also emanate from the destruction of habitats that are critical for breeding , wintering and stopover - mainly through deforestation and change in land use and cover .
