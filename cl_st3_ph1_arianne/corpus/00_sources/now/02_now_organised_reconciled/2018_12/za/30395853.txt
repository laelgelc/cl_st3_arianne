Picture : Bloomberg <p> Trade wars trump other risks <p> One of the key themes at the recent Schroders Investment Conference in Venice , Italy , was the huge impact that economic and disruptive forces are set to have on investors in the coming years . Both investment returns and economic growth are predicted to be lower , while political uncertainty remains high and technological advances are set to change the face of many industries . <p> At the conference , a 100 global fund selectors where asked which issues currently concern them most from an investment viewpoint . <p> According to Philip Haddon , Head of Investment Communications at Schroders , right now the most pressing concern for 35% of those delegates polled is the impact of the ongoing trade wars between the US and China . This is followed by the end of quantitative easing ( 24% ) , political risk ( 20% ) and the prospect of a sustained low growth environment . <p> Where next for central banks ? <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ what lies ahead for interest rates in the US. 55% predicted four more rate rises from the Federal Reserve ( Fed ) . ( The poll was taken prior to the rate hike this week , when the Fed raised interest rates by 25 basis points . ) <p> 28% of delegates predicted there would only be two more , however , while 17.5% of those polled thought there would be six or more yet to come . <p> Sussing out sustainability <p> " Sustainable investing is an ever-more prominent theme among investors . Rather than a niche pursuit , it is becoming the new normal " says Haddon . <p> At the conference , polling showed that 49% of investors in Venice already invest sustainably , with a further 26% considering doing so . However , a third of those polled do not currently invest sustainably and are not considering it yet . <p> It was clear that having a positive impact is not enough ; performance is key for professional investors tasked with getting the best possible returns for their clients . <p> The main reason ( cited @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ their investments in sustainable products is the concern that they may not deliver as good a return as non-sustainable products . <p> Haddon says that greater clarity from fund managers on their approach is needed , as 32% of delegates said they are not investing more because of the lack of information on how sustainable fund managers are engaging with the companies they invest in . <p> Meanwhile , 23% said they were not investing more in sustainable products because of the limited number available . <p> Digging a little deeper into company engagement , the audience was asked which areas they think are most important . Climate change came out on top ( 30% ) , followed by " pollution from operations " ( 23% ) , treatment of workers ( 18% ) and ending bribery &amp; corruption ( 18% ) . <p> Leaning towards liquid <p> " It 's not just sustainable investing that is moving up investors ' agendas , " says Haddon .
