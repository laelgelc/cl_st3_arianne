<h> Making the world hotter : India 's expected air-conditioning explosion <p> With India 's air-conditioner market expected to explode from 30 million to a billion units by 2050 , the world 's second-most populous country could become the planet 's top user of electricity for cooling.PHOTO : EPA-EFE <p> BEHROR , India ( AFP ) - Ratan Kumar once battled India 's brutal summers with damp bedsheets and midnight baths . Now he is among millions upon millions of Indians using air-conditioning - helping make the world hotter still . <p> With India 's air-conditioning market expected to explode from 30 million to a billion units by 2050 , the world 's second-most populous country could become the planet 's top user of electricity for cooling . <p> India is already the No. 3 spewer of greenhouse gases , burning through 800 million tonnes of coal every year - and the predicted air-conditioning boom could mean the country would have to triple its electricity production to meet demand , experts say . <p> But for the hundreds of millions of Indians enduring scorching , even deadly , summers , the air-conditioners are a godsend . <p> " Summers @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 48-year-old laundryman earning US$225 ( S$337 ) a month who this year installed an air-conditioning unit in his two-room house in the town of Behror in the baking-hot desert state of Rajasthan . <p> " Sleeping for few hours is a struggle after a day 's hard work , " the father of two told Agence France-Presse ( AFP ) , running a hot iron over crumpled clothes . " I am not rich but we all aspire to a comfortable life . " <p> Vast swathes of India endure a gruelling four-month-long summer , and the mercury has been inching ever higher in recent years . <p> In 2016 , the Earth 's hottest on record , temperatures in the Indian town of Phalodi soared to 51 deg C , the highest recorded in India . <p> The brutal heat can melt tarmac on the roads and puts millions of people at risk , with nearly 2,500 victims perishing from sunstroke in 2015 . <h> EVERYONE DESERVES AN AIR-CONDITIONER <p> Currently , just 5 per cent of Indian households are equipped with air-conditioning , compared with 90 per cent @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , up from virtually zero 30 years ago . <p> But India 's air-conditioning market is catching up fast , seeing double-digit growth in the past decade as incomes rise and electricity supplies become more reliable . <p> " It 's no longer a luxury product but a necessity , " said Mr Kanwal Jeet Jawa , India head of Japanese manufacturer Daikin , whose factory in Rajasthan churns out 1.2 million air-conditioner units a year . <p> " Air-conditioners increase productivity and life expectancy . Everyone deserves an air-conditioner , " he told AFP . <p> The irony is that as humans try to stay cool , the refrigerants inside air-conditioning units and the generation of electricity needed to power the appliances are exacerbating global warming . <p> In addition , studies - including by the World Health Organisation and UN-Habitat - show that the heat-generating motors inside air-conditioning units can themselves push up temperatures in urban areas , where the appliances are widely used , by a degree or more . <p> As demand grows , the amount of energy consumed globally by air-conditioning units could triple by @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ current capacity of the US , the EU and Japan , the International Energy Agency says . <p> India currently generates about two-thirds of its electricity with coal and gas , and despite ambitious plans for renewable energy , the country is set to remain highly dependent on hydrocarbons for decades to come . <p> One possible source of hope is if Indians buy more energy-efficient air-conditioning units , and manufacturers like Daikin are promoting these over older technologies . <p> But they are pricier , and Indian consumers that have air-conditioners are slow to upgrade , partly due to an ingrained culture of repairing devices rather than buying new ones . <p> In June , the Indian government issued an " advisory " to air-conditioner makers to keep the default setting at 24 deg C to save billions of units of electricity and reduce emissions . <p> But for now , the measure is not mandatory , and with many people expressing outrage , it is unclear how effective it will be . <h> BIGGER WORRIES <p> As a major international climate change meeting , called COP24 , gets @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ owner Ram Vikas Yadav says his customers have bigger worries than global warming . <p> " People want air-conditioners to keep their homes cool , " Mr Yadav told AFP , adding that his sales have rocketed 150 per cent every year .
