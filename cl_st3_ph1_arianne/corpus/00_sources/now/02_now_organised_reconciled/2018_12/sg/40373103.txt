<p> KATOWICE , Poland - The fashion industry has long been associated with large amounts of waste and high emissions , but it seems that going green is now in vogue for some in this sector . <p> Forty-three companies - including brands such as adidas and Burberry , retailers such as Target , and supplier organisations - on Monday ( Dec 10 ) pledged to find ways to reduce emissions in their value chains as part of the Fashion Industry Charter for Climate Action that was launched during the ongoing United Nations ( UN ) climate change talks in Katowice , Poland . <p> The fashion industry encompasses textiles , clothing , leather and footwear industries , from the production of raw materials and manufacturing of garments and accessories to their distribution and consumption . The industry has long supply chains and energy-intensive production . <p> The ever-changing cycles of fashion , disposable nature of clothes and accessories , and resource-intensive methods of production , have made textile production one of the most polluting industries , producing 1.2 billion tonnes of CO2-equivalent emissions every year - more emissions than international flights and maritime shipping , according to a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ charter , an initiative of UN Climate Change , aims to change that by setting a target for the fashion industry to achieve net zero emissions by 2050 . <p> The charter outlines certain ways of doing so , including decarbonising the production phase , selecting climate-friendly and sustainable materials , and using low-carbon transport options , among others . <p> While steps for implementation of these strategies have not been fully worked out , UN Climate Change said in a statement on Monday that the 43 signatories are not adopting a wait-and-see attitude but have already set an initial target to reduce their aggregate greenhouse gas emissions by 30 per cent by 2030 . <p> Concrete measures have also been identified , including the phasing out of coal-fired boilers or other sources of coal-fired heat , and power generation in their own companies and direct suppliers from 2025 . <p> Other signatories include familiar brands such as Esprit , Guess , Gap Inc , Hugo Boss and the H&amp;M Group , as well as other organisations involved in the supply chain , such as textile associations and logistics companies @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the signatories , saying : " The fashion industry is always two steps ahead when it comes to defining world culture , so I am pleased to see it now also leading the way in terms of climate action . <p> " The Charter , like the renowned fashion runways of the world , sets an example that I hope others will follow .
