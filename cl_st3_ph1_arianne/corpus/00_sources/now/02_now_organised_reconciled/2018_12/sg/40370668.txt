<h> You are here <h> Asset managers team up to pressure governments on climate change <p> Institutional investors increasingly concerned about climate change have teamed up to ask governments to set tougher policies . <p> PHOTO : REUTERS <p> LONDON Institutional investors increasingly concerned about climate change have teamed up to ask governments to set tougher policies . <p> A group of 415 investors overseeing US$32 trillion in assets has signed a letter asking governments to phase out thermal coal , set a price on carbon emissions and end fossil-fuel subsidies . The signatories , including Allianz SE , HSBC Global Asset Management and Schroders Plc , are presenting it in conjunction with the COP24 global-climate conference in Poland . <p> The fund managers said climate change could cause economic damage that threatens their holdings , and that government policy was key to reducing risk . Schroders estimated that if no action is taken and the world warms by 4 degrees Celsius ( 7.2 degrees Fahrenheit ) , that could cause US$23 trillion in global economic losses over the next 80 years . <p> " This is permanent economic damage three or four times the scale of the impacts @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ escalate , " the group said in a statement Monday . " Much more needs to be done by governments to accelerate the low-carbon transition . " <h> Market voices on : <p> While letters do n't usually lead to immediate change , the combined efforts of asset managers have already stirred the private sector . Last week , Royal Dutch Shell Plc said it would set new public goals around cutting carbon emissions after months of pressure from some of the same investors who signed the new letter . <p> The investor group also wrote a briefing paper for policy makers that says governments need to update and strengthen their commitments to the Paris climate accord . Among the recommendations are that nations put a price on carbon -- in effect a tax on greenhouse gas emissions -- between US$38 and US$100 a ton . Currently , three-quarters of emissions are covered by carbon prices of less than US$10 a ton , the paper said .
