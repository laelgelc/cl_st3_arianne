<h> NON-LIFE INSURANCE <h> Bangladesh : most underinsured nation <h> Global insurance specialist Lloyd 's finds <p> Bangladesh is the most underinsured country in the non-life insurance category , standing to lose as much as 0.8 percent of GDP from natural disasters a year , said Lloyd 's of London , one of the leading insurance markertplaces in the world . <p> The country is also one of the most exposed to risks such as climate change and the least able to fund recovery efforts , the company said in its latest global report on the insurance sector . The last edition of the report came out in 2012 . <p> In the report , Lloyd 's shed light on the underinsurance or insurance gap , which depicts the value of assets at risk not covered fully by insurance policies . <p> Among the 43 countries covered in the report , Bangladesh has the largest insurance gap , which stands at $5.5 billion , or 2.1 percent of the country 's gross domestic product . <p> Insurance premium per capita in Bangladesh is just $8 , a statistic that masks the fact that most people have no insurance at @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ affordability , which is a major barrier to adoption in emerging economies , some of the reasons people choose not to take out insurance include little understanding about the value and a lack of trust in insurance companies . <p> The insurance penetration in Bangladesh remained static in the last six years to 2012 although 13 new companies entered the market during the period , bringing the total to 77 . The number of non-life insurance companies is 45 . <p> " The insurance penetration remained low just because of the unholy practice of the insurers to hide a big portion of premium collected as they have to share a percentage of their premium with the government , " said Mohammodi Khanam , chief executive of Prime Insurance Company . <p> Some insurers give up to 70 percent commission on the premiums collected by the agents to boost the company 's income , which is unethical , she said . <p> Moreover , a lack of government initiative to bring all properties and businesses under the insurance coverage is also to blame for the low penetration , Khanam added . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ about insurance and zero product diversification have caused the insurance premium to remain low , said Gokul Chand Das , a member of Insurance Development and Regulatory Authority ( IDRA ) . <p> The country 's insurers collected Tk 11,150 crore as premium in 2017 , up 7.39 percent year-on-year , according to data from the IDRA . Of the total premium in 2017 , the contribution of non-life insurance companies was Tk 2,934 crore . <p> " The insurance sector is not keeping pace with the GDP , " Das added . <p> The report also highlighted the insurance penetration , which measures the contribution of insurance premium to the GDP of a country in percentage terms . <p> Bangladesh fell one notch down to 43rd in the global ranking for insurance penetration from 2012 , while the country 's premium as a percentage of GDP remained the same as in 2012 at 0.2 percent . <p> Lloyd 's , however , acknowledged Bangladesh 's efforts to improve its insurance penetration . <p> " In 2017 , in partnership with the World Bank , Bangladesh launched an initiative to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ local regulator and state-owned insurance companies to increase insurance coverage across the country , " it said . <p> Lloyd 's found that the major share of the global insurance gap is coming from the emerging countries . <p> Of the global gap identified , $160 billion comes from emerging nations and just $2.5 billion from developed countries . <p> The Netherlands remains the country with the highest insurance penetration at 7.7 percent .
