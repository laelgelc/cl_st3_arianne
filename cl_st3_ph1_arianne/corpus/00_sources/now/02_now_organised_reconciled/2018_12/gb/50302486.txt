<p> A statement of the Met Office website reveals why the likelihood of snow is now rare at Christmas time . <p> It said : ' For many of us , snow is synonymous with Christmas . <p> ' Bing Crosby famously dreamt of it , while movies , advent calendars and Christmas cards are all decorated with snow-filled scenes of a white Christmas . <p> ' However , for most parts of the United Kingdom , Christmas is only at the beginning of the period when it 's likely to snow . <p> ' We are more likely to see snow between January and March than in December with snow or sleet falling an average 3.9 days in December , compared to 5.3 days in January , 5.6 days in February and 4.2 days in March . <p> ' White Christmases were more frequent in the 18th and 19th centuries , even more so before the change of calendar in 1752 which effectively brought Christmas day back by 12 days . <p> ' Climate change has also brought higher average temperatures over land and sea and this generally reduced the chances of a white Christmas .
