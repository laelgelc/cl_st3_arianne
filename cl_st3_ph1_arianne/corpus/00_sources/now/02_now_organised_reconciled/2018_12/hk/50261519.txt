<p> Hong Kong has fallen 78 per cent short of the 2030 carbon emissions target set by C40 Cities for high emitting cities , a study by NGO CarbonCare InnoLab has suggested . <p> Hong Kong is set to reduce its carbon emissions by 3.3 to 3.8 tonnes per capita by 2030 , according to the Environment Bureau 's " Climate Change Action Plan 2030+ " released last year . But this falls two tonnes short of the target recommended by C40 Cities in accordance with the goals in the Paris Agreement . <p> File photo : Citizen News . <p> C40 Cities is an organisation of 96 major cities worldwide . It coordinates climate change activities based on the 2015 United Nations ' Paris Agreement to cut global greenhouse gas emissions between all nations , apart from Syria and Nicaragua . <p> CarbonCare InnoLab released its report Paris Watch -- Hong Kong Climate Action Report on Tuesday . It examines the city 's current environmental state and future targets against five other major Asian cities from the C40 Cities alliance . They include Singapore , Tokyo , Seoul , Shenzhen and Guangzhou -- chosen for their similar size @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ CarbonCare InnoLab . <p> The NGO said that Hong Kong is trailing behind its counterpart cities in renewable energy , energy efficiency , transitioning to electric mobility , and curbing greenhouse gas emissions . <p> " The Paris Agreement targets should be seen as an opportunity , not a burden , " Albert Lai , Senior Advisor to CarbonCare InnoLab , said . He added that " doing badly on climate change will certainly mean Hong Kong will do badly at business and in other social aspects : from not being ready for climate-related business disruption , to having a poor international reputation . " <p> The findings come as representatives from 195 countries gather in Katowice , Poland , for the start of the United Nations ' climate change conference , COP24 , on Sunday . Attending parties are set to discuss details of the Paris climate accord , which aims to keep the increase in global average temperature to below two degrees Celcius and to limit the increase to 1.5 degrees Celcius . <p> Air pollution in Hong Kong .
