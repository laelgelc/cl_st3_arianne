<p> It 's the annual award that often leaves linguists either in a dander or drooling . Of course we 're speaking of Word of the Year honours -- and of course , there 's rarely consensus among the world 's great linguists . <p> Merriam-Webster picked " justice , " which is not only innocuous and lacks oomph but which , more importantly , hardly reflects our lawless times . <p> Dictionary.com selected " misinformation " as its word of 2018 . Nothing wrong with that choice in a year where fake news reached unprecedented , epic proportions . <p> In fact , the big quarrel surrounding that selection is whether " disinformation " would have been more apt . But Jane Solomon , the Dictionary.com linguist-in-residence , felt that " mis " more than " dis " sent a stronger message " about being vigilant in the battle against fake news , flat-earthers and anti-vaxxers , among other conduits . " <p> Good point . <p> The Collins Dictionary gang of wordsmiths emerged with " single-use " as their word of the year , and it 's hard to argue with that selection as it relates to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ unchecked proliferation " in terms of " damaging the environment and affecting the food chain . " <p> Few , other than producers of plastic or the current president of the United States and like-minded authoritarian rulers , could possibly take issue with that logic . <p> Hell , the Collins gang could simply substitute a photo of the plastic-bottle-covered ocean floor in lieu of a definition of " single-use " in their dictionaries . <p> Not to dismiss " misinformation " and " single-use " as worthy choices , but there is no doubt that the Oxford Dictionary people have really nailed it with their choice of " toxic " as the 2018 word of the year . <p> Interestingly enough , toxic is a critical component in both misinformation and single-use . But toxic -- which is essentially defined as poisonous -- relates to a plethora of subjects that have been popping up in conversation over the last year . <p> Toxic has also been popping up in English dictionaries for more than 300 years , and prior to that , a Latin derivation of the word , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Roman times , generally in reference to taking out an opponent with a lethal poison , often affixed to the point of one 's arrow . <p> While the lethal usage is still applicable today -- particularly in the recent case of a former Russian intelligence operative and his daughter being poisoned with a nerve agent in England -- toxic has also become so effective and widespread in describing a slew of situations . <p> In explaining why toxic was chosen , the Oxford people noted that its parameters were " a word or expression that is judged to reflect the ethos , mood , or preoccupations of the passing year , and have lasting potential as a term of cultural significance . " <p> No doubt about toxic making the grade there . No surprise , either , that Oxford data showed that , as well as a 45-per-cent rise in the number of times toxic had been looked up on oxforddictionaries.com in the last year , was the array of different contexts in which the word was searched . <p> And speaking of U.S. President Donald Trump -- @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a resident of the Oval Office to spew such toxic rhetoric , be it in relation to migrants or Muslims or simply those who oppose his narcissistic point of view ? According to Oxford , the Top 10 words most frequently used alongside toxic are : " chemical , masculinity , substance , gas , environment , relationship , culture , waste , algae and air . " <p> Curiously , rhetoric , in tandem with toxic , did n't make the Oxford list . But almost all of the above words in combination with toxic are fairly self-evident . <p> Toxic relationship and culture have more contemporary meaning .
