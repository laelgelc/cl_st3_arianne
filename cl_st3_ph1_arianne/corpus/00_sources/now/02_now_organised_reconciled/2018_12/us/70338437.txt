<h> YES : Rollback of carbon taxes bad , but progressives determined to impose them <p> By Merrill MatthewsTribune News Service <h> Sunday <p> Dec 16 , 2018 at 2:01 AM <p> The recent French demonstrations against President Emmanuel Macron 's gasoline tax increase may have been the first such uprising , but it probably wo n't be the last -- in France or elsewhere . <p> Hundreds of thousands of French working-class demonstrators took to the streets of Paris and other parts of the country to protest Macron 's 25-cent per gallon gas tax increase , with more increases to follow . The revenue would supposedly be used to fight climate change . <p> It 's not like gasoline in France is cheap . The average price of gas is about $7 a gallon , according to the Associated Press , which adjusted for the European use of liters . That 's $140 to fill up a 20-gallon tank . Ouch ! <p> And that 's in a country where the average income is about two-thirds that of America 's . <p> Macron did n't care because he , like many progressives , wants to be seen as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of how much that legacy costs the working class . <p> But it turns out his French constituents do care -- a lot . <p> Macron was stunned by the size and determination of the spontaneous revolt . After insisting he would n't cave on the gas tax , he did , and he is now promising even more concessions . <p> France may be the most disruptive , but it is n't the first populist pushback . <p> Australia became the first country to repeal its tax on carbon emissions . That 's where the government imposes a tax on each ton of carbon released into the atmosphere . <p> Even though it was considered model legislation , the Aussies did n't want it and the Senate repealed it in 2014 -- after only two years . Prime Minister Tony Abbott called the tax " a useless destructive tax which damaged jobs , which hurt families ' cost of living and which did n't actually help the environment .
