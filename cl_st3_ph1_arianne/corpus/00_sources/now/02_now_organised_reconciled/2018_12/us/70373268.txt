<p> A group of middle and high school students brainstormed and patented a method to rid ponds and streams of algal bloom , an emerging threat to local fish and plant life in New Jersey . <p> The Bergen County teens , dubbed Team HIPE , used their skills learned through project-based competitions to learn how nutrient pollution impacts their community , then they came up with a solution . <p> Alex Howe and Ethan Donovan of Glen Rock , Matt Harris of Hillsdale , Michael Yin of Ridgewood , and Jonathan Chin of Norwood , friends who previously worked together on other projects , patented a method to turn nitrogen found in water into nitrogen gas . The gas , the team said , is then safely released into the air and does not travel to water sources where it causes the algae to bloom . <p> " You 've heard of Red Tide and such on the news . That 's all caused by excess nitrogen in the water , " said Harris . " Algal blooms do n't really require a lot of nitrogen to flourish .
