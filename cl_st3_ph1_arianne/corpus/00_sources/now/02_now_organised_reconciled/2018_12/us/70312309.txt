<h> How will oil producers meet the challenge of climate change ? <p> As an organization of the fossil-fuel industry , OPEC , meets in Vienna , the United Nations ' COP24 climate talks are taking place nearby in Katowice , Poland . The coal-rich province of Silesia , where sits Katowice , was wrested from Austria by Frederick the Great of Prussia . Now , major oil producers must decide whether to fight the new energy transition or try to annex their share . <p> All the leading oil and gas players -- companies and countries -- confront two interlinked challenges . The first , represented by the series of talks that include this month 's 24th Conference of the Parties to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change , the Paris Accord of 2015 and other international climate negotiations , is the growing concern over climate change . <p> The latest report by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) laid out disturbingly the scale of the challenge required to keep the global temperature rise below 1.5 or 2 degrees Celsius , as the Paris Accord requires . New designs of nuclear power plants intended to be cheaper and safer than their forebears are also under way . <p> The leading petroleum producers include national oil companies ( NOCs ) -- such as Saudi Aramco , Qatar Petroleum and Rosneft -- and international corporations , such as Shell , ExxonMobil and Total . Some , such as Equinor ( formerly Statoil ) of Norway , are largely state-owned but behave mostly like the international firms . That would involve switching progressively from oil to gas , which emits 25% less carbon dioxide when burned , and is also cleaner and more efficient . Oil and gas can also be converted into petrochemicals , which will continue to be needed in growing quantities . They can deploy renewable energy at home , but exporting oil and gas remains the key motor of the national economy . <p> Yet for the sake of their host countries , the challenge for the national oil champions is to map out their share of the commanding heights of the new energy economy .
