<h> Heiting : Do n't write off the role of natural gas <p> By Kim Heiting <h> Monday <p> Dec 24 , 2018 at 12:01 AM <p> A Dec. 2 article in The Register-Guard stated Eugene city officials " may impose bold franchise fee increases " on our customers who use natural gas . We agree that bold thinking is needed to combat climate change , and we support the City 's goal of reducing emissions . <p> We want our valued customers and the greater Eugene public to know NW Natural is actively pursuing solutions to help address climate change , and that we have better options to achieving emission reduction goals than raising franchise fees . <p> As a nearly 160-year-old company , NW Natural is in business today because we 've been willing and able to evolve . We 're a leading employer in the region that fuels businesses and serves nearly half of all Eugene residents with an efficient and affordable energy option to heat homes , cook meals and warm water . <p> For our energy future , all of the region 's preeminent studies on deep decarbonization show that natural gas is important @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Energy system planning is complicated , and there are a lot of issues that need to be analyzed and thoughtfully considered in assessing how best to reduce emissions . <p> For example , while Eugene focuses on electrifying passenger vehicles , how much more power would be needed to heat homes and businesses in winter ? At what cost ? How much of that new electricity would be fossil-generated , and to what emissions benefit ? Can the electric system handle all that new energy use ? What if there is a widespread power issue -- is n't an additional energy source important for resiliency ? <p> We 're confident that NW Natural can help Eugene address its climate goals by using our system -- one of the nation 's tightest and most modern pipeline systems -- while also retaining affordability , reliability and customer choice . <p> Today NW Natural delivers more energy in Oregon than any other utility , yet natural gas sales to our residential and commercial customers account for about 5 percent of the state 's greenhouse gas emissions . For scale , in the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ one cold hour , our system delivered about double the amount of energy as the entire Oregon electric system during that same time frame . And we do all of that with a pretty modest carbon footprint . <p> But , we know we can do better . It 's why we were one of the first natural gas utilities in the country to introduce a voluntary carbon savings goal of 30 percent by 2035 . One of the pivotal ways that we 're focused on reducing the carbon intensity of our product is with renewable natural gas . We can capture methane from food or even human waste and convert those gasses to pipeline quality . <p> Just like a power line can deliver renewable energy , our pipeline system can deliver renewable energy too . We have more about our plans at LessWeCan.com.
