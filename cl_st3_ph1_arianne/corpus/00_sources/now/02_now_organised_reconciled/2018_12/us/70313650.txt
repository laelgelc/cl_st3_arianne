<p> Driven by growing energy use , the world 's carbon dioxide emissions from fossil fuels continue to increase after several years of stability , placing the goals of the Paris climate agreement in jeopardy , according to a new Stanford-led analysis . <p> Despite the Paris Agreement , the first global pact aimed at reducing emissions of planet-warming greenhouse gases , concentrations of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere from human activities will increase 2.7 percent in 2018 , up from 1.6 percent growth in 2017 , reports the Global Carbon Project , an international team chaired by Stanford scientist Rob Jackson . <p> " We need to provide energy for everyone while reducing fossil fuel emissions , " he said . " History will judge us harshly if we fail . " <p> It is a disheartening setback , after little to no growth in emissions between 2014 and 2016 . As the second year in a row of growth , it suggests a worrying trend .
