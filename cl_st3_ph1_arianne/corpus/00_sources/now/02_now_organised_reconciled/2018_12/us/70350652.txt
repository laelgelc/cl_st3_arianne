<p> TORONTO -- Standing in a spacious gallery at the Art Gallery of Ontario ( AGO ) in Toronto , I held back tears as I watched piles of confiscated elephant tusks go up in flames . The moment had been captured by filmmakers Jennifer Baichwal and Nicholas de Pencier . The poignant short film is as part of the AGO 's Anthropocene , a deeply moving and thought-provoking exhibition about humanity 's impact on the Earth and its inhabitants . The exhibition includes large murals accompanied by short documentary films , three augmented reality installations , and dozens of photographs by Canadian artist Edward Burtynsky of landscapes forever altered by human activity . <p> The art evokes many emotions : sorrow , anger , grief . Yet a quote attributed to all three artists printed in large lettering on a gallery wall insists that their work was never intended to place blame but , rather , to generate awareness : " Our ambition is for the work to be revelatory , not accusatory , as we examine human influence on the Earth both on a planetary scale and in geological time .
