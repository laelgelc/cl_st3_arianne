Picture : City of Rockingham . <p> A DELEGATION from the City of Rockingham is in Japan this week for a series of meetings that will strengthen the long standing sister-city relationship between the cities of Ako and Rockingham . <p> The cities of Rockingham and Ako have been engaged in a mutually beneficial sister city relationship since April 1997 . <p> For more than 20 years , representatives from both cities have visited each other . <p> A small delegation from Rockingham travelling to Ako in Hyogo Prefecture earlier this week . <p> During this week 's visit , the delegation from Rockingham met with Ako City officials to explore possibilities for wide ranging improvements for both communities . <p> Topics of discussion included employment , economic development , tourism , aged care , disability services , rubbish disposal and recycling , as well as responding to climate change and the future of the Ako -- @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Governor Toshizo Ido . During this meeting Mayor Barry Sammels presented Governor Ido with a letter from Premier Mark McGowan . <p> In his letter to Governor Ido , Premier McGowan said he hoped the visit would provide positive improvements and outcomes for both communities . He also indicated his desire to see the relationship between Ako and Rockingham continue to thrive , along with other sister-city relationships involving cities in Hyogo Prefecture . <p> Cr Sammels said the visit to Ako was playing a vital role in strengthening the sister-city relationship . <p> " The meetings we have had with Governor Ido and many Ako City officials have given the delegation useful insights , ideas and knowledge that we can apply at home , " he said . <p> " While both cities have vastly different histories , we both share the need to develop in areas like tourism , employment and economic development . <p> " Issues surrounding climate change , aged care and disability services are all universal , so by working together we can we can make lasting improvements to both of our communities .
