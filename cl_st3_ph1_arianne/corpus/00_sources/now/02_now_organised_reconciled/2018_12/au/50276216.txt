<h> Labor aims battery lure at key WA seats <h> Topics <p> Labor will use its household battery push to target key WA seats before next year 's Federal election , with figures showing voters in must-win electorates are well placed to benefit from the policy . <p> Opposition Leader Bill Shorten has promised 100,000 households with incomes under $180,000 will get rebates of $2000 to install battery storage systems , in a bid to slash power bills , cut greenhouse gas emissions and take pressure off the electricity grid . <p> The subsidies would go to households with rooftop solar panels , of which there are 276,000 in WA . <p> Analysis by the Federal Parliamentary Library shows thousands of those homes are in at-risk Government-held seats such as Hasluck , held by Aged Care Minister Ken Wyatt by a margin of 2.1 per cent , and Pearce , represented by Attorney-General Christian Porter , with a 3.6 per cent margin . <p> Burt , which is centred on Armadale and held by Labor 's Matt Keogh , had the highest proportion of dwellings with rooftop solar at 42 per cent . <p> Solar panels sit atop more @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Hastie 's electorate of Canning , which is also being targeted by the ALP , compared with about 38 per cent in Hasluck and Pearce . <p> The State 's most marginal seat of Cowan , held by Labor 's Anne Aly , came in fifth with a solar take-up rate of 36.8 per cent . <p> With Labor telling users of solar panels that a household battery could cut their electricity bills by 60 to 90 per cent , the $2000 cash rebates could sway voters sick of being hit by power price hikes . <p> Shadow climate change and energy minister Mark Butler said the figures were evidence that coalition MPs were " deaf to their own electorates " . <p> " Australians are embracing renewables because they know it equals cheaper power bills , and it brings down pollution , " he said .
