<h> Get a newly discovered species of frog named after you for a cool $35,000 <p> Ever wanted to have your name -- or brand name -- etched in the annals of history ? Well , now you can ... if you have enough cash . <p> In early December , American non-profit Rainforest Trust held an auction to sell the rights to name 12 recently discovered plant and animal species in Colombia , Ecuador and Panama . <p> Rainforest Trust is a conservationist organisation that purchases threatened rainforests in order to save endangered wildlife . The company holds Charity Navigator 's highest rating for transparency and accountability and claims to have already helped protect over 19 million acres of forest around the world . <p> The auction , which started at A$13,000 for each species , allowed the winners to slap any name they want on the new flora and fauna . All proceeds from the auction will go to buying the land where the new species live in an attempt to save them from extinction . <p> The auction 's least favoured animal was a red ant from Ecuador that is reminiscent of Predator . They see it as an example of cultural appropriation , and fear it will turn taxonomy and the search for new species into a problematic commercial arena . <p> The Chief Executive of Rainforest Trust , Paul Salaman , defends the initiative . He told The New York Times that an artificial name is n't a concern -- the problem is that they are endangered by climate change and industrial practices . <p> " The name itself does n't really matter , " said Salaman .
