<h> The truth about dark roofs and cool roofs <h> David Thorpe 19 December 2018 <p> It seems like a no-brainer : cooling of buildings and neighbourhoods can be helped by having light coloured roofs . But the question has caused no end of controversy . Let 's settle it once and for all . <h> What does the science say ? <p> The technical name for a roof that reflects heat is that it has a high albedo . <p> High-albedo roofing has two scientifically proven benefits : it helps to mitigate the urban heat island effect and reduce cooling energy demand and costs . It can also increase thermal comfort in non-conditioned buildings . <p> Benefits are both direct and indirect . <p> Direct benefits to individual buildings occur by reducing absorbed shortwave radiation through the roof . Neighbourhood-scale indirect benefits result from reduced ambient air temperatures , particularly when high-albedo surfaces are deployed on a large scale . <p> These conclusions come from several sources , including one , ( published in the journal Energy and Buildings ) which comprised whole-building energy simulations of a set of archetypical single family residential buildings in three locations with @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and two inland ) . This can be used to maximise the proportion of incident solar energy absorbed and control how much is emitted . <p> Absorption is a measure of how much incident radiation is received by a material . The emissivity of the surface of a material is a measure of its effectiveness in emitting energy . <p> Absorption and emittance of incident solar energy in a material . <p> The emitted energy is in the form of long wave or thermal radiation .
