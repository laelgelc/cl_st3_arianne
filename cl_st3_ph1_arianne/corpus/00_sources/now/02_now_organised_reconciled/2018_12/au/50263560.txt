The reasons are stronger for those -- such as the communities of the gilets jaunes -- who have been at the sharp end of other economic changes . <p> Mishandled deindustrialisation undermined entire ways of life in many western countries -- as , indeed , did the decline of mining . The changes now required to address climate change threaten to do the same all over again to the same communities , while satisfying the concerns of the urban middle classes . Climate change may be a once-in-a-millennium civilisational threat -- but the political economy of addressing it is a problem from hell . <h> Fee and dividend <p> There are solutions . The most promising is the carbon " fee and dividend " approach advocated by climate scientist James Hansen . This would levy duties on fossil fuels and redistribute the revenue in equal per capita amounts to all residents . <p> This would create a permanent direct cash stream into everyone 's pocket . Anyone who uses less than average amounts of carbon would receive more in payments than they lost in higher prices . <p> This protects the most vulnerable while rewarding anyone for reducing their carbon intensity . Importantly , it gives struggling communities the wherewithal to respond to those incentives @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ also creates , at a stroke , a pro-environment constituency of the half of the population who benefit from higher environmental taxes . <p> The same principle can be operated between states , returning revenues from emissions trading schemes to countries on an equal per-capita basis . On both levels , the amount could be tilted to favour those with historically high carbon-dependence to recognise the greater adjustment required from them . The 2015 Paris climate change agreement vaguely recognised such a principle in the climate finance that rich countries committed to provide for poor ones . <p> Mr Hansen 's scheme puts at the centre of environmental politics and policymaking the distributional consequences -- and thereby the tricky issue of respect for people already under pressure being asked to somehow give up their way of life .
