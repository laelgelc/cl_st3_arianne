<h> Totnes flood defence scheme gets ? 3.8 million upgrade <p> More than 400 properties better protected from river and tides by longer , higher walls plus other improvements . <p> Philip Rees of the South West Regional Flood and Coastal Committee opens one of the new gates in the Totnes flood defence scheme <p> Totnes ' improved flood defence scheme had its opening ribbon cut by Philip Rees , Chairman of the South West Regional Flood and Coastal Committee , and South Hams District councillor John Tucker on Friday 7 December 2018 . <p> The Environment Agency 's ? 3.8 million improvements were needed to reduce the significant risk of river and tidal flooding . The scheme was financed by Defra grant-in-aid through the South West Flood and Coastal Committee and ? 110,000 from South Hams District Council . <p> Totnes is a historic town and the Environment Agency has made sure the flood defences are sympathetic to the built and natural environment , whilst reducing flood risk to more than 400 homes and businesses . <p> George Arnison , Environment Agency Flood Risk Funding Manager , said : <p> The risk of flooding in Totnes has increased @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ land use in the area and partly due to the effects of climate change on rainfall patterns and sea levels . <p> The scheme that we opened in Totnes on Friday , which raises and extends the towns flood defences over a distance of nearly 1.5 kilometres , reduces that risk significantly . Over 400 houses and businesses are now significantly better protected from river and tidal flooding . <p> The flood defences stretch along the River Dart from Broadmarsh Industrial Estate downstream to the Steam Packet Inn . They comprise a mix of different measures to ensure the works were sensitive and sympathetic to both the built and natural environments . <p> About 500 metres of existing flood defence wall was raised plus 140 metres of new flood wall built at Broadmarsh Industrial Estate and Morrisons ' car park . <p> One of the new flood gates in Totnes <p> The Environment Agency built new flood walls between properties in Totnes town centre and Marsh Quay and added flood resilient balconies , windows and doors . There are also new flood gates , a slipway and 150m of raised @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Waterside Bistro to ensure customers can still enjoy views of the river . <p> At Steamer Quay , the Environment Agency replaced 70m of failing quay wall . This work includes specially designed habitat niches in the masonry cladding for estuary organisms to live in , including the nationally important species of tentacled lagoon worm . <p> Other environmental enhancements included planting over 140 trees and working with the Dartington Estate to restore 7 hectares of wetland upstream of Totnes at Queensmarsh . This has created a mosaic of habitats including grazing marsh , ponds , reed bed and wet woodland , benefiting wetland birds , otters , fish , amphibians and invertebrates . <p> Cllr John Tucker , Leader of South Hams District council , said : <p> It 's very satisfying to see the hard work of all the agencies involved come to fruition in these improved flood defence works . <p> I 'm pleased that we 're able to protect homes and businesses from the worst effects of flooding while still maintaining the character of Totnes , and preserve and protect wildlife habitats at the same time @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ changes in land use in the area and a changing climate . <p> Flooding has devastating costs for people and businesses .
