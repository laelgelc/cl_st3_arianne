<h> Dreamtime Dive and Snorkel takes you on Indigenous journey of Great Barrier Reef <h> Topics <p> Long before hordes of tourists filled the crevices of Australia 's gateway to the most extensive coral reef in the world there lived a hunter . <p> One day this hunter went fishing and while out he speared a black stingray . But this was no ordinary black stingray , it was a sacred black stingray . Infuriated by the hunter 's actions , the spirit of the ocean unleashed a storm causing massive waves and cyclones that threatened to wipe out the hunter 's village . Frightened , the hunter ran home to tell his people what happened . Determined to stop the impending carnage the tribe began throwing rocks into the ocean . Immediately the ocean began to calm down , so they threw more rocks into the sea forming a makeshift barrier . And so the Great Barrier Reef was born . <p> This story of how Australia 's UNESCO world heritage listed marine sanctuary was created is how the local Aboriginal tribes believe it was formed . It is called a creation story and is being told in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Dive and Snorkel . And it is one of a few Great Barrier Reef stories told on this pristine stretch of far north Queensland that is being shared as part of a new wave of Indigenous tourism . <p> Camera IconThe Great Barrier Reef from above . <p> Cultural heritage tourism in Australia has grown over recent decades with numerous experiences on offer from Pudakul Aboriginal Cultural Tours , where you learn about bush tucker and medicine in the Northern Territory , to the Oongkalkada Wilderness Camp in WA , an eco-bush retreat where guests stay with traditional owners . However , the operators behind this new tour , Experience Co , believe there is not enough and say very few international tourists actually engage in these unique cultural activities . <p> Chief executive Anthony Ritter explained that , after recognising a gap in the market , his team decided to enhance one of their already successful business ventures , diving and snorkelling off the Great Barrier Reef , by adding this truly Australian element . <p> " The fact is that 80 per cent of tourists do n't engage @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ because of the lack of choices available , " Mr Ritter said . " Experience Co is looking to address that shortcoming , beginning with Dreamtime Dive and Snorkel . " <p> Camera IconThe Dreamtime Dive and Snorkel vessel . <p> On the surface it might appear as a rebranded dive and snorkel experience , and in some ways it is . Tourists do n't engage in traditional customs such as spear fishing or free-diving out at the reef , they take part in the standard dive and snorkel experiences offered by most tourist operators . But it is the use of Indigenous story-telling that separates this particular tour from other Cairns-based tours . <p> Experience Co invested around $2.5 million in the venture and spent more than a year working with local elders to devise a tour that represented all four tribes and factored in their customs . Extensive meetings were held and eventually a plan was mapped out for how it would operate - and how it would benefit local tribes . <p> Tourism is the lifeblood of Cairns , contributing around $2.5 billion to the Tropical North @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and international people but employment opportunities for the local Aboriginal population is limited . One of the main reasons Experience Co was able to gain the support of the local Indigenous community was its commitment to forging a genuine employment pathway for local Aboriginal youth . <p> The Dreamtime Dive and Snorkel 's crew consists of Indigenous rangers who are all members of the Gimuy Walubara Yidinji , Gunggandji , Mandingalbay Yidinji and Yirrganydji tribes -- the four tribes found along the stretch of coastline from Port Douglas to the Frankland Islands - as well as Torres Strait Islanders . They spend several weeks learning the ropes on the various boats owned by Experience Co ( the adventure tourism company runs Reef Magic Cruises , Big Cat Green Island Reef Cruises as well as GBR Helicopters which all operate out of Cairns ) and are eventually placed on the Dreamtime Dive and Snorkel vessel . <p> While they are part of the crew , their role is mostly to share their connection to Sea Country with those on board . All three come from different parts of Australia , have had different experiences growing up but share one common hope - that this tour will help change the dialogue of Indigenous culture . <p> Camera IconBlake Cedar is one of the many indigenous rangers you will find on board Dreamtime Dive and Snorkel . <p> " Hearing feedback from people already ( about Cairns being connected to traditional culture ) makes me think we must be doing something right , " Mr Cedar explained .
