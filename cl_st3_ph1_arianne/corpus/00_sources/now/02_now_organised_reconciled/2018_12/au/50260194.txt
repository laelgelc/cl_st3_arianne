<h> This crisis could end the Liberal Party <p> There 's much happening in conservative Australian politics that 's very familiar . <p> So familiar , in fact , that party stalwarts are saying - perhaps praying - that poor opinion polls and bickering personalities are neither new nor anything to worry about . <p> After all , the very first united Liberal party , welded from competing interests along a very rough seam in 1909 , forced conservatives and progressives to live together in their quest to quash Labor . <p> That worked for eight years before falling apart . But the new Nationalists fared no better and they collapsed 14 years later . And nor did its replacement , the United Australia Party , that fell into a gooey heap just 12 years after that . <p> Yes , the Liberals have been down this road before , and not only survived but returned to conquer . It 's simply a matter of coming out the other side of a crisis in one piece . <p> The modern Liberals ' first crisis came in the late ' 60s after founder Robert Menzies retired , and a quick @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , Billy McMahon , Bill Snedden - could n't best Labor 's Gough Whitlam or capture the public 's mood for a groovy new Australia . The crisis proved short-lived : the Whitlam government imploded and a skilled Malcolm Fraser gave his party a stronger identity . <p> The second crisis was much greater . Losing in 1983 to Labor 's Bob Hawke - arguably the most popular grassroots politician Australia 's ever seen - was just a symptom of the Liberals ' problem . The party 's real disease was losing the coveted mantles - held since the ' 50s - of being Australia 's natural party of government and its careful economic manager . <p> The ongoing division among the Liberal Party spells disaster for Scott Morrison at the next general election . The Liberals ' third crisis - beginning pretty much after the Abbott government 's 2014 horror Budget - augurs especially badly for Menzies ' grand old party . In fact , so ominous are the storm clouds looming over a divided party room today that no major non-Labor Party has been in this much internal strife since the UAP 's demise in 1943 . <p> This time the Liberals are n't just bitterly divided over personalities - although the hate and animosity between the Abbott and Turnbull camps far surpasses any rivalry between Howard and Peacock . Are they a party that could easily sit with the Labor moderates in their support for minority rights , clean energy and social welfare ? Or are they a conservative outfit geared to lower corporate tax , fossil fuels , the churches and self-reliance ? <p> The Liberals have always been two parties in one , just as Labor has been , at times , a loveless marriage of liberalism and democratic socialism . <p> Now , though , unlike the 1980s and early 1990s , the Liberals are squeezed on the hard right by the likes of One Nation , Katter 's Australian Party , the Australian Conservatives and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . But last week the moderate Julia Banks moved to the crossbench , and rumours circulated - since denied - of the conservative Craig Kelly 's similar departure . <p> Sadly , the odds are we 'll see more defections from the federal Liberals before next May 's election .
