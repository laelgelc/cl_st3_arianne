<p> When experts , such as engineers and geoscientists , submit reports on a project to B.C. 's Environmental Assessment Office , the generally accepted idea is that their information will reflect environmental standards and identify problems , allowing a project design to be changed or rejected if necessary . <p> But , that is not what happens in B.C. according to a study by University of British Columbia researchers that looked at 10 recent environmental impact assessments . <p> Researchers found that experts -- usually hired by a company applying to build a mine , pipeline or other project -- rarely stick to generally accepted thresholds to determine if there is an environmental or health concern . <p> The study also found when impacts are likely to exceed established criteria -- push past those accepted thresholds -- experts find a variety of innovative ways to
