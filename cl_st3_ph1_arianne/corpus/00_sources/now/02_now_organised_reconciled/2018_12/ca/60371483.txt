The flow of refugees and asylum-seekers from poor countries to the United States border is often attributed , incorrectly , to domestic unrest in a far-off nation . Some Americans blame far-off governments for not being " willing to take care of their own country 's problems " , as one New York Times reader commenting on the migrant caravan put it recently . <p> This one-sided view of migration ignores the global forces that bind our world together , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ violence , environmental disasters and grinding poverty that drive people from places like Guatemala , Honduras and Afghanistan are largely the result of global phenomena like colonialism , climate change and trade . <p> Seen through an international lens , both migrant-sending and migrant-receiving countries share responsibility for the people displaced by globalisation . <p> It 's no coincidence that immigration routes today follow the same path European colonisers did but in reverse . <p> France invaded Algeria in 1830 and kept it as a colony until 1962 . Today , the largest immigrant group in France is Algerians . <h> Historical pattern <p> And while Britain today may wish to close its borders , in 1948 it invited citizens of former British colonies into the country to help the United Kingdom rebuild after World War II . Indians and Pakistanis are now the second- and third-largest immigrant groups in the United Kingdom , after Polish people . <p> The Central Americans looking to the United States for refuge are following a similar historical pattern . <p> Technically , the United States was never an empire . These decades-long civil wars killed thousands and displaced hundreds of thousands . <p> War also killed the region 's economy . Average income in El Salvador , Guatemala , Honduras and Nicaragua was actually lower in 1990 than it had been in 1980 . <p> Regional instability caused mass migration to the United States . Between 1981 and 1990 almost one million Salvadoreans and Guatemalans entered the United States clandestinely . <p> Economic links between richer and poorer countries have also spurred mass migration . <h> LIVING STANDARDS <p> More international trade in recent decades has brought jobs and improved living standards in some countries among them Chile , China and South Korea preventing migration by creating the economic conditions that allow people to stay put . <p> Strategic agricultural aid to developing countries , too , has reduced emigration from some rural countries , according to a recent study in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ countries that received aid from 1995 to 2010 . <p> But international commerce has also unleashed migration elsewhere . <p> Mexican immigration to the US surged after the North American Free Trade Agreement went into effect in 1994 . The deal increased Mexico 's manufacturing sector substantially but it hurt farmers by opening Mexican markets to subsidised US agricultural products . <p> Unable to compete with these cheap imports , hundreds of thousands of Mexican farmers lost their jobs . By 2006 , an estimated 2 million peasants had been pushed out of rural areas in Mexico . Many of these displaced farmers migrated to the US , where they found jobs in the construction , agricultural and restaurant sectors . <p> Today , Hispanics make up around a quarter of workers in those industries . The same low-wage immigrant labour helps to keep manufacturing afloat in the US despite the otherwise high costs of doing business . <p> Trump insists only that cheap imports from developing countries threatens US industry . Changes in temperature and rainfall across the region have damaged the coffee and maize crops over the past decade . Some farmers who 've lost their rural livelihood joined the caravan earlier this year . <p> And though China has surpassed the United States as the world 's largest emitter of greenhouse gases , people in rich countries still use a disproportionate share of global resources . <h> Raise serious doubt <p> Per capita carbon emissions in high-income countries is about 30 times higher than in low-income countries because people in the richer countries have bigger and more air-conditioned homes , eat more meat and drive and fly more . <p> Such statistics @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ responsibility for modern climate refugees . <p> Rich countries are not to blame for every problem that drives migration from poor countries . <p> Corrupt , predatory and violent leaders in Central America , Syria , Pakistan and many other places are also accountable for creating hazardous conditions in their countries . <p> And Haiti 's devastating 2010 earthquake , which destroyed the nation 's capital and sent thousands fleeing for their lives , had nothing to do with climate change . <p> Still , too much political rhetoric out of Washington offers a simplistic , one-sided view of migration . It assigns shared responsibility for hosting migrants in ensuring their human rights are respected and addressing the root causes of displacement .
