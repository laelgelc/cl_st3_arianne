" <p> They appear to be as concerned about tackling climate change as Al Gore , Naomi Klein and Bill McKibben . <p> But their prescriptions for dealing with climate change and other environmental issues are very different from the ones offered by the Solutions Project in the U.S. and the Leap Manifesto in Canada . <p> Like the Leapers , ecomodernists also have a manifesto -- signed in 2015 by 18 environmentalists , scientists and economists , including Stewart Brand , one of the godfathers of American environmentalism . <p> It 's a paradigm that may appeal to business people , since it does n't require stopping the economy in its tracks to address climate change , as advocated by the degrowth movement . <p> Ecomodernists promote a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for the energy transition that is needed to address climate change -- especially in the developing world . <p> One of the movement 's co-founders , Michael Shellenberger , is a U.S. environmentalist who advocated against nuclear power and for renewable energy , and then changed his mind . <p> He now advocates nuclear power as the best way to tackle climate change . He also argues for " decoupling " economic growth from emissions reduction . <p> " If you really embrace ecological modernization , all of the things that you 're doing to reduce environmental impact are productivity and efficiency enhancement , " he told Business in Vancouver . " So you 're growing more food on less land with fewer resources . You 're using less land for energy production . So it 's just cheaper to produce more energy on less land , more energy with less mining . " <p> Shellenberger has spent a lot of time in developing countries .
