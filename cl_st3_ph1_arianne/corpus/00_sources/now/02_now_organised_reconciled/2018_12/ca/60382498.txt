The past year has been good , but not great , for the Pallister government . <p> It made a series of controversial decisions to curtail spending and even terminated programs . So far , however , it has not paid a huge political price . The party remains well ahead in the polls , with support percentage running in the mid-40s range . <p> The government faces the difficult challenge of fulfilling its promise to roll back the provincial sales tax while simultaneously tackling deficits and debt . Abandoning the proposed carbon tax and the revenue it represented makes the fiscal challenge even greater , and has antagonized those voters who believe climate change is the existential issue of our time . <p> There are still negatives attached to the leadership of Premier Brian Pallister , who has picked fights with Ottawa and has been guilty of some social gaffes . However , at this time , none of Manitoba 's party leaders would win a popularity contest . <p> Despite @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of discontent within the PC ranks . The party has a strong membership base and is rolling in money . In federal-provincial fights , the premier has allies in Ontario , Saskatchewan and , soon , in all likelihood , in Alberta . <p> It has not been a great year for the NDP , which is still recovering from a crushing defeat in the 2016 election that saw it drop to 14 seats from 37 seats . There is still a negative hangover from the Greg Selinger government 's broken promise not to increase the PST , a leadership revolt that nearly brought down the premier and a failure to seriously address the financial conditions of rising deficits and debt . <p> Wab Kinew , who replaced Selinger , brought a checkered past to his role as party leader , a fact that his political opponents have used against him . Under Kinew , the leadership rivalries within the NDP appear to have disappeared ; however , in May 2018 , a devastating sexual harassment report identified a " sexist and misogynistic culture " within the party . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the government provided ammunition for attacks , the NDP has not be able to capitalize politically , leading left-wing activists and unions to criticize the party for its failure to mobilize public discontent . <p> In July , the party lost a longtime NDP stronghold in the St. Boniface byelection . Recent polls show a small bounce-back in Winnipeg , the main battleground for elections , but beyond the Perimeter Highway , support for the NDP is spread very thin . The party is short of members and money , and recruitment of credible candidates and mounting a strong ground game in the next election will be a major challenge . <p> As Manitoba 's perennial third-place party , the Liberals had a good year in 2018 . New leader Dougald Lamont brought energy and ideas to the job , based on his experience as a backroom strategist . Previous tries at " retail politics " had not brought success , but in October 2018 , he won the St. Boniface byelection . <p> A young and enthusiastic campaign team and the endorsement of leading Liberal figures from the past @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ leadership . Lamont 's victory meant official party status in the legislature , more resources of money and staff , and more speaking opportunities and related media attention . The party lacks money and professional staff to support the leader , however . Recruitment of credible candidates and the development of viable constituency associations across the province is a difficult and time-consuming process that the Liberals began over the past summer . <p> Based on the province 's fixed-date election law , the next election is not scheduled until Oct. 6 , 2020 . This is too far in the future for any sound predictions to be made . There has only been a single one-term government in Manitoba 's history , so it seems clear that it takes at least two terms before Manitobans to become sufficiently disenchanted to dump a governing party . <p> As a non-partisan commentator , my wish for all three parties is that , politically , they each get what they deserve in 2019 . <p> Paul G.
