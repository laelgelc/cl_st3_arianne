<h> After a year of record-breaking fires , the state is bracing for what comes next . <p> Seven years into a severe drought , stuck in a wildfire season that never seems to end , it 's clear that California has entered a new era . The increasing size , intensity , and frequency of wildfires in the state has agencies doubling down on prevention and management measures . California has allocated $1 billion over the next five years for fire protection , and while the future of fire preparedness still involves traditional methods of prevention and protection , like thinning forests with prescribed burns , and adding firefighter personnel and resources , many climate scientists and researchers suggest that we start treating fires more like floods and earthquakes -- not as something we can manage , but rather as disasters beyond our control . <p> Those who were affected by the deadliest wildfire season in history already know how little control we have over these blazes . At least 85 people died in the Camp Fire , which scorched 153,000 acres of Butte County and destroyed nearly 14,000 homes .
