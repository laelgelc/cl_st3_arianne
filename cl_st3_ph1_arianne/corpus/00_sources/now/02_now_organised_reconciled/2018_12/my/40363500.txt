<h> Send radioactive waste out of Malaysia <p> SAHABAT Alam Malaysia ( SAM ) and the Consumers Association of Penang ( CAP ) support the decision of the government to ask Lynas Malaysia Sdn Bhd to remove and dispose of its accumulated 451,464 tonnes of radioactive waste from its plant in Pahang . <p> In our view the radioactive waste should be exported by Lynas to Australia because the raw materials that are processed into rare earth originates in Australia . <p> We also note that the government has asked Lynas to submit a plan to dispose of its neutralisation underflow residue ( NUF ) scheduled waste totalling 1.113 million tonnes , accumulated over six years . <p> The regulations only allow a limit of 20 tonnes of scheduled waste to be stored on site for a maximum of 180 days , according to Minister of Energy , Science , Technology , Environment and Climate Change , Yeo Bee Yin . Lynas has applied for exemption for their scheduled waste . <p> SAM and CAP are of the view that this NUF scheduled waste is also hazardous and should also be shipped out of the country by Lynas . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ fact , our position all along has been that , in the first place , Lynas should not have been allowed to operate its rare earth processing activities in Malaysia at all . We have thus consistently called for the plant to be shut down . <p> The government 's decision is not ideal , as Lynas will still be allowed to operate if it fulfils the two conditions . Even if there is satisfactory waste disposal , there can still be dangers linked to its activities , for instance to the transporting , handling , operation and possible leakages of radioactive and other hazardous materials . And it is almost impossible to safely store or dispose of radioactive materials . <p> The health and safety of residents and workers in the plant and its vicinity come first . The damage to the environment , including water and soil , must also be a major concern . Income and jobs can be derived from safer activities . <p> The government decision to allow it to continue if it deals with the radioactive and the scheduled wastes is the very bare @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ step . <p> We also view with alarm the report of the executive committee on Lynas operations , that found an increase in contamination of heavy metals -- including nickel , chromium , lead and mercury -- in ground water . These metals can cause cancer and other serious ailments such as Minamata disease ( from mercury poisoning ) . This is alarming also because 12.5% of households use the ground water daily , according to the report . <p> The dangers of radioactive waste are very serious , and the radioactivity will remain for billions of years . Thus , the accumulated radioactive waste must be shipped out of the country . The scheduled waste is also hazardous , which is why they are scheduled . There are so far no plans on how the two types of wastes are being dealt with in terms of permanent safe storage or disposal . It should be noted that the Prime Minister referred to the Bukit Merah , Perak , case where 1 sq km of land in Perak was lost to bury the wastes . <p> We also note that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ government for setting the two conditions on waste disposal for renewal of its licence to operate . We congratulate Yeo for not reacting to this threat . We call on the government to stand firm and not be intimidated by such threats , as the lives and health of the people and the safety of the environment should be its top most priority . <p> SAM and CAP therefore call on the government to demand that Lynas ship out both the radioactive and the scheduled waste from Malaysia , and that the plant stop its operations if it does not do so as soon as possible .
