<p> Members of Auckland iwi Ngai Tai ki Tamaki have been given an early Christmas present with a majority decision in their favour from the Supreme Court . <p> Ngai Tai ki Tamaki Trust has mana whenua on Motutapu and Rangitoto , and exercises manaakitanga and kaitiakitanga over these islands . In 2015 the Department of Conservation granted separate concessions to Fullers Group Limited and Motutapu Island Restoration Trust for their respective commercial tour operations on Rangitoto and Motutapu . The Department made its decision under the Conservation Act 1987 and Reserves Act 1977 . Section 4 of the Conservation Act requires the Conservation Act to be interpreted and administered as to give effect to the principles of the Treaty of Waitangi . <p> As mana whenua , Ngai Tai ki Tamaki argued that Treaty principles of partnership , active protection , right to development and redress meant that it was not appropriate or in accordance with tikanga for other groups to provide guided tours on the islands . <p> The contentious point before the Court was that the Department granted the concession on the basis that no preferential entitlement should be given in favour of Ngai Tai ki @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as a result of a concession is not something to take into account . <p> The High Court originally found that the Minister of Conservation had made an error in law in making the decisions to grant the concessions .
