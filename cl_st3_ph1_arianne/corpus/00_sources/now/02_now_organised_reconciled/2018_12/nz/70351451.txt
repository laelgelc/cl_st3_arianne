<h> Edmund Hillary Fellowship 's first Pacific Island recipient <p> STARTSSamoan Faumuina Felolini Maria Tafuna'i is the first Pacific Island person to become an Edmund Hillary Fellowship Fellow . <p> Tafuna'i joins a league of global entrepreneurs , changemakers and investors who have been brought together to build meaningful solutions to global problems . <p> The prestigious fellowship provides access to a global support network that encourages systemic , integrated solutions . <p> Described by Edmund Hillary Fellowship as a navigator , Faumuina has taken the principles of wayfinding ( star navigation ) and ocean voyaging and re-interpreted them into a project design model . <p> Faumuina says she is focussed on using the model in development projects in New Zealand and the Pacific Islands , but also sees how it can be used in small right through to large , international businesses and social projects . <p> " I could see there was a gap between the design of the project and where the project would take place . The gap was usually cultural understanding . From there I started developing the model . <p> " I also benefitted from my background as a journalist in being able @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and interpret it for a general audience . <p> " There are nine questions in the model . The first two questions I ask are : What is my island of success ? And , who is on my canoe ? The people in your canoe must be considered not just their skills and experience but as whole people -- and you need to think are these people I can take on a journey with me , and that the captain must be a person worthy of following .
