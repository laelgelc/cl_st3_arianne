" Image : David Robie/PMC <p> ANALYSIS:By Professor Derrick Armstrong <p> A traditional view of the tension between research and policy suggests that researchers are poor at communicating their research findings to policy-makers in clear and unambiguous ways . <p> I am arguing that this is an outdated view of the relationship between research and policy . Science , including social science , and policy come together in many interesting and creative ways . <p> This does not mean that tensions between the two are dissolved but the conversation between research and policy centre as much on ideological and pragmatic issues as it does upon the strength of the scientific evidence itself . <p> Researchers are increasingly " smart " in the ways that they seek to influence public debate while policy-makers genuinely value the insights that research can provide in supporting political and policy agendas that goes beyond simply legitimating pre-existing policy @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ science can not be seen simply as an arbiter of " truth " that informs policy and political decision-making . Science also plays an advocacy role in alliance with some social interests against others . <p> -Partners- <p> Likewise , policy can draw on science but it can also reject the evidence of science where scientific evidence is weighed against the interests of other powerful voices in the policy-process . <p> Oceans research and policy provides a good example of this more sophisticated relationship between science and policy and suggests some of the significant disconnects and tensions that challenge the relationship as well as how creative tensions between the two operate in practice . Three areas of disconnect can be identified . <p> Practical disconnectionThe first of these is practical disconnection of regulation with regard to the Oceans . It is impossible to credibly talk of " giving voice to the ' disadvantaged ' and ' vulnerable ' " where the research practices of outside researchers and their institutions cripple the ability of local researchers to speak . <p> Yet , researchers in the Pacific are more effectively operating at the cutting-edge of the science-policy interface than many outside the region may understand or recognise . <p> In our own case at USP , genuine collaboration across the boundaries of south and north have been possible but just as our leaders and our communities have had to fight against patronising notions of " vulnerability " our scientific need is to build our own capacity to effectively engage with the priorities of our @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a scientific and research capacity that is neither dominated by or exploited from outside . <p> So , in summary , the tensions that have traditionally been used to characterise the science-policy interface greatly oversimplify the reality .
