<h> Reducing global emissions : New Zealand 's challenge or theirs ? <p> There are some distinct characteristics to New Zealand 's greenhouse gas emissions profile , notably the high proportion associated with livestock farming . <p> OPINION : Successive New Zealand governments have established greenhouse gas ( GHG ) emissions reductions targets for the nation and committed New Zealand to being an active participant in the international response to the challenge of climate change . <p> There are some distinct characteristics to New Zealand 's GHG emissions profile , notably the high proportion ( around 48 per cent ) associated with livestock farming and the low proportion ( around 15 per cent ) from electricity generation . <p> It is also the case that our total emissions are small - less than 0.2 per cent of total global emissions . Actions to reduce New Zealand 's emissions will not make an appreciable difference to the global climate-change trend . Put another way , we could successfully reduce New Zealand 's emissions to net zero , as the Government proposes , but still bear the full impact of climate change if others do not also act to reduce their @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ climate change . It is a classic example of the " tragedy of the commons " . <p> The commons , in this case is the global atmosphere . No-one " owns " it , we all depend on it , no international organisation and no national government alone has the power to ensure that the earth 's atmosphere is used sparingly and sustainably . <p> Globally , we 've treated the atmosphere ( and the oceans ) as an inexhaustible resource able to cope with whatever mankind chooses to draw from , and dispose in , it . <p> After 250 years of burning fossil fuels in bulk and assuming the exhaust plumes can be endlessly and harmlessly absorbed into the atmosphere , we now understand that the atmospheric commons has its limits , collectively we 've reached them , and there are ugly consequences . <p> We know that over 60 per cent of global GHG emissions come from the five largest emitters - China , USA , EU , India and Russia . <p> So it is pretty clear where the big gains must be made . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ like New Zealand , should simply resign themselves to being passengers on this increasingly hazardous ride ? <p> A key characteristic of any tragedy of the commons is that it can only be solved by concerted , collective action around an agreed strategy . <p> Without such an agreed strategy , any individual country contemplating actions to solve the problem is discouraged by the thought that other countries face incentives to free-ride . <p> It is credible commitments to collective actions that are the essential ingredient for successful progress . In that setting , being a small contributor to the problem does not justify inaction . Far from it . <p> Around a quarter of global emissions come from small emitters ( countries with emissions less than 1 per cent of the global total ) . Collectively , small emitters do matter and being an active contributor to the solution provides an opportunity to influence others to act similarly . <p> New Zealand 's influence is likely to be most relevant in areas where New Zealand has particular expertise and experience ( such as techniques for pastoral GHG mitigation ) @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ capacity to influence will be the greater if it can point to its own credible mitigation progress . <p> The Productivity Commission 's report on the transition to a low emissions economy concluded that New Zealand could achieve its targets by 2050 - that the task would be challenging , but achievable . <p> The challenges lie mostly in uncertainties relating to the timing and impact of future technology advances . Electric or other low emissions vehicles , efficient replacements for coal and other fossil fuels in industrial heat , reduced methane and nitrous oxide emissions from ruminant livestock farming , the integration of low cost and renewable electricity generation into the existing networks . <p> Maintaining stable , persistent and consistent policy direction through numerous electoral cycles over the next three decades will also be testing . <p> While the efforts to reduce emissions that New Zealand makes will have no direct influence on what we experience by way of climate change , as a member of the global community we have a shared interest in overcoming this challenge to our collective well-being . <p> We can not expect @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ can not ourselves demonstrate willingness and ability to play our part , to offer our assistance and to share the benefits of our experience .
