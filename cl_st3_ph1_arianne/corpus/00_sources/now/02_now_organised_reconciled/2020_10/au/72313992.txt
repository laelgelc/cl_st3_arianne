<h> Opinion <h> The budget does next to nothing for our greatest economic challenge <h> By Harry Guinness and Steven Hamilton <p> October 7 , 2020 -- 11.12am <p> Only months ago we were fighting bushfires , reminding us of our continued failure to act on climate change . Then that other crisis struck . And ever since , our politicians have been preoccupied with the pandemic . <p> The pandemic has distracted politicians from the climate threat , but it illustrates the community 's willingness to modify its behaviour for the greater good . Credit:Nick Moir <p> That 's understandable . It might even be excusable , if only we had n't already wasted so much time before the pandemic . <p> Tuesday 's budget was an emblem of this distraction . It brought welcome stimulus and promising initiatives to drive growth and jobs . But on climate change , there was next to nothing . Of $240 billion in additional spending , just 1 per cent is on items one could only generously call " green stimulus " . <p> Perhaps all is not lost . Rather than a roadblock , the crisis should in fact be a catalyst for strong action on climate change . <p> Every one of us has spent the past seven months learning how to make personal sacrifices for the benefit of millions of people we 've never met . And many of us have got by without too much complaint . Once you 're forced to go without , you often learn just how little value some things offer . <p> Tackling climate change will come at some cost to our material wealth today . But if we face up to it on our own terms , we might find the necessary sacrifices were not so great after all . What we really need now is for our politicians to show the kind of strong leadership on climate they 've shown on COVID-19 . <p> If the pandemic has taught us anything about our political class , it 's never say never . Who would have predicted a Coalition government would champion a $70 billion @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a 50 per cent increase in the dole ? And look how quickly the " Overton window " -- the range of politically acceptable options -- has moved on energy . Last year the Coalition was arguing about coal-fired power . Now the debate has shifted to gas . <p> Other nations are showing our leaders how to seize this moment . France just announced a third of its $164 billion stimulus measures will be green , including in energy efficiency , green hydrogen , and low-emission vehicles . Britain has pledged to reach net-zero by 2050 , and China by 2060 . US presidential candidate Joe Biden has pledged $2.4 trillion in climate spending . <p> Loading <p> These leaders understand green stimulus promises a triple dividend . Government spending on climate generates activity and jobs today . It lowers our carbon pollution , which will lessen the severity of extreme weather events tomorrow . And it drives the industries and trade opportunities of the future . <p> One of the greatest barriers to our energy transition is the mundane problem of electricity transmission . Transmission lines are the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ population centres . The trouble with renewable energy sources is they 're often located far from existing transmission lines . <p> This poses a challenge similar to the railway lines of the past . For the first town on the line , the cost is likely too high . But if the cost can be spread across all the towns on the line , suddenly it becomes viable . <p> Solving these kinds of collective action problems is a fundamental role of government . And just like the railroads , these transmission lines are massive nation-building projects that generate activity and jobs along their routes , not to mention on the energy projects they enable . <p> In July , the Australian Energy Market Operator detailed the transmission projects critical to driving our energy transition . To the scores of publicly funded roads and bridges and rail lines in the budget , the government should add the most critical of these green transmission projects . <p> Loading <p> And there are of course scores of other commitments that could help propel our transition to net-zero emissions : boosting green R&amp;D @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ far offered , and significantly raising funding for what the Prime Minister calls " the world 's most successful green bank " , Australia 's Clean Energy Finance Corporation . <p> The public is primed to support a civic project of unprecedented scale . The government can borrow for the long term at near-zero interest rates . Our economy is crying out for activity and jobs . And a rapidly warming climate demands immediate action . <p> As he moves on from this budget and starts preparations for the next one , due in just seven months , we hope the Treasurer understands there wo n't ever be a better time to start the transition than today . <p> Harry Guinness and Steven Hamilton are the chief executive and chief economist of the Blueprint Institute , a new think tank pairing market-focused economics with environmental solutions .
