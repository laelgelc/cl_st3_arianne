<h> Partners <p> Review : If the future is to be worth anything , curated by Patrice Sharkey and Rayleen Forester , ACE Open <p> " If the future is to be worth anything " rings true as a question for many in Australia 's art world today . It is an apt title for this ambitious survey exhibition measuring the pulse of contemporary art in South Australia . <p> Partway through the gestation process for the artists making work for this survey , COVID-19 hit and artists retreated to their studios . But this has given a sharper focus to Patrice Sharkey and Rayleen Forester 's curatorial probe . <p> This is the fourth survey exhibition of contemporary South Australian artists over the last two decades , following much larger survey exhibitions at the Art Gallery of South Australia in 2000 and 2013 , and the Contemporary Art Centre of South Australia ( one of the two precursors to ACE Open ) in 2010 . <p> ACE Open 's gallery space is more confined , and gives an overview based on just ten artists and collectives . The subjects of the portraits are overlaid with a signature Aboriginal iconography of dots , their faces showing a mix of emotions : from pride and optimism at a better future , to strength marred by weary endurance . <p> Senior Ngarrindjeri artist Sandra Saunders considers the destruction of wildlife from the recent bushfires in her meticulous oil painting , the Museum of Sorrow . <p> The impact of colonisation , climate change and environmental destruction have been her subjects in recent years in paintings produced in a naive , untutored style . <p> Here , Saunders has appropriated a European quasi-Vermeer style to speak back to colonialism 's litany of damage .
