<h> Climate leaves corporate Australia snoozing <h> Kieran Cooke , Climate News Network <p> Though Australia is considered one of the countries most vulnerable to the vagaries of a changing climate , very few in the Australian corporate sector pay much attention to the issue says a report focusing on business attitudes in the country . <p> ... Australian companies appear to be struggling to move forward in responding to climate change impacts , apparently paralysed by short-term profit-first thinking , uncertain political risks and a corporate culture unused to volatility and disruption , <p> says the report . <p> The study , funded by the Australian Government 's National Climate Change Adaptation Research Facility , says that though more than 100 companies were grilled at length on climate change , it 's clear very few give much weight to the issue , either in terms of corporate planning or in assessing future risks to their businesses . <p> says the report . For example , in the transport sector , very little is known about the potential impacts of climate change - further research is urgently required to explore @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the considerable cascading effects of changes in climate , especially on the tourism sector . <p> Meanwhile the property and real estate sector faces " a phenomenal challenge " , with an estimated A$81bn ( ? 54bn ) worth of property vulnerable to a rise in sea levels , and more than half a million homes at risk of floods . Much of Australia 's infrastructure is aged , says the report , and has not been designed or operated with climate change in mind . <p> The study questions the role of the Australian Government : on one hand it expects the private sector to adapt to climate change , yet on the other it gives few , if any , incentives to promote changes in corporate behaviour . Australian companies are missing out on opportunities and innovations associated with climate change while Asian-owned mining , gas and technology business are cashing in . <p> The report notes that the insurance industry can act as an economic shock absorber and underpins much of present-day economic activity . <p> The costs of insurance are going up - and in some @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ insured . <p> Electoral tussle ahead . <p> The study also highlights what it calls the legal imperative which should be driving businesses to adapt to climate change . <p> One of the key legal findings from the research is that corporations need to identify their climate-related risk , and , once quantified , ensure that such risk forms an integral part of their environmental risk management process , <p> says Mark Baker-Jones , one of the report 's authors . <p> A separate report released in London earlier this month warned of the risk of investing in mining and other companies which have assets that ultimately - if climate change is going to be tackled - might have to stay in the ground . <p> On a per capita basis Australia is one of the world 's leading greenhouse gas emitters , mainly due to its giant coal mining industry .
