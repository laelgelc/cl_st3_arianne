<p> Climate change can seem daunting , but part of the solution is relatively straightforward : stop clearing trees and start planting them . While new ways of generating electricity or storing carbon draw hype and funding , ' rewilding ' is one of the cheapest and most effective strategies , studies have found . <p> According to research recently published in the journal Nature , restoring a third of the most degraded land on the planet and protecting the parts in good condition would store carbon equating to half of all human-caused emissions since the industrial revolution . It would also prevent most of the predicted species extinctions . <p> Rewilding on this scale would mean planting billions of trees , and it would need to be done fast . <p> The Nature study estimated about 2,870 million hectares of land worldwide has been ' converted ' ( i.e. cleared of trees or other vegetation ) . The UN has declared 2021-2030 the ' UN Decade on Ecosystem Restoration ' . The German Government 's Bonn Challenge aims to restore 350 million hectares of degraded and deforested landscape worldwide by 2030 . Dendra is one of several companies that are angling to meet this demand , and betting that drones are the best method . <p> It aims to have planted 500 billion ( half a trillion ) trees by 2060 . <p> Despite all this effort to plant trees and offset greenhouse gas emissions , forests are still being cleared at a blistering rate . In 2019 , humans cleared a football pitch of primary rainforest every six seconds . Australia is the only developed country in the world to make the WWF 's global list of deforestation hotspots . In 2018 , the WWF estimated three million hectares of untouched forest will be bulldozed in eastern Australia by 2030.
