<h> Lack of finance forces Bangladesh to consider shelving coal <h> Chinese and Japanese investors have lost interest in funding coal projects due to high costs , pushing the country towards natural gas -- which will do little to solve problems of overcapacity , expensive power and carbon emissions . <h> Share <p> Bangladesh will retreat from coal power if a recent proposal is approved by Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina . <p> In August , the Ministry of Power , Energy and Mineral Resources sent a letter to the Prime Minister 's Office . The letter , which The Third Pole has seen , seeks approval to convert 13 coal-fired power plants , which are currently in the pipeline , into liquefied natural gas ( LNG ) -based plants . <p> The previous month , Nasrul Hamid , Bangladesh 's minister of power , energy and mineral resources , said during a webinar that the country intended to " review " all but three of 29 planned coal plants that had been granted a licence . Under this , the 18 licensed coal projects were to provide 35 per cent of the country 's total energy . The projects were being delivered through public , private and joint ventures . <p> China is the country 's largest developer and funder . The fact that power production exceeds demand has also hampered profit margins . <p> Ultimately , it is not protests or environmental concerns that have led to the proposal . Lenders have become wary of the cost of coal .
