<p> VANCOUVER --British Columbia 's provincial election is taking place in the middle of a pandemic . But COVID-19 is not top of mind for voters as they mail in their ballots or head to advance polls , new research has found . <p> A new online study from Research Co. , conducted at the beginning of October , suggests that the coronavirus and even health care are not the number one issues for British Columbians planning to vote . <p> Instead , housing , poverty and homelessness came out on top , tied with the economy and jobs , says Mario Canseco , the research company 's president . <p> " What this really shows us is a situation in which British Columbians are no longer looking at COVID-19 as an urgent matter , " he says . " I think we 've made peace with the fact that it is going to be here for a while , and we want to focus on specific things that the politicians can deal with that are going to go beyond COVID-19 . But health care was a close third , with 23 per cent ranking it as their defining concern . <p> And the importance of those three issues also differs by age group . Housing , homelessness and poverty were number one for potential voters from 18 to 34 years old , while the 55 and up category chose health care . The group in between , middle-aged British Columbians , were most concerned with the economy and jobs . <p> " What is really striking about this election , especially when we compare it with other ones , is the absence of a salient issue that is defining everything , " Canseco says . " This makes this campaign unique . There 's no salient issue that ( the parties ) can connect with . You need to talk about all three if you want to get voters from all age groups . " <p> That may come as a surprise in an election campaign unlike any other , defined by the restrictions of the pandemic . The environment , previously a campaign-defining issue in the province , has dropped in the rankings from where it was in previous years . <p> " There was a point when we had the environment somewhere between 15 and 20 per cent - a lot of residents were worried about climate change and environmental aspects of their lives in British Columbia , " Canseco says . " And now we have it at a level that is roughly the same that we have in Saskatchewan -- it 's less than 10 per cent . " <p> That could prove to be a problem for the Green Party , who have a stronger presence in B.C. than in much of the rest of the country . <p> " And now we are in second or sometimes third place because Quebecors are more likely to say that the environment is the number one issue facing the country , " Canseco says . <p> " One of the situations that we see is the same level of discussion @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ago , is what we 're seeing in Quebec right now . " <p> Even though COVID-19 did n't make it into the top three , eight per cent of voters surveyed did mention it as a concern , ahead of the four per cent who said crime was an important issue , the three per cent who chose accountability , and the one per cent who chose education or energy . <p> Research Co. interviewed 750 likely voters in British Columbia online between Oct. 5 and 7 .
