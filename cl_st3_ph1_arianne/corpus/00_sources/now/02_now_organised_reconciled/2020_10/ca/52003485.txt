<h> David Suzuki : Humility , caring , and wisdom make a better future possible <p> For many , the pandemic has renewed our innate appreciation for and connection to nature . People have taken to growing food on windowsills and in backyard and community gardens . We 're cultivating yeasts to bake bread and getting outside more to walk , run , swim and cycle . <p> In the face of uncertainty , nature brings solace and sustenance . Research shows time spent in forests -- and even just looking at trees or photos of them -- boosts immune systems , lowers blood pressure , reduces stress , improves mood and ability to focus , and increases energy levels and sleep quality . <p> As we consider the natural world , we must remember that how we talk about it matters . Steven Nitah , former elected chief of Lutsel K'e Dene First Nation and four-time member of the Northwest Territories legislative assembly , says shifting our language can help shift our understanding . With climate disruption , our refusal to contain carbon emissions has put our well-being and survival at risk . <p> In his essay " The year America melted down , " Omar El Akkad observes : " Mask-wearing has become politicized , just as school shootings became politicized , just as climate change became politicized , just as any instance of communal survival at the expense of personal profit inevitably becomes politicized . " <p> Things that should n't be politicized are , but El Akkad argues battle lines continue to be drawn around issues that pit individual rights against responsibilities to uphold the common good .
