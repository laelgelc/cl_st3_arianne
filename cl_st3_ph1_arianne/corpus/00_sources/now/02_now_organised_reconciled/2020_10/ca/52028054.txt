<h> Climate of support <p> Hannah Muhajarine ( left ) , Laura Cameron and Daniel Friesen ask passersby if they want to see their tax dollars go toward a Green New Deal or the Trans Mountain Pipeline . <p> Hannah Muhajarine remembers how she felt seeing thousands of people at the Legislative Building for the climate strike last year . It was an event the Manitoba Energy Justice Coalition , a group Muhajarine organizes with , had a hand in planning . <p> " It just blew my mind how many people were there , " Muhajarine says of the Sept. 27 , 2019 , strike , which saw about 10,000 supporters leave school and work to demand concrete action on climate change . " We had been working so hard to create this event , and to see that so many people cared about the climate crisis enough to come out was very moving and felt very exciting . " <p> It 's just one of the actions the MEJC has organized since it was established in 2014 . <p> Through grassroots community organizing , the group engages Manitobans @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ group focuses on the sovereignty of Indigenous peoples as it champions a just transition to environmentally and socially responsible energy sources . <p> The group is currently running three campaigns . The first calls attention to the impacts of hydro development in northern Manitoba . <p> Another campaign is in solidarity with Indigenous and front-line communities that are taking action for climate justice and land defence , such as the Wet'suwet'en who are organizing against the Coastal Gaslink Pipeline . The MEJC was heavily involved with the protests at Portage and Main in February . <p> A third MEJC campaign calls on the federal government to implement a Green New Deal as a central piece of Canada 's COVID-19 response .
