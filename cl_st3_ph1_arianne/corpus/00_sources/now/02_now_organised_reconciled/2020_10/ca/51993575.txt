" <h> Elemental differences ? <p> Battery electric vehicles ( BEV ) and hydrogen fuel-cell vehicles ( FCV ) are both electric vehicles . BEVs get their electricity from batteries that have to be plugged in and recharged . <p> Right now , that can take typically anywhere from 30-45 minutes at a fast charger , 5-8 hours at a 240-volt Level 2 charger and overnight , or longer , at a 120-volt household outlet . <p> Fuel-cell vehicles make their own electricity on-board from hydrogen stored in tanks . They have to be refilled at a hydrogen station . For a passenger car with a 500- to 600-kilometre range , that typically takes less than five minutes . <p> Neither car produces CO2 while you drive it . A BEV has no tailpipe emissions at all , while a FCV spits out only water . <p> Story continues below advertisement <p> The vast majority of EVs on the road in Canada are BEVs or plug-in hybrids ( PHEVs ) @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ quarter of 2020 , 8,412 BEV and 3,586 PHEV vehicles were sold in Canada . That 's about 3.8 per cent of total passenger vehicles sold . There still are n't many hydrogen cars in Canada . For instance , there are about 75 hydrogen-powered Toyota Mirais in Canada , Toyota says . <p> Let 's look at how the two technologies compare -- and why we may need both : <h> Practicality <p> For passenger cars making short commutes in a city , batteries make sense , Chatzimarkakis said . <p> " I would never suggest using hydrogen in an urban environment where you need less than 40 kWh , " Chatzimarkakis said . <p> But hydrogen makes sense for longer distances because it can be refilled quickly . For bigger passenger vehicles such as SUVs , a hybrid BEV/FCV might help get more people to switch from gas . <h> Infrastructure <p> There are four public hydrogen stations in Canada -- three in British Columbia and one in Quebec . <p> A fourth B.C. station is expected to open in Victoria at the end of this month . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of 2021 , there should be eight open in B.C , " said Colin Armstrong , president and CEO of HTEC , a company that produces hydrogen and builds hydrogen fuel stations . " There will be another in Quebec City and one in Montreal by the end of 2021 or the beginning of 2022 . " <p> While BEVs fare better when it comes to places to refuel , both technologies will need more infrastructure to get Canadians to buy electric vehicles . <p> " There 's a chicken-and-egg problem for both technologies , " Chatzimarkakis said . <p> Ottawa is set to release a hydrogen strategy this fall that to boost Canada 's ability to produce and distribute hydrogen . <p> Story continues below advertisement <h> Energy efficiency <p> While battery-electric and hydrogen cars do n't produce CO2 when you drive them , they 're only as green as the source of their energy . But if you 're plugging in your car in Alberta , where 92 per cent of electricity comes from coal or natural gas , your BEV is contributing to climate change . <p> That 's where hydrogen might make sense . Green hydrogen is produced by electrolysis -- using cheap electricity from a renewable source to split water into hydrogen and oxygen . <p> Potentially , green hydrogen could be shipped by pipeline to places without a reliable supply of renewable power and be used to fuel vehicles there . <p> Canada already makes about three million tonnes of hydrogen a year by steam-methane reformation of natural gas .
