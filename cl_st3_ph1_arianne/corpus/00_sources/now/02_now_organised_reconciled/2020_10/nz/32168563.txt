<p> Report from RNZ by Harry Lock The Wellington City Council is considering spending $185 million to reduce the amount of sludge the city produces every day . The money would pay for a thermal hydrolysis and digestion plant with a thermal dryer located at Moa Point . <p> It 's reported such a development would reduce sludge volumes by more than 82 percent , and carbon emissions by 63 percent . <p> But it would come at a cost , and a significantly higher one than had first been expected . <p> In the Council 's 2018 Long-Term Plan , just $30 million had been budgeted to minimise sludge -- a sixth of the cost being considered now . <p> At a meeting this morning , councillors agreed to undertake further work into the viability of the project . A more detailed report is expected later this year . <p> What is the problem with sludge ? <p> Sludge is a by-product of the wastewater treatment process . The Moa Point and Western Waste Water Treatment Plants process nearly 400,000 tonnes of it each year . To treat it properly , it is pumped to the Southern Landfill where it must be mixed with a certain amount of solid waste . <p> The city is in a bind -- the landfill 's conditions mean they have to mix the sludge with the waste at a ratio of 4:1 general waste to sludge . <p> As the city 's population increases , so too will the amount of sludge produced ( more people equals more waste water equals more sludge ) . Meanwhile , the council is trying to reduce the amount of household waste being sent to the landfill . <p> The ratio is feared to be unsustainable in the long term as sludge goes up and general waste goes down . <p> " Working toward a waste-free Wellington is a small passion of mine , " said councillor Laurie Foon , " but since I have been on the council , the way we treat our sludge @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ advocates are being told we ca n't go on that journey right now . The short-term solution was to have to truck the sludge to the landfill directly . The fix was to bring in engineers from Germany who repaired the broken pipe , but at considerable cost . <p> A 21st Century solution to a stinky problem <p> In total , 16 options were considered by Wellington Water -- the regional authority which manages Wellington City 's water network . <p> The options varied from a lower budget of $86 million which would fund a thermal dryer only , up to a possible $363 million wet air oxidation option . <p> The recommended option is a $185 million project , which would see the creation of a thermal hydrolysis and digestion plant with a thermal dryer . <p> While it would n't remove sludge completely , it would reduce it by more than 80 percent @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ by the sludge being sent to the Landfill . <p> Councillor Sean Rush described it as a " twenty-first century solution , a sustainable solution , a low-emitting solution , and it does provide the potential to recover the resource " . <p> Concerns were raised over the cost of the new plant , however . <p> " I am concerned about the project management of it , given the experiences of cost blowouts on Omaroro Reservoir , " said Councillor Fleur Fitzsimsons , " and I will as this project develops ask for really detailed commitments to containing costs , and risk management . <p> " And I do maintain concerns around the contracting out model of core infrastructure which I do n't think serves our city well .
