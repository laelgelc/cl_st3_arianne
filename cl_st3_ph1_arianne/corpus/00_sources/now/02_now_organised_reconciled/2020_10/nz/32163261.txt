<h> A mandate equal to the responsibility <p> A red tide has swept Labour into a historic situation , left the National Party reeling , seen two minor parties celebrating and sent one of New Zealand 's most colourful politicians out of Parliament , all in one dramatic election . <p> For the first time since the MMP system was introduced in 1996 a party is in a position to govern alone , with Labour passing the 61 seats needed for a majority in Parliament by three on election night . Polls predicting a Labour victory were actually exceeded as Jacinda Ardern took the party to its biggest win in 50 years . <p> Much of the credit for that must go to Ardern who has been rewarded for steering the country through an extremely difficult term , culminating in the Covid-19 outbreak . <p> While Labour has yet to decide whether to include the Greens in an arrangement like they had in the previous coalition , it has returned the country to unbridled power for the first time since the 1990s . <p> With that comes a mandate equal @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ task of rebuilding the country 's economy while the pandemic keeps spreading elsewhere and the climate change threat remains undiminished . <p> It will also be interesting to see who gets the role of deputy prime minister now Winston Peters and New Zealand First has gone . <p> The Greens made history of a kind too -- the first minor party to spend a term in government and then record greater than 5 percent for the party vote . <p> There was a Cheshire cat grin on the face of ACT 's David Seymour who on Saturday was the first leader to speak on television .
