<h> What happens to a West Coast without tourists ? <p> The loss of international tourism has hit the West Coast hard , amid population decline and a wider battle to keep jobs in the area . Alex Braae spent several days driving down the coast to cover the election . <p> The Owen River Tavern just north of Murchison has bilingual signs alongside the usual trapping of a bustling country pub . They 're there in recognition of the fact that , once upon a time , much of the clientele were tourists from East Asian countries who made a stop there on a trip up or down the West Coast . <p> When I pulled in on a Saturday evening the car park was empty and none of the attached motel rooms were occupied . The campground , down the hill and next to the river , was deserted . I asked the proprietor if it was unusually quiet tonight , and she said it had been ever since Auckland went into level three , and the flow of tourists from the city was cut off . Not one other person set foot in the pub that night . Feeling bad for their bottom line , I got another beer . <p> The Owen River Tavern ( Photo : Alex Braae ) <p> It was an introduction to a regional economy that is going through immense difficulty right now . Before Covid , the West Coast was already pretty hard up , with close to zero population growth , economic growth rates behind those of the rest of the country , severe infrastructure challenges , and a vulnerability to weather and natural disasters . <p> But with tourism making up about 15% of the region 's economy in 2019 , the impact of Covid-19 -- particularly the closing of borders -- has hit particularly hard .
