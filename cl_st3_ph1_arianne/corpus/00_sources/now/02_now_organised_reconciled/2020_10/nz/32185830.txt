<h> New democracy reporter joins The Gisborne Herald <p> The Gisborne Herald 's new local democracy reporter is excited to be taking up the role at a time when the local authority has committed to an anti-racism journey . <p> Alice Angeloni grew up on Whadjuk Noongar country in Perth , Western Australia . <p> She studied politics and journalism at the University of Notre Dame in Perth , including a semester on exchange in London , before moving to Blenheim to work for the Marlborough Express and Stuff two-and-a-half years ago . <p> She was based in Marlborough as the court and environment reporter and enjoyed writing about topics including climate change and marine protection in the Marlborough Sounds . <p> " I 'm excited to learn about and listen to the issues that are important to people in Tairawhiti , " she said . <p> " I 'm particularly interested to come at a time when Gisborne District Council has just committed to its anti-racism journey . " <p> Alice spent many years working with a not-for-profit organisation which used surfing and the ocean as a platform to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the belief that positive experiences , genuine relationships and greater cross-cultural understanding lead to mutual respect . <p> The Local Democracy Reporting ( LDR ) programme is a free-access , public-interest news service dedicated to strengthening coverage of local authorities , runanga and other similar organisations . <p> The Gisborne Herald became one of eight host newsrooms for the service when a trial phase began a year ago . The LDR programme is expanding to at least four additional placements from January to December 2021 . <p> The programme is managed by RNZ with the support of the News Publishers ' Association and NZ On Air . LDR stories are shared with a wide range of media partners .
