<h> Many strains of kumara could be off dinner table by 2070 due to climate change <p> * Source : 1 NEWS <p> Many varieties of kumara could be off the dinner table by 2070 due to climate change , according to new research . <p> Source : 1 NEWS <p> In a new study by the International Potato Centre , published in the journal Nature Climate Change , researchers tested nearly 1973 sweet potato strains , which found that just 132 - or 6.7 per cent - were tolerant to high temperatures . <p> The research suggests the heat-tolerant varities could be used to help the crops adapt to the predicted temperature increases of one to six degrees celsius by 2070 . <p> Plant and food research scientist Dr Steve Lewthwaite told the Science Media Centre that kumara remains an important crop to New Zealand " primarily because it represents a continuation of presence , a co-existence with the first humans to arrive on our shores " . <p> " While there are periods within a sweet potato plant 's life cycle in which it is sensitive to water @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ surplus -- as during its late filling of storage roots -- the sweet potato plant is fairly hardy to surviving drought . As a tropical crop , it is also well adapted to hot conditions . " <p> However , Dr Lewthwaite said current kumara production is " largely reliant on rain-fed systems , so extreme and erratic changes to the seasonal distribution of natural rainfall will be problematic , and may require the industry to adapt in non-conventional ways " . <p> " The research conducted by the International Potato Centre underlines the importance of genetic diversity in acting as a buffer within the intersection of ever-changing physical and biological challenges . "
