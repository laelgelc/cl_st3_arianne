<h> BOPRC to protect Whakatane CBD from floods <p> The floodwall which runs down the middle of the riverbank walkway on Quay Street may be moved to the landward side and raised between 650 to 850 mm . CHARLOTTE JONES / LDR <p> Floodbanks in Whakatane 's town centre are getting a much-needed upgrade to protect people and property against a one-in-100-year flood . <p> Bay of Plenty Regional Council staff presented Project Future Proof to Whakatane district councillors yesterday , which will address seepage issues and upgrade stopbanks and floodwalls surrounding the central business district . <p> The presentation outlined how geotechnical investigations , together with flood data and climate change , have shown the existing flood defences would not protect the town centre in coming decades . <p> Seepage occurs when water makes its way under the stopbank through the sand layer and begins to seep out slowly onto the ground surface on the protected side . <p> In extreme cases , such as occurred in Edgecumbe , ground on the other side can begin to " boil " and the washing-out of the ground underneath leads to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Quay Street apartments in the 2017 flood . <p> " As guardians of the stopbanks we need to stop that seepage , " says Thiel-Lardon . <p> He says the regional council is investigating several measures such as inserting 500mm-wide steel sheets into the Kakahoroa Drive stopbank and the Quay Street floodwall to create an impermeable barrier . <p> This method is used around the world and can be seen in the Kopeopeo Canal near Awakeri . <p> The council was also looking at moving and raising the concrete flood wall along Quay Street , raising the floodbank along Mataatua Reserve and using hundreds of hectares of pasture as water detention basins . <p> None of these options are set in stone . <p> Stopbanks alongside Mataatua Reserve and the reserve itself may be raised as part of the regional council 's work . CHARLOTTE JONES / LDR <p> Councillor Gerard van Beek asked why the regional council is considering sheet piles in the Whakatane stopbanks when the same method was not considered for Edgecumbe . <p> Thiel-Lardon says they are not preferred in Edgecumbe because pressure would build up @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ piles could be inserted and not hit the bedrock . <p> The large metal sheet piles lengthen the seepage path for excess water to then be pumped back to the river . <p> The sheet piles also stop the formation of piping , where water flowing through the flood defence picks up foundation material and can lead to stopbank failure . <p> " This is not about stopping the seepage but rather controlling and slowing it , " says Thiel-Lardon . <p> van Beek says the Wairaka community was not pleased the last time the Mataatua Reserve stopbanks were raised and he wo n't be surprised if there is push back again . <p> He questions whether the stopbanks on both sides of the river will be raised . <p> Thiel-Lardon says if the regional council is to raise the stopbanks , it will also raise the reserve and gradually smooth it to " look nice " rather than like a bund . <p> Stopbanks on the Piripai side of the river might not be raised if other flood protection work is successful . <p> Councillor Wilson James is concerned @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ create water detention basins . <p> Engineering manager Mark Townsend says it will be enhancing land that already suffered from flooding so it could hold more water , thereby alleviating pressure on stopbanks surrounding townships further downriver . <p> " For example , we could create a series of one metre high bunds , once the water enters one it can then cascade into the next . " <p> He says the regional council will complete this work so it looks as natural as possible . <p> It 's consulting with potentially affected landowners now . <p> He says if it is successful , stopbanks might not need to be raised in the CBD . <p> Townsend says regional council will do its " utmost " to protect any settlement , rural or otherwise . <p> The town defence upgrade is one of the community resilience and flood protection projects that the regional council received support for through the Government 's Crown Infrastructure Partners " shovel-ready " fund for projects able to be undertaken in the short to medium term .
