<h> COVID-19 makes a basic meal unaffordable in sub-Saharan Africa <h> Published : Friday 23 October 2020 <p> Sub-Saharan African countries were already off track to meet the Sustainable Development Goals ( SDG ) to end hunger by 2030 , according to the Africa SDG Index and Dashboards Report 2020 . Then the novel coronavirus disease ( COVID-19 ) struck , which may push half of sub-Saharan African population into extreme poverty . <p> Unaffordability of basic food in at least 17 sub-Saharan African countries may fuel the existing crisis . These 17 countries are among the world 's 20 worst-hit countries , and where people spend a significant portion of their income on a basic meal comprising rice and beans , according to a report released by the World Food Programme ( WFP ) titled Cost of Food Plate . <p> The report highlighted that COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated the impact of conflict , climate change and economic crisis . <p> According to the most recent WFP estimates , majority of people suffering acute food insecurity in 2019 were in countries affected by conflict ( 77 million ) , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 24 million people ) . These included people in the Sub-Saharan countries . <p> Daily income spent on food <p> South Sudan fared the worst when it came to affordability : A basic plate of food now costs 186 per cent of a person 's daily income , according to the FAO . <p> The share of daily income spent on food by a person in South Sudan increased by 27 points ; if a resident in New York had to pay the same proportion of their salary for a basic meal , the meal would cost $393 . <p> In 2018 , the cost of food in South Sudan was around 155 per cent of a person 's daily income . <p> According to WFP Executive Director David Beasley : <p> " The new report exposes the destructive impact of conflict , climate change and economic crises , now compounded by COVID-19 , in driving up hunger .
