<h> Where 's the sea ice ? 3 reasons the Arctic freeze is unseasonably late and why it matters <h> Published : Wednesday 28 October 2020 <p> With the setting of the sun and the onset of polar darkness , the Arctic Ocean would normally be crusted with sea ice along the Siberian coast by now . But this year , the water is still open . <p> I 've watched the region 's transformations since the 1980s as an Arctic climate scientist and , since 2008 , as director of the National Snow and Ice Data Center . I can tell you , this is not normal . There 's so much more heat in the ocean now than there used to be that the pattern of autumn ice growth has been completely disrupted . <p> To understand what 's happening to the sea ice this year and why it 's a problem , let 's look back at the summer and into the Arctic Ocean itself . <p> Siberia 's 100-degree summer <p> The summer melt season in the Arctic started early . A Siberian heat wave in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , Russia , for the first time on record and unusual heat extended over much of the Arctic for weeks . <p> The Arctic as a whole this past summer was at its warmest since at least 1979 , when satellite measurements started providing data allowing for full coverage of the Arctic . <p> With that heat , large areas of sea ice melted out early and that melting launched a feedback process : The loss of reflective sea ice exposed dark open ocean , which readily absorbs the sun 's heat , promoting even more ice melt . <p> The Northern Sea Route , along the Russian coast , was essentially free of ice by the middle of July . That may be a dream for shipping interests , but it 's bad news for the rest of the planet . <p> Warmth sneaks in underwater <p> The warm summer is only part of the explanation for this year 's unusual sea ice levels . <p> Streams of warmer water from the Atlantic Ocean flow into the Arctic at the Barents Sea . That heat in the Atlantic water is helping to keep ice from forming and melting existing sea ice from below . <p> It 's a process called " Atlantification " . The ice is now getting hit both from the top by a warming atmosphere and at the bottom by a warming ocean . It 's a real double whammy . <p> While we 're still trying to catch up with all of the processes leading to Atlantification , it 's here and it 's likely to get stronger . <p> Climate change 's assault on sea ice <p> In the background of all of this is global climate change . <p> The Arctic sea ice extent and thickness have been dropping for decades as global temperatures rise . But today , it 's superimposed on the overall thinning of the ice as global temperatures rise . <p> Had the same atmospheric patterns driving this year 's big ice loss off Siberia happened 30 years ago , the impact would have been much less , as the ice was more resilient then and could have taken a punch .
