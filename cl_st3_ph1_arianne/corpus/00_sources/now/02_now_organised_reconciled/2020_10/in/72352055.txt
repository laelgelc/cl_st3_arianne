Abuses of leadership is checked and balanced through the Montesquieu trinity of Executive , Judiciary and Legislature <p> ANDREW SHENG New Delhi October 27 , 2020 12:13 pm <p> Imagine a beautiful beach where families are gathered on a clear day under blue skies . Different families are building sandcastles , whilst some are playing on the beach . No one is watching that a tsunami with different waves is approaching the shore . <p> This is the vivid story told by former UNFCC ( United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change ) Executive Secretary Christiana Figueres . The first wave is the pandemic , followed by economic recession , then financial crisis , and finally the Big Wave of Climate Warming . Why are people and politicians still building sandcastles and looking the other way ? <p> The more intriguing question is : what sandcastles are still being built ? One is called democracy . Another is called freedom . <p> In the next two @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the century . Since the 2008 Great Financial Crisis , many have lamented that democracy has been on the retreat . Electoral democracy is founded on the idea that each society chooses its own leaders through one-citizen-one-vote . Abuses of leadership is checked and balanced through the Montesquieu trinity of Executive , Judiciary and Legislature . But with the emergence of Brexit , European populists and Donald Trump by 2016 , the game became uglier , as political and social views became polarized and wealth and social inequalities widened . <p> Even as land and stock market prices rose when interest rates went near zero , politicians sleepwalked through the seismic shift in social conditions as the bottom half of society became insecure from loss of jobs to technology , globalization and rising levels of immigration . Centrist moderates were seen by both extreme left and right as having sold out to the powerful , and politics became more rigged , fractious and contested . <p> Will building the democracy sandcastle better at this point of time stop the tsunami crashing down on all of us ? Surely the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the tsunami comes , all sandcastles will be swept away and there may not even be a beach anymore . But some will survive , and the re-building must begin . <p> This is where the freedom sandcastle plays a critical role . Individual freedom is an anchor of the Western value system , now spread through globalization . But Individual freedom comes with social obligations . The pandemic posed this dilemma in sharp focus . Many Americans and British felt that wearing a mask impinges on their individual freedom , but not doing so and not following social distancing rules have cost the two societies over a quarter million deaths so far , and rising . <p> On the assumption that each life costs $10 million , the cost to the two societies on the day of writing is $2.2 trillion and $447 billion respectively . Larry Summers has calculated that the total cost of this pandemic so far is $16 trillion for the US economy , or nearly three-quarters of its 2019 GDP . <p> We can not blame democracy or freedom for such loss of life . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Kong , Singapore , Malaysia , Thailand , Vietnam ( combined 2,552 deaths as of 21 October ) have shown that different systems can produce better pandemic management results than say , Sweden ( 5,929 deaths ) with 39 times larger population . <p> We have not yet beaten Covid19 , as the virus is mutating as it spreads . Vaccines may be coming , but their usage will not be widespread till next year . Winter is coming and death rates will increase as the seasonal influenza strikes . More than ever , we should be prepared when the second and third waves of economic distress , unemployment , bankruptcies ( all temporarily covered up by subsidies and debt forgiveness ) surface by spring next year . <p> So why are we still building sandcastles as the waves crash down ? <p> All this boils down to human psychology and reaction to unknowns . Depending on experience and knowledge , as the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami showed , some may still wander down to the beach to figure out what is going on . There were 227,898 deaths then @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Many were brave enough to save others . Others stayed to help many flee . <p> Clearly those who deny the tsunami have an education problem , which simply boils down to a moral issue . Is your individual freedom so important that you deny that this could put your loved ones and many other people at risk ? It is not just a health issue , but the damage to the economy will be even more devastating . Central banks printing money can not stem the losses , because they do not address structural issues exposed by the pandemic . Denial , blaming others , sheer incompetence and unwillingness to work with each other at all levels of society in the face of disaster have been hallmarks of those economies most devastated by the pandemic . All these show that whilst the science-based approach is validated by the health crisis , human beings do not decide by facts alone . Our economic models based on dollars and cents can not measure the agony of the masses when they lose their jobs , health and dignity . At the heart @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ you care only for yourself , or do you care more about other people and Mother Nature ? This is why the US elections is ultimately a moral test , not a democratic or freedom issue . This election could be fair , rigged or legally contested . Irrespective of who wins , and whether the election will result in a constitutional cr isis requir ing Supreme Court decisions , the test is how individuals involved : the voter , Presidential candidates , Supreme Court judges or the Attorney General decide on their personal sense of moral values . Their decisions in the coming days will have far reaching consequences for themselves and the rest of us . <p> We will soon know whether we have four more years of shambolic chaos or perhaps a period of healing after the pandemic . God bless America and pray for us all . <p> The writer is a former central banker and financial regulator . The views are entirely his own .
