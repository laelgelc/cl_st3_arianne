<h> The pros and cons of electric fans <p> I once travelled to Amsterdam via Britain for a conference . The threat of global warming was n't front and centre back then ; even so , I had never experienced such warm weather in the UK . I stayed with a friend in an old mansion house . Homes in the Old Country have radiators , not air-conditioning . I opened the casement windows , which swing up , which means the lower end was out and the upper end in . This made it impossible to draw the curtains . The night was full of blazing sirens from the adjoining St Mary 's Hospital ambulances , and the early sunrise made it impossible for me to sleep . I realised the next day that what I needed was a ceiling fan . <p> After a few days I flew to Amsterdam , which was also experiencing a heatwave . Luckily , I could get accommodated in an airconditioned room . I politely inquired with the young lady at the reception whether requests for air-conditioned rooms was common . Fans do not actually cool the air . They create a wind-chill effect , which helps evaporate sweat and cools the body . Above a certain room temperature , fans may actually cause your body to gain heat . <p> Even though fans cool our body , they also dry the skin . This can be offset by using a moisturiser . They also dry the nasal passage and the mouth , especially if you are a mouth breather . The constant hum of the fan helps turn down the noise of traffic and car alarms outside and promotes sleep . <p> Sleeping with the fan on can occasionally cause muscle soreness . Though I have practised a long time , I have only come across one person who complains about ceiling fans : My wife . She gets violently ill with fever if she sleeps under a fan .
