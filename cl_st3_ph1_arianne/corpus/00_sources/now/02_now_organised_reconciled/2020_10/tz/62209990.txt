<h> In Africa , farmers learn new methods for facing drought , floods <p> IN the sweltering mid-October heat of Matobo , one of Zimbabwe 's hottest and driest districts , Spiwe Moyo tended her ripening tomato crop . Nearby , underneath a baobab tree , a few emaciated donkeys and a small herd of skinny cattle take shelter from the blazing sun . <p> By Tawanda Karombo , Bulawayo <p> Along with the onions , vegetables and green beans grown by other communal farmers as part of the Evergreen Community Market Garden , Moyo 's tomato crop is a virtual oasis of green , surrounded by bare red soil that receives little shade from the sparse leaves of the mopani trees and a few patches of dry grass long desolated by the high temperatures . <p> Despite the punishing heat , unfriendly surroundings and daily struggles for water for humans and domestic animals , she beamed a smile when she spoke of the prospects for her crop , which will ripen in the next week or so . <p> " I am just weeding out the crop and inspecting @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ weather , crops can suddenly suffer diseases or pest attacks .
