<p> LAUNCESTON , Australia , Oct 22 ( Reuters ) - Glencore wants to keep its coal mines but run them down over time , believing that this will be a better outcome for the climate than selling them to another operator or spinning them into a new company . <p> While it 's arguable that this is actually a better outcome for the environment , it 's also possible that Glencore is setting itself up for a conflict with shareholders , both existing and potential , who are likely to demand firmer action on coal than a long goodbye , even if an extended exit is more profitable . <p> Ivan Glasenberg , the chief executive of the diversified miner , told the Financial Times Mining Summit last week that Glencore is not planning to replace its coal mines as the reserves are exhausted . <p> Glencore is the world 's largest coal exporter and pledged in February last year that it would cap output at its then annual capacity of 145 million tonnes . <p> But Glasenberg appears in no rush to join his global peers in selling @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to reduce Scope 3 carbon emissions , which are created when the fuel is burned by the end user . <p> " I do n't see how spinning off coal mines will help us reduce Scope 3 emissions , " Glasenberg told the summit . <p> He may be correct that a better outcome for the environment is for Glencore to maintain ownership of its coal mines and gradually close them over time , rather than selling them to a less scrupulous investor who may seek to extend their lifespan . <p> But investors are increasingly likely to hold a different opinion , given the growing focus on environmental , social and governance ( ESG ) issues . <p> Hardly a day goes by without pension funds , investment companies , banks or development financiers announcing their withdrawal from coal investments . <p> While other fossil fuels such as oil and gas are also called out , coal is still the primary target for any group aiming to boost ESG credentials . <p> A recent example is the decision by Japan 's Mitsui &amp; Co to sell its remaining stakes @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to natural gas to meet a 2050 target of net zero emissions . <p> Another example is the move by a group of investors , managing a combined $5 trillion , to lower their portfolio carbon emissions by as much as 29% over the next five years . <p> The Net-Zero Asset Owner Alliance , a group that includes the biggest U.S. pension scheme CalPERs and German insurer Allianz , aims to align their portfolios with the 2015 Paris Agreement on climate change , Reuters reported on Oct. 13 . <h> COAL ON THE OUTER <p> The trend against investing in coal has been accelerating in recent years and Glencore is likely to find itself swimming increasingly against the tide . <p> Glencore also has a solid renewable energy story to tell , being a major producer of the metals needed for batteries and electrical systems that promote the use of non-fossil sources on energy . <p> The company is the world 's top producer of cobalt and is also a major miner of nickel and copper . <p> However , Glencore may find it hard to sell its role @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of coal is hanging around its neck . <p> Global peers such as Rio Tinto , Anglo American and others have already sold their coal assets , or have announced plans to do so . <p> While it 's true that merely selling the assets does nothing to cut the volume of coal being produced , it does allow these companies to market themselves as part of the solution to climate change , rather than part of the problem . <p> It could also be argued that Glencore 's share price has underperformed relative to its sector competitors , although there are other factors at work beyond ESG concerns . <p> However , Glencore 's shares are down 29.4% in London so far this year , while Anglo American has slipped 10.2% and Rio is actually up 1.9% . <p> Glasenberg faces something of a paradox in that Glencore keeping its coal mines may be better in the long-term for the environment , but by doing so his company may be punished by those seeking to protect the same environment .
