<p> With offshore winds blowing hot air from inland valleys toward the coast , San Francisco has seen above-normal temperatures in recent days . <p> The city was downright balmy last week , reaching the low 90s for three days straight , Thursday to Saturday . Going back through historical records more than 100 years , this has only happened 10 other times , and Golden Gate Weather Services forecaster Jan Null calls it a once-in-a-decade event . <p> But an even more impressive temperature record occurred Friday when the lowest temperature of the day was 69 degrees , the normal high of the day . <p> This shattered the 1892 record high minimum for the date that was previously 64 degrees , Null shared on Twitter . <p> " It 's not often we see a record that is 128 years old get broken , and it got broken by 5 degrees , and that is impressive in itself , " Null said . <p> After hitting a high of 90 degrees Thursday afternoon , San Francisco dipped into the 70s overnight and stayed in the 70s early Friday @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a.m . Friday . <p> During a heat wave , overnight low temperatures are important because they offer people a break from the baking sun . Without the heat relief , heat stress builds and increases the risk of heat illnesses and death . <p> Research has shown that with climate change , nighttime temperatures are rising faster than daytime temperatures across the globe . <p> " The way we cool off at night is on clear nights the radiation escapes out to space and things cool down , " Null said . " When we have more carbon dioxide in the air , that absorbs some of the heat that would normally go out into space and that keeps the overnight temperatures slightly warmer .
