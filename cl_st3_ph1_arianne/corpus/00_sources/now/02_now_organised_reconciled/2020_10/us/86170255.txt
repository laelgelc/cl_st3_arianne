Recorded sightings increased dramatically in the 1980s and 1990s -- possibly due to more public interest in conservation , and better reporting schemes . Numbers have dropped since 2000 , but the reasons for this are unclear . <p> " Lots of factors could affect the changing of numbers of sea turtles sighted , " said Zara Botterell , of the University of Exeter and Plymouth Marine Laboratory . Leatherbacks are thought to be the only sea turtle species that " intentionally " visits these waters , with adults arriving in summer in search of their jellyfish prey . <p> Meanwhile , juvenile loggerheads ( 240 since 1910 ) and Kemp 's ridley turtles ( 61 ) are more often spotted in winter -- likely carried on currents and finding themselves stranded in cold waters . <p> There are seven sea turtle species in total , and the others are much rarer in UK and Irish waters .
