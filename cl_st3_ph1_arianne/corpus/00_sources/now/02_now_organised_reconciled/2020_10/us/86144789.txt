<h> WHO Director-General 's opening remarks at Friends of Europe festival of politics and ideas to transform Europe <p> Good morning , and thank you for the opportunity to share a few reflections with you today . <p> The COVID-19 pandemic is radiating shock waves across countries , their populations and economies . <p> As you know , Europe is now seeing an increase in reported cases . More cases have been reported in each of the past 6 weeks than at the height of the first peak in March . <p> Countries at all income levels are affected , and the rapid emergence and spread of the virus has revealed critical gaps in national health systems ' capacities . <p> The pandemic has disrupted economies and overwhelmed even some of the most advanced health systems , while some of the measures taken to respond to it , such as so-called lockdowns , have badly affected many people 's ability to earn their livelihood . <p> Even as we use every tool at our disposal to end the pandemic , we must all use this period of crisis as an @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ must do differently . <p> Today I would like to suggest three fundamental shifts that WHO believes all countries must make . <p> First , invest in public health . <p> In recent years , many countries have made huge investments and advances in medicine , but too many have neglected their basic public health systems , which are the bedrock for preventing , preparing for , detecting and responding to outbreaks . <p> In other words , too many countries have prioritised the medicalisation of their health systems at the expense of the basic measures that prevent people getting sick in the first place . <p> WHO believes that the single most important action all countries can take is to invest in essential public health functions . <p> This includes immunization , surveillance , laboratory capacity , infection prevention and control , health promotion , vector control and improved water and sanitation that can help us be better prepared . <p> It also includes essential governance functions , such as regulation to ensure quality and accessibility , and taxation to raise revenue . <p> These common goods for health @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ regularly underutilized . <p> Indeed , many aspects of the current crisis can be attributed to disinvestment in core public health functions , as well as short-sighted social , economic and environmental policies . <p> Second , take a One Health approach . <p> The pandemic has highlighted the intimate links between the health of humans , animals and planet , which we can only address with a One Health approach . <p> Any efforts to improve human health are doomed unless they address the critical interface between humans and animals , and the existential threat of climate change that is making our earth less habitable . <p> WHO recently published our " Manifesto for a Healthy and Green Recovery " , with policy prescriptions for protecting natural resources , investing in water and sanitation , renewable energy , healthy food systems , and liveable cities . <p> It includes more than 80 specific actions for governments at all levels . <p> Third , go beyond the health sector . <p> The title of this session is rethinking health systems . I respectfully suggest that is far too narrow . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ only on health systems . <p> The pandemic has affected every sector , so it stands to reason that every sector must be involved in the recovery , and in preparedness . <p> We must also address the reasons get people sick and die , in the air they breathe , the food they eat , the water they drink , and the conditions in which they live and work . <p> Just as the pandemic requires an all-of-government , all-of-society response , so does promoting and protecting health . <p> Invest in public health . Take a One Health Approach . And go beyond the health sector . <p> These are the fundamental shifts I believe our world must make , both to safeguard against future health emergencies , and to promote and protect the health of everyone , everywhere .
