Indeed , the plan has been widely hailed for making environmental and climate justice concerns a top priority . <p> But without diminishing the urgent need for real , actionable legislation that addresses environmental and climate injustices , it is also crucial to ensure that the plan 's good intentions do not backfire on the very people it is meant to benefit . <p> Advertisement <p> Biden 's plan promises that disadvantaged communities will receive 40% of the benefits from federal spending in key areas ( including clean energy , sustainable housing , workforce development , pollution remediation and clean water infrastructure ) . Such measure have coincided with expanding real estate markets and a reshuffling of urban space . A growing body of research documents that investments in urban greening tend to target neighborhoods that are primed for redevelopment . <p> Advertisement <p> In New York City , for example , industrial waterfronts have transformed into high-priced , green urban oases , all in the name of sustainability . From Williamsburg and Greenpoint to Gowanus to Long Island City , and the Far West Side of Manhattan , waterfront parks , greenways , brownfield cleanups and other eco-friendly initiatives have all accompanied dramatic spikes in property values and rent . These neighborhoods then become less affordable , often displacing large portions of the " disadvantaged communities " living in them . <p> As New Yorkers well know , urban space is hardly static .
