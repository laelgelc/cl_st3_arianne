<h> The centerpiece of Jewish prayer is a warning that actions have consequences . <p> I 've always felt we recite the Shema three times a day as a reminder that actions have consequences . <p> After the famous declaration of God 's singularity , the Shema tells us that if we obey God 's commandments , God will grant us rain for our crops and grass for our cattle . If not , if we serve other gods , the sky will dry up and the ground will be infertile . <p> I 'm not a farmer , and I am skeptical of the theological certainty of reward and punishment . I join those who regard the passage as a metaphor for moral ecology : We have been shown what it means to act justly , consume wisely and treat our fellow creatures with dignity , which are the goals of any moral code .
