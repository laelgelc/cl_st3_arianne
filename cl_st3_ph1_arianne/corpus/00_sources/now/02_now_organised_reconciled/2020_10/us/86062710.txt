<h> In Full Bloom : AskHRGreen is making mini-grants available for environmental education <p> By Allissa Bunner <p> Virginian-Pilot Correspondent <p> Oct 03 , 2020 at 7:15 AM <p> On the cusp of serious climate change repercussions , environmental education has never been as important as it is in 2020 . Beyond the politics of policy , students need to be called forward to help address local environmental issues to foster responsible citizenship in the future . <p> AskHRGreen has been supporting environmental education throughout southeastern Virginia through their Green Learning Guides , which were featured in my column on May 9 . The free online study guides have been created to give third and sixth grade students an interactive learning opportunity centered around the environment of the immediate area . The guides include lessons for concepts like pollution prevention , stormwater and wastewater management , recycling and more . Developed by public works experts , the easy to incorporate curricula work in tandem with the accompanying teacher 's guides and to adhere to Virginia 's Standards of Learning . <p> Advertisement <p> Expanding on the effort to bolster local @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ classroom projects tied to environmental topics . Information about the grant application process is available on the askHRGreen website , along with examples of nine projects that meet the requirements of funding . Rebekah Eastep , education planner for Hampton Roads District Commission , highlighted the fact the mini-grants are open to any nonprofits , scout groups , faith based organizations -- anyone working with school-aged students is eligible and with funding available year round . There is no deadline to apply . <p> " ( The grants ) are an important resource that teachers and youth leaders in Hampton Roads can use to bring environmental themes to life for young learners in ways that not only foster an appreciation of the natural world , but also make positive environmental improvements in their community , " Eastep said . <p> Advertisement <p> Some projects proposed for this spring were postponed , but both Hampton and Portsmouth will be moving forward with their projects this fall . The Elizabeth River Project 's Resilient River Star Schools program at Victory Elementary School was awarded the grant to give their schoolyard a sustainable @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ flooding . Victory will plant 100 native trees and 500 native ( and flood-tolerant ) plants . The project will address future flooding , provide habitat and provide future learning opportunities for the student body and nearby community . Partnership for a New Phoebus was also awarded the grant and will use the funds for their Adopt-a-Pot program , which will replant existing city street planters with pollinator plants . <p> Like so many other aspects of pandemic life , the grant program has experienced a sharp decline of applicants with so few schools meeting in person .
