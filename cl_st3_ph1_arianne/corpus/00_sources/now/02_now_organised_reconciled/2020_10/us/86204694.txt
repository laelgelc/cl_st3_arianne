<h> Rising temps will disproportionately affect Kansas farmers <p> WICHITA , Kan . ( AP ) -- Growing corn and soybeans will become increasingly challenging for Kansas farmers as the climate warms , according to a recent Kansas State University study . <p> The study found that drought and heat are currently the biggest reason for crop yield losses and expect that these losses will become more common because of climate change , The Wichita Eagle reports . <p> If temperatures rise another 1.8 degrees Fahrenheit , risks to crop yields will increase by 32% for corn and 11% for soybeans , according to the study .
