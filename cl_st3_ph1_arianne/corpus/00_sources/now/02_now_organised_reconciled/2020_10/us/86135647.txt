<h> We need to pick a leader . Not the winner of a reality TV show . Opinion <p> Updated Oct 13 , 2020 ; Posted Oct 13 , 2020 <p> As the countdown to the election continues , the time is short to think clearly about what is at stake . This is a national election , not a reality TV show or a contested sports event . It is not a business deal , and it should certainly not be a test to see who can hurl the nastiest personal insults , says Brent Ruben , a distinguished communications professor in the Rutgers School of Communication and Information and a senior university fellow at the Rutgers Center for Organizational Leadership . <p> We hear many comments that this is the most important election in memorable history , and this may well be true . In addition to being a time when Americans vigorously express their partisan views and preferences for particular candidates , 2020 is also -- perhaps more fundamentally -- a time to focus on core values and leadership practice . It is a time to affirm @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , rather than crafting messages for convenience or self-promotion ; <p> Advocate and practice civil discourse , not brashness and bullying ; <p> Preserve constitutional commitments to a free press , instead of disparaging critics and privileging only partisan reports , reporters or media ; <p> Promote unity , not division and divisiveness ; <p> Respect religious views of all kinds , not only those of one 's base ; <p> Honor , appreciate and listen to the advice of those who have dedicated their lives to science , public service and military service , regardless of their politics ; <p> Recognize the value and necessity of treating all individuals and groups with respect , rather than adopting rhetoric that inflames and stereotypes , feeding fears and insecurities ; <p> Encourage and enable citizens to vote , regardless of potential benefits to one 's own campaign ; <p> Assure citizens that you will comply with the result of the election , not threaten to challenge and refuse to comply with an outcome one does not like <p> Perhaps most importantly , we need leaders who are not intimidated by differences @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ who welcome differences and who have the insight , self-confidence and skills needed to weave various perspectives together to create the best possible solutions . <p> Research on effective leadership within complex organizational settings demonstrates quite clearly that outstanding leaders do not need to be experts in every technical or policy area -- in fact , they can not reasonably be expected to have such mastery . What they do need , however , are the personal , analytic , managerial and communication competencies necessary to create and benefit from a diverse team of knowledgeable and experienced advisors who collectively have the needed technical and policy expertise . <p> What exemplary leaders do n't do is to surround themselves with individuals selected because they are always ready to agree with a leader 's instincts , and are unable or unwilling to differentiate fact from fiction in the unwavering support they provide . Moreover , we need leaders who do not define loyalty to themselves personally as being nearly as important as loyalty to the broader and longer-term needs of the multiple and diverse communities they were selected to serve and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ continues , the time is short to think clearly about what is at stake . This is a national election , not a reality TV show or a contested sports event . It is not a business deal , and it should certainly not be a test to see who can hurl the nastiest personal insults . Moreover , it is not solely a battle of opposing political strategists , one-liners or false-to-fact claims about the magical powers or accomplishments of a candidate . What is at stake is also not simply the personal financial and social comfort we may enjoy as individuals at a particular moment in time . <p> Our 20:20 vision as voters has never been more critical . There is not much time left to wipe the sleep from our eyes , to clean our glasses or contacts -- or buy new ones -- and to sharpen our focus on the future . This election will be a decision about the kind of country we want to live in , the values to which we are dedicated and the kind of leadership needed at all levels @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is about selecting leaders who understand and advocate these values for others and , most importantly , who embody them in their own lives . The weather will get colder , and let 's do what we can to ensure that the change in weather will be accompanied by a leadership climate change as well . And one day , like a miracle , toxic leaders will be gone . <p> Brent Ruben , Ph.D . is a distinguished professor in the Rutgers School of Communication and Information 's department of communication , and a senior university fellow at the Rutgers Center for Organizational Leadership .
