<h> Australia is full of lizards , so I went bush to find out why <p> Though it may not be as famous a stereotype as shrimps on the barbie , deadly snakes or Vegemite , Australia is renowned in certain scientific circles for being the " land of the lizards . " <p> Australia has a higher diversity of lizards than anywhere else in the world . The number of different species within a single part of remote , central Australia well exceeds similar desert environments , such as the Kalahari in Africa , or the US . <p> Over the last 50 years , scientists have tried to understand the cause of this extraordinary and unique diversity . <p> Some suggest unpredictable resources in the arid outback , such as sporadic rain , favor low-energy animals like lizards over birds and mammals . Others claim a high diversity of termites allows lots of different termite-eating lizards to co-exist . <p> Or perhaps the presence of shrubs , sparse trees and grass clumps provide a variety of niches ( microhabitats ) for tree and litter dwelling species . Understanding how and why lizards use this iconic outback plant can help us conserve them , by predicting how they might respond to disturbances such as habitat loss and climate change . <p> Following many trips to the outback , I was surprised to find locals who had never encountered some of the species I was studying . Taking photographs of these often small and overlooked animals helps me to better engage the community and raise the wider public profile of lizards , compared to other , more " charismatic " native animals . <p> Spinifex typically forms a spiky and impenetrable clump that provides useful , and in some cases essential , resources to lizards , birds , mammals and invertebrates . <p> But despite the close association of many lizard species to spinifex , we still do n't know @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ideas dominate .
