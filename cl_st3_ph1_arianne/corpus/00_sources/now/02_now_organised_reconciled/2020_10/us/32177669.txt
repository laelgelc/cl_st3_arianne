<p> The exhibition , which opens on Thursday , has brought together the largest and most wide-ranging collection of objects from some of the northern-most regions of the world to be displayed in Britain . It focuses not just on details of ancient traditions but on the impact of political and climate change . <p> Alongside other archeological finds and historic artefacts , such as an antique sled from Greenland and a bronze Evenki spirit mask , the public will have the first chance to see a newly-complete mammoth tusk model , originally made almost 200 years ago and depicting an ancient annual summer festival known as " yhyakh " . <p> Fedor Markov with a rare mammoth tusk in his studio . " <p> The semi-nomadic community of Sakha , who call themselves " people of the sun " , and are also known as Yakuts , are a Turkic ethnic group who still celebrate two key religious festivals each year , both revolving around fermented mare 's milk , or koumiss . <p> " The ceremony 's name can be translated as " sprinkling " , because that 's what the shaman does with the mare 's milk , " explained Lincoln . " It is a way of calling for good weather that will lead to a good harvest . The 24-hour sun at that time of year in the arctic means that if the weather is good there can be an abundance of hay that would take them all the way through the winter . So it was a key moment in their year .
