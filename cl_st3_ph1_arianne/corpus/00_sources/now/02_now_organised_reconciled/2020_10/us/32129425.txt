<p> Europe is getting tired of trying to pry open the doors of China 's market . <p> At a summit yesterday ( Oct. 1 ) , with one minute left to midnight , European Union leaders released a statement calling for a fairer and more reciprocal economic relationship with the world 's second largest economy . They also made clear that it 's crunch time for negotiations over an investment agreement eight years in the making that will address some of the EU 's concerns around market access for its companies in China and transparency surrounding Chinese state-owned enterprises , among other things . But experts warn the deal is looking less and less likely to succeed . <h> EU-China relations and the CAI <p> The 27-country bloc is China 's largest trade partner , while the Chinese market is the second largest destination for EU goods and services after the US . And yet the economic relationship between Brussels and Beijing is not well regulated . That 's partly because it has grown quickly , outpacing their ability to legislate around it .
