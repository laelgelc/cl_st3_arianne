<p> The views expressed by Opinion contributors are their own and not the views of Changing America or The Hill . <h> The importance of minority health and environmental health justice in climate action <p> Environmental justice was mentioned in last night 's presidential debate . <p> By <p> Rohan Arora <p> , Opinion Contributor <p> Istock <p> When I was in elementary school , I distinctly remember one day when my dad came back home from his long day of work and went straight to bed . It was n't something out of the ordinary ; I thought he was likely just tired from work . Yet , while I was sitting on our family couch watching an episode of Cyberchase on our TV , I suddenly heard coughing . A lot of coughing . <p> My mom , who was cooking dinner at the time , yelled at me to go check on my dad . I raced to my parent 's bedroom , and I saw something was extremely wrong . <p> My dad has always had " weak lungs , " but today was unlike any @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . I was just a young boy at the time , but my father was able to muster enough air to ask me to grab his inhaler from the family room . I raced downstairs , and luckily , I was able to give him his inhaler in time . That memory , which started off as a regular evening in the Arora household , instilled a sense of fear that I 've been carrying to this day . <p> His asthma attacks became more and more frequent , and his ability to breathe , something most of us take for granted , became increasingly compromised . And , this is the reality for the more than 25 million Americans who suffer from asthma . <p> Although the American Lung Association emphasizes that asthma is often worsened by air pollution , especially for minorities , my dad has never pointed the blame for his worsening asthma to climate change ; in fact , he , like many immigrants , has been so laser-focused on " making it " in the United States that he says he does n't pay much @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ it 's not worth making a fuss over because no one cares about what minorities have to say . And , this sense of forced " immigrant helplessness " is something that motivated me to enter the activism space . <p> But , even after years of fighting for social justice , I froze last night . During the third and final presidential debate , Kristen Welker asked both candidates why Texan families that are becoming sick from oil refineries ' emissions should give each candidate another term in office . Although extremely sad to hear , the sentiment felt by these Texan families is not all that new . In fact , along Louisiana 's " Cancer Alley , " an 85-mile stretch of the Mississippi River lined with oil refineries and petrochemical plants , Americans are 50 times more likely to get cancer . In fact , this line of 150 facilities is the second-biggest producer of petrochemicals in the U.S. , after Texas . These areas represent the epitome of environmental racism that leaves minorities with only three options : move away , get sick , or @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a financially viable option . Failures in urban and regional planning , inaction from our leaders , and the money-minded nature of the fossil-fuel industry has killed Black and Brown communities for years . <p> During that moment when Welker asked that question , flashbacks of my father gasping for air running to get his inhaler flooded my mind . Environmental issues often manifest into medical issues , issues that concern our very survival . <p> The response to this question by President Trump was simple deflection . He began to discuss how the Texan families that are getting sick due to the oil refineries are " employed heavily and making a lot of money . " Now , I 'm not an economist , so I ca n't speak to the financial bearing of what President Trump was referring to ; but , no amount of money can and should ever be equated to the cost of a human life . These people that are living near chemical plants may be currently earning more as he claims , but in the high likelihood that they get sick from the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ with a fatal disease for years . <p> By his dismissal of environmental health injustice , I was left once again questioning our government 's commitment to minority health as a whole , especially in the realm of environmental health justice . For years , our institutions and political leaders have allowed for the continued mistreatment of minorities , especially Black and Brown communities . For centuries , our institutions and leaders act under the hope that those that are poor and that may be from minority backgrounds ca n't and wo n't speak up . <p> You may be conservative , liberal , progressive , or have any other political leaning ; yet , the truth stands . Environmental health instability is something that has plagued our nation for years ; however , our disregard to at least acknowledge the unequal burden environmental issues have is disrespectful to every American . We , as Americans , must start demanding action from our political institutions on finally addressing environmental health injustice . Too many minorities have suffered the ramifications of unhealthy environments on their health . <p> Political and institutional @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ will likely continue to prevail for years to come . The only option we have today is to work within the system and take our passion to the ballot box on November 3rd . I implore people to do their research on each of the candidates and the commitment they place on environmental health action when deciding who to elect on election day . <p> If we do n't take environmental health action , we 'll be continuing to hurt Americans and reaffirming the notion that our leaders do n't care about minority health . <p> Rohan Arora is a climate activist focused on environmental health and minority experience .
