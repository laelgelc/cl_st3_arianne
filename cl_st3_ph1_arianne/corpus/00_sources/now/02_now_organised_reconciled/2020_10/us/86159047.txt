<h> A firefighter battling the Creek Fire in Shaver Lake , Calif. , in September . <h> Jennifer Calfas <h> Jim Carlton <p> The Trump administration reversed its decision refusing California Gov . Gavin Newsom 's request for relief funds following major wildfires that sparked in early September and have since burned through nearly 1.9 million acres , the governor announced . <p> The Democratic governor made the disclosure in a statement Friday afternoon , saying President Trump approved his request for a major disaster declaration after the two spoke on the telephone moments earlier . " Grateful for his quick response , " Mr. Newsom said . <p> Officials from the Federal Emergency Management Agency , which state officials said notified them Thursday they were turning down the request , were not immediately available for comment on the governor 's announcement . <p> " The governor and House Minority Leader McCarthy spoke and presented a convincing case and additional on-the-ground perspective for reconsideration , leading the president to approve the declaration , " said White House spokesman Judd Deere . <p> The turnabout is another example of the unusual relationship between the governor and President Trump , who despite often fiery rhetoric toward one another often cooperate on assistance for California 's frequent disasters in recent years . Mr. Newsom , in particular , has gone out of his way to thank the president for his help , such as when the commander in chief flew out to Sacramento last month for a briefing from state officials on the fire situation . <p> Earlier Friday , FEMA spokeswoman Lizzie Litzow said that following damage assessments , " the early September fires were not of such severity and magnitude to exceed the combined capabilities of the state , affected local governments , voluntary agencies and other responding federal agencies . " She pointed to other potential targets for relief funding , like assistance through the Small Business Administration . <p> The decision comes during a historic fire season with an unprecedented number of blazes fueled by extreme heat , gusty winds and dry conditions . The state had intended to appeal the rejection and look for other forms of financial assistance for recovery efforts , said Brian Ferguson , a spokesman for the California Governor 's Office of Emergency Services . <p> " We have a strong case that California 's request meets the federal requirements for approval , " he said early Friday . The request would allow for hundreds of millions of dollars in federal support and reimbursement for relief efforts , he added . <p> Global satellite data indicate wildfires are becoming bigger and more intense . Noah Berger/Associated Press <p> Mr. Newsom asked President Trump on Sept. 28 to approve an emergency declaration to help fund relief efforts across seven counties where half a dozen major wildfires ignited last month , forcing thousands of evacuations , destroying close to 1,000 homes and killing three people . <p> One of these blazes is the 344,042-acre Creek Fire burning in the Sierra National Forest , now one of the largest fires in the state 's history . It has destroyed 856 structures , led to 23 injuries and is now 60% contained . <h> The remains of vehicles burned in the Glass Fire in Napa Valley , Calif. , at the end of September . <p> Photo : samuel corum/Agence France-Presse/Getty Images <p> In his letter to Mr. Trump last month , Mr. Newsom said infrastructure damage from these six major fires eclipsed $229 million and noted the state was unable to fund relief efforts on its own . California , reeling from the economic impact of the coronavirus pandemic , has a $54.3 billion projected deficit , the Democratic governor said , compared with a $5.6 billion budget surplus at the beginning of the year . <p> The Trump administration previously approved a presidential major-disaster declaration in August after unusual lightning storms triggered explosive fires in several Northern California counties . <p> Scientists have cited these issues as factors in the destruction , but have also pointed to climate change , dry forests that have fueled fast-moving fires and the construction of homes in high-risk areas that have placed more communities in harm 's way . <p> Wildfires in California have grown more dangerous and destructive in recent years . Record-breaking heat , dry conditions and thunderstorms helped spark and spread an unprecedented number of wildfires this year , six of which are now among the top 20 largest recorded in the state 's history . <p> Fires in California have so far scorched more than 4.1 million acres , destroyed 9,200 structures and led to 31 fatalities this year , according to the state 's Department of Forestry and Fire Protection . <p> On Friday , red-flag warnings were issued in Northern California and parts of Southern California as close to 9,000 firefighters continued to battle 21 actively burning blazes across the state . <p> WSJ opens select articles to reader conversation to promote thoughtful dialogue .
