<h> Why Do We Resist Fact-Checking ? <h> The strange psychology behind when and why we ignore the facts . <p> It 's probably fair to say that we live in an era in which fact-checking would seem to be of paramount importance . Especially with the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic , but even before that , flat-out incorrect information spreads like fire throughout the internet and social media . In recent months , however , we 've heard a lot about how fact-checking " does n't work . " <p> So if fact-checking does n't work , should we stop spending so much time doing it ? Not so fast . Whether or not fact-checking works in particular instances is actually quite nuanced . So when and how does fact-checking work ? And what are the psychological drivers that lead people to resist it ? <p> For starters , it 's important to state one thing : fact-checking is still important , even if it does n't work in every instance . Fact-checking is both often very effective and necessary . What 's more , because they are so vocal , it can seem like there are more of them than there actually are . In these cases , human cognition does not filter out emotions before looking at information and so even fact-checks may not be particularly persuasive on their own . <p> But that still does n't mean fact-checks serve no purpose in these cases , because they may still be effective as part of a multi-faceted intervention . It also seems that graphical information , in the form of charts and other data visualization techniques , can be helpful . <p> Importantly , providing an alternative narrative rather than just a refutation on its own can be particularly salient . Better understanding the psychology that makes people cling to incorrect notions can help us build more effective multi-faceted strategies that include an understanding and appreciation of the importance of facts .
