<h> A firefighter and a landfill manager , who fortunately happened to share one particular hobby , worked together to save the bees . <p> Two Irving city employees -- both amateur beekeepers -- rescued thousands of bees from a dumped sofa at an Irving park . <p> A resident reported the abandoned couch at W.O . Harrington Park last week , according to the city 's fire department . When employees from Irving 's Solid Waste Management Department arrived at the scene , they realized the sofa was home to what they at first thought were thousands of wasps . <p> They called the Irving Fire Department to spray an insecticide on the wasps so the sofa could be safely removed . <p> Firefighter James Bruno , an aspiring beekeeper , realized the wasps were instead bees , an integral part of the ecosystem whose population numbers are rapidly declining due to climate change . <p> Irving 's landfill manager Bill Sangster , also an amateur beekeeper , happened to have a hive box and two beekeeper suits . <p> To extricate the bees , the men cut open @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of honeycomb , the internal structure of a hive . Using a bee brush , they gently scraped the bees -- an estimated 10,000 of them -- into the hive box and relocated them . <p> " This was a happy coincidence that two city employees were trained how to save the bees , " Irving Fire spokesman Jason Reid said . <p> Sarah Bahari .
