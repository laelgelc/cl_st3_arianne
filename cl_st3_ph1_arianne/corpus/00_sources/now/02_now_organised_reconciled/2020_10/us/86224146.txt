<p> According to the United Nations , nearly 9% of the population -- or 690 million people -- currently suffer from hunger . With COVID-19 exacerbating the matter , potentially leaving an additional 83 to 123 million individuals facing hunger in 2020 , now is the time to join the fight for food security . <p> In recognition of World Food Day on October 16 , Kellogg 's virtual event , " Fighting Hunger at Every Table , " featured an esteemed lineup of panelists , ranging from farmers and chefs to business leaders and researchers . All were there to discuss the issues surrounding food insecurity -- such as the impact of COVID-19 and the worsening climate crisis . <p> Hosted by Danielle Nierenberg , president of Food Tank , the event shared the progress being made toward achieving the UN 's mission of eliminating world hunger by 2030 , and what everyone can do to contribute . <p> Watch below to see the full event . <p> Here are summaries and takeaways of each panel : <h> Rise and shine : Understanding the intersection of hunger and climate @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Star of the West Milling Co. and farmer , John Schluckebier Farms ; and Bram Govaerts , interim deputy director general of partnerships and research , director of integrated development program , International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center ( CIMMYT ) , discussed how healthy soil , the backbone of our food system , plays a vital role in mitigating the climate crisis . <p> Understanding the correlation between hunger and climate change is fairly easy : " If we can have happy soil with happy microorganisms , then we have a happy plant , and a happy plant means healthy grain and healthy fruits and vegetables , " Govaerts said .
