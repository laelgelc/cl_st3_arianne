<h> Extinctions linked to new assemblages of species <p> IMAGE : Severe mortality of branching corals may lead to the emergence of novel coral communities at Lady Elliott Island reef on the Great Barrier Reef . view more <p> Credit : K-le Gomez . <p> Scientists have found that as the world undergoes profound environmental change , identifying and protecting ' novel ' communities of species can help prevent extinctions within vulnerable ecosystems . <p> Professor John Pandolfi and Dr Timothy Staples from the ARC Centre of Excellence for Coral Reef Studies at The University of Queensland ( CoralCoE at UQ ) are the lead authors of a new study in Science that looked at how combinations of plankton species changed across the world 's marine ecosystems in the past 66 million years . From this , their team developed a world first method to detect ' novel ' communities of species across all ecosystems . <p> " A novel ecological community is one with combinations of species that are different to any past observations from that site , " Prof Pandolfi said . " <p> " Species in the novel community were also more likely to be new arrivals that had never been observed in the community before . " <p> An example of a modern novel community is the coral reefs of the Caribbean , where the two once dominant species of branching coral are now rare . Those reefs are now home to new , or novel , communities of corals . The loss of the branching corals is due to the impacts of overfishing , changes in water quality , and climate change--resulting in new configurations of coral species within the Caribbean reef communities . The researchers used a database of marine plankton over millions of years , but the methodology was designed to be applied more generally . <p> " We came up with a measure of novelty that can be used with community data from any time scale , organism or ecosystem , so comparative approaches to the study of novelty are now possible , " Dr Staples said . " In this study , we applied our methodology to the past 66 million years , but it would work just as well on much shorter time frames . And after novel communities emerged , subsequent communities were more likely to develop into yet other novel states . <p> He said the pressures that cause communities to become novel in the first place need to be relieved .
