<p> LONDON ( Reuters ) - The rising use of nitrogen-based fertilisers is driving up global emissions of nitrous oxide , a lesser-known greenhouse gas , complicating efforts to limit climate change , scientists reported in a study on Wednesday . <p> Most of the focus in curbing climate-warming gas emissions has focused on the most abundant , carbon dioxide , and one of the most potent , methane , with the fossil fuel industry under pressure to drastically curtail both . <p> But nitrous oxide ( N20 ) , also known as " laughing gas " or simply " nitrous , " has received less attention as a long-lasting warming agent . <p> " There 's much less policy attention on nitrous oxide , and not so many mitigation options , so the emissions just continue sailing on upwards , " study co-author Glen Peters , a climate scientist at the Oslo-based CICERO Center for International Climate Research , told Reuters " It makes meeting climate targets even more challenging . Agriculture accounts for more than half of human-caused N20 emissions . <p> While nitrogen fertilisers have been crucial to boosting crop productivity and improving food security worldwide , they also can cause environmental challenges . Nitrogen in agricultural run-off can feed algae blooms that create coastal dead zones . And in the stratosphere , N2O can break down to form other molecules that destroy the ozone layer protecting the planet from ultraviolet radiation . <p> As a climate pollutant , N2O can linger in the atmosphere for decades , and is far more efficient than CO2 in trapping heat . <p> More efficient use of fertilisers could help bring down emissions , the authors wrote . They also urged efforts to curb deforestation , which can increase the amount of N20 produced by soil bacteria .
