<p> Fracking is a central element in the broader conversation on climate change and a constant source of friction between the oil-and-gas industry and environmentalists . Here is what you need to know about fracking : <p> What is fracking ? <p> Fracking is the technology that allows oil and gas to be extracted from shale rock . Types of fracking have been used for decades , but the term now usually refers to a specific technique called horizontal hydraulic fracturing . <p> Under this technique , drillers bore through thousands of feet of earth to reach the shale . Then they turn their drill bits horizontally , piercing through thousands more feet , probing for oil and gas in a layer of earth often just a few feet from its top to bottom . <p> Production crews then shoot water -- mixed with sand and small amounts of various chemicals -- into that rock at high speed . The force of the impact cracks the rock , and the sand grains stick in these tiny fractures to prop them open . It has also been a major source of new jobs over the last decade and helped make energy prices historically low . <p> The U.S. has become the world 's largest oil and gas producer . And , after decades of importing fuels from around the world , the country is now a net exporter , gaining new power internationally . <p> Why is fracking controversial ? <p> Fracking itself has been a concern because of the chemicals used in the process , and the vast amounts of briny wastewater it produces . That waste has contaminated rivers and freshwater supplies at times from spills or from being dumped without proper treatment . Many companies now recycle that water and use other techniques to limit that contamination . <p> The larger concern is n't really over fracking itself but its bigger consequences .
