<p> A survivor of summer wildfires in Utah is a Mojave desert tortoise that was found by the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources ( DWR ) . <p> Ann Mcluckie , a DWR wildlife biologist , said two interns found the injured tortoise while surveying in the Red Cliffs Desert Reserve in Washington County , Utah . Parts of the reserve were burned in this summer 's Cottonwood Trail Fire . The interns were walking in parallel lines back and forth across the desert looking for dead tortoises when they stumbled across an injured one . <p> The injured tortoise had severe burns on its right leg and a scar on its face , said Mcluckie . She said the tortoise was likely in its burrow during the fire but might have stumbled into hot ash after resurfacing . <p> The injured tortoise was taken to a vet , who prescribed antibiotics and cream for its skin . Tire tracks in the desert often become filled with weeds . <p> The invasive grasses are effective competitors that suck up moisture and nutrients from the soil , said Berry . She said the invasive grasses pose an additional threat to tortoises by displacing the native plants they need to survive . <p> Grass takes more water to metabolize than natural wildflowers . The grasses also displace shrubs that the tortoises depend on for shade , said Berry . <p> Mcluckie said the DWR is trying to restore the habitat and prevent invasive grasses from taking over in the wake of the fires .
