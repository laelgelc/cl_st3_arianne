<h> Farm and Food : Perilous Bounty is a journey well worth taking <p> By Alan Guebert <p> Some books are worth more in your hand and on your shelf than they are as electrons in your e-reader . These books , and their authors , are valued friends and you return to them often for information , advice , and comfort . <p> Two downsizing moves in the last 15 years have pared my library to a few shelves of books I need and a few shelves of books I want . The books I need include dictionaries ; the books I want are works by John McPhee , David Halberstam , Wendell Berry , David McCullough , and Aldo Leopold . <p> These friends tell compelling stories simply and take me to places I did n't know I wanted , or even needed , to go . <p> Tom Philpott 's newly published book , Perilous Bounty : The Looming Collapse of American Farming and How We Can Prevent It , is bucking for a space in that latter group . <p> As its title suggests , Bounty @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Big Ag . Philpott drives the bus from California orchards to Iowa cornfields to corporate suites and he has plenty of company along the way : hardworking farmers , exasperated extension agents , brave ag innovators , and a few corporate CEOs . <p> Almost everywhere he stops , however , he and the experts find compounding evidence that today 's production-focused agriculture is swiftly burning through the very resources , mostly soil and water , it needs to sustain itself and us . <p> The lesson at every stop is obvious : there 's nothing cheap and bountiful about today 's cheap and bountiful food . <p> Philpott , a farmer before establishing himself as a respected ag journalist , highlights how recent technologies like deep well irrigation , genetically modified seeds , and concentrated animal feeding operations ( CAFOs ) have helped to make food plentiful and less costly . <p> At the same time , though , each has cost our natural resources far more and the tab for this extractive food system is already coming due . <p> For example , Philpott begins his journey in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that it will grow anything from almonds to alfalfa if there 's enough water . <p> Therein lies the rub . <p> In a normal year , this 11.5-million-acre , " $49 billion desert-agriculture empire " is a thirsty beast .
