<h> Tipperary author 's novel set in a post-climate change Glen of Aherlow is now in bookshops <p> Reporter : <p> Aileen Hahesy <p> 2 Oct 2020 <p> 0 <p> Harrow The Boys author Paul Whyte from Tipperary Town <p> Tipperary Town writer Paul Whyte 's first novel , the post-climate change thriller -- Harrow The Boys - set in a flooded Glen of Aherlow , is now on the shelves of bookshops nationwide . <p> Harrow The Boys was originally due to be physically published in June but publication date was postponed due to the Covid-19 pandemic and it was instead released for sale online that month . <p> Paul , a native of Pearse Park in Tipperary Town , is delighted with the positive response Harrow The Boys has received so far from readers who purchased the novel online . <p> Paul is overwhelmed by the support his book has received in Tipperary , particularly from local writers groups who have contacted him through Facebook and Twitter . <p> But for him seeing his book on sale in a bookshop is a dream come through . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have it in a book shop . <p> " I just wanted to walk down to Eason 's in Dublin and see it on the shelf , " said the writer , who lives in Ballinteer in Dublin . <p> Harrow The Boys is published by Maverick House . The book 's title is a lyric phrase from the famous 1798 ballad Boolavogue , one of his dad 's favourite songs . <p> Harrow The Boys centres on Ram and two friends who live in a dystopian Ireland of the future where much of the mid-west of the country has been flooded by rising ocean and river waters . <p> They live in a small hillside town called Christ King and they spend their days scavenging and selling the scrap they find amongst the flood waters . <p> During a wade through flooded farmland they uncover a hidden and relatively untouched estate and go to investigate . <p> But the story takes a dark turn when a group of armed men close in on them and the estate 's secrets are slowly revealed . <p> Paul is a father of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ writes in his spare time . <p> He is son of Timmy Whyte and Caroline Baccache , who along with his brother and sister live in Tipperary Town . <p> Planned launch nights for the book in a restaurant in Tipperary Town and in Dubray Books in Grafton Street in Dublin have been postponed due to the Covid-19 situation but Paul hopes to have a local launch in his hometown in the future when conditions are suitable . <p> He has signed 100 copies of his book which are on sale through the Maverick Press website . <p> Harrow The Boys is now on sale in Easons and Dubray book stores . <p> If you have a story or want to send a photo or video to us please contact the Derry Now editorial team on 028 7129 6600 for Derry City stories Or 028 7774 3970 for County Derry stories .
