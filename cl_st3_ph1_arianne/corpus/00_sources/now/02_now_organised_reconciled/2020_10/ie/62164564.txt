<h> Ahead of the curve : COVID-19 and short-termism : finding the right balance <p> Finding a way to meet short-term needs without compromising long-term strategy has never been easy for businesses . The radical uncertainty introduced by COVID-19 has made the task much harder . <p> COVID-19 has reversed the shift away from short-termism <p> Companies should be preparing for new capital allocation demands <p> The early 2000s witnessed a troubling trend of companies over-prioritising short-term thinking . Too many were focusing on immediate needs -- such as meeting quarterly earnings targets -- at the expense of value creation and sustainability . A 2006 study of 400 financial executives conducted by our global association , CFA Institute , revealed this was common practice in 80% of companies . <p> These findings were concerning for two reasons . First , a lack of long-term thinking is interlinked with agency problems . Secondly , short-termism can be highly detrimental financially . Engagement and communications between issuers and investors has improved and there are more conversations about ESG issues . <p> However , the COVID-19 pandemic has altered the business landscape even more radically than the 2008 financial crash by changing patterns of consumption , production and government behaviour .
