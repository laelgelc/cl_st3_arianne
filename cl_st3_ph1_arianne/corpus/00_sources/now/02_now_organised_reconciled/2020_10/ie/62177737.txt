<h> Ban on sale of petrol , diesel cars from 2030 not in new Climate Action Bill <p> The new Climate Action Bill published by the Government does not include a section banning the sale of new petrol and diesel cars from 2030 , nor the importation of second-hand petrol and diesel cars from that date . <p> This is despite a commitment in the Programme for Government that the Climate Action Bill would include such a ban . <p> The Bill sets a target to de-carbonise the economy by 2050 at the latest . <p> A spokesperson said the Government remains committed to introducing a ban on fossil fuel cars despite a section banning the sale of new petrol and diesel cars by 2030 being omitted from the New Climate Action Bill published today . <p> A commitment to ban such sales , and also to ban the importation of second hand fossil fuel cars from 2030 , was included in the National Development Plan , the 2019 Climate Action Plan , and the Programme for Government . <p> EU law however , requires that such a provision would have to be notified to the European Commission in advance of its entry into force , when the legislation remains a proposal . <p> Without such advance notification , there was a risk that the enactment of the other provisions in this priority legislation would have been delayed if the proposed ban on new fossil fuel car sales had been included in the Bill . <p> The Department is currently preparing the necessary specific evidential matters to make a case for derogation under EU law , and will continue to engage with the European Commission and the Attorney General 's Office on these matters . <p> We need your consent to load this rte-player contentWe use rte-player to manage extra content that can set cookies on your device and collect data about your activity . It also represents a very radical departure from Ireland 's past approach to reducing greenhouse gas emissions which saw the country fail to achieve almost any reduction in greenhouse gas emissions despite legally binding emissions targets for 2020 . <p> The Programme for Government committed to the publication of this new Climate Bill within the first 100 days of the new Government - a deadline that was reached earlier this week . <p> Taoiseach Miche ? l Martin said the Climate Action Bill is fundamental to the future of Ireland . <p> Speaking at the launching of the Climate Action Bill this afternoon he said : " unless we act now , with a sustained determination , we may well reach a point of no recovery . " <p> There has been quite a deal of consensus in relation to the direction of climate policy and what should be included in the Bill . <p> Its measures have been informed by the recommendations as set out in the Report of the Citizens ' Assembly on ' How the State Can Make Ireland a Leader in Tackling Climate Change ' and the Oireachtas Joint Committee on Climate Action Report 2019 . <p> There was also agreement in relation to the general direction of climate policy among the three parties to the Programme for Government . <p> The new legal commitment that Ireland will achieve climate neutrality by 2050 will be known as the " national 2050 climate objective " . <p> The Minister for Transport , Eamon Ryan , said the target is clear , by 2015 we are a carbon neutral economy . He said the bill sets out the steps that need to be taken to get there and sets out how we will take each step . <p> Mr Ryan said : " It ca n't be a one Government process , it has to be five or six Governments in a row if this is to work . The Carbon Budgets referred to in the Bill will set limits for the total amount of greenhouse gases that can be emitted in the State within a period of five years . They will cover all greenhouse gases and all sectors including agriculture . <p> There will be a legal requirement for Government to adopt a series of three sequential five-year carbon budgets grouped and considered together . The budgets will be set for the whole economy and incorporate decarbonisation ranges for each sector of the economy . <p> A new and powerful Climate Change Advisory Council will be set up on a statutory basis and it will be the job of this council to propose appropriate carbon budgets . <p> The Bill will also result in the National Climate Change Mitigation Plan being replaced with a new annually updated Climate Action Plan starting next year as well as new National Long-Term Climate Action Strategies . <p> These new Climate Action Plans will provide a roadmap of actions including sectoral actions aligned to the periods of the Carbon Budgets . <p> The Bill also requires all local authorities to produce their own Climate Action Plans detailing their commitments to act to mitigate climate change and to adapt to its effects such as increased flooding and new weather extremes . <p> These Local Authority Climate Action Plans must be completed within 18 months of the enactment of the Bill , and not less than once in every five-year period going forward . <p> An Oireachtas committee will have the opportunity to consider the Government 's recommended Carbon Budgets as part of the approval process for Carbon Budgets . <p> There will be greater accountability arrangements with ministers who will have to account for their performance towards sectoral targets and actions before an Oireachtas committee each year . <p> The 2050 emissions reduction target is referred to in the Bill as the " national 2050 climate objective " . The objective is that Ireland commits to transition to a " climate resilient and climate neutral economy " by the end of the year 2050 . <p> A " climate neutral economy " is defined in the Bill as : " a sustainable economy where greenhouse gas emissions are balanced or exceeded by the removal of emissions . " " Climate neutral " objective is consistent with EU Climate Law approach and means a net zero target for all greenhouse gases .
