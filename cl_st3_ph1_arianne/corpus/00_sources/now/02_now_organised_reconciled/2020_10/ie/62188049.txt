<p> In the UK , MPs have rejected attempts to require imported food to meet domestic legal standards when the UK leaves the EU on 1 January . <p> With campaigners warning that the UK could be forced to accept lower standards in order to finalise a US trade deal , a Lords amendment to the Agriculture Bill would have forced trade deals to meet UK animal welfare and food safety rules . <p> However , MPs voted by 332 votes to 279 to support the government and reject the amendment . <p> Farming minister Victoria Prentis said the government was " absolutely committed to high standards " and that existing laws would safeguard them . <p> Kath Dalmeny , chair of the Future British Standards Coalition , told the Guardian : " It 's dismaying that the government has opposed attempts to put into law its own commitment to maintain British food standards .
