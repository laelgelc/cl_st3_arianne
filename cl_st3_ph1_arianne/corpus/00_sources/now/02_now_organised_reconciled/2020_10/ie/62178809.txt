<h> A water company operating in Hertfordshire has committed to reducing its abstraction of chalk streams in the county . <p> Across its supply area , Affinity will be significantly reducing groundwater abstraction in the Ver , Mimram , Upper Lea and Misbourne catchments by 2024 to leave more water in the environment . <p> Jake Rigg , director of corporate affairs at Affinity Water said , after a visit to Chilterns chalk streams : " Our plan sets out how we will work with our partners to reduce the amount of water we take from chalk streams , invest in these critical natural habitats and bring our communities together to help us in this task . <p> " Sixty-five per cent of the public drinking water supplied in the south-east of England comes from the chalk aquifer , the same aquifer that chalk streams rely on for their flow .
