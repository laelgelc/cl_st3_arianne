<h> Letter to the editor : Irish farming needs a ' net carbon footprint ' <p> I note with dismay that Teagasc are making another foray into the climate change debate ; they are promoting their marginal abatement cost curve ( MACC ) . <p> One of their last ventures into this matter was when the government asked them to measure the carbon footprint of agriculture . This they did very diligently , and scientifically . <p> They devised contraptions to measure the methane from livestock and assessed and measured the most minute of details . <p> All the time , these same scientists were well aware that agriculture , as well as producing CO2 , also sequesters thousands of tonnes of CO2 . <p> When asked why they did not measure the CO2 sequestered by forestry , trees , hedges , grass , heather , etc , to give us a net carbon footprint , they said it was too difficult to measure . <h> Need for net carbon footprint <p> They had no problem tooling up to measure what harm we were doing and no interest in tooling @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ one fell swoop they did untold damage to Irish agriculture , much to the delight of the enemies of farming . <p> They lumbered us with an inaccurate and incorrect carbon footprint . Every other sector gets a net CO2 footprint , but we get a gross . <p> If a householder puts up solar panels , then that householder is allowed offset the benefit of the solar panels against their carbon footprint . This is only correct and fair . <p> However , if a farmer plants 10ac of trees he gets no credit for it . This anomaly has been discussed over the last year and numerous scientists and politicians have said that this situation must be corrected , but nothing has been done . <p> For us all to move forward on this pressing issue of climate change , it is vital that every farm gets a net carbon footprint . <h> Regain trust <p> Teagasc have lost the support and trust of farmers and will not regain it until they fix this mess , which they have created . <p> Advertisement <p> When this is eventually @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ agriculture 's share of the national CO2 output from 33% to approximately 9% . <p> There are farmers in this country that are carbon neutral ; there are farmers that are a net carbon sink . They should be acknowledged as such . <p> This will not suit RT ? as they are gearing up to have farmer bashing as the main sport for the winter . <h> Consumption vs. production <p> Another reason why a net CO2 figure is important is , that sooner or later , if the world is serious about climate action , we are going to move to a measurement of CO2 on a consumption basis , rather than on a production basis . <p> Colm McCarthy recently outlined it very well . When we consume diesel , we are allocated the CO2 figure for that consumption , not Saudi Arabia who produced it . <p> Likewise , when Saudi Arabians consume Kerrygold , they should be allocated the CO2 figure for that consumption , not Ireland who produced it . <p> When this happens everything will have a CO2 footprint -- clothing ; cosmetics @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ etc . This will result in consumers favouring food from the most carbon efficient producers . <p> As Ireland is one of the world 's most carbon efficient producers of meat and dairy , the demand for our products will further increase . <p> If RT ? , An Taisce , and the vegan lobby have their way , our output will be reduced , the slack will be taken up somewhere else , with a much higher carbon footprint and climate change will be exacerbated . <p> RT ? might not like it , but the way we farm in Ireland is not part of the problem -- it is part of the solution .
