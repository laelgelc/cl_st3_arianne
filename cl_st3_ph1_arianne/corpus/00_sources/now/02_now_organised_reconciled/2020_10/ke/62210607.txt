<h> By Savithri Sellapperumage Courtesy The Diplomat <p> The focus on Sri Lanka 's deforestation and the conservation of forests this year can be traced back to widespread discussions about deforestation in Wilpattu National Park back in 2019 . Deforestation in Wanathawillu , Anawilundawa and Sinharaja has been the recent focus of discussion . However , reports of more deforestation and illegal commercial or developmental projects have been reported from many parts of the country . Since then , the topic has become affiliated with politics and certain political figures . The blame game has advanced amid the lack of political authority . <p> The forest cover of Sri Lanka in 2019 was reported to be 16.5 percent , down from 29.7 percent in 2017 . According to Dr. Ravindra Kariyawasam , in 1882 , the country 's forest density was around 82 percent . Between 1990 and 2000 , Sri Lanka lost an average of 26,800 hectares of forest per year , which amounts to an average annual deforestation rate of 1.14 percent .
