But it is clear that by extracting too much sediment out of the world 's rivers , unsustainable sand mining will contribute to bank erosion and shrinkage . <p> This will lead to the loss of agriculture land , houses and infrastructure , including roads , dykes and bridges . <p> The problem is that the benefits of natural sediment flow in rivers are usually ' hidden ' from decisionmakers . We can borrow a leaf from Europe where research has shown that developed economies continue to prosper without resorting to river sand . Its supplies now come from crushed quarry rocks , recycled concrete and marine sand . <p> The demand for sand is increasing and preventing likely damage to rivers will require the construction industry to be weaned off river-sourced sand and gravel , either through the substitution of materials or alterations to building designs and methods so that extraction is reduced to levels that are proven to be sustainable . <p> This type of societal shift is similar to that required to address climate change , and will necessitate changes in the way that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and constructed . <p> There 's need to conduct research into economic incentives or certification schemes that could drive a reduction in the extraction of sand from rivers . <p> Research , including short-term ' rapid ' assessments and longer-term investigations , will help also us to understand how rivers and ecosystems respond to change .
