<h> How to hit those higher emissions-reduction targets <p> European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen just raised the stakes significantly in the EU drive for carbon-neutrality by 2050 , setting much higher targets for emissions reduction over the next decade . <p> In other words , an already tough task just got tougher . Making the Green Deal real will require rethinking the way the EU approaches renewable energy , clean mobility and energy taxation policy . And it will require making an impact not only in the coming decades , but also in the last few months of this year . <p> This is especially true in transport , a sector that has proven stubbornly difficult to decarbonize even with less-ambitious targets and shorter timeframes . EU transport is still overwhelmingly reliant on oil and its emissions are 19 percent higher than in 1990 . When you consider that EU member countries are still struggling to meet their 2020 targets under the Renewable Energy Directive ( RED ) and the Fuel Quality Directive ( FQD ) , you can see the scale of the challenge . <p> The @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ transport has to increase to 24 percent . <p> But making real progress in reducing transport emissions does n't necessarily require throwing out the existing playbook or taking the overly drastic step of including road transport in the EU Emissions Trading System . It requires setting targets and sticking to them -- including for increased energy efficiency , increased renewable energy and the reduction of greenhouse gas ( GHG ) intensity of fuels -- as well as making better use of solutions that work in the short , medium and long-term . Renewable European ethanol has an important role to play in all of those timeframes , including this year . <p> If the EU does n't hit or surpass its 2020 targets it will have an especially difficult time hitting the new 2030 goals . The Commission now estimates that the share of renewable energy in transport has to increase to 24 percent , up from the recently agreed 14 percent objective under RED II , and from the 6 percent the EU is at today .
