<h> Gardening : What gardeners can do to reduce their peat use <h> Gardening : What gardeners can do to reduce their peat use <p> As a charity launches a campaign for peat-free gardening , Hannah Stephenson asks experts about the alternatives . <p> A charity is calling for a complete ban on the use of peat in horticulture , declaring a man-made emergency which it claims will take 1,000 years to rectify . <p> Garden Organic says that the garden industry has done too little too late to meet Defra 's demand to stop retail sales of peat by January 2020 and has launched its ' For Peat 's Sake ' campaign in an effort to push the issue to the top of the political agenda . <p> " Reducing peat use in horticulture is absolutely vital for protecting our planet , " says James Campbell , the charity 's chief executive . <p> " Environmental awareness is once more at the top of the political and personal agenda . " <p> What is peat ? <p> It is a type of soil made up of waterlogged , partially-decomposed plant material which has built up over nearly 10,000 years in wetland habitats . <p> Historically , peat has been used in compost because it 's good at holding water and retaining nutrients . <p> Why should we stop using it ? <p> Campbell explains : " Ninety-five per cent of the UK 's peat bogs are now degraded or completely destroyed , meaning it would take 1,000 years before they could start functioning again . <p> " Peat bogs are actually a hugely important defence against climate change as they are the most efficient land-based store of carbon . Destroying them releases disastrous amounts of carbon dioxide into the atmosphere , having the opposite effect .
