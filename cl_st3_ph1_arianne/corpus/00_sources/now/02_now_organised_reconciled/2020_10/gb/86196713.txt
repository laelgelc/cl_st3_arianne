<p> One man 's trash is another man 's treasure , or , in Burger King 's case , another man 's coffee cup . That could very well be our reality starting next year , when the fast-food chain begins testing reusable containers as part of a larger effort to reduce waste . This initiative includes , but is not limited to , changing cows ' diets to reduce farts ( aka methane emissions ) . But we digress . <p> This new pilot program is a partnership with zero-waste delivery platform Loop , which already works with nearly 300 brands . It offers well-known household products in reusable packaging that customers can then recycle via the company to be cleaned , refilled and shipped out once again . At Burger King , folks will be charged a small deposit for their reusable Whopper box or aforementioned coffee cup ( like the $.25 Aldi charges you for a cart ) and , once the packaging is returned , they 'll receive a refund . Items will be collected , sanitized and used for the next person 's meal . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , though it 's unlikely given Loop 's general practices . Expect the eco-friendly packaging will be rolled out next year at select restaurants in New York , Portland and Tokyo , with more cities planned for later in 2021 .
