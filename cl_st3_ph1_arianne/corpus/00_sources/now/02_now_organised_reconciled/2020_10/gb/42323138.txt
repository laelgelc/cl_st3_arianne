<h> Australian woman suffers headaches caused by tapeworm larvae in her brain <h> The woman had reportedly complained of headaches for the past seven years that would regularly occur two or three times a month <p> A woman in Australia suffering from a week long headache discovered she was nurturing tapeworm larvae in her brain . According to the study , which was published in the American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene on September 21 , the 25-year-old woman was said to have never travelled overseas and is the first local case recorded in Australia . <p> Previous cases of this parasite infection in the country stemmed from immigrants or residents who have returned from travels in regions where such parasitic infections are endemic such as Africa , Latin America and Asia . <p> The woman had reportedly complained of headaches for the past seven years that would regularly occur two or three times a month . But after taking prescribed medications , the aches would go away . A brain surgery was scheduled and upon removing the woman 's supposed tumour , doctors realised the lesion was actually a cyst filled with tapeworm larvae . <p> The woman required no other further treatment after the " cyst ' was removed . <p> The infection , also known as neurocysticercosis , causes neurological related symptoms . Individuals become infected with these parasites when they ingest eggs from faecal contact from a person who has intestinal tapeworm . <p> The US Centres for Disease Control and Prevention ( CDC ) said neurocysticercosis is a deadly infection that is the leading cause of adult epilepsy . Tapeworms are usually found in the intestines which can be undiagnosed as some individuals can get rid of them without medication . <p> The tapeworms are commonly transmitted when people consume undercooked pork , as pigs are intermediary tapeworm hosts .
