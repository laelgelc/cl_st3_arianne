<p> The dams feeding the Western Cape are finally full , but the extra costs associated with the water crisis are still in place . This has unnerved residents of Cape Town who believe they should not still be paying exorbitant costs for water . <p> On September 30 , the Western Cape Department of Water and Sanitation ( DWS ) requested that the City of Cape Town ( CoCT ) revise its current water tariffs . This also applies to all other local authorities who make use of water supplied by the Western Cape Water Supply System ( WCWSS ) . <p> " As a result of the water weighted storage of the system dam reaching 88% on September 1 , 2020 , all restrictions are repealed , " the directive from regional acting head Simphiwe Mashicila read , as reported by IOL . <p> " It must be acknowledged that the municipalities are autonomous in their decision-making in respect of setting their own local restriction levels and determination of tariffs ; and secondly municipalities have varied inherent factors that contribute to the cost of service delivery in their @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> There have been calls for both residents and number of organisations for the City to revise the water tariffs , as the dams supplying the municipality have exceeded an overall capacity of 100% -- this is the first time this has happened in two years . <p> According to civic organisation Stop CoCT , they have also called on the City to revise the entire water tariff system , including water levies and water restriction policies . <p> " The Central Department of Water Services already lifted the water restrictions for the Western Cape catchment area in September 2020 , " it said . " Yet the City of Cape Town persists with their high tariffs , water levy and some water restrictions . In 2019 the City renamed the Level 3 tariff block to Level 1 .
