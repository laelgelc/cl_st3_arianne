<p> The Covid-19 pandemic has thrust into the limelight the far-reaching effects that business has on vulnerable people in work forces and communities across the world , putting the imperative of tackling inequalities on a political par with climate change . What will this mean in practice ? <p> Efforts are already underway to develop a single , coherent reporting system that would allow investors and other stakeholders to assess a company 's impact on the environment . These efforts are worthy : We need less competition and choice in what companies report regarding their impact on the world , and more clarity and consistency so better decisions can be made by managers and investors alike . However -- and this caveat matters -- we must balance the enthusiasm for this ambition with attention to an important reality : When it comes to measuring the effects company operations have on people , there has been far less scrutiny , standardization and innovation . <p> In 2018 , Shift , a nonprofit dedicated to promoting the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights that one of us ( Caroline ) @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in the reporting of nearly 500 companies and in 8 major environmental , social and governance rankings , ratings and bench marks . We have developed these over two years of consultation with business , investors , civil society leaders and other experts . Indicators like these should be a valuable addition to the human-focused provisions of a corporate accounting and reporting system . <p> Third , when it comes to those areas for which current indicators are known to be inadequate , we should not assume that " something is better than nothing . A coherent reporting system could promote clear and robust criteria for companies to develop targets and indicators that are tailored to their operating realities . Such targets should be time-bound , tied to specific improved outcomes for people affected by the business , capable of evidence-based evaluation and informed by inputs from all stakeholders . <p> As companies report against their targets , based on clear evidence , shareholders , employees , nonprofits and other stakeholders can then assess year-on-year progress . Investors will be better positioned to identify and reward companies that are taking meaningful action . And it will be possible to compare company trajectories without pretending their operating contexts are the same . <p> When it comes to the effects of business over people and the world , we need new thinking and new approaches .
