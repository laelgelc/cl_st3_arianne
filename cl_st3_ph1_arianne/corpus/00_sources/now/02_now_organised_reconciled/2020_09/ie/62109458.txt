<h> Rutger Bregman : Cynicism is out , hope is in <p> This week 's guest editor sets out why the progressive generational shift should bring hope of a better future even in the shadow of Covid-19 <p> September 7 , 2020 <p> By Rutger Bregman <p> If you had told anyone , just 10 years ago , that by now a 16-year-old Swedish girl would have kickstarted the biggest climate justice movement the world has ever seen , that the death of a 46-year-old black man at the hands of a white police officer would ignite the largest protests in the history of the United States , that even the Financial Times -- the world 's leading business paper -- would write in an editorial that " radical reforms ( ... ) will need to be put on the table " , that , of all people , Joe Biden would become the US presidential candidate with the most progressive platform ever , that the vast majority of people in developed countries would be in favour of things like higher taxes on the rich , equal marriage and a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ global pandemic that would make us question everything about how we 've organised our societies and economies -- well , I think most people would have thought you 'd lost your mind . <p> And yet that 's what has happened . Ideas that used to be dismissed as unrealistic , unreasonable and impossible have moved into the mainstream . In my interview with filmmaker Richard Curtis for this week 's magazine , Curtis told me about a conversation he had , 15 years ago , with Gordon Brown . It was about tax evasion and inequality , and the then-Chancellor said : " It wo n't come up during our lifetime . " He could n't be more wrong . Tax evasion and inequality have become major political issues . A 2019 survey of 22,000 people in 21 countries found that the vast majority of people want higher taxes on the rich in order to support the poor . <p> Six years ago I published a book called Utopia for Realists about ideas that may seem crazy now , but might just change the world . One of the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ giving everyone a monthly grant that 's enough to pay for basic needs . In 2014 , UBI was an almost forgotten idea , and seen as wildly unrealistic by pundits and politicians . It 's been amazing to see how quickly it 's become so much more popular . A 2020 poll by researchers from Oxford University found that more than 70 per cent of Europeans now support the idea . <p> Behind the notion of a basic income for everyone ( call it ' venture capital for the people ' ) lies a totally new view of human nature . For centuries Western culture has been permeated by the idea that we humans are just selfish creatures . That cynical image of man has been proclaimed in many places -- in films and novels , in history books and in scientific research . But in the last 20 years scientists from all over the world and from many disciplines have switched to a more hopeful view of mankind . This development is still so young that researchers often do n't even know about each other . Or as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ new trends in biology : " Oh god , so it 's happening there as well ? " <p> For my new book Humankind I wanted to tell the story of this revolution , one that has come at exactly the right time . Just look at the jaw-dropping generational shift of the past three decades . If you were young in the 1990s , it was avant-garde to be cynical and nihilistic . Irony was cool , values were pass ? . If you 're young now , it 's avant-garde to protest and occupy , to engage and stay true to your values . Activism is the new realism . <p> In Western Europe , young people are much more progressive than the generations of their parents and grandparents . They 're more supportive of LGBT+ rights , more positive about the contribution of immigrants to society and more supportive of the European Union . ( In the UK , pollsters YouGov have found that more than 300,000 Leave voters die each year , while Remain is getting almost 400,000 new supporters of voting age . With the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the people ' would now be to stay in the EU . ) <p> In the US , young people are even more progressive . The Pew Research Center has found that Generation Z , born in the mid-90s , is the most educated , most diverse and most worried about climate change , in favour of higher taxes on the rich and enthusiastic about bold plans like Medicare for All and a UBI to eradicate poverty . <p> You may think that these are just youthful sentiments . " If you 're not a liberal when you 're 25 , you have no heart . If you 're not a conservative by the time you 're 35 , you have no brain , " is something Churchill never said -- but many people still think he would have been right if he had said it . And yes , there is limited evidence that people become more conservative with age . <p> But there 's way more evidence that your political outlook is mostly formed in your twenties . Generations are shaped by great historical events like the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Twin Towers or the great recession of 2008 . <p> And now we have Covid-19 . <p> Cynicism is a gift to those in power , a legitimisation of hierarchy and inequality <p> Historians have long known that crises can be shifting points , for better or for worse . And yes , it 's easy to imagine us going down a dark path . Our democracies ( or should I say ' elective aristocracies ' ? ) have weakened . Our institutions are far from indestructible , the rule of law is never a given , and politics seems to be all about ' survival of the shameless ' these days . The prospect of Donald Trump getting another term in the US is genuinely terrifying . <p> At the same time , it 's hard not to feel hope . Just think about it : how many George Floyds have there been before George Floyd ? How many brutal police killings did not ignite a protest movement of tens of millions of people across the globe ? For how many years have we talked about climate change -- @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ started to realise that we need a massive transformation of the whole economy , a mobilisation that 's equivalent to a mobilisation during wartime ? <p> Yes , there 's a long way to go , but we 've also come a long way . The biggest injustices happen in silence -- when there are no protests , when there 's no anger , and when politicians believe the real issues " wo n't come up during our lifetime " . That silence is no more . <p> And remember , those at the top want us to be distrustful of one another . Cynicism is a gift to those in power , a legitimisation of hierarchy and inequality . Because if we ca n't trust each other , then we need them . We need the kings and the queens , the presidents and the CEOs . But if we can come together , if we have the courage to look each other in the eye and organise , then those at the top will get worried . Throughout history , elites have understood that a hopeful view of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> Today , it 's an act of defiance to believe in the good of humanity . So whether you 're young or old , boomer or Gen Z : do n't be afraid to be dismissed as gullible or naive . What 's naive today may be common sense tomorrow . Cynicism is out , hope is in.
