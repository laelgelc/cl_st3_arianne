<p> Macfrut Digital , the first fruit and vegetable industry trade fair to be held entirely online , reportedly attracted an audience of more than 18,000 individuals -- with more than half of those people apparently attending the virtual event via a smartphone . <p> Heralded by the show 's president Renzo Piraccini as " a great communication project " that overcame the challenges laid down by the Covid-19 crisis , the show 's reinvention as a digital-only exhibition on 8-10 September encouraged each participating company to create content that could be accessed by all visitors , primarily in the form of videos . <p> As a result , the show 's 400 exhibitors were given the opportunity to present themselves to potential customers -- including around 600 buyers , 550 from abroad . In total , the 18,164 unique visitors accessed the show 32,000 times across the three days . <p> Among the international pavilions , China was out in force with a total of 87 companies participating , alongside exhibitors from Chile , Dominican Republic , Senegal , Mozambique , Kenya , Somalia , Indonesia , Uzbekistan and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ per cent of the show 's exhibitors . <p> A total of 11 technical forums took place during the event . Among these , one of the most popular was one entitled ' Overview of the China-Europe fruit and vegetable sector ( see earlier report here ) , which underlined the great interest shown by China towards the European continent and the major opportunities for growth in trade between the two regions . <p> Sustainability was also a hot topic , with a number of different environmentally friendly solutions presented as possible ways of combating climate change . Two of the forums , for example , focused on irrigation techniques and the importance of water conservation . <p> The first , promoted by ANBI , presented the Goccia Verde project , a voluntary certification programme for the sustainable use of water and land .
