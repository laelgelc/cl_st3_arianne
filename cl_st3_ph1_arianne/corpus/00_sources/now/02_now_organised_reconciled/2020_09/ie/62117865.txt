<h> Viewpoint : Adding to financial thinking <p> Peter Kraneveld applies statistical theory to COVID-19 and concludes that investors should start thinking about events that have no mean regression <p> Financial economists like to think in terms of cycles and standard distributions : Gaussian curves ( bell curves ) . Growth , interest rates and real estate are thought of as cyclical and short-term equity prices are said to be regressing to mean . <p> These are very handy assumptions , because they make things as predictable as seasons . <p> Now consider Moore 's law : " the number of transistors in a dense integrated circuit doubles about every two years . " The law suggests a more or less straight line that is neither cyclical nor Gaussian . <p> Intuitively , we suspect that there is a physical limit to the law . However , we will not know what the mean is until that limit is reached and we can not reasonably expect the number of transistors to go down after it reached its zenith . <p> There will be no mean regression . In other words , no matter how extreme the most extreme of the event was , it is possible that a next event will be worse and no matter for how long such events do not occur , they can return .
