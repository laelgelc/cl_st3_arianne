<p> The cars that typically throng the huge highways weaving through Los Angeles are such an established part of the city 's fabric that when the coronavirus pandemic hit , their sudden absence felt bizarre to locals even eerie . But many Angelenos have now discovered a new sort of relationship with their streets . <p> " People have felt they own their neighborhood again , they feel connected to it again , " Eric Garcetti , Los Angeles 's mayor , told The Guardian in reference to streets that have reduced traffic , or even had it closed off , as offices , retailers and restaurants shut down . <p> Garcetti added : " People started walking in their neighborhood , biking , rollerskating . I think out of this pandemic you will see dozens of streets keeping it this way .
