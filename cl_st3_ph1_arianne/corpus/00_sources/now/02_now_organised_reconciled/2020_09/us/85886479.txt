<p> Washington , D.C. -- A new report from the Center for American Progress outlines how North Carolina can reduce the impact of climate change in a way that addresses systemic racism and environmental justice . <p> The recommendations come after the state issued findings earlier this year that show North Carolina will see more extreme heat , damaging storms and floods , and rising sea levels -- all to the detriment of the state 's public health , economy , and environment . <p> Like many other states , systemic and historic disparities in North Carolina , driven by discriminatory zoning and housing discrimination , have exacerbated the risks and effects of more extreme weather and other climate change impacts , the report finds . <p> Gov . Roy Cooper ( D-NC ) has committed to pursuing a comprehensive approach to tackling climate change and transitioning to a clean energy economy in ways that also advance equity and justice in the state -- an approach that should serve as a model for other states in the region .
