<h> ' Black-ish ' stars get real about making sure their sitcom came back during the upheaval of 2020 <p> The cast and producers of ABC 's " Black-ish " do n't want to be quiet at this point in 2020 . <p> In a virtual panel for the upcoming seventh season of ABC 's family sitcom , creator Kenya Barris and stars Anthony Anderson and Tracee Ellis Ross emphasized the importance of the series -- which focuses on an upper-middle class Black family and often tackles complex social issues -- in a year as fraught as 2020 . <p> " I had a conversation with ( ABC ) and thought that it would be doing a disservice to our audience , a disservice to the community , and a disservice to our show to have our voices muted in a time like this , " Anderson , who is also an executive producer on the show , told reporters . " To not have the show come back until midseason ( was ) problematic for me . That was my conversation with the powers-that-be , and everybody was on @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ about the fall premiere of our show . " <p> A day after they announced the fall schedule in June , ABC reversed course and said it would hold back new series " Call Your Mother " for midseason and instead return " Black-ish " to the schedule , reflecting apparent concern that the series , its spinoff " Mixed-ish " and another show with a Black lead , " For Life , " were all being held back from the fall . <p> Anderson , Barris and Ross praised ABC , and president Karey Burke , for ultimately deciding to bring " Black-ish " back sooner . It was " a solid pivot , " according to Ross . <p> " Hulu was the right place for it , " Barris said of the episode , which discussed topics including President Donald Trump , Colin Kaepernick 's protests against police brutality , climate change and white supremacists .
