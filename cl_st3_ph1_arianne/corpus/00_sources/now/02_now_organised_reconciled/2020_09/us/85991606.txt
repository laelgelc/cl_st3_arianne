<p> LOS ANGELES - Five weeks after California erupted in deadly wildfires supercharged by record heat and howling winds , crews battling flames pushed Monday to consolidate their gains before the return of the blistering , gusty weather . <p> California has lost far more landscape to wildfires this summer than during any previous entire year , with scores of conflagrations , many sparked by catastrophic lightning storms , scorching about 3.4 million acres since mid-August . <p> The previous record was just less than 2 million acres burned in 2018 . <p> As of Monday , more than 19,000 firefighters continued to wage war on 27 major blazes across the state , according to the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection ( CalFire ) . <p> FILE - A firefighter with the San Bernardino County Fire Department hoses down hot spots from the Bobcat Fire in Valyermo , Calif. , Sept. 19 , 2020 . <p> The fires , stoked by extreme weather conditions that scientists have pointed to as signs of climate change , have destroyed an estimated 6,100 homes and other structures and killed 26 people @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Another 2 million acres have gone up in flames in Oregon and Washington state during an overlapping outbreak of wildfires that started earlier this month , destroying more than 4,400 structures in all and claiming 10 lives . <p> But a weekend of intermittently heavy showers across the western Cascade mountain range helped fire crews in the Pacific Northwest tamp down blazes in those two states . <p> Although California has seen little or no rain in recent days , bouts of extreme heat and gale-force winds that had produced incendiary conditions for weeks have given way to lower temperatures and lighter breezes , enabling firefighters to gain ground around most fires . <p> " They 're going to take advantage of this cool weather while they can , " CalFire spokeswoman Lynne Tolmachoff told Reuters . <p> The break in the weather is not expected to last much longer . Tolmachoff said forecasts call for rising temperatures , lower humidity and a return of strong , erratic winds around midweek in Southern California and by the weekend across the state 's northern half . <p> Bobcat Fire proves stubborn @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ One in particular , dubbed the Bobcat Fire , grew to more than 100,000 acres Monday in the San Gabriel Mountains north of Los Angeles , with containment levels achieved by firefighters holding steady at just 15% , CalFire said . <p> The Bobcat last week spread perilously close to a famed astronomical observatory and complex of vital communications towers at the summit of Mount Wilson , while forcing evacuations of communities in the foothills below . <p> Several more areas , including Pasadena , a city of 140,000 people , remained under an evacuation warning , advising residents to be ready to flee at a moment 's notice . At the opposite end of the sprawling mountain range to the north , the fire was reported to have destroyed some homes and other structures in the high desert of the Antelope Valley . <p> Across the Bobcat Fire zone and others , ground crews with axes , shovels and bulldozers clambered through rugged canyons and mountain slopes , hacking away tinder-dry brush and scrub before it could burn , creating containment lines around the perimeter of advancing flames . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ airplane tankers dumping flame retardant on the blazes . <p> Regardless of the progress they make this week , California 's record fire season remains far from over .
