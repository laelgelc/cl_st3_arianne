<h> UK charges 26 over printing works climate protest <p> The protests were part of 10 days of action by the group Extinction Rebellion and caused delays to deliveries of newspapers including The Times and its tabloid stablemate The Sun <p> Twenty-six people have been charged with aggravated trespass after a climate protest at a printing works in northwest England disrupted the delivery of several newspapers , police said on Sunday . <p> Merseyside Police said the men and women , aged between 19 and 60 , were released on bail and ordered to appear in court in Liverpool and St Helens between January 8 and 13 next year . <p> Fifty other people were in custody after a similar protest at another print site at Waltham Cross , north of London , Hertfordshire Police said . <p> The protests were part of 10 days of action by the group Extinction Rebellion and caused delays to deliveries of newspapers including The Times and its tabloid stablemate The Sun . <p> Activists blocked roads outside the sites using vehicles and attached themselves to obstacles to expose what they said was the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ climate and ecological emergency " . <p> The Times and The Sun are owned by News Corp , which is controlled by media magnate Rupert Murdoch , who has been accused of denying climate change . <p> Extinction Rebellion accused News Corp and right-wing publications such as the Daily Mail and the London Evening Standard of pushing " personal and political agendas " . <p> Prime Minister Boris Johnson , who is a former newspaper reporter and columnist , led condemnation of the action , calling it an attempt to stifle freedom of speech . <p> " A free press is vital in holding the government and other powerful institutions to account on issues critical for the future of our country , including the fight against climate change , " he said .
