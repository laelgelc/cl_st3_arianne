<h> Funds and firms call for tougher 2030 EU climate target <p> BRUSSELS ( Reuters ) - Investors managing trillions in assets and more than 120 business leaders called on the European Union to commit to cut emissions by at least 55% by 2030 on Tuesday , saying anything less would fail to unlock the private financing needed . <p> This would be in line with a proposal due to be put forward on Thursday by the European Commission to curb EU greenhouse gas emissions by at least 55% from 1990 levels by 2030 , draft plans seen by Reuters show . <p> The existing target is a 40% cut , and some poorer fossil fuel-dependent EU states are unwilling to commit to deeper cuts . <p> A lesser target would derail EU 's plan to reach net zero emissions by 2050 , a report by the Institutional Investors Group on Climate Change ( IIGCC ) , who together manage more than 33 trillion euros in assets , said . <p> " Weak climate targets would fail to provide the strength of market signal needed to mobilise investors and attract @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ IIGCC said . <p> Investment decisions taken this decade will determine the EU 's long-term emissions as infrastructure could stand for decades , whereas industries must invest in low-carbon tech before 2030 to be ready for large-scale use by 2050 . <p> In a separate letter , 127 business leaders and 30 investors , some of whom are in the IIGCC , also backed an emissions-cutting target of " at least 55% " by 2030 . <p> This would help ensure Europe 's economic recovery from the coronavirus pandemic is driven by green growth , said the companies , which include Unilever , Google , EDF , Deutsche Bank and H&amp;M . <p> The EU expects a 55% 2030 emissions-cutting goal to require additional energy investments of 350 billion euros ( $415 billion ) a year from 2021 to 2030 , compared with the previous decade .
