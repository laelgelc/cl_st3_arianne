<h> Consideration of both the potential and constraints of reforestation is needed to achieve climate mitigation goals <p> Nursery seedlings beginning to flourish in Sungai Tohor , Riau , after a community-based peatland restoration project . Credit : National University of Singapore <p> Reforestation is a promising nature-based climate solution . However , there are practical considerations beyond the suitability of land for planting trees , such as financial , land-use and operational constraints , which can limit its outcomes and thus need to be taken into account . A recent study led by researchers from the National University of Singapore ( NUS ) found that these constraints could limit the climate mitigation potential of reforestation in the Southeast Asian context . Understanding how these constraints operate helps inform the prioritization of cost-effective reforestation and investment opportunities . <p> Their findings were published in the journal Nature Climate Change on 17 August 2020 . <p> Reforestation and climate change mitigation <p> During the annual meeting in Davos in January this year , the World Economic Forum launched an ambitious initiative to plant one trillion trees worldwide to restore biodiversity and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in general , have often been touted as a cost-effective nature-based solution to address climate change . This is due to the ability of trees and other natural vegetation to convert atmospheric carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) to biomass through the process of photosynthesis , which is nature 's way of capturing and locking up carbon . However , broader considerations beyond where trees could be planted may have impacts on the effectiveness of reforestation as a climate solution . <p> Barriers to reforestation <p> In the study , the research team led by Professor Koh Lian Pin , who is from the NUS Department of Biological Sciences , estimated the climate mitigation potential of reforestation across Southeast Asia , and assessed the effects of biophysical , financial , land-use , and operational considerations on different constraint scenarios . <p> The researchers found that 121 million hectares of land across Southeast Asia are suitable for reforestation , and could potentially contribute to climate mitigation at a rate of 3.4 gigatonnes of CO2 per year . <p> " However , only a fraction of that mitigation potential may be achievable if @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Koh , who is also Director of the NUS Center for Nature-based Climate Solutions . <p> One example is the many pockets of " barren land " in Southeast Asia that may actually be in use by farmers to grow crops for subsistence , or for sale in local markets . NUS postdoctoral research fellow Dr. Zeng Yiwen , who is the first author of the paper , explained , " If we exclude these small farmlands , the available land for reforestation in Southeast Asia would be reduced to about 76 million hectares , and its climate mitigation potential would drop to 2.2 gigatonnes of CO2 per year . " <p> The team noted that there may also be a need for reforestation sites to be near mature forests which could act as sources of seeds or seedlings that are critical for the reforestation process . For example , by involving smallholder farmers in the reforestation process , such as through agroforestry or tree planting on their farms , climate mitigation potential can be realized while balancing trade-offs with food security and local livelihoods .
