<p> A chimp may not always be just a chimp when it comes to behavior . In fact , the more varied the conditions the primates face , the more diverse their behavior and culture . <p> " Chimps have a great degree of behavioral flexibility , " said Ammie Kalan , a postdoctoral researcher at the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology and the lead author of the study published Sept. 15 in Nature Communications . <p> In 2019 , Kalan was part of a large team of researchers that found that humans may be negatively affecting the diverse world of chimpanzee behavior . For this study , the researchers looked for relationships between a group 's behavioral repertoire and the type of environmental and climatic conditions it experienced in its home range . <p> They found the chimpanzees that lived in variable conditions had a larger repertoire of behaviors . For example , those living in savanna woodlands , which include open areas and patches of forest , engaged in more types of behavior than chimps that lived in comparatively uniform dense forests . Also , the chimps that experienced more variation between seasons or even between years had a larger behavioral repertoire than those that lived in more even climates . <p> Kalan said this discovery gives the researchers hope that the chimps will be able to adapt to the ongoing problems they face . <p> " There might be a great degree of promise for this species as we 're heading further into this era of the Anthropocene where we have climate change and other unprecedented changes to habitats , " she said . <p> It 's also interesting from an evolutionary point of view , she said , especially @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ said she would like to see follow-up genetic research analyzing whether the diversity in behavior between groups is reflected in genetic differences . <p> Inside Science is an editorially independent nonprofit print , electronic and video journalism news service owned and operated by the American Institute of Physics .
