<p> MEXICO CITY - A Mexican court will later this week hear a case brought on by 15 young people demanding the government of President Andres Manuel Lopez Obrador set out clear policies on climate change , documents show . <p> Lopez Obrador is under increased pressure to help mitigate the effects of climate change . <p> The plaintiffs from the state Baja California filed a legal stay of proceedings , known locally as amparo , before a district court in administrative matters , several documents related to the case show . <p> In it , the youths , aged 17 to 23 , demand clearer regulations and public policies derived from the country 's existing General Law on Climate Change and the Mexican constitution , the documents showed . <p> All of the documents , which have not been made public , were provided by a representative of the plaintiffs . <p> The hearing is scheduled for Sept. 4 and comes just days after the country 's environment minister quit . <p> " There 's no bigger mistake than doing nothing based on a belief that one can only @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ seem , they sow what future generations will reap , " said Gema Osorio , one of the plaintiffs , aged 20 . <p> " My wish is that even if we do n't manage to repair the damages , at least we stop continuing to harm the planet , " she said on Wednesday . <p> Mexico 's previous government had laid out targets to reduce emissions . Lopez Obrador has not reaffirmed those goals but highlights projects such as a large-scale tree-planting scheme as evidence of commitment to the environment . <p> The environment ministry did not immediately respond to a request for comment . <p> National oil company Petroleos Mexicanos , or Pemex , has come under particular pressure from investors looking to reduce the carbon footprint of their investments . <p> Pemex alone was the ninth biggest energy producer of carbon and methane emissions globally between 1965 and 2018 , according to data from the Climate Accountability Institute , a non-profit . <p> With emissions of some 23 billion tonnes of CO2 equivalent , the troubled oil company was the largest emitter among its Latin American peers .
