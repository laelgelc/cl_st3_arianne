<h> The Forest Service 's rule changes undercut its once-proud mission <p> In 1910 , Gifford Pinchot faced a momentous decision . As the first chief of the U.S. Forest Service , which had been established five years earlier , he had to decide how to respond to news that President William Howard Taft and Interior Secretary Richard Ballinger secretly had cut a deal with New York investors to mine coalfields in Alaska 's Chugach National Forest . Pinchot was not opposed to coal mining per se , but he was adamant that his agency be involved in any such discussions and that it could reject any deal . <p> He felt he had no choice but publicly to contest the decision , because otherwise the Forest Service would lose its authority to sustainably manage the national forests . He knew he would be fired for insubordination , and he was .
