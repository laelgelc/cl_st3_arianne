<p> A major new report released by The World Wildlife Fund has found that global wildlife populations have declined by more than two thirds in 50 years and that nature is still being destroyed by humans at a rate unprecedented in history . The Living Planet Index tracked 20,811 populations of 4,392 vertebrate species and it found that over-consumption by humans has been the driving force behind the catastrophic population collapse with deforestation and agricultural expansion key contributing factors . Between 1970 and 2016 , populations of mammals , birds , amphibians and fish declined 68% . <p> The tropical regions of Central and South America have suffered the worst impact of any global region , registering a 94% decline in wildlife stocks since 1970 . In this case , the conversion of grasslands , savannahs , forests and wetlands , the overexploitation of species , climate change , and the introduction of alien species are all key drivers of the grim trend .
