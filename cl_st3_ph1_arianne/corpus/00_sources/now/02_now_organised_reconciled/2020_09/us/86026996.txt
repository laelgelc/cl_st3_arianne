<p> COVID-19 is proof that pandemics are a real and catastrophic risk for global economies and businesses . This crisis has shown that many businesses are under prepared for such global-scale disruptions , with those that lack agility and resilience most impacted . As businesses prepare for future systemic risks , they can no longer ignore environmental , social and governance ( ESG ) issues that have the potential to bring widespread damage to our world and put the brakes on current operating models . Impacts of climate change , exploitation of our natural resources , social inequality , poor stakeholder relations and lack of sound management structures all have the potential to escalate rapidly into crises . <p> Shutterstock <p> To thrive , businesses must commit to acting responsibly with a long-term perspective , investing in corporate stewardship , sustainability and governance to build resilience and drive systemic socio-economic transformation . <p> Corporate sustainability is an outcome-based phenomenon . It refers to the ability of an organisation to nurture and sustain itself , its natural resources and communities over the long run by effectively meeting the expectations of diverse @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ organisation 's operations on the environment and society . Corporate governance refers to the rules and guiding principles used to utilise the resources within an organisation and regulate the behaviour of its board , management and employees . <p> Finally , corporate stewardship provides the leadership will and intent to act in the protection of others ' long-term welfare , and is embodied as a leadership process and management mindset that proactively informs strategic and operational decisions made throughout a company 's value chain . A well-stewarded company ensures the sustainable management of all physical , social and natural resources to maintain and secure growth and resilience for its future . It will implement enlightened governance mechanisms to balance the interests of all stakeholders based on both legal and internal value systems . <p> Meeting Stakeholders ' Needs Beyond the Pandemic <p> Battling the crisis as a society has reshaped the ESG perspectives along the entire value chain from investors to employees . According to global research agency Morningstar , the average annual return for a sustainable fund invested in large global companies over the last 10 years has been 6.9% a year , while a traditionally invested fund has made 6.3% a year.1 The trend continued during the coronavirus crisis , with average excess returns in Q1 2020 ranging between 0.09% and 1.83% . Sustainability-focused funds also attracted a record amount of capital in the first quarter of this year . Global sustainable funds saw inflows of $45.7 billion , while the broader fund universe had an outflow of $384.7 billion.2 In addition , companies with a higher governance score outperformed their peers globally this year by a wide margin . The MSCI World Governance Quality index is down 5% year to date , while MSCI World is down 10%.3 <p> The message is clear : responsible corporate behaviour is important .
