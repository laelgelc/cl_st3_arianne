<h> Final redesign of Dublin 's bus network to see services rise 23% <h> BusConnects will create 230km of dedicated bus lanes and 200km of cycle tracks <p> Following three rounds of public consultation and 72,000 submissions , this is the final version of the network redesign . Image : National Transport Authority <p> A new bus network for Dublin , which will result in the first complete overhaul of the capital 's bus services with changes to all existing routes , will be implemented from next year . <p> Following three rounds of public consultation and 72,000 submissions , the final version of the network redesign has been published by the National Transport Authority ( NTA ) . <p> The network redesign is part of the NTA 's BusConnects programme , which aims to overhaul the bus system in Dublin and has two main components : creating 230km of dedicated bus lanes in tandem with 200km of cycle tracks , and redesigning the bus network . <p> The provision of 16 designated bus corridors to accommodate the new network will potentially require widening roads and the loss of parking @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ A third round of public consultation in relation to these corridors , some of which have proved controversial , will take place later this year . <p> The NTA said the new network would see increased evening and weekend services , with most frequent routes operating every 15 minutes or better on weekdays and Saturdays , most on Sundays also . <p> There will be a number of routes that will operate 24 hours a day . Overall , the level of bus services in the Dublin network will increase by 23 per cent as a result of the new network , according to the NTA . <p> The network aims to create a more coherent service for the capital that will eliminate overlapping routes and improve journey times . <p> A lettering system from A to H will identify eight " spines " through the city , with buses running every four to eight minutes . Numbers will be used to indicate different branches of a spine . When the NTA first published its plan in July 2018 , more than 50,000 submissions were made on the redesign , with more than 70 per cent objecting to the loss of existing services or having to change buses . <p> Under the 2018 plan , up to 15 per cent of users would have lost their direct routes to the city .
