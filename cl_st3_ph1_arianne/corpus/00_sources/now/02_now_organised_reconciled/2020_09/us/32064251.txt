<h> Changing what we eat could offset years of climate-warming emissions , new analysis finds <p> Plant protein foods -- like lentils , beans , and nuts -- can provide vital nutrients using a small fraction of the land required to produce meat and dairy . By shifting to these foods , much of the remaining land could support ecosystems that absorb CO2 , according to a new study appearing in the journal Nature Sustainability . <p> In their study , the researchers analyzed and mapped areas where extensive production of animal-sourced food , which requires 83 percent of Earth 's agricultural land , suppresses native vegetation , including forests . <p> The study highlights places where changing what people grow and eat could free up space for ecosystems to regrow , offsetting our CO2 emissions in the process . <p> " The greatest potential for forest regrowth , and the climate benefits it entails , exists in high- and upper-middle income countries , places where scaling back on land-hungry meat and dairy would have relatively minor impacts on food security , " says Matthew Hayek , the principal author @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ University 's Department of Environmental Studies . <p> Burning fossil fuels for energy emits CO2 , warming the planet . When warming reaches 1.5 ? C ( 2.7 ? F ) above pre-industrial levels , more severe impacts like droughts and sea level rise are expected . Scientists describe how much fossil fuel we can burn before hitting that limit using the global " carbon budget . " <p> According to the authors ' findings , vegetation regrowth could remove as much as nine to 16 years of global fossil fuel CO2 emissions , if demand for meat were to drastically plummet in the coming decades along with its massive land requirements . That much CO2 removal would effectively double Earth 's rapidly shrinking carbon budget . <p> " We can think of shifting our eating habits toward land-friendly diets as a supplement to shifting energy , rather than a substitute , " says Hayek . " Restoring native forests could buy some much-needed time for countries to transition their energy grids to renewable , fossil-free infrastructure .
