Ethics leaders need to work closely with human resources to ensure that incentives are appropriate to local conditions . <p> While some companies are subjecting senior leaders to formal ethics evaluations ( such as evaluating how a growth target is achieved , not just whether it is achieved ) and sustainability targets ( such as formal goals around reducing carbon emissions at oil-and-gas companies ) , these goals must be given sufficient weight in the overall compensation structure to change behavior . Too often , these incentives play a minor role in compensation , giving leaders little financial reason for changing their behavior . <h> Empower your ethics leader <p> Integrity starts at the top . An independent , empowered chief ethics officer should be able to hold senior leadership to account , ideally with a direct reporting line to the board . For example , say a company has a commitment to fighting climate change . That obviously would entail measuring the company 's carbon emissions . But it could also mean refusing business from oil-and-gas companies -- or changing lobbying practices if the lobbying runs counter to the stated climate goals . <p> It must be clear to all employees what role an ethics leader is playing , and that it carries authority . The current fashion is to have multiple reporting lines and shared responsibilities across teams , part of an effort to drive collaboration . Beware , though : That can muddle the accountability that 's essential for safeguarding ethical conduct . Too often , when everybody is responsible for monitoring ethical behavior , nobody is responsible . <p> The board also can play a role here . Board members should also have ways to assess and drive ethical behavior . <h> Measure your culture <p> Building and sustaining a culture in which employees are happy , motivated and ethical is the overarching goal of an ethics program . New employees can be told about a company 's ethical commitments , but it 's only by watching leaders and peers that we learn what norms we must adopt to succeed . Nothing degrades organizational culture faster than the perception that leaders evade accountability and operate with impunity . Human beings are highly prone to justify and rationalize unethical behavior and are far more influenced by our social context than most of us care to admit . <p> An annual , independent culture assessment can help companies determine whether employees perceive a gap between stated values and actual behavior , find whether there are barriers to speaking up about unethical conduct , identify challenges regarding equity and inclusion , and detect problems in particular regions or teams before it becomes too late . <h> This is n't a marketing gimmick <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cynical " greenwashing , " where corporate social-responsibility efforts are marketing spin and little more . Remember : No one is impressed by empty phrases such as " Integrity is in our DNA . " <p> None of this is easy . And it is n't getting any easier . Regulatory requirements are growing more complex and inconsistent across jurisdictions , while ethical performance has become a separate critical dimension in managing risk and reputation . Creating a credible ethics program requires conscious consideration of compliance , culture and corporate responsibility .
