<h> In busy Atlantic hurricane season , storm names to be all Greek <p> GENEVA ( Reuters ) - This year 's Atlantic hurricane season has been so busy that meteorologists will soon have exhausted all the designated storm names . <p> So for just the second time in history , the Greek alphabet will be used , the World Meteorological Organization ( WMO ) said . <p> Arthur was the first storm on the 21-name list prepared for the 2020 season , which ends on Nov. 30 . <p> Hurricanes Paulette and Sally and Tropical Storms Teddy and Vicky are still active , and a low pressure system off West Africa could develop into a cyclone and would be christened Wilfred . <p> " Once we exhaust the last name on the list , which is Wilfred , we then have to switch to the Greek alphabet and you will start to see names such as Alpha , Beta , Gamma , Delta , Epsilon , Zeta etc , " the WMO 's Clare Nullis told reporters . <p> This year 's season is set to be the busiest @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a record 25 storms which it attributes to warm sea temperatures and low wind-shear . Climate change is thought to be increasing their intensity . <p> As of Monday , there were advisories for five cyclones swirling in the Atlantic for only the second time in history . However one of them , Rene , has since dissipated but it still means the ' R ' name choice had gone . <p> No names have been prepared X , Y or Z this year because there are few appropriate names that begin with them , WMO said . <p> Since , 1953 , tropical storms have been named for ease of communication with mariners and the public and were initially only female . Historical storms were identified by their coordinates or the things they destroyed . <p> Meteorologists now rotate through six , alphabetical stock lists of alternating male and female names maintained by a WMO committee . The last time it resorted to Greek names was in the busy 2005 season which included Hurricane Katrina . <p> Occasionally , a storm name is " retired " by the committee @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Mitch which struck Honduras in 1998 . <p> Nullis said the WMO is then bombarded with replacement requests .
