<p> GRAND RAPIDS , MI -- Between 2010 and 2016 , the city of Grand Rapids processed about four permits per year for residents and businesses wanting to construct small-scale solar energy arrays . <p> From 2017 to the current date , the number of new permits averaged about 22 each year . <p> It 's that type of growth in residential and business investment in solar energy that city officials worry Consumers Energy 's proposed solar tariffs will undermine . The tariffs , Grand Rapids officials say , would also partially hinder the city 's own sustainability goal of powering all municipal buildings with 100 percent renewable energy by 2025 . <p> The proposed new rates , which are under consideration by the Michigan Public Service Commission , would reduce the credits small-scale solar generators receive for excess energy flowing onto Consumers Energy 's grid . " I want to believe we 've been doing work to make it easier for residents and small businesses to put solar in . If we hit this cap or if it 's too expensive , I fear that we 're going to not continue to see that growth trend . " <p> Waske Sutter said the importance of local investment in solar energy is three-fold : cost savings long term , reduction of air pollutants and a decrease in carbon footprint and climate change impacts . <p> Sutter pointed to an analysis by Margrethe Kearney , a staff attorney with the Environmental Law &amp; Policy Center , that showed the average smaller-scale solar installation customer would see a 62% increase in their bill if switched over to the proposed tariffs . <p> Brandon Hofmeister , senior vice @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Energy , put the new tariffs at about a 40% percent increase for smaller-scale installations . <p> Last week , the city joined a number of organizations and businesses that , either in support or in opposition to the proposed rate change , filed briefs in the rate case . <p> In their brief , city officials claim the utility company 's proposed rates undervalue the excess energy generated by solar installations that is fed back into the Consumers Energy grid , make it nearly impossible to understand the return on investment of solar projects and undermine public and private investment in solar . <p> The city proposed Consumers Energy retain the current credit system until a cost-benefit study on small-scale solar is undertaken .
