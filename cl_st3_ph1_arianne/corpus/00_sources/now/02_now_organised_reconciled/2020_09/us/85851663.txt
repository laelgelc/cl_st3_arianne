<h> Amendment 3 goes too far <p> I read with interest your editorial in Sunday 's Sun Sentinel ( Aug. 3 ) endorsing a Yes vote for Amendment 3 , which does away with political party primaries for governor , Cabinet and the Legislature in Florida . While I think a more open primary system in Florida could be beneficial , I am not comfortable with a primary voting system that eliminates political party affiliation in its entirety . When I lived in Massachusetts years ago , I was a registered Democrat . One year , former Gov . Bill Weld was running as a presidential candidate in the Republican primary . I really liked Weld , and I wanted to support his run for president . But , I was a registered Democrat . However , the Massachusetts primary system allowed the following : when registered voters came to the polling place , they were asked which primary ballet they wanted . If a registered Democrat requested a Republican ballot , that voter 's party affiliation was changed to Republican and the voter was given a Republican primary ballot @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ballot ) . After the voter deposited the ballot in the ballot box , they could go to the adjoining desk and change their political party affiliation back to their original party affiliation . No , under this system , a voter could not vote in both parties ' primary . I would be more likely to vote for Amendment 3 if it were more like the Massachusetts system described above . <p> Michael S.
