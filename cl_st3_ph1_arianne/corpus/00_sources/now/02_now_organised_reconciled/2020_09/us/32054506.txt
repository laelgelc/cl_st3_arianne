<h> Greenland 's Tradition of Narwhal Hunting , in " The Other World " <p> The film , directed by David McLain and written and narrated by Michael Paterniti , presents a vision of independence and community among the rugged whale hunters of Qaanaaq . <p> Qaanaaq , one of the northernmost villages in Greenland , has winters where the sun does not appear for more than three months . The town is so remote that its six hundred or so residents remain virtually isolated from the rest of the country year-round . In the spring , when the ice thaws , some of the community 's Inuit members begin to hunt narwhal , small whales with large , sharply pointed tusks which live only in the Arctic . <p> The film " The Other World " peers into this tradition of whale hunting , the heart of which is the sharing of the catch . " Greenlanders believe in communion , with each other and nature , " Michael Paterniti , the writer and narrator of the film , says . <p> Hunting in Qaanaaq is perilous . In winter , the Inuit hunt seal and walrus that approach breathing holes in the ice , which , already mercurial , gets thinner every year . The hunters are pulled on handmade sleds by dogs that are only partly domesticated -- there are cases annually of people being attacked by hunting dogs . When the ice melts , the hunters focus on narwhal , using kayaks and harpoons that they build themselves . There are less than a hundred people who continue this tradition , and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . When they return from a hunt , they often post on Facebook to announce that there is fresh whale meat and skin in town . McLain first visited Qaanaaq in 2006 , on a photography assignment for National Geographic . He showed up in a custom padded jacket that , although designed for Mt . Everest ( " picture the Michelin Man but , instead of filled with tires and air , you 're filled with down " ) , was inadequate for the Arctic cold . A group of narwhal hunters , dressed in traditional winter clothes -- polar-bear-fur pants and sealskin boots -- laughed when they saw him . " They were taking a bet on how soon I would die , " McLain said . <p> Qaanaaq is difficult to reach , even for other Greenlanders . Many have never met narwhal hunters , who , " even by Greenland standards , are extreme , " McLain said . Yet the figure of the Qaanaaq hunter looms large in the country 's cultural imagination , the way the cowboy might for Americans .
