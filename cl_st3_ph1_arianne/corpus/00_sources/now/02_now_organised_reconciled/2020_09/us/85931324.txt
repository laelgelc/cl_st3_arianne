Reuters <p> More than 150 major corporations and investors are calling on the EU to set more rigorous carbon emission targets . <p> In an open letter , companies including Google , Microsoft , Unilever , IKEA , and H&amp;M are asking EU leaders to reduce domestic greenhouse gas emissions by at least 55% by 2030 . <p> The news comes one day before Ursula von der Leyen , president of the European Commission , is set to deliver her 2020 State of the Union , the annual address that sets the stage for policy initiatives the government will focus on . <p> The initiative is led by the European Corporate Leaders Group , a group of European businesses advocating for more sustainable business and government practices . <p> In a statement , Lisa Jackson , vice president of environment , policy , and social initiatives at Apple , said , " The choice between a healthy planet and good business strategy has always been a false one @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ runs on 100% clean energy and a supply chain transitioning to do the same . " <p> The move is part of a larger trend away from shareholder primacy , or the idea that a company 's sole responsibility is to drive profit , and toward stakeholder capitalism , which says corporate leaders must be responsible to all stakeholders , including the environment . <p> EU greenhouse gas emissions decreased by 23% between 1990 and 2018 , while the economy grew by 61% over the same period , per a government report . <p> Still , the summer of 2020 was one of the Earth 's hottest on record , and the US currently faces unprecedented wildfires , raising concerns over the dangers of climate change .
