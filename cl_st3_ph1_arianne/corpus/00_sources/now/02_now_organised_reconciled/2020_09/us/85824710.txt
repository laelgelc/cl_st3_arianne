<h> 1 <h> 2 <h> Scientists unlock crops ' power to resist floods <h> Tweaking proteins in staple foods could help feed a warming world <p> Date : <p> August 31 , 2020 <p> Source : <p> University of Sydney <p> Summary : <p> Foundational science has discovered the molecular structure of plant enzymes that could be manipulated to create flood-resistant crops , vital as weather events become more extreme due to global warming . <p> Share : <p> FULL STORY <p> Enzymes that control a plant 's response to lower oxygen levels could be manipulated to make vital crops resistant to the impacts of flooding triggered by climate change , new research shows . <p> advertisement <p> Co-author Dr Mark White in the School of Chemistry at the University of Sydney said : " Climate change is a major global issue , not least for its impact on food security . We hope these findings can help produce flood-tolerant crops to help mitigate the devasting social and economic impact of extreme weather events on food production . " <p> The research , largely done at the University of Oxford @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Academy of Sciences . <p> Climate change has increased the number and intensity of global flooding events , threatening food security through significant crop loss . Plants , including staple crops such as rice , wheat and barley , can survive temporary periods of flooding by activating energy pathways that do n't rely on air in response to the low oxygen conditions in water . <p> These responses are controlled by oxygen-sensing enzymes called the Plant Cysteine Oxidases ( PCOs ) , which use oxygen to regulate the stability of proteins that control gene activity . <p> The research describes the molecular structures of the PCOs for the first time , identifying chemical features that are required for enzyme activity . <p> " The results provide a platform for future efforts to manipulate the enzyme function in an attempt to create flood-resistant crops that can mitigate the impact of extreme weather events , " Dr White said . <p> University of Sydney .
