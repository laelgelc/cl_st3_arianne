<p> California 's wildfire season is off to a brutal start . Through August , this year already ranks as the second most destructive in the state 's history , with more than 1.6 million acres burned . <p> Sparked by lightning strikes and record heat , fires in Northern California have destroyed thousands of structures , wrecked air quality in the San Francisco Bay Area and carried smoke plumes as far away as Nebraska . With hot , dry weather likely to persist until November , the worst may be yet to come . <p> Looking farther ahead , the picture does not improve . As climate change worsens , wildfires are growing in number , scale and duration across the American West , overwhelming local firefighting capacities and putting property and lives in peril . <p> Since 2017 , fires have consumed more than 20 million acres and caused at least $50 billion in economic losses .
