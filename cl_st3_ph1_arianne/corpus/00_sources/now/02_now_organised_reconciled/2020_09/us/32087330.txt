<h> Column : In defense of common values <p> I have returned with some regularity over the years to compare what is going on in our country today to what was going on in the 1850s , the years preceding the Civil War . <p> America has always been about freedom of expression . And that freedom has often led to protests and tension . Generally , we 've gotten through these tough and challenging times and moved on . <p> But what caused everything to break down in the 1850s , leading to a horrible Civil War ? <p> The answer , I think , is to understand the distinction between plurality of opinions and plurality of values . <p> Plurality of opinion is natural and vital to human reality and why freedom and democracy are so important . Each individual is unique and sees the world in his or her unique way . Each must be able to bring his or her own unique perspective , his or her own opinion , to the table . <p> But plurality of values is different altogether . We must have the same core values . <p> When we no longer have any common ground in our most basic beliefs , in our most basic values , we lose all basis of being able to communicate and conduct civil discourse . <p> We may have different opinions about health care , education , foreign policy and climate change . <p> But in the 1850s , the differences were about the nature of man , whether blacks were human beings and whether slavery should be tolerated . <p> This is a breakdown in common values , not in opinion . Once the most common values were gone , the basis for civil discourse was gone , and the country broke into war , with brother killing brother . <p> Current tensions are supposedly about police brutality , though I think this is a smoke screen . <p> Police brutality is indeed a problem .
