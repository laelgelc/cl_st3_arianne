<p> On September 22 , 2020 the report " COVID-19 and Inequality : A Test of Corporate Purpose " by KKS Advisors and the Test of Corporate Purpose ( TCP ) initiative was published . TCP was founded by Mark Tulay . I co-chaired this effort with Hiro Mizuno , former Chief Investment Officer of Government Pension Investment Fund , and Sacha Sadan , Director of Corporate Governance and board member , of Legal &amp; General Investment Management . The report is based on a global survey by GlobeScan of 561 individuals and an empirical analysis of 800 large companies by KKS Advisors using data provided by Truvalue Labs . In a post yesterday I summarized the key results from the empirical analysis . In this one I will focus on the survey results . <p> The survey had responses from people in 53 countries , with a fairly even gender split , and around one-third holding senior positions in their organizations . Is there more they can do to support companies in addressing COVID-19 and inequality ? Should they be doing more themselves in their own organizations ? <p> When asked about the future of purposeful leadership based on a multi-stakeholder model , 84 percent expected it to be higher than today . " For companies , in addition to the suggestions I made in my previous post , the report includes work force audits on diversity , compensation , and benefits which are publicly reported ; developing and publishing plans that show how political spending , tax payments , and capital allocation are in line with the company 's stated purpose ; and separating the role of Chair and CEO . <p> These recommendations can not be made without the support of a company 's shareholders -- and recall that investors were perceived as being the least effective of all groups in addressing COVID-19 and inequality .
