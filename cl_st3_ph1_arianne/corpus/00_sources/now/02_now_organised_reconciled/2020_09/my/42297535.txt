<h> Sustainable Development Goals more relevant than ever <h> Share this article <p> THE year 2020 is a significant and defining year for the international community . <p> Today marks five years that the Sustainable Development Goals ( SDG ) framework or Agenda 2030 was unanimously endorsed by the 193 member states of the United Nations on September 25 , 2015 . <p> Because progress to date has been too slow , this is also the year that the decade of action was announced to accelerate the SDG . And all of these coincide with the 75th anniversary of the UN . <p> Rewinding the clock back to five years ago , the powerful 15-year framework of the SDG that emerged from the largest global consultation ever was formulated to ensure all member states work towards a common goal -- a renewed and reinvigorated global commitment to a more inclusive , prosperous and environmentally sustainable future for all underpinned by good governance . <p> Guided by the principle of leaving no one behind , the SDG is a call to action to all parts of society -- government , businesses @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ achieve the most ambitious common vision that the world has ever had . <p> Additionally , the SDG marked a step change in ambition over the foregoing Millennium Development Goals , not only in their coverage -- 17 versus 8 goals , 169 versus 48 targets -- but also in the level of aspiration -- seeking the absolute elimination of deprivations . <p> Moreover , what marks the SDG out as especially innovative is their practicality -- offering a policy and implementation " steer " to governments and combining this with a synchronised monitoring framework . <p> The SDG approach allows countries to adapt the targets to national characteristics and adopt the framework into domestic planning and delivery arrangements . And I commend the Malaysian government in its efforts to do just this . <p> However , as all countries began planning and executing their SDG vision , many have faced challenges . The UN secretary-general in early 2020 announced a dire situation -- progress towards sustainable development is seriously off-track . <p> We have made inroads into extreme poverty and child mortality , access to energy and decent work @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ UN support , private sector committing to various goals , civil society on the ground pushing the envelope , the youth raising their voices and challenging the business as usual . But much more needs to be done . <p> The decade of action -- to accelerate SDG achievement was called due to the worrisome progress . <p> The SDGs can only be moved if all parts are moving as one -- from government , private sector , civil society and the people . <p> Each has a role to play from identifying SDG bottlenecks and designing policies to address them , effective implementation , channelling of financial resources to where it is needed most and effecting behavioural change to simply doing the right thing for people , planet , peace and prosperity . <p> The goals under these four " Ps " being highly interdependent and achievable through integrated approaches , strategic investments and strong partnerships . <p> Today , we have a new common enemy -- the Covid-19 pandemic generated first , a health crisis , and then an economic crisis -- halting progress and threatening to reverse @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the challenge can not be underplayed . An additional dimension is the distribution of impacts , with the vulnerable suffering the most . <p> We need also to think about the dimensions beyond income -- health , directly via Covid-19 infections , but also via increased reticence to attend hospitals , and education , where schooling has been heavily interrupted . <p> But we must see the opportunity before us .
