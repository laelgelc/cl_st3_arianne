<h> Call to repave Rundle Mall ( again ) to combat climate change <p> Seven years after the Adelaide City Council replaced Rundle Mall 's patchy pavers with slick granite , it is being advised to again rip up the paving and install " cooler materials " to help combat the effects of climate change . <p> Forty-four years ago today , then Premier Don Dunstan arrived in a horse and cart to a crowd of about 10,000 people gathered around a champagne-flowing fountain to mark the official opening of Rundle Mall . <p> It followed a $782,000 repaving job involving 580,000 bricks and the closure of Rundle Mall to traffic , making it Australia 's first pedestrianised main street . <p> Since then , the Mall has undergone several face-lifts , most recently in 2013 , when the council spent about $30 million replacing trees , installing new lighting and outdoor seating , and ripping up the old 44-milimetre-thick pavers and replacing them with 80 millimetre-thick granite . <h> Advertisement <p>
