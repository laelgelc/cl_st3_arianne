<h> Protests have been criminalised under COVID . What is incitement ? How is it being used in the pandemic ? <h> Author <h> Disclosure statement <p> Maria O'Sullivan has previously received funding from the Commonwealth Attorney-Generals department to undertake legal research . Maria O'Sullivan also volunteers as supervisor of the Rights Advocacy Project for Liberty Victoria .. <h> Partners <p> The Victorian government is taking a hard line against protests as it tries to get COVID-19 under control . As Premier Daniel Andrews said on Thursday , <p> it 's not the time to protest ... regardless of what you 're protesting about . <p> In this climate , several people have recently been charged with incitement in relation to " anti-lockdown " protests in the state . <p> So , what is incitement ? And what makes it so complicated during a pandemic ? <h> What is incitement ? <p> The offence of " incitement " criminalises behaviour that encourages others to commit a crime before the crime takes place . <p> Interestingly , the incitement need not be acted on for an offence to be committed . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , murder , that the " inciter " encourages another person to commit murder , even if that crime does not actually take place . <p> Three men have also been charged with incitement over allegedly coordinating a " Freedom Day " protest in Melbourne on Saturday . <p> These charges were also made in a different context back in April . Then , protesters staged a car convoy in Melbourne to highlight the plight of refugees . The organiser of the protest was arrested in his home under the charge of incitement , and again , before the protest had begun . <h> What is significant about these charges ? <p> The application of incitement to protest is controversial for two main reasons . <p> Firstly , incitement is normally linked to the commission of a serious crime , such as murder or assault . The act of protest is not , of itself , a crime . <p> Secondly , breaching COVID restrictions is an offence under public health legislation and can lead to the issuing of a fine . But charging someone with incitement makes this an @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to much more serious punishment - such as imprisonment and , importantly , the recording of a criminal conviction . This may have serious consequences for a person 's employment , if they have a criminal record . <h> The complexity of COVID <p> Because of the very serious nature of the charge of incitement , it has very rarely been used in relation to protest . This is because normally , protesting is not of itself a criminal offence . <p> In ordinary circumstances , protesting would only be considered a crime if protesters damage property , commit trespass or pose a threat to public order . <p> Normally , protesting is not an offence.David Crosling/AAP <p> For example , climate change activists in Queensland were recently charged with a number of offences , including using a dangerous attachment device to interfere with transport infrastructure and trespassing on a railway ( their convictions were thrown out on Wednesday ) . <p> Using incitement to protest under COVID is particularly complex , because the use of such a criminal charge for a breach of a public health restriction is unclear @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , various " normal " activities , such as organising public gatherings and assembling in public places , are subject to a penalty . <p> The application of incitement to protest during COVID is also messy as there is a significant lack of clarity about what some terms mean in Victoria 's stay at home directions . For instance , can protest be seen as an " essential " activity for the purpose of the directions ? I have argued elsewhere that is should . <h> New territory <p> The use of incitement for protest under COVID poses new and complex legal questions . <p> These may well be tested in courts in time to come . Until then , Victorians under COVID need to look at incitement in a very different manner than before .
