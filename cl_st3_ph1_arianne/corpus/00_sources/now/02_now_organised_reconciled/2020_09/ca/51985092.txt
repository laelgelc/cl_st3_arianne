John Lappa/Sudbury Star <p> City council this week approved a plan to put into action the Community Energy and Emissions Plan ( CEEP ) . <p> The plan was hatched three years ago and aims to make Greater Sudbury carbon-neutral by 2050 . In May 2019 the city declared a climate emergency and this is the next step in taking action to mitigate the effects of climate change . <p> " A CEEP was under preparation in 2018 and 2019 with input from local energy stakeholders and the public , " a staff report noted . " In addition to assessing local energy and greenhouse gas emission profiles from the various sectors , the CEEP outlines the actions needed to reach council 's stated target @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ expressed in council 's climate emergency declaration of May 2019 . " <p> The city identified 18 goals to help it achieve its objectives . Some fall under municipal control , while others will require the participation of senior levels of government and corporations . All will require buy-in from the community . <p> " The goals must be achieved for the target of net-zero GHG emissions by 2050 to be attained , " the report continued . " Achieving these goals relies on co-ordinated efforts among stakeholders , upper level government incentives , innovation , education and awareness moving forward . " <p> Some of the goals are ambitious , like net-zero emissions in city buildings by 2040 and the total electrification of the city fleet by 2035 . The city also plans to enhance its reforestation efforts through the highly successful regreening program . <p> " The CEEP also includes goals over which the municipality has far less control , such as increasing industrial energy efficiency 35 per cent by 2040 and 100 per cent of new vehicle sales being electric by 2030 , " the report continued @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is a shared responsibility .
