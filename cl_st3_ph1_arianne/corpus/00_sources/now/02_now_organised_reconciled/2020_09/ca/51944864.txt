<h> Farming community mourns sudden loss of Peter Dowling <p> Peter Dowling died on Sunday . He was an integral part of the National Farmers Union and a vocal advocate for farmers , both locally and nationally . ( Andree Thorpe/Supplied Photo ) AndreeThorpe / jpg , KI <p> The Kingston-area farming community is mourning the sudden loss of one of its own . <p> Peter Dowling died unexpectedly but peacefully on Sunday . <p> The Howe Island dairy farmer was a central figure in the National Farmers Union for much of his life , having had a hand in some of the most influential agricultural policy battles and farm movements for the past several decades , not only locally but also nationally . <p> Dowling graduated from Regiopolis-Notre Dame Catholic High School and then received a bachelor of science in agriculture degree from the University of Guelph in 1973 . He worked for the Ontario Ministry of Agriculture before returning to his ancestral farm in 1975 to carry on his parents ' work . <p> In a tribute written by his brother-in-law , Rick Munroe , Dowling is described as being " raised in the NFU " by his parents , John and Joyce Dowling , who were founding members of the national organization . All three worked within the union locally , regionally and nationally . <p> Dowling was the provincial co-ordinator for the union for many years and spent time working on files such as supporting supply management systems , advocating for farmers nationally on free trade issues as well as on their right to unionize and bargain collectively , raising the alarm on climate change and advocating for food safety laws , inspection and enforcement , among many other issues . <p> He was an instrumental part of the group that successfully opposed the introduction of recombinant bovine growth hormone to Canada 's dairy system , synthetic hormones used to increase milk production in cows in the United States . <p> " He was very generous with his time and very devoted to those issues , " Munroe told the Whig-Standard on Wednesday . " How a person does all that they do on their farm and still has time for political issues and a farm organization , that 's part of the problem that farmers face . ... Pete , and Dianne , too , were absolutely devoted to farm issues . " <p> Dowling and his wife , Dianne , ran Doublejay Farms , the Dowling family farm on Howe Island , for more than four decades . When they converted their operation to organic , it was one of the first of its kind in the region , and they produced organic milk until selling their milk quota in 2019 . <p> " They were one of the first ( to go organic ) , and at the time it was a bit of a novelty , " Munroe said . " There was a lot to learn . It 's not easy to control weeds and dairy herd health without antibiotics , without chemicals . He was an attentive person and learned how to manage those things organically as he went . He was a hard worker . " <p> Munroe said Dowling 's commitment to national and local farm issues was no doubt a sacrifice , but it was time that Dowling believed was necessary to invest in the future of food production in Canada . <p> " Those are hours that you do n't have for something else , " he said . " He certainly believed that Canada would have the sort of food system that we work for . It 's not just going to happen magically , especially when you have large corporations always working to maximize their returns , which , unfortunately , often comes at the expense of farmers . <p> Munroe admired Dowling 's dedication . <p> " It 's one thing to have commitment and interest and concern , " he said . " It 's another to stick with it for decades and remain that active , that persistent . We need more people like that . " <p> Mara Shaw , executive director of the National Farmers Union , described Dowling as a core voice of progressive farming and collaborative activism . <p> " The whole concept ( in the union ) is that our voices are stronger together than each one of us trying to make our voices heard , " she said . " Peter was born into the NFU but quietly , his entire life , has been dogged in making sure that that message continues , even in the face of the forces that try to make us think we 're not connected to each other . " <p> Closer to home , Dowling was a soft-spoken but persistent advocate for local food and small family farms . He was involved with the Save Our Prison Farms efforts and was among those who took cattle to Parliament Hill to protest the closure of prison farms in Kingston . <p> " Peter had really innate leadership skills , " longtime friend , fellow union member and past local president Andrea Cumpson said . " He was wise , quiet-spoken , an extremely gentle man but a very focused individual . I saw him also as a nurturer . ... The way he said things and expressed himself , you know he cared about people in his community and in the farming community . He had a strong sense of social justice and a good understanding of policy . " <p> Dowling was one of those behind local movements such as Food Down the Road and Feast of Fields , working to grow local food connections and generate interest in locally grown food . <p> " He and others were integral in making local food popular in Kingston , " Josh Suppan , president of NFU Local 316 , said of his friend . " We 're really lucky in Kingston compared to other communities in Canada . There is demand here for local food and a lot of that has to do with the work that the NFU did , and that 's specifically work Peter did . He helped lay the groundwork . " <p> Dowling was a ready and listening ear for young farmers , willing to offer advice and mentorship to the industry 's next generation . His interest in national farm policy had to do with his thoughts for the future of farmers , Munroe said . <p> " As the years went on , he was always thinking of the next generation , whether it was to do with environmental issues or young farmers getting started , getting financed , " Munroe said . <p> Dowling was influential during the time Suppan was working to establish his own farm business in Frontenac County . <p> " If it was n't for Peter and others , it would have been a lot harder , " Suppan said . " When we were first looking for land to farm , I was going to Peter all the time . ... I 'd ask him about soil conditions , equipment . He was always more than happy to field my questions . I think he enjoyed it , too . He lived and breathed agriculture , so to see young people wanting to be part of that ( made him happy ) . " <p> Cumpson believes Dowling 's profound impact will carry on in the future work done in the farming community , through not only policies he helped influence but also the young people he mentored who now take on leadership roles in Canada 's farm organizations such as the National Farmers Union . <p> " It seems crazy that such an important voice is gone , " she said . " But I do n't think it is gone . I know it will carry through . " <p> Dowling 's family has asked for donations in his memory to be directed to the National Farmers Foundation ( **40;791;TOOLONG ) , Howe Island Fire and Rescue ( via Township of Frontenac Islands ) or the Kingston Chapter of Parkinson 's Canada .
