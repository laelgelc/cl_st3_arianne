<p> If we did not know it before , we know it now : pandemics present dire threats to our lives , similar to climate change and nuclear proliferation . Confronting these threats requires social and technical innovation and the willingness to view potential solutions in entirely new ways . <p> As Canada struggles with calibrating its response to COVID-19 , the limits of our existing crisis strategies are plain to see . <p> Political leaders are stuck between controlling the spread of the pandemic and resuming commercial and economic activity . How quickly should restrictions on confinement and social distancing be relaxed ? And for whom ? Their responses rely largely on the extensive use of personal protective equipment ( notably masks ) , deployment of immunity tests and test-and-tracing technologies . <p> There are two problems with this approach : first , they are based on after-the-fact views of COVID-19 's spread . And second , this approach treats the pandemic as a medical problem . <p> Managing the unknowns <p> The facts of this virus are becoming clear . It could protect medical systems from being overwhelmed and communities from the economic pain of indiscriminate lock-downs . It could help build herd immunity with lower mortality -- and fast .
