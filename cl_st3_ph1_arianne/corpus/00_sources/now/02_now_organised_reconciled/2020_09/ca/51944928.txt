With the UN 's Food and Agriculture Organization 's Second International Conference on Nutrition ending in Rome on November 21 , once again the West is being faced with its responsibility to confront the problem of hunger . <p> The world produces nearly 3,000 calories per person , per day . It 's beyond sad . It 's absurd . <p> So why is it so hard to get the right food to the right people at the right time ? To begin with , staggering levels of food waste occur all along the food chain . Post-harvest losses in poorer countries run anywhere from 20 to 50 per cent of yield , depending on the country and whether we 're talking about grains , fresh fruits , vegetables or animal products . A few years ago the world 's 7 billionth baby was born ; since then there has much talk about the planet 's population growth to 9 billion in the coming decades . On the other hand , many demographers have started focusing on the lower end of UN population projections showing growth leveling off -- even reversing , perhaps much sooner and at lower levels than initially expected . <p> Even sub-Saharan Africa -- a place where traditionally the average woman gives birth to six children -- is showing signs of slower population growth , in part due to high economic growth rates and the changing role and status of women . For example , experts such as socio-economist Nicolas Bricas have argued that one of the biggest emitters of greenhouse gases in the global food system is not the international shipping of foodstuffs -- but middle-class consumers driving to the grocery store .
