<h> LETTER OF THE WEEK : Municipalities a secret weapon in fight against climate change <p> Premium content <p> Contributed <p> Published : Sep 11 at 11:47 a.m . <p> Updated : Sep 12 at 6 a.m . <p> Sorry you must be at least 19 years of age to consume this content . <p> Stock image - Contributed <p> If you care about the planet , you should engage with municipal politics . Here is why : <p> In 2018 , the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change warned we had 12 years to slash emissions by 45 per cent to limit the catastrophic impacts of climate change . Record high temperatures , fires , storms , droughts and melting ice caps are grim reminders that time is ticking . The situation can feel hopeless , particularly for young people such as myself . <p> Although frequently overlooked as dull and irrelevant , municipalities may be the secret weapon in the fight against climate change . The Federation of Canadian Municipalities calculates that 44 per cent of Canadian greenhouse gas emissions are under municipal control . Municipalities directly control emissions from sources such as landfills and municipal operations . They indirectly influence pollution through tools such as land-use bylaws to require efficient building standards or policies to encourage public and active transport . <p> Municipal governments are beginning to flex their environmental muscles to the benefit of the communities they serve . For example , while Nova Scotia Power still relies on coal , petcoke and natural gas , the Alternative Resource Energy Authority ( AREA ) supplies 40 per cent of the energy requirements of Mahone Bay , Antigonish and Berwick from renewable sources . Thanks to its partnership with AREA , Antigonish 's electric utility has gross revenues in excess of $12 million . Furthermore , many Nova Scotians struggle to afford their energy bills . The award-winning Energize Bridgewater plan aims to achieve a clean and affordable energy economy and cut energy poverty in the community in half by 2028 . Just imagine the possibilities if these types of initiatives were replicated across the province , and country . <p> On Oct. 17 , Nova Scotians will return to the polls to select their municipal councils . This election is a critical opportunity to tackle climate change . Environmental sustainability must be a priority on every candidate 's platform . Individuals who care about maintaining a liveable planet must get out and vote . <p> Community members from Truro and Colchester have organized an event to bring renewable energy to the forefront of the election campaign . " Plug in ! Community Renewable Energy Forum " will take place via Zoom on Sept. 19 from 2:30 p.m. to 4:30 p.m . The event features four different community renewable energy initiatives from across the province . Community members and candidates are invited to attend .
