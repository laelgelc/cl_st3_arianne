Raymond Bowe/BarrieToday <p> All lakes have their mysteries and Lake Simcoe 's is a doozie . <p> The lake 's health is now improving even while harmful phosphorus loading is increasing , according to officials at the Lake Simcoe Region Conservation Authority ( LSRCA ) . <p> " It is unusual in that it 's defying these lake ecology laws or rules that we 've had set down for hundreds of years now , " Brian Ginn , the LSRCA 's limnologist or lake doctor , told BarrieToday . " The basic law is that if you increase the amount of phosphorus going into the lake , you are going to increase the amount that 's in the lake water and you 're going to have changes from it -- more algae , more plants . And when these things die and decompose , the oxygen is going to decrease . <p> " It 's this lack of oxygen that was the problem in Lake Simcoe , that caused the problems for the cold-water fish @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 1990s , " Ginn added . <p> Phosphorus loads are calculated on a hydrological year -- from June until May of the following year -- and the conservation authority 's most recent data shows 131 tonnes of phosphorus entering the lake in 2017-18 , which should have resulted in 13 to 18 micrograms per litre , which is too high . <p> Instead , the phosphorus concentration stayed at 6.8 micrograms per litre , which meets provincial water quality guidelines of less than 10 micrograms per litre , and the oxygen levels were 6.5 milligrams per litre , or very close to the Lake Simcoe Protection Plan 's target of 7.0 milligrams per litre . <p> " So it 's below the Lake Simcoe Protection Plan goal , but it 's very close to it , which is a good-news story , " Ginn said . " We 're trying to figure it out . " <p> The LSRCA has phosphorus-load data combined from Lake Simcoe , Kempenfelt Bay and Cook 's Bay dating back to 2000 . Ginn says they are basically gone from Lake Simcoe , with only a few left in Cook 's Bay . <p> " The problem with the second species is they are pretty much version 2.0 of zebra mussels , " Ginn said . " Zebra mussels were limited in the fact they could n't exist on like a muddy bottom , because they 'd be smothered .
