<h> A flight plan to marvel at <p> Sitting on Manitoulin Island 's north shore one evening recently , my wife and I witnessed what National Geographic bills as one of the greatest natural events on Earth -- the annual migration of Danaus plexippus , the monarch butterfly . <p> We 'd noticed an occasional monarch fluttering overhead . But what we 'd first regarded as an occasional fly-by became a steady stream . The monarchs , we realized , were executing a flight plan . Their bearing was southward , and they were using the shoreline as a navigational guide . <p> They were en route from their summer breeding grounds in eastern Canada to over-wintering grounds in Mexico and California . It 's a journey of some 3,000 miles -- remarkable for a creature weighing 15-20 grams . No other insect species on Earth performs such a feat . <p> With their showy orange , black-veined and white-spotted wings , monarchs are perhaps the most recognizable lepidopterans on the planet . <p> More than beautiful , they contribute to the planet 's health . It takes the birth and death of four generations , each life cycle lasting four to five weeks , before the species as such will reach its summer habitat . <p> Then , when autumn rolls around , a " super generation " of monarchs will wing its way back to Mexico . That cohort can live up to eight months . <p> It 's one of nature 's great unexplained phenomena : that despite four generations of monarchs having died over the course of their complete migration , the species knows to return to the same winter habitat every year . <p> On their long journey southward , monarchs conserve energy by riding columns of rising warm air , reducing the need to flap their wings for sustained periods . They cleverly evolved to mimic monarchs and , although smaller , look nearly identical . <p> They do n't pack the poisonous punch that monarchs do . But birds and other animals with a taste for butterflies do n't know that . As they avoid monarchs so they avoid viceroys . <p> Monarchs have inspired a rich folklore .
