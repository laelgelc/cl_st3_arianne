<p> Live fast , die young is a truism often applied to rock stars but could just as easily describe trees , according to new research . Trees that grow rapidly have a shorter lifespan , which could spell bad news for tackling the climate crisis . <p> Trees grow faster in warmer conditions , and this should act as a natural brake on global heating , as they take up and store more carbon dioxide from the air as they grow . But the new study casts doubt on this beneficial cycle , finding that the faster trees grow , the sooner they die -- and therefore stop storing carbon . <p> Some fast-growing trees , including conifer species in cold regions , have long been known to show shorter lifespans , but what was not known was the impact of warmer conditions that can spur growth as global heating accelerates .
