<p> TORONTO , Oct. 26 , 2016 /CNW/ - Global wildlife populations face a plunge of more than two thirds during the 50-year period ending in 2020 as a result of human activities , according to WWF 's Living Planet Report 2016 released today . The report , produced in collaboration with the Zoological Society of London , shows how people are overpowering the planet for the first time in Earth 's history , and highlights the changes needed in the way society is fed , fuelled and financed . <p> According to the report , global populations of mammals , birds , fish , amphibians and reptiles have already declined by 58 per cent between 1970 and 2012 , the most recent year with available data . At this trajectory , the decline could reach 67 per cent by 2020 . <p> For more on the Canadian context , please see addendum . <p> Key findings <p> Wildlife are disappearing at an unprecedented rate , with : <p> A 38 per cent decline in land-based populations . <p> A 36 per cent decline in ocean-based populations . <p> An @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ biggest threats to species are : <p> Loss and degradation of habitat ( through agriculture and logging , and man-made changes to freshwater systems ) . <p> Overexploitation ( through overfishing , hunting and poaching ) . <p> Invasive species and disease . <p> Pollution . <p> Growing climate change impact . <p> Humans are exceeding planetary boundaries : <p> The resources of 1.6 planets each year are used to provide the goods and services consumed annually .
