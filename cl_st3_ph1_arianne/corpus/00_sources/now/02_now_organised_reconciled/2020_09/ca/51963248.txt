<p> Extreme weather and climate events causing extensive damage are a fact of the Canadian climate , and this year is no exception . <p> On June 13 , a mammoth hailstorm pounded Calgary with damage in excess of $1 billion dollars , the most expensive hailstorm in Canadian history . In early July , eastern Canada was subjected to both persistent extreme heat coupled with high humidity and major flooding . <p> As we cope with these events , questions invariably arise about what role climate change may have played . Has a particular extreme been made worse because of our changing climate ? How will these extremes change in the future ? <p> Water cycle accelerating <p> Many of these questions are linked with the hydrologic cycle -- the evaporation of water from the Earth 's surface and its vegetation , the transport of water vapour in the atmosphere from one place to another and the ultimate return of the water to the surface as precipitation . <p> The water cycle speeds up when the climate warms . Satellite data available since 1988 indicate that the atmosphere has moistened , and that this is primarily due to the human-induced warming of the climate . <p> Individual extreme events are , however , influenced by many other factors . A storm can leave behind moisture at the surface that can re-evaporate and strengthen subsequent events . <p> The collision between a cold front and a lake breeze can lead to heavy precipitation . A delayed lake freeze-up during a warm winter can enhance lake-effect snowfall . Statistical analyses of data from these rain gauges confirm that rainfall extremes have grown more intense at the global and continental levels , in agreement with climate models . <p> There are broad indications that these changes in rainfall extremes are due to human influence on the climate at global and continental scales . Extreme one-day rainfall events that occurred about once every 20 years in the past are now occurring about once every 15 years . <p> Even so , scientists still struggle to confidently say that a particular extreme rainfall event is the result of climate change .
