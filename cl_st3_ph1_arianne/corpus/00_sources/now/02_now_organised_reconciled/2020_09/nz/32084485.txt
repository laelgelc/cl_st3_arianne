This was evident with the decision of the New Zealand courts to deport a Pacific man , Ioane Teitiota , facing displacement due to climate change in 2015 . The courts insisted Teitiota and his family could return to lives of dignity in Kiribati , despite compelling expert evidence and heart-breaking testimonies from the family making it clear their particular homes were uninhabitable . This explains why many Pacific peoples and governments have rejected mass relocation schemes that have put forward as quick fix alternatives to climate change adaptation and mitigation . In other words , why is it failing to help " family " clearly in need of assistance ? <p> For many black , indigenous and other peoples of colour , well-accustomed to being let down ( and outright abused ) by their governments , the answer is easy -- the Government 's inaction in the Pacific is rooted in racism . The predominantly white Government considers the suffering of black and brown bodies in the Pacific unimportant and unworthy of intervention , especially because intervening could threaten their own economic and political interests . In other words , the Government views Pacific peoples as disposable , especially when weighed up against the benefits of saving money , and maintaining fossil fuel profits and a trade relationship with Indonesia . <p> In seeing Pacific peoples in this way , the New Zealand Government commits to the " politics of disposability " in which racially marginalised populations are imagined to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and therefore become " collateral damage in the construction of the neoliberal order " . <p> In addition to its violent colonisation of Pacific nations , New Zealand 's politics of disposability were evident when the government lured Pacific migrants to the country to take up factory jobs following the post-war boom in the 1950s and 60s . But when the 1973 oil crisis hit and recession followed , the government made these migrants their scapegoats . Racist anti-Pacific media campaigns blamed them for mass unemployment and ' dawn raids ' were carried out to " dispose of " ( deport ) people deemed no longer of any use . <p> Even today , New Zealand 's politics of disposability is evident with , for example , the egregious exploitation of Pacific migrants via the fundamentally flawed Regional Seasonal Employment ( RSE ) Scheme ; current struggles to get the Government to grant permanent residency to Pacific peoples whose visas have expired ; and the Government 's inequitable wage subsidy scheme which allowed some employers to fund obscenely large senior salaries while making large numbers of low-paid workers redundant . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is not just a result of our capitalist neoliberal order . Rather , it is part of New Zealand 's faithfulness to racial capitalism which refers to " the process in which social and economic value is derived from the racial identity of others " , when " white individuals and predominantly white institutions use non-white people to acquire social and economic value " . These processes can range from the exploitation of black and brown bodies as property or tools for cheap labour , to hiring practices and ' diversity and inclusion ' programmes that claim to address under-representation of peoples of colour in the name of racial equality , but are in fact corporate strategies designed to advance profit in an increasingly multi-cultural society . Essentially , racial capitalism allows for laws , policies and programmes that commodify and promote the identities , labour and genius of black , indigenous , peoples of colour , without ever compromising or weakening white supremacy . <p> One of the most painful injustices of New Zealand 's politics of disposability and racial capitalism comes from knowing that if white people from @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ world were facing displacement due to climate change or being routinely brutalised and murdered by armed forces -- New Zealand would never remain silent because these white people would be seen and treated as " family " . <p> But us Pacific peoples have come to learn that we are only seen as " family " by the government and wider society when we win rugby tournaments , or when we receive praise and acclaim on the international stage , or when politicians are seeking clout and political power here or overseas . Yet , when we need New Zealand 's predominantly white Government to compromise economic gain or its political relationships to help and protect us , we are simply treated like disposable pieces of property whose pain and suffering can be freely ignored . <p> So how can politics of disposability and racial capitalism in New Zealand be dismantled ?
