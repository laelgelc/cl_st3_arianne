<h> ' Wintec women in engineering are making a stand ' <p> Wintec 's women engineers featured in an image on social media recently with the words " We are a diverse engineering team " and were surprised at the attention they got . The post was so popular , it generated a notification from LinkedIn they were trending on #engineering . <p> Engineering may often be considered a man 's world but Wintec Engineering teachers , Dr Maryam Moridnejad , Sarla Kumari , Josy Cooper , Elena Eskandarymalayery and their manager , Dr Trudy Harris do n't agree . They want to see more diversity in their engineering classes , and they are on a mission to change up the ratio . <p> The five women have a mix of mechanical , civil and electrical qualifications . <p> Wintec Group Director , Trades and Engineering and Industrial Design , Dr Shelley Wilson says women make up 5-10 percent of engineering students at Wintec and the future is looking bright for graduates who can expect diverse opportunities . <p> " We work closely with industry to train engineers for an @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , climate change and the influence of COVID-19 . <p> " The scope for engineering careers is exciting and graduates can aim for careers in the manufacturing , roading and other areas of infrastructure , power industry , chemical and mechatronics areas for example . These environments are attracting a diverse workforce . " <p> Kumari says the perception of engineering sometimes does n't help . <p> " Many people believe engineering is about working outside , getting your hands dirty and a study path with a maths-heavy study component . <p> " Engineering offers broad opportunities and engineers mainly work in an office designing and creating projects for people . " <p> Cooper agrees that engineering is people orientated as well as project focussed . <p> " Everything you design has to work with and be for people . It can be really compelling as a career . " <p> Dr Moridnejad , who shared the trending post about diversity on LinkedIn says that in her home country , Iran , with more restrictions on women , there are more female engineers than in New Zealand . <p> Surprised @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and curious , ( a trait with engineers ) , she wanted to find out why . <p> Since last year , she collaborated with Cooper and Associate Professor Wendy Fox-Turnbull from the University of Waikato on a research project to find out influencing factors and barriers for selection of engineering pathways for women in New Zealand . <p> " So far , in the New Zealand context , we found that barriers to selection of engineering pathway for females include the school system ; a lack of career and subject choice guidance available to students at school , a lack of promotion of the profession , and society 's perception of engineers as being masculine - ' a tradie working in a workshop ' . <p> " Unfortunately , the low number of women studying engineering at tertiary level is not just a problem here in New Zealand . Worldwide around 10 percent of students studying engineering are females .
