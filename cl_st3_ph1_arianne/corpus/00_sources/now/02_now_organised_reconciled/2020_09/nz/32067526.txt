<h> Here 's an idea for the Greens <p> I have been very critical of the Greens -- as much as anything for their righteous promotion of policies they must know will do immeasurable damage to our economy , let alone those poor buggers who are really struggling to keep afloat . <p> For my trouble , l have been accused of being ( among other things ) a racist , a dangerous denier of climate change , an " out of touch " pale , stale male -- the list goes on . <p> I do admit to having a healthy scepticism about much of the mantra that masquerades as pure science in this volatile environment , but regard the rest of the accusations as nonsense . I 'm happy to wear these insults as a badge of honour in a world that seems hell-bent on escaping from the old standards of reason and truth . <p> It is a small price to pay if even one of my suggestions for change is adopted by those who have the power to lead us to a better , more equitable @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , l have decided to select one area where there might be enough common ground to reach agreement on a way forward . <p> Although , l would prefer that some of my potentially more significant ideas for growth in mitigation against climate change could be chosen as the topic of conversation , the silence of my detractors regarding these possibilities probably rules them out as viable options . <p> So , here 's the suggestion . <p> Most people involved with the agricultural industry know of the parlous state of the crossbred wool industry . Not sure how much the average city dweller will know about the recent dramatic decline in returns for this once great contributor to our nation 's coffers . <p> Our local provincial history is littered with stories and structural evidence of how we financed much of that development from one commodity . Remnants of this proud age ( buildings like the NZ Shipping Company wool store at Tokomaru Bay ) are still to be seen around the region , where they once operated as critical parts of the nation 's export infrastructure .
