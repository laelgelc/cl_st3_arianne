<h> Last Updated : Tuesday 01 September 2020 <p> Climate change projections have yielded predictions about African regions more vulnerable to future malaria outbreaks : The growing climate crisis could affect the distribution and intensity of disease transmission in Africa over the next century , according to a recent study . <p> Malaria is a climate-sensitive vector-borne disease responsible for an estimated 435,000 deaths from 219 million malaria cases worldwide in 2017 . At least 92 per cent of these deaths were reported in Africa . <p> A detailed mapping of malaria transmission is vital for the distribution of health resources and targeting of control measures . <p> According to the study , published in Nature Communicationson August 28 , 2020 , ambient air temperature controls the rate of several components of malaria transmission . <p> Earlier , monthly rainfall was typically used as a proxy for habitat availability . However , more complex hydrological processes such as infiltration , soil moisture , evaporation etc , need to be factored in to assess the intensity of malaria transmission in Africa more accurately . <p> The study , led by Universities @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a malaria climatic suitability model with a continental-scale hydrological model that represented real-world processes of evaporation , infiltration and flow through rivers . <p> The study found that while flowing water in large river channels did not provide suitable larval habitat for African vector mosquitoes , associated smaller water bodies in adjacent bankside and floodplain areas could make for ideal larval breeding ground . <p> The river corridors could be the most prominent hot spots of malaria transmission , according to the study . <p> The study identified the Niger and Senegal rivers in Mali and Senegal , and the Webi Juba and Webi Shabeelie rivers in Somalia , as suitable for malaria transmission despite extending beyond the geographical ranges predicted to be climatically suitable for malaria by all rainfall thresholds . <p> Overall , the findings showed very minor future changes in the total area suitable for malaria transmission ; the geographical location of many of those areas , however , was found to shift substantially . <p> In the majority of models studied , an increase in malaria transmission is preceded by a decrease from 1971-2005 to 2011-2040 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> Mark Smith , the study 's lead author , said : <p> " Since the huge efforts to eradicate malaria from parts of the world , the areas where we observe malaria today are only a part of the total area that would otherwise be suitable for malaria transmission .
