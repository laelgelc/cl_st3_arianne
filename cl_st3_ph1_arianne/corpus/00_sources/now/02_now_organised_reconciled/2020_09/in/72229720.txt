The extra levy on coal was first imposed in 2010 at Rs.50 per tonne . Gradually the current regime led by Prime Minister Narendra Modi hiked the cess to Rs.200 in 2015 and then to Rs.400 per tonne of coal and lignite in March 2016 . In July 2017 the Coal cess ( or the carbon tax ) was subsumed under GST Compensation Cess ( at Rs.400/tonne ) . <p> Till the implementation of GST in July 2017 , coal cess collection was more than Rs.56,000 crores ( as very little was used from this fund ) . Under GST input tax credit on coal cess is limited to only the tax liability of the cess . However , steel and aluminium manufacturing companies use coal as an input but are not eligible for the compensation cess credit . The unutilised input credit can be claimed as refund , but only if the product ( i.e. steel or aluminum ) is exported . Reality check -- The volume of steel exported from India is not high .
