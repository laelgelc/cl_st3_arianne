<h> Daily Court Digest : Major environment orders ( September 10 , 2020 ) <h> Last Updated : Thursday 10 September 2020 <p> Marina beach pollution <p> The NGT directed the Tamil Nadu State Pollution Control Board ( TNPCB ) to strictly monitor the sources of pollution affecting the Marina beach in Chennai , including the illegal release of sewage and other effluents from sewage treatment plants ( STP ) and common effluent treatment plants ( CETP ) , in coordination with concerned authorities . <p> The order was in pursuance to a joint committee report on the huge collection of foam at Marina Beach , Chennai . <p> According to the report , the following could be the reasons for the collection of foam at Marina beach : <p> Due to heavy rain , the flow of the Adyar river had increased causing disturbance of debris at the bottom and sludge , in turn leading to froth formation due to high mixing / turbulence . <p> Another reason for the frothing of the sea could be the sediments ( containing organic matter ) from the desilting of the Adyar @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ( CRRT ) could have reached the sea . <p> An analysis report showed variations in the water quality on the day of froth formation and a normal day , indicating that rain water and sewage had mixed . There was a chance of bypassing of untreated / partially treated sewage from Common Sewage Treatment Plants ( CSTP ) of capacity 23 million litres per day ( MLD ) , 54 MLD extension 1 and 40 MLD extension 2 , located at Nesapakkam operated by the Chennai Metro Water Supply and Sewerage Board into the river during the rains before the date of sea foam occurrence . <p> The TNPCB would monitor the CSTPs at Nesapakkam so as to avoid recurrence of sea foam at the mouth of the Adyar .
