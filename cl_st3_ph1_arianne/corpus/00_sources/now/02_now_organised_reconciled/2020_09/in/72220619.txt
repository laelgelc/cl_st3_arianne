<p> One of the key learnings from the raging pandemic across economies is to use resources more efficiently . It is also an opportunity for a re-look at the principles of sustainability and circular economy of recycling and reuse , especially of Li-ion batteries , for which the possibilities are endless in India . <p> ByRichard LoboExploiting the potential for recycling in this sector alone could be the first major step for India to shift towards a circular economy and lead the way for other sectors within the larger economy . As we learn to pick up the pieces from the impact of the unprecedented pandemic upon us and adapt to the new normal , we find new respect for doing more with less . One of the key learnings across economies is to use resources more efficiently . <p> Today , with the global outlook on the back foot and the recovery likely to be challenging and long-drawn , it is perhaps a good time to look at the principles of sustainable living with a lot more commitment than before . <p> The concept of creating a circular economy as a means of promoting sustainability has been around for a while . The recycling of used and discarded materials through a process-oriented recovery mechanism on an industrial scale has been in vogue across sectors . <p> According to a recent report by FICCI the potential size of the global circular economy is estimated to be about USD4.5 trillion by 2030 , of which around USD1.3 trillion can come from recovering wasted and embedded value . The same report also evaluates the size of India 's circular economy to be in the region of half a trillion dollars . <p> Today , the share of EV in the Indian automobile sector is less than 1% . Of the 21.5 million vehicles sold in 2019-20 , only 1.53 lakh ( or around 0.7% ) were electric , and of these more than 95% were two-wheelers . <p> The most exciting aspect of the circular economy is its ability to manage scarce resources effectively while protecting the environment we live in , which in turn helps us handle our broader health-linked concerns better . In that sense , the outcome of a well-developed circular economy goes beyond the economic outcomes . <p> Recycling provides not only for prudent management of resources and saving the environment , both from environmental disruption from mining for resources and waste ending up in landfills , but also for meaningful employment along the value chain , strengthening the local economy . <p> While the concept of a circular economy is gaining momentum in India , perhaps at a measured pace , the possibilities are endless . <p> One of the areas having some movement is in the recycling of Lithium-ion batteries ( LiB ) recovered mainly from mobile phones , laptops , and electric vehicles . <p> Recycling of spent batteries provides valuable metals such as lithium , cobalt , nickel , and manganese along with by-products like graphite from the anode , iron , copper , and aluminium . Almost all of these materials can find their way back to the value chain in the manufacturing of lithium-ion batteries , or industries such as glass , ceramic , pigments , adhesive , and paint , reducing the pressure on mining and in turn reduction of associated ecological imbalances . <p> The two commonly used methods for recovering a critical metal - cobalt from used LiB are Pyrometallurgy and Hydrometallurgy . While the former uses high temperatures in the extraction process , the latter uses aqueous solutions ( or water-based ) for the same making it more energy-efficient and sustainable . <p> It can help reduce CO2 emissions , by up to 90% , through the production cycle , thus leading to a reduction in the overall carbon footprint . <p> The potential size of the global circular economy is estimated to be about USD4.5 trillion by 2030 , of which around USD1.3 trillion can come from recovering wasted and embedded value . India 's circular economy to be in the region of half a trillion dollars . <p> Recovering cobalt in this method also reduces indirect pressure on mining , which is also associated with social concerns of artisanal mining linked to exploitation and the use of child labour , as witnessed in an East African country , as per UNICEF study and reported by Amnesty International . <p> The potential for recovering critical metals and minerals like cobalt , lithium , nickel , and manganese from used and discarded LiB is going be significant as India moves towards a larger share in the global electric vehicle ( EV ) market . In pure volume terms , the potential within the EV sector is going to be huge . <p> Today , the share of EV in the Indian automobile sector is less than 1% . Of the 21.5 million vehicles sold in 2019-20 , only 1.53 lakh ( or around 0.7% ) were electric , and of these more than 95% were two-wheelers . <p> The shift from conventional gasoline-powered vehicles to EVs in India has been very slow , and the main reason for this is high TCO or total cost of ownership for EV buyers . However , recent developments in this market are indicating accelerated growth in EV adoption in India , and this is likely to come from the two-wheeler market . <p> LiB is poised to become the most preferred source of energy for two-wheeler EVs in India . The popularity of LiB is mainly due to its efficiency in terms of better range it offers than the lead-acid batteries . The cost of LiB in the total cost of the vehicle is also expected to drop drastically from around 50-60% to about 30% in the next few years . <p> The Ministry of Environment , Forest and Climate Change ( MoEFCC ) rolled out the E-Waste ( Management ) Rules in 2016 to reduce e-waste production and increase recycling . Under these rules , the government introduced EPR , which makes producers liable to collect 30% to 70% ( over seven years ) of the e-waste they produce . <p> Also , MoEFCC released the draft Battery Management Rules in February 2020 that covers LiB and lead-acid batteries . This step is crucial in the handling and management of LiB and will apply to all stakeholders involved in the life of LiB including its components , consumables , and spare parts which make the product operational . The draft rules also cover manufacturers , producers , collection centres , importers , assemblers , dealers , recyclers , and consumers . <p> The bottom line <p> What emerges from all this is that the demand for LiB from the EV market alone is likely to grow drastically and thus also create a huge potential for recycling the same on a much larger scale than what is being done today . <p> Exploiting the potential for recycling in this sector alone could be the first major step for India to shift towards a circular economy and lead the way for other sectors within the larger economy . <p> LiB recycling by Tata Chemicals <p> Tata Chemicals recycles Li-ion batteries of all forms -- from consumer electronics to energy storage batteries to EVs , leveraging its deep understanding of chemistry and ensuring environmentally safe operations . The company has achieved 99% purity of extracted lithium , cobalt , nickel and manganese , in the form of metal salts . Tata Chemicals views this as a vital step in delivering sustainability and protecting the environment . With this development in place , it launched InsperiCoTM , the world 's first branded recycled cobalt - rooted in sustainably responsible practices ( Zero Liquid Discharge , Zero Solid Wastes , Zero link to Child labour in raw material supply and an approximate reduction of 4.9 tonne CO2 / tonne of Li recycled ) .
