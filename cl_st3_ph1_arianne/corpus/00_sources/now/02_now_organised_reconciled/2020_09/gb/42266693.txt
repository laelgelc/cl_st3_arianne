<h> GINGER GAIRDNER : Trees are living wonders that go back to the roots of history <p> by Brian Cunningham <p> September 12 2020 , 6.00pm <p> This week , Brian Cunningham pauses to admire the most amazing plants of all -- they are the lungs of the Earth and a constant through time ... <p> In the kingdom of plants , trees have to be the most amazing species of all . They affect -- and are a part of -- our lives in so many ways . I suspect that , most of the time , they do so without the majority of us even realising it . <p> Most crucial of all , they are the lungs of our Earth , cleaning the air we breathe . Practically , they provide the timber required for construction , not to mention visually adding shape , structure and interest to our landscapes and gardens . <p> Trees are an essential source of food and a place of safety and shelter for many of our native birds , mammals and insects . Those that are loved and cared for , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , can be around for long time . <p> When I walk past the 400-year-old sycamore in the grounds of Scone Palace planted by King James VI of Scotland in 1617 , I ca n't help but think of all the things that have happened in our history during that tree 's lifetime : the Union of the Crowns , a Jacobite uprising , an industrial revolution and two world wars have all taken place while it was quietly growing away . <p> In Scotland , we are privileged to have plenty trees of similar heritage value and , every year , the Woodland Trust gives us the opportunity to share their stories and celebrate them . <p> Individuals and communities nominate a tree that means something to them , next follows the chance to vote for one to receive the acclaimed title of Scotland 's Tree of the Year . <p> This year there are five trees from which to choose , and they can be found all around the country . <p> A symbol of our times is the sycamore Climate Change Tree growing at the former colliery @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ near Alloa . Flourishing atop the eroding remains of the fossil fuel industry , it displays an extraordinary tangle of roots triumphing as the colliery spoil diminishes . <p> Trees can make wonderful focal points in landscape photography , particularly when using the light of the sunset and sunrise or to capture the scale of mountain views . <p> The Milarrochy Oak has been the subject for many a photographer walking the West Highland Way , unable to resist the opportunity to stop and capture the spot where it grows on a picturesque bay by Loch Lomond . Notable for its contorted appearance , the tree manages to survive standing not-quite-in -- and not-quite-out-of -- the waters of the loch . <p> Down in the Borders is a tree inspiring a project that , to me , represents hope for the future of our natural landscapes . Twenty years ago The Survivor tree was a lone rowan clinging to a stream bank in the Carrifran Valley . Now it shares the landscape not only with its own children but also over half a million other fellow Scottish native trees , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of southern Scotland . <p> At the other end of the country , near Inverness , we have the the Lord President 's Oak . Duncan Forbes , Lord Culloden ( 1685-1747 ) would take his wife Mary to sit on a large stone where together they would enjoy the views of the Moray Firth . To make way for the new Highland railway the stone was moved to its present site in 1855 , when it is thought the oak was planted . It is now loved by the many who pass beneath it , as they enjoy the grandeur of the Culloden forest . <p> The final tree -- without me showing any bias whatsoever , saving the best until last -- we have the clear and absolute winner direct from Courier Country and my home town of St Andrews , Queen Mary 's Thorn . <p> This ancient hawthorn was reputed to have been planted by Mary , Queen of Scots , during one of her frequent visits to the old town back in 1563 . <p> Situated in the quadrant of St Mary 's College at @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the original old stump , which continues to thrive with the aid of props to support it . I know I 'm a plant geek and this is what I enjoy , but take a walk in any garden or park and I guarantee there 's a story to tell behind every tree you see .
