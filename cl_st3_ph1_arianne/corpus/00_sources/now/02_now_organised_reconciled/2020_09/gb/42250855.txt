<p> Police have been criticised for failing to halt a demonstration intended to stifle the freedom of the press as Extinction Rebellion was accused of crossing the line " from protest to planned criminality " . <p> The condemnation came as it emerged warnings the group - known as XR - was planning to target newspaper printworks were reported nine months ago . <p> David Blunkett , the former Labour home secretary , said the anarchic behaviour of the protesters should have been quashed quickly by Hertfordshire Constabulary , which apparently sent only six officers to the Broxbourne plant when the alarm was raised shortly after 10pm on Friday . <p> Asked about the failure of police to remove more than 100 demonstrators who had sat down in the road or chained themselves to infrastructure , Mr Blunkett said : " I think they 're mixing up historic debate about union picketing with protests relating to political issues , which can be dealt with through the normal democratic process . " <p> He said officers should have used " whatever powers were available " to allow people to go about their business . <p> Meanwhile , Richard Walton , former head of the Met 's Counter Terrorism Command and now senior fellow at Policy Exchange , said the group had shifted from mere protesters into organising " planned criminality , and should be treated as such " . <p> He urged police to get better at gathering intelligence to uphold the law and intervene " before such acts of criminality " . <p> " Their reticence to do so undermines our democracy and strengthens extremist groups like XR , " he said . <p> Ian Austin , a Labour MP , said : " The police should uphold the law , enable people to go to work and read the papers they choose . " <p> No warning of the protest was given to local police <p> Officers should have used " whatever powers were available " to allow people to go about their business , according to the former Home Secretary Credit : Paul Grover/The Telegraph <p> No warning about the demonstrations had been made with the local police . Within minutes staff at Broxbourne dialled 999 . However , Donnachadh McCarthy , XR spokesman at the Broxbourne demonstration , said only six officers attended at around 10.15pm . <p> A force spokesman said no intelligence about the protests was received . She refused to say how many police were initially sent or the total number eventually in attendance . She confirmed other southeast forces - including the Met - were summoned to help . <p> Hertfordshire 's Chief Constable Charlie Hall was informed and " immediately " went to headquarters . A total of 48 protesters were removed by mid-morning on Saturday . There were 42 arrests . <p> Merseyside Police also refused to say how many officers went to the Knowsley presses after the 10.30pm alert on Friday . <p> Due to the smaller nature of that protest - there were 30 arrests - no neighbouring force was called in for support . <p> It is understood that no activists or organiser had been fined up to ? 10,000 for breaching rules meant to prevent gatherings of 30 or more people during coronavirus restrictions . <p> Although many protesters were seen wearing masks , there appeared little attempt to avoid close contact with fellow activists and police . <p> Despite claiming to be climate change protesters , many activists said they were against the perceived political stance of some newspapers . <p> One XR statement said the action was about racism , as well " immigration policy , the rights and treatment of minority groups and dozens of other issues " . <p> The shift was seen by many as making XR a political movement , rather than a campaign group . <p> Asked where Hertfordshire Constabulary stands on the right to protests versus freedom of speech , the force issued a statement which reads : " The rights to protest are well established in this country and we remain committed to facilitating peaceful protest and ensuring compliance with the law . " <p> XR plans to target the media began in October last year , said Mr McCarthy who also writes a column for The Independent . <p> It wanted maximum disruption to papers published by Rupert Murdoch , Telegraph Media Group and Daily Mail and General Trust . <p> A document obtained by a Sunday newspaper last December showed organisers spotted how " vulnerable " Broxbourne was because it had only one exit leading to a busy road . <p> However , the demonstration planned for April this year was cancelled due to the Covid lockdown . <p> " The Great March for Truth &amp; Blockade said : " The Broxbourne plant has only one exit road leading to the A10 , which makes it very vulnerable to a mass blockade . <p> " This would block almost all national newspapers for the South of England from being delivered from this plant . <p> " The disruptive impact of the capital and entire south of the country waking up to almost empty newspaper stands would send a shock through the system , as a wake-up call to the seriousness with which the media needs to take the crises . " <p> They hoped to maintain the blockade for at worst 12 hours or run it " continuously " for three days of the weekend . <p> It concedes it would require " high-risk , arrestable , undercover operations " . <p> Perhaps ironically , it even suggests printing its own newspaper to fill empty newspaper stands . <p> Mr McCarthy , who was a co-author of that report , insisted he was not an organiser of the latest demonstration despite being at Broxbourne . <p> This weekend 's demonstration far exceeded last year 's proposed plans , suggesting lockdown had provided an opportunity for organisers to galvanise support and plan how to maximise their disruption . <p> Ian Murray , executive director of the Society of Editors , added : " Everyone has the right to peacefully protest and make their voices heard , after all that is what a free press is all about . But it is not acceptable for those who wish only their voices to be heard to attempt to silence others . " <p> Newsprinters condemned the protests as an " attack on all of the free press " which had affected workers going about their jobs , and others such as newsagents who face a " financial penalty " . <p> The Telegraph 's cartoonist Bob gives his take on the protest <p> The Federation of Independent Retailers ( NFRN ) said the protests had hit home-delivery operations , including for the " elderly and vulnerable " , with its members having to deal with " angry customers " . <p> Asked about the financial impact for newsagents , an XR spokesman said : " Sorry . " <p> Politicians from all sides condemned XR for launching what was seen as a blatant attack on freedom of speech . <p> Boris Johnson insisted a free press was " vital " to fight climate change because it encouraged debate about the issue . <p> His views were echoed by Carrie Symonds , the prime minister 's fianc ? e , who said despite being a longstanding environmental campaigner " preventing a free press to spread this message further is just wrong " . <p> Robert Jenrick , the housing secretary , said protesters were the " intolerant minority " while fellow Tory , Tobias Ellwood , said the group was alienating people from their cause .
