Daniel Kelemen is professor of political science and law at Rutgers University . <p> In her first State of the Union speech , European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen laid out ambitious plans on everything from public health and the fight against the pandemic to climate change , human rights and migration . <p> But for all its strengths , her maiden speech was exceptionally weak on one of the most pressing threats facing the EU : the rise of increasingly autocratic governments in a handful of EU member countries , most notably Hungary and Poland . <p> Although von der Leyen 's speech did include a pledge to defend the rule of law , it painted a picture that was distressingly detached from reality . <p> She suggested that threats to the rule of law inside the EU are a new challenge or one looming on the horizon .
