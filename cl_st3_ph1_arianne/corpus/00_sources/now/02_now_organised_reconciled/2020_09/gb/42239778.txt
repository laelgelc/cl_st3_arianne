<h> Climate action is Europe 's ' roadmap ' out of COVID-19 crisis , top official says <p> BRUSSELS ( Reuters ) - The European Union will redouble efforts to meet its green goals to help mitigate the economic havoc wrought by COVID-19 , its top climate official said on Tuesday , rejecting any notion that the pandemic had made ambitious climate action unaffordable . <p> FILE PHOTO : European Commissioner for European Green Deal Frans Timmermans speaks during a news conference at the European Commission headquarters in Brussels , Belgium July 8 , 2020 . Virginia Mayo/Pool via REUTERS <p> The EU is gearing up for a mammoth few months of climate policymaking . It is due to unveil a tougher 2030 emissions-cutting target , a strategy to curb methane emissions , and new plans to clean up the financial , chemicals and buildings sectors by the end of the year . <p> EU climate chief Frans Timmermans said the pandemic had not dampened these plans , despite plunging the bloc into its worst economic recession . <p> " As uncertainty grows , so does the pressure on governments @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ not just maintaining our ambitions , but doubling down on the Green Deal , " he said , referring to the bloc 's plan to reach net zero emissions by 2050 . <p> " It was our growth strategy , and now it is also our roadmap out of this crisis . " <p> The Commission will this month propose a new EU climate target for 2030 , to cut emissions by 50% or 55% against 1990 levels . The current goal is for a 40% cut . <p> Speaking at an online event hosted by the Bruegel think-tank , Timmermans said making deeper emissions cuts this decade was " doable " and made economic sense . <p> The cost of tackling climate change would be " dwarfed " by the price tag of inaction , he said , as a failure to curb emissions would see destructive climate impacts worsen , with poorer people bearing the brunt of it . <p> Last year was Europe 's hottest on record , and the continent is already facing weather phenomena exacerbated by climate change , such as drought , wildfires and heatwaves.
