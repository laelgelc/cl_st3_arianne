Whether it is a story about water quality or greenhouse gas emissions , a great deal of blame tends to get placed in their camp . <p> Instead of placing constant blame on them , a new approach is needed to work collaboratively to improve our world . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ one feels about climate change , we can all agree that seeking out the best environmental practices is a good thing . Farmers and ranchers are ultimately stewards of their land , and if you take care of your soil , your soil will take care of you . <p> We hear quite a bit of who is to blame when it comes to climate change . What we do n't hear much about are actual solutions . When people attempt to have a solutions conversation , too often the ideas fall on a top-down governmental approach . <p> What we seem to be looking for is an easy way out -- one approach that will magically fix the issue . Instead , we should be exploring how we can better incentivize different people and industries to make a change . <p> First , there are already farmers and ranchers out there doing great things . Here in the St. Cloud Times , a number of articles have been posted in the past year that focus on different farmers and their land management . <p> One term used in different @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ thing to be sustainable , but being sustainable does n't mean much unless you 've already installed a model of improvement . <p> Those practicing regenerative agriculture are really looking to improve soil health . The healthier the soil , the more carbon that can be sequestered . This can be achieved with rotational grazing of livestock . <p> Here 's how it works : A farmer will typically divide pasture land into smaller sections . Animals are allowed to graze on the grasses for a period of time before being moved into another pasture to allow the grasses to grow again . If it is in the winter when grasses are out of reach , bails are used but placed in different areas so that the animals wo n't trample the same spot . While animals are in the pasture , their droppings are worked into the ground by their hooves , acting as a natural fertilizer . <p> Expanding the practice of cover crops is another good approach . If you have ever owned a lawn , odds are good you have noticed if grass is n't planted @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Mother Nature tends to not let ground sit uncovered for very long . By planting cover crops , farmers can help preserve their much-needed top soil while helping to absorb excess moisture . <p> Here in the U.S. , cover crop usage is on the rise . According to the 2017 Census of Agriculture , there was a 50% rise in cover cropping between 2012 and 2017 . <p> Modern technology can also be of use . Drones , for example , are quickly becoming a tool for more than just taking nice pictures and video . <p> Farmers can now determine which parts of their land need fertilizer , chemical application , etc. , simply by utilizing this new technology . It wo n't be long before drones are able to have the capacity to spray fields at a lower altitude than airplanes and helicopters are currently capable of . This targeted approach will cut down on any type of drift that may occur and will allow for a more efficient use of resources . <p> Arguably the largest driver of all of this is consumer demand . As consumers become more environmentally conscious , farmers will be incentivized to adopt practices to their liking . <p> To a growing degree , this is already happening . Last year , Minnesota 's own General Mills announced it will incorporate regenerative practices on 1 million acres of land in the next 10 years . You should expect to see more companies follow suite . <p> So what can the government do to help assist these efforts ? <p> One immediate action item is to open up more land owned by the Minnesota Department of Natural Resources to grazing . The DNR oversees 5.6 million acres of state land . Although they do allow some grazing , additional availability could be mutually beneficial . <p> Agriculture and environmentally friendly practices are not mutually exclusive . As time goes on , new ways of farming and ranching will emerge to meet the needs of consumers .
