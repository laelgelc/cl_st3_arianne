<p> Te Herenga Waka -- Victoria University of Wellington and NIWA scientists are exploring the ongoing effects of climate change , including possible impacts from the Australian bushfires , on New Zealand 's glaciers . <p> Every year a group of scientists from the University and NIWA head out into the field for the End of Summer Snowline Survey . The survey has been running since the 1970s , surveying around 50 glaciers from the Nelson Lakes down to Fiordland to assess how much of the previous winter 's snow remains on each glacier to contribute towards long-term glacial ice accumulation . <p> " This year we actually excluded the glaciers furthest south , in Fiordland , and furthest north in the Nelson Lakes region -- this is because they are just snow patches now and no longer glaciers , " says Dr. Lauren Vargo from the University 's Antarctic Research Centre . <p> The survey involves taking thousands of photos which , along with 3-D models created using the photos , will be used to assess changing snow levels on the glaciers . <p> In total , scientists estimate that about 25-30 percent of the ice that was @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ last few years have seen dramatic loss of mass in the glaciers , and Dr. Vargo says these high mass loss years will likely become more common . <p> " We did see some snow on the glaciers this year , which is better compared to the last two years of the survey , " Dr. Vargo says .
