<p> Talk about a beach bummer -- severe erosion spurred by global warming could wash away half of the world 's shorelines by the end of the century . <p> That 's according to a new report published by the European Union 's Joint Research Center , which drew on satellite images to study the way beaches have changed over the past 35 years , and then simulated how climate change , rising sea levels and storm events could reshape shorelines in the future . <p> Their findings predict that even by 2050 , 14% to 15% of the planet 's coastlines could already be unrecognizable after " severe erosion , " meaning 22,430 to 25,172 miles of shoreline could disappear . And by 2100 , the Earth could see 36% to 50% of beaches covering 59,068 to 81,862 miles washed away . <p> " A substantial proportion of the world 's sandy coastline is already eroding , a situation that could be exacerbated by climate change , " researchers wrote in the study 's abstract . Some of the most densely-populated oceanfronts -- including America 's East Coast , South Asia and parts of Europe -- could see coastlines retreat by almost 330 feet inland . <p> The study 's lead author Michalis Vousdoukas told the AP that half of these beaches " will experience erosion that is more than 100 meters . It 's likely that they will be lost . " <p> And that 's worrisome for many reasons . Coastal zones are among the most developed and densely populated areas in the world , for one thing , so a disappearing shoreline has a direct impact on homes and livelihoods . Many economies in these coastal areas also rely heavily on tourism to these sandy beaches , which would take a hit if said beaches no longer exist .
