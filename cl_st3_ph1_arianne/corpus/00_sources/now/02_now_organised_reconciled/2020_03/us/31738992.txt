I am a Head and Neck Surgeon in Ottawa and I see patients at three sites : Carleton Place Hospital ; I rent a room at a hearing aid clinic ( in which I see everything from stuffy noses to ear infections to hearing loss ) ; and at a private practice . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Carleton Place Hospital , like The Ottawa Hospital , has been providing frequent and excellent updates as to the functioning of the hospital , as well as guidelines for managing the COVID-19 pandemic . The public should be assured that the hospitals are being proactive and making excellent decisions . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; What is concerning is that initially , there was limited to no direction for those of us who run and manage out-of-hospital facilities . Thus far , those recommendations we did receive are somewhat confusing and contradictory . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; On March 17 , the Ontario Medical Association offered guidance on elective surgery ( non-emergency ) and non-essential medical visits . I am a surgeon here in Ottawa , so this was of interest . The OMA advice was to postpone routine and non-urgent assessments ( e.g. annual assessment screening programs and non-urgent , elective consults , and elective surgeries ) . This prompted the cancellation of all surgery at Carleton Place Hospital . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; The &lt;a href= " https : //www.cpso.on.ca/ " &gt;College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario&lt;/a&gt; , which is responsible for licensing and monitoring physicians , indicated that during a &amp;ldquo ; Public Health Emergency&amp;rdquo ; physicians must be available to provide physician services such as providing direct medical care to people in need . When deciding what role to take during public health emergencies , physicians must do so in accordance with the values , principles and duties of medical professionalism. &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; All physicians in my situation understand that our role is to continue to care for patients in the best way we can , while at the same time not harming patients or putting them at risk . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; My medical practice at the hearing aid centre , for the most part , only assesses non-urgent patients . The problem is , I have one examination chair in which all patients will sit . Half of my patients are elderly and/or with medical co-morbidities ( transplants , asthma , heart disease , etc ) . Although many are young and healthy , I see between 30 and 45 patients a day . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Due to being a small clinic , we do not have enough Personal Protective Equipment ( PPE , gowns , gloves , masks ) to protect ourselves and our patients with a high volume in clinical outpatient care outside the hospital in this current environment . Furthermore , we do not have isolation rooms , nor are we prepared to isolate a patient . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; We have received some advice from the Canadian Medical Protective Agency ( CMPA ) with respect to some of our responsibilities when we do not have the ability to protect ourselves and patients with PPE . The CMPA indicated that if we do not practise due to not having personal protective equipment , we put ourselves at risk in terms of medical liability and investigations by the CPSO . I certainly hope in these circumstances , the CPSO and patients understand the difficulty in situations like ours . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; My private clinic is a bit simpler as it is non-essential services . However , there are significant economic impacts for all private medical clinics . Like most out-of-hospital physician offices , we have staff who work in our clinic . This includes nurses , administrators and other health care providers . If the doctors don&amp;rsquo ; t work , the clinic can not create income for the clinic and this puts jobs at risk . This is the case in all industries right now ; medical clinics are no different . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; However , right now we need to think bigger . This is a public health issue . We have been diligent with respect to screening , cleaning and ensuring patients are safe . It is a low risk environment at most times . However , recent studies on COVID-19 indicate that a large proportion of COVID-positive patients may be asymptomatic carriers who can spread the disease . Nevertheless , some important decisions need to be made in the short term to ensure we do our part to protect everyone . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Personally , I am taking proactive steps and I hope my decisions are the right ones . My biggest worry is my elderly patients and those with comorbidities . Do I cancel all patients and thus , new patients who have been waiting six to nine months wait longer ? Do I only cancel older patients or those with a co-morbidity ? Do I screen patients and hope that no asymptomatic carriers enter the clinic ? Am I helping or hurting with either decision ? Do I see fewer patients ? Do I cancel all my patients or do I work as scheduled ? Right now , I am using my own personal judgment and knowledge of population health to guide me. &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Initially , we planned on seeing patients in our clinic . However new evidence of asymptomatic COVID-19 spreading , referred to as &amp;ldquo ; stealth spreading , &amp;rdquo ; as well as recommendations from the government of Canada altered our view . Maintaining social distancing prevents spread but in a medical practice , there is only so much we can do to maintain distance . We can limit the number of patients in the waiting room , see fewer patients and screen diligently . Even with this , risks remain and I question if in-person clinic visits are safe . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; In the end , I decided to adopt a tele-health model by calling new patients and conducting the medical assessment over the phone . The OMA and the Ontario Ministry of Health have been exceptionally helpful in arranging this for all physicians . This is my first time using this model of health care and I still have concerns , but it is the best option for me right now . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; The biggest problem is patient safety without a comprehensive physician examination . How can I look in someone&amp;rsquo ; s mouth/ears or nose from a phone ? I can&amp;rsquo ; t , so I may miss something . It is stressful as a physician trying to decide what is the best method to ensure this doesn&amp;rsquo ; t happen . We do have the option of e-mail and Skype consults , but with that come more concerns , primarily confidentiality . There is always a risk of hacking , email errors and putting patients&amp;rsquo ; confidentiality at risk . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; In the end , I am going to do what I can . I just finished my first morning of tele-health-based ENT &amp;ndash ; Head and Neck Surgery clinic . I will continue to help as many as I can and prevent putting people at risk . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; However , medical societies , Health Canada and public health should provide specific guidelines and advice for my clinic and similar medical clinics ( such as walk-in clinics ) . Or is this finally the right time to recognize that we can&amp;rsquo ; t keep counting on the Bank of Canada to do all of the economic heavy lifting in a crisis ? &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; This rate cut signals the central bank&amp;rsquo ; s willingness to do what it can to counteract the economic consequences of the virus&amp;rsquo ; s spread . Yet there is only so much that lower rates can do . They can make it easier for people and businesses to borrow and they will reduce payments on a flexible rate mortgage . But low rates won&amp;rsquo ; t help companies continue to make the products that rely on parts made in hard-hit countries such as China and South Korea , and they won&amp;rsquo ; t help people afford to take time off of work if they get sick . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; While it isn&amp;rsquo ; t yet clear yet how serious the economic effects of the virus will be , this is a good time to take stock of what tools we have to respond to the next economic crisis . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Since the 2008 financial crisis , the Canadian government , like governments around the world , has relied an awful lot on the super powers of the central bank . G7 politicians decided that they didn&amp;rsquo ; t want to have to keep using fiscal policy to stimulate the economy and started treating central banks like &amp;ldquo ; &lt;a href= " http : **34;24693;TOOLONG ... Only Game in Town&lt;/a&gt; , &amp;rdquo ; as former Bank of England deputy governor Paul Tucker put it . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Yet the last decade has made it clear that there are very real drawbacks to assuming that central banks can always save the day . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; The Bank of Canada has had to keep interest rates very low for a very long time to keep the economy going . While this has worked , to a point , it has had perverse consequences . Canadians took advantage of the low interest rates to go on a spending spree &amp;mdash ; building up debts worth as much as 177 per cent of their annual income . This borrowing binge and the housing bubble that has gone with it have limited the Bank of Canada&amp;rsquo ; s options moving forwards : by cutting rates , they run the risk of pushing debt even higher , but by increasing rates they could precipitate a crisis for the many families who can barely make their interest payments . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Given these limits , one option for the Bank of Canada would be to pursue unconventional monetary policy . In doing so , it would be following the lead of the U.S. , Japanese , British and European central banks that have dabbled in more esoteric monetary policies after the 2008 crisis , including &amp;ldquo ; quantitative easing , &amp;rdquo ; which has central banks creating new money to buy up government and private sector bonds and securities . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; While these unconventional tools may be useful and even necessary , they do produce winners and losers . Top-down strategies such as quantitative easing have actively contributed to growing inequality ( by increasing the value assets that are mostly held by the wealthy ) , and have accelerated the climate crisis by disproportionately investing in carbon-intensive firms . On the other hand , bottom-up strategies , such as &amp;ldquo ; helicopter money , &amp;rdquo ; where the central bank distributes new money to individuals , have been overlooked to date . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Politicians had hoped in the last crisis that they could avoid making difficult political decisions by passing the buck to central bankers who are insulated from the democratic process . Unfortunately , this past decade has taught us that there is no such thing as an apolitical solution to an economic crisis . Whatever role the Bank of Canada plays , it needs to be guided by democratic &amp;mdash ; and not just technocratic &amp;mdash ; priorities . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; We need central banks to play their part now &amp;mdash ; and we will need them again in the future .
