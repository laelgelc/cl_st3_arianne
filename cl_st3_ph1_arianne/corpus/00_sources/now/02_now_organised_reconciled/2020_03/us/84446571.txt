<p> The company behind the proposed Keystone XL pipeline said Tuesday it 's going ahead with the project , while a leading opponent in Nebraska vowed it will never be built . <p> The proposed Keystone XL pipeline would carry 830,000 barrels per day from the oil sands of Alberta , Canada , through Nebraska to refineries on the Gulf Coast . It 's been a political football for more than a decade , with supporters saying it would promote economic development and energy security , and critics predicting it would pollute the land and water , and worsen climate change . <p> Until now TC Energy , formerly TransCanada , the company behind the pipeline , had not made a final decision to invest in the project . Tuesday , the company announced it will proceed . In a statement , company President and CEO Russ Girling said " this important energy and infrastructure project is poised to put thousands of people to work , generate substantial economic benefits and strengthen the continent 's energy security . " <p> The announcement comes as the provincial government in Alberta announced financial support for the project .
