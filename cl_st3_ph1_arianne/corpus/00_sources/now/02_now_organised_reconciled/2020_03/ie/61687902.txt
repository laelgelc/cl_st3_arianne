<p> Northern Ireland 's Agriculture Minister has announced that Tranches 3 and 4 of the delayed Environmental Farming Scheme ( EFS ) will now proceed -- albeit with amendments . <p> EFS supports farmers to implement **26;279;TOOLONG actions on their land to protect and enhance biodiversity and water quality , and mitigate against climate change by sequestering carbon . <p> A total of 3,950 agreements are in place after two tranches . However , the third tranche was put under review after options such as fencing were heavily oversubscribed . <p> Making the announcement , Minister Edwin Poots said successful applicants will now be issued with their agreements for Tranche 3 and applications for Tranche 4 will be invited later in the year . <p> The move comes after EFS was delayed and reviewed to ensure value for money and that it delivers on its core environmental goals . <p> However , the tranches contain several amendments in order to assure the department that the scheme continues to meet the objectives set out in the original business case . <p> Minister Poots said : " I am very pleased to provide clarity to almost 3,000 farmers who applied for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to proceed with Tranche 3 and given the green light to open Tranche 4 for applications later in the year . <p> " Several amendments to both Tranche 3 and 4 have been made to ensure that the scheme continues to provide value for money . <p> " While I appreciate that this may be disappointing for some , the approval of the revised scheme is welcome news and enables EFS to provide continued support for the farming sector to play its part in protecting our environment .
