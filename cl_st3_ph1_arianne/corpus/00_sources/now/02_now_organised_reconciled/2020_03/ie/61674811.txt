<h> Opinion : ' Climate change and the GAA ' - Martin Kiely <p> The events of recent weeks and indeed recent years have confirmed that climate change is a major challenge that the World is coping to catch up with . <p> We do n't have to look too far to see its impact and how people will have to change the habits of generations to assist in halting it . <p> It will have a major impact too in sport and the GAA calendar now sees more games played in winter than it does in summer particularly at club level . <p> County grounds are always under pressure in winter and we saw good examples of that a couple of weeks ago when games had to be called off due to very heavy rain over a sustained period . Only a hand full of counties have quality facilities with lights which would be capable of holding fixtures . <p> The time has come in Munster for the Munster Council to purchase and develop facilities . They have the funds to get this project off the ground and they also have a product that continues to produce good attendances at games . Sitting back is not an option . <p> We heard recently that Croke Park have purchased Clonliffe College in Dublin . Just down the road from Croke Park they intend to put in pitches , hotels and other commercial facilities . The benefit of building new all weather pitches would far exceed the value of plush offices . <p> Man of vision <p> It might take a bit of leadership from someone to suggest the building of new pitches , dressing rooms and indeed they could also build new offices . <p> There is no doubt that the capacity to do such a project exists in the Munster Council . They have a lot of experience to call upon and they would also be able to call on the experience of Croke Park . <p> Building such a project could also provide long term revenue as clubs , colleges and schools would be happy to rent during winter . <p> Peter Quinn , the former President of the GAA was @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ vision at Munster Council level to get such a project off the ground . <p> Like always I am sure there will be plenty that will say this is not needed but that would be wrong and a negative view of where climate change is at now and more importantly where it will be at in the future . <p> The vast majority of money goes each year to Dublin while the rest of the counties sit back and allow it happen . The GAA is bigger than Dublin and while new pitches with hotels attached rented on long leases will look great it will do very little for the great good of GAA in the rest of Ireland . <p> These projects might build a lads CV but will do nothing to help the roots of an association struggling to survive at parish level . <p> Fantastic record <p> Rain fall in the West of Ireland has grown steadily over the past few years and all the signs are that it will continue in the years ahead . <p> Games will need to be played and unless leadership is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a real challenge to play those games . It would take a few million to build such facilities but it would be well spent . <p> When the GAA decides to build something they have a fantastic record of achievement . They know how to get things done and more than any other organisation in the country they have played a significant part in providing facilities in every part of Ireland . <p> Sea levels are rising at an alarming rate and the challenge of playing games at club and county will be all the more difficult in the years ahead . <p> Now , the GAA can sit back and wait but I really do n't think they have the luxury of doing nothing .
