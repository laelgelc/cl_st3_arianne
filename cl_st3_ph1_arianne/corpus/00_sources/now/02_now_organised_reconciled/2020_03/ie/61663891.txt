<p> As this column was being written , a summit of EU leaders had ended in deadlock after negotiations over the future EU long-term budget ( called the Multi-annual Financial Framework or MFF in Eurospeak ) at an extraordinary meeting of the European Council in Brussels . <p> However , given the dependence of Irish farm incomes on CAP payments , there is inevitably concern over what future negotiations might mean for future CAP payments for Irish farmers . <p> Even with uncertainty around the final outcome , a reduction in CAP payments has been on the cards since the Commission put forward its original budget proposal in May 2018 . <p> Negotiations over the EU 's long-term budget are always difficult . While the budget supports projects and initiatives of common interest to all member states , the largest expenditure items , namely cohesion and agriculture funds , have a strong redistributive character . <p> Thus EU budget negotiations often come down to a battle between the net payer countries ( represented this time by the ' frugal five ' of Austria , Denmark , Germany , Netherlands and Sweden ) and the net recipient countries ( represented @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the ' friends of CAP ' ) . <p> The battle on this occasion is complicated by two elements .
