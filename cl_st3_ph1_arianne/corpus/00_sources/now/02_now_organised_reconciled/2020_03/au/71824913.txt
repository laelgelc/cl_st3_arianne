<h> Suddenly we 're all collectivists , including the PM , and we only pass this test if we act together <p> How quickly we all became socialists . Believe it or not , the National Rugby League was first to be re-educated . Australia was still blinking as a match was lit to our way of life , our economy , to the very structure of our families , when NRL chief Peter V'landys redefined audacity by asking for government assistance . <p> The money or the scrum ... Australian Rugby League Commission chairman Peter V'landys . <p> The NRL is a big employer and is important to public morale , at least in NSW . It also reported a $30 million surplus in 2018/19 . But V'landys was not the only virus-era red crawling out from under the bed . <p> The Morrison government quickly prepared its second round of stimulus , and it seemed cute that a few weeks ago a surplus was considered important . The government indicated it would probably announce a ( temporary ) raise in the dole -- something business and social welfare groups have been clamouring for for years . ( Whisper it : if everyone is buying one , maybe you should too ? ) They say to never waste a good crisis , and the bigger the crisis , the bigger the potential waste . <p> The pushing of personal barrows was worst on social media , which has been a hive of panic . To scroll through a Twitter feed right now is to half-read countless snatches of information -- bits of reports , alarming spiky graphs , photos of exhausted medicos -- tweeted from all around the world . These snippets are not processed in the conscious mind , which is equipped to sort and weigh them according to their relevance and veracity . Instead they form an anxious fog which hangs in our subconscious . <p> Overnight , we all have pandemic brain . Perhaps this explains why many people who would ordinarily advocate collective action on , say , climate change have become individualists who pull their children from schools , against the advice of health authorities . That can work to muddy a central , unified message . <p> Not to let the government off the hook . Morrison has at times appeared slow and complacent . The government is clearly feeling its way ( who is n't ? ) and messages on basic measures such as hand-shaking and whether or not it 's safe to go to the football have been confusing . It seems they were too late to close the borders entirely . <p> But this week Morrison seemed to find his @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ where he projected calm and addressed head-on concerns over schools staying open , for example . <p> There is a considerable segment of the public who dislike or distrust Morrison , and were never going to be satisfied with his response to the crisis . The trouble for those people , and for all of us , will be knowing when criticism of the government is valid and when it is unnecessary and unhelpful . <p> It seems obvious , for example , that someone , somewhere at the top , failed to order enough COVID-19 testing kits , back when we knew the virus was spreading but there was not yet a global run on stock . The government seemed to obfuscate on this early in the week before announcing it had procured an extra 100,000 testing kits . <p> Conservative governments are not used to asking the public to engage in collectivism or solidarity , and we are not used to hearing such requests from them . Perhaps that 's why some of us seem reluctant to comply . <p> The call to keep schools open , for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the whole . " <p> The Morrison government , like so many around the world , is living the reality of what happens when low public trust in politicians combines with the cacophony of social media , where we can watch thousands of people emotionally react to incremental news breaks . <p> When this is over , hopefully our politicians will absorb some long-term lessons about how to communicate with people as though you actually want them to understand you , rather than deliberately hiding your meaning through obfuscation .
