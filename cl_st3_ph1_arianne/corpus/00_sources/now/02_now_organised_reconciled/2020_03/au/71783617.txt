<h> Indian Ocean reveals future climate risks <p> A study published in Nature on Tuesday has found a particular kind of Indian Ocean weather event - which leads to less rainfall in Australia - has become more frequent and intense . <p> A positive Indian Ocean Dipole ( IOD ) , which is similar to El Nino events in the Pacific Ocean , occurs when oceans to Australia 's northwest cool more than usual , leading to less cloud and rain across the nation 's centre and southeast . <p> Ten such events have occurred since 1240 but four of them within the past 60 years . <p> Lead researcher Nerilie Abram from the Australian National University says a positive IDO last year led to the hot , dry summer that saw the nation swelter and burn . <p> " These events are becoming more common so we do expect them to play an increasing role in Australia 's climate variability in the future , " Professor Abram told AAP . <p> " All of the evidence points to a role of human-caused climate change in making these events more frequent and making them stronger . <p> " The @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ get climate warming under control and stabilise the climate , the better off we 'll be in terms of also stabilising just how extreme these events get in the future .
