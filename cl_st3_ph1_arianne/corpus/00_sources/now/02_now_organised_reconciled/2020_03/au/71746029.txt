<h> NASA satellite captures Antarctica melting during heat wave <p> This satellite image from Feb. 13 shows extensive melting of the the ice cap on Eagle Island in Antarctica . <p> Joshua Stevens/Landsat data from the USGS/GEOS-5 data/NASA GSFC <p> Antarctica , home to the coldest place on Earth , experienced a worrying heat wave earlier in February , possibly even setting a record high temperature for the normally frigid continent . A NASA satellite has taken stock of what that heat meant to the local ice cap and glaciers . <p> NASA 's Earth Observatory released two images from the agency 's Landsat 8 satellite . They highlight dramatic changes in the ice and snow along the northern tip of the Antarctic peninsula . <h> Climate change is real <p> A World Meteorological Organization committee is working to verify the record temperature reading , but the results of the warm spell are easy to spot in the satellite views . The heat wave liquefied around 20% of Eagle Island 's seasonal snow accumulation in a matter of days . <p> The heat and related meltwater formation is part of a pattern .
