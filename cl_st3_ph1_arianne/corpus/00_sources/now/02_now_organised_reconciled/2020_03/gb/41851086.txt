Winter is the traditional time of death , the time when the vulnerable succumb . And not as bad as , say , the Black Death , the plague in the 14th century that killed -- estimates vary wildly -- perhaps 200 million people . <p> The seriousness of the current situation was made clear when they cancelled sport . For those of us with a taste for the stuff , sport is a routine treat that lets us off the normal cares of life for a while . The last thing nature is doing is keeping calm , for this is the maddest time of the year . Human lives may be circumscribed right now , but out in the wild places everything is happening without constraint . The race to become an ancestor is currently revving up to its peak . <p> We are losing nature , and we are doing so in two ways . First , we are destroying it . It 's there waiting : and the action is hotting up . <p> So here 's a suggestion : try something that in normal times you would never get round to . It 's something that I have called the Bottomless Sit . Useful tip : always carry a supermarket plastic bag in a pocket or bag : now you can sit dry-bummed anywhere you choose . <p> So sit . Sit for just a few minutes in a nice place and you will have your reward . No , do n't check your phone . Look at the new leaves . Feel the breeze . Listen . You might hear a bird that sings a phrase , repeats it two or three times , and then turns to another phrase : that 's a song thrush . It 's not about philosophy : it 's about the mysterious sense of good cheer that comes from association with nature . <p> Last weekend I was speaking at a meeting ; the good people there were objecting to a deranged project to build an enormous nuclear power station right next-door to the most important nature reserve in Britain .
