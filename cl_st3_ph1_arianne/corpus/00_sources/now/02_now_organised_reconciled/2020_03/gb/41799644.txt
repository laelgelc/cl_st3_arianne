<h> Climate focus in Bishop 's sermon <h> Climate focus in Bishop 's sermon <p> THE Bishop of Salisbury addressed the issue of climate change in his Ash Wednesday sermon at Salisbury Cathedral . <p> At the service on Wednesday , February 26 , Bishop Nicholas Holtam said that Lent was an opportunity to " renew our relationships with God and one another and the planet . " <p> He said : " There is a climate emergency . It is a crisis for God 's creation , and a fundamental injustice . <p> " It has raised big questions from young people about whether we really care and about intergenerational fairness . <p> " It raises big questions about climate justice because the poorest of the earth , who have had least benefit from the industrial revolution , are the most vulnerable to the effects of climate change . <p> " Because we human beings have organised life around our own needs , the destruction of species is also increasing . <p> " What we know is that we need a diverse and healthy ecology for our own health and wellbeing . <p> " In other words , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ industrial revolution and by the creation of people-centred world is now threatening our very existence . <p> " There are scientific , economic and political dimensions to the problems of how we care for the earth and some Christians will be involved in working in those areas and seeking solutions . " <p> The Bishop also quoted well-known scientist James Gustave Speth in his speech . He quoted : " I used to think the top environmental problems were biodiversity loss , eco system collapse and climate change . <p> " I thought that with 30 years of good science we could address those problems . <p> " But I was wrong . The top environmental problems are selfishness , greed and apathy -- and to deal with those we need a spiritual and cultural transformation -- and we scientists do n't know how to do that . " <p> The Bishop concluded his sermon by saying : " I hope we will have a holy and joyful Lent , Renewing Hope as we Pray , Serve , Grow .
