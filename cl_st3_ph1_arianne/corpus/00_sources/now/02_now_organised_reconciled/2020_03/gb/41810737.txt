<p> A GENEROUS donation by Rotarians in Weymouth will help hundreds of people in remote Bangladesh see the world clearly again . <p> The Rotary Club of Melcombe Regis has sponsored the purchase of a new ' lensometer ' for The Friendship Hospital of Shyamnagar , in the district of Satkhira - one of the most remote and climate-sensitive regions of Southern Bangladesh . <p> Underprivileged river island communities in the area have very limited access to proper eyecare -- many people suffer from blindness that could be cured with proper treatment . <p> But now there is hope -- thanks to the newly donated lensometer , the hospital 's Eye Outpatient Department will be able to treat a minimum of 1,000 patients per month . <p> Melcombe Regis Rotary Club joined forces with Rotary International to make the donation , and are " delighted " to be able to contribute to saving the sight of so many people , in such a remote area of the world . <p> Jim Wilkinson , chairman of the Rotary Foundation and International Committee , said : " Avoidable blindness is one of the major public health problems in Bangladesh , one @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ most hit by the impacts of climate change . About five million people -- including children -- suffer from varying degrees of eye ailments . According to the latest national survey on blindness and low vision , the number of blind people in the country will double by the end of this year if immediate action is not taken . " <p> He added : " 80 per cent of the country 's diagnosed blind suffer from cataracts , which are completely curable through the right medical treatment . " <p> The hospital at Shyamnagar has the capacity to treat more than 120,000 people per year , with an extensive focus on eye-care . <p> The Rotary Club identified that the donation of a lensometer would significantly contribute to better quality treatment for the Friendship Hospital 's eye patients .
