<p> BRISTOL CATHEDRAL provided a base for the Swedish climate activist Greta Thunberg , before her speech on Friday at a rally in the city calling for greater action to tackle the climate crisis . <p> Despite heavy rain , the 17-year-old , who began the school-strike movement by sitting outside the Swedish Parliament every Friday , was met by a crowd of 25,000 people , including numerous schoolchildren . Many schools had closed , allowing their pupils to attend the rally , while people left their offices to come and hear her speak . <p> Dressed in a yellow mac and woolly hat , she praised the recent decision on climate grounds by North Somerset Council to reject plans to expand Bristol Airport . <p> She said : " The other week , the plans to expand Bristol Airport were cancelled -- a lot thanks to climate activists . And , of course , this is far from enough ; but it shows that it does actually make a difference . Activism works . So I 'm telling you to act . <p> " If you look throughout history , all the great changes have come from the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ power , and they are failing us , but we will not back down . If you feel threatened by that , then I have some very bad news for you : we will not be silenced , because we are the change , and change is coming , whether you like it or not . " <p> Before she addressed the crowds , and joined them on the subsequent march through the city , she spent time preparing in Bristol Cathedral . <p> The Bishop of Bristol , the Rt Revd Vivienne Faull , met Miss Thunberg in the Chapter House .
