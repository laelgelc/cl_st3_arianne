<p> Professor Freeman Dyson receives the Templeton Prize from the Duke of Edinburgh at Buckingham Palace , in 2000 <p> Professor Freeman Dyson receives the Templeton Prize from the Duke of Edinburgh at Buckingham Palace , in 2000 <p> PHYSICS and theology are not always natural bedfellows . But a physicist who encouraged me to think that the two disciplines could have fruitful conversations was Freeman Dyson , the British-born physicist who died at the end of last month , aged 96 . His interests spanned quantum theory , nuclear reactors , climate change , and space travel . <p> His religious views went back to his immersion in what he described as " watered-down Church of England Christianity " . In later life , he described himself as a non-denominational Christian . He was vehemently criticised by Richard Dawkins in 2000 for accepting the Templeton Prize . <p> Dyson was unusual in his determination to hold together an understanding both of the cosmos as a whole and the phenomenon of intelligence . For him , it was inescapable that mind was part of the fabric of the universe . He also relished the diversity of nature , believing @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the development of novelty and variety . <p> Though he was quite a shy person , he was regarded by scientific colleagues as somewhat subversive . He was a naturally divergent thinker : when he spotted consensus , he looked out for a contrary point of view . <p> This , in part , explains his controversial views on climate change . While convinced that it was happening , he rejected disaster scenarios and believed that green adaptation was possible and would prove beneficial . He never won a Nobel Prize : having such a wide range of interests excluded him from supreme achievement in one field . <p> His openness to religion came from his recognition that matter was , as he put it , " weird stuff " . The more science investigates matter , the more it encounters a strangeness that suggests a transcendent reality at work in its shaping . The emergence of intelligence is not to be explained away as a chance occurrence .
