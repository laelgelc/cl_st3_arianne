<p> The Great Labyrinth Project , which aims to create Africa 's largest 13-circuit structure of only spekboom , is nearly ready to receive visitors . More than 6 000 trees have been planted since November 2019 , and there are still 84 000 that are yet to be planted by volunteers . <p> According to Peter Shrimpton , creator of the Project , the labyrinth will be open to the public to volunteer on Saturday , March 6 . Western Cape Premier Alan Winde will be there as well , and will help volunteers plant the remaining trees this weekend . <p> Shrimpton collaborated with a non-profit organisation called the Entrepreneurs Organisation , which believes that everyone should help to reduce carbon emissions where they can . <p> Speaking to IOL , President of the Organisation , Waheed Adams said that the campaign has been spearheaded to inspire local entrepreneurs , as well as raise climate change awareness . <p> " The spekboom tree has an amazing ability to turn the harmful emissions to oxygen needed to sustain our world as we know it , so we would hope that this project would bring to life many more @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " It 's not just about the Great Labyrinth , it 's about inspiring people to plant spekboom and reduce their carbon emissions , " Shrimpton added . <p> The public is encouraged to participate in the Great Labyrinth Project at the East Hill Farm in Stellenbosch , which will take place between 9am and 12.30pm on Saturday , March 6.
