<h> Safe water and COVID-19 -- where the twain meet <h> Last Updated : Friday 27 March 2020 <p> We are in the middle of a pandemic . The novel coronavirus disease ( COVID-19 ) has made its presence felt in every continent except Antarctica , and all we can do is maintain social distancing and wash hands frequently . <p> Yet , as we flood social media with messages urging each other to wash hands , let us pause and reflect for a moment . <p> Keeping hands clean is one of the most important things we can do to prevent COVID-19 spread . The catch , however , is clean running water -- which is in short or no supply in several parts of the world . <p> There is a behavorial problem to the scarcity -- wastage of water . Ideally , the privileged few who do have access to safe running water should rinse hands , turn off the tap and apply soap . <p> Turning off the faucet after rinsing hands saves water , and there is little data available to prove whether germs get transferred from hands to the faucet . <p> Determining @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Nonetheless , evidence suggests that washing hands for about 15-30 seconds removes more germs . <p> Do we realise how much water we use and waste ? <p> Supply-led water deprivation was found in major cities in India in 2007 , according to a study titled Water Consumption Patterns in Domestic Households in Major Cities . <p> The per capita water availability in these cities was nowhere near the standards laid down by the World Health Organisation ( WHO ) or the Bureau of Indian Standards ( 1993 ) , and was also far lower than that in other large cities in the world . <p> Moreover , the issue was of disparity and inequity . Every municipal corporation has defined the requirement of water per capita per day in its own way . <p> This wide variation in recommendations for domestic use of water is , therefore , hard to understand . <p> About 100 litres per capita per day ( LPCD ) is required to maintain a minimum standard of health and hygiene . According to the study above , consumption of water per capita was much lower in all cities . In many parts of the world , especially where surface water supplies are not available , domestic , agricultural , and industrial water needs can only be met by using groundwater . <p> The United States Geological Survey compared groundwater levels to money kept in a bank account . If the money was withdrawn faster than it was deposited , there was eventually an account-supply problem . <p> Pumping water at a faster rate over a long time leads to similar problems . <p> Some of the negative effects of groundwater depletion are : <p> Lowering of the water table due to excessive pumping <p> Increased cost of water <p> Reduced surface water supplies .
