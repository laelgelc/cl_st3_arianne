<h> The Ramayana and climate change <p> Getting Greta to meet Gandhi opens up new political affiliations across time and space . It also releases new moral energies for an ongoing movement <p> Two short plays written and directed by Prasanna , Greta and Moola Ramayana , premiered at Ranga Shankara on Sunday . The occasion was the International Women 's Day . <p> Greta is set in Sweden but Greta Thunberg ( Lahari Barighat ) is a pigtailed girl in the school uniform that high school girls often wear in India . The other character in this play is the Joker ( Durga Devi ) , who transforms successively into Greta 's mother , Mahatma Gandhi and , in the end , a dog . <p> The set is minimalist . There is nothing besides a closed school door on which hangs a board , " Vathavaranada Rakshanegagi Shalaa Mushkara " ( " Boycott School for Climate Protection " ) . The dramatic action unfolds more in the lines uttered by the characters than on the space of the stage . <p> The setting is contemporary and global . The Joker -- and the audience -- comes to get acquainted with the essentials of Greta 's climate change activist concerns soon enough . The activist lessons arrive in simple , straightforward terms : Air travel injures the environment ; the air conditioners heat up the environment and drive the demand for more air conditioners and bring in a deadly spiral . <p> Getting Greta to meet Gandhi opens up new political affiliations across time and space . It also releases new moral energies for an ongoing movement . Further , the Joker 's final avatar as a dog discloses the animal world , preventing the drama from staying an all-human affair . <p> The second play , Moola Ramayana , builds on the first chapter of Prasanna 's recent book , Moola Ramayana - Part 1 ( Ontidani Prakashana , 2019 ) , which retells select parts of Bala Kanda and Aranya Kanda of The Ramayana . Opening with the episode of Valmiki witnessing a hunter kill the male krounca bird at the time of mating and then cursing him for his act , the play ruminates on the violent separation of the krounca birds as an episode of the separation of Purusha ( human ) and Prakriti ( nature ) . <p> The play alternates between a seated narrator ( Prasanna ) who narrates the incidents in short instalments , each of which is picked up for a gamaka rendition by a singer ( MD Pallavi ) sitting beside him . Alternating between narration and song , the form of the play powerfully brought alive the founding moments of The Ramayana which result in Valmiki taking up the task of composing the epic . <p> Viewing Rama and Sita as metaphors for Purusha and Prakriti , Moola Ramayana suggests that their separation has demonic consequences . The point however needed more stage time for adequate addressal . <p> The juxtaposition of the two short plays is clearly unusual : one is set in the contemporary world , the other in mythic times . " The world has kept the two worlds apart , " Prasanna notes . " They are stylistically different . The sensibilities are different , but the women are the same . Let the questions emerge in the audience .
