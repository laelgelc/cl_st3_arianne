It is continental in the extent of its geography and in the size of its population . It is now the world 's fifth-largest economy and even with slower growth , its profile in the global economy is destined to grow . This is the asymmetry that lies at the heart of its complex engagement with the world . <p> On the one hand , India is conscious of its weight on the global stage , demanding that it sit among the rule-makers shaping a new world order . At the same time , it is a demander , seeking access to global public goods and differential benefits as a still developing country . <p> India 's decision to stay out of the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership ( RCEP ) speaks to the latter compulsion . Its demand to seek permanent membership of the UN Security Council and be accepted into the Nuclear Suppliers Group reflects the ambition of an " emerged power " . <p> India wishes to be benchmarked with China politically , but it must seek to be differentiated from the latter when it comes to economic and trade issues . Where must the balance lie ?
