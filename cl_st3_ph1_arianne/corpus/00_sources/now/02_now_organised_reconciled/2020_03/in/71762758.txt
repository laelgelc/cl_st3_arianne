In a 2011 paper in the journal Global Health Action , scientists Boris A. Revich and Marina A. Podolnaya pointed out that a permafrost thaw can disturb cattle burial grounds in East Siberia . " As a consequence of permafrost melting , the vectors of deadly infections of the 18th and 19th centuries may come back , " they wrote . <p> What about older viruses ? In 2005 , a team of scientists from US space agency Nasa managed to revive microbes called Carnobacterium pleistocenium that had been frozen in an Alaskan pond for over 30,000 years . In 2007 , a bacterium eight million years old , lying dormant under a glacier in Antarctica , was revived . In a world of rapid global heating , nothing is impossible . And given our interconnected world , we can see how fast a new disease like the coronavirus can travel and infect people .
