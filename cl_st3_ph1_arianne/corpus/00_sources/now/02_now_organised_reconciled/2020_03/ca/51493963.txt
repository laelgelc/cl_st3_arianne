( Scott Dunn/The Sun Times/Postmedia Network ) <p> Intense snow squalls off Lake Huron continued to blow across Southern Ontario Friday , prompting more blizzard warnings , including in southern Grey and Bruce counties , bringing with it warnings of 50 cm of total accumulation . <p> A weather station in Chatsworth recorded that 54 centimetres of snow fell between Thursday morning and Friday morning . <p> That 's a lot of snow . Not a record for an area like Grey-Bruce , but unusual enough even for here , said Peter Kimbell , a warning preparedness meteorologist with Environment and Climate Change Canada . <p> Blame a particularly intense , narrow and far-reaching snow band that was heading toward the Greater Toronto Area , Kimbell said in an interview just before noon Friday . <p> Snow squalls were expected to taper off into @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ winds Saturday , when combined with forecast flurries , there 's still the potential for squall-like conditions , he said . Snow squalls are loosely defined as those creating at least 15 cm of snowfall over a 12-hour period . <p> Kimbell said it 's unusual to see these intense snow squalls this late into winter here . They 're more typical of December and January because by now , Lake Huron and Georgian Bay would have frozen over . That did n't happen this year . <p> So the cold northwest winds pick up moisture over the relatively warmer water and produce the kind of storm that has socked Grey-Bruce over the past few of days . <p> Average monthly snowfall in Wiarton in December is 109 cm , in January it 's 112 cm , and with lakes freezing over in February , drops to an average of 78 cm of snow . <p> The Chatsworth measurement is by a weather-watching volunteer for Environment Canada for which Kimbell had no historical data .
