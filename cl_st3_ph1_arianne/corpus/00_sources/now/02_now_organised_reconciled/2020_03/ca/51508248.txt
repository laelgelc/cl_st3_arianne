<p> AYACUCHO , Peru -- ( Thomson Reuters Foundation ) -- About 40 years ago , the snow that once covered the Andes mountains near the Peruvian city of Ayacucho started to disappear . <p> Water became scarce for more than 200,000 people in the south-central region , most of them from the Quechua indigenous community . <p> " We had to ration water . Some years , we had water for only two hours a day , " said Dersi Zevallos , a coordinator with Peru 's water and sanitation regulator , SUNASS . <p> Then , Quechua sisters Magdalena and Marcela Machaca -- both agricultural engineers -- found a solution by looking to the past . They built reservoirs high in the mountains to harvest and " cultivate " rainwater , the same way their ancestors did . <p> Climate change has led to increasingly dry conditions for communities in the Peruvian Andes , data shows . <p> In 1984 , around 130 cm ( 50 inches ) of rainwater fell in Ayacucho , according to SUNASS .
