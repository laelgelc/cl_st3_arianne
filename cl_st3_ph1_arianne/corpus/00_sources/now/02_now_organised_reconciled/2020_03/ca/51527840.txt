<p> As OPP officers broke up a rail blockade in Tyendinaga township Monday morning , a chorus of commentators denounced the police operation as yet another in a long tradition of offences by a colonial federal government against Canada 's Indigenous Peoples . <p> Neither the federal nor provincial governments , they said , had learned a thing -- not from Oka , not from Ipperwash , not from Caledonia . Canadian attitudes toward Indigenous Peoples , they asserted , had changed not a bit in a half-century , despite the lip service paid by Ottawa to healing and reconciliation . <p> At best , that 's a bald exaggeration . At worst , it 's a mischievous deceit . <p> At Oka during the late summer of 1990 , both the Quebec and federal governments learned the limits of intimidation , court orders and brute force in confronting activists over their Kanesatake land claim . It could easily be seen in the interactions between police and protesters at Tyendinaga over the past weeks . <p> The Caledonia land dispute , which has roots in the late 1700s but flared in 2006 and onward , taught the provincial and federal governments about the high cost in dollars , goodwill and political capital of inattention to historical land claims .
