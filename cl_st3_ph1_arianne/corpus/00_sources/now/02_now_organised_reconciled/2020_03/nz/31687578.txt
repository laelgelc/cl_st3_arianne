<p> Avian experts have repeatedly demonstrated the remarkable brainpower of birds . Parrots , in particular , have established a reputation as skillful imitators -- a talent that requires a complex network of neural connections . <p> Now , researchers Alex Taylor and Amalia Bastos from the University of Auckland have once again observed parrots beating the odds when it comes to intelligence . <p> Working with kea ( Nestor notabilis ) at Christchurch 's Willowbank Wildlife Reserve , their research has revealed this species ' ability to understand probability . Apart from humans and great apes , kea are the only animals to demonstrate this . <p> The study , published today in Nature Communications , details how these fascinating feathered creatures calculate the chance of something happening . <h> Clever , calculating kea <p> The study involved presenting a few different kea with two jars , each containing different-coloured tokens : orange and black . The birds were first made to understand that black tokens could be exchanged for food . <p> Then , one person discretely removed one token from each jar and presented the closed hands to the kea .
