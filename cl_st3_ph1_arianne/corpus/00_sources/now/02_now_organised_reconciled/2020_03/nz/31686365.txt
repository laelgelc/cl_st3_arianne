<h> Debris flow at Bay of Plenty town ' more likely ' to kill than an earthquake in Wellington <p> Experts say a debris flow at Awatarariki is more likely to cause deaths than an earthquake in Wellington and it is " extraordinarily lucky " that no one died in the 2005 event . <p> The plan change hearings that will determine the fate of residents living on the Awatarariki fanhead at Matata , in the Bay of Plenty , continued at Eastbay REAP in Whakatane on Tuesday with submissions from a further 12 witnesses appearing on behalf of the Whakatane District Council . <p> Several Awatarariki residents were present along with district councillors Lesley Immink , Gerard van Beek , John Pullar and Gavin Dennis . <p> Tonkin and Taylor technical director Kevin Hind told the independent commissioners that no single debris flow was ever the same and it would be impossible to predict when the next would strike . <p> In 2005 , Matata was hit by a massive debris flow after the area had more than 300mm of rain in 24 hours . <p> He said through climate change the region would most likely see an @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ event , however , this might not necessarily trigger another debris flow . <p> There would need to be some time before rainfall events for debris to build-up in the catchment . <p> The commissioners , barrister Trevor Robinson , consulting engineer Fraser Campbell , consultant Rob van Voorthuysen and consultant Rauru Kirikiri flew over the catchment on Monday with Commissioner Campbell commenting that the scars from 2005 were still obvious . <p> " Is there enough debris available now for a similar event ? " he asked . <p> Mr Hind said a lot of the original debris was not just from the stream ; a lot came from landslides triggered by the rainfall . <p> " There is no reason why rainfall would n't continue to trigger landslides , the hillside is highly erodible , " he said . <p> " I have been investigating landslides something in the order of 15 years .
