<h> Trump reverses yet another Obama initiative <p> Words : NZ Autocar <p> 1 Apr 2020 <p> The Trump administration has said that it intends to relax emissions and fuel economy requirements through to the middle of the decade . <p> In an attempt to undo yet another initiative instituted by his predecessor , President Obama , Trump 's new guidelines require a roughly 1.5 per cent annual improvement in the fleet average fuel efficiency between 2021 and 2026 . <p> This replaces the five per cent annual increments outlined by the Obama administration aimed at aggressively countering climate change and reducing America 's dependence on oil . <p> The result is " dramatically relaxed " rules for fuel efficiency . By 2026 , the new rules would reduce the required average fuel efficiency figure from 46.7mpg under the current Obama-era rules to 40.4mpg . <p> Various " off-cycle provisions " will make this figure even lower . The current CO2 targets that are set by vehicle footprint will carry over , so light trucks have more wiggle room than passenger cars . <p> The requirements were penned by the EPA and the NHTSA but they 've repeatedly been @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ( OMB ) . <p> While incentives remain for automakers to produce hybrids and plug-in hybrids , there 's now no financial incentive to develop electric vehicles . <p> The EPA claims that the revised rules will cut the prices of new vehicles by about $1000 and reduce traffic deaths but environmental groups have countered , suggesting more will die or be sickened by the additional pollution . <p> Consumer Reports estimates the new rules will cost Americans roughly $300billion extra in fuel costs . It suggests that every dollar in fuel-saving technology installed in new vehicles saves $3 in fuel over the lifetime of the vehicle . <p> The initial plan proposed by Trump was to freeze emissions and fuel economy requirements at 2020 levels but some governors and carmakers opposed this . <p> The Trump administration is also trying to revoke California 's authority to regulate tailpipe emissions , which permits a zero emissions vehicle programme . Ford , Honda , Volkswagen , and BMW have all sided with California , and volunteered to comply with higher emissions standards . <p> Other automakers want some relaxation of standards @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . GM backs a national electric-vehicle mandate in exchange for easier fuel targets for light trucks . <p> However , in the face of the coronavirus pandemic any concept of a national electrification scheme for America seems unlikely , especially under the current administration . <p> And before the new so-called Safer Affordable Fuel Efficient ( SAFE ) Vehicle rule is enacted , expect a flurry of lawsuits and court action .
