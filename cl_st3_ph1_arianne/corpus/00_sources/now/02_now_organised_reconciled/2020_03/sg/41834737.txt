That 's according to a new study , published in Nature Climate Change . <p> Even assuming a better outcome for action on climate change , where global emissions peak around 2040 , well over one-third of the world 's beaches would be lost by 2100 . <p> Advertisement <p> Advertisement <p> Researchers had previously analysed satellite images showing shoreline change from 1984 to 2016 . They found that a quarter of sandy beaches worldwide had already eroded at a rate of more than 0.5m per year , shedding over 28,000 sq kilometres of land to the sea . <p> The rate at which sea levels are rising is accelerating by about 0.1mm per year each year . Instead , sandy stretches of coastline are at risk of being eroded and washed away entirely . <p> Warming seas also promise more intense and frequent storms , which are capable of moving entire beaches overnight . Porthleven Beach in Cornwall , UK lost all of its sand during a storm in January 2015 , to be returned by the tide a few days later . <p> Soft sandy beaches are continuously moved by waves and currents -- depleting them in certain areas and depositing them in others . In Africa alone , up to 40,000 people per year could be forced to migrate due to land loss by coastal erosion if no adaptive measures are in place by 2100 . <p> But it is n't just climate change . Humans are actively accelerating coastal erosion by removing sand from beaches in enormous quantities and at much faster rates than it can be naturally renewed . Gravel and sand is extracted from rivers and on beaches for use in construction -- and at a faster rate than fossil fuel extraction in some areas . <p> Coastal ecosystems that bind and trap sediment , like mangrove swamps , are also being destroyed . The world lost almost 10,000 sq km of these habitats between 1996 and 2016 .
