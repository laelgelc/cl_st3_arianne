<h> How the circular economy could help developing countries grow sustainably <p> Laura Wellesley of Chatham House says countries like Tanzania , India and South Africa could leapfrog the West to embed responsible production and consumption in their economies -- but co-operation at a global level is urgently needed <p> It is now well understood that a radical global shift to more sustainable growth and consumption patterns is required . Demand for raw materials and natural resources is increasing as the global population gets larger and richer . At the same time climate change means we need to use fewer resources and find cleaner means of production . <p> In this context , interest in the idea of the circular economy is growing fast , most notably in the EU and China . But the model 's potential for developing countries has so far been overlooked by policymakers and businesses . With the right enabling conditions , the circular economy could provide new opportunities for economic diversification , value-creation and skills development . <p> Developing countries are in a strong position to take advantage . The first regional alliances on this topic in Africa and South America are starting to bear fruit . <p> A transition to a circular economy nevertheless brings certain trade-offs that require careful management .
