<h> Darina Allen : The meaning of sustainable food and where to source it <p> Wednesday , May 29 , 2019 - 06:00 PM <p> The word sustainable is quite the buzzword nowadays , endlessly bandied about in conversations about climate change , food security , the state of the oceans , farming and food but what exactly do we mean by sustainable food ... and where can we source it ? <p> Food is unquestionably the crucial issue of our time . Some forms of food production are a major contributor to climate change , responsible for one-fifth of all global carbon emissions . <p> It 's a key driver of resource depletion , species and biodiversity loss . Food production slurps up 70% of all fresh water . <p> At present , the priority in agribusiness is rarely to produce healthy wholesome food to nourish the nation , more often the primary focus is to produce the maximum amount of food at the minimum cost with maximum profit to the processor and retailer but rarely the primary producer . <p> Consequently , one in nine go hungry at a time in history when more than two billion @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is wasted -- an estimated 10 million tons . <p> Almost two million tons never even make it to the market as a result of the demands of supermarkets for uniformity and cheap food . That 's bad enough but it 's even more shocking to learn that seven million tons are wasted in our homes . We appear to have far less regard or respect for food when it 's cheap . Easy come easy go in the rich world , whereas the poor count every grain of rice . <p> There are many reasons for these statistics , industrialisation has resulted in cheap food -- ultra processed , convenient , time saving , but at considerable cost in health , socio economic and environmental terms . <p> The reality is that unless we are engaged in farming or food production , we have little understanding of the work that goes into growing or producing food . Could it be that we are on the cusp of change ? <p> Some millennials at least seem more interested and prepared to spend a greater proportion of their income on healthy produce and are beginning to grow some of their own food . <p> But , how to create a sustainable food system ? It 's abundantly clear that business as usual is no longer an option . <p> Farmers are doing their best to respond and move to sustainable farming systems but a paradigm shift in thinking and methodology is required .
