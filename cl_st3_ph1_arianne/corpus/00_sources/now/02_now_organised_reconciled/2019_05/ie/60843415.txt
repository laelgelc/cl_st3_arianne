<p> Aspiring journalist Waad Al-Kateab made it safely out of Syria in 2016 with her husband , her infant daughter Sama , and 300 hours of footage she had recorded of family , friends and neighbours during life under siege . <p> Al-Kateab , now 28 and living in London with her family , has spent two years turning that raw footage into the powerful documentary For Sama , which played as a special screening in Cannes ' official selection . <p> With her UK co-director Edward Watts , Al-Kateab had to find the right story to tell from those hundreds of hours of footage . It was Watts who convinced her the most powerful way to navigate that " labyrinth " of material was to tell her own personal story , as a mother talking to her young daughter . <p> " I wanted to help her make the best story and accessible for western audiences , for audiences who might be fatigued by Syria , " says Watts . " She did n't want to have herself as the heart of it , but that evolved .
