<h> Campaigners Lose High Court Challenge Over Heathrow Third Runway <p> Leading judges dismissed four separate cases brought by a group of councils , residents , environmental charities and Mayor of London Sadiq Khan . <p> Share this article <p> Campaigners trying to block Heathrow expansion have lost a High Court challenge against controversial plans for a third runway . <p> A group of councils , residents , environmental charities and Mayor of London Sadiq Khan brought four separate judicial reviews of the Government 's decision to approve the plans . <p> During a two-week hearing in March , they argued the plans would effectively create a " new airport " with the capacity of Gatwick and have " severe " consequences for Londoners . <p> But their cases were dismissed by two leading judges on Wednesday . <p> Lord Justice Hickinbottom , sitting with Mr Justice Holgate , said in the ruling : " We understand that these claims involve underlying issues upon which the parties -- and indeed many members of the public -- hold strong and sincere views . <p> " There was a tendency for the substance of the parties ' positions to take more @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , in a hearing that was only concerned with the legality , and not the merits , of the Airports National Policy Statement . " <p> Secretary of State for Transport Chris Grayling ( Ben Birchall/PA ) <p> The case was brought against Transport Secretary Chris Grayling by local authorities and residents in London affected by the expansion , and charities including Greenpeace , Friends Of The Earth and Plan B. <p> The campaigners claimed the Government 's National Policy Statement ( NPS ) setting out its support for the project failed to properly deal with the impact on air quality , climate change , noise and congestion . <p> Support from Labour MPs helped push through the proposals to expand Europe 's busiest airport with an overwhelming majority of 296 in a Commons vote in June last year . <p> Mr Grayling said at the time that the new runway would set a " clear path to our future as a global nation in the post-Brexit world " . <p> Construction could begin in 2021 , with the third runway operational by 2026.
