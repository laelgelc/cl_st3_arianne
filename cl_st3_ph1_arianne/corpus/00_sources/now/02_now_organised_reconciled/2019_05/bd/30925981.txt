<h> From sky farms to lab-grown shrimp , Singapore eyes food future <p> Singapore , the tiny Southeast Asian city-state , is an unlikely place for a farming revolution . <p> With tiered fish farms , vegetable plots atop office buildings and lab-grown shrimp , the island aims to beef up its own food production and rely less on imports to feed its 5.6 million people . <p> Singapore produces about 10 percent of its food but as climate change and population growth threatens global food supplies , it aims to raise that to 30 percent by 2030 under a plan known as ' 30-by-30 ' . <p> The challenge is space . <p> With only 1 percent of Singapore 's 724 sq km ( 280 sq miles ) land area devoted to agriculture and production costs higher than the rest of Southeast Asia , the pressure is on new urban farmers to answer the government 's call to " grow more with less " . <p> " Whenever I talk about food security in Singapore , I tell folks do n't think land - think space .
