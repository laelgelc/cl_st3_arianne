Photo : AFP <p> AFP , Paris <p> Industries in northeastern China have spewed large quantities of an ozone-depleting gas into the atmosphere in violation of an international treaty , scientists said Wednesday . <p> Since 2013 , annual emissions from northeastern China of the banned chemical CFC-11 have increased by about 7,000 tonnes , they reported in the peer-reviewed journal Nature . <p> " CFCs are the main culprit in depletion of the stratospheric ozone layer , which protects us from the Sun 's ultra-violet radiation , " said lead author Matt Rigby , an atmospheric chemist at the University of Bristol . <p> Chlorofluorocarbon-11 was widely used in the 1970s and 1980s as a refrigerant and to make foam insulation @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ global concentrations of CFC-11 declined steadily until about 2012 . <p> But last year startled scientists discovered that the pace of that slowdown dropped by half from 2013 to 2017 . Because the chemical does not occur in Nature , the change could only have been produced by new emissions . <p> Evidence pointed to East Asia , but could not nail down the exact origin . <p> " Our monitoring stations were set up in remote locations far from potential sources , " explained co-author Ron Prinn , a professor at MIT . <p> Reports last year from the Environmental Investigation Agency fingered Chinese foam factories in the coastal province of Shandong and the inland province of Hebei , which surrounds Beijing . <p> Suspicions were strengthened when authorities subsequently shut down some of these facilities without explanation . <p> To probe further , an international team of atmospheric scientists gathered additional data from monitoring stations in Japan and Taiwan . <p> " Our measurements showed ' spikes ' in pollution when air arrived from industrialised areas " in China , said another lead author , Sunyoung Park from @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ also ran computer simulations that confirmed the origin of the CFC-11 molecules . <p> " We did n't find evidence of increased emissions from Japan , the Korean peninsula or any other country , " added Luke Western , a post-doctoral researcher at the University of Bristol . <p> The findings also has implications for the fight against climate change . <p> " Perhaps even more serious is the role of CFCs as long-lived greenhouse gases , " Joanna Haigh , a professor at Imperial College London , noted last year in reaction to the initial report .
