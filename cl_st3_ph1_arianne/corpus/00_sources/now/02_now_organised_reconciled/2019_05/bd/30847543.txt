<h> The power of persuasion <p> Today 's consumers are no longer simply on the receiving end of advertising through mass media channels <p> There was a time when persuasion was easy to recognize . <p> It was the stuff on billboards and flyers or between the articles in newspapers and magazines . It interrupted TV and radio programs and it was obviously trying to sell us something . We called it " advertising . " <p> Today 's consumers are no longer simply on the receiving end of advertising through mass media channels . The billions of us who use Facebook , Instagram , and the like every day , have become a key part of the process of persuasion itself . <p> Through these social media channels we present highly edited , stylized versions of our lifestyles and are able to trade ideas and information in an instant . This activity places us not at the end of one of the linear processes of marketing , but at the very heart of our own unique world of communication , content , and commerce . Almost every activity we engage in now involves some form of commercial exchange @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ began taking an interest in consumption outside the narrow parameters laid down by pure economics . And they were quick to recognize that the influence of persuasion had extended well beyond simply encouraging people to buy goods that they otherwise would not . <p> Persuasion has become fundamental to shaping people 's view of what is attractive or unattractive , good or bad , acceptable or unacceptable . In essence , it has changed human interaction itself to the extent that consumption -- rather than class , region , geography , or occupation -- has now become the primary form of self-identity and self-expression . <p> The digital world has exacerbated the situation . For all that it has revolutionized human communication , arguably the web 's most profound achievement has been its impact on trade and consumption . In 2018 , the FAANG group of technology companies -- Facebook , Amazon , Apple , Netflix , and Alphabet ( Google 's parent company ) -- was worth more than the whole of the FTSE 100 combined . <p> And these are just the biggest of the bunch . From choosing where to eat lunch to finding a life partner , today very little human interaction remains untouched by the tropes of trade . <p> Among younger age groups this is widely accepted as a fact of life . A generation has grown up knowing nothing of life before Google , Snapchat , or Instagram . <p> Concepts such as losing contact with friends or family , the frustration at not being able to find something out , or having to rely on gatekeepers to provide you with rations of entertainment and experiences now seem quaintly old-fashioned . <p> Consumers understand that if a site or application is free to use then that simply means ( usually ) that it is funded by advertising . And there really is no purchase too insignificant and no activity too ephemeral for a review . Opinion has been commodified . <p> Of course , you can get used to anything . This is how the world now works : We use things for free and we pay for that privilege by allowing companies to advertise to us . However , in this context the term " advertising " is somewhat disingenuous . <p> It is too small and narrow a word to describe the oil that fuels and lubricates all digital media -- a conflation of a vast suite of corporate brand communication activities that include branding , marketing , public affairs , public relations , and a whole heap of other things as well as advertising . <p> At the centre of the universe <p> These persuasion industries have little in common in terms of execution , but they do share a communal objective -- to affect a change in the opinion or behaviour of their target audience . Moreover , they provide the imperative to share that information . <p> The key question is : How did we get here ? <p> It is my belief that at the heart of almost all of these problems -- from Brexit to climate change -- there lies a communication issue . The ability to assimilate and exchange complex information is a uniquely human characteristic . However , despite tens of thousands of years of evolution , our understanding of the processes involved in human communication remains extremely limited . People , it seems , are forever trying to explain what they really meant . <p> Misunderstanding is the normal state of things . <p> Whether we like it or not , the persuasion industries and their activities have become integral features of advanced capitalist culture .
