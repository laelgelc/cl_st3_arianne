<h> Philippine govt needs to invest in environmental R&amp;D <p> Despite being tagged as a biodiversity hot spot , the economic and ecological potential of the country 's indigenous plant species remains untapped , as the majority of its flora have yet to be discovered and studied . <p> There is a huge potential in the Philippines 's indigenous plants , according to the United Nations Development Programme ( UNDP ) , placing a net present sustainable bioprospecting value of Philippine forests at approximately $36 million annually in perpetuity . <p> However , the sad reality is this -- there is a dire need for the government to allocate more budget into environmental research and development ( R&amp;D ) to buttress its conservation of native trees and their protection from deforestation . <p> Biodiversity , when managed effectively , entails significant national economic growth , according to the UNDP . More than half of the country continues to depend on the Philippines 's natural resources for livelihood . <p> Thus , protecting these natural resources in all fronts -- including the R&amp;D of native trees for better understanding and appreciation -- is highly imperative and is nonnegotiable . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ regions . The country has lost more than 90 percent of its original forest habitat , which is home to more than a thousand endemic species . <p> Data from the Department of Environment and Natural Resources ( DENR ) showed that around 99 plant species are critically endangered , 187 are endangered , 176 are vulnerable and 64 are threatened . <p> " Most of our natural resources , especially native trees , remain untapped @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ lost to rapid deforestation and illegal logging .
