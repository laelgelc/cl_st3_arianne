<h> UN chief concerned nuclear ' coffin ' leaking in Pacific <h> In the Marshalls , numerous islanders were forcibly evacuated from ancestral lands and resettled . <p> UN secretary-general Antonio Guterres raised concerns Thursday that a concrete dome built last century to contain waste from atomic bomb tests is leaking radioactive material into the Pacific . <p> Speaking to students in Fiji , Guterres described the structure on Enewetak atoll in the Marshall Islands as " a kind of coffin " and said it was a legacy of Cold War-era nuclear tests in the Pacific <p> " The Pacific was victimised in the past as we all know , " he said , referring to nuclear explosions carried out by the United States and France in the region . <p> In the Marshalls , numerous islanders were forcibly evacuated from ancestral lands and resettled , while thousands more were exposed to radioactive fallout . <p> The island nation was ground zero for 67 American nuclear weapons tests from 1946-58 at Bikini and Enewetak atolls , when it was under US administration . <p> The tests included the 1954 " Bravo " hydrogen bomb , the most powerful ever @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ than the atomic bomb dropped on Hiroshima . <p> Guterres , who is touring the South Pacific to raise awareness of climate change issues , said Pacific Islanders still needed help to deal with the fallout of the nuclear testing . <p> " The consequences of these have been quite dramatic , in relation to health , in relation to the poisoning of waters in some areas , " he said . <p> " I 've just been with the president of the Marshall Islands ( Hilda Heine ) , who is very worried because there is a risk of leaking of radioactive materials that are contained in a kind of coffin in the area . " <p> The " coffin " is a concrete dome , built in the late 1970s on Runit Island , part of Enewetak atoll , as a dumping ground for waste from the nuclear tests . <p> Radioactive soil and ash from the explosions was tipped into a crater and capped with a concrete dome 45 centimetres ( 18 inches ) thick . <p> However , it was only envisaged as a temporary fix @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ leading to fears the waste is leaching into the Pacific . <p> Cracks have also developed in the concrete after decades of exposure and there are concerns it could break apart if hit by a tropical cyclone . <p> Guterres did not directly address what should be done with the dome but said the Pacific 's nuclear history still needed to be addressed . <p> " A lot needs to be done in relation to the explosions that took place in French Polynesia and the Marshall Islands , " he said . <p> " This is in relation to the health consequences , the impact on communities and other aspects . <p> " Of course there are questions of compensation and mechanisms to allow these impacts to be minimised .
