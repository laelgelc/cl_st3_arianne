<h> Late Night Talk <p> A big part of the carbon crisis is agriculture , and a big part of that is the meat industry . Agriculture contributes 11% of the global CO2 emissions which is significant as it makes it second only to energy . But besides for the CO2 contribution about 16% of the greenhouse gases emitted by volume is methane and that comes almost exclusively from meat farming and cows in particular . <p> If we reduced the number of cows , we reduce both methane and CO2 . We also get a lot more land and water back as much of the crops that are grown are not for human consumption but to feed to animals . <p> You may be surprised then that we are not doing more to reduce the number of cows and the answer is because we had little way to reduce the demand for beef . In fact , as nations become more affluent , meat consumption increases . <p> Solving this not only averts a climate crisis , but it can also address a major health issue as meat-based diets come with significant health issues . <p> Here follows @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and a plan to make meat without using animals . <h> Two men named Brown helping us go green <p> It is a story of science , marketing and an idea whose time has come . <p> The first Brown is Patrick , a Stanford professor of biochemistry . Besides the critical work to keep cells alive , it is also the taste we best associate with meat . <p> The good news is that heme occurs in plants too , just not as much .
