<h> The madness behind SA 's growth agenda <h> The idea that growth can be infinite is foolish and the pursuit of it has resulted in unprecedented levels of inequality while accelerating the climate change crisis . <p> Global growth is running out of steam . The world 's economy is estimated to grow at a mere 3% in 2019 and 2020 . It 's a gloomy future that looms as economies stutter to a halt after pursuing the all-powerful growth agenda for some 75 years . But is this stagnation really such a bad thing ? <p> The idea that growth can be infinite is foolish and the pursuit of it has resulted in unprecedented levels of inequality while accelerating the climate change crisis . <p> The world 's 26 richest people now own the same wealth as the poorest half of humanity . If that were n't frightening enough , the UK 's House of Commons library calculates that by 2030 , the richest 1% will control as much as two-thirds of the world 's wealth . <p> It 's a fine mess we find ourselves in . As countries , particularly in the Western world , continue this trajectory , believing that the growth agenda is the panacea to solve all , the nature of our failure is larger . It 's both systemic and philosophical . <p> Growth and GDP , its measurement which calculates the goods and services produced in an economy every year , offers the promise that a rising tide lifts all boats . That a higher GDP will eventually even out inequality -- making it essential to a country 's stability and prosperity . But lurking in the corner is the underlying assumption that the pie is ever growing . <p> A growth economy is an extractive system that can only continue for as long as the planet 's resources can sustain it . More efficient technology has been suggested as a remedy that will let us keep the status quo and grow as we have been while limiting our impact on the natural world . <p> Unfortunately , it 's proving clearer and clearer that this wo n't work on a global scale . Those resources still have to come from somewhere . <p> Rethinking what economic prosperity means takes a philosophical mindshift . To survive , a growth economy needs people to consume . Land needs to be privatised and commercialised -- it has less value if communal and none as a park or nature reserve . <p> Buying things new once " built-in obsolescence " kicks in , rather than fixing them , boosts GDP ( not to mention waste ) . What if the focus was n't on growth but on wellbeing ? <p> Bhutan is the world 's case study in this philosophical shift , with policies that serve both the planet and its people . Instead of focusing on growth , the Bhutanese implemented a Gross National Happiness ( GNH ) approach . They see a fulfilling life mapped out in four main tenets : Cultural values , sustainable development , environmental conservation and socially responsible government . <p> It may be true that Bhutan was already mostly covered with forest and its people have capitalised on its natural environment ; however , implementing GNH policies have contributed to Bhutan being the only carbon-negative country in the world .
