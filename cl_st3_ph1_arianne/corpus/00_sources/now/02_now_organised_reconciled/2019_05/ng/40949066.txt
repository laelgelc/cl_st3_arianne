<h> Recharging the vanishing Lake Chad <p> THE fast receding Lake Chad , once a vast body of water in the North-East of the country , has remained a constant source of worry to the millions in the immediate vicinity that depend on its water for sustenance and the international community concerned about the negative impact of the vanishing lake on the environment . This concern was deservedly raised by the President of the United Nations General Assembly , Maria Espinosa-Garces , during her visit to Nigeria last week . She aptly described what is left of the great lake as one of Africa 's major climate change disasters . <p> Espinosa-Garces could not have depicted the tragedy better , as the lake has become a miserable imitation of what it used to be . From a large expanse of water that covered 25,000 square kilometres in the 1960s , the lake has now contracted to about 2,500 km2 , while the depth , which has also taken a hit , on average , now stands at 1.5 metres , from an erstwhile depth of 11 metres . " For us , it is an existential issue because it is a lake that almost 30 million people depend on for ( their ) livelihood and we have seen it shrink by 90 per cent over the years , " the Foreign Affairs Minister , Geoffrey Onyema , lamented . The map of Nigeria without a depiction of Lake Chad , as is customary , will soon become a reality . <p> The corollary has been the escalation of conflicts , especially terrorism , banditry and other forms of violent extremism , in the North-East of the country and beyond , caused in part by the changing landscape . Deprived of their grazing land , arms-bearing Fulani herdsmen have taken to invading people 's farmland , even in the most southerly states , and unleashing terror on whoever dares to stand in their way . Describing Lake Chad as a major climate change disaster implies that the forces of nature had been responsible for its degeneration . But , though desertification and dwindling rainfall have had a major role to play in the shrinkage of the lake , the impact of human activities in its misfortunes should never be discountenanced . The flow of some of the rivers that fed the lake , namely Chari-Logone , Hadejia , Yobe , Jama'are , Yedseram and Ngadda , has been disrupted by careless and indiscriminate construction of dams and abstraction of water for irrigation . <p> Despite the gradual disappearance of the lake water , over a period of more than 40 years , very little was done to stop the shrinkage . " The humanitarian disaster that could follow the ecological catastrophe needs urgent interventions , " FAO 's Director of Land and Water Division , Parvjs Koohafkan , said then .
