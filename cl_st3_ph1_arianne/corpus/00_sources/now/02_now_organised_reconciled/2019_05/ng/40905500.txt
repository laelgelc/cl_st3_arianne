<h> The common sense in Murray-Bruce 's electric car bill <p> A fortnight ago , the Nigerian Senate rejected a bill sponsored by Senator Ben Murray-Bruce seeking to phase out petrol vehicles by 2035 . On the surface , it would be easy to dismiss the bill with scorn , and deride it for being too outlandish considering that our country -- a petrol dependent entity -- is still at the rudimentary level of economic survival . <p> However , an attempt to take a second look at the " common sense " Bayelsa-born senator 's green-inspired bill would definitely reveal that the Red Chamber might have been a bit too hasty in rejecting the bill without a robust legislative interrogation of its spirit , merit and wider implications . <p> When Murray-Bruce brought up the idea on the floor of the Senate , one could feel the roof literally descend on his head as his colleagues unequivocally rejected the bill , making him immediately withdraw it . <p> For instance , the Deputy Senate President , Ike Ekweremadu , said there was no need for a law to be passed for Nigerians to switch from use of petrol @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ its provision for " Freedom of Movement " . <p> Ekweremadu 's argument was , to me , wishy-washy at best . He had argued that going down history , people moved from the use of animals as the sole means of transport to use of bicycles , motor bikes , cars and other advanced means of transport , and therefore there was no law that caused people to begin to use cars . <p> He then made his main point for the reason to move for the withdrawal of the bill : Nigeria is an oil producing country , and should not encourage any kind of phase-out of petrol , in order not to harm our cash cow ! <p> In his words , " I congratulate Ben Murray Bruce for his uncommon common sense and brilliant ideas in the lead debate but what is not common is the need to introduce a law to mandate the use of electric cars . " <p> The idea of protecting our turf by preventing sale of electric cars in Nigeria must have sounded hollow even to the senators because they are aware that the oil market we desperately depend on is as unpredictable as the sky . It is a well-known fact that Nigeria does not influence the oil market ; rather it is the other way round .
