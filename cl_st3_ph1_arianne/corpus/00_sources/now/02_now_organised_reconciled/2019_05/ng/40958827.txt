<h> Facts and figures in Australian elections on Saturday <p> CANBERRA , Australia ( AP ) -- More than 16 million Australians are eligible to vote at elections on Saturday that are likely to deliver Australia 's eighth prime minister in 12 years . <p> Opinion polls suggest conservative Prime Minister Scott Morrison will have one of the shortest tenures of the 29 men and one woman who have served in the post since 1901 . He was only installed in August by government colleagues who had lost confidence in his predecessor Malcolm Turnbull . <p> His rival Bill Shorten is one of the longest serving opposition leaders in Australian history , having led the center-left Labor Party since it was last voted out of office in 2013 . <p> Some election facts and figures : <p> <p> COMPULSORY VOTING <p> Australia is one of a few countries that have made voting compulsory , so voter turnout is always high regardless of the level of interest in a campaign . The 91% of eligible voters who cast ballots at the last election in 2016 was the lowest turnout rate since failing to vote became illegal in 1924 . Labor was formed by striking sheep shearers meeting under an Outback tree in 1891 . <p> In 65 years of competing at elections , Liberal-led coalitions have been more successful than Labor . The conservatives have ruled for 47 of those years and have been led by the longest and second-longest serving prime ministers in Australian history . <p> <p> ISSUES <p> Opinion polls show climate change has become a major concern for Australian voters .
