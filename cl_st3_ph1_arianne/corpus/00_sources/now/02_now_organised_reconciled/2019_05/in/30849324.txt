<p> We may qualify as the single most destructive species in the history of Earth . The rate of human development has accelerated so much over the last 50 years , that it could wipe off as many as 1 million plant and animal species from the face of the planet . These warnings are coming out of a report by the Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services ( IPBES ) . <h> The IPBES report <p> The landmark report by the IPBES , a United Nations body , highlights the rate at which the planet 's biodiversity is rapidly declining as a result of human interference . The IPBES 's biodiversity report is the first intergovernmental report of its kind . It has been compiled by 145 expert authors from 50 countries over the past three years , and assesses global changes over the past five decades . For the first time ever on such a massive scale , the report also draws on knowledge sourced from indigenous people and local communities and tackles issues from the very grassroots . <h> What 's at stake ? <p> Here 's what the IPBES report says : <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Earth , 1 million are threatened with extinction . <p> More than 40% of amphibian species , almost 33% of corals and more than a third of all marine mammals are threatened <p> 66% of the marine environment has been significantly altered and 33% marine mammals are threatened with extinction <p> A Caspian Tern caught in a fishing net in the Gulf of Thailand . Or , to put it simply , humans . Here 's how we are causing most damage : <p> Population : Since 1970 the global human population has more than doubled ( from 3.7 to 7.6 billion ) <p> Pollution : Since 1980 , greenhouse gas emissions have doubled , raising average global temperatures by at least 0.7 degrees Celsius . The report calls for ' transformative change ' and a fundamental global reorganisation . Some of the key areas being , promoting sustainable agricultural practices , reducing food waste , protecting key biodiversity areas , reducing run-off pollution into oceans , and encouraging the participation of indigenous people and local communities . <p> This comes after another report published previously by WWF that puts the worsening condition of Planet Earth into perspective .
