<h> Rainwater harvesting : Way ahead to encash the alternative water resource <h> Last Updated : Monday 13 May 2019 <p> Around the world , cities are facing huge water-related risks that are aggravated by climate change , rapid urbanisation and outdated infrastructure resulting in flooding , water scarcity and rehabilitation costs on a scale that overwhelm the capacities of cities . <p> However , at times , cities or regions are geographically well placed in terms of their resources , but due to existing water management practices , which include enormous concretisation in the name of urbanisation , our cities suffer . <p> Take the example of Rwanda , a sovereign ( and landlocked ) country in central Africa with a varied geography covering roughly 25,000 square kilometre of land and 1,400 sq km of water . Rwanda receives average annual precipitation of 1,200 millimetre ( mm ) , and the rainfall ranges from as low as 800 mm in Eastern Province to about 2,000 mm in high altitude of north and west . Thus , there is ample scope to practice rainwater harvesting ( RWH ) . <p> Looking at the urban trend , the country has @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ people/sq km ) . As per the 2012 census , Rwanda had approximately 12 million citizens and an annual population growth of 2.6 per cent . Around 19.4 per cent of the total inhabitants live in urban areas . <p> Its capital , Kigali , is a major urban centre with 76 per cent of the population categorised as " urban " . At the same time , Rwanda is also among the countries having the lowest per capita water availability ( 670 cubic metres per capita per year ) and storage capacity in Africa . Besides inadequate per-capita availability , floods accompanied with soil erosion are a common issue in the country . <p> Considering the current scenario , RWH must be promoted as part of infrastructure development to achieve water augmentation and stormwater management . At national level , provisions like VISION 2020 , Economic Development and Poverty Reduction Strategy highlight rainwater as the most feasible option for providing alternative source of water . Still , RWH is not considered as an immediate response to the issue of water stress in the country . <p> A recent report @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ CSE ) , submitted to Rwanda Water and Forestry Authority ( RWFA ) , assesses the applicability of RWH as one of the potential measures to achieve sustainable water management in urban areas of Rwanda . <p> Through the report , an attempt is made to understand and scale up RWH by accessing the existing framework and practices involved in implementing RWH in the city of Kigali . The report focuses on high visibility and high impact urban RWH interventions or case studies at various levels like schools , neighbourhoods , hotels , and industries . <p> The case studies have been viewed on various parameters such as the type of RWH structure ( storage or recharge ) , socio-economic and environmental impacts , and water supply scenario . <p> The key challenges found were divided into various categories : <p> Legal and institutional framework <p> No status report or data available to showcase the enforcement of law to implement RWH <p> Lack of coordination between state and nonstate stakeholders <p> Majorly RWH systems are implemented by individual users/owners <p> Technical knowhow of RWH structures and technologies is minimal <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ systems is absent <p> Lack of research on importance and use of RWH for non-potable purposes in Rwanda <p> Resistance among people to use harvested rainwater as an alternate source of water <p> Infrastructural aspect <p> Availability of funds is a concern in practising RWH <p> No alternative provision for water supply augmentation <p> Lack of adequate storm water management infrastructures <p> After analysing the challenges with regard to scaling up RWH in Kigali , a roadmap was developed defining the strategies required , in a holistic and sustainable manner . What came out clear is that in order to have a buy-in for RWH , legal and institutional framework , social and environmental aspects should be considered and worked on . <p> Strong leadership and coordination among state and non-state stakeholders would be necessary for a successful wide-ranging RWH implementation project . In addition , a coordinated educational programme will have to be conducted to dispel myths about stored water and to create the required skills base . <p> The statutes and bylaws regarding water would also have to be reviewed to avoid legal impediments .
