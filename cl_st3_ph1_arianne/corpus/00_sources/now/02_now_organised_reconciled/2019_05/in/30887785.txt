<h> Scientists look for artificial habitat for bats <p> ( Clockwise from left above ) Artificial roosting in Panama ; call records of nine species have been logged within IISER ; leaf-nosed and mouse-tailed bats <p> Study underway to understand their roostings and diversity to help identify safe zones around the city for these nightly creatures <p> Scientists at the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research ( IISER ) are working on conservation of bats , a species whose existence is under threat due to the declining green cover . Researchers at the institute are making an effort to study the species and create artificial roosting habitats for these nightly creatures . <p> Study is afoot to understand their habitats and diversity , which will then lead to identifying zones around the city to designate as environments safe for these mammals . <p> " The number of bats is stable but threatened due to the loss of habitats . Though still not included in the list of endangered wildlife population , protecting the mammal is important , " said Ram Mohan , project assistant at IISER , Pune . <p> Bats are threatened for a variety of reasons @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and bearers of ill omen according to superstitious beliefs . The fact that they are not a charismatic species does not help their cause either . The species is also comparatively less studied in science . <p> Ram said that , at present , the bat population is quantified through passive acoustic monitoring ( PAM ) methods . " So far , call records of nine species have been logged within IISER campus and would be extended to other areas known for being their habitats such as caves , hills , north-western Ghats of the state and Lonavala , " he added . <p> The researcher said that bats have a role in the ecosystem and play different parts during their life cycles . " Bats are natural pest controllers . The fruiteating bats are good pollinators . Being nocturnal mammals , they benefit many plants which pollinate at night , " he said . <p> Studies are underway on the changes to the habitat due to human interference . " Activities of bats at night are being monitored . Their behavioural changes during the peak time of the day @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ behaviour is also known to change according to the seasons and weather patterns , " Ram added . <p> The researcher further said that the presence of the bats is being quantified along with animal behaviour through the bird calls and interaction within the species . Understanding the changes in forest areas could be found as a bat is an indicator species . " Studying their behavioural patterns might suggest changes in the environment due to climate change and thereby mitigating plan for the conservation in the region , " Ram told Mirror . <p> Ram said that Latin and Central American countries and European countries already have established artificial roosting spaces for bats . " Asian countries like Japan and Hong Kong have also experimented with these methods . Concrete and wooden bird boxes are set up and natural methods are used to attract these mammals . These methods have proven successful in other parts of the world , " he added . <p> Anand Krishnan , DST-INSPIRE faculty fellow at the department of biology , said , " The acoustic method of recording calls make easy to detect @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ night . " <p> Krishnan added that different species of bats emit signals at specific frequencies , making them distinct from each other .
