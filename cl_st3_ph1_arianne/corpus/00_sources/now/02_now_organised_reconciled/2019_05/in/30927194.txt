( Photo : Representational/Pixabay ) <p> Washington : The researchers found a connection between recent changes in the movement of North Atlantic right whales to decreased food availability and rising temperatures in the Gulf of Maine 's deep waters . <p> Right whales have been showing up in unexpected places in recent years , putting the endangered species at increased risk . The study , which was published in Oceanography and conducted by scientists from more than 10 institutions , provides insights to this key issue complicating conservation efforts . <p> According to the recent study , the climate-driven changes rippling throughout the Gulf of Maine have serious consequences for the small number of remaining right whales . Climate change is outdating many of our conservation and management efforts , and it 's difficult to keep up with the rapid evolution of this ecosystem . <p> " The climate-driven changes rippling throughout the Gulf of Maine have serious consequences for the small number of remaining @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ conservation and management efforts , and it 's difficult to keep up with the rapid evolution of this ecosystem , " said Nick Record , a senior researcher of the study . <p> Climate change has shifted circulation patterns in the North Atlantic Ocean , including the currents that flow into the Gulf of Maine 's depths . This study found that some of these deep waters have warmed nearly 9 degrees Fahrenheit since 2004 - twice as much as the fastest warming waters at the surface . <p> These changes have drastically reduced the supply of right whale 's primary prey - a high-fat , rice-sized crustacean called Calanus finmarchicus . Right whales have historically made an autumn journey to the mouth of the Bay of Fundy to feast in preparation for winter . In the absence of abundant Calanus in that region , right whales are following their food -- which means foraging well outside of the areas established to protect them . <p> The misalignment between conservation measures and the whales ' current behaviour makes them much more vulnerable to lethal encounters with ships and fishing gear @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ connection between water temperature , Calanus , and right whales makes it possible to predict where new right whale habitats develop , and to plan accordingly . <p> As part of previous research in 2012 , Record and Pendleton used data about Calanus and oceanographic conditions to develop an algorithm to identify right whale habitats . Working with collaborators , including Andrew Pershing from the Gulf of Maine Research Institute , they determined that the region south of Nantucket might be a previously unknown right whale habitat . <p> Recent surveys have revealed the area is indeed a hotspot for the species and the researchers now hope to develop similar tools to help people predict and prepare for the future movement of right whales .
