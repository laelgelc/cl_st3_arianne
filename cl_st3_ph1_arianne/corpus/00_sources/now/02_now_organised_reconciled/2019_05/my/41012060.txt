<p> KUALA LUMPUR ( May 31 ) : It is too early to tell if the issue of overcharged bills would have any financial impact on Tenaga Nasional Bhd ( TNB ) , said chief executive officer Amir Hamzah Azizan . <p> He said TNB could only make a conclusion after finding out the amount of refunds to be made . <p> " However , the number of complaints , which amounts to 14,469 cases to-date , is relatively small compared with TNB 's total customer base of nine million , " he told a press conference here today . <p> Amir Hamzah , however , stressed that any complaint from the customer was still a complaint . <p> " We still have to go and check , and try to resolve the cases , " he said . <p> Of the 14,469 complaints received from April to May , 9,028 cases were received in May while the remaining 5,621 were received in April , he said . <p> Amir Hamzah said thus far , 11,331 cases had been resolved while the remaining 3,318 were still under investigation . <p> However , he said he could not provide the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as the investigations differed from case to case in terms of complexity . <p> Meanwhile , asked if TNB had received any letter from the government on the possible fine that the company could be facing , Amir Hamzah only said that the company had engaged in regular communications with Energy , Science , Technology , Environment and Climate Change Minister Yeo Bee Yin and Energy Commission officers , and would give full cooperation to them . <p> However , he stressed that TNB will currently only focus on resolving complaints , rather than other matters . <p> Earlier today , Yeo was reported as saying that TNB would still be fined , even after it rectified the electricity billing problem . -- Bernama
