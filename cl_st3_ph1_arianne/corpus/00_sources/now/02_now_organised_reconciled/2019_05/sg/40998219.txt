<h> As drought persists , Australia restricts water use in populous state <h> FILE PHOTO : A truck stirs up dust on a road behind a dam on the outskirts of Walgett <p> A truck stirs up dust on a road behind a dam on farmer May McKeown 's drought-affected property located on the outskirts of the northwestern New South Wales town of Walgett in Australia , July 19 , 2018 . REUTERS/David Gray/Files <p> By Colin Packham <p> SYDNEY ( Reuters ) - Australia 's most populous state of New South Wales on Tuesday imposed water restrictions for the first time in a decade with dam levels at just over 50% due to a prolonged drought . <p> The country 's east coast has suffered through two years of below average rainfall , devastating agricultural production and stoking a political debate over the impact of climate change . <p> With the drought showing no sign of abating , state authorities said residents can only water their lawns and gardens between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m . Washing of hard surfaces such as driveways will be prohibited from June 1 . <p> New South Wales , home to about @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ biggest city Sydney , last imposed water restrictions in 2009 . <p> New South Wales Water Minister Melinda Pavey said the latest outlook for dry , hot conditions had forced the government 's hand . <p> " The Bureau of Meteorology 's latest forecast is predicting below-average rainfall and higher temperatures for June to August , which are key drivers of stronger water demand , " Pavey said in a statement . <p> Dam levels in the state are now just over 50% , down sharply from 96% in April 2017 , she said . <p> The state has received less than 70% of its typical average rainfall since May 2017 , according to meteorology bureau data . <p> While the threat of El Nino - which is associated with dry weather across Australia 's east coast - has eased in recent weeks , the dry weather persists . <p> With less than 50mm of rain in May , wheat farmers in the state face a third straight year of below average production , a drain on the state 's economy .
