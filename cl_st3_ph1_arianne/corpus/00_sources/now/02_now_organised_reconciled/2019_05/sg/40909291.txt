<h> U.S. House backs Paris agreement in first climate bill in a decade <p> WASHINGTON ( Reuters ) - The U.S. House of Representatives on Thursday passed its first climate-change bill in a decade , voting 231-190 to require that Trump administration keep the United States as a party to the Paris Climate Agreement . <p> The Climate Action Now Act would require President Donald Trump to develop a plan for the United States to meet the goals it committed to in the Paris agreement to reduce its greenhouse gas emissions and block federal funds from being used to advance the formal U.S. withdrawal from the pact . <p> Trump has stood by his 2017 decision to withdraw the United States from the 2015 climate accord and has been dismissive of regulations aimed at slashing greenhouse gas emissions . <p> The bill , which passed along party lines , as expected , with three Republicans backing the measure , was meant to signal to the international community that many Americans support the Paris agreement regardless of Trump 's decision to abandon it . <p> " Today we sent a message to the president , to the American people and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ climate crisis , and that we intend to do our part to address it .
