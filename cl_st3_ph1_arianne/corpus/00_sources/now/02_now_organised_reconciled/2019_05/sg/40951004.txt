( Fiji Broadcasting via AP ) <p> WELLINGTON , New Zealand ( AP ) -- U.N . Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said Wednesday that he 's traveling to three South Pacific island nations to see the effects of climate change firsthand . <p> Speaking in Fiji , the U.N . leader said he wanted to learn about the work being undertaken by island communities to bolster resilience . He said the Pacific needs stronger international support because climate change is taking place faster than efforts to address it . <p> " The last four years were the hottest on record . " Here in the Pacific , sea-level rise in some countries is four times greater than the global average and is an existential threat to some island states . " <p> Guterres said island nations should speak out . <p> " As we look ahead , your voices will remain crucial in global negotiations , " he said . " Your experiences underscore the urgency of the threat , and the Pacific has a unique moral authority to speak out . It is time for the world to listen . " <p> Guterres made the comments at a meeting with officials from the Pacific Islands Forum in Suva , Fiji 's capital . <p> He also plans to visit the island nations of Tuvalu and Vanuatu . <p> His trip comes ahead of the Climate Action Summit that he plans to convene in September in New York .
