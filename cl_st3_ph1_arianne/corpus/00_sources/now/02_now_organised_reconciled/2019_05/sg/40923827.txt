<p> The tourist destination famous for its charming gondolas and waterways is threatened by rising sea levels and could disappear under water within a century , say experts . <p> Which makes it an apt - if sobering - canvas for a performance art project by Lynn Lu , who will head to different parts of the city at dawn and use brine , a high concentration of salt in water , to inscribe the names of real and mythical lands that have vanished beneath the sea . <p> Early risers in the Piazza St Marco and other areas susceptible to especially high tides , as well as along the sinking eastern edge of Venice , may very well catch a glimpse of the Singaporean artist " painting " these names onto the city 's cobblestones this week . <p> As the sun rises higher and the water evaporates , salt crystals will form , spelling the names of submerged lands : Zealandia , Atlantis , Hyperborea and more . <p> Near the water 's edge in other areas , Lu will use chalk to form a giant outline of Atlantis across Venice , composed of a continuous litany @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ rising tide of pedestrians will efface her crystalline and chalk calligraphy . <h> VIEW IT / THE PALACE OF RITUAL <p> WHERE : Palazzo Dona Brusa , Campo San Polo , Venice <p> WHEN : Thursday to Saturday , 2 to 6pm <p> ADMISSION : Free , with optional donation <p> INFO : Lynn Lu 's performance art piece will be at various locations at different timings . <p> " I wanted it to be an ephemeral work ... something that almost magically appears in the city and gradually gets worn away , " says the 44-year-old artist in a phone call from London , where she is based . <p> Her project , titled The Ocean 's Refusal To Stop Kissing The Shore , considers -
