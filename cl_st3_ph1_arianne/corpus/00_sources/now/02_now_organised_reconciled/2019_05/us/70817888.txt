<p> This month I turned 30 . I was born less than a year after two events that are , in retrospect , quite stunning . <p> First , on June 24 , 1988 , James Hansen testified before Congress , telling America 's political leadership that NASA was 99 per cent certain that global warming was being caused by human activity . Senator Timothy Wirth said after the hearing that " the scientific evidence is compelling : the global climate is changing as the earth 's atmosphere gets warmer ... Congress must begin to consider how we are going to slow or halt that warming trend and how we are going to cope with the changes that may already be inevitable . " <p> Six days later , on June 30 , a conference in Toronto entitled " Our Changing Atmosphere : Implications for Global Security , " sponsored by the Canadian government , brought together scientists and policymakers from around the world to discuss international climate action , concluding with the following statement : " Humanity is conducting an unintended , uncontrolled , globally pervasive experiment whose ultimate consequences could be second only to a global nuclear @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , the governments of Canada and the U.S. heard the message about human-caused climate change clearly , and signalled their intent to meet the challenge . <p> Fast forward to today : alarmingly little has changed . Despite endless promises and unprecedented international agreement encapsulated by the 2015 Paris Accord , our emissions are far from under control , and governments in Canada continue to fight over and dole out major subsidies to new fossil fuel infrastructure . <p> A relative latecomer , I first became attuned to this issue around 2010 . My initial reaction , like many others , was to make changes to my own lifestyle , and those changes became more significant over time . Six years ago I sold my car ; four years ago I became vegetarian ; and in the last four years I have taken just two return flights -- one of them , ironically , to attend a climate change conference . <p> It eventually became clear that lifestyle choices were doing nothing to curb the problem , as overall emissions continued to increase . Advocates for a Green New Deal are drawing up policy proposals that can rapidly and drastically cut emissions in ways that create jobs and support communities . Here at home I have been privileged to be part of a growing inter-generational group of organizers in Climate Justice Saskatoon . We take inspiration from the work of Indigenous peoples and communities who continue to highlight and challenge the inextricable links between climate change and colonialism , pointing towards de-colonial frameworks integral to addressing climate change in a just and sustainable way . <p> What has changed over 30 years ? <p> To start , people are not leaving it up to politicians to make the right choices .
