<h> Lifetime achiever : Frances Crowe is the Gazette 's Person of the Year <p> NORTHAMPTON -- Frances Crowe has inspired countless people over the course of her life and career . She even inspired a book series about an elderly cyber-detective and activist who protects the public good . But when asked who most inspires her , the 100-year-old activist pointed to youth , including Swedish climate change activist Greta Thunberg , students in the Pioneer Valley , and her own grandchildren . She has five . <p> " The future belongs to them , " Crowe said . <p> Crowe is legendary around these parts and beyond for promoting peace that includes anti-war and anti-nuclear work . But when asked about her record of achievement , she gave much of the credit to her longevity . <p> " It is n't that I 'm different from anybody else , " said Crowe . " I 've just been at it longer . " <p> These days , Crowe said she looks forward to getting through each week " without too many mishaps . " As for other hopes for the near future , Crowe said she 'd @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ all the flat roofs downtown , and more people " living simply so we can simply live . " <p> Crowe , a Quaker , is a longtime practitioner of this philosophy . She does not fly and is a vegetarian who tries to eat local food as much as possible . She air-dries her clothing and even when she drove often elected to walk . <p> Originally from Carthage , Missouri , Crowe has lived in Northampton since 1951 . <p> Among other things , she appreciates that " we have a very good newspaper that listens to the people , " she said . " And we have a very good library that listens to the people . " She helps organize the Resistance Film Series at Forbes Library . <p> Crowe also praised the local hospital , the presence of the five colleges and the " good soil " of the area . <p> " I feel very fortunate to live here , " said Crowe , who raised three children in the area with her husband , Thomas Crowe , who died in 1997 .
