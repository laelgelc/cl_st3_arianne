Photograph : Anne Davies/The Guardian <p> Independent candidate for Warringah , Zali Steggall , has pledged to address the health impacts of climate change if she wins Warringah as an independent . <p> Like her fellow independent , Kerryn Phelps in Wentworth , Steggall signed up on Monday to the strategy developed by the Climate Health Alliance , which has more than one million health professionals behind it through their representative groups . It is pushing governments to start factoring climate change into their thinking about health policy , warning that a rise of 3C in world temperatures would have catastrophic consequences for the health of Australians . <p> Among the health impacts of climate change are an expected jump in severe asthma attacks , more disease due to severe weather events such as flooding , increased mental illness due to prolonged droughts and higher death rates among the elderly and chronically ill due to more frequent very hot days . <p> " Economist Warwick McKibbon says no action is not a zero sum game . Just because you @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ mean it will deliver a zero cost , " said Steggall . <p> " We are in one of the most exposed regions to climate change , " she said . <p> Steggall said she would be pushing for the expert panel to look into the climate change impacts on health outcomes and to advise the government on its response . <p> " My point of difference with Tony Abbott is I do like facts and data , " she said , a reference to the criticism that Abbott has made of her expert panel proposal . <p> At a debate last week , Abbott said Steggall would be shirking her responsibilities as a parliamentarian when she said she would be " led by experts " on climate change policy and what emissions cuts the nation should commit to . <p> She also criticised Abbott 's focus on power costs due to measures to address climate change . <p> " The more people understand the other impacts on them personally , the more the case for action , " she said .
