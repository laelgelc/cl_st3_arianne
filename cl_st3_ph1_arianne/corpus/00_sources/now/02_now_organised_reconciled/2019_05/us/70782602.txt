<p> Winston Churchill is often cited for the famous quote " never let a good crisis go to waste . " <p> Many decades later , U.S. President Barack Obama 's chief of staff , Rahm Emanuel , made a similar observation when he stated , " You never want a serious crisis to go to waste . " <p> Smart folks . <p> Churchill and Emanuel shared the understanding that something new and better is only possible when the status quo is interrupted and a new path must be found . <p> Making the best use of the crisis of the day is good advice for any government . <p> The trouble is politicians are allergic to crises . <p> Everything else aside , governments are in the business of getting re-elected . That is not a business that celebrates a crisis , particularly when solutions are risky . <p> By default , the operating thesis for governments is never let a good crisis go unmanaged . <p> Witness the political reaction to the unfolding crisis surrounding climate change ( or its less politically correct twin , global warming ) . <p> Canada is about to stumble into @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ there is a crisis and none have a clue as to how to solve it . <p> The Trudeau government is committed to a tax on carbon , a policy that will not have an effect on climate change unless the level of taxation is staggeringly high . <p> In other words , the current litmus test for climate change policy is a program that will not solve climate change . <p> That is a long , long way from making the best use of a crisis . <p> In Ontario , the crisis du jour is mounting public debt and the resulting interest payments . <p> For 15 years , governments have managed down the issue of debt ( see McGuinty , Wynne , et al . ) . <p> But the problem of mounting interest payments has grown so large ( interest on debt is the fourth largest item in the Ontario budget ) that it is no longer manageable . <p> The Ford government , to its immense credit , is taking the task of stemming the flow of red ink seriously . In his first budget @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ reduce the growth of expenditures and balance the budget over five years . <p> In aggregate , it is a measured , small-c conservative plan . But on the ground , the individual spending reductions have generated pushback on issues as diverse as public health and tree planting . <p> What could be a defining crisis that reshapes delivery of government services might now devolve into a bitter debate over downloading . <p> That debate , like the squabble over carbon taxes , would miss the bigger point : What is the best use of the crisis ? <p> Governments of all political stripes like to talk about transformation . But transformative change is risky in a business built on four-year re-election cycles . <p> The best use of a crisis is almost always a step too far . <p> Transforming the effectiveness of green energy by making high-risk investments in energy storage technology could be a " best use " response to climate change . Simply taxing conventional energy is easier . <p> Bending the cost curve on the provincial budget could include things like a revolution in the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ truly competitive health-care services ( gasp -- privatization ! ) and replacing all social service supports with a guaranteed minimum income . <p> But those are all ultra high-risk initiatives . <p> Perfectly good crises go to waste all the time , smothered by half-step improvements and manage-down tactics because governments think you and I ca n't stomach change . <h> This Week 's Flyers <h> Comments <p> We encourage all readers to share their views on our articles and blog posts .
