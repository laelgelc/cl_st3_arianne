<p> After learning about the alarming decline of the monarch butterfly , a Novato Girl Scouts troop decided to do something about it with the hope other Marin residents will follow suit . <p> After more than a year of work and with the aid of local monarch experts and businesses , the eight scouts in Troop 31068 built a monarch waystation at the Marin Humane campus in Novato , complete with the food and habitat the orange-and-black butterflies need for their long migration and to lay their eggs . At the grand opening this week , the fruits of the troop 's efforts were already beginning to take form . <p> " A monarch had already found it , already laid eggs and there were already caterpillars , " said Mia Monroe , a Xerces Society for Invertebrate Conservation volunteer and former Girl Scout who helped with the project . " So it really shows that the effort is worth it .
