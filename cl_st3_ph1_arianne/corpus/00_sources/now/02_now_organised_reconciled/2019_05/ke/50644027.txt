<h> ' Islands of wealth in a sea of slums ' ? Kenya divided over satellite cities <p> A lorry unloads rock dust by newly built homes at Tatu City in Kiambu County , Kenya . February 23 , 2019 . Photo : Thomson Reuters Foundation/Kevin Mwanza <p> On the outskirts of Nairobi , vast tracts of agricultural land are seeing a new type of growth , as steel rods and concrete pillars set into the red earth reach into the Kenyan sky . <p> More than 17,000 acres ( 6,900 hectares ) of farmland that was home to coffee bushes and cattle is being turned into satellite cities as developers eye the nation 's growing middle-class . <p> Nairobi , Kenya 's capital , is home to about 4 million people , but -- said urban development agency UN-Habitat -- decades of unplanned growth amid rapid urbanisation mean more than half of its residents live in slums . <p> Satellite cities Tilisi , Tatu City and Northlands City offer an alternative , say developers , and will provide homes , offices , schools and recreational facilities for half a million people . <p> " People are desperate to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ worry about power outages or water shortages or traffic congestion , " said Ranee Nanji , co-chief executive officer at Tilisi Developments , which manages the 400-acre Tilisi site . <p> " That is why plans like ours work , " she said . <p> But satellite cities are not without controversy . Urban planners like Constant Cap , who lives in Nairobi , warn they could increase inequality . <p> " These ( new ) developments rarely explain how they will deal with challenges like low-cost housing , " Cap told the Thomson Reuters Foundation . <p> " They run the risk of creating further divisions between the rich and the poor , which will be visible by the proliferation of informal settlements at the edges of these cities . " <p> INCENTIVES <p> Kenya has one of Africa 's fastest-growing middle-classes , according to services firm Deloitte , and has seen greater investment in real estate as private developers capitalise on high housing demand . <p> According to property agency Knight Frank , Kenya has Africa 's fourth-highest number of wealthy individuals , and is one of the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ super-rich . <p> Nairobi is also one of Africa 's most expensive cities for housing , the World Bank has said , with prices in 2013 nearly three times those of 2000 . <p> Tilisi , which lies 30 kilometres ( 19 miles ) west of Nairobi , is the smallest of the four planned satellite cities . <p> Like the others , which include the government-led Konza Technopolis , it will have residential and commercial areas , with schools and shopping centres promising residents " an all-round lifestyle experience " , Nanji said . <p> Other African countries are following the same path , with more than a dozen satellite cities being developed , said Alfred Omenya , an architect and principal researcher at Eco-Build Africa , a sustainable urban-planning and housing organisation . <p> They include Rwanda 's Vision City , Waterfall City in South Africa , Roma Park in Zambia and Appolonia in Ghana . <p> Their focus on housing the middle- and upper-class is one bone of contention , as is the domination of foreign developers , who he said enjoy unfair government incentives like tax @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ deepen class exclusion , segregation and conflicts , " said Omenya . <p> " These new cities are also drawing resources like taxes from existing ones as authorities try to lure foreign real estate investors .
