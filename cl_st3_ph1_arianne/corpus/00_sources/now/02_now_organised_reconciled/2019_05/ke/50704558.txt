<p> East African countries lack the institutional capacity to implement policies on clean and environment friendly technologies , which is stifling sustainable growth of green economies across the region , according to a new report by UK-based research firm Chatham House . <p> The report , released last week and titled An Inclusive Circular Economy : Priorities for Developing Countries , shows that although Rwanda and Kenya have imposed total bans on plastic bags in an attempt to reduce pollution , there are still a large number of plastic bags being smuggled into the countries . <p> The two countries have also failed to give retailers , manufacturers and consumers cheap and good-quality alternatives to plastic bags . <p> " In Kenya , the ban has affected exports of food and flower products as there is not enough alternative packaging , " the report says . <p> It is understood that Burundi , South Sudan , Tanzania and Uganda are also considering imposing bans on plastic bags . in the construction sector and compromised the sector 's ability to recycle . <p> The report notes that a shift to a circular economy would allow countries to reduce environmental degradation and reap the benefits of industrialisation through a green economy . <p> A circular economy is one in which products and materials are recycled , repaired and reused rather than thrown away , and in which waste from industrial processes becomes a valued input . <p> Among other constraints to implementing a circular economy are lack of access to finance and technology , lack of appropriate infrastructure and skills and resistance from incumbent industries . <p> " For all of the excitement around the circular economy as a new development paradigm , progress in its implementation has been slow .
