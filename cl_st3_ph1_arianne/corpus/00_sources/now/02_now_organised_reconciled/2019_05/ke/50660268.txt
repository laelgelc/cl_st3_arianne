<h> Davis and Shirtliff calls on Kenyans to install rain water harvesting systems <p> The Water Technology firm Davis and Shirtliff has asked the government to consider putting in place a law that will require all new buildings to be fitted with rainwater harvesting systems that divert rainwater into storage tanks . <p> The Water and Energy solutions company said the installation of the water systems on houses , apartments , offices , schools and industrial sites will reduce consumption and lower water bills . <p> The Davis and Shirtliff CEO , David Gatende said the government should partner with the private sector to provide water harvesting technologies , as wells as consider changing building plan approvals . <p> " People respond to legislation and it is the government 's responsibility to nudge people in the right direction , " said Gatende . <p> He observed that in 2012 the Energy Regulatory Commission enacted regulations requiring that premises with hot water demand exceeding 100 litres per day install solar water heating systems to cater for at least 60 per cent of the demand . <p> In a press statement sent to newsrooms , Gatende said enactment of the ERC @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ installations and added if the government pushes for the adoption of water harvesting structures in the designs of buildings the uptake will be similarly hastened . <p> " By 2030 , the country 's population will be approximately 68 million , a need that requires a sustainability in water <p> According to the Ministry of Environment , Water and Natural Resources , the last National Water Master Plan was developed in 1992 when the country 's population was 20 million people while the total water demand was around 1 billion cubic metres per year .
