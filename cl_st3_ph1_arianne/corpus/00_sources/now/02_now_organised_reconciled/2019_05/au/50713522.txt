<p> Since the late 1990s , Australian politics on climate change has been divisive . Although Australia signed the Kyoto Protocol in 1998 , it did not ratify it until 2007 . Then , in 2011 , the Clean Energy Act purporting to reduce greenhouse emissions was passed , only to be repealed in 2014 . In 2016 , Australia ratified the Paris Agreement and the Doha Amendment to the Kyoto Protocol ; however , any serious action on climate change remains to be seen . At the same time , some states and territories also have emissions reduction targets . The uncoordinated approach is a problem for at least two important reasons . First , climate change is an ever-increasing phenomenon , with tremendous impact on corporate , social and political discourse . Any meaningful legal framework to govern climate change requires the development of a legal consensus at the federal level , in line with international commitments . Second , there is a rising wave of climate change-related litigation globally which is headed for Australia .
