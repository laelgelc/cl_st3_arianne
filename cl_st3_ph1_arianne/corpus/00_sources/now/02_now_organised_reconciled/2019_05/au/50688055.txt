<p> It was supposed to be the big issue of the 2019 Australian federal election : climate change . <p> A range of polls and surveys had left many analysts , myself included , with the sense that this would be a crucial issue at the ballot box . <p> The annual Lowy Institute Poll demonstrated stronger support for climate change action in Australia in 2019 than in any previous survey since 2006 . <p> In the survey more than 60 per cent of Australians agreed with the sentiment that " Global warming is a serious and pressing problem . We should begin taking steps now even if this involves significant cost " . <p> Crucially , those identifying it as the most important issue had risen from 9 per cent in 2016 to 29 per cent in 2019 . <p> Advocacy groups and even media outlets also encouraged the view that 2019 was , and should be , Australia 's climate election . <p> This was prominent in pre-election statements from NGOs like ACF and Oxfam . More consequentially , of course , we saw the re-election of a Government with limited ambition on emissions reductions . <h> How did this happen ? <p> While it 's too early for fine-grained analysis , we can draw a few conclusions at this point . <p> First , the seats where climate change was significant as an issue at the election tells us something . Conversely , climate concern has been at its lowest as the Government began to pursue substantive climate action , bottoming out when the so-called carbon tax was legislated in 2012 . <p> In this election , Australians were suddenly faced with a prospective Labor Government ready with a suite of measures to tackle climate change . <p> And they were presented with an account of these measures as a devastating economic blow to Australian prosperity and growth . <p> However discredited much of this modelling ultimately was , and the broader fear campaign about everything from electricity prices to the end of petrol-based cars , it raised the spectre of immediate economic sacrifice for Australians . <h> We 're already in a climate emergency <p> So what would it take to make climate change a major political concern in Australia , and a crucial issue in future Australian elections ? <p> A climate emergency , perhaps ?
