<p> We live in extraordinary times . Those in prosperous countries enjoy longer and healthier lives than at any time in human history . Increasing numbers of patients with formerly lethal tumours ( like melanoma ) are in long-term remission as a consequence of the new immune checkpoint inhibitor therapies . Heart medications , or having recourse to rapid surgical intervention ( stenting ) , means that , unlike in my father 's generation , few die early of heart attacks . However , with increased lifespans , many are developing dementia requiring long-term care that , along with the enormous cost of some new therapies , is massively straining national budgets . International air fares are cheap , and we think nothing of flying to the northern hemisphere to see friends or go on vacation . Countries such as China and India that were previously mired in poverty are home to increasing numbers of people who enjoy many of the benefits , including high-consumption life styles that we have come to regard as our right . The ways we communicate , locally and globally , via messaging , e-mail , and skype are extraordinary . Much of the discovery and innovation that led to these technology revolutions resulted from massive public investment in research combined with the entrepreneurism of relatively unconstrained , free-market capitalism . While refusing to acknowledge the former , many conservatives embrace the latter as the basic ideology of our time .
