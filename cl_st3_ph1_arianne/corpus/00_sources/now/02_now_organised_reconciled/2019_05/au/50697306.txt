<p> Calm has returned to the streets of Jakarta after a second night of clashes between security forces and protesters over the outcome of last month 's election , which handed Indonesian President Joko Widodo a second term . Downtown areas of the capital became a battlefield of tear gas , rubber bullets , rocks and firecrackers overnight , with protesters tearing up slabs of pavement , destroying signs and setting fire to food stalls . However , their numbers thinned as the night wore on and police spokesman Dedi Praseyto said the last remaining protesters had dispersed by 7am . Roads that were quiet on Wednesday as office workers stayed away from the city centre were abuzz with rush-hour traffic . Government workers in orange overalls swept debris off city streets . Jakarta Governor Anies Baswedan said six people were killed in the first night of rioting , concentrated in the textile market neighbourhood of Tanah Abang . Three hospitals have reportedly treated more than 350 people . The unrest followed a General Election Commission announcement confirming Widodo had beaten challenger Prabowo Subianto in the April 17 poll .
