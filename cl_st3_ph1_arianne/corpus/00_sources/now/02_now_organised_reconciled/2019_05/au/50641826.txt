<h> Labor needs to level on the real costs of carbon <p> The Financial Review 's take on the principles at stake in major domestic and global stories . <p> May 2 , 2019 -- 12.00am <p> At least voters now are getting a reasonable steer on the cost to the economy , jobs and wages from Labor 's more ambitious targets for reducing carbon emissions . The party 's climate change spokesman Mark Butler , like Bill Shorten before him , suggested on Wednesday this was impossible to estimate because Labor was not imposing a direct carbon price on business . All the policy did was to set a limit on carbon pollution , he said . It would be up to business to decide how to meet this limit . " It wo n't be dictated by Canberra , so it ca n't be costed by Canberra , ' ' Mr Butler said . <p> Unfortunately , this political doublespeak just continues the fatal flaw of Australia 's climate change debacle of the past decade-plus : the failure to be upfront about the challenge of decarbonising a fossil fuel intensive economy such as Australia 's . Contrary @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ regulatory imposition on business will be dictated by Canberra . And of course that will come with a cost . Business may have some allowable options over how to deal with it . Labor 's cap and trade policy will set a carbon target , and then leave the 250 biggest emitters to work out how to stay under it . But one of the central tenets of the economic reform era is that such regulatory interventions do have a cost . Just as import quotas in the 1970s had a cost that could be estimated , so a carbon quota now will too . <p> By trying to deny this truth , Mr Butler just provokes concern that Labor has not even thought about how big the costs conceivably could be from reducing Australia 's carbon emissions by 45 per cent on 2005 levels by 2030 and requiring 50 per cent of energy to be generated from renewable sources such as unreliable wind and solar . It may be , as Mr Butler and Mr Shorten suggest , that many voters would be prepared to wear these costs -- @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , jobs and wages -- in order for Australia to play a leading role in combating global warming . But not clearly recognising these costs up front may end up undermining , again , public support for the cause Labor supposedly promotes . <p> Of course , estimates of the costs of reducing carbon emissions are inexact . And the future defies prediction . But the results of the modelling from Brian Fisher , the federal government 's former chief commodities researcher , provide some reasonable orders of magnitude . They are likely to be sizeable : a hip-pocket hit to workers of $3000 to $11,000 from an average 2030 wage of $106,000 . But the key finding is that that these costs can be minimised , including by Labor policies that the Coalition opposes . Allowing business to meet its emissions limits by purchasing international carbon credits would help emissions to be reduced where it is least expensive to do so . The effect on combating global warming would be the same . But it would cost less , by giving Australian business more flexibility and options for meeting @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that it would be cheaper to buy international permits than to close an Australian coal-fired power station before the end of its economic life . <p> In key ways , Labor 's climate change policy is more market-based and rational than the Coalition 's sorry mish-mash , including its threatened big stick against energy companies and their investors . The pity is that the political threat on its left flank has led Labor to adopt an ambitious emissions reduction target even though Australia has failed over more than a decade to bed down a politically durable framework or mechanism for hitting it . It would be wiser on climate and economic terms to aim for the Coalition 's 26 to 28 per cent reduction target , but in a more cost-effective manner that could be scaled up in the future . Ignoring the costs of climate action highlights Labor 's most chronic tendency -- to burden the economy when it has no wider strategy to generate the higher wages , the jobs and the economic growth that would allow these costs to be readily absorbed .
