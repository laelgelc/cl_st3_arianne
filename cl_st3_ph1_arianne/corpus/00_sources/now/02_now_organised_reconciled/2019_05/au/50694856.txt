In short , our changing society is producing a larger invisible vote . <p> Associated with this is a new phenomenon from a dramatically expanding media and social media landscape . <p> Polling on the popularity of leaders is a regular feature of news reporting and contributes to prominent narratives around the plight of parties . <p> The UK inquiries found that one implication of this is that people do not report their voting intention accurately when it contradicts what they see as the dominant media view . <p> To put this another way , media commentary around polls is producing a larger silent vote . <h> Identity politics trump social class strata @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ political convention and elites ( including pollsters ) losing touch with voters . <p> Admittedly , the culture of politics is much more egalitarian in Australia than the UK , but recent UK research drawing on the British Electoral Study points to an important electoral change . <p> Rather than traditional ideological categories shaping voting intention , people increasingly allocate their vote according to identity and issues . <p> In the UK , significant local variation occurred around cosmopolitan , provincial and marginalised ( rather than class ) identities and issues . <p> Yeah , it 's blue book .
