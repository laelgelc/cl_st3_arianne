<p> The University of New England ( UNE ) promotes itself as " one of the most beautiful campuses in Australia " . The large trees across the campus , some planted , some part of the remnant grassy woodland , help moderate the local climate by sheltering against extremes of heat in summer and freezing winds in winter . In January 2018 , UNE management cut down approximately 70 of the largest old trees on campus . As the trees were cut down , the trunks and branches were put straight into a massive industrial wood-chipper , destroying the hollows where gliders , possums , bats and baby birds take refuge . Wildlife hiding inside the dark and twisting hollows of trees had little chance to escape . The sad consequences of this habitat destruction are unknown because there has been no scientific study of the tree ecology on campus . UNE management were taken by surprise when students , staff and members of the broader community expressed their concern and outrage by blockading trees which were under threat from the chainsaw . A temporary halt prevented more of them from being chopped down and turned into woodchips @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and 125 of these are known to be koala food trees . Past landscape management and tree removal at UNE has taken an ad hoc approach . The university still does n't have a landscape management plan , but with painstaking negotiations UNE management has begun to treat these old campus giants with better respect , so that tree removal is not the only option in managing trees . With continued pressure from the local community we can achieve much more : protection for Endangered Ecological Communities such as the grassy woodlands on campus ; habitat restoration to increase wildlife sightings ; seedbanking for local revegetation projects ; research to discover the age and strength of remnant trees ; tree population genetics and demographics ; and landscape resilience to climate change .
