<h> New PM must reconcile Australia 's widening economic divide <p> Scott Morrison and the Liberals can bask in their " miraculous " victory , but is the Coalition any better prepared than Labor to deal with the divisions in the electorate that produced Saturday 's result ? <p> While Queensland 's regional voters swung to the Coalition , Victorians leaned towards Labor , and voters in one of Sydney 's most blue-ribbon Liberal seats ejected a former Liberal prime minister in the knowledge that their decision could hand victory to Labor . <p> A major cause of that division is climate policy . The cost of cutting greenhouse emissions effects regional Queensland and the securely well-to-do electorates of Sydney and Melbourne very differently . <p> But the widening divisions in the Australian electorate encompass more than climate change policy . They are being driven by the dramatic emergence of China and its Asian neighbours as the new centre of global manufacturing and the information technology revolution with its explosion of global supply chains and international trade in services .
