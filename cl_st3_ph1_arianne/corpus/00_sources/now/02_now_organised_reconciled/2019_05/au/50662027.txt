<p> On Saturday I had the delight of representing council at the March for Science get together at Port Observatory . We heard from some amazing local school students working in robotics and life sciences , all of whom have been selected to represent their fields in overseas projects in coming months - which is fantastic . We also heard from their mentors . What I noticed most about not only all the presenters but also the audience was the simple joy , the sparkling eyes and smiling faces and sheer enthusiasm all round for scientific learning and its application . Science really does rock . Or as the sign said at the entrance : " Science ( keeps us off the ) rocks " . Science has for many centuries been a powerful force for constructive change . It is a wonderful tool that enables us to discover reproducible facts on which we can all agree . However , as I noted to the gathering , science can not deliver all truth , and in particular science can not tell us what to do with the facts that science delivers . That 's a job for our own @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ scientist I find it greatly disturbing to hear science denounced in some circles , especially recently concerning climate change . In such conversations I find it 's never the science that is the problem but how scientific findings can be misrepresented with people cherry-picking data to suit their own personal or political agenda . To my mind , the facts are in : human-made climate change is real and the only question is how we deal with the situation . We absolutely need young people to be inspired by science , and it was truly a delight on Saturday to see that in the flesh . But all of us have responsibility to keep faith in science ; listen dispassionately to what 's uncovered by it , then apply common sense moving forward .
