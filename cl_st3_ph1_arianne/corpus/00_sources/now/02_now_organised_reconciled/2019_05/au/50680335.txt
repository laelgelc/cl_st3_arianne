Without a major overhaul of our democratic institutions , this Saturday 's poll could be Australia 's last-ever , ' legitimate ' national election . <p> Australian democracy is facing a crisis of falling trust and engagement . Picture : Getty Images <h> Compulsory Voting Masks Our Crisis <p> The growing fault-lines of democratic disconnect are more tangible and head-line grabbing in other countries . Trumpism , Brexit and the mainstreaming of extremism in Europe provide clear pointers to how long-established democracies are spiralling into a crisis of legitimacy . <p> In Australia , the full impacts of similar levels distrust and disengagement are partially masked by our system of compulsory voting . <p> On the surface all looks relatively business as usual . <p> Australians ( motivated by the prospect of a fine if they do not ) continue to cast their ballots at election time . Winners at elections are duly decided by voters ' majority preferences . And a government is formed amid the usual claims by winners that their poll victory gives them a mandate to introduce change . <p> Yet obliged as they are to turn up @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of engagement and trust in the outcome . It is the political equivalent of workplace ' presenteeism ' . <p> As a result , the political authority they appear to bestow upon the winning government is in reality a hollow crown . It becomes more legitimate for the public to resist , rather than support , the changes governments seek to implement . <p> Past existential threats to democracy have been external , like the rise of fascism or communism . Picture : German Federal Archives/Wikipedia <p> This gives the impression that Australian democracy 's moving parts , while under strain , appear to still be ticking over . It also lends itself to a sense of complacency that the system will somehow automatically right itself . <p> But without its heart and soul -- with the public trust and voter engagement that gives democracy its authority and legitimacy -- it is surely morphing into a ' zombified ' version of its former self . <h> ' Move on , Nothing to See Here ... Picture : Getty Images <p> Australia 's political system and institutions remain largely in the same configuration that was bequeathed by our founding fathers in 1901 . <p> It remains organised around a major party system that divides the world into 19th century , class-based ideologies , a system of combative and highly-contrived communication with voters , while largely relying on a mass head-counting exercise every few years for citizen 's to have some input . <p> Our political class , which makes and preserves the laws of our democratic governance system , is in a unique position to resist the same renewal that other parts of our society -- business , telecommunication and transport as examples -- have been forced to make in response @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ two decades . <p> In effect by not adapting to our new , globalised , digital world , Australia 's democracy is being left stranded on a sand bank as the 21st century flows on past it . <p> The mask that covers Australia 's slow-burn legitimacy crisis will inevitably slip , and our benign-looking disconnects and dysfunctions will quickly snowball and morph into ones that are as toxic as those now emerging in Britain , Europe and America . <h> Reform Challenge of Our Time <p> But whoever forms government after 18 May needs to recognise that democratic renewal is the other crucial reform challenge of our time , and is arguably a pre-condition for all other reforms . <p> The election is called the climate change election , but that does n't mean effective policy action on climate change . Picture : Getty Images <p> Climate change , along with the myriad of other complex policy problems Australia now faces , wo n't be resolved unless our democratic system can regenerate public engagement , and deliver the legitimacy and consensus needed to underwrite the long-term and difficult decisions @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ multi-level and sequenced democratic renewal is urgently needed to start restoring public trust in democracy and , by extension , its functionality . <p> Some reforms can be adopted relatively quickly , like real-time disclosure of political donations and aggressively capping campaign spending . <p> These initial shoots of reform will not only help to reverse the free fall in trust , but will provide a platform for more ambitious action like reforming our constitution and creating citizen juries to shadow and advise our existing parliaments . <p> However politicians tackle it , strategic democratic renewal must start happening soon .
