<h> Climate Change and the Causes of Migration <p> A global study , undertaken by researchers at the University of Otago in New Zealand , has examined the link between climate change and migration . The study examined migration data from 198 countries covering the period between 1980 and 2015 , concluding that migration is an adaptation strategy to cope with the impacts of climate change . The authors also concluded that the higher temperatures and the increase in natural disasters caused by climate change , contribute more to migration patterns than income and political pressures combined . <p> Climate change has increasingly influenced temperature changes and weather patterns globally . While the changes have not been uniform , they have tended to cause higher temperatures , erratic rainfall and an increase in extreme weather events . Predictions indicate that by 2050 , global temperatures will increase by between 0.3 ? C and 2.5 ? C , which will exacerbate the crisis . Increasing temperatures are putting pressure on food and freshwater supplies , especially in developing countries . A study published last year , predicted that 200,000 people living on the coast of Bangladesh will be forced to migrate inland because of rising sea levels and increasing soil salinity . While farmers have largely adapted to the frequent floods that the country experiences , rising salinity poses a new set of challenges for rural communities . <p> Rising sea levels have contaminated soils with salt , reducing crop yields . Moderate saline contamination reduces crop revenue by 21 per cent , compared to soils that are only mildly contaminated . As a result , households that face increased salinity are often forced to move to urban centres , particularly Dhaka , which is already one of the world 's most densely populated cities .
