<h> APRA : Climate change risk at the heart of financial decisions <p> The prudential regulator 's head of insurance Geoff Summerhayes said climate change risks will soon be central to all financial decisions made by APRA-regulated entities . <p> " In the future there will not be sustainable finance and other finance , all finance will need to be sustainable . Ultimately there will only be one form of finance , and that will be green finance , " Summerhayes said at the ACSI conference on Wednesday . <p> During a panel discussion , he confirmed that APRA did not view climate change as a moral issue , but as distinctly financial in nature . <p> However , he said after two years of research into climate change , APRA has decided that Australia does n't need new prudential regulation because the current framework covers all financial risk . <p> Summerhayes did concede that the prudential regulator 's investment guidance could be updated to reflect ESG integration . <p> Currently , climate change risks are seen in the guidelines as non-financial matters rather than risks integral to prudent portfolio construction and management . <p> One of the biggest @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ building climate change impact models which the APRA official puts down to a " data deficit " . <p> " Because of the unprecedented nature of climate change risk , and the fact that it is accelerating , solving the data deficit as it relates to management of risk requires a multidisciplinary response , " Summerhayes told the conference . <p> This could take years .
