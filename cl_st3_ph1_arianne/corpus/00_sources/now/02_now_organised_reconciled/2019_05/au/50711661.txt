<p> THE Hunter coal mine refusal that made headlines around the world in February should have no impact on greenhouse gas emission assessments in future coal mine proposals , says the NSW Department of Planning . The Land and Environment Court 's refusal of the Gloucester Rocky Hill mine was hailed as a landmark decision for considering a mine 's climate change impacts for the first time . But the absence of federal and state policy frameworks seeking to restrict private development as a way for Australia to meet its Paris Agreement commitments means business as usual , the department told the Independent Planning Commission this month . " While the department fully recognises the importance of reducing emissions to limit increased climate change impacts , it does not consider it necessary to change its assessment approach or weighting of emissions impacts in light of the recent Land and Environment Court case , " the department said after the commission asked it to " clarify the relevance " of the Rocky Hill decision . The commission was " not obliged to adopt , consider or follow any particular aspect of the Rocky Hill decision " , the mine submission said . Australian governments had not passed any climate change law or policy indicating new coal mines , or the expansion of existing ones , should be " prohibited or restricted in any way " to achieve the Paris Agreement commitment , the mining companies said . The Department of Planning acknowledged the NSW Government 's climate change policy framework included " aspirational objectives " to align with Australia 's Paris Agreement commitments , but said it did not include " any emission reduction criteria , targets or other outcomes " that would apply to the private sector .
