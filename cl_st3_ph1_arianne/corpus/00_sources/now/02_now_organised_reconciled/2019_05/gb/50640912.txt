<p> Scientists found cocaine in freshwater shrimps when testing rivers for chemicals , a study said . <p> Researchers at King 's College London , in collaboration with the University of Suffolk , tested 15 different locations across Suffolk . <p> Their report said cocaine was found in all samples tested . Other illicit drugs , such as ketamine , were also widespread in the shrimp . <p> The researchers said it was a " surprising " finding . <p> Professor Nic Bury , from the University of Suffolk , said : " Whether the presence of cocaine in aquatic animals is an issue for Suffolk , or more widespread an occurrence in the UK and abroad , awaits further research . <p> " Environmental health has attracted much attention from the public due to challenges associated with climate change and microplastic pollution . <p> " However , the impact of ' invisible ' chemical pollution ( such as drugs ) on wildlife health needs more focus in the UK .
