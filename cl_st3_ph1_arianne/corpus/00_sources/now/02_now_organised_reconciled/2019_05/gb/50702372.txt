<h> DfT accused of ' going rogue ' on climate change <p> The Department for Transport ( DfT ) has been accused of " going rogue " on climate change , side-lining climate considerations from its plans . <p> The accusation is included within a new report commissioned by environmental organisation Friends of the Earth , and features a raft of recommendations and institutional changes required to bring the Chris Grayling-led department " back on track " . <p> It points to the Climate Change Act in particular , claiming that the legislation should ensure that all departments contribute towards the UK 's climate targets , before pointing the finger at DfT for pursuing projects including road building which stand to make emissions worse . <p> Instead , the department should establish a carbon budget to run parallel to its financial budget , and draw up a plan to demonstrate how it will cut carbon emissions covering aviation and shipping as well as road transport . <p> It also argues that no more money should be spent on road building initiatives unless DfT can show that the programme is consistent with the Climate Change Act , and suggests that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ its road network . <p> Mike Childs , head of science at Friends of the Earth , said that it appeared that the Department for Transport " is n't just stuck in the slow lane , but has completely veered off course " . <p> " Transport is the single largest climate emitter in the UK -- and this government 's obsession with road-building and backing for airport expansion is making things worse . <p> " The effects of climate breakdown , from wildfires , floods , and rising sea levels are already harming people right here in the UK .
