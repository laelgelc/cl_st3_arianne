<h> Ports Part of the Solution , Not the Problem -- Chair of British Ports Association <p> A record turnout of over 300 guests gathered in London today for the British Ports Association 's annual lunch and AGM , which struck an optimistic tone about the role of ports in supporting a range of economic and environmental goals . <p> Maritime Minister Nusrat Ghani MP , speaking at the lunch , said : <p> " Ports are key to the vitality of the UK economy -- underpinning regional and national growth . <p> Congratulations to Martin and Neil , who I look forward to working with to champion the important work of ports and to meet the environmental challenge to be as clean and green as possible . " <p> In his address , Mr Lawlor spoke of the foundational role that ports play in UK economy . They are important hubs of regional economic employment in coastal towns and cities as well as the bedrock of the burgeoning " blue economy " . He said : <p> " Ports are gateways for numerous maritime sectors including cruise , fishing , offshore energy , leisure and of course general @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ nation 's goods . <p> We are not just enablers of trade but the foundation of the entire blue economy , supporting the safe navigation of vessels from jet skis to oil tankers and doing so independently and at no cost to the taxpayer . <p> Shipping remains the most efficient way to move freight -- whether it be from Brazil to the UK or from England to Scotland : your average bulk carrier is more efficient than a Toyota Prius . <p> So whether it 's climate change or regenerating coastal towns , ports are a part of the solution not part of the problem . " <p> Mr Lawlor praised the close working relationship between the BPA and Government . He welcomed the Government 's ambitious Maritime 2050 strategy , which matches the ambition and determination from industry to deliver jobs and prosperity .
