<h> New Zealand targets agricultural emissions in climate change bill <p> WELLINGTON ( Reuters ) - New Zealand 's government will introduce legislation to tackle climate change on Wednesday which includes a target for cutting methane emissions from livestock by at least 10 percent by 2030 . <p> The agriculture sector slammed the bill as a threat to one of the largest contributors to New Zealand 's economy , though environmentalists say it is also a major polluter . <p> Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern , who has set a goal for the country to be carbon neutral by 2025 , said on Wednesday the Climate Change Response ( Zero Carbon ) Amendment Bill is a " landmark action " on climate change . <p> The bill treats methane emissions from animals differently than other greenhouse gas emissions , but still targets a 10 percent reduction in biological methane by 2030 , and a reduction of up to 47 percent by 2050 . <p> Carbon emissions would be reduced to net zero by 2050 , according to the legislation . <p> " We 've built a practical consensus across government that creates a plan for the next 30 years , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of this challenge , " Ardern said in a statement . <p> 2030 and 2052 . <p> " Agriculture is incredibly important to New Zealand , but it also needs to be part of the solution , " Shaw said . <p> The U.N . says livestock farming alone is responsible for up to 18 percent of the greenhouse gases that contribute to global warming . <p> New Zealand 's main agriculture lobby group said the methane target would hurt the country and do little to help the
