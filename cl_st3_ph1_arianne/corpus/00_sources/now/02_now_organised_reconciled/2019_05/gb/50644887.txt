<p> Scientists have discovered levels of cocaine in all samples of shrimp in Suffolk , with traces of ketamine also found by researchers . <p> Researchers from King 's College London in collaboration with the University of Suffolk , made the surprising discovery after sampling 15 locations across the county . <p> What were the findings ? <p> The study , published in Environmental International , looked at the levels of various ' micro-pollutants ' in freshwater shrimp . <p> The animals were sampled from the rivers Alde , Box , Deben , Gipping and Waveney in Suffolk . <p> It said , " Cocaine was found in all samples tested , and other illicit drugs such as ketamine , pesticides and pharmaceuticals were also widespread in the shrimp that were collected . " <p> According to the Guardian , lead author Thomas Miller from King 's said the concentrations of the drugs were low , but that the compounds might pose a risk to wildlife . <p> Leon Barron , one of Mr Miller 's colleagues , said the discovery of a regular occurence of drugs in wildlife was " surprising " and would be expected to be @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ areas like Suffolk . <p> What is the risk to wildlife health ? <p> Professor Nic Bury , from the University of Suffolk , said , " Whether the presence of cocaine in aquatic animals is an issue for Suffolk , or more widespread an occurrence in the UK and abroad , awaits further research . <p> " Environmental health has attracted much attention from the public due to challenges associated with climate change and microplastic pollution . <p> " However , the impact of ' invisible ' chemical pollution ( such as drugs ) on wildlife health needs more focus in the UK.
