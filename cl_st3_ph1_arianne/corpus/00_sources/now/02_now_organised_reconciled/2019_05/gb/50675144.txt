C. <p> That amount equals almost one third of the current global carbon emissions . <p> **26;1054;TOOLONG technology will therefore have to move to the mainstream , and for that to happen various challenges need to be tackled , including cost and a seeming lack of economic interest . <p> Zurich-based company Climeworks , a spin-off from ETH Zurich ( the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology ) , is one of a handful of pioneers looking for a solution . <p> The start-up hopes its transportable technology that captures CO2 from the atmosphere will be removing 1% of the world 's emissions every year by 2025 . The firm 's head of sales Daniel Egger told Tereza Pultarova how the technology works . <h> How do you capture carbon emissions ? <p> We have developed Direct Air Capture technology that does n't need to be installed at a power station or any other industrial facility to efficiently remove emissions @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ filter made of a porous material that contains amines -- chemical compounds derived from ammonia . The filter binds CO2 together with moisture , and CO2-free air exits the unit on the other side . <p> Once the filter is saturated , we heat it up to 100 ? The plant is fully automated , and can be controlled remotely . <h> How much energy does the process require ? <p> It 's fairly energy efficient . Our first plant in Hinwil , near Zurich , which we launched in May 2017 , is built on top of a waste incinerator , and uses solely waste heat from the incinerator 's operations . <h> How big are your carbon-capture plants ? <p> Our technology is modular . The cost of CO2 in the market depends largely on the location and distance from a source .
