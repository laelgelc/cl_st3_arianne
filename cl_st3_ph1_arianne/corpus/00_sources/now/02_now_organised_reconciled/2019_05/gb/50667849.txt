<h> Letters : The conservation work of gamekeepers and land managers has proved to be vital for many species <p> THE hypocrisy in Mark Smith 's article was truly stunning ( " Scotland needs to ditch its hypocritical attitude to birds " , The Herald , May 10 ) . The fact that he has never touched a bird before now says volumes about him rather than acting as some criticism of those of us who have raised , rescued and cared for birds , both domestic and wild , for most of our lives . The fact that he mixes up racing , wood and feral pigeons clearly shows his Enid Blyton attitude to birds . <p> Mr Smith ignores the fact that farming is the process of rearing , husbanding and culling the sustainable surplus to sell as foodstuff . This is exactly what the game-shooting fraternity does but Mr Smith conveniently ignores that the pre-prepared , plastic-wrapped chicken in the supermarket was also a bird with feathers and delicate bones . Recent deaths at the Queen Elizabeth University Hospital surely must convince even one who is determined to ignore the facts like Mr Smith . <p> The hypocrisy does not stop there . Chris Packham did indeed cause the withdrawal of general licences to shoot vermin species in England at a time when it would have maximum impact on farmers trying to protect their young crops . This is crass hypocrisy as Mr Packham is vice-president of the RSPB . This organisation is on record of having shot almost 800 crows alone in the last few years . Why ? To protect other species of birds , of course . <p> Our environment has been managed for 5,000 years and shooting and vermin control have had the benefit that species which would no longer survive in our islands are still thriving .
