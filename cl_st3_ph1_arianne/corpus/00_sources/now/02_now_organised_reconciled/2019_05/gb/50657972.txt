<p> Conservationists have ramped up efforts to monitor the Farne Islands ' famous puffins amid fears the birds could be gone from Northumberland in as little as 30 years . <p> National Trust rangers on the islands have now begun their first annual puffin count . For the last 50 years , they have assessed the numbers of the distinctive birds once every five years but now , they say , a count is needed every 12 months to ensure any drop in the population is n't missed . <p> A lack of ground predators and the work of the rangers on the Farne Islands , off the Northumberland coast , means puffins have generally thrived there . And the 2018 ' puffin census ' showed a fairly stable population , with 43,956 pairs of birds living on the archipelago . <p> Sandeel populations are hit by both overfishing and rising temperatures caused by climate change . The plankton the sandeels feed on are driven further North , to colder waters , and the sandeels follow .
