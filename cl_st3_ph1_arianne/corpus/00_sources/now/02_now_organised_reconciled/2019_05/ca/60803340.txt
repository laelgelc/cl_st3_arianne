<h> Western Canada : Court rulings on the carbon tax set stage for federal election <p> Provincial premiers -- notably Alberta 's newly elected Jason Kenney -- have turned a dizzying array of lawsuits and threats of lawsuits into cannon fodder in advance of the next general election . <p> This week has demonstrated that the partisan battle over the balance between economic development and climate action will be fought this fall at the ballot box with the use of legal filings to add gravitas <p> Story continues below advertisement <p> On Friday , the Province of Saskatchewan lost a pivotal challenge to the federal government 's imposition of a carbon tax when its highest court concluded climate change is a vital national issue . But the five judges split , with two dissenting saying the tax should be struck down . <p> While the ruling was a temporary political win for Prime Minister Justin Trudeau , the court 's opinion will likely be appealed to the Supreme Court of Canada . <p> The case is also a harbinger of rulings to come : Ontario has made a similar argument to its highest court and a ruling is pending @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ plan similar legal challenges . <p> But the carbon tax is just one court challenge that Mr. Kenney plans . 's northern coast and said both bills are stoking the flames of Albertans ' alienation . <p> On Tuesday , when Mr. Kenney was sworn in , his cabinet proclaimed an NDP-drafted law allowing Alberta to restrict the flow of refined fuels to British Columbia in retaliation for that province 's opposition to the Trans Mountain pipeline expansion . <p> B.C.
