Michael Bell / The Canadian Press <p> It comes as no surprise that the Saskatchewan Court of Appeal ruled on Friday that Ottawa 's carbon tax is constitutional . <p> What has always surprised me is that several provincial governments even thought to challenge the tax 's constitutionality in the first place . <p> If the carbon tax is to be defeated , it was always going to have to be torn down politically , not legally . <p> Friday 's ruling is not a decision by a bunch of activist judges appointed by the federal government with a bias towards Ottawa . Section 91 of our Constitution gives the federal government the power to raise money " by any mode or system of taxation . " <p> That seems pretty clear cut . <p> Over the years , the courts have placed a few limitations @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ For instance , Ottawa may not levy a tax only within one province or for an activity that is solely the responsibility of the provinces under the Constitution 's division of powers . <p> Whether one liked Prime Minister Justin Trudeau 's carbon tax or not , it seemed simple enough that it was within Ottawa 's jurisdiction . <p> The Saskatchewan court has n't rewritten the Constitution or " read-in " new powers for Ottawa . Rather , the justices rather plainly upheld Canada 's supreme law . <p> If provincial politicians want the tax changed , they 'll have to win the argument democratically , not judicially . <p> There was some disagreement among the Saskatchewan justices about whether it was allowed for Ottawa to impose a tax in some provinces , but not others .
