I have doubts . <p> Most importantly , Canada 's single-member plurality ( SMP ) electoral system will continue to bedevil the Greens and other minor parties . The system is designed to produce majority government by giving big parties a boost and small parties a penalty . Prime Minister Justin Trudeau won 40 per cent of the vote in the last election , but the SMP rewarded him with 54 per cent of the seats in Parliament . That bonus came at the expense of small and minor parties such as the Green party , which are typically penalized when votes are translated into seats . <p> Political scientists @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ small parties . One way parties can avoid this is to concentrate support in certain regions . The Greens , who hold two seats on Vancouver Island , are likely learning this . If the party concentrates its efforts on a small number of winnable seats rather than on satisfying the quaintly Canadian notion that they must run a campaign that is national in scope , they might just score a breakthrough . <p> But SMP also exercises psychological effects on voters . Even if Canadians prefer small parties such as the Greens and would like to vote for them , they may balk at the idea of " wasting their vote " : casting their ballots for a party that they think has little hope of winning . In this case , a hypothetical progressive voter who would love to vote for the Greens might nevertheless vote for the Liberals or the NDP solely to prevent a Conservative victory . Strategic voting can be terribly damaging for small parties . <p> Are the Greens up to the challenge ? Maybe .
