<p> The Ochapowace and Muskowekan First Nations are coming together to develop water infrastructure . <p> The First Nations , along with Tell Properties Ltd. , formed the Atamipek Land Corp. , which announced Friday their desire to create a long-term solution for storing treated waste water . <p> " There are so many issues when we look at how do we balance industry , " said FSIN Vice Chief Heather Bear . " We need to drive here , we need to fly here , but what we can do in terms of efficencies to reduce climate change ? " <p> The partners have purchased a pit which will provide two million cubic metres of storage , as well as eliminate the need to discharge sewage to downstream users . <p> Ochapowace First Nation First Nation spokesman John Bear noted they 've invested alot of their own money in this initiative . <p> " On the waste water plant , we 're into it for about $1.3 million , " he said . " That 's totally our own finances . "
