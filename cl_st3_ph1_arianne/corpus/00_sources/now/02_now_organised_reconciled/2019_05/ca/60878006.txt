<p> Thousands of tufted puffins in the Bering Sea are dead partly because of starvation and stress brought on by changing climate conditions , researchers say . <p> The puffins ' food supply has been disrupted by changes in air and sea temperature , and in winter ice levels , according to a new study in the journal PLOS ONE . <p> Tufted puffins normally feed on krill and small fish ; food that is now going to bigger predators than the orange-beaked seabirds . <p> One of the researchers , Julia Parrish , likens this change to going to the grocery store and not finding your usual goods . <p> " Except puffins are experiencing that throughout the entire Bering Sea , " she said . <p> Starvation , she said , is inevitable given how much food puffins need . They normally eat about 30 to 50 per cent of their body mass every day . <p> " That 's a lot of food , if you do n't get any food in one day , you 're in big trouble .
