<p> Canadians can expect an average year for hurricane activity , with as many as 15 named storms , including up to four major hurricanes in the Atlantic Ocean . <p> Environment and Climate Change Canada meteorologist Bob Robichaud presented the 2019 hurricane forecast in Dartmouth , N.S. , on Thursday , based on that of the U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration 's ( NOAA ) Climate Prediction Center . <p> " Basically , what they 're calling for is a season that is close to the overall average , " Robichaud told reporters . " So we 're looking at a season that is close to a little bit below what we had last year in terms of hurricane activity . " <p> The NOAA anticipates there will be nine to 15 named storms , with four to eight of those storms becoming hurricanes and two to four of them developing into major hurricanes with winds of at least 179 km/h . <p> The Atlantic hurricane season officially starts June 1 and ends Nov. 30 , but subtropical storm Andrea kicked off the season a bit early this week , sweeping by Bermuda with minimal effect on @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , atmospheric winds and long-term cycles of hurricane activity when making yearly predictions . <p> Bob Robichaud is a meteorologist with the Canadian Hurricane Centre , part of Environment and Climate Change Canada .
