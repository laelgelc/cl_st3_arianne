<h> UV system still crippled <p> O'LEARY , P.E.I. -- <p> Aggressive testing and monitoring is underway to try to get to the bottom of problems plaguing O'Leary 's wastewater treatment facility . <p> The town 's lagoon has been plagued with performance issues for the past year , including an inability to get its ultraviolet light water treatment system working . The system 's computer has been crippled by corrosion , believed to be caused , at least in part , by a blue-green algae . <p> Mayor Eric Gavin said the overall cause of the problems still needs to be determined . <p> " Is the wastewater going in with the problem already there , or is it being caused once ( the wastewater ) gets into the cell ? " he said of the questions the testing will try to answer . <p> Gavin said the testing and subsequent work will seek to improve the quality of the treated effluent leaving the lagoon . <p> The town agreed to a plan of action following a May 16 meeting with P.E.I. Department of Environment , Water and Climate Change and WSP Canada Inc . <p> The plan @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ an inventory of the bio-solids ( sludge inventory ) in both lagoon cells and a de-sludging pre-consultation . <p> Testing will be carried out over a full one-year period , but Gavin said the town wo n't be waiting that long before starting some of the remediation work . <p> " They 're going to test hard for a month , " he said . " After a month , we 're going to start doing some stuff . " <p> Sludge removal from the first cell is one of the possibilities , he said . He acknowledged replacing some of the equipment in the UV system is also a possibility , but not before the corrosion issue is addressed .
