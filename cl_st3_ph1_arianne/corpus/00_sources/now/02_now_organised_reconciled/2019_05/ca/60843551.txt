<h> Community Forest launching wildfire awareness initiative <p> A stretch of cool rainy weather in mid-May has allowed the Coastal Fire Centre to drop the wildfire danger rating to low for the Sunshine Coast , but the season has already gotten off to a busier start than last year . <p> As of May 15 there had been 27 wildfires in the Coastal Fire Centre , including two on the Sunshine Coast , and all were person caused . By the same time last season there had been 13 fires in the Coastal zone . <p> 2017 and 2018 were record-setting wildfire seasons in B.C. , and experts are predicting the 2019 season could be equally severe . The Canadian Forest Service said in a recent briefing that current forecasting models show above-average fire danger and severity for most of B.C. from June onwards . <p> Wildfire risk was also a major theme of operations manager Dave Lasser 's comments at the Sunshine Coast Community Forest AGM on May 13 . <p> " In Sechelt , we had about four continuous weeks of extreme , Danger Class 5 fire hazard in August of 2018 , " Lasser noted in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ That is the longest continuous period I 've experienced in my career on the Coast . Combined with our local water restrictions , we were poised for a disaster had a forest fire started locally . " <p> Lasser said the risk of fire remained high well into last fall , and a small fire flared up in October , likely caused by a spark from a chainsaw as someone was cutting firewood . Fortunately , the fire was spotted quickly and put out before gusty winds pushed it into the standing trees . <p> " We 've been lucky to avoid the catastrophes that have occurred in the Interior , " Lasser said . <p> Lasser also announced that the Community Forest is ready to launch a significant community awareness campaign around wildfire dangers in the urban interface areas of the Sunshine Coast , starting with a public presentation from four experts June 10 at Chatelech Secondary School in Sechelt . <p> Lasser told Coast Reporter the speaker lineup will include Bruce Blackwell , who assisted with the Filmon report on the devastating 2003 fires in Kelowna . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ professor of forest ecology at UBC and expert in the impact of climate change on forests ;
