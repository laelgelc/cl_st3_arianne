<h> Social Sharing <h> Legislation calls for 10 per cent of new cars sold in 2025 to be zero emission <p> According to a new B.C. law , all new light-duty car and truck sales in the province must be zero emission by 2040 . ( Evan Mitsui/CBC ) <p> Zero emission vehicles are quickly gaining traction in B.C. -- sales went from four per cent of new cars last year to six per cent in the first quarter of 2019 -- but the provincial government is aiming for much more than that . <p> The Zero-Emission Vehicles Act ( ZEVA ) , passed on Wednesday , sets a target of 10 per cent of all new light-duty cars and trucks sold in B.C. to be zero emission by 2025 . By 2040 , they 'll all need to be emission free . <p> For Clean Energy Canada policy director Dan Woynillowicz , the new law is a welcome tool to make sure consumers can actually find the cars they 're looking for . <p> " This is going to go a long way toward addressing a problem we 've had in British Columbia , which is we have @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have electric cars on dealership lots , " said Woynillowicz . <p> He said often the stock just is n't there ; buyers ca n't take test drives and have to wait for vehicles to be ordered . <p> According to Woynillowicz , the targets laid out in the new act will be subject to adjustments over the next 20 years , as officials keep an eye on the demand and availability of zero emission vehicles . <h> Luke-warm opposition support <p> The opposition B.C. Liberals voted for ZEVA , but environment and climate change critic Peter Milobar said he has issues with the law , arguing it will largely prove ineffective . <p> Milobar said the fact that consumers can take their business to Alberta if they are n't interested in zero emission cars is n't ideal .
