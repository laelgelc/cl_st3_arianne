<p> The book begins with an ominous quote from American environmental lawyer Gus Speth : " I used to think that the top environmental problems were biodiversity loss , ecosystem collapse and climate change . I thought that 30 years of good science could address these problems . I was wrong . The top environmental problems are selfishness , greed and apathy . To deal with these we need a cultural and spiritual transformation . And we scientists do n't know how to do that . " <p> This Speth quote serves both as an opener and a catalyst for Montreal tech wizard Dax Dasilva 's rousing new , call-to-action book Age of Union . <p> Dasilva , the founder and CEO of the Montreal-based software giant Lightspeed , sounds a lot more David Suzuki than Silicon Valley tycoon in advocating " unseparation in a fractured world " and in declaring " the days of being a spectator are over . " <p> This kind of thinking may seem incongruous with the tech world where greed and selfishness are more the rule than the exception , but Dasilva , soon to be 43 , does n't subscribe to the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the selfie , " says Dasilva , in his Lightspeed office in a former train station-hotel complex in the Old Port . " This is the age of the self . We are in this age of individualism which has now reached its apex . There are 8 billion people thinking for the individual . But we have to transform that into cultural and spiritual solutions . " <p> Dasilva sets out a foundation of four pillars of " unseparation " in his book : leadership , culture , spirituality and nature . He then lists 40 specific acts of union he feels will ignite a better future . <p> " A few years ago , people were looking to tech CEOs for a lot of the answers .
