That 's my advice in one word . Compassion . <p> Please keep compassion in mind as you plot your career path . There 's danger in choosing jobs based solely on money . Yes , paycheques are necessary , especially with student loans coming due , but be wary of the self-centred allure of materialism . <p> A compassionate alternative is to seek out jobs that provide a reasonable living but also leave space to let you help others . Do pro bono work , take sabbaticals to share your skills in underdeveloped countries , choose to ply your profession in the gritty inner city where people most need help . <p> It 's telling that compassion is cited as a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Judaism , Hinduism and Christianity . When this many traditions agree , we should take notice . <p> Unfortunately , despite universal agreement on the importance of compassion , this virtue wo n't automatically abound in workplaces that hire you . Inevitably , some of your colleagues will be blinded by personal ambition , caring only for the people above them in the workplace hierarchy , jostling for position to shine the shoes of the bosses . <p> Please do n't become someone who only cares upward . Be the colleague who cares downward . Mentor newcomers , take a sincere interest in the work of interns , offer a listening ear when a co-worker is going through tough times . <p> Using our gifts to connect compassionately with people is social collaboration of the highest calling . That 's why it feels good when we do it -- we realize at a deep level that helping others is the right way to live . <p> Outside of your job , please use the privilege of your education to extend a compassionate hand to people who are struggling . Many have scant hope of improving their lot unless someone helps them . <p> You 've learned to think and recognize the larger societal forces that keep people down . Now , it 's your turn to act and use your knowledge to effectively navigate the oppressive systems on behalf of people who ca n't do it themselves . Lead a social-justice crusade , lobby bureaucrats and politicians , tutor refugees , help compassionate people run for political office or run for office yourself . <p> And while beaming compassion at people within your orbit , please do n't forget the generations of your parents and grandparents . The baby boomers are easy targets for criticism , even resentment , because of the messes they 're leaving behind as they age out of positions of power . As only one example , the devastating effects of climate change have been known for more than 20 years but , for all our talk , Canadians have resisted the tough decisions necessary to come anywhere close to meeting emission-reduction targets . <p> Boomers likely regret @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a better job of solving economic , societal and ecological problems . Their only defence is that making the world a better place can be more complex than it seems in textbooks . <p> Finally , graduates , please extend compassion to yourselves . Classroom culture has conditioned you to strive for A-plus perfection . It 's important , though , to allow yourselves to be imperfect . When you make mistakes , learn from them and move on . Be gentle with yourselves . <p> A benefit of living a compassionate life is that compassion often rebounds . When you try to help others , people tend to notice and treat you in turn with respectful concern . Relationships within the compassion club are profound and heartfelt . That makes a life well-lived . <p> The Dalai Lama summed it up nicely : " If you want others to be happy , practise compassion . If you want to be happy , practise compassion .
