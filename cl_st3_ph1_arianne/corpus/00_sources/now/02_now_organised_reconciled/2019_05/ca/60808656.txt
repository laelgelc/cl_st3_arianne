<p> A UN report that concludes a million species are threatened with extinction and that " fundamental , structural change " is required to halt a steep decline in the natural environment has lessons for Canada , says one of its authors . <p> " The current fight that we 're having between provinces and the feds around oilsands , pipelines , climate change and local environmental impacts ... is not actually a fight that we should be having , " said Kai Chan , a University of British Columbia professor , who helped write the report from the International Panel on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services . <p> That report was released Monday after a three-year effort by hundreds of scientists from 50 countries . <p> " The fact that we are faced with such a stark choice is actually the product of a broken 20th-century economy that 's not fit for purpose in the 21st century . " <p> The UN report is full of scary numbers compiled from more than 15,000 papers . <p> It says the current extinction rate is tens to hundreds of times higher than the 10-million-year average .
