<h> More water wells in villages , but ensure quality , conservation <h> Sunday May 12 2019 <p> Advertisement <p> By Saumu Jumanne Saumu.j@gmail.com <p> We have heard about old but true stories of women and children in developing countries , Tanzania not an exception , walking many kilometres every day to fetch water for their households . Those of us who were born in rural areas , it was a way of life . I remember it was always a mad rush after school , to go to fetch water . It was the core duty for rural women and girls . In some areas , girls miss out classes as they spend much time in fetching water . <p> Talking of good health and well being of the community , provision of adequate , clean , quality and safe water is one of the most important concerns . Yes , water is life ! and without it , there can not be life . <p> We all know that " water poverty " ( lack of adequate , clean , quality and safe water ) makes the community vulnerable to waterborne illnesses and high infant mortality rates @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ dysentery , and cholera kill more children under the age of five years than malaria , measles , and HIV/Aids when combined together . It is estimated that more than 80 per cent of illnesses in developing countries are associated with poor water and sanitary conditions . For some of us living in urban areas , we have tap water , which is billed on a monthly basis . When there is water shortage and the tap does n't produce any , we make so much noise , and the crisis can be felt far and wide . <p> While some rural areas today have piped water , many still depend on streams/rivers , springs , and wells/boreholes . The wells/boreholes offer the most hope for villagers , unlike streams ( seasonal ) , and piped water ( few and unequally distributed ) which they do n't meet people 's needs and demands . <p> Over the years , the government has done a lot to ensure availability and access to clean drinking water for the citizens by establishing various water authorities , in some places it has set up @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Mbarawa , the minister for Water , who informed Parliament that the government has scrapped charges on boreholes for domestic use , was very considerate ! <p> Water debates in Parliament for years have been very emotional and heated . Every MP knows that water is one of the most important resources for citizens , so lack of it in one 's constituency , can lead to future loss of votes . <p> Villages without piped water should have safe boreholes . But as we talk of water , there is a need to heed Vice President Samia Suluhu Hassan 's call to " wisely manage the natural resources . " <p> According to the World Bank 's report , rapid population growth , increasing urbanisation , and climate change are some of the factors affecting natural resources including water . There is little doubt that some of our natural resources have been depleted . For example , streams that we used to fetch water in my village some dried up long time ago , unless it rains . We had large forests , which have been declining . If @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ let 's protect our wetlands , forests and plant trees . Trees are the greatest sources of freshwater . <p> So as we advocate for villages to establish boreholes/wells , it 's prudent to put in place environmental programmes that value tree planting . There are water committees in many villages but do they spearhead environmental protection ? <p> Tanzania is a farming nation ; all farmers should be educated about the importance of resource conservation , which could be the key to our future prosperity or doom , if we do n't take care . <p> Community 's health depends on access to quality water to all , and implementing the 2019 World Water Day ( celebrated 22nd March ) theme , " ' Leaving no one behind , " will help us achieve the UN Sustainable Development Goal 6 calling for " ensuring the availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all by 2030. "
