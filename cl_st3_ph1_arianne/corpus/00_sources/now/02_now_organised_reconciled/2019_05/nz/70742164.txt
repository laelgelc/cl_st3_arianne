Thames is at the front line of climate change with rising sea levels a threat to the town . <p> OPINION : Our New Zealand research on adaptation shows that transformational actions will be necessary to address what the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) describe as " rapid , far-reaching and unprecedented changes " . These changes require greenhouse gas emissions reductions and adaptation to the impacts of climate change . <p> We have a culture of reacting to climate ' events ' when they happen and treating climate change impacts as though we can somehow stop them by ' defending ' ourselves from nature 's ongoing changes . This approach is tantamount to policy failure . <p> Our history as a nation is littered with examples where we fail to act soon enough , to foreseeable problems or we create the conditions that expose people and buildings to ongoing damage @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that lead to mine safety failures and 29 deaths at Pike River in 2012 , the change to building regulations that resulted in leaky buildings in 2002 to 2004 and billions of ongoing public and private costs ; and the inadequate consideration of ground conditions in local government planning that lead to the widespread liquefaction damage from the Canterbury earthquakes in 2010 and 2011 . <p> These policy failures were a combination of national and local government failing to join the dots between functions and lack of proactive attention to the risks . And some councils around New Zealand are still doing this by continuing to issue planning and building consents in low-lying areas exposed to ongoing sea-level rise that is causing coastal inundation and rising groundwater . It has somehow become too hard for many agencies and councils to exercise their responsibilities in laws that were designed to reduce and avoid such damages and loss . So what does this mean for how New Zealand can approach the climate change adaptation challenge ? <p> Transformational change means rethinking where and how we live and designing institutional and governance arrangements fit for a changing world . We need to put the brakes on further developments in exposed areas , like at the coast and rethink how we use our land and water and shift to more sustainable uses .
