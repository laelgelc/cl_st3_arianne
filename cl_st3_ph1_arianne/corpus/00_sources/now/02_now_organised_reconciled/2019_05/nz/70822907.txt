<h> Up to 90% of northern New Zealand seabirds at risk of extinction , report reveals <p> Northern New Zealand seabirds are in serious decline , with scientists estimating up to 90 per cent are at risk of extinction , a new report has revealed . <p> A major review by Northern New Zealand Seabird Trust , released today , looked at seabird populations in northern New Zealand - considered a biodiversity hotspot with more than 28 species breeding in the region and five native to the area . Currently , very little is known about the status of , and threats to , many of the birds , the trust said . <p> " We must first understand what threats these seabirds are up against before we can establish a baseline from which to work on researching and conserving them , " explained lead author Edin Whitehead , a University of Auckland doctoral student . <p> Threats to seabird populations can be broadly categorised into six major areas : invasive species , fisheries , pollution , climate change , disease and direct human impacts such as disturbance and coastal development . <p> Some of the most vulnerable species @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ by-catch , and the fairy tern , the country 's most endangered bird , which is down to around one dozen breeding pairs restricted to an area between Whangarei and Auckland . The tern population is directly threatened by human impacts . <p> While some threats to seabird populations are well-known , such as introduced mammalian predators and fisheries , others are only just emerging , such as climate change , pollution - especially plastic - and disease , the trust said . <p> The report should be eye-opening , said University of Auckland associate professor and report co-author James Russell . <p> " Although there has been some progress in eradicating invasive species from islands to restore seabird populations , this is n't enough when they are facing multiple threats both on land and at sea , " he said .
