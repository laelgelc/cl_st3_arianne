<h> Meat processors and exporters alarmed by govt climate change target <p> New Zealand 's meat processors and exporters are alarmed at the government 's proposed methane emissions reduction target . <p> Tim Ritchie , chief executive of the Meat Industry Association ( MIA ) , says the government appears to have based its target on a range of global emissions scenarios in the IPCC report . <p> " We are concerned the government has cherrypicked aspects of a report summarising global emission pathway scenarios and applied it to New Zealand . <p> " Their target is contrary to science-based policy . The science indicates that to achieve a goal of no increased global warming from agriculture , nitrous oxide emissions from agriculture must come down to net zero as quickly as possible , but methane only requires a slow decline in the order of 0.3% a year . <p> " This equates to a methane reduction of 10% by 2050 ( up to 22% if the rest of the world achieves significant emissions reductions ) . It would lead to the bizarre situation where fossil fuel emitters can offset their emissions by buying forest , but a farmer can not offset their emissions by planting forest on their own land . " <p> Mr Ritchie says the government 's proposed emissions targets are simply not possible with current technologies and farming methods , short of a significant decrease in pastoral agriculture . <p> " This will impose enormous economic costs on the country and threaten many regional communities who depend on pastoral agriculture . <p> " Such a reduction in farm production will have a serious domino effect onto meat processors , exporters and the wider economy .
