<h> Mechanised future for fruit orchards <p> The orchard of the future will be highly digitised and more productive , with fruit being grown in a protected environment and tended by robots , says Plant and Food Research ( PFR ) scientist Dr Jill Stanley . <p> She said human workers would still be in demand as labour requirements would be the same but there would be less pressure at peak times . <p> Dr Stanley was the guest speaker at the Alexandra , Clyde and Districts Business Group 's monthly breakfast meeting last Friday and talked about what the horticulture sector would look like by 2050 . <p> Central Otago orchardists may well use robotics , as well as high precision and vertical farming systems to produce healthier fruit that lasts up to three weeks in a consumer 's fruit bowl . <p> She said the world 's population was expected to reach 9 billion by 2050 and the World Bank predicted global food production would need to increase by 50% to feed them . <p> ' ' How we will grow sufficient food to do that ?
