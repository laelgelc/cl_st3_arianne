<h> Farming and forestry can be partners , not enemies <p> The total amount of land planted in forestry does not suggest an alarming shift from pastoral farming , but more a gradual redistribution in land use , argues Julie Collins of Te Uru Rakau Forestry NZ . <p> OPINION : The One Billion Trees Programme offers an opportunity to drive integrated land management and build greater resilience for rural communities . <p> Reaching the one billion trees target will see 230,000 to 430,000 hectares planted across New Zealand over 10 years . That will take the total land planted in forestry from 1.7 million hectares to about 2m . <p> By comparison , farming covers 12.6m ha . That does not suggest an alarming shift , more a gradual redistribution in land use . <p> Rural communities are a key partner in achieving our goals as a country . <p> The right species , planted in the right place , and for the right purpose will enhance land management outcomes and build resilience , particularly to environmental shocks and a changing climate . <p> BENN BATHGATE/STUFF <p> Forestry Minister Shane Jones at the opening of a standalone forestry @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ already an impressive uptake of planting and regenerating forests on farms for a number of reasons . To dispel a myth that is swirling : whole farm conversion is not being subsidised by the Government . Rather , we are supporting landowners -- particularly farmers -- to get the best out of their land .
