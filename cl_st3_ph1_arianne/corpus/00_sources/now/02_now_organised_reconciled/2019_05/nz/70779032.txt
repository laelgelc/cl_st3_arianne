<h> Advance Australia fairer ? <p> Sunrise over the Sydney Opera House . Will there be a new dawn in Australian politics ? <p> EDITORIAL : Pity the Australian voters , who are so unmoved by their choice of prime minister they would rather have someone else 's . Ours , in fact . Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern has reportedly scored higher on an Australian " believability index " than any homegrown politician . Ardern was top at 77 per cent believability ; current Prime Minister Scott Morrison was at 43 per cent and his rival , Bill Shorten , was at 42 per cent . <p> Ardern scored high on trust , unlike Morrison or Shorten . Both men are generally seen as grey and uninspiring . <p> We like to make fun of Australian politics , with its lacklustre options , its prime ministers who have hardly got their feet under the desk before they are rolled and its bizarre right-wingers , perhaps because it is one of the few times we get to feel superior to a bigger , more prosperous neighbour . But apart from the enjoyable sport of it , there are real issues @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> Australians go to the polls on Saturday . There are strong indications that Australia may have , in Shorten , its third prime minister in 12 months . A Guardian Essential poll has Shorten 's Labor narrowly ahead of the governing coalition at 51.5 per cent to 48.5 per cent . A Roy Morgan poll says 52 to 48 . <p> This is potentially good news for New Zealand because Labor has promised to champion the rights of the 650,000 of us who choose to live on that side of the ditch . Most of them have been unable to access social security , student allowances and disability insurance , even if they are working and paying taxes , after a law change in 2001 . <p> But we are more generous . Australians living in New Zealand have full access to welfare , student loans and allowances , along with an easy path to citizenship . <p> Australians can vote here , but New Zealanders can not vote there . Nor can New Zealanders join the Australian Defence Force , although we allow the reverse . It is at odds with Australia 's status as an open and free democracy and makes a mockery of the long , close relationship between the two countries . <p> It is hard to find any mention of a pro-Kiwi policy on the Australian Labor Party website . But in a speech in October 2018 , Shorten said that " no country is a closer friend to Australia than New Zealand , no nation understands us better " , and " improved treatment of the hundreds of thousands of long-term New Zealand permanent residents -- and taxpayers -- in Australia would be on my agenda " . <p> In a paragraph in Labor 's National Platform , published in December 2018 , we read that " Labor believes that there is an inequity for New Zealand citizens living in Australia under the terms of the Trans-Tasman Travel Arrangements ( TTTA ) " . That comes within a longer section on Australia as a nation built on @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ treatment of New Zealanders since his first meeting with Ardern in 2017 . <p> Of course it 's highly unlikely that when they cast their votes , many Australians will have the rights of New Zealanders on their minds , and New Zealanders themselves are unable to vote . Climate change , security and the economy are of greater interest to most Australian voters . But if advance polls are accurate , fairer treatment of Kiwis in Australia may finally be a positive side-effect.
