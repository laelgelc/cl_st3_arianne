<h> Lobby group 50 Shades of Green calls for pause on blanket forestry <p> The Government needs to hit the pause button on policies which have led to thousands of hectares of hill country farmland being converted to blanket forestry in the last year , a newly-formed lobby group says . <p> 50 Shades of Green spokesman Mike Butterick said significant land use change was happening and its speed and scale had caught everyone by surprise . <p> " It has snowballed so quickly that we need to hit the pause button and ask whether this is what we intended to happen . <p> " We are not against dealing with climate change , just the way we get there . Blanket forestry is only a 50 year solution to the carbon and climate change problem . " <p> The group , which has gathered supporters from around New Zealand since forming about two weeks ago , plans to march on Parliament with its concerns . <p> It said Government incentives to plant trees , to offset greenhouse gas emissions and meet reduction targets , were tilting the market too far in favour of both domestic and overseas forestry @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ it is all about moderation and having the right tree in the right place , " Butterick said , who is a Wairarapa sheep and beef farmer . <p> " We are some of the most efficient food producers in the world and the social , economic and environmental consequences of blanket forestry are significant . " <p> Seven Wairarapa and Tararua farms have been sold in the last few months to be planted in trees . Farmland sales to forestry totalled about 10,000 hectares in Wairarapa and 6000 to 8000ha in the Pongaroa area of Tararua . <p> At Wairoa , south of Gisborne , about 10,000ha had been purchased , 7 per cent of the district 's pastoral land . <p> Three factors were believed to be skewing the market when hill country farmland came up for sale : grants available under the Government 's one billion trees programme , Emissions Trading Scheme carbon credit income and the less stringent criteria overseas buyers must meet when investing in forestry versus farmland . <p> SUPPLIED <p> 50 Shades of Green spokesman Mike Butterick , a farmer , says the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ forestry has caught everyone by surprise . <p> Traditionally to get an application to buy farmland approved by the Overseas Investment Office , potential buyers had to show that it would be of benefit to New Zealand through such things as more jobs and enhanced environment stewardship and public access . <p> " To go to pine trees , none of that matters now .
