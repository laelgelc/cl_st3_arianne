<h> Global extinction rates accelerating -- Expert Reaction <p> One million species are threatened with extinction and the rate of species extinction is accelerating , a global report warns . <p> The UN 's Intergovernmental Science Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services ( IPBES ) paints a grim portrait of life on Earth , with human-led changes in the form of habitat loss , invasive species and climate change all contributing to the decline of species worldwide . <p> The report examines changes to biodiversity over the past five decades and suggests radical change is the only way we can halt or reverse high rates of extinction over the coming decades . <p> The SMC gathered expert comments on the #GlobalAssessment report , feel free to use these comments in your reporting . Dr Andrea Byrom , Director of the NZ 's Biological Heritage National Science Challenge , comments : <p> " This report could not be more clear . Nature is in peril , and humans are the cause . Even more importantly however , it makes a very explicit link between biodiversity and ecosystem function : a loss of biodiversity -- which has accelerated massively in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ development , economic productivity , security , and the societies we live in . <p> " Put simply , we are making irrevocable changes to nature , and we need to take urgent action to prevent such changes before it is too late . In every domain , from the high mountaintops to the bottom of the oceans , from the Arctic to the tropics , in freshwaters and forests and oceans , human impacts are significant and wide-reaching . <p> " The report is also crystal clear about the big drivers of global environmental change : ( 1 ) Changes in land and sea use ; ( 2 ) Exploitation ; ( 3 ) Climate change ; ( 4 ) Pollution ; and ( 5 ) Invasive alien species . These are prioritised in the above order , but it is acknowledged that different regions , and across different domains ( for example freshwater cf. oceans ) , one or other of these drivers need to be afforded higher priority . <p> " In Aotearoa , I suspect that invasive species are more front of mind for us than @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ local example of the growing awareness of the plight of our biodiversity ) , but the take-home message is that together , these human-induced drivers of global environmental change need to be tackled head-on . And most importantly in my view , it highlights that the global growth-at-all cost economic paradigm simply can not continue without significant environmental impact .
