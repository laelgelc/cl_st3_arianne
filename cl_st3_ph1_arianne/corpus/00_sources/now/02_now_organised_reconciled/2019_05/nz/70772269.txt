<h> It was 28.9 degrees Celsius near the Arctic Ocean this weekend as carbon dioxide hit its highest level in human history <p> Over the weekend , the climate system sounded simultaneous alarms . Near the entrance to the Arctic Ocean in northwest Russia , the temperature surged to 84 degrees Fahrenheit , or 28.9 degrees Celsius . <p> Meanwhile , the concentration of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere eclipsed 415 parts per million for the first time in human history . <p> By themselves , these are just data points . But taken together with so many indicators of an altered atmosphere and rising temperatures , they blend into the unmistakable portrait of human-induced climate change . <p> Saturday 's steamy 28.9C reading was posted in Arkhangelsk , Russia , where the average high temperature is around 12C ( 54 Fahrenheit ) this time of year . The city of 350,000 people sits next to the White Sea , which feeds into the Arctic Ocean 's Barents Sea . <p> In Koynas , a rural area to the east of Arkhangelsk , it was even hotter on Sunday , soaring to 30.5C .
