<h> For term wellbeing to be meaningful , Budget spending must be assessed across society <p> Greater wellbeing is going to require a greater tax take , because as things stand , my wellbeing would be higher if the Government had $100 extra to help struggling children than if I spent that money on a new pair of shoes . <p> OPINION : This Budget does n't deliver a transformation -- but does lay the intellectual foundation for one . <p> We 're all trying to work out what a " WellBeing Budget " actually means . In presentation terms , the main , 150-page Budget document looks the part . Pages and pages are devoted to issues like child poverty , outlining measures that will enhance people 's ability to lead flourishing lives , the simplest definition of wellbeing . <p> The financial analyses and forecasts , normally first-up , now come later : economic growth matters , but only where it supports that human flourishing . This is all very different to previous Budgets , and all as it should be . <p> But it could also be oversold . Despite what the prime minister implies , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have always pursued myriad goals . So what makes a wellbeing agenda more than a simple repackaging of things that would have happened anyway ? <p> Two things count here . The first is taking seriously the evidence about what helps people flourish . So the $1.9 billion for mental health is massive . As a policy area , it has long been neglected , but is crucial to wellbeing . <p> Equally important are the measures that take pressure off parents , like the $150 per pupil for decile 1-7 schools that stop asking for donations ( although its effectiveness will have to be carefully watched ) . Reducing basic stresses helps parents hold it together , be kind to their children , and generally thrive , not just survive . <p> Ensuring that benefits rise with wages will stop the living standards of beneficiaries falling further and further adrift of those in the middle . Then there is an extra $80 million funding for Whanau Ora , a scheme that radically reshapes services around the wellbeing needs that families themselves have identified . And a more holistic world @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ like te reo promotion . <p> Some of this investment , though , might still have happened under a government that had never pronounced the word " wellbeing " . If the term is to be meaningful , the second point is that Budget spending must be assessed on its impact across the spectrum of wellbeing . <p> In other words , ministers would have to submit joint Budget bids , and explain which of the dozens of indicators of wellbeing -- trust in others , say , or school results -- would rise under their proposal , and which fall . Human flourishing , that many-splendored ideal , would have to be always in view . <p> People familiar with these processes say this has partly happened . The Treasury employed a more coherent framework to assess bids , and received more joined-up proposals . This of course builds on the previous government 's Social Investment approach and its public sector targets . This is very much evolution , not revolution , no matter what ministers tell us . <p> But the Budget process still fell far short of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Robertson touts . The indicators are too new , the abilities of public servants too limited , and in some cases the data too scarce . <p> Furthermore , the Government 's predetermined fiscal rules severely limit its ability to enhance wellbeing . It plans to keep public spending at 28.8 per cent of GDP by 2023 , even though many developed nations spend 40 per cent or more . Yet greater wellbeing is going to require a greater tax take , because as things stand , my wellbeing would be higher if the Government had $100 extra to help struggling children -- with all its consequent long-term social benefits -- than if I spent that money on a new pair of shoes . <p> We 're also going to need to spend serious cash on things like renewable energy if we 're to avert disastrous climate change . The absence of any notable spending here is the Budget 's greatest blind spot . It also avoids a real test of the wellbeing approach , in which climate change policies that might hurt the poor -- higher fuel taxes , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cash injections for those families . <p> That , though , is all to come . Despite the frenetic activity of its first 100 days , the Government has only ever promised what Education Minister Chris Hipkins calls " radical incrementalism " -- small initial steps that ultimately lead to big change . Several years ' worth of Wellbeing Budgets , if done better and freed from unnecessary fiscal constraints , could set the country on that genuinely different path . <p> Max Rashbrooke is the author of Government for the Public Good ( BWB 2018 ) .
