<h> Climate change may impact kiwifruit <p> Climate change may force the kiwifruit industry to look for new places to grow its vines -- especially the green hayward variety . <p> NIWA 's new chief scientist for climate , atmosphere and hazards , Dr Andrew Tait , says the hayward variety needs a cold winter to induce bud break and flowering in spring . But as Bay of Plenty becomes warmer in winter , achieving this will naturally become more difficult , he says . <p> " At present , one of the adaptation strategies to counter the warmer winters is to apply a chemical called Hi Cane , short for hydrogen cyanimide , used to stimulate some of that bud break when they do n't get the sufficient winter chilling they want naturally , " Tait told Hort News . <p> " There are ways of fooling the plant to do what nature does , but during our study of the kiwifruit industry there were questions being asked about the use of this chemical in respect of the market accepting it .
