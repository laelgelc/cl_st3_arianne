<h> Lure of the ice entices bright young researchers <p> Antarctica continues to call bright young researchers to her icy shores , and scholarships are making their ground-breaking research at University of Canterbury ( UC ) possible . <p> " They are the top of the top , " UC 's Gateway Antarctica director Professor Wolfgang Rack says of the two students who accepted scholarships last week , Shinae Montie and Rose Foster . " I am looking forward to the research they produce . It is amazing , the difference they can make , because they are really at the forefront of new knowledge that is relevant to global change . " <p> The Christchurch City Council ( CCC ) , ChristchurchNZ and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Trade ( MFAT ) contribute to scholarships for Antarctic research at UC . <p> Montie was awarded a $5000 MFAT scholarship for her research using satellite remote sensing to detect heatwaves in the Antarctic Ocean . <p> " We should be more worried about heatwaves in the ocean than gradual warming , " she says . <p> Foster will explore the microhabitat variables , such as fast ice ( @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ penguin colonies in the Ross Sea area , joining an international Antarctic-wide project , thanks to a $10,000 scholarship from CCC and the ChristchurchNZ 's Antarctic Office . <p> At the recent scholarship presentation , the students spoke about their visit to ' the ice ' earlier this year as part of the UC summer Antarctic programme , and their enduring fascination with the southernmost continent . <p> Twice they flew to Antarctica but adverse weather prevented the plane landing .
