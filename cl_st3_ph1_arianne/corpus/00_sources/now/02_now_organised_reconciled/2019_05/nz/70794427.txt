<h> Deer farmers disappointed by target <p> The deer sector has joined a growing chorus of farming groups -- and others -- describing the Government 's targets for methane reductions as unrealistic . <p> Deer Industry New Zealand says it is disappointed by the announced emissions reduction targets for agriculture . <p> Dr Ian Walker , chair of Deer Industry NZ ( DINZ ) , says that under current conditions these targets will only result in significant reductions in stock numbers . <p> " Even if tools and technologies were available to reduce methane and nitrous oxide in the future , the level of reduction would effectively mean that the agriculture sector was being asked not just to cease its own contribution to global warming , but also offset the contribution of other sectors . " <p> Walker says the deer industry , as part of the pastoral sector , is prepared to play its part in climate change mitigation . <p> " We do not deny human-induced climate change nor our responsibility to mitigate . The pastoral sector is willing to target net zero global warming impact from agricultural gasses.But the targets for methane announced by the Government @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ not support these targets , " he says . <p> " The rationale for methane to be reduced by between 24 and 47% by 2050 has not been made clear to us . <p> " We can only assume that the Government expects agriculture to make ' headroom ' for other sectors to continue emitting . " <p> Walker says this is unfair for the sector , but also a bad choice for NZ because in the absence of new mitigation technology a 47% reduction in agricultural methane emissions will require a 47% reduction in pastoral farming outputs . <p> " This could reduce rural employment and export revenues from meat , milk and fibre by half , or about $12 billion a year , " he says . <p> " The Government has expressed no plan for how this employment and export revenue could be replaced and NZers ' living standards maintained . " <p> It buys time but does not address the fundamental cause of climate change , which is the release of fossil carbon into the atmosphere . <p> " The deer industry alongside other pastoral industries @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and gradually reducing and stabilising emissions of methane so that its levels in the atmosphere do not increase ( i.e. no additional warming effect from livestock agriculture ) . <p> " The pastoral sector including DINZ will continue to support research on agricultural greenhouse gas mitigation options and development of a robust framework to enable farms to transition to lower emissions . But DINZ considers there is little sense in sacrificing NZ 's economic backbone . "
