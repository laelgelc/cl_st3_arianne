<h> Caritas welcomes stronger focus on vulnerable in first Wellbeing Budget <p> Caritas Aotearoa New Zealand welcomes a stronger focus on New Zealand 's most vulnerable people and indications of a long-term , integrated approach to climate change in this year 's Budget . <p> " Earlier in the year , we supported the intergenerational wellbeing approach being taken in this year 's Budget , " said Caritas Director Julianne Hickey . " It is good to see a longer-term approach being adopted for social investment . " <p> Caritas welcomes the greater focus on mental health , young people and children , housing long-term homeless people and increased support for beneficiaries . The litmus test of a society is how it treats its most vulnerable citizens . <p> However , Caritas is disappointed to see no significant increases for refugee resettlement and support and refugee family reunification in 2019/20 . While increases are projected for the years 2020-2023 , it is only an additional $69,000 per year . <p> " With the annual refugee quota rising next year , and more settlement centres planned , we need to ensure new arrivals are properly welcomed and included , " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of our nation . They add to the cultural diversity and richness of our communities . We still have some way to go to truly ' welcome the stranger ' . " <p> Caritas is seeking a commitment to expand the Community Organisation Refugee Sponsorship scheme , following the successful pilot scheme last year . " This has made good progress and is an excellent example of a community-led initiative supported by government . " said Mrs Hickey . <p> Caritas also welcomes a stronger commitment in the Budget to cut carbon emissions , encourage sustainable technology , and adapt to climate change . " But the transition to a zero- carbon economy must not come at the expense or neglect of the poor , " said Mrs Hickey . " Adaptation to climate change in Aotearoa New Zealand needs to take into account the needs of low-paid workers and people living in isolated or poorer areas .
