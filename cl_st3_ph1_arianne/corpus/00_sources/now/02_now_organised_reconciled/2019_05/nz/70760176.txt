<h> Global Report : Urgent Need for Transformative Change <p> * Paris , France , 6 May 2019 - The Intergovernmental Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services ' ( IPBES ) launched landmark Global Assessment Report providing persuasive evidence on the rapid deterioration of nature and its contributions to people across the world . The report which overlaps with the G7 environment ministers meeting in Metz , Paris , is a wakeup call to policy-makers and businesses to take decisive action stressing on an urgent need for a New Deal for Nature and Peopleby 2020 . <p> The 1,800 page scientific study is the first comprehensive snapshot of the state of the world 's biodiversity since 2005 . Evidence was provided by 400 world 's leading experts from across 50 countries . Echoing many of the findings of WWF 's Living Planet Report published in 2018 , it paints an alarming picture of species extinctions , wildlife population declines , habitat loss and depletion of ecosystem services crucial for our sustenance and economic development . <p> The IPBES report shows that human actions have significantly altered nature across the globe . Around 1 million animal and plant species are now threatened with extinction , many within decades - more than ever before in human history . <p> This is well aligned with observed trends in the Danube-Carpathian Region . Since the 19th century , over 80% of floodplains have been cut off from river flows in the Danube Basin . The situation is due to the expansion of intensive agricultural land and river engineering measures for flood control , navigation and hydropower generation ; all of which severely impact fish production and bird populations . The Danube once sustained six species of sturgeons , including the world 's largest freshwater fish the Beluga sturgeon . Today , four are critically endangered and one is extinct . In Romania , total annual catches fell from 1,144 tons in 1940 to less than 8 tons in 1995 . The current sturgeon fishing ban aims to save these animals from extinction . Among the major contributors of ecosystem changes , human-driven climate change is identified among the key drivers exacerbating the impact of other drivers on nature and human wellbeing . Greenhouse gas emissions have doubled , raising average global temperatures by at least 0.7 degrees Celsius while the global average sea level has risen by 16 to 21 centimetres since 1900 . These changes have contributed to widespread impacts in many aspects of biodiversity including species distributions . <p> " The Global Assessment Report offers irrefutable evidence of not only the unprecedented decline of nature but its risks to human lives and prosperity .
