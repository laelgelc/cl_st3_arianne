<h> More Topics <h> Weather Forecast <h> City Councilor 's View : March for Science , climate march : good reminders of local policy initiatives <p> Two recent Saturdays brought excellent demonstrations of community support behind science and preparing for the threat of climate change . I was glad to speak alongside so many other excellent organizations and individuals at both the March for Science on April 22 and the People 's Climate March on April 29 . <p> I am not a scientist , but I wanted to talk about how we need science more than ever to develop tangible policies . While we often think of climate change and environmental policy on a national level , we can bring that science-based approach when it comes to local policies as well . <p> A good example is the Duluth Steam Plant in Canal Park . If you are walking downtown you might sometimes see steam coming from the manholes . That is wasted energy . Technology has advanced since 1932 when the steam plant was built , and Duluth has the opportunity to make the system more efficient . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the state Legislature and a stalled bonding bill . I have been proud to advocate at the Capitol for its much-needed funding and for other community projects over the past two years . Our proposal for the steam plant would replace the current leaky steam system to a closed-loop hot water system where the water would be reused instead of being dumped into the lake . The conversion project also would give Duluth the opportunity to be able to burn low-carbon biomass that can be sourced locally . That is the system I want to heat our downtown and waterfront district . <p> Mayor Emily Larson announced recently that Duluth is not going to burn coal in the steam plant for the next seven months . This is part of a forward-thinking vision to reduce carbon emissions in Duluth by 80 percent by 2050 . We have a hypothesis that this hiatus is going to lower emissions , and we will be capturing these results for further action after the seven months . Mayor Larson is to be commended for her leadership . <p> Duluth must promote walkability and bikeability @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ preserving our environment but also to save our city and taxpayers money in the long-term . <p> With our city 's more than 400 miles of streets and utilities , we must strategize how to most efficiently repair and replace existing infrastructure instead of adding to our system to repair later . <p> We need to use a science-based approach to positively influence policy on a national and local level . <p> We need your voice , too .
