<p> Beginning on Friday , May 5 , the two-day event featured performances and speeches from Yakima , Tulalip , and Nimiipuu peoples to celebrate indigenous foods , culture , and ecology . <p> The symposium 's performer was 16-year-old musician , songwriter , and activist Kalilah Rampanen . Rampanen first began playing the guitar at age 8 and credited her father for inspiring her love for the instrument . Her styles include blues , acoustic , and alternative , and encompass stories of the Native struggle to preserve their land , language , and memory . <p> For Rampanen , music is a way of continuing her family 's long history of environmental and civil activism begun by her grandparents in the late 1970s . <p> On Saturday , Rampanen performed several of her songs , including " Anthem of the Oppressed , " a ballad about the discrimination of Native peoples , and " Higher and Higher , " chronicling her experience of witnessing climate change as a young person . <p> " There are times when it 's important that we fall back on these stories , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and the land will take care of you .
