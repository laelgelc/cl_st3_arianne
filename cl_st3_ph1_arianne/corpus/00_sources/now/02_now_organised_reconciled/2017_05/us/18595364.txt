<p> If you want to plant a pine tree that might survive the climate upheavals that are already remaking northern Minnesota 's boreal forest , where should it go ? <p> Scientists from the Nature Conservancy and elsewhere now think they know . This summer they 're embarking on a project to plant 400 acres with cold-loving evergreens like jack pine and tamarack in carefully selected " conifer strongholds " -- places that they predict will stay cooler or wetter or have better soil , increasing the chances that a few of each species will survive for the next generation as Minnesota grows warmer . <p> " We are trying to get us in better shape for the centuries to come , " said Meredith Cornett , a forest scientist with the Nature Conservancy in Minnesota who is heading up the project . <p> The aim is to preserve northern forest species -- not just the trees but also the mosaic of plants and animals that rely on them -- to maintain biodiversity . <p> Both will be exceedingly difficult thanks to a double whammy of the region 's past @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ pine , white cedar , and tamarack once made up two-thirds of Minnesota 's northern forest .
