<h> Browse NCJ Daily <h> Browse Classifieds <h> In Praise of Science <p> Editor : <p> The recent marches in support of science , and the changes in national policy , have renewed the contentious political debate about climate change ( NCJ Daily , April 20 ) . We need to stop arguing whether climate change is real , and start focusing on its many economic and social impacts that are now happening , and will continue in the future . <p> All the scientific studies over the past 30 years have predicted extreme changes in weather patterns , which go far beyond just record global heat , melting icecaps , and rising sea levels . Here in Humboldt County , U.S. Highway 101 south has been closed for over a week due to a massive landslide . Routes north and east have fared no better this winter . We 're coming out of years of drought with record rainfall causing the earth to " breathe " with landslides and other land movements . <p> These road closures are more than just travel inconveniences . The reduced flow of goods @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ on businesses , our lives and our overall economy . Huge repair costs affect all Californians . This is just one miniscule " climate disaster , " with more extremes predicted to come . Every community across the globe is facing and will continue to face the impacts from increasing record rainfall and flooding , record cold and snowfall , record heat and droughts , record tornados and winds , crop failures , forest destruction , recreation and tourism decline , and the cost of shoring up infrastructure . These impacts are not going away , and in the " debate , " nobody has proved otherwise . <p> I challenge the scientific community , the media , and the informed public to start connecting the dots , and vigorously begin to educate our politicians and citizenry . The costs are too high to ignore .
