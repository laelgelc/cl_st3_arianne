<p> Is this the end of the beginning for Alberta 's NDP government , or the beginning of the end ? <p> I pose the question because we are , of course , at the halfway point in the government 's mandate . <p> It was two years ago on May 5 that the untried NDP won its first-ever election victory over the geriatric Progressive Conservatives . <p> If the NDP follows Alberta 's fixed-election-date law , the next provincial election will be held sometime around May 2019 . <p> Then again , as we saw in 2015 when PC premier Jim Prentice called an early election , that law is as malleable as Silly Putty . <p> Indeed , Alberta 's opposition parties already suspect Premier Rachel Notley will call an early election to catch the Wildrose and Progressive Conservatives with their pants down , so to speak , as they try to consummate a political merger . <p> But I 'm getting ahead of myself . <p> Before we look at where the NDP government might be going , let 's look at where it has already taken @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> In the past two years the government has banned political donations from unions and corporations , ( somewhat ) revamped energy royalties , introduced an Alberta Child Benefit , and reduced school fees . <p> It has also consulted Albertans , with varying degrees of success , on a litany of issues including farm safety , climate change , energy efficiency , grizzly bear protection , the Municipal Government Act , the Employment Standards Code , the Labour Relations Code , and the Workers ' Compensation Board . <p> This is a socially conscious government that came in to office with the intent to fix stuff broken or ignored by 44 years of PC government . <p> " There was a lot of work that needed to be done , sometimes referred to as ' pent up governance , ' " Notley told journalists Thursday when asked if her government was moving too far too quickly with the pace of change .
