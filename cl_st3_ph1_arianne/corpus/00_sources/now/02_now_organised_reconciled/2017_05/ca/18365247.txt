<p> The unprecedented spring flooding in parts of Quebec , including the West Island and Vaudreuil-Soulanges region , has been a nightmare for thousands of homeowners living near cresting rivers or lakes . <p> After touring flood-ravaged Terrasse-Vaudreuil on Sunday , Prime Minister Justin Trudeau tweeted about how inspiring it is to see " friends and neighbours , civilians and soldiers coming together to help those affected by floods . " <p> The offers of help for flood victims , from filling sandbags to providing food or coffee , are reminiscent of what happened during the ice storm in January 1998 , when West Islanders came together to support and comfort each other . <p> That ice storm significantly affected West Islanders . Every community faced prolonged blackouts , with some residents fleeing their freezing homes to take comfort in shelters set up by their municipality . Much of the West Island had its power restored within a few days or a week , although for some residents in the more remote and forested Off-Island region , the blackout endured for weeks . <p> The massive floods this spring did @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ did 19 years ago . However , there is more cause for concern when it comes to potential fallout for affected homeowners . Once the waters abate in a week or two , the damage to homes could include rotting floors and walls and moist , prime conditions for the spread of mould . While homes were damaged during the ice storm , they remained habitable and in many cases required relatively minor repairs . Some of the flooded homes today might need to be gutted or demolished and rebuilt . That 's a major upheaval to endure , especially for young families or the elderly . <p> Buying a home is a major investment for families .
