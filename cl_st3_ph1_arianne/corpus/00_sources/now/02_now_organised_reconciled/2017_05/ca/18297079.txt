Yet during the summer , meltwater is everywhere . It flows in small rivulets and larger streams , past multicoloured houses built against a sloping hill and down to the Inglefield Bredning , as it is called in Danish -- a broad body of water at the confluence of several fiords . <p> Since the Inugguit people arrived in this village in 1953 , life has been focused on hunting in and around this gulf , a summer habitat for narwhals , the rare whales with long , spearlike tusks that give them a mythic quality . They voyaged many miles , up and down the coast . Even today , Qaanaaq 's residents collectively own hundreds of dogs , chained to nearly every home , it seems , and constantly howling . <p> But the age-old Inugguit lifestyle is changing fast as the climate warms , disrupting long-held patterns and possibilities and forcing economic challenges as a traditional hunting culture weighs new industries such as fishing and even tourism . <p> Qaanaaq has the trappings of modernity -- a hotel , retirement home , diesel power plant , museum , church , school -- but it is extremely remote .
