<h> U of S opens water research facility to study climate change <p> University of Saskatchewan researcher John Pomeroy can be closer to his field work at mountain glaciers thanks to the new Coldwater Lab in Canmore . ( Supplied/Photo courtesy of Stacey Dumanski , University of Saskatchewan ) ( Supplied/Photo courtesy of Stacey Dumanski , University of Saskatchewan ) / Saskatoon <p> The University of Saskatchewan is opening a research facility near the Rocky Mountains to study the effects of climate change on Canada 's waters . <p> The new Coldwater Laboratory in Canmore , Alberta will be used to study the retreating glaciers and nearby water sources in the mountains to get a better understanding of how climate change will affect nearby communities . <p> John Pomeroy , a professor of geography at the U of S and Canada Research Chair in Water Resources and Climate Change , said his team can better understand how waterways work in Saskatchewan by conducting research at the western edges of Alberta . <p> " We identified about 15 years ago that the university hydrology research needed to focus on the source @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Pomeroy said , noting Saskatchewan 's economy and the livelihood of all its residents is connected to the province 's waters . <p> With the university 's budget in flux after a reduction in funding from the province , the Coldwater Laboratory and upcoming research were funded by a grant from the federal government . <p> The Global Water Futures program , funded through the Canada First Research Excellence Fund , awarded the water research program at the U of S $77.8 million -- the largest grant ever given to the university , and the largest grant for any university water research program in the world . <p> " We have so many serious water problems and issues facing Saskatchewan , " Pomeroy said .
