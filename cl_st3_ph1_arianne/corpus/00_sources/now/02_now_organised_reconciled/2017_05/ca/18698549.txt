<p> The Canadian Press VATICAN CITY -- Prime Minister Justin Trudeau has had a private visit with Pope Francis at the Vatican . <p> It 's unclear what was said in the meeting , which lasted more than 30 minutes , but Trudeau was expected to discuss reconciliation with Indigenous peoples , religious diversity , and climate change . <p> The prime minister has given the Pope a rare set of Jesuit Relations books , which have become an important source detailing the beginnings of Canada . <p> Trudeau also presented Pope Francis with a Montagnais-French dictionary written by a French Jesuit in the 17th century . <p> In return , the Pope gave the prime minister a gold medal marking the fourth year of his pontificate , an autographed copy of his message for World Peace Day and three papal letters about family , environment and evangelism . <p> Trudeau was expected to ask the pontiff to issue a formal apology in Canada for the role of the Catholic Church in the residential school system . <p> The Truth and Reconciliation Commission included the demand for a papal apology @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ recommendations in its report on the dark history and legacy of residential schools . <p> The Liberal government has promised to act on all of them . <p> Trudeau , who is religious , is also expected to discuss the Catholic community in Canada . <p> Former prime minister Stephen Harper , who issued a residential schools apology on behalf of the Canadian government in 2008 , did not raise the issue directly during a 10-minute audience with Pope Francis two years ago . Harper did mention the report of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission . <p> In 2009 , the previous pope , Benedict XVI , did express " sorrow " on behalf of the Catholic Church for the " deplorable conduct " by some members of the church in their treatment of Indigenous children in residential schools . <p> The Truth and Reconciliation Commission report said this did not go far enough , especially since it was not made in public .
