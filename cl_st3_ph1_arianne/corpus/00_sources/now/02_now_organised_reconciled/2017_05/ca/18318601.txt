<p> Just as the provincial leadership candidates were heading into the last weekend of campaigning a few days ago , when voters should have more or less firmed-up their views on who to support Tuesday , the good people at the Angus Reid polling firm came out with a survey that I found a bit depressing . <p> Twenty-nine per cent of respondents " remained unsure who would be the best candidate for the job , " reported Ip . <p> Of those who would say which leader would be the best premier , only 28 per cent named Liberal Leader Christy Clark , who has been doing the job for six years . Even fewer -- 25 per cent -- said NDP Leader John Horgan would make the best premier , with Green party Leader Andrew Weaver only named by 18 per cent of respondents . <p> I know it 's become de rigour to adopt a cynical view of politicians , but are n't we taking things a bit too far ? <p> Setting aside one 's personal politics for a minute and possible deep-boiling animosity toward certain @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ impressive British Columbians ? <p> Clark has been involved in provincial politics as a candidate on-and-off since first being elected in 1996 , where she stood out as one of the fiercest , most-effective opposition critics . When the Liberals came to power , she held a variety of complex , high-profile cabinet positions . I 've met a lot of politicians over my career and Clark stands out as one of those rare people who can fill-up a room with the force of her optimistic personality . <p> Horgan has had a similar political life , putting himself through university , including gaining a master 's degree , before going to work in Ottawa and later in B.C. in a wide variety of non-elected political and government roles , working his way up through increasingly senior positions until he was an associate deputy minister " in the Ministry of Finance on energy projects , " according to a website run by former NDP MLA David Schreck .
