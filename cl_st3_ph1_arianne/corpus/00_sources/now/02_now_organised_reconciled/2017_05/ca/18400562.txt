<p> Rows of white tents have been erected on the Ste-Catherine St. esplanade like those that shelter desperate people who have run for their lives . Old suitcases snake up the steps to the plaza , as if dragged there by migrants hauling their hastily packed possessions . <p> This is not a real refugee camp , of course , but part of an art project called Cuisine Ta Ville by the activist collective ATSA , which translates from French to " When Art Takes Action . " <p> Visually , the lines of tents are cold , uniform and austere , meant to convey a landscape of limbo and disorientation . But what ATSA has planned for the interiors will warm the heart . <p> Inside , real refugees who have come to this city to build new lives , will make soup for small groups of Montrealers . As Roy pointed out , they are real people who will be sharing very personal and , in some cases , difficult ordeals . <p> " These are ordinary folks . They 're not actors , " said Roy . " They are working very hard preparing their testimony . They have a need to communicate what has happened in their lives so we understand what they 've been through . ... These people have resilience , humility , courage . " <p> All the elements of Cuisine Ta Ville have been carefully considered to cultivate an exchange between cultures and individuals . And soup itself is full of significance . It is common to every ethnicity . It is comfort food . It warms . It nourishes . <p> " Cooking food is a universal experience , " said Roy . " There 's love in food . It transmits culture . Human warmth comes from food . " <p> ATSA 's body of work , carried out over the past 20 years , address contemporary social issues such as globalization , climate change and homelessness . Like all their projects , Cuisine Ta Ville is designed to foster dialogue , build understanding , and push people beyond their own little bubbles by relying on physical experiences . <p> In this case , the aim is to create a convivial and intimate atmosphere between perfect strangers . Groups will be limited to 10 to 15 to keep the atmosphere intimate . There are servings almost every hour Saturday and Sunday . <p> A second event will also take place over warm bowls of soup . Strangers will be paired up , positioned in chairs face to face , and encouraged to have a spontaneous conversation on a topic to be proposed by a moderator . <p> Films and documentaries on refugee experiences will be screened in some tents and a large map that plots the history of immigration to Montreal is on display . <p> Cuisine Ta Ville is part of the arts programming for the city 's 375th birthday . It celebrates Montreal 's history as a city shaped by waves of newcomers . And it attempts to recognize that different groups and communities have contributed to creating the only French-speaking metropolis in North America . <p> But make no mistake : Cuisine Ta Ville also has a political message , said Roy .
