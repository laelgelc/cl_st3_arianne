<h> Chelsea Flower Show 2017 : pick of the new plants by Val Bourne <p> Getting a plant to the peak of perfection for Chelsea week is always a delicate balancing act , and this year 's warm winter followed by a dry spring with high day-time temperatures and extremely chilly nights , has n't made it easy for the growers . Marina Christopher of Phoenix Perennials , who has been raising various specimens for Chelsea , says , " Seed germination and plants were slow off the mark , then a few weeks ago there was a change in the weather to warm , almost balmy days and nights . What usually grows and flowers over a two-month period has done it in less than a month . " A stressful time . The liquorice plant ( Glycyrrhiza glabra ) , a member of the pea family reputedly brought to Britain by the Crusaders and once widely grown in Yorkshire , features on this garden and also appears on Dr Catherine MacDonald 's Seedlip Artisan Garden . It has been chosen by Catherine because it 's one of the plants featured in the 17th-century book The Art of Distillation . This book inspired Seedlip 's founder , Ben Branson , to get distilling and his sugar-free , non-alcoholic drinks have been a great success . Ben , whose family farms in Yorkshire and Lincolnshire , chose the name Seedlip because it 's an old name for an oval basket used for broadcasting seeds . The Seedlip garden features copper pipework , used in distilling . A stunning rust-red false indigo called Baptisia ' Cherries Jubilee ' picks up the copper metalwork . In 500 Years of Covent Garden there is a dainty white umbel with edible tubers called Bunium bulbocastanum ( great pignut ) . This caraway-flavoured plant is positioned under ancient apple trees in Lee Bestall 's recreation of Covent Garden in the days when it was the orchard garden attached to Westminster Abbey . <p> Also on Main Avenue , designers Andrew Wilson and Gavin McWilliam use " exploding umbels " to add drama to Breaking Ground , their garden inspired by the heath land around Wellington College in Berkshire . In the past 200 years , 97 per cent of Berkshire heath has been lost , making it more endangered than the world 's rainforests .
