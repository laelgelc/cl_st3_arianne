The report seemed to suggest that he wanted to prevent people seeing the film . <p> That would have been an unacceptable intrusion on freedom of expression . Section 14 of the Bill of Rights Act rightly protects the right to free speech , " including the freedom to seek , receive , and impart information and opinions of any kind in any form " . <p> The moment we start suppressing opinions , no matter how overwhelming the arguments against them may seem , we are on a slippery slope . The true test of free speech is our willingness to uphold the right of people to say things that we do n't like . <p> But then I watched a video of O'Sullivan speaking from the stage at Kaitaia and he did n't appear to be making any attempt to stop people watching the film . It 's not for O'Sullivan to decide what opinions other health professionals should hold , or be exposed to . <p> At this point , his legitimate espousal of the pro-immunisation viewpoint transmuted into an authoritarian insistence that anyone who did n't fall into line with the officially " correct " view should be punished . <p> This is bullying . Unfortunately , however , it 's typical of the ideological totalitarianism that increasingly taints public debate -- the more so since social media platforms made it easy to gang up on dissenters and intimidate them into silence . <p> We see this manifested in all sorts of ways . Climate change doubters are constantly shouted down on the spurious basis that " the science is settled " ( it 's not ) . In Australia , family-owned brewery Coopers was recently subjected to an aggressive boycott simply because it sponsors the Bible Society , which opposes gay marriage . <p> Intolerance of dissent takes a variety of forms , but the ultimate aim is always the same : to silence the dissenters .
