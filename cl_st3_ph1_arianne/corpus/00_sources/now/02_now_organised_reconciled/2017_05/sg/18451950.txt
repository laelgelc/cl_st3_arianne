<p> Transport today accounts for about 14 percent of global human-caused emissions of greenhouse gases . But even that substantial figure could be an underestimate in the longer term : After all , emissions from this sector currently are expected to double by mid-century . <p> Even as demographics , behaviour , business and technology are driving major increases in transport demand , the sector remains overwhelmingly dependent on fossil fuels , which power a staggering 98 percent of global transport options . Meanwhile , another 2 to 3 billion people are expected to live in cities by 2050 , placing further pressure on urban transport systems even while creating an explosion in urban passenger trips and freight deliveries . <p> The transport sector also is somewhat unique in how decisions made today on related infrastructure " lock in " demand for tomorrow . Thus , urban policy in the next few years will determine whether we set a course for a high- or low-carbon transport future , especially in rapidly motorizing countries .
