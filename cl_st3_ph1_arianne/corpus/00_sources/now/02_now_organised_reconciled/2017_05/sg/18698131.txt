<h> Volcanic Eruption Might Have Caused The First Mass Extinction <p> AsianScientist ( May 29 , 2017 ) - Researchers in America and Japan say they may have found the cause of the first mass extinction of life : a gigantic volcanic eruption at the end of the Ordovician . These findings have been published in Geology . <p> There have been five mass extinctions since the divergent evolution of early animals 600-450 million years ago . The cause of the third and fourth was volcanic activity , while an asteroid impact led to the fifth . But triggers of the first and second mass extinctions had , until now , been unknown . <p> The first mass extinction occurred at the end of the Ordovician . This age is between the divergence of the Ordovician and land invasion of vascular land plant and animals . Animals in the Ordovician-Silurian comprised marine animals like corals , trilobites , sea scorpion , orthoceras , brachiopods , graptolite , crinoid and jawless fish . Approximately 80 percent of species disappeared at the end of the Ordovician . <p> A team led by @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Kaiho of Tohoku University , looked into possible triggers of the first mass extinction . They took sedimentary rock samples from two places -- North America and southern China -- and analyzed the mercury ( Hg ) in them . They found Hg enrichments coinciding with the mass extinction in both areas . <p> This , they believe , is the product of large volcanic eruptions because Hg anomaly was also observed in other large igneous province volcanisms . <p> Huge volcanic eruptions can produce sulfate aerosols in the stratosphere . Sulfate aerosols are strong , light-reflecting aerosols , and cause global cooling . This rapid climate change is believed to be behind the loss of marine creatures . <p> Kaiho 's team is now studying the second mass extinction in the hopes of further understanding the cause and processes behind it .
