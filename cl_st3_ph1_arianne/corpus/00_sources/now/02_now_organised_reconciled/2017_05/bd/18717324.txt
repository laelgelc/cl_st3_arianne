<h> In the wake of Mora <h> With better preparation , we can hope to one day reduce the casualty number to zero <p> It is always a matter of great sadness when an extreme weather event like Cyclone Mora claims lives , displaces people , and causes suffering . <p> The cyclonic storm has passed through the country , resulting in the tragic deaths of six people . <p> Furthermore , dozens of others were hurt , and thousands of houses and some coastal shelters , including Rohingya camps , were destroyed . The numbers are still coming in . <p> But we have come a long way -- Cyclone Mora could have been a disaster for us . <p> But thanks to the efforts of the government , the cyclone has caused far less damage than in the past . <p> The efficient execution of the Disaster Management Program has allowed many people to take refuge and stay safe . <p> With shelters hosting upwards of 500,000 people , the relief efforts by the government thankfully kept casualty numbers to a minimum . <p> It was also a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ food and relief distribution channels , coordinate volunteer efforts , and form almost a hundred medical teams . <p> With natural disasters a common factor in the Bangladeshi seasonal cycle -- it is , after all , one of the countries most susceptible to the effects of climate change -- it is encouraging to see that numerous steps were taken , and lessons were learnt from previous years . <p> The 1970 Bhola cyclone resulted in the deaths of 500,000 people . Today , we are far better equipped to handle such events . <p> That is not to say there is no more room for improvement -- with better preparation , we can hope to one day reduce the casualty number to zero .
