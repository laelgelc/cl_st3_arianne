<h> Food security crisis deserves greater attention <h> share <p> The government 's decision to cushion millions of Kenyans from the sky-rocketing cost of staple food by offering subsidised maize flour is welcome , but we seem not to have learned from past experiences . <p> On several occasions , this column has discussed the need to take a closer look at the food security situation , noting that it directly affects the livelihoods of the majority of the population . <p> Last September , the ministry of Agriculture announced the production of maize had significantly increased following the previous long-rains season harvest . The report came after concerns had been raised that drought was affecting several parts of the country , with hunger and demand for relief food . <p> According to the ministry report , the price of a two-kilogramme packet of maize flour had dropped on average by Sh2 from Sh106 in July to Sh104 in August . <p> The statement added that the food security situation was stable and maize stocks at national silos stood at 9.6 million bags . The State must support smallholder farmers to tackle food insecurity and poverty by adopting research-based solutions to agricultural problems . <p> Kenya is experiencing the challenges posed by climate change , a rapidly growing urban population and unemployment . Agriculture can help alleviate the problems by offering food security and employment opportunities . <p> Policy-makers and implementers , agricultural experts and officers must directly engage and interact with farmers as useful partners in the agriculture value-chain . Poor smallholder farmers are confronted with multiple problems . <p> They lack good seeds and their soil is depleted . They also suffer from unreliable water supplies and access to markets . So they need to be engaged and empowered . For food security and increased agricultural productivity , smallholder farmers must embrace technology and modern farming . <p> The State must intensify the use of agricultural extension as a crucial component to transform the farmers ' fortunes .
