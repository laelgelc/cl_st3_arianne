<h> Landmark ruling in Ogieks ' land case expected tomorrow <p> The African Court on Human Rights will rule on Friday on the Ogiek community 's request to be allowed to remain in Mau Forest . <p> This is the first time the Arusha-based court will rule on a case involving indigenous peoples ' rights . It is regarded as a landmark case . <p> The group sued Kenyan authorities over what it called continued and systematic rights violations and denial of land rights by the government . <p> The Ogiek , 35,000 of whom are complainants , live in the Mau Forest complex in the Rift Valley . <p> They are among the last remaining forest-dwelling communities and the most marginalised indigenous peoples in Kenya . <p> Ogieks are hunter-gatherers who have been fighting the government for more than 10 years over invasion of the forest by illegal settlers and loggers . <p> They have accused Kenyan authorities of violating their rights to life , property , natural resources , development , religion and culture . <p> The community said this violates the African Charter on Human and Peoples @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> " This case is of fundamental importance for indigenous peoples in Africa , particularly in the context of the continent-wide conflicts between communities , " said Lucy Claridge , director of Minority Rights Group International 's legal division . <p> She added that conflicts are sparked by pressures on land and resources . <p> " Ultimately the court will rule on the crucial role of indigenous peoples in the conservation of land and natural resources . This has implications for mitigation of climate change in a region ravaged by drought and famine , " she said . <p> Daniel Kobei , executive director of the Ogiek Peoples ' Development Progra , said : " This judgment will be a huge milestone for the Ogiek community .
