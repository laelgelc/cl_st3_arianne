<h> Antarctic ice sheets stable since millions of years <p> The ice sheets of central Antarctica have been stable for millions of years when conditions were warmer than now , a new research has found . <p> Researchers from the Universities of Edinburgh and Northumbria studied rocks on slopes of the Ellsworth Mountains in Antarctica , whose peaks protrude through the ice sheet . <p> However , the scientists are concerned that ice at the coastline is vulnerable to rising temperature , though the discovery points towards the long-term stability of Antarctica 's ice sheet . <p> Scientists calculated that the mountains have been shaped by an ice sheet over a million-year period , beginning in a climate some 20 degrees warmer than at present . <p> " The preservation of old rock surfaces is testimony to the stability of at least the central parts of the Antarctic ice sheet -- but we are still very concerned over other parts of Antarctica amid climate change , " said David Sugden , professor at University of Edinburgh . <p> The last time such climates existed in the mountains of Antarctica was @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and beetles thrived , the paper published in Earth and Planetary Science Letters noted . <p> This time marked the start of a period of cooling and the growth of a large ice sheet that extended offshore around the Antarctic continent . <p> Glaciers have subsequently cut deep into the landscape , leaving a high-tide mark -- known as a trimline -- in the exposed peaks of the Ellsworth range . <p> The extended ice sheet cooled the oceans and atmosphere , helping form the world of today , researchers stated .
