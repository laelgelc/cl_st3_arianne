<h> Chennai 's ' man-made ' heatwave : City has only rapid concretisation , loss of green spaces to blame <p> Girish Visvanathan is a deceptively mild young man . He also says he dislikes sitting in air-conditioned rooms , preferring the heat outside . And truth be told , his work depends on it . <p> Visvanathan and his firm Ecologikol , a consulting firm on green buildings , have taken up a new project purely out of passion . They have been recording temperatures -- both recorded and " real feel " figures -- in various parts of Chennai . And they have come up with some eye openers . <p> On 18 May , between 12:50 pm and 01:30 pm , Visvanathan and Co. found a big difference in actual recorded temperatures and the heat experienced by people . This was one of the hottest days of the month -- the peak of " agni nakshatram " or katthiri veyyil , as Tamilians call the dog days . <p> Representational image . Temperature recorded here was also 42 degrees Celsius . But the " real heat " felt here was the same : 42 degrees . Humidity also remained at a constant 60 percent . <p> Thirteen kilometres from IIT Madras is the Old Mahabalipuram Road ( OMR ) , home to Chennai 's IT corridor . This concrete jungle , which has barely any tree cover , recorded a temperature of 43 degrees Celsius , and a real feel of a sizzling 51 degrees . <p> Vellore city 's recorded temperature on the same day was 43 degrees with real feel at 52 degrees , and Tiruvannamalai 's recorded temperature was 47 degrees , while real feel was 50 degrees . <p> So what exactly is the inference ? <p> Visvanathan says that the entire issue is linked to urbanisation and the indiscriminate concretisation of our @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ recording in IIT Madras , " he began .
