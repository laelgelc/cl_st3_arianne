<h> Logging evidence backed up <p> The UK and US-based Environmental Investigation Agency has published documents to support the results of its investigations into the massive illegal timber trade between Ratanakkiri province and Vietnam . <p> The agency reported this month that about 300,000 cubic metres of wood were harvested on an unprecedented scale from two national parks and one wildlife sanctuary between November and March . <p> It alleged that systemic bribery in Cambodia and Vietnam enabled wood logged in protected forests to be smuggled over the border . <p> The documents include satellite images of major log depots in Vietnamese military-controlled territory on the border , plus customs and tax documents for specific shipments of thousands of cubic metres of prohibited logs . <p> Documents included sales invoices , packing lists and Vietnamese customs records detailing the importation of about $300 million of wood and wood products from Cambodia since January 2016 . <p> The agency said : " The EIA is encouraged to hear that on Sunday , May 14 , Cambodia 's Environment Minister announced the launch of an intensive investigation into the timber smuggling underway @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 8 said the wood was taken from Community Protected Areas in Virachey and Ou Ya Dav national parks , as well as Lumphat Wildlife Sanctuary . <p> Over five months , the report said kickbacks received by both Cambodian and Vietnamese officials in what they termed " organised crime " were estimated to top $13 million . <p> " This is the single largest log-smuggling operation that we have seen for years . Vietnam must address this weak approach to any agreement with the EU to combat illegal logging and the associated trade , " EIA senior forests campaigner Jago Wadley said . <p> On May 11 , Vietnam and the EU concluded six years of negotiations for a voluntary partnership agreement on forest law enforcement , governance and trade . <p> The agreement aims to ensure that all timber and timber products destined for the EU from a partner country comply with the law . <p> The deal was expected to improve forest management , deter illegal logging and boost trade of legal timber products with the EU and other markets . <p> Vietnam has been making strenuous efforts @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ on domestic logging . <p> In 1990 , the country only had nine million hectares of forest cover , but by 2015 , that had increased by 14.7 million hectares amounting to 44.6 percent of total land areas . <p> Ouch Leng , director of the Cambodian Human Rights Task Force , said the EU is eyeing one billion dollars of timber business with Vietnam , despite proof that Vietnam logged , transported and imported timber from Cambodia illegally . <p> " The EU is ignoring the evidence of illegal logging by Vietnam and ignoring the commitment to conserving the forest , protecting the environment and preventing climate change , " he said . " Money is more important for the EU than forests . " <p> The Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry Administration could not be reached for comment .
