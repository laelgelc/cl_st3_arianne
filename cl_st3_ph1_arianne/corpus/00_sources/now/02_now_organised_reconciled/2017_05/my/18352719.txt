<h> Embassy ducks forestry dispute <p> The South Korean Embassy yesterday declined to get involved in a dispute between a group of residents and a company which wants to clear forest near their homes . <p> Activists from Prey Lang Community Network went to the embassy at Koh Pich to deliver a petition protesting the action by the Korean company Think Biotech . <p> However the 14 protesters , who claim the clearance would damage their farms and community forest at Koh Pich in Phnom Penh 's Chamkarmon district , emerged just five minutes later to say the embassy had refused to receive their petition . <p> Prey Lang spokesman Hul Vet said , " They would not receive our petition , nor would they tell us why . They just told us to try to find a resolution and said they would speak with the company . " <p> Mr Vet said his group had lost faith in the embassy because they had long made known their concern over the deforestation and it continued nonetheless . <p> The residents claim the company is not only clearing the land but @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ rice crops and " spirit of the land " . <p> Another Prey Lang activist , Ya Smak , said they were " very concerned about this company 's activities " and added : " We want the government to help to solve the problem for our community . " <p> Environment Ministry spokesman Sao Sopheap said he did not know about the case and referred questions to Chea Sam Ang , director of natural protection at the ministry . He also declined to comment . <p> The protesters , however , said the Environment Ministry must know about the case because it had already accepted their petition and pledged to work hard to resolve the dispute . <p> A 2009 agreement between South Korea 's Forest Service and the Forestry Administration of Cambodia allowed for Think Biotech to develop new plantations to mitigate the effects of climate change .
