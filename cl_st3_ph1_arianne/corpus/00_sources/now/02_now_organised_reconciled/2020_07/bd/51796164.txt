<h> OP-ED : The untold stories of Bhola slum <p> It was a dizzy winter morning when I reached the Bhola slum . The area was very congested with nearly 500 families living inside . There is no space between the houses and they hardly ever see sunlight and feel the wind . <p> Bhola is an island located on the southern coast of Bangladesh , and the area is very prone to the effects of riverbank erosion and frequent cyclones . <p> Many people migrated to Dhaka for climate-induced factors but some moved due to social reasons , for better jobs and brighter futures . A few people moved to get a better education for their children . <p> A 50-year-old woman shared her story of coming to a new life . She started from the very beginning : <p> " I was a newly married bride . It was about 30 years ago . Life went so well . But , suddenly , everything changed with the blink of an eye . We witnessed how the river swallowed our little paradise from us . That was a nightmare . With empty hands , my husband and I @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . We had no idea where to go , whom to go to . <p> " After a lot of struggle , we managed a room at Gabtoli slum . Fortunately , he got a job at a shop . After five years , we moved to Bhola slum . He started a small business and I used to help him . We started our life with new hope . <p> " We forgot all our sorrows soon when our daughter came to the world . She was like a blessing to us . We were very happy with what we had . <p> " But the happiness did not last for long . Suddenly , my husband became very sick and left us . We were so alone in this vast world . Even then , I tried to manage his business and educated my daughter . Now she is living with her husband and I am a lonely woman . This is how life is ; it 's never easy . " <p> An old man drew my attention . He was sitting at the end corner of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a story from 20 years back : <p> " I hate myself for making this decision to come to Dhaka . I just hate myself for this decision . There is nothing I like about this city and this does n't make any difference . If we get to manage food then we eat . Otherwise , we do n't . This is how life is here . <p> " Days and nights pass and I pray for death . But I have a desire to go back to my village and die there . A month ago , I had a dream regarding my village and was thinking of visiting my village one last time . I am dying to go there but it 's not possible . " <p> These are just two examples but , here in the slums , there are thousands of untold stories of the people who lost their belongings due to climate change and were forced to migrate to Dhaka for a better life . But do they really get a better life ? <p> Their lives are not easy and simple . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ always have the fear of eviction and losing their new homes . <p> Mosquitoes are another huge problem . Heavy rain and high temperature affect their everyday lives . Waterlogging has always been a problem here -- last year , the water rose up by five feet . <p> They are struggling everyday with inadequate food and clean water . The medical treatment and education facilities are very expensive in Dhaka -- nearly impossible to afford for them . <p> Some local and international NGOs work closely with this community . UNDP provided grants and training on small businesses to the local people . <p> They have conducted some surveys here and , according to the data , they found the most vulnerable people and granted them financial aid . They are going to launch a new project on residence here and help people rebuild their damaged houses . <p> PDAP was the very first organization that started working at Bhola slum . They work for children and teenage girls . They provide training on tailoring and crafting . They also help the people explore their leadership roles . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ institutes . Through this commission , successful people work as motivators to others . <p> World Vision particularly works for children . They are working on child nutrition , children 's education facilities , and providing accessories for newborn children . They also provide gifts to children during festivals . <p> According to the slum people , this is not enough and everyone does n't have access to this aid . They are looking forward to support from the government and other organizations . <p> While I was wrapping up my journey and was about to leave , I heard a woman whisper : " The life of the poor is itself a problem . Talking about these problems is just a waste of time . Our problems and sufferings are endless . " <p> Afsana Alam is a student of Mass Communication and Journalism at University of Dhaka .
