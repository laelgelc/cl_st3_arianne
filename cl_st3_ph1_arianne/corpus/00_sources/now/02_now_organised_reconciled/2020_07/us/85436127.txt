Energy funds have become a tougher sell , with some institutional investors turning toward cleaner power . <p> Energy-focused private-equity firms are finding it increasingly difficult to raise capital in the face of depressed oil prices and growing pressure on investors to stop investing in fossil-fuels . <p> Private-equity firms closed 20 energy-focused funds world-wide in the year to June 30 , down 39% from the 33 such vehicles they wrapped up in the same period last year , and the poorest first-half showing by such funds since at least 2015 , according to data provider Preqin Ltd . <p> The $20.5 billion total in capital that energy-focused firms raised during this year 's first six months was slightly higher than the $19.9 billion collected in the year-ago period , but 35% less than the $31.5 billion haul in the first half of 2018 , Preqin 's data showed . <p> A number of private equity-backed energy firms struggled to raise capital after oil prices began falling in mid-2014 . The performance disparity was even wider in 2019 alone : 1.1% for energy-focused funds and 20.5% for private-equity buyout funds . <p> " In aggregate , this is a sector that has not delivered compelling risk-adjusted returns for institutions for a long time , " said Brent Burnett , co-head of real assets at alternative-investments manager Hamilton Lane Inc . Poor performance is one of the main reasons investors are shunning energy-focused funds , he added . <p> Besides paltry performance , a rising number of institutional investors have banned conventional energy-focused funds and some are even purging their portfolios of any investment linked to fossil fuels . University endowments , in particular , are under increased pressure from students and activists concerned about climate change , The Wall Street Journal has reported .
