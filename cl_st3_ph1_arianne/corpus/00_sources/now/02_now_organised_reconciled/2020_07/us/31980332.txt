Methane seeps have been found throughout the world 's oceans , but the one discovered in the Ross Sea was the first active seep found in Antarctica , said Andrew Thurber , a marine ecologist at Oregon State University . <p> " Methane is the second-most effective gas at warming our atmosphere and the Antarctic has vast reservoirs that are likely to open up as ice sheets retreat due to climate change , " Thurber said . Most methane in the ocean water and sediment is kept out of the atmosphere by microbes that consume it . <p> Thurber and his colleagues discovered that the microbes around the Antarctic seep are fundamentally different that those found elsewhere in the world 's oceans .
