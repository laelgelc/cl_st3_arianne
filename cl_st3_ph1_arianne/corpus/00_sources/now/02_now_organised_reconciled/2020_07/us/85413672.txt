<h> Methane emissions rise nine percent in decade <p> Methane ( CH4 ) has a warming potential 28 times higher than CO2 over a 100-year period ( AFP Photo/Pablo PORCIUNCULA BRUNE ) <p> Paris ( AFP ) - Emissions of methane -- a planet-warming gas several times more potent than carbon dioxide -- have risen by nine percent in a decade driven by mankind 's insatiable hunger for energy and food , a major international study concluded Wednesday . <p> Methane ( CH4 ) has a warming potential 28 times greater than CO2 over a 100-year period and its concentration in the atmosphere has more than doubled since the Industrial Revolution . <p> Over a 20-year period , it is more than 80 times as potent . <p> While there are a number of natural methane sources such as wetlands and lakes , the team behind the study concluded that 60 percent of CH4 emissions are now manmade . <p> These sources fall principally into three categories : extracting and burning fossil fuels for power , agriculture including livestock , and waste management . <p> While emissions are expected to fall somewhat this year due to the pandemic , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ parts per billion each year . <p> This trajectory is in line with a scenario modelled by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) that sees Earth warming as much as 3-4C by 2100 . <p> " Regular updates of the global methane budget are necessary ... because reducing methane emissions would have a rapid positive effect on climate , " said Marielle Saunois , a researcher at France 's Laboratoire des Sciences du Climat et de l'Environment and lead on the study . <p> " To meet the objectives of the Paris Agreement , not only do CO2 emissions need to be reduced but also methane emissions . " <p> The Global Carbon Project , a consortium of more than 50 research institutions around the world , has gathered data from more than 100 observation stations . <p> The world now produces around 50 million additional tonnes of methane every year than it did between 2000-2006 . <p> Around 60 percent of manmade CH4 emissions were estimated to come from agriculture and waste , including as much as 30 percent from the digestive processes of cattle and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ burning of oil and gas , while 11 percent leaks from the world 's coal mines , the study found . <p> But recent studies based on new techniques for spotting methane leaks using satellite data suggest that emissions from the oil and gas sector may be significantly higher than those shown in the study , which only included data through 2017 . <p> - Short-term threat - <p> While the overall trend is upwards , emissions levels fluctuate between regions . <p> For instance , Africa , China and Asia each produce 10-15 million tonnes annually .
