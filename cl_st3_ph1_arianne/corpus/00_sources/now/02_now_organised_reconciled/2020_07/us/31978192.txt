" data-portal-copyright= " Jean Levac " **28;14635;TOOLONG " 1 " data-license-id= " 409572 " /&gt; &lt;p&gt; The price of oil has fallen by almost half in the last six months . This affects both our transport choices as well as investments in oil extraction , so it is difficult to pin down exactly what the effect is on greenhouse gas emissions . World leaders met in Lima this week to negotiate an agreement on climate change ; it is important to consider what effect the major slump in world oil prices over the last six months has on the environment . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Recent history provides some clues . The long surge in the &lt;a href= " https : **32;14665;TOOLONG ... target= " blank " &gt;&lt;span style= " **25;14699;TOOLONG ; " &gt;price of **28;14726;TOOLONG oil from around $20 per barrel at the beginning of the millennium to a high of almost $150 per barrel in 2008 brought about fundamental changes in energy markets . Car companies made significant investments in alternative drive trains , such that fuel-sipping hybrid vehicles now make up a small but growing share of vehicles , and such that the fuel efficiency of the entire new vehicle stock is poised to reach double today&amp;rsquo ; s level within a decade . Last year , Tesla&amp;rsquo ; s elegant new sedan was the first ever electric vehicle to be universally praised as the &lt;a href= " http : **34;14756;TOOLONG ... target= " blank " &gt;&lt;span style= " **25;14792;TOOLONG ; " &gt;car of the **27;14819;TOOLONG , and a wave of similar vehicles from other companies is likely to carve out an important niche in the car market of tomorrow . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; On the supply side , the high price of oil during the last decade has led to a frantic search for alternative sources . This has fuelled expansion in the Canadian oil sands , which are among the highest cost sources of oil worldwide , requiring relatively high prices to remain profitable . It has also provided the impetus for the technology investments underlying the shale boom currently underway in the United States , which has transformed world energy markets . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; The price of oil affects not just technology , but also behaviour . After rising every year since 1960 ( when the U.S. Bureau of Transport started collecting data ) , the distance driven by U.S. passengers has&amp;nbsp ; &lt;a href= " https : **31;14848;TOOLONG ... target= " blank " &gt;&lt;span style= " **25;14881;TOOLONG ; " &gt;fallen every **27;14908;TOOLONG since about 2008 , driven in part by high fuel prices . And high fuel prices have even motivated people to move out of car-centric suburbs and into city centres . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Clearly , these major changes in technology and behaviour affect not only our economy , but also the environment . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Similarly , when the world price of oil drops as it has over the past months , there are a number of impacts for a country like Canada , each of which can have implications for greenhouse gas emissions . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; The obvious effect of a big swing in oil prices is its effect on gasoline prices , and on our resulting demand . Crude oil accounts for about half of the price of gasoline at the pump , and a portion of the tax on gasoline is dependent on its price , so a 40 per cent drop in crude oil prices &amp;mdash ; as occurred over the last few months &amp;mdash ; results in roughly a 25 per cent drop in the retail price of gasoline . This has predictable impacts on consumer demand : when prices drop we buy larger and less fuel efficient vehicles , and we drive them more . &amp;nbsp ; As a result , our consumption of gasoline increases . &amp;nbsp ; Estimates from economists suggest that a 25 percent drop in gasoline price likely increases gasoline consumption by around 2 to 5 per cent immediately , and by 10 to 20 percent over the longer term . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Aside from this obvious effect , there are a number of other more subtle effects . For an oil-exporting country like Canada , a big drop in oil prices results in a significant weakening of purchasing power . Lower income means less consumption , and less emissions . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Lower oil prices also discourage new exploration and investment in oil and gas projects , especially in high cost regions like Canada&amp;rsquo ; s oil sands . Since oil sands represent Canada&amp;rsquo ; s &lt;a href= " http : **34;14937;TOOLONG ... target= " blank " &gt;fastest-growing source of emissions&lt;/a&gt; , reducing expansion in the oil sands has obvious implications for emissions . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; Finally , just as high oil prices over the past decade have fuelled a search for alternative technologies that can displace oil , a sustained period of low oil prices will dampen investment in alternative technologies . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; The net impact of these and other more subtle effects is not immediately clear . However , Environment Canada&amp;rsquo ; s &lt;a href= " http : **34;14973;TOOLONG ... target= " blank " &gt;recent report&lt;/a&gt; on Canada&amp;rsquo ; s emissions provides an estimate of the overall effect of sustained changes in world energy prices on Canadian emissions . They estimate that a sustained fall in oil price from $102 to $70 per barrel ( along with a similar fall in natural gas prices ) is likely to have a negligible overall effect on Canada&amp;rsquo ; s emissions by 2020 , changing overall emissions by much less than one per cent . In their model , increased emissions from driving are cancelled out by reduced emissions from oil and gas operations . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; As a result , even if oil prices remain at today&amp;rsquo ; s levels for a prolonged period , Canada is unlikely to meet its international commitments to reduce greenhouse gases . &lt;/p&gt; &lt;p&gt; There is , however , one important avenue through which oil prices can affect the environment that is perhaps more important . Low oil prices may make it more politically feasible to implement the type of carbon pricing reforms that are considered necessary for significantly reducing emissions . A modest carbon tax , as implemented in British Columbia , or cap and trade system , as in Quebec , would increase the price of gasoline , but by an amount much smaller than that caused by the recent drop in oil prices . Consumers may more readily accept such a policy at a time when gasoline prices are low than when they are already high .
