<h> Cities are trying to reduce carbon emissions at office buildings , but that goal is now colliding with efforts to fight the coronavirus pandemic <h> KEY TAKEAWAYS <h> 1 . Studies show that Covid-19 is airborne and can linger in indoor spaces , putting office workers at risk . <p> Landlords and property managers are pumping more outside air into their buildings , a procedure thought to reduce the likelihood of workers catching an airborne virus . Because outside air typically has to be heated in the winter or cooled in the summer , it uses up more energy than merely recirculating indoor air . <h> 2 . The practice leads to an uptick in emissions that can contribute to climate change . <p> Following the most extreme air-safety measures would increase the carbon emissions for a typical office building by about 50% , says Brenden Millstein , chief executive of Carbon Lighthouse , which helps property owners cut emissions . Making real estate more energy-efficient is considered crucial to slowing down global warming .
