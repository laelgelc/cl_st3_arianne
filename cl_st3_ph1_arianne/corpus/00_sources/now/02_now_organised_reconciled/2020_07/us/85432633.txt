<h> Siberia heat ' almost impossible ' without climate change <p> by Patrick Galey <p> The study found that the prolonged heat this year across Siberia would happen less than once every 80,000 years without human-induced climate change <p> A heatwave in Siberia that saw temperature records tumble as the region sweltered in 38-degree Celsius highs was " almost impossible " without the influence of manmade climate change , leading scientists said . <p> An international team of researchers found that the record-breaking warm period was more than 2C hotter than it would have been if humans had not warmed the planet through decades of greenhouse gas emissions . <p> The five hottest years in history have occurred in the last five years and there 's a better-than-even chance that 2020 will be the hottest ever recorded . <p> Earth 's poles are warming faster than the rest of the planet , and temperatures in Siberia -- home to much of the world 's carbon-rich permafrost -- were more than 5C hotter than average between January and June . <p> One town , Verkhoyansk , recorded a temperature of 38C ( 100.4 Fahrenheit ) -- smashing previous records . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Britain 's Met Office " he said . <p> The impact of climate change on extreme weather events such as super storms and droughts is now well-established , but until fairly recently scientists have been unable to definitively link an individual event to global warming . <p> As part of a growing area of climate research known as attribution science , the team ran computer simulations of temperatures with the climate as it is today -- around 1C hotter than the pre-Industrial era baseline . <p> They then compared this to a model generating temperatures over Siberia this year without human influence -- that is , without the additional manmade 1C . <p> They found that the prolonged heat would happen less than once every 80,000 years without human induced climate change . <p> This makes the heatwave " almost impossible atmosphere . At the same time , the wildfires and sustained heatwaves accelerated the region 's permafrost melt . <p> This caused an oil tank built on frozen soil to collapse in May , leading to one of the region 's worst-ever oil spills . <p> Russia 's aerial forest protection service , the agency that specialises in forest fires , said Wednesday that more than 5,000 people are working on 197 fires across Russia in an area of over 40,000 hectares , including eight in nature reserves . <p> " Such a prolonged heat wave is important , not only for its influence on people , but also from a scientific point of view , " said Olga Zolina , from Russia 's P.P.
