<h> Social media inspired models show winter warming hits fish stocks <p> Mathematical modeling inspired by social media is identifying the significant impacts of warming seas on the world 's fisheries . Credit : The University of Queensland <p> Mathematical modeling inspired by social media is identifying the significant impacts of warming seas on the world 's fisheries . <p> University of Queensland School of Veterinary Science researcher Dr. Nicholas Clark and colleagues from the University of Otago and James Cook University have assembled a holistic picture of climate change 's impacts on fish stocks in the Mediterranean Sea . <p> " Usually , when modeling ecosystems to understand how nature is changing , we build models that only focus on the effects of the environment , " Dr. Clark said . <p> " But it 's just not accurate enough . <p> " Newer models -- commonly used in social media to document people 's social interactions -- offer an exciting way to address this gap in scientific knowledge . <p> " These innovative network models give us a more accurate picture of reality by incorporating biology , allowing us to ask how one species responds to both @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ including humans . " <p> The team used this technique to analyze fish populations in the Mediterranean Sea , a fisheries-based biodiversity hotspot with its future under threat from rapidly warming seas . <p> " Experts from fisheries , ecology and the geographical sciences have compiled decades of research to describe the geographical ranges for more than 600 Mediterranean fish species , " Dr. Clark said . <p> " We put this information , along with data from the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change 's sophisticated climate models into our network model . <p> " We found that warming seas -- particularly in winter -- have widespread effects on fish biodiversity . " <p> The University of Otago 's Associate Professor Ceridwen Fraser said winter warming was often overlooked when people thought about the impacts of climate change . <p> " A great deal of research and media attention has been on the impacts of extreme summer temperatures on people and nature , but winters are getting warmer too , " Dr. Fraser said . <p> " Interestingly , coastal water temperatures are expected to increase at a faster rate @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ winter warming might not reach the extreme high temperatures of summer heatwaves , this research shows that warmer winters could also lead to ecosystem disruption , in some cases more than hotter summer warming will . <p> " Our results suggest that winter warming will cause fish species to hang out together in different ways , and some species will disappear from some areas entirely . " <p> The researchers hope the study will emphasize the need to understand and address climate change . <p> " If fish communities are more strongly regulated by winter temperatures as our model suggests , this means that fish diversity may change more quickly than we previously thought , " Dr. Clark said . <p> " Catches for many bottom-dwelling and open-ocean fishery species in the Mediterranean Sea have been steadily declining , so any changes to fish communities may have widespread economic impacts . <p> " For the sake of marine ecosystems and the people whose livelihoods depend on them , we need to gain a better understanding of how ocean warming will influence both species and economies .
