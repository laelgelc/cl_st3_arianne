<p> California 's air regulators have boldly signaled to truck manufacturers that it 's time to prioritize our health and lungs over the status quo . <p> Day after day , diesel trucks thunder through our neighborhoods -- including near the Port of San Diego -- releasing soot that make residents more likely to suffer from asthma attacks , lung cancer and premature death . This pollution could be stopped by transitioning to electric trucks . <p> During its June board hearing , the California Air Resources Board ( CARB ) took a critical step toward that transition by approving the Advanced Clean Trucks rule . <p> This rule will help improve air quality and accelerate the supply of electric trucks that the state desperately needs . <p> The rule does this by establishing targets requiring manufacturers to produce electric trucks -- from large pickup trucks to delivery trucks and semi-trucks -- starting in 2024 . The rule calls for an estimated 100,000 electric trucks by 2030 ; and an estimated 300,000 electric trucks in 2035 . There are currently 1.5 million conventional trucks operating in the state , so this is just a drop in the bucket . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> Some California cities and delivery companies have already started transitioning to electric trucks , but widespread adoption will only occur if manufacturers are required to produce electric trucks . Opponents of electric trucks , including some manufacturers and fossil fuel producers , lobbied the state to stall the rule and then to include benefits for methane gas trucks . They fought to keep the status quo , which meant continuing air pollution . <p> Thankfully , CARB members were n't swayed and kept the rule focused on zero-emission trucks . <p> Before the coronavirus pandemic , California projected a rise in trucks on the road from an economy oriented toward moving and delivering freight . Now , while teleworking has meant fewer passenger cars on our streets and freeways , the surge in e-commerce due to stay-at-home orders has meant an even greater dependence on polluting trucks . <p> The clean trucks rule is a vital public health solution and will save the state approximately $9 billion in health costs . These residents have borne the brunt of deadly diesel pollution and particulate matter for far too long . <p> The transportation sector is the largest contributor to California 's planet-warming emissions , and , according to climate scientists , we have less than 10 years left to dramatically reduce transportation emissions to avoid the worst effects of climate change . The climate emergency calls us to urgently slash pollution from vehicles . This is the right moment to shift from toxic diesel trucks to electric trucks . <p> Some companies now see electric trucks as a smart investment . More than 75% of heavy-duty trucks travel about 120 miles per day . This distance is well within the range that current electric trucks can travel on a single charge . Electric trucks are more efficient than their diesel or methane gas counterparts . <p> Researchers at the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and UCLA Luskin Center for Innovation found that electric trucks have equal or lower total cost of ownership , including the cost to purchase , maintain and operate .
