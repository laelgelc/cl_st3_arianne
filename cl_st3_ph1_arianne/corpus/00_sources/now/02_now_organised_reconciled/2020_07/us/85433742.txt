<h> Historic San Francisco building to be lifted 10 feet due to rising sea levels <p> A historic San Francisco waterfront building where ships were built during World War II is getting a heavy lift due to rising sea levels . <p> The 2,000-ton building ( that 's 300 elephants if you like to count in elephants ) that covers an area larger than a football field will be raised 10 feet in a gargantuan effort to stave off rising waters due to climate change . <p> The developing Mission Bay neighborhood , which sits right on the Bay in the southeast corner of San Francisco , needs protection against rising waters , that are expected to increase between 10 and 40 inches by the end of the century . <p> The feat , being undertaken by developer Brookfield Properties , is part of the wider $3.5 billion redevelopment of San Francisco 's historic Pier 70 shipyard into retail , offices and housing . <p> How do you raise a giant building ? It takes a lot of planning . Over the last nine months , 136 hydraulic jacks have been installed under the building , along with 68 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to anchor footings to the bedrock and steel beams have been placed between the original columns to stabilize the building . <p> The lift itself , due to take place this month , will take around two weeks . Each hydraulic jack will lift the structure 5.5 inches over two hours , with no more than a half-inch height difference among segments as the building slowly makes its ascent . After each lift , wood is stacked underneath the building and then replaced by steel . A computer will monitor the lift to ensure minimal discrepancies between the jacks . <p> " These jacks are all part of the symphony , the computer is the conductor , " Mike Tzortzis , vice president at Plant Construction , told the SF Chronicle . <p> Built in 1941 by the Navy for America 's wartime shipbuilding effort , Building 12 was originally used for the fabrication of steel plates for warships ' hulls . The redeveloped and raised site is due to open to the public in 2022 and host food producers and other retail outlets .
