<h> Children need green pandemic recovery , celebrity parents tell UK PM <p> LONDON , July 26 ( Thomson Reuters Foundation ) - More than 100 high-profile parents from tech entrepreneur Martha Lane-Fox to fashion designer Vivienne Westwood urged Britain 's government on Sunday to ensure economic recovery from the pandemic tackles climate change and puts children at its centre . <p> The 115 business leaders , musicians , scientists , actors and campaigners used an open letter to urge Britain 's Prime Minister Boris Johnson not to " build our way out of one disaster by super-charging the next " . <p> Actress Julie Walters , who was among the signatories , said governments should ramp up their efforts to tackle climate change , in the same way they have responded to protect people from COVID-19 . <p> " For the sake of our children , we need governments to put the same energy and investment into preventing an even greater climate catastrophe from unfolding across the globe , " she said . <p> If planet-warming emissions rebound to pre-pandemic levels , it would have " catastrophic consequences for children 's lives and livelihoods " , the letter @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ communities being hit hardest . <p> Young people have been most likely to lose their jobs or see their income drop during the virus lockdown , it noted . <p> " Investing in a low-carbon recovery makes sense whether you are worried about the economy or the environment . The two are inextricably linked , " said the former chief executive of Unilever Paul Polman , who signed the letter . <p> Rosamund Kissi-Debrah , who has campaigned to have the 2013 death of her nine-year-old daughter from asthma officially linked to illegal levels of air pollution , said she hoped that as a father with a baby , Johnson would understand the impact . <p> " This is not an elitist conversation , air pollution affects the poorest and vulnerable the most , and we need to continue the fight , " she told the Thomson Reuters Foundation . <p> " Every time the prime minister goes ' build , build , build ' , I worry there 's nothing green about the initiatives , " said Kissi-Debrah , who is the World Health Organization 's advocate for health @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the prime minister 's residence in Downing Street on Sunday to coincide with the letter , coordinated by climate activist groups Mothers Rise Up and Parents for Future UK . <p> The parents , standing a metre apart , will carry hand-held wind turbines to symbolise the need for low-carbon investment . <p> The British government has a binding target to cut its greenhouse gas emissions to net-zero by 2050 . <p> In June , Britain 's independent Committee on Climate Change said the country would have to move faster to achieve that goal . <p> Its chairman John Gummer emphasised the need to take advantage of a " once-in-a-lifetime opportunity " presented by the pandemic to shift the economy onto a greener path .
