<h> New study sparks fresh call for seagrass preservation <p> Known as ' Blue Carbon ' , seagrass meadows have been estimated to store CO2 in their soils about 30 times faster than most terrestrial forests . Credit : Centre for Marine Ecosystems Research at Edith Cowan University <p> An increase in carbon dioxide emissions equivalent to 5 million cars a year has been caused by the loss of seagrass meadows around the Australian coastline since the 1950s . <p> The stark finding was made possible by new modelling done by marine scientists at the Centre for Marine Ecosystems Research at Edith Cowan University ( ECU ) in Western Australia . <p> Ph.D . student Cristian Salinas calculated that around 161,150 hectares of seagrass have been lost from Australian coasts since the 1950s , resulting in a 2 per cent increase in annual carbon dioxide emissions from land-use change . <p> The figures derive from Mr Salinas 's research into the current carbon stocks of Cockburn Sound off the coast of Western Australia . <p> Cockburn Sound lost around 23 sqkm of seagrass between the 1960s and 1990s due to nutrient overflow caused by urban , port and industrial @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ because seagrass meadows play such a vital role in mitigating the impacts of climate change . <p> " Known as ' Blue Carbon ' , seagrass meadows have been estimated to store CO2 in their soils about 30 times faster than most terrestrial forests , " he said . <p> " Seagrass meadows have been under constant threat in Australia through coastal development and nutrient run off since the 1960s . On top of that climate change is causing marine heatwaves that are catastrophic to the seagrasses . <p> " This study serves as a stark reminder of how important these environments are . " <p> Mr Salinas said the study provided a clear baseline for carbon emissions from seagrass losses in Australia and warned of the need to preserve and restore the meadows . The inclusion of seagrass into the Australian Emission Reduction Fund could contribute to achieve this goal , he said . <p> Carbon flushed away <p> The ECU researchers assessed how environmental factors such as water depth , hydrodynamic energy , soil accumulation rates and soil grain size related to changes in soil carbon storage following @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ loss of seagrass alone was not enough to cause the carbon loss from the soil -- hydrodynamic energy from waves , tides and currents also played a significant role . <p> " Without seagrass acting as a buffer , the hydrodynamic energy from the ocean releases the carbon by moving the seabed sand around , " Mr Salinas Zapata said . <p> Researchers found hydrodynamic energy from water movement was much higher in the shallow water and associated low levels of carbon were recorded in these bare areas . <p> However , seagrass meadows established in shallow waters were found to have significantly more carbon stored compared to those growing in deeper areas . <p> " This means that nearshore meadows are particularly important to preserve , " Mr Salinas said . <p> This document is subject to copyright .
