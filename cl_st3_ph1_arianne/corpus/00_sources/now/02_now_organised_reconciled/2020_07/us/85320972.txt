Dahlke via AP ) Associated Press <p> Global warming looks like it will be a bigger problem for the world 's fish species than scientists first thought : A new study shows that when fish are spawning or are embryos they are more vulnerable to hotter water . <p> With medium-level human-caused climate change expected by the end of the century , the world 's oceans , rivers and lakes will be too hot for about 40% of the world 's fish species in the spawning or embryonic life stages , according to a study in Thursday 's . That means they could go extinct or be forced to change how and where they live and reproduce . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ For adult fish , around 2% to 3% of the species would be in the too-hot zone in the year 2100 with similar projected warming . So using this new approach reveals a previously unknown problem for the future of fish , scientists said . <p> In a worst-case climate change scenario , which some scientists said is increasingly unlikely , the figure for species in trouble jumps to 60% . <p> These vulnerable times in the life of a fish make this a " bottleneck " in the future health of species , said study co-author Hans-Otto Portner , a marine biologist at the Alfred Wegener Institute in Germany . <p> A marine heatwave last year , known as a blob , caused large numbers of migrating throughout Alaska 's rivers .
