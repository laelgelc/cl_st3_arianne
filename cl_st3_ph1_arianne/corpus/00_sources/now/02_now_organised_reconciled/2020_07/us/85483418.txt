<p> GAKUNGA , Kenya ( Thomson Reuters Foundation ) - When Lucyline Wanja Silas installed a 12-volt solar power unit at her home to help her children study at night , little did she know it would become essential to her and her neighbours in Gakunga village , central Kenya , during the coronavirus pandemic . <p> Wanja , a 48-year-old farm worker , said she had not made any money since the country 's lockdown started in March , but the solar photovoltaic ( PV ) unit she purchased in January means she no longer needs to buy kerosene for lamplight . <p> And she can also help others in her area who are without electricity , either because of faults on power lines around the country due to heavy rains since March or because they could not pay their bills after losing their jobs during the pandemic . <p> " My neighbours who are experiencing blackouts now come to me so that I can charge their phones for them for a fee , " she said , adding that they pay 20 to 50 Kenyan shillings ( $0.19-0.46 ) . <p> " There is no money out @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ using to buy kerosene if I had not installed this solar unit .
