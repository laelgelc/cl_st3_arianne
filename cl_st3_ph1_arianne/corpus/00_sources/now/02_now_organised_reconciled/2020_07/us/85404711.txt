<h> New models detail how major rivers will respond to changing environmental conditions <p> From the Nile to the Mississippi and from the Amazon to the Yangzi , human civilization is inextricably linked to the great rivers along which our societies developed . But rivers are mutable , and the benefits they bestow can quickly become disasters when these waterways change course . <p> Scientists are working to understand how environmental changes alter river dynamics . A new study in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences coauthored by UC Santa Barbara geomorphologist Vamsi Ganti has outlined the factors that dictate how often rivers jump course , or avulse , and the effects this will have on river deltas . The results promise to help scientists and planners prepare for a future of sea-level rise and changing land use . <p> Deltas counteract sea level rise by building up sediment , which mostly occurs near a river channel itself . Every once in a while , the river will switch course through an avulsion and begin building up the delta somewhere else .
