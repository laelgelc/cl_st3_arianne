Any carbon sequestration involving soils is a formidable challenge to incentivize , predict and monitor9 , because the sequestration technologies must be used on vast areas of land that are operated by hundreds of millions of farmers . Inevitably , there will be individual cases in which positive-yield projections are not met or crop yields even decrease , where incentives fail to persuade farmers , or where supply chains break down . But scientists should not be deterred from evaluating such technologies , and should instead accept that farmers need to be in the driving seat in adapting soil management to meet their specific site and crop-production goals .
