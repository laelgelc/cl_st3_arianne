<h> A Conversation About Being Black in the Newsroom <h> Pulitzer-winning reporter Wesley Lowery breaks down the myth of objectivity . <p> America is at a breaking point . How can journalists properly cover it ? Citizens have flooded the streets in cities large , and small , in protest of racism , police violence and other forms of systemic oppression . But media institutions have been shaped by , and contributed to , those same systems . <p> The same rage that drives Black folks into the streets is carried around by Black journalists , who have lived through decades of retrograde newsroom policy and are beginning to hold the media 's gatekeepers to account for it . <p> That frustration was the crux of a sweeping discussion between Wesley Lowery , a journalist at CBS News , and me . This edition of Conversations , a limited live series for Slate , felt slightly more personal than others . For a young Black journalist who has been told to be wary of " pigeonholing " myself while writing about racism , having this conversation with Lowery felt cathartic in a way .
