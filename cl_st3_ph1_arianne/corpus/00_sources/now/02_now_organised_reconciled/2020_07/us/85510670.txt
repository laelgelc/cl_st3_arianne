I have n't , " the former Republican House Speaker John Boehner said during a 2012 interview about his party 's manifesto . Last week , the Democratic National Committee released a draft of its new platform . But in this case , when it comes to China , the document merits a close read . It shows how dramatically President Donald Trump has reframed the debate about relations with Beijing -- and how challenging those terms will prove for Democrats , even if they 're lucky enough to regain power . <p> The first notable element of the draft platform is how large China looms . In 2016 , the Democratic platform mentioned " China " or " Chinese " eight times . This year 's document references the rising superpower and its people 25 times . Trump has moved to substantially decouple the world 's two largest economies . He has not only boosted tariffs on most Chinese products , but he 's made it harder for American and Chinese individuals and firms to invest in each other 's country . Experts warn that this de-linking could leave the United States substantially poorer , and according to polls , Democrats overwhelmingly oppose it . <p> But the platform offers no positive argument for trade and investment with China . It never challenges the notion -- which Trump is making mainstream in the GOP -- that the U.S. would be better if it disengaged economically from Beijing . " But they 'll do so " by working with our allies to stand up to China . " Working with allies is fine , but this language accepts Trump 's terms of debate . For years , American farmers have benefited immensely from exporting to China . Trump disrupted that relationship by launching a ruinous trade war .
