<h> Environmental news from California and beyond <h> Huge new boreal forest preserve in Manitoba <p> June 16 , 2011 7:52 pm <p> Efforts to combat climate change and diminishing wildlands have increasingly focused on the vast belt of northern forest that rings the globe south of the Arctic . The boreal forest is a vast repository of stored carbon and , in much of northern Canada , a pristine region populated by wolves and caribou along rivers still teeming with fish . <p> The forest in Canada 's Manitoba province has been under threat in recent years by expanding hydropower development . A major new electrical transmission line has been sought through the wild woodlands east of Lake Winnipeg , a plan many indigenous residents say would threaten a region they have called home for thousands of years . <p> Now the government of Manitoba has granted permanent legal protection to nearly 2 million acres -- an area the size of Yellowstone National Park -- on the ancestral lands of the Poplar River First Nation people . <p> The " Asatiwisipe Aki Management Plan " is being celebrated by conservation groups across Canada and the United States , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ boreal forest not only for the people of the Poplar River and the wildlife there , but as a hedge against a warming planet . <p> Manitoba Premier Greg Selinger said the Poplar River reserve , about 250 miles north of Winnipeg , is part of a larger , 10-million-acre area being proposed for designation as a UNESCO World Heritage Site . <p> " This is really important for us , because we do think it 's a priceless world asset , and you only get one chance to do it right , " Selinger said in an interview . <p> " What 's unique about it is not only do the First Nations still occupy it , but it 's intact . There 's a complete ecosystem that generates clean water , oxygen ; there 's a huge storage of peat that 's one of the most effective ways to store carbon dioxide . " <p> Planning for the area was led by the Poplar River First Nation , which will now oversee its preservation and limited development .
