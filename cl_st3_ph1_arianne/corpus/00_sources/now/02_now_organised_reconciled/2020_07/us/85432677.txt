<p> We 're just 44 years away from " peak human " -- when the world 's population will begin to decrease , according to new projections published in The Lancet . <p> Breaking it down : By 2100 , the populations of China , Japan and Italy are expected to be half of what they are today , while sub-Saharan Africa will be home to three times as many people . <p> This forecast puts the global population in 2100 some 2 billion below the UN 's estimate of 11 billion , with the peak also expected much sooner . <p> It factors in greater decreases in fertility rates due to increased access to education and contraception . <p> Immigration patterns and climate change will change the outlook , the authors note . <p> As birth rates fall and life expectancies climb , the world 's population will get much older . <p> The big picture : The global population rose over the last 80 years from around 2.3 billion to 7.8 billion .
