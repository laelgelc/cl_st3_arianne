( Diane Krauthamer/Flickr ) ( Diane Krauthamer ) <p> There are several factors converging Wednesday afternoon and evening that will probably lead to scattered to widespread , strong to severe storms . <p> The main driver will be a very hot , humid and unstable air mass -- given winds from the south and strong heating . Instability levels -- as measured by the expected amount of " total buoyant energy " in deep clouds ( called CAPE , or Convective Available Potential Energy ) -- are expected to become significant . <p> This is coupled with several potential triggers that could cause the storms to be more widespread . <p> The first trigger is heating of elevated terrain to our west , which is expected to generate a batch of storms that may then move into our region between midafternoon and early evening hours , on mid-level winds from the west . Several of the high resolution forecast models support this idea . <p> The second trigger is the approach of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in the map below , which will help air rise in a coherent manner . While the main , more dynamic core of this wave will remain to our north , there will probably still be enough energy to intensify our storm potential . <p> The third trigger is a stationary boundary or front located through the Maryland-D . C. region . This front , while weak , will help low-level winds converge , stoking cloud updrafts . <p> The one mitigating factor is a lack of strong wind shear , which is the increase in winds with altitude . Wind shear helps to organize individual storm cells into longer-lived , organized clusters , which in turn leads to stronger storm complexes . The shear level in Wednesday morning 's weather balloon launch at Dulles was not impressive , but there may be a bit of an uptick later in the day , as mid-level winds strengthen with the approach of the aforementioned disturbance . <p> Couple all these factors with a very humid air mass , and the potential for heavy , gusty storms increases . <p> All said @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ widespread Wednesday afternoon and early evening , as was the case over the past couple of days , but perhaps it will trigger more frequent warnings .
