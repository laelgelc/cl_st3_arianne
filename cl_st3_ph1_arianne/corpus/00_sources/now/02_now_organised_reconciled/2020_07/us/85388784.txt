<p> FILE PHOTO : A view shows water barriers during recovery works following a diesel fuel leak at a thermal power station in Krasnoyarsk Region Reuters <p> By Andrew Osborn and Tom Balmforth <p> MOSCOW ( Reuters ) - Pressure is mounting on Russian mining company Norilsk Nickel over an Arctic oil spill that has wiped about 17% off its share price , left it with a hefty compensation bill and exposed it to accusations of covering up the full extent of the damage . <p> Nornickel , as it is better known , denies the cover-up allegations by green campaigners , a regional governor and a former official at Russia 's environmental watchdog , who have spoken out publicly and , in some cases , published tests , photographs and witness accounts to support their allegations . <p> Their evidence , which Reuters has been unable to verify , shows diesel has spread into a lake and , according to witnesses , into a river that feeds into the Arctic Ocean . <p> Nornickel , citing official tests and satellite images , denies pollution has spread into either body of water . <p> It says it has contained @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ city of Norilsk on May 29 , and that it has recovered 90% of the diesel by using containment booms and pumps . <p> It also denies hiding the extent of the damage , saying the vast number of companies , regulators and others officials involved in the clean-up operation guarantees full transparency . <p> " We can not imagine how it would be possible to hide anything in such conditions , " a company spokeswoman said . " An investigation into the incident is being carried out by regulatory and monitoring agencies . " <p> Reuters has no independent evidence to support the allegations that Nornickel has suppressed information . <p> Nornickel 's shares fell 5% in one day in Moscow on Monday after Russia 's environmental watchdog demanded over $2 billion in damages . Nornickel said on Wednesday it disputed the size of the claim and the methodology used for its calculation . <p> President Vladimir Putin in June chided Vladimir Potanin , the billionaire boss of Norilsk , for not replacing the source of the spill , a fuel tank , in timely fashion and said significant @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Potanin told Putin Nornickel would spare no expense to restore the environment and was committed to fund programs that would increase the deer population and maintain rare fish stocks . Nornickel has said publicly it is also committed to a federal investigation into the incident , takes full responsibility for what happened , and wants to avoid a repeat . <p> At the market close on Thursday , Nornickel 's shares were almost 17.3% lower than at the May 29 opening . <p> CALLS FOR MORE INFORMATION <p> The spill was caused by the failure of a reserve fuel tank at the power station , owned by NTEK , a subsidiary of Nornickel . About 21,000 tonnes of diesel leaked into rivers and subsoil near Norilsk . Nornickel , one of the world 's largest producers of nickel and palladium , blamed melting permafrost driven by climate change for eroding the tank 's foundations . <p> Federal investigators have blamed the poor state of the fuel tank , and have detained three of the power station 's managers and charged Norilsk 's mayor with criminal negligence . <p> Environmental @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ regard as a lack of information about how far the pollution has spread towards the Arctic Ocean . <p> " It 's obvious that a cover-up is under way , " said Vasily Ryabinin , who quit as an official at state environmental regulator Rosprirodnadzor in Norilsk after the spill . <p> " Most of the fuel got into the lake before they set up the containment booms , " he said . <p> Ryabinin cited as evidence photographs he took on June 4 which appear to show riverbanks beyond the booms towards the lake stained with diesel and says he was obstructed from carrying out a proper inspection of the site on the day of the accident by the power station 's security guards and police . <p> Ryabinin said he and his boss at Rosprirodnadzor were turned back even though his boss was carrying the required identity documents . Rosprirodnadzor later removed him from the case and he then resigned , judging that his employer was complicit in environmental damage . <p> Rosprirodnadzor did not respond when asked about the circumstances of his departure and his assertions . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> Alexander Uss , governor of the Krasnoyarsk region where the leak occurred , also expressed concern after inspecting the accident site and talking with company officials . <p> He said NTEK had first said that a car caught fire at the site and then changed its version of events to say the fuel tank may have leaked but that there was no ecological damage . Two days passed , he said , before the real picture became clear . <p> " There was clearly disinformation ( from Nornickel ) that you can not describe as anything else other than a desire to hide what happened or the scale of what happened , " he told state television in June . <p> Declaring a state of emergency in the area on June 3 , Putin criticized what he called a bungled and delayed response . <p> " Why did authorities find out about what happened only two days later ? , " Putin , visibly irritated , asked Sergei Lipin , the head of NTEK . " What , are we going to find out about emergency situations from @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ? " <p> Lipin defended NTEK 's handling of the accident to Putin , saying it had passed on information about the accident according to its standard plan for fuel spills . He did not comment on Uss 's assertion that NTEK had altered its version of events . <p> U.S. OFFER OF HELP REJECTED <p> Despite the concerns about Russia 's worst oil spill since 1994 , Moscow has turned down a U.S. offer to help with the clean-up , saying it can manage on its own . <p> Some environmental campaigners say they doubt Nornickel 's assertions that it has been able to recover 90% of the leaked diesel , citing similar clean-ups in the past around the world . <p> Nornickel said it stood by its estimate , but that precise data would be available only when the fuel was separated from the collected water . <p> Nornickel acts as the main employer and benefactor in the Siberian Arctic city over 2,350 km ( 1,460 miles ) from Moscow . In a video posted online , he showed , using technical equipment , how it contained more than double the normal concentration of toxic oil products . <p> A spokeswoman for Rosprirodnadzor shared data with Reuters that did not detect critical pollution levels there on June 30 .
