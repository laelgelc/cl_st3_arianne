Eva Hambach/AFP via Getty Images <p> A new national Jewish group dedicated to tackling the climate crisis is emphasizing the connections between racial injustice and a degraded environment . <p> Minority communities , says Rabbi Jennie Rosenn , founder and CEO of the new group , Dayenu , " are more likely to have polluting industries close by , less access to cooling in extreme heat , and significantly limited access to quality medical care . " <p> Launched one month before George Floyd 's killing in police custody led to weeks of demonstrations against police brutality and racial injustice , Dayenu aims to link the issues in its push for clean energy and a " Just Green Recovery " -- a pledge by the presidential candidates to rebuild the world after the coronavirus so that it is more just and sustainable rather than supporting the coal and gas industries . <p> " We want an economy that runs @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , Dayenu 's chief strategy officer . A meta-analysis in the journal of the American Medical Association confirmed the correlation and concluded that the damage to babies ' health will continue to grow as climate change worsens . <p> The climate crisis disproportionately impacts " those who have been historically marginalized : people living in the global south , in poverty , in particularly vulnerable areas , and people who experience racism and other kinds of bigotry , " Rabbi Rosenn told The Jewish Week . <p> Noting that Dayenu is Hebrew for " enough , " Rabbi Rosenn said the name @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ with this destruction and being on the path that is neither just nor sustainable , " and it is also saying " we have enough " in terms of the " science , technology and capacity to ensure a just and sustainable world so all can have enough .
