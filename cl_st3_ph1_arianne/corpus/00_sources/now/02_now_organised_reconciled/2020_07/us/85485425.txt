The recommendations , which touch on everything from climate change to health care , are intended to influence both the Democratic platform and the policies of the new administration should Biden win the presidential election . <p> On higher education , the recommendations include some items from the traditional progressive wish list , such as free public college . There are also some less-discussed ideas , including a new direct grant program for colleges and universities . Among the highlights of the task force 's ideas : <p> Free College for Most . Modifying an old favorite of Sanders , the task force proposes making four-year public colleges and universities tuition-free for students with family income below $125,000 , and making community colleges free for all . While the policy is sold as progressive , the vast majority of low-income students already pay nothing in tuition , and middle-income students usually pay less than $2,500 after existing sources of financial aid are taken into account . Upper middle-class families therefore stand to gain the most from this policy . Free college will also make institutions more reliant on volatile state budgets for their funding , which could hurt college finances in a time when state governments are already under serious budgetary pressure . <p> New Direct Funding for Higher Education . Additionally , the task force endorses a new program that would provide direct grant funding to public and private nonprofit colleges . Schools would receive more money for enrolling a greater proportion of low-income students . New direct funding for institutions risks undermining that successful student-led model , and also raises the specter of intrusive new federal regulation for colleges receiving funds . <p> Double Pell Grants . The task force proposes doubling the maximum Pell Grant , a federal program that provides means-tested financial aid for low- and middle-income students . While expanding Pell Grants would be a better use of new money than free college or direct funding for institutions , an expansion is not necessary . Contrary to perceptions , the Pell Grant 's value has largely kept up with inflation . In addition , Congress has created new college-aid programs such as the American Opportunity Tax Credit . States have also made their grant programs more generous . Moreover , even though the Pell Grant is means-tested , students from higher-income families would capture a significant share of the benefits from an expansion due to the way the program is designed . <p> Student Debt Cancellation . The recommendations resurrect a failed @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ minimum of $10,000 in student loans for every borrower , at a cost of $370 billion . Additional proposed forgiveness for borrowers in public service and those who attended certain colleges will inflate this figure further . For comparison , the total cost of the proposal exceeds that of the $1,200 economic impact payments that the IRS sent out earlier this year . If policymakers are willing to spend upwards of $370 billion on relief from the COVID-19 recession , why target those funds toward student borrowers specifically ? The typical student borrower is higher-income than the average American , making student debt relief a regressive option for spending those funds . Moreover , loan cancellation could create a general expectation of future forgiveness , leading students to borrow more than they otherwise would . <p> Modify Student Loan Repayment Plans . In addition to outright debt cancellation , the task force endorses expanding a backdoor to student loan forgiveness by making loan repayment plans more generous . While income-based repayment can be a lifeline for borrowers in genuine financial distress , the generous loan forgiveness provisions already in place mostly benefit borrowers with graduate degrees , who also tend to have higher incomes . Expanding income-based repayment , absent other reforms , will further subsidize expensive graduate degrees and reward borrowers for taking out bigger loans . <p> Crack Down on For-Profit Colleges . The recommendations propose that the Department of Education require that for-profit colleges " demonstrate their value and effectiveness before becoming eligible for federal student loans . Indeed , between a quarter and a third of students at public and private nonprofit institutions are currently enrolled in degree programs that do not provide adequate value for money , by an Obama administration standard .
