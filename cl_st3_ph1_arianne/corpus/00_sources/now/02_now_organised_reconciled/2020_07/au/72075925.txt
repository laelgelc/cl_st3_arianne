<h> First State Super to sell out of thermal coal <p> One of the nation 's largest superannuation funds has promised to ditch its shares in companies that earn more than 10 per cent of revenue from thermal coal mining . <p> First State Super , which looks after more than $120 billion in retirement savings , announced its plans to sell out of thermal coal as part of a broader climate action plan released on Thursday . <p> The superannuation fund has set an emissions reduction target of 30 per cent across its listed equities portfolio by 2023 and 45 per cent across its portfolio by 2030 . <p> First State Super CEO Deanne Stewart said climate change was one of the biggest risks to members ' retirement savings , and that now was the time for " bold and decisive action " . <p> It is essential that as a responsible owner super funds set strong , ambitious and transparent targets to deliver the kind of action we need now to prepare for a more prosperous and sustainable future , " Ms Stewart said in a statement . <p> " We have seen over the past 10 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and increasingly insurance companies are signalling their intent to exit this sector in response to medium-term climate-related risks . " <p> Ms Stewart said the fund would meet its targets by engaging with the boards of heavy polluters , divesting whenever engagement fails , and setting new fund-wide targets for investments in renewable energy and new technologies -- among other things . <p> Starting in October , the fund 's first major step will be to divest from businesses that earn more than 10 per cent of their revenue from thermal coal mining . <p> Last year , it took a one-third stake in the $1.1 billion South Australian wind farm Snowtown 2 . <p> Its most recent announcement comes after HESTA , the superannuation fund representing the health and community services sector , announced a target of net zero emissions across its investment portfolio by 2050 . <p> Dan Gocher , who is the director of climate and environment at the Australasian Centre for Corporate Responsibility ( ACCR ) , expects many more funds will follow suit . <p> " This has been building for some time , " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ probably getting a fair bit of member feedback , I think community concern after the bushfires was probably a big influence , and then you 've got regulators all demanding that funds and companies do more on climate -- from the RBA to APRA to ASIC , you name it .
