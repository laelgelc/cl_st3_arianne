<h> Old versus young : The coronavirus has drawn the wrong battlelines <p> The financial effect of the coronavirus and the measures taken to curb its spread have been pitched as a battle between young and old . <p> Some commentators describe it as a war between generations , with young people having to sacrifice their freedom , lifestyles and their economic growth and security for older vulnerable people who have already established themselves economically in the world . <p> This is a misleading argument . <p> It conveniently papers over the real problem : structural inequities created by successive conservative governments that have embraced neo-liberal economic policy . <p> Giri Sivaraman . Photo : Supplied <p> These inequities have impacted all generations and will continue to impact all generations unless we organise our society and economic system in a fundamentally different way . <p> There is no doubt that older people have benefited from a range of government measures that have enhanced their wealth . <p> But it is n't older people that are the problem . To some extent , you would expect older generations to be better off . <p> They have worked for 40 years @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ long periods of time , the intended effect of that being that they would be more secure and less reliant on government benefits in retirement . <p> However , the reality is that boomers are n't necessarily more secure .
