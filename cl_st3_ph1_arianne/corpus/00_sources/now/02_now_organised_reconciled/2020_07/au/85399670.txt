This may not seem like a huge decrease , but other studiesestimate that if the rate of chick production falls below 0.48 chicks per pair per year , then the number of loon adults may also start to fall . <p> Our study shows that Ontario may be on its way to dipping below the 0.48 threshold . <p> My research has found that loon chick numbers are declining faster on lakes with higher acidity and mercury levels . <p> Decades ago , large amounts of sulphur dioxide ( SO2 ) and nitrogen oxides ( NOx ) were released into the atmosphere by vehicles and industrial processes , like metal smelting , manufacturing and oil refining , and the burning of fossil fuels . These chemicals caused acid rain and made many lakes acidic . <p> Mercury can also decrease the reproductive success of loons @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ top of a lake 's food chain . Every time one animal eats another , the pollutants in the prey accumulate in the predator . Mercury can build up , reaching high levels in top predators , like loons . <p> High mercury concentrations essentially make loons bad parents . They incubate less , they feed chicks less and , as a result , they raise fewer chicks . <p> Although we 've reduced our acid rain and mercury emissions , many lakes were so severely damaged by acid rain that they are still acidic , and it may take many more decades for these lakes to recover . Likewise , mercury can stick around for a long time in the environment . <p> We also think that climate change may be making things worse for loons . In acidic lakes , warmer temperatures allow more mercury to enter the food chain . This means that with global warming , a larger dose of mercury flows up the food chain and into loons -- and boom -- you get fewer chicks . <h> How can we help loons ? <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ efforts is to become a member and support Birds Canada .
