<h> The fog of peace <p> It would be easier for national leaders to justify increases in defence spending if they could identify imminent challenges that could require military force . <p> However , crises tend to come out of the blue . A senior security official says that if anyone had suggested NATO would operate " out of area " in the Balkans in the 1990s , he would have recommended " psychiatric treatment " . Daniel Keohane , now at the think-tank Fride , was a lone voice when he wrote in European Voice in 2006 that within a decade the EU 's member states could find themselves fighting in north Africa ( it took five years for his prediction to come true , though in Libya rather than -- as he had suggested -- Algeria ) . <p> Identifying longer-term threats is no simpler . When Estonia came under cyber-attack in 2007 , its government accused Russia . But , while Russia is spending more on weaponry -- it is now surpassed only by the US and China -- the former Soviet states do not suggest that a military confrontation with Russia is on the horizon @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ has more men in uniform than the US , more nuclear weapons and aircraft carriers than China , and more experience in the field than Russia , China or India . " We 're the best after the Americans , " is how one official puts it . The EU 's national leaders therefore face a challenge to find a narrative that might convince voters to exempt defence from further budget cuts . <p> Generic arguments about the need for eternal vigilance and the need for Europe to contribute to the stability of the international system may not be sufficiently specific for the public . The argument that Europe needs to maintain its place in the world may smell too much of machismo for war-weary and war-wary Europeans . The West 's wars in Iraq and Afghanistan have , as the UK 's prime minister , David Cameron , pointed out during his recent failed bid to secure parliamentary backing for a military strike in Syria , " poisoned the well " . <p> Government leaders are faced with a gap between the warnings of their experts and the wishful @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Officials describe the combination of the US pivot , unrest in the Middle East and budget cuts as a " perfect storm " . There are many longer-term concerns : constraints on the US , the rise of energy-hungry powers , Russia 's fragility , resource shortages , demographic growth , and climate change . <p> And there are persistent concerns : the shortcomings in Europe 's military equipment that were identified in Kosovo in 1999 remain ; the lack of spending . Just three of the EU 's 28 member states -- the UK , France and Greece -- meet NATO 's target that 2% of gross domestic product should be spent on defence . " Jeez , you look at all that and you know there 's going to be trouble , " Keohane says . <p> While Europe is spending less , and its defence industry is shrinking , other countries are spending more : 2012 was the first year in which Asian countries spent more on defence than European states . <p> One senior defence official pins his hope for a change of attitude on a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ forces " eviscerated " . <p> That might limit cuts by the two countries that boast Europe 's biggest traditional military forces , the UK and France , but will carry less weight in states with more marginal power . Expectations for the EU 's summit discussions are consequently low . One official set the bar at needing the leaders to show that they are " aware that defence matters " . <p> Peer pressure and geopolitical arguments may yet come to bear . Observers , such as Keohane and Sven Biscop of the Egmont think-tank , attribute Herman Van Rompuy 's decision to put defence on the agenda of December 's summit down to his experience at NATO 's conference in Chicago in 2012 , where the gaps in Europe 's capabilities , Europe 's lack of defence spending and pressure from the US and NATO made him realise that Europe might one day have to do much more militarily than it does now . Between now and mid-December , other leaders may come round to this view .
