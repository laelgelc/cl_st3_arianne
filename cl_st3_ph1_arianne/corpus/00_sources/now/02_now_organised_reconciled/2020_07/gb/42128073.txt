<h> New House Farm , Canterbury starts harvest of new climate resistant blackcurrant for Ribena <p> The first harvest of a brand new type of berry designed to cope with climate change is this week being harvested in Canterbury . <p> New House Farm in Chartham is one of 35 across the UK able to harvest the new blackcurrant crop which will be used to make Ribena . <p> Farmer John Hinchliff explains more <p> The new variety called ' Ben Lawers ' has been bred to cope through warmer winters and has taken more than 20 years to develop . <p> Farmer John Hinchliff from New House Farm says the new berries means this year 's harvest has been a huge success . <p> He said : " It has behaved exactly as we all thought it would so we are very pleased . We 're about 25% of the way through the harvest and we 've got three to four very long weeks ahead of us . All we need now is for the sun to shine , but not too much . <p> He added : " This year we have a great crop so the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and it 's also good from my point of view because if we have another mild winter , I know that the crops will still be good . " <p> Usually , blackcurrant bushes need a ' winter chill ' in order to bear fruit in the summer . As British winters have been getting warmer , it has become more difficult for farmers like Mr Hinchliff to grow the fruit . <p> John Hinchliff from New House Farm in Canterbury is harvesting the new crop . Picture : New House Farm <p> The new climate resilient berry was designed to overcome this hurdle by thriving in the slightly warmer conditions . <p> Researchers from the James Hutton Institute are responsible for the groundbreaking work which started in 1956 . <p> Mr Hinchliff says thanks to them , this year he is able to harvest more than 150 acres . <p> He added : " Many years ago when we always had cold winters it was n't a problem .
