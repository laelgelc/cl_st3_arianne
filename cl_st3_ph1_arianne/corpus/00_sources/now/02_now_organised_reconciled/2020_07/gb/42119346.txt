This process can run from anything from 21 days to as long as 42 days . <p> Wet ageing : Wet-aged beef will typically be aged in a vacuum-sealed bag to retain its moisture . This is the dominant mode of ageing beef in the Americas and is very popular in Argentinian style steak restaurants . <p> Cuts on the bone : this refers to quite literally cuts of beef on the bone , such as a tomahawk steak . <p> Marbling : This refers to the intramuscular fat which is , ideally , evenly dispersed within the meat . <p> Wagyu : This term usually refers to Japanese breeds of cattle that are known for their intense marbling . <p> Grass-Fed : Cattle that have been raised exclusively on forage from pasture . <p> Grain-Fed : Cattle raised primarily on grass , but they are usually fed with grains later in life to fatten them up . <p> Bone marrow : The flexible tissue found in the interior of bones . sometimes used as an accompaniment to steak <p> Blue : Nearly raw , cooked for a very short time . <p> Tomahawk Steak : This dramatic steak is basically a rib steak with a long section of the rib bone attached . It 's got fantastic marbling and looks mightily impressive on a bbq ! <p> Ribeye Steak : If you picture the classic carvery rib roast , the ribeye is cut from the central muscle of this . It 's got a really rich flavour and juicy texture , largely due to the small ' eye ' of fat running through the steak . It 's best cooked to at least medium-rare , to give this fat time to render down and add that spectacular juiciness . <p> Rib Steak : This is simply a thicker cut Ribeye Steak with a short piece of the rib bone attached -- a bit like a mini Tomahawk . <p> Sirloin Steak:The sirloin is cut from a relatively unworked muscle , which means it 's got fantastic tenderness -- second only to fillet . Full , rich flavour means this is a really popular option in restaurants . <p> T-Bone Steak : This bone-in cut includes a section of the fillet and a section of sirloin , separated by a T-shaped bone ( hence the name ) . It 's similar to a Porterhouse steak , which has only sirloin without the fillet . <p> Chateaubriand:This tender , luxurious steak for two is actually more of a mini roast . A whole beef fillet has a pointed end ( the ' tail ' ) and a blunt end ( the ' head ' ) . Chateaubriand is cut from the fillet head and has all the luxurious tenderness you 'd expect from this prized cut . <p> Centre Cut Fillet Steak : Possibly the most prestigious steak of them all , this is sliced from the premium centre of the fillet , between the head and the tail . It 's traditionally cut into thick , cylinder-shaped steaks that are the ultimate in tenderness . <p> Skirt Steak:Skirt Steak is cut from the diaphragm area , and is a long thin steak with a loose grain . It 's got a really rich , beefy flavour , and is best cooked rare . Flank steak is similar , but a little thicker and wider , and can be tough unless it 's cut across the grain . <p> Pav ? Rump Steak:A ' classic ' rump is cut straight across the full face of the rump , and includes a fair bit of fat and gristle . The ' pav ? ' is cut only from a single muscle of the rump , and is trimmed of all fat , making it a much more tender option than a classic rump . Its name comes from the French for cobblestone , whose shape it resembles . <h> A Butcher 's guide to buying steak <p> When shopping for steaks lookout for grass-fed Scottish beef -- grain-fed is inferior in flavour and quality . <p> Look for a good maturation time . You 'll lose some quality by freezing it yourself , but a professionally frozen steak will eat better , and you can always tell how long it has been matured for . <h> Cooking the perfect steak <p> First off , buy good quality meat where you can , use online services like ours , or your local butcher where possible so you can be assured of its provenance . <p> Make sure to bring any of your cuts to room temperature before you cook . About 20 minutes before you start cooking , remove the defrosted meat from its vacuum packaging and pat dry with kitchen paper . Spread out your steaks on a board in a single layer to ' bloom ' and come to room temperature . <p> Use a little vegetable oil or butter -- in the pan or brushed on the steak , then make sure your pan is hot -- you should hear a sizzle as you add the steak .
