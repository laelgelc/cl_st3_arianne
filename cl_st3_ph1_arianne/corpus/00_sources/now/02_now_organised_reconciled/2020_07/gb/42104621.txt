<h> World has 20% chance of exceeding 1.5C in next five years -- climate forecast <h> World has 20% chance of exceeding 1.5C in next five years -- climate forecast <p> There is a one-in-five chance annual global temperatures will be at least 1.5C warmer than in pre-industrial times in the next five years , experts said . <p> Annual global temperatures are likely to be at least 1C above the levels they were before the industrial era in each year between 2020 and 2024 , a long range forecast by experts led by the Met Office shows . <p> And there is a 20% likelihood that annual temperatures will exceed 1.5C above pre-industrial levels in at least one year , with the chances increasing over time , according to the analysis published by the World Meteorological Organisation ( WMO ) . <p> Under the global Paris Agreement countries have committed to keep temperature rises " well below " 2C above pre-industrial levels and to pursue efforts to curb them to 1.5C to avoid the worst impacts of climate change . <p> While experts say a single year exceeding 1.5C does not mean the targets have been breached , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ world already is . <p> The forecast marks a new international collaboration co-ordinated by the WMO and led by the Met Office , drawing on analyses from climate prediction centres in the UK and nine other countries including the US and China . <p> The climate forecast also predicts there will be enhanced warming of the Arctic , compared to other regions of the world , and there will be an increased risk of storminess across the Atlantic Ocean . <p> The Earth 's average temperature is already more than 1C over the pre-industrial era , and the last five-year period has been the warmest five years on record . <p> WMO secretary-general Petteri Taalas said : " This study shows -- with a high level of scientific skill -- the enormous challenge ahead in meeting the Paris Agreement on climate change target of keeping a global temperature rise this century well below 2C above pre-industrial levels and to pursue efforts to limit the temperature increase even further to 1.5C .
