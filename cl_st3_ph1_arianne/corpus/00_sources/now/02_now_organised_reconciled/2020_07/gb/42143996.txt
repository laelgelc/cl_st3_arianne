Indeed , there are few people who have played a more integral part in the history of rock ' n roll over the last 60 years than Kingsley and Charles Ward and they made it all happen on the farm where they grew up . <p> You may know it as Rockfield Studios : the place where Bohemian Rhapsody , Wonderwall and Yellow and , at one point in 1997 , seven of the top 10 albums were recorded . <p> Either fate or a combination of George Martin 's diary commitments and the impatience of the Ward brothers drove them into the arms of legendary producer and sound engineer Joe Meek in 1961 . <p> " A friend of mine suggested we talk to him so we took him an acetate with another couple of songs on it , " says Kingsley . " I was playing the organ on one track and it sounded a bit like Telstar the instrumental written and produced by Meek for the Tornados which would become the first record @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ when it was released the following year . He nearly had a heart attack and signed us up on the spot because he did n't want us taking that sound anywhere else . ' <p> They stayed with Meek until 1965 , learning the rudiments of production from one of the most influential sound engineers of all time and taking that knowledge back to Monmouth and the little studio they had built in the attic space at the farm . <p> Soon , so many local bands were queueing up to have their music recorded in the hope they would be able to chase down a deal with the major record labels that the brothers were able to build a second , bigger studio at the farm ( ' The Granary ' ) . <p> By accident , if you take Kingsley at face value , but more likely by design , they were now the owners of the one of the only professional recording studios outside London . News travelled fast . Dave Edmunds turned up from Cardiff . <p> " He was such a good musician we had @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , " says Kingsley . " We 'd caught up with the majors , " he says now . <p> So , with little to choose in terms of technology , and an offer that included beautiful surroundings and tranquillity away from the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ hippie zeitgeist -- and privacy plus ( in theory ) a distance from the manifold temptations and distractions readily available elsewhere , Rockfield became the go to destination for labels who needed their artists to deliver . <p> The artists loved it too . " Just to go from Birmingham to Monmouth was an adventure , " Ozzy Osbourne tells the makers of new BBC documentary Rockfield : The Studio on the Farm . He was there with Black Sabbath in 1970 to record their album Paranoid . " We 'd never been in a studio , we 'd never been on a farm or seen a cow in a field . It was brilliant ... it allowed us the freedom to play it as loud as we liked , there were roof tiles rattling off . " <p> I Hear You Knocking by Dave Edmunds was recorded at the studio and became a worldwide hit in 1972 . Queen had recorded Sheer Heart Attack at the studio in 1974 but had so little money that Kingsley had to lend them the train fare to get back to London . When they returned a year later to record A Night at the Opera it was on slightly different terms . <p> " They were loaded by then , " remembers their erstwhile benefactor . " They were still the same lovely guys but they had crew and a tour manager and a bit more time on their hands .
