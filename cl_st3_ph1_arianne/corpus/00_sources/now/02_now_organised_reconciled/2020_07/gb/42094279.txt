It 's half true : a party at war with itself is seldom an attractive sight . The problem is that divisions almost always reflect genuine differences of opinion -- say , over Europe , or ideology , or nuclear weapons , or social reform . The choice party leaders have is not whether to ' allow ' divisions but how to deal with them when they arise . <p> The temptation is to strive for a party unity that papers over the cracks . Within the Conservative Party , David Cameron tried this before the Brexit referendum -- and Theresa May afterwards . Much good did it do them . <p> Neil Kinnock showed what to do . Faced with the unholy trinity of Militant , Tony Benn and Arthur Scargill in the mid-1980s , he took them on and defeated them .
