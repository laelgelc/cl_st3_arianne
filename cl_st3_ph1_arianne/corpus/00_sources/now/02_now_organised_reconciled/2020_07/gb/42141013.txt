This will introduce in primary legislation both a principle of mutual recognition and a principle of non-discrimination to " guarantee the continued right of all UK companies to trade unhindered in every part of the UK " . <p> Mutual recognition ensures that goods or services that satisfy regulatory standards in one part of the UK are eligible to enter the market anywhere in the UK . <p> Non-discrimination prevents UK businesses or individuals trading across @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ local traders , either directly on the grounds of residence or geographical origin . <p> So , if the Scottish parliament decided to pass a law to limit the sugar content of goods produced in Scotland to tackle the problem of obesity , it could not impose those standards on goods coming into Scotland from other parts of the UK , nor could it prevent those goods from entering the Scottish market , provided these satisfy regulations set anywhere in the UK . <p> Such a law may also be challenged by Scottish producers as an unnecessary barrier to their ability to trade freely across the UK . <p> There are many uncertainties in the proposals . The devolved governments had been concerned that an independent regulatory body , potentially with little understanding of devolution , could be tasked with enforcing UK internal market rules . <p> The white paper seems to rule this out . But who gets to determine the necessity , or otherwise , of distinctive regulations ? <p> When the Scottish parliament passed legislation to introduce minimum unit pricing for alcohol , the disruption to trade in alcohol resulting from the policy was defended as a legitimate and proportionate means of tackling adverse health and social effects of alcohol abuse . <p> After lengthy legal challenges led by the Scotch Whisky Association , the Supreme Court sided with the Scottish government . <p> But it is not at all clear that public health , environmental or any other policy goals will be justifiable exemptions from UK internal market principles . That consent is unlikely to be secured . Will that matter ? Recent experience suggests not . <p> In other Brexit-related legislation , the UK government has demonstrated its willingness to press ahead despite devolved institutions withholding consent .
