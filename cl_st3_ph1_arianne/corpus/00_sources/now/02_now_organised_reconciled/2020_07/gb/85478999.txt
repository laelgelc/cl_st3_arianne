<h> A super-cool research station is being built under the Caribbean Sea <p> While we learn more about outer space with every new rocket launch , the earth 's expansive oceans remain relatively unexplored . They cover nearly three quarters of the world 's surface , and yet only five percent of the ocean floor has been mapped . And there are likely millions of marine species we have no idea even exist . <p> So say hello to Proteus -- dubbed the ' ocean 's answer to the International Space Station ' -- which is set to be the world 's largest and most advanced underwater research complex . <p> It will be built off the coast of the Dutch Caribbean island of Cura ? ao and aims to ' advance science to benefit the future of the planet ' . It 's being officially launched today by environmentalist Fabien Cousteau -- grandson of pioneering ocean explorer Jacques-Yves Cousteau . <p> Once Proteus is complete , academics , governments and companies will lease state-of-the-art labs to research marine species , medicine , food sustainability and the effects of climate change on sealife . <p> Photograph : fuseproject <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ underwater station , Proteus will also house the world 's first underwater greenhouse , allowing inhabitants to grow fresh food , and a ' moon pool ' hatch for exploring the ocean floor . <p> In a breakthrough for underwater research , the centre will allow scientists to spend an entire day conducting experiments on the seafloor .
