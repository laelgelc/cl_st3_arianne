HUNDREDS of women seaweed farmers in Zanzibar are set to benefit with the pilot programme that will help them to adopt best practices for increased productivity and environmental performance . <p> The pilot programme which will work with farmers in Pemba and Unguja Islands in the Zanzibar archipelago in the Indian Ocean is implemented through a partnership between The Nature Conservancy and Cargill . <p> The programme will be supported by a local partner -- C-Weed Corporation and conducted in collaboration with the government of Zanzibar . C-Weed Corporation is the major buyer of seaweed in Tanzania , supporting around 2,500 seaweed farmers . <p> International buyers predict increased demand for seaweed since it is a raw material used in a wide range of increasingly popular products as diverse as confectionary , yogurts , and cosmetics . <p> When done sustainably , seaweed aquaculture also provides co-benefits to the planet , including improved water quality and conservation of wildlife habitats . <p> But warming oceans due to climate change , impacts of coastal development , limited aquaculture knowledge and poor seed stocks @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ keep up their yields and maintain their livelihoods through seaweed farming . <p> This drives them to use inappropriate farming practices that amplify negative impacts to the fragile marine environments that their businesses need to thrive , which have already been under threat by a broad suite of human activities . <p> Seaweed farming is one of Zanzibar 's largest export businesses , employing more than 25,000 people , 80 percent of whom are women . <p> A seaweed farmer from Tumbe village , Sada Himidi Selemani , said : " I am happy to participate in the new training program .
