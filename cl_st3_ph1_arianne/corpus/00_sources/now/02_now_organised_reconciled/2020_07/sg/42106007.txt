<h> Silence has descended on Mumbai 's Dharavi slum but workers fear a long-term hit to their income . <h> Share <p> Samita Chavan used to wake up at 3am every day to the sound of her neighbour 's wet grinder in Mumbai 's Dharavi slum . Now , she is adjusting to a rare silence after three months of lockdown that saw tens of thousands of migrant workers leave the city . <p> Dharavi , believed to be Asia 's largest slum , has been hailed as a success story in the battle against the coronavirus pandemic , bringing cases from a daily average of 100 in April to about 10 in July , say city officials , thanks to a strict lockdown . <p> But cash-starved slum dwellers and informal traders fear a long-term hit to their income , as about 150,000 migrant workers have left Dharavi - according to official estimates - and small businesses face permanent closure . <p> " Many factories have exhausted their capital and there is still no idea when things will start improving ... the small factories of Dharavi do not have the capacity to suffer this scale of loss , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Association . <p> " These factories ca n't afford to stop work even for a week .
