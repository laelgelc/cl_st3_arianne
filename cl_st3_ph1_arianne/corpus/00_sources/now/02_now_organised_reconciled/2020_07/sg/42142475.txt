Their excessive use and misuse results in contamination of soil and water resources , which reduces biodiversity , destroys beneficial insect populations , and makes our food less safe . <p> Declining insect populations have become a hot topic in Europe since a study in 2017 revealed that , in some parts of Germany , more than 75 per cent of flying insects had disappeared over the previous three decades . Soon afterward , researchers at the University of Sydney estimated that 41 per cent of all insect species worldwide were declining , and one-third were threatened with extinction . <p> These studies offer a first glimpse of a worrying environmental trend . Long-term scientific data on insect populations is rare , and it is virtually non-existent in regions where the pace of agricultural industrialisation is accelerating , such as Asia , Africa , and Latin America . The overall trend in pesticide use in Europe , however , is not uniform : Some European countries , such as Denmark , use pesticides less , while others , such as Poland , use them more . Still , overall , tougher regulations and reduced demand have made the European market less profitable for leading global pesticide producers . <p> The four largest producers -- BASF and Bayer in Germany , the Swiss-based but Chinese-owned firm Syngenta , and Corteva Agriscience , formerly the agriculture division of DowDuPont -- together account for two-thirds of the global pesticides market , and are seeking new revenue sources . <p> They are targeting developing countries , where agriculture sectors are under pressure to feed growing populations while adapting to the effects of climate change . <p> Here , the major producers benefit from fact that the relatively strict pesticide standards enforced by European governments are used only within their borders .
