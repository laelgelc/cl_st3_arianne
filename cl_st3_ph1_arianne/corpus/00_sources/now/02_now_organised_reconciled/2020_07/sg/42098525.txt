The consortium says the technology will be ready in five to 10 years . <h> Share <p> Singapore is planning to research and develop carbon capture , use and storage ( CCUS ) technology to reduce the climate impact of some its most carbon-intensive industries . <p> A consortium that includes oil giant Chevron , infrastructure consultancy Surbana Jurong , concrete producer Pan-United , and data center operator Keppel Data Centres aims to introduce a range of carbon capture systems for the energy , construction , chemicals and IT sectors within five to10 years . <p> The plan is to develop commercially viable systems that can be deployed locally in Singapore , and potentially exported overseas , said the consortium , which is being supported by Singapore 's National Research Foundation ( NRF ) . <p> Carbon capture technologies are seen as one way to cut the carbon footprint of heavy industry without hurting the economy .
