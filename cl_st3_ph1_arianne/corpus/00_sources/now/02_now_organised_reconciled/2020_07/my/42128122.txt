<h> UN chief says Covid-19 reveals ' fragility of our world ' <p> JOHANNESBURG , July 18 -- Coronavirus has revealed the " fragile skeleton " of societies and could push 100 million people into extreme poverty , United Nations Secretary General Antonio Guterres said today . <p> Speaking on the 102nd birthday anniversary of the late Nelson Mandela -- South Africa 's first black president -- Guterres said coronavirus was " shining a spotlight " on global injustice . <p> " We have been brought to our knees -- by a microscopic virus . The pandemic has demonstrated the fragility of our world . " <p> " Entire regions that were making progress on eradicating poverty and narrowing inequality have been set back years , in a matter of months , " he warned at a virtual memorial lecture organised by the Johannesburg-based Nelson Mandela Foundation . <p> The economic fallout of the pandemic , which has infected more than 14 million and killed close to 600,000 people worldwide , is being disproportionately felt among informal workers , small businesses and women , Guterres said . <p> " We face the deepest global recession since World War II @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ people could be pushed into extreme poverty . We could see famines of historic proportions . " <p> Coronavirus is an " x-ray " that has revealed " fractures in the fragile skeleton of the societies we have built " , he added , citing unequal healthcare provision , unpaid care work , income disparity and climate change as some of the concerns . <p> " It is exposing fallacies and falsehoods everywhere ... The delusion that we live in a post-racist world . The myth that we are all in the same boat . " <p> He said the world 's 26 richest people hold as much wealth as half the global population . <p> " But income , pay and wealth are not the only measures of inequality , " he added . <p> Guterres said people were running out of patience at the glaring disparities and discrimination across societies . <p> He singled out the global anti-racism movement stoked by the death of George Floyd -- an African-American man killed by a white policeman in May -- as simply " one more sign that people have had @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that treats people as criminals on the basis of their skin colour " , enough of " structural racism " and " systematic injustice " . <p> Coronavirus , he said , had also created an opportunity for world leaders to build a " more equal and sustainable world " . <p> " We are at breaking point . But we know which side of history we are on . " -- AFP
