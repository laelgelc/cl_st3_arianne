<h> Kokako rescue mission a success <p> Auckland Council rangers swooped to the rescue of a juvenile kokako at the weekend . <p> The bird turned up several weeks ago in the Cossey Dam area in the Hunua Ranges , an area outside the Kokako Management Area ( KMA ) . <p> What makes it a " bird of interest " ? <p> Not only was it living in an area kokako has not been seen in recent history , it was hopping more like a kangaroo than flying like a bird ! <p> " We 'd been alerted last week of its existence by members of the public walking the tracks , " says Senior Park Ranger Miranda Bennett . <p> " There were numerous sightings and reports of an injured bird ; a kokako was hopping about and spending an unhealthy amount of time on the ground putting itself at risk from predators . Pest control is carried out in this area although it is not as intensively managed as the Kokako Management Area ; feral cats and mustelids could easily have seen the injured bird as easy prey . Saturday was not a successful day but early Sunday morning " Hoppy " was located roadside feeding on daisy and dandelion flowers . " <p> " It was a lengthy job requiring patience and delicate movements to shepherd " Hoppy " into the net . We also had to navigate disruptions from runners and dog walkers but eventually we had a " bird in the net , " chuckles Miranda . <p> " Our rangers do such an amazing job , working hard to protect our native species . Auckland Council has persevered with the kokako breeding programme out in the ranges and keeping each one of them safe is important , " says Councillor Richard Hills , Chair of the Environment and Climate Change Committee . <p> " Hoppy " was weighed in the field and found to be significantly underweight with no obvious external injuries .
