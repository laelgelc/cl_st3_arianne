Photo : Lynn Grieveson <h> Wellington flooding a Covid recovery reminder <p> Last week 's flooding is a timely reminder for New Zealand to include adaptation risk in Covid-19 recovery planning , write a team of University of Otago academics <p> For those of us living inland , or on less vulnerable coasts , it was easy to ignore last week 's massive waves and flooding ; we were living under Level 4 lock-down , preoccupied with stopping the spread of coronavirus . <p> But Tuesday night saw huge waves crashing against people 's walls in Brighton on the South Island , leaving some residents with their gardens washed away and their homes flooded . By Wednesday , waves around five metres high were hitting Wellington 's Owhiro Bay , damaging properties , forcing evacuations , and washing one person out to sea . What 's worse , in terms of effects on our everyday life , these relatively rare severe flooding events pale in comparison with the effects of more frequent lower-level inundations . Some decisions about new activities and investments may be expedient in the short term , but may have inequitable consequences in the long term . <p> We need to focus on 21st century problems , not 20th century problems . For example , investing in major roading projects that have been ' on the books ' for years may slightly improve travel times for some commuters in the near term , and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ decrease as our modes of transport change . More importantly , funds spent on short-run commute-time reduction will not be available to assist communities that are directly in the path of sea level rise . <p> The sooner we direct resources towards climate adaptation , the cheaper that adaptation will be . At the moment , some climate-vulnerable cities and regions are attempting pro-active adaptation measures , while others , particularly those that already struggle with funding , have done little or nothing . <p> Is it fair that citizens facing similar impacts have inequitable access to support ? Without leadership from central government , local governments will struggle to fulfil their hazard-reduction mandates without unduly damaging their residents ' near-term interests . Directing funding now towards climate-vulnerable communities would not only express our solidarity with those facing extraordinary risks , it would also provide much-needed stability for communities and businesses . <p> We have already demonstrated a world-leading level of solidarity in the face of Covid-19 , cheerfully shouldering the burdens of lockdown together while aiming support at those most in need .
