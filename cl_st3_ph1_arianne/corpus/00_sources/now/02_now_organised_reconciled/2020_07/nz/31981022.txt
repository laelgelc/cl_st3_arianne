<h> Technology to serve humankind : a New Zealand studio with an ethics-first mission <p> Russell Brown talks to Auckland business RUSH about the challenges and rewards of being an ethical business in the competitive world of technology design . <p> " We design technology to better serve humankind . " <p> It 's the first thing you read on RUSH 's website , and it 's also written on a banner in the middle of its Parnell office . It 's the purpose statement that emerged from a staff-led process to define what the Auckland-based tech-design company exists for . And it is , acknowledges creative director Terry Williams-Willcock , a high bar to meet . <p> " We certainly found that when we first wrote the words , " he says . " It 's easy to challenge and it 's sometimes hard to justify . It means we 're constantly ensuring that we are being challenged by our own staff as much as ourselves and we 're going , ' is this the right thing to do ? The design and build process behind the popular translation app involved working with Spark , Google and -- crucially -- advisors like Dean Mahuta , senior lecturer at AUT and Maori language researcher at Te Ipukarea , the National Maori Language Institute . <p> When asked about where the business ' ethical focus originated , Williams-Willcock gestures across the table to Danushka Abeysuriya , who founded RUSH in 2009 .
