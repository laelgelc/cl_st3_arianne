<h> The man who looked the apocalypse in the face -- and laughed <p> Kiran Dass interviews Mark O'Connell , whose new book sprang from terror about what climate change meant for his kids . <p> Dublin writer Mark O'Connell reckons we 're alive in a time of worst-case scenarios , and that we can only really survive in a meaningful sense as part of a community . <p> Following his first book , 2017 's To Be a Machine : Adventures Among Cyborgs , Utopians , Hackers , and the Futurists Solving the Modest Problem of Death , his new book Notes from an Apocalypse : A Personal Journey to the End of the World and Back is a thoroughly engaging work of reportage where he seeks out preppers -- a subculture of people prepping for the end of the world , the great civilisational crack up . <p> It was triggered by " a surge of shame and sadness at the world my son would be forced to live in " , he explains in the opening chapter . " It was not obvious to me how a person was supposed to raise children , to live @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in the quickening shadow of that future ... Listen . Attune your ear to the general discord , and you will hear the cracking of the ice caps , the rising of the waters , the sinister whisper of the near future . " <p> Fittingly , it 's also his children that help him arrive at some sort of peace -- or at least an ability to dance and blow raspberries . The book is equal parts terrifying and hilarious .
