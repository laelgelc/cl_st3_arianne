<p> University of California , Los Angeles ( UCLA ) researchers have received a two-year $2m grant from the US Department of Energy ( DOE ) to develop a process that can convert CO2 emissions into construction materials . <p> That grant , and an additional $905,000 in new funding from UCLA discretionary funds and industry partners , will advance research led by Gaurav Sant , a Professor of Civil and Environmental Engineering and of Materials Science and Engineering at the UCLA Samueli School of Engineering . <p> A team headed by Sant invented CO2Concrete , a form of concrete that is made in part from CO2 emissions , which are an underlying cause of climate change . <p> The technology they devised captures CO2 from raw flue gas as it exits power plants , cement plants and other producers of CO2 , reducing emissions to the atmosphere . <p> Source : Gabriel Falzone/UCLA <p> The technology for producing CO2Concrete is being demonstrated at the Wyoming Integrated Test Center at the Dry Fork Station in Gillette , Wyoming . <p> The process also cuts down on the use of traditional cement , the binding agent in concrete .
