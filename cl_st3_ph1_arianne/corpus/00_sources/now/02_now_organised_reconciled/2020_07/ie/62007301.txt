<p> Growers have picked the first crop of a new " climate resilient " blackcurrant developed by The James Hutton Institute . <p> The new ' Ben Lawers ' variety is the culmination of an incredible 20 years of research requiring 75,000 bushes , and will help guarantee the future of British blackcurrant production , of which 90 per cent goes into making popular drink Ribena . <p> The new variety is the fruit of a longstanding partnership between Lucozade Ribena Suntory , and the world-leading research centre the James Hutton Institute . <p> According to the Dundee-based researchers , blackcurrants are particularly at risk from the UK 's changing climate , where winters have been gradually getting warmer . <p> The bushes require a ' winter chill ' in order to bear fruit come summertime - a process known as vernalisation .
