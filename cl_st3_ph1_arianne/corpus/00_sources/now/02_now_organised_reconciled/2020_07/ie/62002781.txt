<h> Sovereign investors take advantage of COVID-19 buying opportunity <p> The company 's eighth Global Sovereign Asset Management Study revealed that many SWFs had been well-prepared for the drop in valuations caused by the pandemic , due to lessons learned from the global financial crisis . <p> These included building large cash reserves and improving the management of liquidity . <p> However , it is fixed income and illiquids rather than equities that have been the beneficiaries of this spending spree . <p> At end-2019 , equity allocations were already at their lowest level since 2013 -- 26% of SWF portfolios , compared with 34% for fixed income . <p> This trend is likely to continue according to the survey , which found 37% of SWFs aim to decrease their equity allocations , with only 22% intending to increase them , over the next 12 months . <p> In contrast , 43% aim to increase allocations to fixed income , and the same percentage to private equity and infrastructure . <p> Rod Ringrow , head of official institutions , Invesco , said : " Many investors have considered infrastructure investments expensive because there is a lot of competition for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ creating potential distressed assets . " <p> Ringrow told IPE : " For example , airports are looking for capital injections on favourable terms to investors taking a long-term view . While transport has been hit in the short-term by COVID-19 , countries with citizens experiencing a long-term increase in disposable income will help revive the industry : people will always want to travel . " <p> Source : Invesco <p> The survey also brought out the shortage of talent at middle manager level : for SWFs , the greatest capability gap is for private assets , investment strategy and asset allocation . <p> Invesco said the internalisation of investment teams , driven by the need for greater control rather than cost , is leading to particular challenges sourcing appropriate talent , particularly in emerging markets . <p> It said these problems are being addressed through a greater emphasis on internal development and retention -- 71% of SWFs now have internal development programmes -- plus pooling resources through co-investments and platform deals . <p> Meanwhile , COVID-19 has provoked an increased interest in gold , with 23% of SWFs planning @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> Exchange traded funds -- used by 40% of SWFs who are gold investors -- are seen as a way of dipping a toe in the market , as well as avoiding the political controversy sometimes attached to the concept of " gold reserves " . <p> For SWFs , environmental , social and governance ( ESG ) investing is now " at the front and centre of investment decisions " , said Ringrow . <p> Over half -- 56% -- of all investors surveyed said that they have an obligation to consider climate change in their portfolio . <p> While this year 's field work was carried out too early to reflect any reaction to China 's perceived role in precipitating the pandemic , or the moves to curtail the autonomy of Hong Kong , Ringrow said he did not expect SWFs to reduce their weightings to the country in the short-term , since they expect Asia to be the leader of the post-COVID global economic recovery .
