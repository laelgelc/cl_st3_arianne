<p> Direct emissions of a powerful greenhouse gas from certain pasture types are lower than previously thought -- meaning the climate impact of grass-fed cattle herds may be overestimated , according to Rothamsted Research . <p> A team from Rothamsted has shown urine from animals reared on pasture where white clover grows -- a plant commonly sown onto grazing land to reduce the need for additional nitrogen fertiliser -- results in just over half the amount of nitrous oxide previously assumed by scientists to be released . <p> Nitrous oxide is a potent greenhouse gas that is 265 times more harmful than CO2 and can account for 40% of beef supply chain emissions , and the group says these findings may help farming achieve its ' net zero ' ambition by 2040 . <p> The researchers measured emissions from one herd on their experimental beef and sheep farm in Devon , England . <p> " Due to technical and logistical challenges , field experiments which measure losses of nitrous oxide from soils usually add livestock faeces and urine they have sourced from other farms or other parts of the farm , meaning that the emissions captured do not necessarily @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ pasture .
