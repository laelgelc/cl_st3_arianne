<h> Dust pollution in Sub-Saharan Africa raised infant mortality : Study <h> Last Updated : Thursday 02 July 2020 <p> Air pollution due to dust from the Sahara desert led to a significant increase in infant mortality rate in Sub-Saharan Africa , according to a recent study . <p> The researchers from Stanford University , United States , found that a roughly 25 per cent increase in local annual mean particulate concentrations in West Africa caused an 18 per cent increase in infant mortality . The study was published in Nature Sustainabilityon June 29 , 2020 . <p> They analysed 15 years of household surveys from 30 countries across Sub-Saharan Africa covering nearly a million births . They combined this survey with satellite-detected changes in particulate levels driven by the Bodele Depression in Chad . <p> Bodele Depression located at the southern edge of the Sahara Desert in north central Africa , is the lowest point in Chad . Dust storms from the Bodele Depression occur on average about 100 days per year . It is the largest source of dust emissions in the world . <p> The researchers compared the birth and death data of children with satellite-detected @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the Bodele dust storms . Association between the two parameters was studied to see if poor air quality was associated with adverse health outcomes in children . <p> Emissions from Bodele contributed greatly to annual average particulate matter 2.5 concentrations across much of Africa , particularly in the north and west . <p> The study highlighted that air pollution , even from natural sources , is extremely critical for child health . Children under five are particularly vulnerable to the tiny particles in air pollution that can have a range of negative health impacts , including lower birth weight and impaired growth in the first year of life . <p> The study concluded that air pollution from natural as well as artificial sources could be linked with a high infant mortality . <p> It attributed changing climate as the reason behind natural sources of air pollution .
