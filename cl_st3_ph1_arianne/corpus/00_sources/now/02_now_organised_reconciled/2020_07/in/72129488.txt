<h> Beyond conservation , why our natural ecosystems need restoration <h> Last Updated : Thursday 30 July 2020 <p> Ecosystem degradation has emerged as one of the biggest environmental threats around the world over the last three decades . Almost all our ecosystems , terrestrial or otherwise , stand degraded to varying degrees due to a mix of factors such as developmental pressures , population growth , over-exploitation , etc . <p> Other stressors such as invasive alien species and climate change also impact many ecosystems . <p> While the impact of ecological degradation on biodiversity is well-known , its impact on ecosystem services is less appreciated . Degradation of natural ecosystems in the Western Ghats pose a direct threat to water security , and in turn , to livelihoods of millions of people in the plains . Degradation , therefore , has serious implications for human well-being and economic sustainability . <p> Traditional approaches to ecological conservation focus on protecting and keeping natural spaces inviolate . However , this has proved to be nearly impossible , thanks to a persistent increase in anthropogenic pressures . <p> Many of our ecosystems today are damaged beyond unassisted self-recovery . Ecosystems are in a constant state of evolution . <p> Hence , the objective of restoration is to assist an ecosystem to recover to its pre-degradation trajectory ( or close to it ) . This is done through a scientific process of benchmarking with reference models from the same native ecosystem . <p> Ecosystem integrity , covering both biotic and abiotic aspects , forms the foundation of ecological restoration . A restored site is expected to have the key structural and species characteristics that are representative of the native ecosystem type it belongs to . <p> Restoration adopts a holistic approach focused on all elements of an ecosystem such as soil , hydrology , flora , fauna , etc . Functional specialisation has grown in many aspects such as seed science , soil conservation , invasive species management , etc . <p> Restoration methodologies have been developed worldwide for different degradation scenarios including for extreme ones like landslides , mining , forest fires , etc . This should encourage climate change programmes to embrace restoration principles so that biodiversity goals are achieved simultaneously . <p> The Indian context <p> Ecological restoration is still a developing discipline in India , with a limited @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and site-specific . On the other hand , the need for restoration is considerable and urgent . <p> For example , most forests lying outside protected areas have been degraded due to high human pressure . Our protected areas , in turn , are affected by factors like invasive alien species . <p> At a ballpark estimate , around 40 per cent of our forests may need to be restored . If we extrapolate this to other ecosystems , one can see that the opportunity to improve biodiversity , improve quality of human lives and increase good habitats for fauna is significant . More importantly , restoration has the capacity to generate substantial rural livelihoods and reduce human-wildlife conflicts . <p> India has an ambitious target of restoring 26 million hectares of degraded lands by 2030 . Reaching this target will require certain key enablers to be in place . <p> First , achieving this scale will need a large number of well-trained restoration practitioners . Restoration ecology has a rather low presence in academic curriculum in India at present and this needs attention . <p> We will also require @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ implementation and monitoring of programmes . Second , we have to make sure that projects adhere to the core principles of restoration . The international standards can help in this regard , with suitable adaptation for Indian conditions . <p> Last , a typical restoration project lasts well over five-six years , and calls for sustained funding and focus .
