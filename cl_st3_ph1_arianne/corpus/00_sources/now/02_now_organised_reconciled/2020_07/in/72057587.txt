<h> Extreme weather in North East : Floods in some districts , others stay dry <h> Last Updated : Thursday 25 June 2020 <p> The North East India has been reeling under a worrying weather trend : Prolonged periods of no rainfall , followed by sudden bouts of rain , sometimes with an overall rainfall deficit . <p> The most recent case is that of Assam , which is experiencing a second wave of floods caused by incessant rainfall on June 23 , 2020 . The first wave was end-May 2020 . According to a situation report by the Assam State Disaster Management Authority , one person died and 37,675 people were affected as on June 24 . <p> The North Eastern states have been receiving less rainfall than normal for several years and the distribution pattern is skewed . In 2019 , the region recorded a deficit of 12 per cent while the rest of the country had an excess rainfall of 10 per cent -- the highest in the last 25 years . <p> The intense activity of southwest monsoon season in the region is behind the floods this year in Assam . First , the northward shift of monsoon trough that runs from north Punjab to northwest Bay of Bengal . Monsoon rains are generally clustered around such troughs . <p> Second , the coming together of strong winds from the south and southwest directions carrying moisture from the Bay of Bengal . <p> The floods impacted five districts in upper Assam the most -- Dhemaji , Jorhat , Majuli , Sivasagar and Dibrugarh . The situation in Dhemaji is the worst -- the flood affected 61 villages , destroying crops over 3,474 hectares and impacting over 15,000 people there . At least 12 roads were damaged . <p> Dhemaji received 86 per cent excess rainfall on June 23 that soared to 163 per cent on June 24 . Between June 1 and June 24 , the district received 150 per cent more rainfall than normal . <p> Dibrugarh is the second-most affected district : Over 10,000 people were affected by the floods there . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that more than doubled to 273 per cent on June 24 .
