<h> India has highest number of extremely thin children : World Hunger Index <h> Last Updated : Tuesday 11 September 2018 <p> Hunger has been on the rise over the past three years , returning to levels from a decade ago . Credit : Kumar Sambhav Srivastava/CSE <p> The prevalence of overweight children under 5 and prevalence of obesity in the population over 18 years is increasing in India , says the latest World Hunger Index . The prevalence of anemia among women between 15-49 years-old has also increased . At the same time , prevalence of exclusive breastfeeding among infants has increased . The report also pointed out that India has the highest number ( 25 per cent ) of school children suffering from extreme thinness . <p> The report urged countries to pay special attention to vulnerable groups like infant , children aged under five , school aged children , adolescent girls and women . <p> According to the report , The State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World 2018 " published by United Nation 's organization FAO , one in every nine persons are hungry . <p> The report shows the number of hungry @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ million in 2016 . It has shown a reversal trend in achieving Sustainable Development Goal to eradicate hunger by 2030 . <p> Progress is limited in addressing the multiple forms of malnutrition , ranging from child stunting to adult obesity , putting the health of hundreds of millions of people at risk . <p> Hunger has been on the rise over the past three years , returning to levels from a decade ago . This reversal in progress sends a clear warning that more must be done and urgently if the Sustainable Development Goal of Zero Hunger is to be achieved by 2030 . <p> Climate change perpetuates hunger The rise in hunger is also due to increasing climate variability in recent past . India has faced 6 times climate variability between 2011-2016 . The extreme weather climate lead to decreasing of yields , which further perpetuates hunger . <p> The annual UN report found that climate variability affecting rainfall patterns and agricultural seasons , and climate extremes such as droughts and floods , are among the key drivers behind the rise in hunger , together with conflict and economic @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ malnutrition Poor progress has been made in reducing child stunting , the report says , with nearly 151 million children aged under five too short for their age due to malnutrition in 2017 , compared to 165 million in 2012 . <p> Globally , Africa and Asia accounted for 39 per cent and 55 per cent of all stunted children , respectively . Prevalence of child wasting remains extremely high in Asia where almost one in 10 children under five has low weight for their height , compared to just one in 100 in Latin America and the Caribbean . <p> No region has shown a decline in anaemia among women of reproductive age , and the prevalence in Africa and Asia is nearly three times higher than in North America . Rates of exclusive breastfeeding in Africa and Asia are 1.5 times higher than those in North America where only 26 percent of infants under six months receive breastmilk exclusively . <p> Obesity on the rise Adult obesity is worsening , and more than one in eight adults in the world is obese .
