<h> World to need 10 bln-plus more cooling machines : Report <h> Last Updated : Friday 17 July 2020 <p> The world will need more than 10 billion new cooling appliances by 2050 , according to a recent estimate . That would take the total up to around 14 billion such machines in a warming world . <p> An estimated 3.6 billion appliances are currently in use . This number , however , is increasing by up to 10 cooling appliances every second , said the Cooling Emissions and Policy Synthesis Report : Benefits of cooling efficiency and the Kigali Amendment report released July 17 , 2020 . <p> At this rate , the world will have four times more or at least 14 billion cooling appliances by 2050 , contributing significantly to rising temperatures , the report by the UN Environment Programme ( UNEP ) and the International Energy Agency ( IEA ) pointed out . <p> This includes access to essential cooling services for over a billion people and maintaining the ' cold chain ' , a temperature-controlled supply chain necessary to ensure food safety , refrigeration for vaccines and ensuring comfort and productivity . <p> The @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a 15-member steering committee . <p> The committee was co-chaired by Nobel laureate Mario Molina , the president of Centro Mario Molina , Mexico , and Durwood Zaelke , the president of the Institute for Governance and Sustainable Development , United States . <p> Cooling appliances a double burden <p> Air conditioners ( AC ) are a double burden , said the report , citing the sector 's growth being responsible for increasing its contribution to greenhouse gas emissions , further warming the planet . <p> Most ACs use hydrofluorocarbons ( HFCs ) , extremely potent greenhouse gases and also need significant energy to run . Without policy intervention , direct and indirect emissions from air conditioning and refrigeration are projected to rise 90 per cent above 2017 levels by the year 2050 . <p> Coordinated international action on energy-efficient , climate-friendly cooling can , however , avoid as much as 460 billion tonnes of greenhouse gas emissions -- roughly equal to eight years of global emissions at 2018 levels -- over the next four decades , said the report . <p> Between 210 and 460 billion tonnes of carbon @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ actions to improve the cooling industry 's energy efficiency together with the transition to climate-friendly refrigerants , according to the report . <p> World gains by energy-efficient cooling <p> Doubling energy efficiency of air conditioners worldwide by 2050 can reduce the need for 1,300 gigawatts of additional electricity generation capacity to meet peak demand . This is equal to the entire coal-fired power generation capacity of China and India combined in 2018 . <p> The world will also save up to $2.9 trillion by 2050 in reduced electricity generation , transmission and distribution costs alone , according to IEA estimates . <p> " Getting cooling right " <p> Billions of ACs , refrigerators and air-conditioned vehicles that will be produced to meet the growing demand for cooling have not been designed or manufactured yet . <p> Most of the building stock where these appliances will be used is yet to be built or expected to be refurbished as well . <p> There is , thus , an opportunity to shift the future of cooling by changing the trajectory of technologies , solutions and behaviours that drive cooling demand and determine @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ be seen as an opportunity for a transition to climate-friendly and energy-efficient cooling . The world can , for example , avoid up to 85 per cent of global CO2 emissions between 2015 and 2050 . <p> Actions including policy response <p> Policy options that make cooling a part of climate and sustainable development solutions were suggested by the report . <p> The Kigali Amendment -- that targets a massive reduction in the production and use of climate-warming refrigerant gases , including HFCs -- was ratified by 100 countries as on July 14 , 2020 . <p> HFCs became widely used refrigerant substitutes for ozone-depleting substances that were phased out under the Montreal Protocol . <p> HFC is a potent greenhouse gas with a global warming potential ( GWP ) thousands of times that of CO2 in some cases @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ has a 100-year GWP of 1,810 , almost 2,000 times the potency of CO2 . <p> Most of this gas is consumed in the cooling sector , comprising refrigeration , air conditioning and heat pumps ( RACHP ) in mobile and stationary applications . <p> These sectors accounted for 86 per cent of the GWP-weighted share of global HFC consumption in 2012 .
