<p> As the COVID-19 pandemic unfolds , the dominant narrative in the climate change space is that ' nature is healing ' . This narrative has emerged from the decrease in air pollution across hotspots , and other climate wins . <p> While it is important to highlight this , as it demonstrates the impact of human activity on nature , it also leaves out key considerations about how economies will recover , and communities will cope with climate change . The financial impact from COVID-19 may , in the longer-term , leave millions more vulnerable to climate change . <p> Will climate change take a backseat during and after COVID-19 ? <p> As governments in India and across the world focus on containing COVID-19 , it is likely that climate change will be seen as a less relevant issue in the next few years . Governments may experience fatigue , which will reduce their political will and bandwidth to commit to climate action , and they may instead focus efforts on recovering economies by any means possible . <p> As per the United Nations ' Emission Gaps Report 2019 , we have already fallen behind the 2030 target @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Celsius and bring down global emissions . Millions are already grappling with the effects of the nationwide lockdown , as seen in the case of stranded migrant workers , the informal workforce , and the farmers . Given the urgent need to revive the economy , there is pressure on the government to lower environmental standards and reduce monitoring requirements . <p> Historically , disasters have led to manufacturing booms , reversing any climate gains . For developing nations such as India , focusing on manufacturing and extractive industries to recover the economy might be low-hanging fruit , especially given the global fall in oil and coal prices . A few countries , such as those in the European Union , are planning for a green recovery , or ' building back better ' , by being conscious of the climate in their COVID-19 recovery plans . Such a green recovery would be hinged upon clean or less polluting technologies , electric mobility and transport , and smart infrastructure as part of a longer-term stimulus for financial growth . <p> However , in India , where an Rs 20 lakh crore stimulus plan was announced , it is not clear whether climate change will be taken into consideration , as the package barely provides social protection to the country 's population of 1.3 billion .
