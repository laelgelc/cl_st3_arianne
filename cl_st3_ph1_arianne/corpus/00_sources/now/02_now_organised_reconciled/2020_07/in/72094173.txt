<h> Nano technology enables scientists to study migrations of Pied Cuckoo <h> Last Updated : Thursday 16 July 2020 <p> A new project by the Wildlife Institute of India and the Indian Institute of Remote Sensing has used nano technology to track the enigmatic Pied Cuckoo . Photo : Mohammad Shakur <p> A new project by a number of agencies is using advancements in nano technology to study migratory patterns of the Pied Cuckoo , a bird closely linked with the arrival of the south west monsoon in India . <p> The project is a joint effort by the Wildlife Institute of India , Dehradun and the Indian Institute of Remote Sensing ( IIRS ) , which comes under the Indian Space Research Organisation or ISRO . <p> " There are basically three subspecies of the Pied Cuckoo of which one is resident in Africa while another is resident in South India . The third is a migrant moving between India and Africa . It moves to India during the summer , " Suresh Kumar , a scientist in the Department of Endangered Species Management , WII , told Down To Earth . <p> " Being a small , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ risk for this cuckoo . Before it migrates back to its home in the southern African region , by flying over the Arabian Sea and the Indian Ocean , it must be stopping somewhere . It is these stopovers that we want to find out about , " he said . <p> In the long term , the scientists hope to find out how the birds respond to climatic variation . <p> The Pied Cuckoo is famous in North Indian folklore as ' chatak ' , a bird that quenches its thirst only with raindrops . From Southern Africa , it comes to the Himalayan foothills stretching from Jammu to Assam to breed every year . The birds come to the same localities every year . <p> It is also a brood parasite in that it does not make its own nest and instead lays its egg in the nest of other birds , particularly the Jungle Babbler . <p> In 2019 , the team had banded a Pied Cuckoo on the outskirts of Dehradun to check whether it would return to the same site the following year .
