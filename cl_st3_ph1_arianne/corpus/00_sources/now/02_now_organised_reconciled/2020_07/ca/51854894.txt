One major strategy being pursued around the world to reduce the carbon footprint of transportation is electrification : replacing vehicles that operate on conventional gas or diesel fuel with alternatives powered 100 per cent by electricity . In tandem with ongoing efforts to increase the proportion of renewables in our electricity mix ( currently at 30 per cent year-to-date ) , the transition to electric vehicles will support significant carbon reductions . <p> Right now , there 's a rare opportunity for Nova Scotia to take significant steps to decarbonize the largest transit fleet in Atlantic Canada , and at the same time to stimulate a clean economic recovery in a post-COVID world . <p> The Halifax Transit plan would take the better part of a decade to complete and cost an estimated $710 million to $782 million . <p> Halifax would bear just over one-quarter of the cost and needs support from both the governments @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . That 's a lot of money , and it 's fair to ask what the return is . <p> For a start , the Halifax Transit proposal envisions major and much-needed retrofits to its two major transit centres at Ragged Lake and Burnside to support the charging of the e-bus fleet that would provide opportunities for local employment and contracts . Then there 's the projected cost savings for Halifax Transit for the charging and maintenance of e-buses , which has been estimated to net Halifax Transit $6 million per year . <p> There are also non-economic benefits . There 's GHG emissions reductions , of course . The electrification of half of Halifax Transit 's fleet will result in a reduction of 222,600 to 264,600 tonnes of GHGs over a 20-year period . And like all electric vehicles , e-buses have no tailpipe emissions , meaning there 's no noxious chemicals like carbon monoxide and particulates to wind up in our lungs . <p> The service improvements envisioned by the bus rapid transit plan will further support emissions reductions .
