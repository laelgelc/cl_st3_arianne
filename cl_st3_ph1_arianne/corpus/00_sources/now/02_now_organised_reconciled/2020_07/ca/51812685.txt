<h> Market News <h> EU 's Michel offers please-all recovery plan ahead of tense summit <p> BRUSSELS ( Reuters ) - European Council President Charles Michel on Friday offered concessions to countries across the EU on their long-term budget and economic stimulus plan in a bid to bridge gaps before national leaders haggle next week over coronavirus recovery . <p> With the EU economies in free fall due to the pandemic , Michel will chair the first face-to-face talks of the 27 European Union heads since lockdowns took hold in March and feuds over how to tackle coronavirus divided the bloc . <p> " The COVID-19 crisis presents Europe with a challenge of historic proportions , " Michel said . " We are slowly exiting the acute health crisis ... the emphasis is now shifting to mitigating the socio-economic damage . " <p> Hoping to bridge divisions over how to revive economic growth between the wealthy , thrifty north and the high-debt south hit harder by COVID-19 , he proposed a smaller next joint EU budget for 2021-27 than previously envisaged , an attempt to make the mass stimulus scheme more palatable to the frugals . <p> He proposed @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ recovery fund of 750 billion euros for pandemic-hammered economies , with two-thirds in free grants and a third in repayable loans . <p> The COVID-19 pandemic is the latest big challenge for the EU after it struggled with a debt crisis a decade ago , chaotic mass immigration , and then the trauma of Brexit . <p> Some have even framed it as existential for the bloc , saying it can not be seen to fail this time as eurosceptic feeling mounts in countries such as Italy . <p> Michel proposed maintaining the so-called rebates that wealthier countries receive on their budget contributions and , in a couched reference to Poland , Hungary and others , said funding would be conditional on respect for the rule of law . <p> Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orban -
