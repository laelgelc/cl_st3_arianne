<h> LETTER : Lack of support hurting municipalities , says Cape Breton MLA <p> COVID-19 has revealed many gaps in the services and supports needed in our communities . Some of these services rely on strong municipal governments . <p> Municipalities are a critical frontline provider of services to Nova Scotians , and their importance will be heightened in the coming months and years . Public transit , recreational programs , climate change mitigation and planning and development support are some of the municipal functions that our province will need operating fully as we navigate the economic recovery from COVID-19 . <p> We have heard from municipalities that they are facing significant revenue shortfalls as a result of the pandemic . And , unfortunately , the level of provincial support being made available to municipalities during the COVID-19 pandemic and recovery period has been seriously lacking . <p> Municipalities can not be forced into substantial budget cuts and service clawbacks . Not only does it not make economic sense to cut staff and needed services in a downturn , we will need municipalities operating at full tilt to help steward our way through the recovery period . <p> Partnering @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ground . For example , the Nova Scotia Federation of Municipalities has asked for the ability to provide targeted commercial tax support to small businesses , but this proposal was rebuffed by the Liberal government . Municipalities need a provincial partner who will sit down with them and work together - not a unilateral decision maker . <p> The time is overdue to address municipal funding mechanisms in order to ensure that Nova Scotia 's municipalities are adequately supported . Like so many issues , COVID-19 is exposing what we have long known - in this case , that our municipalities need more robust support from the province .
