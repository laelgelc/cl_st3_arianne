<h> University of Regina researchers find that " Ghost Fleas " in some Prairie lakes contribute to high levels of methylmercury in fish <p> REGINA , SK , July 21 , 2020 /CNW/ - Dr. Britt Hall , a biology professor in the Faculty of Science at the University of Regina , has lead a study that helps to answer a decades-old question -- why do fish in southern Saskatchewan 's Katepwa Lake contain such high levels of methylmercury ? After years of research and around-the-clock water sampling , Hall 's study found that the high levels of methylmercury are ultimately caused by large water fleas called Leptodora , or " Ghost fleas " . <p> Hall , whose research focuses on how mercury moves in the environment , worked with data that Dr. Peter Leavitt , a professor of limnology at the U of R , began collecting 23 years ago . " But in Katepwa Lake , the data was showing that methylmercury concentrations are actually quite high and we did n't know why that was . " <p> Hall 's study found that the Leptodora , a large water flea , migrates up from the mercury-rich sediments at the bottom of the lake at night to feed near the surface of the water , transferring the methylmercury to fish when they are eaten . They carry mercury in a way that is similar to an elevator carrying people from ground level to their offices or apartments on upper floors . This feeding boosts the amount of toxin in the fish , which are in turn eaten by larger and larger fish , resulting in a bioaccumulation of mercury when humans consume the fish . <p> " Leptodora are very interesting creatures , " explains Hall . When he began to study the methylmercury levels in Katepwa Lake , one question remained unanswered -- how do fish hunt these ghost fleas at night if they are so transparent ? <p> Dr. Richard Vogt , a former post-doctoral student at the University of Regina , conducted an experiment that determined the fish in Katepwa Lake have a way of hunting without being able to see their prey . The fish can sense the pressure waves the Leptodora make as they swim through the water , which allows the fish to target them at night .
