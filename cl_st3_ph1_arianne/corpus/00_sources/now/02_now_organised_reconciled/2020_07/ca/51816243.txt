Shaughn Butts/Postmedia <p> Ah , but what my generation did , the elderly woman replies , was recycle milk bottles , use cloth diapers and clothesline . What we did was watch our one television while wearing hand-me-downs . What we did was mail packages without bubble wrap , drink water from the tap , cook unprocessed food instead of getting takeout , and ride a bicycle to school . <p> What we did was manage without fast food , fast fashion , fast cars , cellphones and every other imaginable industrialized convenience and electronic diversion that created the disposable lives we lead today . <p> Oh , and while we 're at it , we would have been taken out behind the wood shed if we had littered . <p> The older woman had , in fact , lived a life of green @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ shared much of that women 's " green " history -- the cloth diapers , paper not plastic , tap not bottled . And then , like millions of others maturing into adulthood , I fell headlong under the magic spell of Madison Avenue , where postwar optimism and cheap overseas labour heralded a new age of rampant consumerism . <p> Soon , my First World life was awash in excess . So much plastic . So many possessions . So . Much .
