Pexels <p> You may have missed it , but an interesting evolution happened here in Canada over the past two months : the country 's clean energy sector , which employs 298,000 Canadians , rallied together as it has never rallied before . <p> Canada 's clean energy sector has long been especially quiet compared to its fossil fuel counterpart . Unlike in oil and gas , clean energy jobs are not regionally centralized , most companies are relatively small , and industry associations are numerous . Wind power , solar power , electric vehicles , biofuels , cleantech , hydro : there 's an industry association for each @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the fundamental strengths of clean energy -- just how diverse it is . And on the other hand , this same fragmentation is why the sector struggles to make itself heard . <p> But that changed this year . In the wake of COVID-19 lockdowns , over 360 signatories representing more than 2,100 companies -- mostly in the clean energy sector -- came together around a shared vision for Canada 's economic recovery . They asked for clean stimulus measures and a commitment to maintain and build on Canada 's climate efforts , key ingredients for a resilient recovery . <p> In short , the sector found its voice . And you should hear them out , because their argument may be more compelling than you think . While the idea of a clean ( or green ) recovery has made the news rounds in recent weeks , less covered were a number of new reports that together draw the same conclusion -- that clean stimulus is n't idealistic , it 's good economic policy . <p> For starters , consider that over the first four months of 2020 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ while fossil fuel stocks plummeted 40.5 per cent . Compared to funding for fossil fuels , clean stimulus creates nearly three times as many jobs for every $10 million invested by governments , according to McKinsey . Another recent study from U.S. and U.K. economists found that clean stimulus policies have historically been the most cost-effective way to jumpstart economies -- and would likely be so now . <p> Clean Energy Canada 's own research has estimated that , here in Canada , where 50,000 jobs could be lost in fossil fuels over the next decade , over 160,000 will be created in clean energy .
