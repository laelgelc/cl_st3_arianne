<h> Farmer advocates reintroduction of earthen dams to transform lives <p> The farmer wants Ghana to learn from India 's transformation with earthen dams <p> A farmer at Awutu Bereku in the Central region says the reconstruction of traditional earthen dams will help transform the lives of thousands of villagers in Ghana 's most arid regions and even across Africa . <p> Osman Moro Fukuyama says if the fast disappearing traditional technology has helped transform the lives of thousands of villagers in one of India 's most arid regions , there is no point in Ghana abandoning it . <p> He said environmental risks including natural disasters and inconsistent rainfall are high adding to the insufficient access to climate smart technologies , adaptive services remains a key barrier to the development of agriculture in Ghana . <p> Mr Fukuyama who is a fish and vegetable farmer said this in response to questions about the realities of climate change and the threat of desertification creeping at an estimated 20,000 hectares per year in Ghana . <p> The United Nations Development Programme ( UNDP ) in its 2019 report revealed that 44 per cent of Ghanaians are unaware of climate change @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ making our voices heard in climate action , policymakers and governments in Africa must learn from India 's Rajendra Singh who with the help of community leaders in Gopalpura and Bsikaipura . <p> " He has brought back water to over 1,000 villages and more than 21 irrigation systems to provide water for smallholder farmers through traditional earthen dams . This is something we must learn from , " he said . <p> On his part , coordinator for Tarun Bharat Sangh , an NGO in India , Suresh Raikwai explained that after their first success in Gopalpura ; the idea spread like fire in the parched villages in 2009 . <p> " Village people started approaching us to do the same in their communities ; the impact of these traditional earthen dams was spectacular . <p> " The water table ; the depth underground from where water may be extracted rose from about 100 metres to about 13 metres and 3 metres . <p> " The area under single cropping increased from 11% to 70% , and the area under double cropping went from 3% to 50% . And for water , these days ' women need to walk no farther than the village well . <p> Mr Raikwai believes that the earthen dam can help reduce the effects of desertification and land restoration across the African continent . <p> " It 's not that there is no enough money in Africa , maybe governments alone ca n't do it ; we are fast running out of time to build our resilience to climate change " he whispered .
