<h> Govt quietly abandons electric vehicle target <p> The Government has quietly backed away from a pledge to make its fleet emissions-free by mid-2025 . The goal was part of the Labour-NZ First coalition agreement , Marc Daalder reports . <p> There are 15,473 vehicles in the government fleet and only 78 are electric . When the coalition Government came into power in late 2017 , the agreement between Labour and New Zealand First stipulated that the entire fleet would be emissions-free by mid-2025 , " where practicable " . <p> Although it was repeated as recently as June , that goal has been quietly revised to a commitment that , after mid-2025 , all new vehicles entering the fleet will be emissions-free . <p> Greenpeace climate and energy campaigner Amanda Larsson criticised the decision . " The Government 's quiet back down on electrifying its vehicle fleet is surprising and incredibly disappointing , " she said . <p> " This is a climate emergency and the Government should be leading by example to cut our dependence on the dirty fuels driving this crisis . The Government 's progress so far has been abysmal . <p> Out of around 15,000 vehicles , less than half of a percent of the Government 's fleet is electric . This has been the case for at least the past nine months , according to data from the Ministry of Business , Innovation and Employment . <p> Electric vehicles have trickled into the fleet at a snail 's pace . In the third quarter of the 2018/2019 financial year , there were 71 electric vehicles . The next quarter , that number rose to 73 and has now reached 78 . <p> In the most recent quarter , in addition to adding five electric vehicles , the Government added a net of 514 non-electric vehicles . <p> Were the Government to have attempted to maintain the same number of vehicles in the fleet , it would have had to cycle in 226 electric cars every month beginning next month in order to meet its target . <p> Under National , the government committed to making a third of its fleet electric or hybrid @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in the fleet - 514 in the most recent quarter - this short deadline would have been equally difficult to achieve . <p> If the fleet were to remain the same size , the Government would have had to replace 326 fossil fuel vehicles with hybrids or electrics every single month between November 2019 and December 2020 . <p> Plan was still feasible <p> Jeff Vickers is the technical director of Thinkstep Australasia , a sustainable policy shop and consultancy firm .
