<h> KCB to go fully green , readies to sow 80,000 saplings across areas <p> The saplings are being propagated at the KCB office by its own staff , as well as at the residence of the new CEO , whose brainchild this whole initiative is ; PIC : NIKHIL GHORPADE <p> Cantt board is nurturing these plants after collecting seeds and stems from under its own limits ; plan is to increase green cover and beautify jurisdiction <p> Amid increasingly urgent calls to boost green cover across all urban zones in times of climate change , Khadki Cantonment Board ( KCB ) has undertaken a first-of-its-kind initiative to make its entire jurisdiction go green , with over 80,000 plants of 30 species set to be sown across KCB limits . <p> Interestingly , the financially dithering board will not spend a penny on buying these plants , instead collecting seeds and stems from trees that are strewn around the cantonment at present , and growing them till they can be replanted . Till date , the board has propagated some 30,000 banyan saplings and over 50,000 of other species like mango , peepal or bodhi , wild tamarind @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and more . These will also be used to beautify areas administered by the board . <p> This drive is the brainchild of KCB chief executive officer ( CEO ) Pramod Kumar Singh , who shared , " KCB staff was told to collect seeds found in different areas of the board , which they are propagating at the board office premises and my residence . The most number among these is of the banyan species , as they have a minimum life span of 200 years and the climate of Maharashtra is perfect for them . " <p> KCB garden superintendent BS Naik informed , " We have also collected a huge number of seeds and made seed balls . These will be used in different areas to boost green cover . The number of seeds is in lakhs and even if thousands survive , it will make a huge difference . Our plants are being grown completely naturally and once they turn the desired age , we will start planting them . Spaces for the same are already being identified .
