<h> Nilgiris tribals allege they were duped of compensation <h> Last Updated : Tuesday 15 October 2019 <p> It 's a case of lies , deception and dubious land deals . And communities living in the pristine Nilgiris district of Tamil Nadu are at the receiving end of it all . Fifty-something S Kannan , for instance , who belongs to the Paniya tribe , used to live in Pulliyalam village set deep inside the district 's Mudumalai Tiger Reserve ( MTR ) till September 2018 . <p> But fraudulent activities by the officials responsible for a relocation programme from the reserve 's core area have left him squatting on a tiny 14 square metres ( sq m ) patch of land outside the forest which he can never own . <p> As the first instalment , the state government paid Kannan Rs 4 lakh to leave his ancestral home inside MTR to create inviolate space for tigers and elephants . But land brokers , in connivance with the forest range officer and an advocate , extracted the amount from Kannan by fraudulently selling him poramboke land in Gudalur block . <p> Poramboke land is common land that can @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ tell me this was not their private property , " he says . <p> Kannan is not the only one cheated . As many as 21 tribal people , looking forward to the relocation programme that would have finally given them land ownership , have suffered a serious setback . Collectively , they have been swindled of Rs 2 crore . They filed a first information report ( FIR ) on September 3 , this year , in which Kannan is the main complainant . <p> But Adivasi Munnetra Sangam , a tribal-led non-profit working in Gudalur , believes the scam has a much bigger magnitude . In January this year , it sent a letter to the National Commission for Scheduled Castes stating that 93 families have been duped of Rs 6.7 lakh each . <p> It all began in October 2016 after the relocation programme took off in MTR in three phases . The first phase , completed in 2016-17 , involved relocation of 58 tribal and 177 non-tribal families from Bennai and Nellikarai villages . <p> This was followed by the relocation of 55 tribal and 200 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 2017-18 . The last phase , that should get over in 2019 , involves 25 tribal and 186 non-tribal families from Mudugulli and Gundital villages . <p> According to the 2008 relocation plan of the National Tiger Conservation Authority , people can choose between Option I ( money for land ) and Option II ( land for land ) as compensation for their own land . <p> The Chetti community , living in the core area as landowners , chose Option II , and has been rehabilitated in Aiyankolli village . But people like Kannan , who did not have land titles , were " given " Option I. This meant each family would get a total of Rs 10 lakh in three instalments . <p> Lawyer C Sukumaran and Nellakottai range officer Suresh Kumar , who are on the district-level committee ( DLC ) in charge of relocating people , swung into action on June 9 , 2018 , when the Union Ministry of Environment , Forest and Climate Change sanctioned Rs 25.5 crore for the scheme . He then showed poramboke lands at Machhi-kolli and Patavail villages in the fringe areas of MTR . <p> This was their strategy . " The brokers would visit the village , take four to five people along and show them the land . Kumar and Sukumaran would also take people there in forest department vehicles . They never told us that it was government land that we could not buy , " says Kannan . <p> He decided to buy 14 sq m in Machhikolli because it has road connectivity .
