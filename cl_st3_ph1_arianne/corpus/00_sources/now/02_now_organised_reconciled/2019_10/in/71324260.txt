<h> What if not single-use plastics ? Why not leaf cutlery ? <h> Last Updated : Tuesday 01 October 2019 <p> Prime Minister Narendra Modi 's exhortation to Indians to ban single-use plastics has once again put the spotlight on traditional Indian leaf cutlery as a possible alternative . <p> The prime minister recently talked about the menace of single-use plastics in his radio programme , Mann Ki Baat and asked citizens to participate in putting them off use . <p> Approximately 22,000 metric tonnes plastic waste is generated every month by all the food delivery aggregators in India combined . A proactive step by these companies to adopt leaf-based utensils will be a huge step towards decreasing and ultimately , finishing plastic waste . <p> Chitta Ranjan Pani , a tribal livelihood activist told Down To Earth that urban Indian households should discard the usage of their waste-producing plastic utensils and adopt leaf-based cutlery , which is a zero-emission product . <p> A shift to biodegradable cutlery would enrich the soil , reduce carbon footprints , arrest deforestation and hence diminish the impact of climate change . <p> The biggest advantage of using leaf cutlery however , would @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , who have traditionally engaged in manufacturing it . <p> If the ban , which the prime minister talks about , comes through , it may prove to be a boon for the forest-based leaf cutlery industry of India . <p> Adivasis and leaf cutlery <p> Indian tribals or adivasis , who constitute just 8.6 per cent of India 's population , account for about one-fourth of the population in the poorest wealth quintile , according to the World Bank . <p> More than one -- third of Scheduled Tribes and Castes under five years of age were underweight , compared to 215 in the general population , a 2017 study by the National Institute of Nutrition , Hyderabad , showed . <p> A ban on single-use plastics might result in the usage of leaf plates and spoons , which provide livelihoods for nearly five million adivasis in Odisha alone . <p> The leaf cutlery industry in India is scattered . If given an impetus , it is very much possible to develop this market for alternative cutlery , " Pani said . <p> According to him , the market for leaf-based cutlery in India is worth Rs 2,000 crore . The forest region of Betnoti in Odisha alone produces Rs 400 crore worth of sal leaf cutlery . <p> A large-scale substitution form plastic-based utensils to plant-based cutlery can be a game-changer for forest dwellers . According to media reports , in some districts , tribal women have already started forming federations for better trade deals . <p> In addition to the domestic market , Indian leaf-based cutlery is in huge demand in international markets as well . <p> A German startup , Leaf Republic , imports leaf plates from its registered Indian company , Bilotech and processes it further before it enters the German markets . The imposition of this dual financial burden had negatively affected the sal leaf trade . <p> At present , both these economic obligations are eliminated . Siali leaves too came under the GST bracket of 18 per cent , which was brought down to 5 per cent and is presently Nil . <p> Ahead of the 2019 Odisha elections , the state government had retracted the royalties on sal leaves on January 21 , 2019 . <p> The government now offers a minimum support price ( MSP ) for sal leaves , to the tune of Rs 30 per kilogram .
