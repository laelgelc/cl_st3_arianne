This possibility is what inspires my recent work with colleague Beth Osnes to get messages out about climate change through comedy and humour . <p> I have studied and practiced climate communication for about 20 years . My new book , " Creative ( Climate ) Communications , " integrates social science and humanities research and practices to connect people more effectively through issues they care about . It temporarily suspends social rules and connects people with ideas and new ways of thinking or acting . <p> Comedy exploits cracks in arguments . It wiggles in , pokes , prods and draws attention to the incongruous , hypocritical , false and pretentious . It can make the complex dimensions of climate change seem more accessible and its challenges seem more manageable . <p> Many disciplines can inform comedy , including theatre , performance and media studies .
