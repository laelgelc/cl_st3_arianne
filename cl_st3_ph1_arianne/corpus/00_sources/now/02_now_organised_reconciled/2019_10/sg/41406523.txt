<h> Indigenous leadership delivers natural climate solutions <h> Indigenous communities , like the Sungai Utik Dayak Iban Longhouse community in Indonesia , are guardians of the land on which they reside . Across the world , there is growing recognition of their work towards protecting the environment and mitigating climate change . <p> One of the last remaining indigenous tribes living in the rainforests of Kalimantan received the United Nations Equator Prize for their work in protecting and preserving the forest . Image : Mike Arrison , UNDP <h> Share this story <p> It 's often said that indigenous people are the best stewards of the forest . Here 's the proof . <p> During the recent United Nations General Assembly Climate Summit in New York , United States , global leaders focused on natural climate solutions -- maintaining and restoring natural areas like forests and wetlands . Studies estimate that natural climate solutions can help reduce carbon emissions and store carbon in the landscape , delivering 37 per cent of the emissions reductions the globe needs to stop dangerous climate change . <p> Indigenous leadership is a critical nature-based solution . Therefore , indigenous land rights are essential to mitigate climate change . <p> The prestigious Equator Prize rewards indigenous groups that are managing their lands and waters in a climate friendly way . This year 22 groups from 18 countries were awarded the prestigious prize and invited to an award ceremony in New York . Indonesia was proudly represented by the Sungai Utik Dayak Iban longhouse community from West Kalimantan . <p> " The land is our mother , the forest is our father and the water is our blood " said Apai Janggut , customary leader of Sungai Utik longhouse .
