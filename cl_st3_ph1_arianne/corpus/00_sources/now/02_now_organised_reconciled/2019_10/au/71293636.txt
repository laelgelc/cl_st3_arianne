<p> In the wake of Greta Thunberg 's powerful and damning United Nations speech , Prime Minister Scott Morrison has rebuked her and the student strikes she began . <p> The PM stated that he does n't want children to feel " needless anxiety " andwishes to protect our children from the harmful effects of climate grief . But this kind of rhetoric seems to have the opposite effect . <h> WHAT DO THE EXPERTS SAY ? <p> In the 2017 article , ' Raising Children to thrive in a Climate Changed World ' , the Australian Psychological Society ( APS ) stated that the best way to ' handle anxiety is action ' . Our children are starting to learn that they have to be the enactors of change and that but by striking , they allow themselves to feel less helpless . <p> The article goes on to say : <p> This generation of children will need to adapt to faster and more wide-ranging changes than we have ever seen before ... our children -- the next generation -- will need to keep working so they can help to restore a safe climate , and to adapt @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ future , the APS recommends that parents should teach their children a series of life skills . This includes personal self-regulation , social justice and flexibility and adaptability skills . The examples given were the recycling of plastics and having discussions about how our lifestyles will be affected . <p> The APS recommends teaching children about climate change at a very young age . It also recommends they be involved in climate action such as recycling scraps from as young as primary school . It 's also important , the article states , to have our children engage in civic duties such as volunteering , writing letters to our members of parliament and you guessed it -- protesting . <h> WHAT ARE REAL PEOPLE DOING ? <p> Brisbane resident and bee enthusiast Ms Wemyss uses her four native beehives to teach her children , not only about the complex systems in nature but also the possible effects of climate change . <p> Ms Wemyss says : <p> We currently have four native beehives . They are incredibly important to us , as I feel they teach caring without reward , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ nature is connected . The boys love to watch them forage in our garden and return with pollen to their hives . Having bees opens discussion on deforestation , ecosystems and the importance of doing what we can to help support our local area . <p> For her , it is important that her children are aware of what their futures have in store . <p> I tell them what is happening but in ways that they can understand . We talk about why we are noticing less rain ; we talk about why the coral is bleaching ; we talk about carbon emissions and fracking . We discuss clean energy and why looking after the Earth is important . They have a lifetime to be afraid , so I would love them to have a childhood of fun and laughter but also ensure they learn what is necessary to take action -- and to teach other as they grow older . <p> When prompted about the possible anxieties they might face Wemyss had this to say : <p> Creating panic in small children will achieve nothing but fear , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ come as they see the extent of what is happening . I feel allowing my children to be unaware of what is happening would be negligent on my part . The main way I can help my children is through education . How to stay calm and make effective plans . How to be a leader and how to take steps to help secure our future . <p> The Prime Minister 's comments dismiss what real Australians are doing to help fight against the anxieties of climate change . <p> His Morrison 's attitude to the kids was dismissive and patronising . What he ignores is that many of their leaders -- and indeed most of the children -- understand what is happening to their world . They know the physics , chemistry and biology that underpins that understanding .
