In his recent book How Democracy Ends , David Runciman argues that democracy is in fact at its most powerful when it serves as an end in itself . Historically , democracy has derived energy and sustenance from its own promise of expansion , renewing itself by opening ever-widening circles of participation . Democratic participation was a mark of acceptance and dignity . However , Runciman suggests , we may now have reached a stalemate . With no clear potential for further expansion , democracy may have become backward-looking , appealing more and more for its legitimacy to past successes rather than future possibilities .
