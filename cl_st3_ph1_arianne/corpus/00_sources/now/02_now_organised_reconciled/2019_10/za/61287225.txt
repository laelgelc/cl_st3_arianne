<h> Doctor Doom #1 - Review <h> Doom is a man of contradictions . <p> It 's honestly surprising Marvel has n't attempted more solo Doctor Doom comics over the years . He 's the only real competition to Magneto when it comes to best Marvel villain . Those few Doom comics Marvel has published ( Books of Doom , Infamous Iron Man ) have been very enjoyable . This new Doctor Doom series reads like a modest but very welcome attempt at making up for lost time . It 's hardly a flawless debut for the good Doctor , but still one well worth reading . <p> With Doctor Doom #1 , writer Christopher Cantwell and artist Salvador Larroca loosely build on the status quo established in Dan Slott 's Fantastic Four run . Compared to Infamous Iron Man , which focused on a heroic Victor von Doom unafraid to show his face to the world , this is a far more traditional take on the character . That makes for an accessible debut issue , if nothing else . <p> In terms of plot and tone , issue #1 is all over the map . The series mostly hinges on the creation of a new climate change-reversing device that Doom alone recognizes as a clear and present danger to humanity . But that plot point recedes to the background after a certain point , with the issue shifting instead to unrest in and around Latveria 's borders . A sudden , unexplained appearances by another major Marvel villain further muddies the waters . It 's not entirely clear what kind of book Cantwell and Larroca are trying to create here . Is it a social satire or a political drama ? Is it grounded in real-world trappings or embracing the zaniest elements of the Marvel U ? None of that is clear after reading this messy first issue . <p> For now , strong characterization is Doctor Doom 's saving grace .
