<p> I used to think of myself as progressive , ready to defend any left-leaning cause , from social justice and unionization to climate justice and animal rights . Lately , though , I have become worried by the reckless self-sabotage I see among people who hold such views . <p> I 'm a researcher and I have long studied processes of individual and community resilience . These days , I 'm wondering why the political left is doing everything it can to destroy its own future . <p> Four years ago , we replaced a very conservative government that threatened to roll back women 's rights , put Indigenous activists on CSIS watch lists , muzzled climate scientists and did everything it could to thwart social justice , including changing the national census to make populations they wanted to ignore invisible . <p> We voted for a change in 2015 and we got change . Suddenly , Canada roared back onto the international stage as a credible alternative to our southern neighbour and its right-wing politics . <p> We were winning -- or so it seemed , for one delicious moment . <p> If I was in government @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ I 'd likely be saying : " Why did we bother to try ? " As a country , we made an earnest effort to be more progressive , more environmentally just and lead the world on human rights , but we wo n't get there if we become our own worst enemy . <p> Despite the embarrassment of defending SNC-Lavalin , wearing " brownface " and other missteps , Justin Trudeau at the head of our current federal government has advanced women 's rights with extended parental leave and made sincere efforts to bring more women into politics . <p> There have also been Herculean efforts to advance reconciliation with our Indigenous peoples and to investigate the national tragedy of missing and murdered women from those same communities ( though the results have , as expected , satisfied no one , the issues being too complex to solve with a single gesture ) . <p> There have also been genuine efforts to address climate change , even ( remarkably ) negotiating a plan for a national carbon tax that included Alberta . But none of this has been enough @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ only one way to be in the world : as the fierce opposition . <p> It 's a voice we need when we are out of power , when we are not being heard , but it risks igniting the right and pushing political moderates away from the " nothing is ever good enough " left . <p> I make no excuses for the ploddingly slow progress we are witnessing on so many important issues , or the compromises that have had to be made in the name of national unity , but are we on the left experiencing a case of collective amnesia ? <p> If we think back to before Oct. 19 , 2015 , we should experience a bone-chilling shudder . As a scientist , I do . And yet , we are turning our backs , literally , on people who are actually taking steps to fix complex problems . <p> Consider pipelines . Those who oppose them are playing a chess game one move at a time , when what we need is a chessmaster who can see a dozen moves into the future . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Montreal think that delaying the building of pipelines would result in a domino effect : the return of conservative governments now sweeping the country , the fracturing of the national accord on carbon pricing , and increased and far more dangerous shipments of oil by rail ? <p> These are the real threats to our global and local climate . Not to mention a weakened central government , a threatened federation and an underperforming economy at a time when we need investment in diversity and to move away from carbon-intensive industries . <p> A game played with a longer perspective would have involved agreeing to export oil , but tax it heavily and use that income to invest in green technology to position Canada as the next great thing . <p> That title will now slip to China in just a few short years . A better chess player would have negotiated ( there is an old-fashioned word ! ) with Alberta and given the province its pipeline , but balanced that with efforts to make oil obsolete . <p> Other countries , like Norway , are managing this sleight @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ make every Canadian eligible for a 30 per cent rebate on the purchase of a zero-emission vehicle ( with 65 per cent of those vehicles ' parts built in Canada ) ? Maybe some of the oil revenue could have been used to retool the GM plant now being partially shuttered in Oshawa to build electric buses , millions of which are being manufactured in China while only a paltry few are assembled in the G7 . <p> What about we take a pause and realize , as Joni Mitchell reminds us , " That you do n't know what you 've got till it 's gone . " Right now , my prediction is the left 's toehold on power will be gone this October , and we wo n't have anyone to blame but ourselves . <p> Michael Ungar , PhD , is the Canada research chair in Child , Family and Community Resilience at Dalhousie University , and author of Change Your World : The Science of Resilience and the True Path to Success ( Sutherland House , 2019 )
