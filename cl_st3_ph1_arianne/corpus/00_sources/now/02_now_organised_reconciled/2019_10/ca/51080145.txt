<p> The federal candidates in Nipissing-Timiskaming will take part in a roundtable discussion on the climate Sunday as part of a national campaign called 100 Debates on the Environment . <p> The event will be held from 4 to 6 p.m. at Coworking176 on Lakeshore Drive and include questions posed by community members and a chance for open questions from the floor . <p> Coordinated by GreenPAC and Equiterre , 100 Debates on the Environment is meant to be a non-partisan event , with similar forums taking place across the country . <p> The campaigns for Liberal incumbent Anthony Rota , New Democrat Rob Boulet and People 's Party candidate Mark King all confirmed that their candidates will be attending Sunday 's event . <p> Green Party candidate Alexander Gomm also told The Nugget he will be taking part . <p> As of Wednesday afternoon , Conservative candidate Jordy Carr had not confirmed to the paper whether she would be present . <p> " We 're really interested in the conversation more than a debate , more than point scoring , " said Brennain Lloyd , project co-ordinator for the environmental group Northwatch , which is leading the event @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Oct. 7 , but the date had to be changed due to a scheduling conflict with the national federal leaders debate . <p> Lloyd said Northwatch has done non-partisan work in elections for many years , but is interested in making this election period a time for a real examination of the issues , the candidates ' positions and policy . <p> " We really want a conversation , we want the people who come out for the event to really be able to get a sense of how the candidates are thinking , how fixed is their thinking ... so this is what we 're going to try . " <p> The roundtable follows a two-hour televised debate Tuesday on YourTV and a series of marches inspired by Swedish teenager Greta Thunberg that took place across the country Friday calling for greater action on climate change . <p> During the march in North Bay , each of the federal candidates was asked to commit to a series of expectations if elected , including making the global climate crisis their top priority , ensuring parliament honours Canada 's commitment to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to no more than 1.5 C , and committing to end all fossil fuel subsidies and retraining workers in the fossil fuel industry . <p> Organizers of the march say as many as 382 people were involved . <p> A climate rally also took place in North Bay Sept. 20 , and last week , climate change proved to be the central theme at a candidates ' debate hosted at Nipissing University . <p> The most recent rallies and focused attention on the climate this election come in light of a study last month from the Angus Reid Institute , which found that 69 per cent of Canadians believe climate change should be a top priority for whichever party forms government . <p> The same report also showed that 58 per cent of Canadians say oil and gas development should be a top priority alongside climate action .
