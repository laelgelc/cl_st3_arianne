The Paul Martin Liberal government announced the $5-billion Kelowna accord , which covered education , health services , housing , economic development and water on reserves . A Human Rights Watch article explains that some First Nations ' water is " contaminated , hard to access , or at risk due to faulty treatment systems " across the country . <p> So what gets in the way of fixes getting done ? <p> Unlike every province and territory in Canada , no specific water legislation exists on exactly what the federal government is supposed to deliver , nor which communities are eligible . <p> There is no consistency to how water problems are dealt with from reserve to reserve , nor are there binding regulations on water quality . <p> When problems arise , it 's unclear whether First Nations leaders or the federal government should be held @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ funding is doled out in yearly contracts , making the back and forth paperwork an administrative burden for resource-strapped reserves . The funding mechanism is also problematic . The federal government covers about 80 per cent of operations and maintenance , and First Nations are expected to pick up the remaining 20 . However , most First Nations have insufficient revenue , which the federal government does not take into account . As a result , repairs are often delayed or reduced . <p> " What happens if there is negligence on the part of the contractor ? Who has the authority ? Who signs off on inspections ?
