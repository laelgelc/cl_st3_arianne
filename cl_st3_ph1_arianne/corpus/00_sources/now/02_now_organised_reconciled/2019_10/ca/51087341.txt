Melissa Renwick/Toronto Star <p> It 's election time , so we 've come to expect politicians out on the hustings promising voters the world , even though they must know that half of what they say is either stupid or undoable , or both . <p> Yet they keep on doing it , and this election cycle all parties seem to have completely lost it , promising massive tax cuts , huge expansions of the social safety net , and boutique tax breaks and spending tweaks for every possible voter demographic . For a time , it seemed as if every party leader was making a promise a day @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ way to get any attention above the media roar . <p> Unfortunately , it 's the sign of a fundamental flaw in our democratic system . Politics is short-term . Governing is long-term . Politics is simple . Governing is incredibly complex . " We 'll do anything and say anything to get us past Oct. 21 , " the politicians tell themselves . " If we win , then we 'll figure it out . " <p> But over-promising can get you in trouble as the Liberals should know . When you look at what they promised in 2015 , they have n't done badly . They legalized cannabis . They reformed the Senate . They expanded the Canada Pension Plan . They brought in an enhanced Child Benefit and a Middle-Class Tax Cut . <p> Yet despite these achievements , they 're not spending too much time reminding the voters of the promises they fulfilled . That 's because they broke at least two of their major promises of 2015 . First , they broke their vow to run modest deficits and balance the budget by 2019 . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ but they still abandoned their promise to balance the budget and never were very convincing about why they did it . <p> Perhaps more damaging to their credibility was breaking the promise to scrap the first-past-the-post electoral system . It was an amazingly foolish promise in the first place , something worthy of a naive first-year political science student , not of a sophisticated political party who should know of all the obstacles to changing the only electoral system we 've ever known for something that 's bound to be more complicated . And within four years ? It was never going to happen . <p> You would think that the Liberals had learned not to overpromise . But no , they 've gone crazy with promises , for higher pensions , lower income taxes , more university grants , gun buybacks , lower cellphone bills , taxes on luxury boats , flood insurance and taxes on Google , and yes , that send-every-kid camping initiative . It 's one thing to say that there 's nothing wrong with running modest deficits in an economy as healthy as Canada 's @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ there 's so much money sloshing around that the government of Canada can afford $547.4 million on a learn-to-camp plan . <p> The Liberals have borrowed a page out of the Tory playbook with the camping plan , figuring it will appeal to some subset of the voting demographic that it was missing . The Tories are of course past masters at this , promising you money to take the bus or send your daughter for ballet lessons . My next prediction is for a flossing and brushing tax credit , which would give $100 or $150 to each taxpayer who promised to floss daily , enough to pay for toothpaste , brushes and floss for the year . <p> Then there 's the NDP . Remember Thomas Mulcair in 2015 ? Trying to look prime ministerial and responsible , he vowed to balance the budget in two years if his party made it into power . It irritated supporters and allowed Justin Trudeau to do a leftward end run around the NDP by promising deficit spending . <p> The new NDP under Jagmeet Singh has decided to go full @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ national pharmacare , public dental care , eye care , mental health care , a national school nutrition program . As for deficits , it only promises to ensure that the debt-to-GDP falls over the next 10 years , which means pretty sizable deficits for the foreseeable future . <p> I would be remiss if I did n't mention the Greens , who have clearly lost the plot , vowing $60 billion to $70 billion a year in new social spending annually , financed by a series of tax-the-rich schemes that will lead to a full-scale corporate revolt and a flight of capital that will almost inevitably lead to fewer tax receipts rather than more . <p> The Greens have also come up with what might be the most bizarre election promise of all , a tax on robots . One might have thought that if any party understood the value of technology it would be the Greens . If we 're going to tackle the climate change crisis , the consensus is that technology will have to be a key part of the solution . <p> But Canada 's @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ side with the 19th-century British Luddites and treat robots and automation as enemies of the workers . So , according to Elizabeth May , a Green Party government would make sure that every time a worker is laid off due to automation , the employer would have to pay a tax equivalent to the income tax paid by that worker . <p> It 's unclear how long the tax would have to be paid . For the life of the robot ? Imposing a punitive tax such as this can only mean that the Greens have opted to be the party of the past . The Greens will not be forming a government anytime soon , so I guess it does n't matter . But it remains strange and disturbing nevertheless . <p> Is there a solution to this over-promising ? How about sticking to three or four signature promises and do what it takes to explain why they 're a good idea , how you 're going to implement them and who will pay .
