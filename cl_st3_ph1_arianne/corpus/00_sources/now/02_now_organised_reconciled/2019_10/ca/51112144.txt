<p> A hard drive containing the personal health information for 650 patients is missing . <p> Alberta Health Services is notifying approximately 650 patients that their information was kept on an external hard drive that has gone missing from the Cardiac Sciences outpatient clinical procedure lab at the Mazanowski Alberta Heart Institute . <p> The provincial health authority speculates the missing hard drive could be linked to a theft that occurred inside the clinical procedure lab on Aug. 5 . It was not until weeks later that hospital officials realized the hard drive was gone . <p> " We know this news will be stressful for those affected and we apologize for any worry this may cause . We would like to note that the nature of the data on the drive would make it very difficult for someone to use just on its own , " said Dr. Mark Joffe , Vice President and Medical Director , Northern Alberta , AHS , in a release . <p> The theft was thoroughly investigated by AHS Protective Services and AHS Privacy , as well as the Edmonton Police Service . The external hard drive was unencrypted and non-password protected . Personal patient information accessible on the drive included study dates of cardiac mapping tests , patient names , gender , dates of birth , physician names and medical record numbers . Alberta personal health care numbers were unaffected . <p> " Certain mandatory encryption policies were not followed in this case , and that is unacceptable . The policies were reviewed with staff to prevent any future issues . Security measures and building access for statutory holidays have also been reviewed by AHS Security and new changes will prevent future issues with inappropriate access to the lab , " said Joffe . <p> AHS has informed the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner and Alberta Health of the incident .
