<p> In the dying days of this election campaign , spare a thought for the lone rangers , the dreamers , the no-hopers , the independent candidates who stand tall against long odds and get virtually no attention , yet remain convinced they can somehow win a seat in Parliament , usually through sheer force of will . <p> Typical of every election , there 's a scattering of them across several Ottawa ridings , although you 'd be hard-pressed to know who they are or anything about them . Your first and likely only encounter with an independent might be in the ballot booth where their names appear alongside those of more familiar registered candidates . <p> It 's highly unusual for an independent candidate to win election , although this campaign may be an exception as two high-profile former Liberal cabinet ministers , Jody Wilson-Raybould and Jane Philpott , are contesting their seats as independents . <p> But without the same kind of profile , without money or organization , the vast majority of independent candidates are hard-pressed to find support beyond friends and family . Truth is , in most cases , independent candidates do n't @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ either delusional or run on a lark just to see their names in print . But sometimes their political ambitions are fuelled by big , bold ideas and that 's a good thing at a time when our elections have become dominated by raw politics , polls , gotcha journalism , even fake news . Maybe it 's time we start injecting more Big Ideas into our campaigns . <p> Retired firefighter Butch Moore is a Big Ideas guy who is convinced he 'll be the next MP for Ottawa West-Nepean as an independent despite the fact he has n't spent a dime or knocked on a single door . He has a binder full of ideas , though , and he 's just bursting to share them , if anybody is interested . <p> " It 's a calling , something that 's pushing you , " Moore explains over bacon and eggs at a west-end diner . " It 's disappointment in things I see , " he says . <p> " By not being with one of the jet setting , media attracting , political mega machines @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ players ' resources or attention , " he admits . That 's an understatement . <p> Still , I like him . He has a good sense of humour and even did a few standup routines at Yuk-Yuks back in the day . He does n't appear to take himself too seriously , except that he seems to think he can win election as an independent , despite every indicator pointing otherwise . <p> He has no campaign signs , he ca n't afford newspaper ads , or television spots , or radio blurbs , he admits . Still , he is convinced that he has big , bold ideas that will attract wide support once the public hears all about them . So , here goes : <p> But first , this is not an endorsement of candidate Moore or his ideas . It is , however , a shout-out to anyone brave enough to stand up with a Big Idea , if only to generate a healthy discussion on the limits of our imagination . <p> Moore 's ideas are imaginative and fanciful in a crazy kind of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ wildly outlandish and are initially dismissed as foolish or even insane . How about the Rideau Canal ? The national railway ? The St. Lawrence Seaway ? Would any of these major initiatives have happened without somebody having a Big Idea ? <p> Moore thinks big . His ideas include everything from a gasoline purchase system intended to stabilize the price at the pumps to turning the Sir John A. Macdonald parkway into the world 's largest flower garden . <p> But his biggest , craziest idea is to construct a fully glass-enclosed , climate-controlled , elevated , two-storey , four-lane bicycle highway along the Queensway from Carp Road in the West end to Trim Road in the east . His bicycle highway would eventually include feeder lines to reach deep into the city 's urban and suburban communities , he explains . <p> Moore is convinced the idea is doable and would take thousands of cars off the road , allowing Ottawans to commute by pedal or electric bike regardless of weather conditions . <p> I 'm not so sure a bicycle highway is a matter of federal jurisdiction @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ spokes of his idea so quickly . <p> " If we really want to take cars off the road , this is the No. 1 , most aggressive action that we can take to battle climate change , " he says . <p> Just do n't ask him what it will cost . He has no idea . Whatever the cost , he believes the offsetting benefit would make it worthwhile , especially if other countries followed Canada 's lead . <p> " It is the most direct , aggressive , provable action that we can take that will cause immediate changes to rescue our world , " he says . <p> Now , before dismissing the proposal outright , remember : you just spent the past couple of minutes considering a Big Idea .
