<p> At the Arts Cafe on Fairmount St. last Monday , the dominant conversation topic was the big event on Sept. 27 , when half-a-million people , inspired by Swedish teen Greta Thunberg , marched for climate change awareness and action in downtown Montreal . Von Carolsfeld -- an award-winning director and now novelist -- was among them . <p> " I grew up in Germany , " said the ebullient 53-year-old . " We went to a lot of demonstrations . " <p> For von Carolsfeld , a deep-rooted activism first spurred by the presence of American cruise missiles in Germany has led to an artistic vocation marked by a championing of society 's most vulnerable . But she took a roundabout route . Originally wanting to be a book editor , the Cologne resident did a two-year apprenticeship with a German publisher before coming to Canada to pursue a relationship . That was 30 years ago -- the first 15 spent in Toronto , the last 15 in Montreal . <p> On arrival , von Carolsfeld 's career path soon detoured into film editing . <p> " It was work where it did n't matter that I @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , " she said . <p> A quick study , she soon attained English fluency ( " there were no Germans here to speak German to " ) and eventually graduated to full-blown filmmaking : her 2002 debut full-length , Marion Bridge , was named Best First Feature at the Toronto International Film Festival .
