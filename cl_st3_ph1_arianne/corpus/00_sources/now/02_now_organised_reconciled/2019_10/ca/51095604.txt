<p> Eight years ago , when I was pregnant with my son and solo-parenting my daughter -- while her dad , a field biologist , worked long shifts away from home up north in the forests near Fort McMurray , doing migratory bird surveys and environmental impact assessments -- I thought a lot about resilience . I liked the sound of the word , which seemed to promise that something might spring back up of its own accord out of what had been ruined , if only it were cared for and provided with the right conditions . <p> By the time I entered my third trimester , I was tired -- to put it mildly . To persevere , I listened to audio books by Pema Chodron . I watched my in-breath , and followed my out-breath . <p> I was in search of that mysterious capacity to be with fatigue , and strain , and difficulty -- in the midst of struggle -- to relate to it all with some kind of equanimity . <p> After my son was born , when he was a few weeks old ( or a few months old maybe , I @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ haze of extended sleep-deprivation and all of the pressures that have applied themselves to my brain in the intervening years ) I remember watching him lying on his back on my bed when suddenly his face balled up in distress . He kicked his legs and cried out as some kind of pain passed through . And then , just as I tried to swoop in to figure out how to soothe him , before I could even get my hands behind his little body to lift him , his pain changed completely and resolved in an instant and a huge smile washed across his face -- and a few seconds after that , the pain came back , and then it passed , and his face just kept changing completely , from moment to moment , like the sky with weather passing through . <p> He was so little he did n't know how to do anything else with the feelings that came ; he just felt what he was feeling . It is easy to be preoccupied with what we need to tell them , what we need to make sure they understand . Everywhere , we are bombarded by assertions of certain knowledge : Here are the top 10 things parents of successful children do , etc . <p> In this context , it might be even easier than usual to lose sight of what our children can help us understand . But what we learn from them , what they know and remind us , is just as essential as what we can teach them -- especially now -- maybe more . <p> In a moment when the scientific consensus is that we only have 11 years left to reverse or mitigate the worst effects of climate change @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ -- and when many people who continue to benefit from the way things are remain unwilling to change the systems and habits that are aggressively fueling both human and planetary crisis -- it can seem that the only urgent and important work to do is work that directly interfaces with and significantly affects these massive systems -- whether political and economic systems or ecosystems . And while drawing this conclusion might cause more people to mobilize for change , to protest , to turn out in the streets -- and while we need that embodied revolt , that felt political will -- some of us also need to be reminded that we ca n't actually live our lives at that scale all the time . <p> How , as parents , can we handle these simultaneous pressures -- the urgency of collective action to create a safer future for our children , on one hand , and on the other hand the need to care for them , today , in the world we have ? <p> Maybe our children can remind us that we are simply here -- not @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ assertions , but in these lives , at this moment . Imperfectly , really here . <p> On the wall of our house , I have hung a metal bird a friend gave me that was made by an artist in Fort McMurray out of a reclaimed oil barrel . <p> The small things we make and reclaim and that we give : these sustain us . <p> On their own , they wo n't save us . <p> We need to fix what we have collectively ruined , and that is going to take a lot of collective work and intentional change and focused political will . Can we get there in time ? Will we ? We have inherited ecological and political crises that predate our own arrival on the scene , but that are reaching their critical moment now . <p> As I sometimes say to my children , when they protest their innocence in an argument with each other : " You 're right , it is n't your fault .
