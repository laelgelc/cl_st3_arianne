<p> In a tight election where every race counts and every vote counts , the enthusiasm of your supporters really starts to matter . Ask any campaign veteran and they will tell you that the " get out the vote " or the GOTV effort on election day is crucial . <p> This election , more so than ever . <p> Yet , the latest poll from Campaign Research shows the Liberals at a disadvantage , not because their polling numbers are down but because their voters are less motivated than those planning to cast ballots for the Conservatives . This is exactly what senior Liberals have told me worries them this election . <p> In the largest poll conducted so far in this election , 4,755 people were asked about their voting intentions between Sept. 30-Oct. 2 . The topline national numbers show a statistical tie 34% for the Conservatives and 32% for the Liberals but a wide difference in enthusiasm for the two parties . <p> When asked who they would consider voting for , the Conservatives had stronger numbers than the Liberals . By comparison , 15% of voters said they are leaning Liberal but will consider other parties while just 9% said they are leaning Conservative but will consider other parties . <p> " This analysis allows us to look closer at each party 's ' hard ' and ' soft ' supporters and gives us insight into which party may have a ' voter turnout ' advantage , " said pollster Nick Kouvalis , principal at Campaign Research . <p> Right now , that voter turnout advantage is with the Conservatives . <p> " The Liberals will either need to convert more of their ' soft ' supporters into ' hard ' supporters or convince existing NDP or Green voters to get behind Trudeau , " Kouvalis said . <p> That need to convince NDP and Green voters to switch their loyalty , is part of why Liberal Leader Justin Trudeau has been invoking the name of Ontario Premier Doug Ford at every turn .
