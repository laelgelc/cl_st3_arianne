This can make life intolerable for people living in affected areas : at the worst of times , eating and drinking outside becomes all but impossible , and preparing meals indoors is a massive issue too . <p> I 've seen flypaper strips left out overnight and completely covered in flies by the following morning . And although the problem is at its worst over the summer , when heat and humidity combine to make the infestation even worse , it risks becoming a year-round headache as climate change causes more unseasonably hot days . <p> The root cause is partly bad companies which flout the terms of their environmental permits by improperly storing waste , storing too much of it or failing to cover bales of rubbish @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ was found to have committed more than a dozen breaches inside of a year -- and the Environment Agency , whose job it is to enforce these conditions , has been getting more proactive at doing so . But it 's increasingly clear that the underlying issue is more systemic : there are simply too many of these facilities concentrated in too small an area , near too many people . <p> The science is simple -- flies will always thrive in the presence of decaying organic matter -- and it means that even companies which do everything right are adding to the aggregate problem . <p> In this context the first thing we need is more intelligent regulation , and that 's why I 've secured a Westminster Hall debate on the subject . <p> As things stand , the only option for dealing with the fly infestation is through identifying rule-breakers , which means even the best-case scenario is one of slow , piecemeal progress . It also means giving the Agency the teeth to enforce its decisions : at present , operators who have their environmental permits revoked because of non-compliance can trigger a lengthy appeal process during which the initial decision does not take effect . The cost and time constraints this process imposes deter the Agency from serving notice in the first place , to the great frustration of local people who desperately need a permanent solution . <p> The fly presence in Avonmouth leaves what should be vibrant outdoor spaces unusable on warm days , taking a toll on community life , and it makes homes harder to sell , leaving residents feeling doubly trapped . <p> In the meantime , people in Avonmouth do what they can -- installing nets and fly-traps , stocking up on repellent and insecticide sprays , and keeping windows and doors closed when temperatures rise .
