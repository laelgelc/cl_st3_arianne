<h> Sustainable Wallingford : How to help the environment at home <p> Some actions are more obviously connected to climate change than others , but if we all do these common sense things , it will make a difference . <p> 1 . Hang your washing out to dry . Tumble dryers are hard on your clothes , the environment and your bills . <p> Weather permitting , hang your clothes outside to reduce pollution , cut your energy bill , get more exercise and extend the life of your clothes . <p> If space ( or aesthetics ) is an issue , look for a retractable clothesline which takes up almost no space when not in use . <p> 2 . Install a living air filter -- houseplants ! Some of the most efficient air -- cleaning houseplants include spider plants , English ivy , rubber plants , and peace lilies . <p> Place a few plants in the room where you spend the most time . <p> 3 . De-clutter your wardrobe . Donate unwanted items to charity , where they 'll get a second life . <p> Replace those stinky mothballs with a natural and better @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cinnamon sticks , bay leaves and whole cloves and tie it at the end . <p> 4 . If putting on a fresh coat of paint , consider the VOC content when choosing your paint . VOCs , or Volatile Organic Compounds , are chemicals that form vapours at room temperature . <p> Some VOC 's , like the ones in many paints , contribute to smog and indoor air pollution , and can cause a host of short- and long-term health problems . <p> The good news is that many paint manufacturers have started making low -- or no -- VOC paints . The bad news is that many of those manufacturers have simply substituted VOC 's with other non -- VOCs , yet are still toxic chemicals . You just have to read the labels . <p> 5 . Instead of continually buying expensive single-use mop pads , invest in a reusable mop . Wash the head in your washing machine , dry , and use again and again . <p> 6 . Ditch the paper towels .
