<p> Researchers examined data from 1970 to the present day relating to almost 7,000 species , to produce the clearest picture yet of how Britain 's wildlife landscape has changed in recent decades , says The Guardian . <p> " We know more about the UK 's wildlife than any other country on the planet , and what it is telling us should make us sit up and listen , " said lead study author Daniel Hayhow , of the Royal Society for the Protection of Birds ( RSPB ) . " We need to respond more urgently across the board . " <p> The analysis found that 26% of mammal species are at risk of disappearing altogether , while 41% of species have decreased in number , the BBC reports . <p> And " the losses to all animals , plants and marine life show no sign of letting up " , adds The Guardian . <p> The species most under threat include hedgehogs , hares , bats , birds such as the willow tit and the turtle dove , and insects such as the high brown fritillary butterfly . <p> The report cites the intensification of agriculture @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ total area of crops treated with pesticides increasing by 53% between 1990 and 2010 .
