<p> A century after pilot and photographer Walter Mittelholzer soared above the mountain in a biplane to capture stunning images of the alpine landscape , two specialists from Dundee University returned with the very latest technology to see how the glaciers had shifted in 100 years . <p> Dr Kieran Baxter and Dr Alice Watterson from the 3DVisLab at Duncan of Jordanstone College of Art and Design , which is part of the university , found evidence of massive ice loss . <p> Dr Baxter said it had been " a breathtaking and heartbreaking experience " and he hoped the images would help to convince people to take action on climate change before it is too late . <p> The pair used a process called monoplotting to triangulate the original camera position in airspace . By using the mountain tops as anchor points they were able to find the exact spots where the historical shots were taken . <p> The resulting photographs of the Argentiere , Mont Blanc Bossons and Mer de Glace glaciers show large scale ice loss in the region . <p> Dr Baxter , equipped with multiple GPS devices , hung from the side of the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ metres , just below the summit of Mont Blanc , to capture the photographs . <p> He said : " The scale of the ice loss was immediately evident as we reached altitude but it was only by comparing the images side-by-side that the last 100 years of change were made visible . <p> " It was both a breathtaking and heartbreaking experience , particularly knowing that the melt has accelerated massively in the last few decades .
