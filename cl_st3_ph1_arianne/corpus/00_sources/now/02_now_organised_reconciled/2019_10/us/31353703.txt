<p> A vast region of unusually warm water has formed in the northeastern Pacific Ocean , and scientists are worried that it could devastate sea life in the area and fuel the formation of harmful algal blooms . <p> The broad swath of warm water , now known as the Northeast Pacific Marine Heat Wave of 2019 , was first detected in early June . Now data from weather satellites and buoys show that it measures six to seven times the size of Alaska , which spans more than 600,000 square miles . <p> Given its size and location , the marine heat wave rivals a similar one that arose in 2014 and persisted for two years .
