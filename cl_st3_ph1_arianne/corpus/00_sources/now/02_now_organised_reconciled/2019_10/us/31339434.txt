<h> New study shows the evolution and changes of the vegetation of the Orinoco Delta of the last 6200 years <p> Individuals of " Mauritia flexuosa " dominating the canopy with abundant " Montrichardia arborescens " along the shore of Ca ? o Tigre . Credit : Encarni Montoya <p> Mauritia flexuosa is the most widespread plant in the Orinoco Delta , in north-western Venezuela . This has not always been the case in the past . This palm , locally known as " moriche , " started its domination over the vegetal community of the area 3000 years ago , according to new research that has reconstructed for the first time the ecological dynamics and evolution of the Orinoco 's Delta for the last 6200 years . The authors of the study , which has been published in Quaternary Science Reviews , have been able to identify 3 different periods characterized by different types of vegetation . <p> " With this research we have seen the importance of both local and superregional natural drivers in the vegetation dynamics , " says Encarni Montoya , researcher at Institute of Earth Sciences Jaume Almera of the Spanish National Research Council @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Researchers extracted a 141 cm-long sediment core from a swamp near " ca ? o Tigre . " The sediment samples were analysed by means of paleomagnetic techniques , X Ray diffraction , and stable isotope analysis . <p> Moreover , scientists analysed pollen samples from the recovered sediment core to know the vegetation type that existed in the area throughout the studied period . A radiocarbon analysis was carried out to date exactly the different layers of the sediments . Charcoal particles were also analysed as a proxy to obtain information about the fires that occurred in the region , from which were obtained clues about human settlement periods . <p> The researchers note that , although forested during the last 6200 years , the composition of the forests of the region have greatly varied through time .
