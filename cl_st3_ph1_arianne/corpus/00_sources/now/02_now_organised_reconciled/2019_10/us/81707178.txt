One of many on the Milk Weed <p> KANSAS CITY , Mo . -- A study indicates that climate change could be threatening many insects in Missouri . <p> University of Missouri at St. Louis researchers examined 250 insect species whose populations plummeted after mid-spring frosts and summer droughts . The findings , published in the science journal Frontiers , pointed to a population decrease by 95% for some insect species . <p> KCUR-FM reports that the study shows populations were eventually able to recover . But Robert Marquis , a professor emeritus of biology at the university , says the populations may not climb back up if climate change makes droughts and spring frost events more frequent . <p> The 2019 study focused on insects that eat leaves of white oak and black oak trees in Missouri .
