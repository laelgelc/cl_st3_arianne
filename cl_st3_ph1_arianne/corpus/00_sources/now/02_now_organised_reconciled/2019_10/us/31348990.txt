<p> Every year , 7 trillion tiny pieces of plastic , roughly equal to 1 million pieces each for every man , woman and child in the Bay Area , flow into San Francisco Bay , according to the most comprehensive scientific study yet on the subject . <p> The three-year study found that billions of pieces of " microplastic " -- particles smaller than 5 millimeters each , or roughly the size of a pencil eraser -- pour through the Bay Area 's 40 sewage treatment plants every year . The particles come from synthetic fibers in clothing , like fleece jackets that shed in washing machines or baby wipes flushed down toilets , and then wash down sewer pipes , pass through treatment plant filters and empty into bay waters . <p> But 300 times more of the relentless toxic confetti , the study revealed , comes from storm drains , the largest source of the particles .
