<p> A New Mexico Native American tribe is leading efforts to promote renewable energy across the country on tribal land , with the construction of a 50-megawatt solar farm in the north-central region of the state . <p> The Jicarilla Apache tribe 's solar power project would send electricity from solar panels to Albuquerque via the Public Service Company of New Mexico ( PNM ) , read a study from the Institute for Energy Economics and Financial Analysis ( IEEFA ) . <p> The project was part of a larger effort to replace power generation lost by the closure of the San Juan Generating Station , a coal-fired power plant in the Four Corners region in northwest New Mexico . <p> The plants closure caused regional power rates to increase , read the study , and the solar project should help alleviate the problem . <p> The solar farm would be joined by a 20-megawatt battery storage unit , and the project was the first of its kind on tribal land , the study read . <p> " Jicarilla Solar is seen regionally as a pilot project of sorts -- first , to test the marketability of utility-scale solar @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ second , to position the participants -- and ratepayers -- to benefit from rapid gains in utility-scale solar technology , " read the study . <p> Power from Jicarilla would be brought to PNM to support a new program , PNM Solar Direct , allowing the company 's larger customers -- with an aggregate demand of at least 2.5 megawatts or more -- to buy solar power directly from the utility . <p> ( Photo : Getty Images ) <p> PNM would pay $21.73 per megawatt hour . <p> Situated in Rio Arriba County , the 500-acres project would be built on land already set aside for business development and unencumbered by grazing rights or other property claims , the report read .
