<h> Alex Kent : Communities must to better on affordable housing <p> A beaver hut at Lake Fitzgerald in Northampton.Gazette file photo <p> Published : 10/20/2019 11:00:10 PM <p> Northampton needs open space ... and housing . Along with John Paradis ( " We can have our land , and protect it too , " Oct. 11 ) , I applaud Northampton 's success in preserving open land for future generations . <p> I agree with his criticism of " the so-called progress of pavement , business offices , malls , and McMansions . " <p> I also note that the median house price in Northampton ( Zillow ) has climbed to nearly $300,000 , and the average rental unit of around 900 square feet is $1,600 per month . Yet the median household income in 2017 was $62,838 , with 15% of the city 's population living under the poverty line , according to the U.S. Census . <p> High real estate and rental prices put decent housing out of reach for far too many people in Northampton . Some say that the way to make housing more affordable is to ensure ample supply in the market . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ supply of housing and stronger competition for buyers and renters indeed might lead to lower prices . <p> But like Mr. Paradis , I do not want the beautiful land around Northampton to be paved , sodded and turned into endless , car-dependent , and resource-wasting subdivisions . The use of public money to buy and preserve open land is sound public policy . <p> Like most American cities , however , Northampton has so far failed to implement stronger policies to promote an adequate supply of affordable housing . Without strong housing policies , developers will be only too pleased to move in and build housing for those who can pay top dollar , an unfortunate trend which is abundantly in evidence in downtown Amherst . <p> In the face of climate change , it is increasingly urgent that people relearn how to live with one another in close proximity -- in cities and towns . That goal will not be achieved unless everyone can afford to live in decent housing .
