<p> Some suburbs of Tucson and Phoenix will struggle to find enough water to keep growing without damaging underground aquifers by overpumping groundwater , a new report warns . <p> This could lead to land subsidence , including ground fissures ; lower water quality ; and even the possibility of wells drying up . <p> The report from Arizona State University 's water institute also warns of potentially catastrophic financial problems for the agency that finds renewable water supplies for development in far-flung suburban areas . <p> That , in turn , could ratchet up already soaring rates for homeowners there . <p> The state 's system for insuring there 's enough water for much of the new suburban growth -- in accord with the landmark 1980 Groundwater Management Act -- is environmentally unsustainable and needs an overhaul , the report suggests . <p> The findings raise questions about how much more growth should continue in areas without renewable supplies such as Central Arizona Project water , said Kathleen Ferris , a former Arizona Department of Water Resources director and chief counsel who co-authored the report .
