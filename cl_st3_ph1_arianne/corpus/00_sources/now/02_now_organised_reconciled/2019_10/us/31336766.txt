<h> Still hoping to get into next week 's Democratic debate in Ohio ? You 're probably out of luck . <p> COLUMBUS , Ohio -- Most Ohioans who plan to watch the Democratic presidential debate will view it on their television or computer screens since the hard-to-get tickets are being distributed to just 1,600 people . <p> The Rike Center at Otterbein University in Westerville , where basketball games and events such as commencement are staged , can seat 3,000 people . But CNN , one of the debate hosts , is reconfiguring the center with a stage to fit 12 candidates , lighting and camera crews . <h> Otterbein <p> Otterbein University officials said earlier this week that it received about a fourth of the available tickets . School administrators decided 300 would go to students , the rest to faculty and staff except a few going to Otterbein President John Comerford -- who will speak at the beginning of the debate -- the Westerville mayor and others . <h> Ohio Democratic Party contest <p> The Ohio Democratic Party received a few hundred tickets that it divvied up to the public in a contest in which people @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the debate . <p> Organizers said some of the winners of those tickets include families of gun violence victims , climate change advocates , faith leaders , small business owners , immigrants , union members and members of the LGBTQ community . <p> " We had more than 25,000 people participate in the drawing , " a party spokeswoman said . <h> Candidates <p> Obviously , each campaign will have a supporters in the audience . Organizers said that each candidate 's campaign was allotted an equal , unspecified number of tickets . <p> Organizers said other elected officials were offered tickets , such as Ohio 's Democratic members of Congress .
