<h> Letter : Heat waves , droughts have no party affiliation <p> We have recently experienced some of the hottest weather ever recorded in our area . The high temperature in Greenville on Oct. 3 was 97 F , shattering the previous record of 92 F for that date . To make matters worse , we are under drought conditions , and as The Greenville News reported recently , area farmers are losing thousands of dollars . <p> We all know this heat is hurting and costing us -- big time . It is costing our farmers in crops and water . It is costing our children who have asthma and can not play outside . And yet , the issue seems stuck in the partisan swamp ; opinions on climate change have become an ingrained part of the liberal/conservative litmus test . <p> But does it have to be this way ? Droughts and heat waves affect Democrats and Republicans alike , and there are solutions that both Republicans and Democrats can get behind . <p> One is a carbon fee and dividend , such as the one proposed by Rep. ( Francis ) Rooney ( R-FL ) @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ This bill would put a price on carbon emissions , and refund the money to all taxpayers in the form of a dividend . <p> Prominent Republicans like Rooney and our own former Rep. ( Bob ) Inglis like this idea because it is a free-market solution that does not grow the size of government . Democrats like it because the carbon dividend is progressive . Economists and environmentalists have endorsed it because it 's effective at combatting CO2 emissions without appreciable negative impacts on the economy . <p> In this age of partisan tribalism , it 's very easy for us to become resigned to inaction . But as our children have reminded us in the recent Climate Strike protests , our future depends on being able to look past those instincts . Can we look past our differences and find a common-sense solution ?
