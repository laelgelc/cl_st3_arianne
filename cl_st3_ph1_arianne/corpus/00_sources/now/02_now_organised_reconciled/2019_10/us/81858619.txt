<p> SEOUL/TOKYO , Oct 29 ( Reuters ) - Japan and South Korea on Tuesday rejected a media report that the two governments were studying plans for a joint economic programme that aims to ease strains over the issue of forced Korean labour in World War Two . <p> Kyodo news reported on Monday that South Korea and Japan were considering a programme involving companies from both countries , but that the Japanese government would not provide funds in line with its position that claims over forced labour were settled in a 1965 treaty . <p> Japan 's Chief Cabinet Secretary Yoshihide Suga said that the report was not true . South Korea 's foreign ministry also said in a statement it was " not true " . <p> " While respecting judicial judgment , the ( South Korean ) government is open to finding reasonable ways that can be accepted by victims and the people of both countries , and continues to communicate with the Japanese diplomatic authorities , " the South Korean statement said . <p> Suga declined comment on whether such an idea for a programme might have been in a letter from South Korean President @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ week . <p> South Korea and Japan 's bilateral relations have recently deteriorated to some of its worst in decades over a bitter history that includes the 1910-45 Japanese colonisation of the Korean peninsula , the forced mobilisation of labour at Japanese companies and the use of " comfort women " - Japan 's euphemism for girls and women , many of them Korean - forced to work in military brothels . <p> ( Reporting by Joyce Lee in Seoul and Makiko Yamazaki in Tokyo ; Editing by Michael Perry ) <p> Our global editorial team of more than 50 journalists and about 250 freelancers covers the world 's under-reported stories at the heart of aid , development , women 's and LGBT+ rights , human trafficking , property rights , and climate change .
