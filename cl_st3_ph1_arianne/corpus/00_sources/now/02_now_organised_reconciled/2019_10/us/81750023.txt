In France , for example , rural folks dependent on cars for a living rioted last year over a government hike in gas prices , forcing President Emmanuel Macron to retreat and then revamp how he governs . Now it is Canada 's turn to deal with its own rupture over carbon issues . <p> After an election on Monday , Prime Minister Justin Trudeau faces a possible rebellion in two big energy-rich provinces , Alberta and Saskatchewan , which contain the world 's third-largest proven reserves of oil . In a grand political bargain last year , he was able to impose a national carbon tax while at the same time promising to rescue a proposed project to expand an oil pipeline from Alberta to the Pacific coast . <p> Now that pipeline may not be built as a result of the election , raising talk in Alberta of splitting the province from Canada . The country 's old east-west divide has been revived , requiring the young prime minister to heal this national breach before it grows . One solution lies in helping Alberta better use its oil revenues to diversify its economy -- as Norway has done with its petroleum wealth -- lessening the need to extract oil for global markets .
