<p> WASHINGTON ( AP ) -- Expect more preventative power blackouts in California as the climate gets hotter and drier and the wildfire season gets nastier and longer , scientists say . <p> The Golden State already is fire-prone with lots of dry plants and woodlands -- but add high winds that can knock down power lines or cause them to spark , then watch out , wildfire experts say . <p> The darker outlook hits close to home for Stanford University climate scientist Chris Field , who like so many others had his electricity cut off Thursday by Pacific Gas &amp; Electric Co . <p> " At this point we do n't have a better option for reducing risk than shutting electricity off , " Field said . " It 's better than having a whole community burn down . " <p> Mike Flannigan , a professor of wildland fire at the University of Alberta in Canada , said " the new reality " is that there will be more fires with drier and hotter weather from man-made global warming . The more fuel to burn means more intense " fires . <p> The area burned in California wildfires has increased fivefold from 1972 to 2018 and that 's been " driven by drying of fuels promoted by human-induced warming , " according to a June study in the scientific journal Earth 's Future . <p> Summer " warm season " days in California have increased in temperature by 2.5 degrees ( 1.4 degrees Celsius ) in the past century , the study said . <p> " Mostly we see a strong summertime effect , " study co-author Jennifer Balch , a fire scientist at the University of Colorado , said in an email . It 's especially dangerous in fall , when high winds can knock down power lines and spark fires that feed on the vegetation that dried out during the hot summer months . <p> The key , said Field of Stanford , is the time between the start of the high winds and the start of winter rainstorms .
