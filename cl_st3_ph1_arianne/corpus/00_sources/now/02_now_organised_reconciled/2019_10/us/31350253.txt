<h> We are a nation of refugees . We must not end refugee resettlement because of Trump , group says <p> Posted Oct 16 , 2019 <p> Adrienne Romero The Jersey Journal <p> Despite President Trump 's blow to the U.S. refugee resettlement program , hundreds of New Jerseyans in Union County and beyond stand ready with open arms to welcome refugee families to our community , according to the Immigration Committee of Westfield 20/20 . Above , dozens rallied for refugees at Newark Pedestrian Plaza this summer . <p> This country was founded on the hard work , determination , and skills of generations of immigrants from all countries , religions , and backgrounds . Many of us are descended from refugees -- people who survived war , famine , and political or religious persecution and came here seeking freedom and safety . They wanted to give back to their adopted communities and build a better future for their children . And we are living proof that they did just that . <p> For decades refugees have enriched our state and nation in many ways . They have become our friends and neighbors , opening businesses , volunteering @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ They 've become self-sufficient and contributed to the U.S. economy . <p> Yet this month the Trump administration dealt a huge blow to the refugee resettlement program that has saved the lives of so many by slashing the annual admissions goal to only 18,000 . This number is less than 16% of the average annual authorization over the past 30 years , and only a quarter of the number admitted since 2017 . This cutback comes at a time when there are nearly 30 million refugees worldwide , a number that is on the rise because of the effects of climate change , food insecurity , and political instability . The damaging impact of this decision will only be amplified as other resettlement countries follow the lead of the U.S. and reduce their acceptance rates . <p> This draconian cutback , on the heels of the Supreme Court 's decision to allow President Trump to bar refugees from seeking asylum if they travel through another country to reach the U.S. , amounts to an end to our nation 's commitment to refugees . The administration has sent a firm message @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ here . <p> These policies put the lives of thousands of vulnerable people -- most of them women and children -- at dire risk of persecution and death . <p> The 75% cut to refugee resettlement since 2017 has already had a devastating effect . Parents forced to leave their children behind when they fled have endured separation for years while waiting for them to be approved for resettlement here . Vulnerable refugees have been abandoned in unsafe situations . The U.S. has turned its back on refugees we promised to protect in Iraq and Afghanistan , who are in grave danger as a result of their service to our military and government . The decimation of the refugee resettlement program greatly diminishes America 's international standing . <p> We can not allow this to continue . Through a national network of community-based , private non-profit religious and charitable organizations working in partnership with the U.S. government , a robust infrastructure and ample resources are available to help 95,000 refugees next year . Congress must hold the administration accountable to its commitments as well as to the people fleeing persecution who see us as a beacon of hope . <p> Catherine Picker is a member of the Immigration Committee of Westfield 20/20 , a grassroots community organization .
