<p> Shoppers are buying more clothes and discarding them faster than ever , a trend that is sending an increasing amount of textiles to the dump and propelling the fashion industry to search for new technology to recycle used garments . <p> The growth of fast-fashion retailers like Hennes &amp; Mauritz AB , Inditex SA ' s Zara and Gap Inc. -- each vying to deliver quicker and cheaper styles -- has flooded the world with affordable clothing that is worn just a few times . <p> Globally , the number of garments purchased annually by the average consumer jumped 60% from 2000 to 2014 , according to McKinsey &amp; Co . The number of times an item is worn before it is discarded dropped 36% between 2002 and 2016 , the Ellen MacArthur Foundation , a U.K.-based nonprofit organization focused on cutting waste says . In the U.S. , clothes are worn for around a quarter of the global average . <p> Despite the buildup of used clothes , the technology to recycle old textiles into fiber to make new ones has remained embryonic , meaning clothes eventually end up in the dump or incinerator .
