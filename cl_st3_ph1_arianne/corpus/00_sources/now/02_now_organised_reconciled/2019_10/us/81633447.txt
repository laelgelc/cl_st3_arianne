<p> In it , Professor Williams cites a series of faulty predictions by scientists about the climate dating from 1939 . He 's right : they certainly were faulty . He then concludes that " today 's ( predictions ) are likely to be just as true as yesteryear 's , " and the only result of fighting climate change is that " we 'll be much poorer and less free . " <p> There are two problems . First , he believes that it is only " likely " that the predictions are wrong . Suppose that there is , let 's say , a 40 percent chance that they 're true . And if they are true , the earth will become uninhabitable by humans . Not only will we be poorer and less free ; we wo n't exist at all . If there 's a chance of such a catastrophe , should n't we try to prevent it ? That would be the conservative thing to do . <p> Second , we do n't need mere predictions any longer . We can see climate change with our own eyes . <p> Some examples : Arctic @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ much of its ice ; the Amazon rain forest is disappearing ; hurricanes , flooding and wildfires are more frequent ; and the five hottest years on record have occurred since 2010 . Surely Professor Williams knows this . <p> Professor Williams teaches economics , a worthy calling . Competent economists must consider all available evidence in reaching conclusions , which in his column Professor Williams fails to do .
