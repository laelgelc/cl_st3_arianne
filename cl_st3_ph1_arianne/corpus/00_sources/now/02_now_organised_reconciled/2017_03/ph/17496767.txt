<h> Making the future of transport <p> Will the cars of the future have four wheels ? Or three ? Or none at all ? Will they even have windows ? And what will we use to fuel them ? The Shell Eco-marathon Asia taking place at Singapore 's Changi Exhibition Center provides a little preview of what the future has in store , as students from around the region compete to drive the farthest distance on the least amount of energy . <p> You may be surprised that Shell should put its weight behind an event that showcases so many alternatives to traditional fuels . You should n't be . <p> ADVERTISEMENT <p> The world is undergoing a transition to lower-carbon forms of energy . If it is to succeed -- and it will take decades , not years -- transport must be at the heart of that transition . If Shell is to continue to succeed long into the future , the company must be part of that transition . <p> Climate change poses a challenge and opportunity to every one of us . The development of lower-carbon @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is critical to global efforts to tackle it , as transport accounts for 28 percent of world energy consumption . <p> And as Asia 's economies grow , there will be more people on the roads , eager to travel and to own and drive cars . Today , there are roughly 1 billion passenger vehicles on the world 's roads . By 2040 , the International Energy Agency expects this number to reach around 2 billion . <p> It is crucial , therefore , to cut emissions by boosting the efficiency of vehicles . But make no mistake . There is no simple , single answer when over 90 percent of transport runs on liquid fuels . <p> The world will need mass-produced and affordable battery-electric cars ; it will need hydrogen fuel cell electric vehicles , too , with their greater range and quicker refueling . The infrastructure to support these vehicles must be put in place . Using current technology , they would require roughly two-thirds of the world 's annual lithium production for their batteries . Supplies for other minerals like cobalt could also come under pressure . And with over a billion cars on the roads , 500,000 is , in any case , less than one two-thousandth of the world 's fleet . <p> Finally , an electric car is only as clean as the source of its electricity -- that means lower-carbon natural gas power generation or renewable energy , or a combination of both . <p> As the world moves increasingly toward lower-emission transport , cleaner and more economical fuels , more efficient lubricants and better engines will help .
