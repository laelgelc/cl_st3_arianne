<h> Metro bus project gets $64.6-m loan <p> The Transportation Department has secured a $64.6-million loan from the World Bank to partly finance the first bus rapid transit ( BRT ) system in Metro Manila . <p> The Metro Manila BRT Line 1 Project will cost $109.4 million , with $64.6 million to be funded by the World Bank and the Clean Technology Fund . <p> The funding for Metro Manila BRT was approved by World Bank 's board of executive directors . <p> The Philippine government will provide a counterpart funding equivalent to $44.8 million . <p> Like trains , BRTs run on dedicated lanes , carrying passengers in large numbers . Unlike trains that run on rails , however , BRTs deploy buses , making the system simpler and cheaper to construct , operate and maintain . <p> " By providing an affordable and convenient public transport option , this project will help make job and education opportunities more accessible , especially for the poor residing around the BRT route , " said World Bank country director Mara Warwick . <p> The project will also develop support infrastructure @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ bus terminals and stations , segregation barriers , sidewalks , warning and direction signs , and pedestrian crossing facilities . Women make up 55 percent of public transport users in Metro Manila . <p> " Bus systems like BRT are cost-effective options for reducing emissions of harmful gases that cause climate change , " said Zhihong Zhang , senior program coordinator of the Clean Technology Fund . <p> " Implementation of this project alone will prevent the release of around 2.6 million tons of carbon dioxide equivalents into the atmosphere in the next 20 years . Transport is the fastest growing source of greenhouse gas emissions globally and projects like this show the road to a cleaner future , " he said . <p> To be implemented by the Department of Transportation in coordination with the local governments of Manila and Quezon City , the Manila BRT Line 1 is expected to be operational by 2020 . <p> Pioneered in Curitiba , Brazil in 1974 , BRT systems are growing in popularity throughout the world for efficiency and affordability . <p> Managed by the World Bank , the Clean Technology @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ expand clean technologies that have strong potential for reducing greenhouse gas emissions . <p> The fund globally has provided $3.8 billon to support clean technologies , such as renewable energy , energy efficiency and transport . <h> More from this Category : <p> COMMENT DISCLAIMER : Reader comments posted on this Web site are not in any way endorsed by The Standard .
