<h> Boosting the efficiency of vehicles <p> Will the cars of the future have four wheels ? Or three ? Or none at all ? Will they even have windows ? And what will we use to fuel them ? The Shell Eco-marathon Asia taking place at Singapore 's Changi Exhibition Center will provide a little preview of what the future has in store , as students from around the region compete to drive the farthest distance on the least amount of energy . <p> You may be surprised that Shell should put its weight behind an event that showcases so many alternatives to traditional fuels . You should n't be . <p> The world is undergoing a transition to lower-carbon forms of energy . If it is to succeed -- and it will take decades , not years -- transport must be at the heart of that transition . If Shell is to continue to succeed long into the future , the company must be part of that transition . <p> Climate change poses a challenge and opportunity to every one of us . The development of lower-carbon fuels @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is critical to global efforts to tackle it , as transport accounts for 28 percent of world energy consumption . <p> And as Asia 's economies grow , there will be more people on the roads , eager to travel and to own and drive cars . Today , there are roughly 1 billion passenger vehicles on the world 's roads . By 2040 , the International Energy Agency ( IEA ) expects this number to reach around 2 billion . <p> It is crucial , therefore , to cut emissions by boosting the efficiency of vehicles . But make no mistake . There is no simple , single answer when over 90 percent of transport runs on liquid fuels . <p> The world will need mass-produced and affordable battery-electric cars ; it will need hydrogen fuel cell electric vehicles , too , with their greater range and quicker refuelling . The infrastructure to support these vehicles must be put in place . And most importantly , consumers must be willing to make the change . <p> BMW , Tesla and others are making great advances on battery-electric cars and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ better . <p> Electric vehicles have made much progress but there is a long way to go . According to the IEA , there are now over 1.26 million electric cars -- a global market share of around 0.1 percent . An electric car is only as clean as the source of its electricity -- that means lower-carbon natural gas power generation or renewable energy , or a combination of both . <p> As the world moves increasingly towards lower-emission transport , cleaner and more economical fuels , more efficient lubricants and better engines will help . Low-carbon biofuels will be important , too . <p> Shell 's New Technology Center in Bangalore intends to develop a technology called IH2 that converts waste to fuel . Such innovations , among many others , will help the world make the transition to a low-carbon and more energy-efficient future . <p> Ultimately , if the huge transport sector is going to be transformed successfully over time , we will need all the creativity we can muster among the designers and the engineers of the future . Just like a man called Konstantinos Laskaris who competed in the event five years ago . Today , he is chief motor engineer at Tesla . <p> Now , as then , we wish him -- and everybody involved in this year 's Shell Eco-marathon Asia at Make the Future Singapore 2017 -- the best of luck .
