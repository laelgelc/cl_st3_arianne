<h> So Now There Are 7,000 Methane Bubbles Beneath Siberia <p> Back in 2016 , Siberia 's amusingly named Bely Island made headlines around the world after sections of its grassy landscape became somewhat bouncy . <p> As it turned out , the island was leaking greenhouse gases at a remarkable rate . In fact , the air escaping from the ground there contained 100 times more methane and 25 times more carbon dioxide -- the two most potent greenhouse gases by far -- than the surrounding atmosphere . <p> This time last year , just 15 of these near-surface , water-coated methane bubbles had been identified . Now , as reported by the Siberian Times , there are 7,000 of them . However , thanks to the rapid pace of climate change , the region is warming incredibly fast -- sometimes twice as fast as the rest of the planet . <p> When permafrost melts , it unleashes the trapped methane and carbon dioxide within it . Although carbon dioxide lasts longer in the atmosphere than methane , the latter gas is actually around 28-36 times more potent at trapping heat . <p> As the permafrost mainly stores methane , its continued rapid release -- as documented quite extravagantly by these underground bubbles -- may be able to warm the planet rapidly , if there 's enough of it there . <p> A suddenly warmer planet means not only melting ice caps and expanding seas , but less stable stores of permafrost worldwide .
