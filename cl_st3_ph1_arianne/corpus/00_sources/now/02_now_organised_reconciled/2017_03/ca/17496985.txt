<h> Recovery of endangered plants and animals is possible <p> I lost my Cracker Jack wildlife cards sometime in the 1980s , but the images printed on them are still vivid in my mind . <p> The small cards came wrapped in clear plastic and featured a holographic image of a wildlife species in danger . I can still see the pencil marks in the top drawer of my old desk where I marked lines to keep them organized . There was a special area in my drawer reserved for the handful of species that lived in my country , and were at risk of extinction . <p> I started collecting my Cracker Jack wildlife cards around the time when Canada started to systematically identify the wildlife species most at risk . Formed in 1977 , the Committee on the Status of Endangered Wildlife in Canada ( COSEWIC ) has provided science-based assessments of wildlife most at risk of being lost from Canada . <p> That list of wildlife in danger has almost doubled since I started working at the Nature Conservancy of Canada ( NCC ) in 2002 . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as at risk in Canada by COSEWIC . <p> Part of this steep increase has resulted from more species being assessed . COSEWIC meets twice a year to assess species based on factors such as range size , number of individuals , population trends and projected changes in habitat . <p> Species are then categorized as extinct , extirpated , endangered , threatened , special concern , data deficient or not at risk . <p> Species assessed by COSEWIC then go through a second assessment by Environment Canada and Climate Change to identify how they will be listed under the federal Species at Risk Act ( SARA ) . Once listed under SARA , there are efforts to develop a plan to recover or manage the species . <p> The increase in the number of assessments and better tools to identify species that could be endangered definitely account for many " new " species at risk . There are many species that were common 40 years ago that have genuinely declined . <p> Many bat species are now endangered because the introduction of white nose syndrome has devastated colonies across @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ including barn swallow , Sprague 's pipit and eastern whip-poor-will , have declined by over 70 per cent since I was kid in the ' 70s . Nine caribou populations are now at risk . <p> The rapid , steady growth in the number of at-risk species worries me . The challenge is going to be how do we recover all of those species and keep the list from growing ? <p> Most species at risk are restricted to specific habitats in specific geographies . If we focus our conservation efforts on these hot spots we simultaneously protect the habitat and many rare species . <p> The Nature Conservancy of Canada has been protecting species-at-risk hot spots for many years . This work is supported in part by Environment and Climate Change Canada through the Natural Areas Conservation Program . Most of these hot spots are within landscapes dominated by human activities where we are continuing to lose coastal shorelines , forests , wetlands and prairie grasslands . By focusing our conservation efforts on some of the best habitat remnants and restoring areas around them , land protection and management @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ help their recovery . <p> Species that have their habitats within conservation lands generally have fewer threats . When we reduce the threat , we reduce the risk of extinction . In time , their status can be improved , and we can start to see species shift into lower risk categories or , ideally , be removed from the list . <p> By restoring forests , savannahs and wetlands , the amount of habitat available for at-risk species increases . In some cases we need to help restore populations by reintroducing animals back to properties that are within their historic range . Increasing the number of locations a species occupies also reduces its risk , and starts the recovery process . <p> The factors leading to endangerment for some of Canada 's species is complicated , and ca n't always be solved by land conservation alone . There are threats from invasive diseases , climate change and poaching . <p> But we do know how to recover endangered species , even when it 's complicated . My old Cracker Jack wildlife cards included osprey . Their numbers had dwindled @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ their eggs so thin and fragile they often broke when the parents sat on the nest . <p> While never officially listed in Canada , their numbers had plummeted by the ' 70s and they were rare . Today , I 've stopped pointing out osprey nests in the light towers at baseball diamonds to my son . <p> The opportunity to recover Canada 's at- risk wildlife can inspire us to see the world that could be . Places and habitats that have almost been lost can be restored . Species that were at the edge of extinction can be gently pulled back . <p> Recovery of our most endangered plants and animals is possible , but we need to do more . <p> Dan Kraus is Weston Conservation Scientist with the Nature Conservancy of Canada
