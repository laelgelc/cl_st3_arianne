<p> For those waiting for reconstruction of Second Avenue , a street in Grand Rapids , Mich. , can serve as an example and , if adopted , could satisfy many traffic and safety concerns plus result in early implementation . The two-km Burton Street in Grand Rapids is similar to Second Avenue with the same volume of traffic , both residential and retail properties along its length , and with parks and schools in the neighbourhood . <p> The Ministry of Environment and Climate Change has directed the city roads and transportation department to come up with a traffic safety plan for Second Avenue , in consultation with interested citizens , prior to road reconstruction . This plan must address safety issues with respect to motorists and others , including pedestrians and cyclists <p> The Burton Street example answered a number of safety issues with respect to concerns about excessive vehicle speeds , congestion and accommodation of school and transit buses and smaller intersections with reduced queues for left turns , improved bicycle accessibility and enhanced pedestrian environment . <p> The road design is simple with two traffic lanes @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and designated bike lanes that serve the dual purpose of accommodating bicycle traffic and providing a space for transit buses to make routine stops , leaving most of the through lane for motorists to pass . It is a practical solution that has , according to a Grand Rapids roads engineer , balanced safety with quality of life . <p> Burton Street was previously a four-lane roadway with concerns regarding the frequency of accidents , speeding , congestion , transit operation and provision for cyclists . Since reduction to three lanes and the installation of bike lanes , there has been a reduction of accidents , slower speeds and an increased use of the roadway by cyclists , plus improved transit operation . <p> Adopting of the Burton Street model for Second Avenue would make this roadway compatible with other streets in Minnow Lake and reduce the surface road area from the present five-lane design , thereby limiting road salt contamination into Lake Ramsey .
