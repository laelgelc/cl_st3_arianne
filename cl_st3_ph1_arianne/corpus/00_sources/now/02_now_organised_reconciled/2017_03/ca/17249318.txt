It immediately turned out that the proprietors of JM 's Butterfly B&amp;B , Joel Moreno Rojas and Ellen Sharp , had a magnificent website , full of useful information . Since the 1990s , Suzuki reports that the eastern monarch population has declined by 90 per cent . Each year , the monarchs make a three-generation flutter to Canada ( arriving in a broad band from south Saskatchewan to the Maritimes ) , launching from four high altitude sites in the Sierra Madre early in March . <p> We would be arriving just before the mass liftoff from a forest of oyamel fir trees . In peak years , more than one billion monarchs start the 4,800-km journey to Canada . The chief impediment to the monarchs ' success is lack of food -- specifically milkweed bushes . And so we did . <p> The road trip was relatively uneventful , with one exception . About an hour from our destination , as we drove through a small agricultural village , a convoy of five camouflage-painted pickup trucks passed us going in the opposite direction . Each had an enormous machine-gun bolted to its cargo bed , manned by a three-soldier crew . The centre man had his finger on the automatic trigger . On either side were soldiers with menacing black submachine-guns . All three , plus the driver , wore military fatigues and black balaclavas with only eye-slits .
