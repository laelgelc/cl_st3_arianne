<h> Great Barrier Reef <p> The Great Barrier Reef in Australia has long been one of the world 's most magnificent natural wonders , so enormous it can be seen from space , so beautiful it can move visitors to tears . <p> But the reef , and the profusion of sea creatures living near it , are in profound trouble . <p> Huge sections of the Great Barrier Reef , stretching across hundreds of miles of its most pristine northern sector , were recently found to be dead , killed last year by overheated seawater . More southerly sections around the middle of the reefthat barely escaped then are bleaching now , a potential precursor to another die-off that could rob some of the reef 's most visited areas of colour and life . <p> " We did n't expect to see this level of destruction to the Great Barrier Reef for another 30 years , " said Terry P. " <p> The damage to the Great Barrier Reef , one of the world 's largest living organisms , is part of a global calamity that has been unfolding intermittently for nearly two decades and seems to be intensifying . In the paper , dozens of scientists described the recent disaster as the third worldwide mass bleaching of coral reefs since 1998 , but by far the most widespread and damaging . <p> The state of coral reefs is a telling sign of the health of the seas . Their distress and death are yet another marker of the ravages of global climate change . <p> If most of the world 's coral reefs die , as scientists fear is increasingly likely , some of the richest and most colourful life in the ocean could be lost , along with huge sums from reef tourism . Emissions continued to rise , and now the background ocean temperature is high enough that any temporary spike poses a critical risk to reefs . <p> " Climate change is not a future threat , " Hughes said . The corals themselves are tiny polyps that act like farmers , capturing colourful single-celled plants called algae that convert sunlight into food .
