<h> March for Science planned in Vancouver to support work of scientific community in face of Trump era <p> On January 20 , following Donald Trump 's inauguration as the 45th president of the United States , all mentions of climate change were seemingly erased from the White House website . <p> It 's a move that immediately raised red flags among the country 's scientific community , ones that continue to fly high as the Trump administration adamantly denies the realities of global warming , lends time to anti-vaxxer advocates , and prevents government scientists from communicating with the press -- all while consistently spewing " alternative facts " as truth . <p> And thus , the March for Science was born . What began as an offhand suggestion on Reddit has become an international movement that will see thousands of people convening in Washington , D.C. , on April 22 -- Earth Day -- in support of the country 's scientific community . In Vancouver , hundreds of citizens are also expected to march in one of 350-plus satellite events taking place around the world in solidarity . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ SFU and volunteer communications officer for Vancouver 's March for Science , tells the Straight that the goal of the rally is to celebrate and support individuals employed in scientific fields and the work that they do . <p> " I think science is a really important way to look at the world and discover how things work and how we interact with one another , " she explains by phone . " So if scientists are n't able to talk about their science ... we definitely need to take those extra steps so that information is open and accessible to everybody . " <p> The march , which is scheduled to begin at 10 a.m. at Queen Elizabeth Theatre , is open to anyone interested in championing the values of science and scientifically based research , including students , scientists , professors , and more . Attendees are encouraged to carry signs advocating for their beliefs . <p> Although Trump 's problematic rhetoric and policies may seem removed from Vancouver , Charania sees parallels in the president 's actions and past events in Canada . She alludes to the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ approval of the Kinder Morgan Pipeline expansion . <p> " We 've seen cases in Canada and the States where the government is silencing scientists , " she says , " and they 're not able to talk about their research like they should be able to without it being distorted or censored . " <p> So far , over 200 people have RSVP 'd to Vancouver 's March for Science . The event 's core organizing committee , which is made up of primarily women working or studying in science industries , has set up a crowdfunding page to help raise money for permits , equipment rentals , and other fees required for the rally . Any unused funds , the page notes , will be donated to a nonprofit group that aids science and scientists . <p> The coordinators are also working to bring together an inclusive lineup of speakers from the city 's scientific community , which may include social scientists , science interpreters , and others . <p> " The diversity in our opinions and where we come from and who we are not only makes @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ it also makes our communities a lot stronger . " <p> March for Science Vancouver takes place on April 22 , from 10 a.m. to 1:30 p.m. , beginning at Queen Elizabeth Theatre ( 650 Hamilton Street ) .
