<p> Canadian leadership in global food production could help drive an economic growth agenda for the country for generations . This is the aspiration put in front of the Minister of Finance in a report released recently from the Advisory Council on Economic Growth . <p> Last year , the Council was charged by Bill Morneau to recommend bold actions to drive inclusive economic growth . Among many ideas to improve Canada 's economy , it declared that now is the time to leverage those sectors where we could be global leaders so to unlock Canada 's full potential . <p> The Council identified that one of Canada 's most promising sectors is agriculture and food ( ag-food ) . The sector is one of the nation 's largest employers . Being responsible for 2.1 million direct and indirect jobs , it is a force for economic inclusion across rural and urban Canada alike . <p> The sector occupies a position of strength . Canadian ingredients and food have a reputation of safety , quality and trustworthiness . Canada 's food brand is enhanced by the country 's natural advantage @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ arable land -- and an ecological footprint in producing food that is among the best in the world . <p> Global market trends are also in Canada 's favour . By 2050 , global food demand is expected to rise by 70 per cent on the back of three billion more middle-class consumers -- mostly in Asia -- whose diets are shifting , including to more protein . A sophisticated , ethnically diverse consumer base stimulates product development at home and , as the world seeks out food from places it trusts , Canada 's brand will become even more appealing to discriminating consumers both here and abroad . <p> Despite our strengths , Canada ranks fifth globally in agriculture exports and only 11th in processed-food product exports , behind far smaller countries such as Holland , and behind less economically advanced countries such as Brazil . Few of our food companies achieve world scale , and only 50 per cent of our agricultural production is processed here . Increasing our global market share of ag-food exports could add tens of billions of dollars to our economy . <p> Meanwhile @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ agricultural output often supported by an array of subsidies ( which often come at the expense of local ecosystems ) . On trade , Canada 's signature deal with the EU stands in contrast to no deal with China , India and Japan , which represent three of our highest potential ag-food export destination markets . <p> Winning globally requires bolder ambitions , an urgent strategy , and a new form of co-operation between the private and public sector . Continually adding value to what we produce -- based on our country 's advantages -- is our opportunity . <p> If Canada wants to be a nimble global competitor , we ca n't constrain ourselves with a conventional regulatory structure . <p> " Food " issues fall under the domain of several departments which could be better co-ordinated -- for instance to reconcile health and economic policy priorities . While federal ministerial mandate letters now encourage interdepartmental accountabilities , Canada needs to deploy a whole-of-government approach to policy making to make big headway against such issues . <p> Now , more than ever , Canada needs to be clear about @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in its national best interest . This starts by having a bold vision about what we can accomplish . Canada 's ag-food sector could become the trusted global leader in safe , nutritious and sustainable food for the 21st century . <p> Making this common goal a reality will require commitment from across Canada 's diverse food system , and collaboration with related sectors . For example , the ag-food and health sectors can work together to improve nutrition and lower the incidence of diet-related chronic diseases and help reduce future health-care costs . <p> The sector can work with the environmental sector and other industries to respond to climate change and even aim to enhance our performance on managing water , soil , carbon and biodiversity . We can more fully realize the benefits of new digital technologies , including analytics ( " big data " ) , automation and genomics . The sector can also work together with transportation , manufacturing and infrastructure players to improve the competitiveness of both producers and processors . Bringing people together on mutual objectives can attract the talent and investment needed to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ both government and the private sector to set a bold aspiration for growth in Canada 's national strategic interest . While this call to action can start with the Prime Minister and his ministers , leaders from across the food system and adjacent players from finance , technology , health and research , among others , should be encouraged to step up and help make it happen . <p> Being a global food champion for tomorrow can not be held back by how we have worked yesterday . <p> Dominic Barton is the global managing partner of McKinsey &amp; Company .
