<p> Canada 's economy is already taking a hit from global warming , including more frequent droughts and forest fires plus a burst of carbon-tax-induced inflation , a top Bank of Canada official says . <p> " Climate change and actions to address it will have material and pervasive effects on Canada 's economy and financial system , " deputy governor Timothy Lane said in a speech in Montreal Thursday . <p> The effects so far are relatively small , but will become more severe over the longer term . Mr. Lane cited federal government-sponsored research that estimates the annual costs to the Canadian economy will reach $21-billion to $43-billion within four decades . He said Canada is particularly at risk because it 's a major oil and gas producer , manufactures autos and aircraft , and consumes more energy per capita than many other countries . <p> " Adapting to a lower-carbon economy will likely mean more profound structural changes for Canada than many other countries , " he said . <p> Over the longer term , it 's a " near-certainty " that the economy will grow more @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ climate change , Mr. Lane said . <p> Climate change is a " downside risk " that will hang over the economy for some time , leaving the central bank in reaction mode , he added . <p> " We can react to events as they occur , " he said .
