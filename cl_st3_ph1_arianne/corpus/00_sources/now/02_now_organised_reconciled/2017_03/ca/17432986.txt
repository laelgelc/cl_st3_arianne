<p> Long before the term " fake news " barged into the popular lexicon , a niche version of it was quietly infecting the scientific community . It consisted of journals that published fact-bereft mush as legitimate research , assuming few would notice the absence of intellectual rigour or actual evidence . <p> Now , the Citizen 's Tom Spears reports , the publishers of these " predatory " journals , of which there are hundreds , have gone farther : they 're running fake academic conferences . <p> Spears has long reported on companies such as OMICS International , which claim to subject submitted articles to scientific peer review , but do n't . Instead , for a fee , they 'll publish anything that " sounds " scientific . Spears has tested this by deliberately sending obviously fake papers to these journals , which have been eager to publish them . <p> In the newest version of such scientific fraud , OMICS and at least one other company invite academics to submit papers for " official " conferences in various cities .
