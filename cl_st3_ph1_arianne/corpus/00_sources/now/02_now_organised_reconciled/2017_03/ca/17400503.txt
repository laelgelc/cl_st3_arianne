<h> Huge swathe of Australian mangroves ' die of thirst ' <p> The 7,400 hectare die-back of mangroves in Australia 's Gulf of Carpentaria has been confirmed by aerial and satellite surveys ( AFP Photo/Norman Duke ) <p> Sydney ( AFP ) - Thousands of hectares of mangroves in Australia 's remote north " died of thirst " last year , scientists said Tuesday , in the largest climate-related incident of its kind ever recorded . <p> Some 7,400 hectares ( 18,000 acres ) , stretching 1,000 kilometres across the semi-arid Gulf of Carpentaria , perished , according to researchers from Australia 's James Cook University . <p> The so-called die-back -- where mangroves are either dead or defoliated -- was confirmed by aerial and satellite surveys , with subsequent analysis of weather and climate records leading to the conclusion that they died of thirst . <p> World-renowned mangrove ecologist Norm Duke , from James Cook University , said three factors came together to produce the unprecedented event . <p> " From 2011 the coastline had experienced below-average rainfalls , and the 2015/16 drought was particularly severe , " he @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Marine and Freshwater Research . <p> " Secondly the temperatures in the area were at record levels and thirdly some mangroves were left high and dry as the sea level dropped about 20 centimetres ( eight inches ) during a particularly strong El Nino . " <p> El Nino is a climate phenomenon which occurs every four to five years , affecting rainfall patterns and causing both drought and flooding . <p> Duke said these factors were enough to produce what scientists regard as the worst instance of climate-related die-back of mangroves ever reported . <p> " Essentially , they died of thirst , " he said . <p> Mangroves play an important ecological role , not only protecting seagrass and corals by filtering water runoff from the land , but acting as breeding grounds for fish stock and absorbing large amounts of carbon from the atmosphere . <p> Duke said scientists now know that mangroves , like coral reefs , are vulnerable to changes in climate and extreme weather events , with the situation being monitored closely . <p> " The relative dominance of climate influences in this region @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to climate change , " he said . <p> Researchers believe the die-back took place in late November or early December with passing fisherman and scientists conducting unrelated work the first to notice it in the sparsely-populated region . <p> Local rangers at the time reported that creatures like shellfish , which need the shade of the trees , were dying and that turtles and dugongs that are dependent on the ecosystem could soon starve .
