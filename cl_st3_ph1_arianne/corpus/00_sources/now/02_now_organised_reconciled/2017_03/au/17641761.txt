<h> World Science Festival : How music and science combine to monitor climate change <p> Bubbling beakers and Bunsen burners are being traded in for drum machines , sound generators and music software at the World Science Festival in Brisbane . <p> Music researcher Dr Leah Barclay told the 100 Ways To Listen project that artists and scientists working together could unlock the secrets of climate change . <p> " The way we think about music and the way sound artists listen , can really influence and inspire how scientists are responding to climate change , " she said . <p> Recording sound from different environments , allowed music scientists to monitor climate change , by using hydrophones and binaural microphones that mimicked the same technology as the human ear . <p> " Dramatic changes in aquatic ecosystems can go unnoticed simply due to visibility , " said Dr Barclay , whose hydrology piece featured recordings of the world 's water systems collected over a decade . <p> This non-invasive technique called acoustic ecology enabled the researchers " to listen to an active and healthy reef and hear active fish @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ whales , she said . <p> A lack of noise or increased noise levels could be indicators of the ecosystem 's health . <p> " Listening from the perspectives of environmental changes , but also listening from trained musical ears , there 's true possibilities to respond to some of the greatest challenges of our time , " Dr Barclay said . <p> Sonic environment recordings were launched at the festival and arranged into music for augmented reality river walks , surround-sound presentations and virtual reality . <p> " These experiences are designed to inspire people to engage with the environment , to listen to the reef , to listen to climate change and to understand those implications , " Dr Barclay said .
