<p> Bookstore owners , writers , authors , family , friends and a group hopeful of West Papuan independence squeezed into the Women 's Bookshop in Ponsonby last night to celebrate the work of young New Zealand author Bonnie Etherington and her novel The Earth Cries Out . <p> Not only is the novel being celebrated and praised for Etherington 's mastery of the written word , but because of its ability to make the public more aware of life in West Papua , a region controversially ruled by Indonesia since the 1960s . <p> Plagued by media freedom and human rights violations , many media freedom and human rights organisations and several Pacific nations have condemned the widespread arrests and imprisonment of West Papuans for non-violent expression of their political views . <p> These are issues Etherington herself acknowledged speaking with Asia Pacific Report earlier this week , saying she wanted to show readers West Papua 's rich and diverse history , not only its complex political situation . <p> " I really wanted to show multiple sides of West Papua because it is so often forgotten or stereotyped @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is something those who have already read The Earth Cries Out praise . <p> -Advertisement- <p> Harriet Allan , fiction publisher for Penguin Books New Zealand , commended Etherington in a speech on her ability to provide insight into West Papua through the eyes of a child , that of female protagonist Ruth . <p> " As Ruth bears witness to what she sees , we too start to hear the voices that have been silenced by politics , sickness , violence and poverty .
