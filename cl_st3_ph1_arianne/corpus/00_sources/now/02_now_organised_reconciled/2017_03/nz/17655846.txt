Photo/File <p> GP funding <p> In response to a Chronicle story on March 9 , Whanganui District Health Board notes the article incorrectly referred to " removal of subsidies from the Ministry of Health " . The reason this is incorrect is that general practices can choose to opt in and out of the VLCA funding scheme . <p> GP practices with more than 50 per cent high-need patients can choose to become a very low cost access ( VLCA ) practice . High need includes Maori and Pacific populations , and people who live in high-need areas . <p> This entitles practices to receive an additional funding subsidy from the Ministry of Health to offer patients reduced fees . <p> The maximum fee for VLCA practices is $18 per visit during normal business hours . The reduced fees apply to all patients of the practice , regardless of whether they are high-need . While the health board supports the approach that funding be allocated to those most at need , we explained that Government policy requires that VLCA funding is applied across everyone enrolled in the practice . <p> It is unfortunate that Castlecliff Health wrote to patients before options could be explored through their primary health organisation , the National Hauora Coalition ( NHC ) . <p> The NHC has highlighted the inequities in primary care funding . Its report demonstrated evidence that some practices are struggling to remain financially viable due to large numbers of Maori , Pacific and poor patients . <p> While it is disappointing that patient fees have been raised , the NHC believes primary care funding arrangements pose an unfair cost burden on these practices , which ultimately leaves them unsustainable , both fiscally and clinically .
