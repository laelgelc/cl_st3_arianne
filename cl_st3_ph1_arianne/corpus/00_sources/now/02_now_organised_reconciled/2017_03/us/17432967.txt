" The question is how fast are we going to see these changes . " <p> Three different species shrank noticeably about 54 million years ago when the planet suddenly heated up . It shrank about 4 percent ; while it may not seem like much , it 's noticeable because studies of the animal over millions of years showed it was usually getting bigger over time , D'Ambrosia said . <p> Previous studies have documented a similar shrinking of mammals , including another early horse ancestor , during an earlier warming about 56 million years ago . D'Ambrosia said it 's unlikely that the shrinking only happened there . <p> In hotter climates , mammals and other warm-blooded animals need to shed heat so they shrink . Smaller animals have more skin -- or fur -- per pound than bigger animals so more heat can escape , making them better adapted for warmer climate .
