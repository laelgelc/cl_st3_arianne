<h> Top Trump Aide Accused of Breaking Criminal Conflict of Interest Law <p> Another member of President Donald Trump 's inner circle may have blurred ethical lines , this time by breaking criminal conflict of interest laws . <p> Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington ( CREW ) filed a complaint Tuesday with White House counsel Don McGhan , accusing assistant to the president and director of strategic initiatives Christopher Liddell of improperly participating in meetings that took place between Trump and companies in which Liddell held millions of dollars in stock . <p> According to CREW : <p> Criminal law prohibits a federal employee like Liddell from participating personally and substantially in any particular matter to which , to his knowledge , he has a financial interest . Liddell and his wife apparently held $72,000 of stock in Ford and GM . On February 3rd , Liddell again participated in a meeting with business leaders representing companies in which he apparently owned $2.3 million of stock : BlackRock , GM , IHS Markit LTD , IMB , JP Morgan Chase , Pepsi , Tesla , and Walmart . <p> The Office of Government Ethics ( OGE ) has since issued a certificate of divestiture covering those holdings -- but as the CREW complaint ( pdf ) notes , that certificate is dated February 9 , well after the meetings in question . <p> As such , the complaint reads , and given Liddell 's likely role in organizing those meetings and developing and/or approving the invite lists , " he may be viewed as having personally and substantially participated in the White House meetings ... that would have a direct and predictable effect on his financial interests .
