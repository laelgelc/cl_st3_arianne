<h> Global emissions slowing - but not fast enough <p> What would a US withdrawal from the Paris Agreement mean for the emissions that cause climate warming ? <p> A growing array of evidence suggests that twists and turns at the federal level wo n't automatically change how US firms behave . The Trump administration may roll back US regulations on clean power and on methane leaks from oil and gas operations , for example , but many states already have their rules in place , and the courts will likely halt some of Mr Donald Trump 's most ambitious rollbacks . <p> Indeed , states such as California and New York that account for most of the nation 's economic growth - and thus most of the innovation and technology and policy - are the bluest politically and poised to do even more to cut emissions . <p> Here 's the good news : New analysis in a World Economic Forum ( WEF ) report argues that global efforts to promote clean energy have turned a corner . Every major success in international environmental diplomacy can be traced back to underlying technology and economic patterns demonstrating how to solve the problem at a reasonable cost . Cutting pollutants harmful to the ozone layer , for example , was possible in large part because the prospect of regulation unleashed innovation - and deployment of more benign substitutes . <p> Much of Europe is getting cleaner at a faster rate than the global average , but so are important emerging economies such as Mexico , which is in the midst of massive energy @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Consistently , the top performers have been small countries with large hydropower resources . Other countries such as France and South Korea fare well with an energy mix that includes a great deal of nuclear power . <p> Increasingly , countries are improving their energy performance by relying more on renewables . Technology improvements have boosted energy gains in many countries . But the global emissions curve , which has been rising almost steadily ever since the industrial era , has begun to grow at a slower rate and might now be on the cusp of bending down . <p> Here 's why the Paris Agreement matters : The policies that countries pledged in Paris - most of which largely reflected what governments had already planned - will help create transparency that can make it possible to coordinate policies to bend the curve even further . <p> These fundamental trends suggest that diplomats should be less focused on who is formally " in " and " out " in the Paris Agreement . The rate of bending is n't enough to stop warming at 2 deg C above pre-industrial levels , a widely discussed goal that will soon need revision . Emissions have risen so high and for so long that there is n't enough time to reverse course . <p> Most diplomats have looked at this fact and called on countries to radically increase their effort - including the rapid deployment of technologies that probably are n't viable in the real world . More mitigation efforts will be needed , but the bigger lesson for diplomats is to focus even more on how to help the most vulnerable societies adapt to the climate change that is all but inevitable . <p> 2 ) International agreements need to be flexible and improve cooperation on the problems of climate change and energy-related policy challenges . The genius of the Paris Agreement is that its bottom-up approach is designed to achieve exactly that high level of flexibility . <p> The Trump administration is seeking flexibility of its own , but it is hard to imagine a better framework , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Given the flexibility of the Paris Agreement , exiting this agreement would run exactly contrary to US interests . <p> Rapid and complete decoupling of emissions from the economy wo n't be easy , but many countries and firms are trying to achieve that outcome in many different ways . They are running experiments in policy and business , and the rest of the world will benefit if the right lessons are learnt . This new WEF study can help put perspective on what 's working . <p> Developing nations offer important lessons . What are some key takeaways ? Already , it is clear that while the world 's top energy performers remain in the advanced industrialised world , there are important lessons coming from developing and emerging economies . They include countries that have found ways to rationalise their consumption of coal , such as China . They include market-friendly reforms - such as in Mexico - to boost economic output along with cleaner energy systems . And they include countries that broke through political roadblocks to improving energy utilisation , such as Uruguay . <p> With @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ emerging economies , these will be important lessons to share . After all , the problem of global warming is a problem of global emissions . Understanding what works and how international policy coordination can help bend emissions curves will be the central challenge for policymakers . <p> In this process , the United States remains relevant , and with US leadership the Paris Agreement could evolve further to become more effective . Without that leadership , it is likely that other countries will step in to fill the vacuum . Indeed , China is already poised to play that role in part because the flexibility of the Paris approach aligns well with how China would like to cooperate internationally on a wide range of topics .
