<p> DAVIS AND SANTA BARBARA ( REUTERS ) - It is one of the most valuable agricultural commodities in the world and yet scientists know surprisingly little about coffee . <p> " When we started looking at coffee we started to realise that it is kind of like an orphan crop , " said Juan Medrano , Professor of Animal Genetics at the University of California , Davis . " Very little has been done in introducing advance genetic technology to improve coffee . " <p> But now the secrets behind what makes an espresso so tasty are starting to be revealed . <p> Scientists in California have mapped the genome for Arabica coffee , the species that accounts for 70 per cent of global consumption . <p> " DNA is the book of life . So if we can understand the genes that are involved in relation to different traits , related to production , related to quality , related to adaptation , that adds value to our knowledge .
