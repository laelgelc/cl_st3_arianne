<h> Does ' Brown Fat ' Explain a Link Between Temperature and Diabetes ? <p> Are rising temperatures around the world also increasing the rates of diabetes ? A new study from the Netherlands suggests that there may be a link between warming global temperatures and a higher prevalence of the disease , but not all experts are convinced . <p> When the researchers analyzed average global temperatures and the rates of type 2 diabetes , they found that a 1.8-degree Fahrenheit ( 1 degree Celsius ) increase in temperature was associated with an increase of 0.3 cases of diabetes per 1,000 people . In addition , the researchers looked at data on the average temperatures in each state and territory for the same years . <p> Overall , the rate of people being diagnosed with type 2 diabetes was higher in warmer years , the researchers found . <p> Data on worldwide type 2 diabetes rates was not available , the researchers noted . Previous research has found that colder temperatures can activate brown fat and may lead to modest weight loss , according to the study . <p> Additionally , in a small study published in the journal Nature Medicine in 2015 , a group of researchers found that when patients with type 2 diabetes were exposed to moderately cold temperatures for 10 days , their insulin sensitivity improved . If temperature played a role , the findings would be similar in both states , he said . <p> The study authors , led by Lisanne Blauw , an endocrinology researcher at the Leiden University Medical Center in the Netherlands , noted that their findings do not prove cause and effect . In addition , the researchers were not able to account for changes in body mass index , which is associated with type 2 diabetes , over time because of a lack of available data .
