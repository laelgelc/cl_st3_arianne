<h> Noting that the announcement of the water price hike provoked a strong reaction from Singaporeans , PM Lee acknowledged that the Government should have taken the time to explain the matter first . <p> SINGAPORE : The 30 per cent increase in the price of water is " absolutely necessary " , as it is a " scarce " resource and " not cheap to produce " , said Prime Minister Lee Hsien Loong on Saturday ( Mar 25 ) . <p> Mr Lee noted that the announcement , made by Finance Minister Heng Swee Keat in his Budget statement " provoked a strong reaction from Singaporeans " . <p> " In retrospect , perhaps we should have spent more time explaining this before announcing the increase , then people would n't have been so surprised , " he said . <p> But the increase is " absolutely necessary " , he added , underlining just how precious water is as a resource . National water agency PUB is also investing in various infrastructure like NEWater and desalination plants , pipelines and sewage treatment plants . <p> " If @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , PUB would still have to build all this , and we would still have to pay for this . But instead of paying for it through the water tariff , we would have to pay for it through our taxes and I think it 's fairer to pay for it through the water tariff . Those who use the water pay for the water , rather than from general taxes and we use the GST or your income taxes , or your car taxes to pay for water . " <p> As the economy grows , the country will need more water , said Mr Lee , adding that climate change will also cause the supply of water to be less predictable . <p> And as the population in the Malaysian state of Johor continues to grow , this means that the resource will come under pressure due to a corresponding growth in demand , he said . <p> Singapore draws more than half of its water supply from Linggiu Reservoir .
