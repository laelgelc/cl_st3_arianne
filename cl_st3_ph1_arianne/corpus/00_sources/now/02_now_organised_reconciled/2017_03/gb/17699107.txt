<h> Deadly heat stress could threaten hundreds of millions even if climate targets reached <p> Deadly levels of heat stress could become more common in megacities , if global temperatures continue to rise . Even the climate change measures set out in the Paris Agreement might not be enough to prevent it <p> Deadly heat stress is projected to affect hundreds of millions more people each year under relatively little additional climate warming . The Paris Agreement commits the international community to limit global warming to no more than 2C above pre-industrial ( late 19th century ) air temperatures , with an aspirational target of 1.5C . In our latest research , which looked at the impact of global temperature rises on megacities , we found that even if 1.5C is achieved , large increases in the frequency of deadly heat are expected . <p> By 2050 about 350 million more people living in megacities could be exposed to deadly heat each year . <p> Humans become " heat stressed " when the body absorbs more heat than is tolerable . This mechanism works better in a drier atmosphere ( which is why steam rooms feel hotter than saunas -- even at the same air temperature ) . The heat index is a measure that combines this humidity effect with air temperature to provide a " feels like " temperature . A heat index in excess of about 40.6C is considered dangerous to human health . <p> As global air temperatures rise , observations and experiments with climate models suggest that atmospheric moisture content also climbs . This means that the heat index ( and how hot it feels ) rises faster than air temperature . The situation in Lagos illustrates this well . If global warming reaches 1.5C by the end of the century ( at which time the population of Lagos may have increased elevenfold and dangerous heat may be 100 times more common ) the heat stress burden could be more than a thousand times greater than the recent past . <p> Across all megacity regions , if the 1.5C limit is breached by the 2050s , as many as 350 million people globally could be regularly exposed to dangerous heat stress . This is more than a fourfold increase compared with 1979-2005 . <p> Heat stress sensitivity to global temperature rise and the potential human impacts -- even at 1.5C above pre-industrial levels -- provide a strong incentive for limiting global warming .
