<p> Tamborine Mountain and its 7,500 residents might well be the largest community in Queensland , if not Australia , to prosper without a drop of town water . <p> The popular Gold Coast hinterland tourist retreat has cafes , restaurants , galleries , gift shops , wineries , schools , a swimming pool , a golf course , farms , other businesses and thousands of homes that all rely on groundwater bores or rainwater tanks . <p> Its underground aquifers have also been the source of hundreds of millions of litres of water for beverage giants such as Coca-Cola . <p> Tamborine " spring water " sold under brands such as Mount Franklin and Neverfail , while pristine , is a marketing exercise . The water comes from bores . And when the bores that households and other businesses rely on slow to a trickle or run dry , fingers of blame point towards the water sellers . <p> The water sellers , a small but resolute bunch , argue with some justification that their portrayal as community villains is at wildly odds with the facts .
