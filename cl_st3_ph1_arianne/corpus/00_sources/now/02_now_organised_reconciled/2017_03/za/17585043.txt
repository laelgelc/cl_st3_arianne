But the moment of darkness should also serve as a reminder , activists say , of another problem that gets far less attention : light pollution . <p> In the United States and western Europe , that <p> Even the most ardent critics of light pollution are not saying cities should go dark , or that lighting is not an essential element of urban life . <p> But society needs to address a growing list of concerns , they suggest . <p> " In general , it 's getting worse , " Diana Umpierre , president of the International Dark-Sky Association , said of light pollution in her home state of Florida . <p> And things are moving in the wrong direction , she told AFP . <p> " We are predicted to have 15 million more residents in the next 50 years " -- with all the extra lighting that entails . <p> By contrast , in Chad , the Central African Republic and Madagascar -- not coincidentally among the poorest countries in the world -- three-quarters of people have a clear view of the heavens . <p>
