<h> California is on fire . From across the Pacific , Australians watch on and buckle up <p> California is ablaze , again . Currently , the second and third largest fires in the US state 's history are burning at the same time , and are only partially controlled . Already , seven people have died and 2,144 structures are damaged -- and their fire season still has months to run . <p> The outbreak continues a relentless trend of bigger and more destructive fires in the western US , including California ' largest fire in 2018 . <p> For Australians , the spectacle of California burning is deeply concerning . It 's just months since our last fire season , concentrated in a band of eucalyptus forests along the continent 's southeast coast . <p> There are strong parallels between the two disasters : drought , parched landscapes , high temperatures , prolonged heatwaves and dry lightning storms to set it all off . And both Australia and California are particularly vulnerable as climate change makes bushfires worse .
