<h> A record $37.7 million : Why Australia 's corporate chiefs are paid so well <p> Elizabeth Sheedy <p> August 24 , 2020 <p> IDP Education chief executive Andrew Barkla . <p> The annual chief executive pay report produced by the Australian Council of Superannuation Investors is a must-read for shareholders and members of superannuation funds . <p> The latest report , relating to the financial year that ended in 2019 , reveals a new record for the money actually paid in one year , so-called realised remuneration , of $37.7 million . The extraordinary sum went to Andrew Barkla , the chief executive of little-known company IDP Education . <p> He beat the previous record set by the chief executive of Domino 's Pizza Don Meij who took home $36.84 million in 2017 . <p> Paul Perreault from CSL took out second place with realised pay of $30.5 million , followed by Philippe Wolgen from Clinuvel Pharmaceuticals with $20.6 million and Michael Clarke from Treasury Wine Estates with $19.9 million . <h> Long-term incentives that are n't long-term <p> They are given shares which they wo n't be able @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ years in the future . <p> How much they get when they sell them depends on the share price when their shares vest , meaning they can boost their payout by maximising the price . One way they can do it is through share buybacks . A company buying back its own shares is ostensibly returning capital . But it is also pushing up its share price , and can do it near to vesting day . <p> A recent US study points to a surge in buybacks , and hence share prices , ahead of vesting dates . After vesting dates , share prices tend to fall , perhaps because companies that have bought back shares are left with less money with which to expand and withstand shocks . <p> This suggests buybacks serve short-term rather than long-term goals . <p> Regardless of whether or not share prices are ramped up ahead of vesting dates ( and there is no suggestion this happened at IDP Education ) most long-term incentives are n't long-term at all . <p> A typical ' long-term ' incentive for a chief executive lasts three @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ longer 10-20 year horizons . <p> The time-limited nature of ' long-term ' incentives might explain why firms fail to come to grips with climate change and other long-term threats . <p> Why would you , as chief executive , make investments that will make sense over 10 to 20 years , when you are only paid to consider three years ? <p> Andrew Barkla 's case is instructive . <p> He was paid $37.7 million for outstanding performance up to the middle of 2019 , before the pandemic hit . His firm 's share price peaked at $24.60 in February this year and now sits at $19.25 , a fall of 22% . <p> The risks inherent in its business model have become clear in a way they were n't when he received the payout . <h> There 's hope <p> The Australian Council of Superannuation Investors represents institutional investors including super funds .
