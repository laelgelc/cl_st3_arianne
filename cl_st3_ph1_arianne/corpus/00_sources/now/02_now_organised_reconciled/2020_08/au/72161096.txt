<p> Industry and conservation groups have criticised changes to the federal government 's " safeguard mechanism " that allow companies to increase emissions depending on how their business is performing . The Clean Energy Regulator in late July granted companies including BHP and Anglo American the right to increase emissions by a combined 1.6 million tonnes of carbon dioxide a year , reports Guardian Australia . The system is changing to set limits depending on a company 's emissions intensity per unit of production , rather than total emissions . The permitted increases are almost equivalent to the 1.7 million tonnes of emissions reduction purchased by the federal government through the $2.5 billion emissions reduction fund at an auction in March . Those cuts cost taxpayers $27.6 million . " It is achieving the exact opposite of what it was designed to achieve , " Australian Conservation Foundation climate change program manager Gavan McFadzean said .
