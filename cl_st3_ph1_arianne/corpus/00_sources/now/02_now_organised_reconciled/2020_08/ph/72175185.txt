<h> ASEAN at 53 : building resilience , gearing for recovery <p> Responding to the challenge of biodiversity loss is vital in addressing the current health and ecological crises , as well as treading the path towards recovery . Photo by ACB . <p> LAGUNA , August 8 -- Since its establishment 53 years ago , the Association of Southeast Asian Nations ( ASEAN ) has forged ahead and stood united in the face of adversities . As the Region faces larger challenges , its robust and enduring cooperation is needed now more than ever . <p> This year 's theme for the ASEAN summit " Cohesive and Responsive ASEAN in 2020 " is most apt in the light of the global health crisis and the compounded consequences of climate change . The ASEAN Member States ( AMS ) unite on joint strategies to keep the people and the environment safe . <p> The ASEAN Centre for Biodiversity ( ACB ) is one with the Member States in commemoration of the ASEAN 's foundation . The ACB reaffirms its commitment to the Region 's principles and vision through its continued @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ agenda in consideration of the COVID-19 pandemic and its immediate and long-term consequences . <p> The global pandemic has underscored the urgency of more robust health systems and economic structures . Moreover , the crisis also brought to light the value of integrating biodiversity concerns into health and socio-economic programmes and policies . Clearly , ensuring intersectoral cohesion will not only guarantee the sustainable use of our rich biological resources , but will also redound to our well-being and survival and contribute to national and regional economic recovery , as well . <p> The ACB recently supported a study on the state of use of economic values of ecosystems services in decision-making processes in the ASEAN . Results of the Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services Assessment and Economic Analysis for Management , Policy and Innovative Financing Applications ( BESA++ ) pointed out the need for countries to recognise the contribution of biodiversity to economic development . The results may be utilised for determining trade-offs and future priority actions as we move towards building back without compromising the environment . <p> Among sectors most affected by the pandemic is the tourism sector @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the ASEAN economy . In fact , a large percentage of the types of tourism in the ASEAN region are nature-based and some 80 percent of the ASEAN Heritage Parks are tourism destinations . Through the ASEAN Heritage Parks Programme , a flagship initiative of the ASEAN , the ACB is mobilising necessary support to help maintain the quality of biodiversity attractions in AHPs during this crisis in preparation for the gradual re-opening of tourism . <p> We , in the ACB , also acknowledge Viet Nam 's leadership of the ASEAN in this most challenging year . As the current chair of the ASEAN , Viet Nam advanced cooperation on environmental protection and biodiversity conservation . Last month , Viet Nam , led by Nature and Biodiversity Conservation Agency and Viet Nam Environment Administration , initiated a national consultation workshop on the post-2020 global biodiversity framework to flesh out inputs and insights on realigning their national biodiversity goals with the global targets . This initiative is valuable as the ASEAN lends a strong voice to the development of the landmark biodiversity framework so that it may reflect the context @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ loss , tackling outbreaks such as COVID-19 , and responding to the climate emergency . <p> The active participation of all the AMS in the crafting of the global biodiversity framework is an opportunity for the Region to develop long-term strategies to create a new normal that further integrates environmental protection and conservation into its various pillars . This endeavour will only be possible through meaningful partnerships and greater regional cooperation .
