<h> Dr Miles Mack : Fleeting glimpse of a low carbon future <p> Union Street , Aberdeen , during the local lockdown . Picture by Scott Baxter <p> In the depths of lockdown , driving to work was an eerie experience . <p> Arriving at the junction with the trunk road was strange , as rather than waiting for a gap in the traffic I could pull right out because , while the rest of the population was largely confined to their homes , I was an essential worker still on my short morning commute . <p> When not working , like most folk , I was spending far more time in my garden and it was noticeable that there was no background hum of traffic but a more vivid soundtrack from nature . <p> Skylarks were singing and the blackbirds ' liquid song was clear in the air . There were no aeroplane trails in the sky . <p> It was as if the Earth was sighing with relief that the human onslaught had been turned down . <p> Human behaviour changed too . Bike shops sold out of new bikes , old bikes were dusted off and tyres pumped up . <p> Roads that were previously seen as too dangerous saw a steady stream of cyclists out to get some fresh air and respite from the monotony of lockdown . <p> At the health centre , work also changed . <p> Routine appointments have been replaced by phone calls and video consultations and for once , there is space in the car park . <p> I was in the habit of making regular trips to Edinburgh , Glasgow and London to attend meetings and I have learned that it is possible to do all of them by Zoom , phone , email or some other means . Or not at all . <p> Was this what a low carbon future might look like ? Crossing main roads , like the one close to my house , is often a thrill-ride of risk and reward for those on foot , however cautious . <p> The main road is a wide , straight hill , about two miles long and it 's hard to know how fast approaching vehicles are going , so we have learned not only patience but also , perhaps during lockdown , complacency . <p> " Watch the junction ! " is a mantra which we have had to revive now that it 's busy again . <p> I am concerned that walking for leisure is going to diminish again , as we return to our cars . <p> The pandemic may have made things worse for the environment as travelling by car must surely be seen not only as convenient but also , as having the lowest risk of virus infection . <p> And who can blame people for trying to stay safe ? <p> Why expose yourself to possible infection on public transport , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ highway when you can cocoon yourself and your children in the protective bubble of your own vehicle ? <p> It 's a no-brainer for those lucky enough to have the choice . <p> The threat from Covid-19 is still immediate and will require further sacrifices and difficult decisions to overcome . <p> Right now , global warming feels much less scary . We had a glimpse of what a low carbon economy would be like but that is not how many want , or are able , to live . <p> The roads were quiet and safe , but we lost the ability to travel far and so found ourselves separated from our colleagues , friends and loved ones . <p> I hope we can find the energy and commitment we will need to tackle the challenges of climate change in a way that sticks . <p> Right now , it seems that reversing climate change is once more on hold .
