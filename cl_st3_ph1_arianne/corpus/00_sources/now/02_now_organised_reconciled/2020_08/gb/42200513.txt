<h> Richard Lochhead takes issue of farm tenants ' absolute right to buy to new level <p> by Ewan Pate <p> September 20 2013 , 9.14am <p> Luke Borwick , chairman of Scottish Land and Estates . <p> Scotland 's Cabinet Secretary for Rural Affairs , Richard Lochhead , has raised the issue of giving farm tenants an absolute right to buy ( ARTB ) to a new level . <p> At the Highland Show , in a rather off-the cuff comment to agricultural journalists , he said , to the consternation of interested parties , that ARTB was back on the agenda . <p> After giving evidence to the Scottish Parliament 's Rural Affairs , Climate Change and Environment Committee , Mr Lochhead said it was now " inconceivable " that ARTB would not be included in an imminent review of tenant farming legislation . <p> This is a considerable shift in position . <p> The Highland Show comments came as a shock to both landowners ' and tenants ' representatives if only because the measure had until then been conspicuously shelved . <p> Mr Lochhead had considered that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of letting land , but in his Highland Show comments he said that he had lost patience with this approach . <p> In his view insufficient land had been made available to let since the last change in legislation in 2003 . <p> He used Wednesday 's evidence-giving session as an opportunity to indicate that this opinion had hardened , but at the same time he was very clear that ARTB , if introduced , would only apply to secure tenancies granted under the 1991 Act . <p> Mr Lochhead said : " I have taken this opportunity to confirm more detail about the Scottish Government 's review of tenant farming legislation . <p> " I have already made clear that it would be inconceivable for this review not to include consideration of absolute right to buy . <p> " Today I was pleased to clarify , for the avoidance of any doubt , that consideration of absolute right to buy will be restricted to secure 1991 Act this . <p> " Landlords ' views must also be heard . " <p> The fact that Mr Lochhead himself intends to lead the review is a significant indication of the importance he is giving to land reform and agricultural holdings legislation . <p> Scottish Land &amp; Estates , which represents landowners across Scotland , responded , saying it supports the Cabinet Secretary 's call to ensure a vibrant tenant farming sector but maintains that the introduction of an ARTB will prevent this from being achieved . <p> Luke Borwick , SL&amp;E chairman , said : " We have made some very progressive proposals to help take the sector forward and are @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ change that will benefit agriculture . <p> " We welcome the Cabinet Secretary 's general comments today to the committee about the forthcoming agricultural holdings review , and like him hope that it delivers exciting measures which benefit the sector . <p> " We will play an active role in that review . <p> " Our members operate land-based businesses , and tenant farming is an integral part of these businesses . <p> " It is in our members ' interest to let land and , above all , they want to see a vibrant and successful tenanted sector that gives the next generation of farmers a chance to get going . <p> " Our stance remains the same with regard to ARTB and the negative impact of continuing calls for its introduction are clearly evident through the ever-increasing loss of confidence in letting . <p> " The debate should be based on evidence that clearly investigates the impact of ARTB on a vibrant tenanted sector and agriculture as a whole . <p> " The review must provide an opportunity for all those involved in agriculture to have a say @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ look at all issues rather than overly focusing on one area .
