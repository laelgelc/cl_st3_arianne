<h> We found the least-visited country on each continent - Here 's why you should go <h> We found the least-visited country on each continent - Here 's why you should go <p> Want to escape the crowds on your next trip ? These gems are as hidden as can be , says Luke Rix-Standing . <p> Modern holidays place tremendous value on getting off the beaten track , and it sometimes seems that the most coveted commodity for today 's tourists is staying as far away from each other as possible . <p> It sounds obvious , but if you really want somewhere not-too-touristy , go somewhere that does n't have many tourists . We looked at the least visited country from each continent - based on the latest annual visitor figures compiled in 2017 by the UN World Tourism Organisation ( UNWTO ) - and found a collection of tourist-neglected nations with plenty to offer intrepid travellers . <p> Looking for authentic experiences away from the usual tourist trail ? Here they are ... <p> 1 . Europe - San Marino - 78,000 <p> A teeny-tiny microstate encased @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Brentford , and the floor space of Woking . Nevertheless , it packs plenty of national pride , and with roots dating back to the 4th century AD , it claims to be the world 's oldest existing republic . <p> With hilltop fortresses , duty-free shopping and panoramic views from the peak of Monte Titano , there 's plenty to reward the modern tourist . The problem is that most of them do n't know it 's there . <p> The irony is that it 's proportionally still quite well-trodden . Around 78,000 tourists is a paltry intake , but it dwarfs the native population by more than two to one . <p> 2 . North America - St Vincent &amp; the Grenadines - 76,000 <p> From the same island chain as Aruba , Antigua and Barbados , this 32-piece archipelago comes complete with sun , sand and salsa . <p> Thatched beach huts rise over azure seas , emerald palms stand guard above sparkling sands , and colourful corals glint alluringly beneath the waves . Otherwise , St Vincent is a production-line Caribbean paradise - and we mean that in the best possible way . <p> 3 . South America - Guyana - 247,000 <p> Narrowly beating out next door neighbour Suriname as South America 's least visited nation , Guyana is not so much under-touristed , as under-populated full stop . Fewer than 800,000 people fill an area larger than Britain , most of it coated in a thick layer of Amazonian jungle . <p> There are few roads , so tours employ a mixture of planes , riverboats and good old-fashioned trekking to hop between traditional tribal communities . Comoros is tiny , and miles under the radar . <p> Shaped and reshaped repeatedly by the eruptions of Karthala Volcano , main island Grande Comore is a photogenic cocktail of black volcanic rock and dazzlingly bright white sand . A ferry ride to Moheli Island yields one the world 's premier turtle nesting sites , and seasonal visitors are all but guaranteed sightings by land and by sea . <p> The capital city , Moroni , is a rickety jumble of spiky minarets and crumbling colonial facades , headed up by the 13th century Badjanani Mosque . <p> Untouched by foreign investment and so cheap it 's almost uncomfortable , most current tourists are curiosity-seekers in search of adventure and oddity . And that is exactly the appeal . <p> 5 . Asia - Timor-Leste - 74,000 <p> Go back a few years and it 's easy to see @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . At the turn of the millennium , the not-yet-nation was engaged in a bitter independence struggle against Indonesia , and has only been a sovereign state since 2002 . <p> Now peaceful if not prosperous , the island 's pristine reefs , jungle caves and misty mountain trails sit waiting for a tourism influx that just has n't come . Capital city Dili is a tranquil waterfront town perfect for evening strolls , with a smattering of museums and memorials . <p> To travel in Timor-Leste is to enter a world without tourism , and by land or sea the biodiversity is of the very highest level . Consider the accurate motto of the Timorese tourist board : " Discover the unexplored . " <p> 6 .
