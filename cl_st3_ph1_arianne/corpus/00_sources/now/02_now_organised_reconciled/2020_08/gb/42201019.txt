<h> Revamp of botanical glasshouses in Edinburgh advances <p> THEY are centuries old and their contribution to our knowledge of the world 's plants is unparalleled . <p> Now historic glasshouses dating from 1670 are to be restored at the Royal Botanic Gardens in Edinburgh ( RBGE ) in an attempt to safeguard thousands of species of flora . <p> Clearing work is again under way as part of the ? 70 million Edinburgh Biomes project . <p> The initiative will offer accommodation for 4,000 species being moved to allow refurbishment of the 350-year-old public and research glasshouses . <p> The deteriorating A-listed heritage buildings are in desperate need of upgrading . <p> Their overhaul will also avoid the loss of plants that are considered central to research work taking place in more than 35 countries . <p> Bosses say that work to the old glasshouses -- which would have been deemed unsafe by 2025 -- will involve replacing heating and power systems with energy-efficient installations . <p> The Edinburgh Biomes project will also help support conservation and provide education surrounding topics such as biodiversity and climate change . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ including apprenticeships , and provide opportunities for local people , graduates , the long-term unemployed and those from disadvantaged backgrounds . <p> Welcoming the latest development , Simon Milne , who is regius keeper at RBGE , said : " Amidst the many personal , social and economic tragedies and challenges of the last few months , there is heightened awareness of interdependencies between human and environmental health , social justice , sustainable development and economic recovery . <p> " We are hugely grateful for the support of Environment Secretary Roseanna Cunningham and for the investment of the Scottish Government to date .
