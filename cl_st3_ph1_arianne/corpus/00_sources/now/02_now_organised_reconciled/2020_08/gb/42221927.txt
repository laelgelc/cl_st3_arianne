<p> Two thousand metres up in the mountains above Switzerland 's Engadin Valley , Felix Keller stands in a rocky ridge overlooking the Morteratsch glacier , one of the biggest in the Alps . He points to a rock-strewn plateau speckled with spruces and bushes far beyond him . " In the early 1990s , all this used to be ice , " he says . " But now , look : it 's all trees and boulders . Things are changing very , very quickly . " <p> Like nearly all its Alpine neighbours , this frozen giant has retreated like a dying snake recoiling into its pit , as temperatures across the region have warmed faster than most other places on the planet over the past century .
