" <p> This was the post-Warhol , post-punk generation , people with something to say who were n't quite sure how to say it yet but until they 'd worked out what it was they were going to have a ball anyway . For all the show 's scattergun enthusiasm and air of cultural zeitgeist in search of a cause , an act would take the stage that week that would change everything ; for the event , for himself and arguably for the entire New York music and cabaret scene . <p> It was towards the end of the bill when compere David McDermott announced , " Ladies and gentlemen , what you are about to hear is not a recording . His dark hair was styled into three points and his face was heavily made up , his eyes regarding the audience with a rare intensity . A few titters ran through the audience as this strange apparition leaned forward into the microphone . <p> Then he began to sing . <p> To a crowd who 'd spent the evening watching acts who could hit barely one note in three , a counter-tenor singing in a register that was effectively soprano was stunning . Klaus Nomi had arrived . <p> " Everyone else in the show was charming , inept , funny , " said Nomi 's future musical collaborator Kristian Hofmann .
