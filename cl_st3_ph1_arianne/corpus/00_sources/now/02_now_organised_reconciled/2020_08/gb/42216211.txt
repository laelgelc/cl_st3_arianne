Australian subsidiaries that are 100 per cent owned by a foreign company and that have no common Australian head company between the nonresident parent and the Australian resident subsidiaries are also allowed to consolidate . <p> Groups that choose to consolidate must include all 100 per cent owned entities and the choice is irrevocable . <p> Consolidated groups file a single tax return and calculate their taxable income or loss ignoring all intra-group transactions . There are a number of other benefits of grouping . <p> Other implications of forming a tax consolidated group include a resetting of the tax cost base of certain assets to market value . <p> Dividends paid by an Australian company <p> If fully franked dividends ( that is , dividends paid out of profits which have been subject to Australian corporate tax ) are paid by an Australian subsidiary to its foreign parent , no dividend withholding tax is payable . To the extent that dividends are unfranked , dividend withholding tax of 30 per cent ( or as reduced under the relevant double tax treaty ) is payable on the gross unfranked amount . <p> Non-resident companies who hold investments on capital account are exempt from Australian CGT in most circumstances except where they dispose of Taxable Australian Property which broadly consists of : <p> Australian real property ; or <p> Non-portfolio interests ( 10 per cent or more ) in entities where the value of the interest is wholly or principally attributable to Australian real property . <p> Subject to some limited exclusions ( eg property with a market value of less than A$2.5 million ) , as from 1 July 2016 , purchasers will be obliged to withhold 10 per cent of any sale proceeds from the disposal of Taxable Australian Property and remit this to the ATO , with the vendor needing to file a tax return to claim back any withheld amounts in excess of their final tax liability . <p> Debt funding of an Australian company <p> Interest withholding tax of 10 per cent is imposed on interest paid by an Australian company to a foreign non-resident lending entity unless an exemption applies . If , however , the beneficial owner of the nonresident lender has a permanent establishment in Australia and the interest is effectively connected with the permanent establishment , interest withholding tax should not be applied . <p> Under debt and equity classification rules there may be situations where interest payable is treated as if it were a dividend . Interest paid on instruments that are classified as equity interests will not be deductible for the entity paying the return , but may be frankable . Similarly , dividends paid on interests that are classified as debt interests may be deductible to the entity making the payment and not frankable . <p> PwC <p> 33 <p> Corporate tax <p> Thin capitalisation measures apply to the total debt of the Australian operations of multinational groups ( including branches of those groups ) . The rules aim to prevent excessive gearing of an Australian entity . Interest deductions are denied to the extent that borrowing exceeds the safe-harbour ratio . The safe harbour threshold is ( broadly ) 60 per cent of the entity 's Australian net assets , and applies to the Australian group on an associate-inclusive basis . <p> Where borrowing exceeds the safe-harbour ratio , multinational groups are not affected by the rules if they can satisfy the arm 's length debt test or the worldwide gearing test : <p> The arm 's length debt test permits taxpayers to identify the maximum level of debt that an arm 's length lender would loan to the Australian group on a stand-alone basis . Supporting documentation must be prepared to evidence the arm 's length analysis . <p> The worldwide gearing test permits the taxpayer to gear to a similar gearing level to the global gearing level , even if this exceeds the safe harbour threshold . <p> The Taxation of Financial Arrangements ( TOFA ) <p> Special rules apply to the taxation of financial arrangements ( TOFA ) . The default methods are the accruals method and the realisation method . In broad terms , the accruals method will apply to spread an overall gain or loss over the life of the financial arrangement where there is sufficient certainty that the expected gain or loss will actually occur . A gain or loss that is not sufficiently certain is dealt with under the realisation method . <p> Royalties payable to a foreign company <p> If an Australian company pays royalties to a foreign resident , the royalties will be subject to royalty withholding tax at the rate of 30 per cent ( or as reduced under the relevant double tax treaty ) and may give rise to transfer pricing issues . <p> Base erosion and profit shifting <p> Australia is committed to combatting multinational tax avoidance and actively participated in the negotiation and drafting of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development ( OECD ) Action Plan on Base Erosion and Profit Shifting ( BEPS ) .
