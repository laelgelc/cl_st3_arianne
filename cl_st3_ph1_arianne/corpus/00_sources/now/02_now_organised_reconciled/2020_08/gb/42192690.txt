<h> ' That 's not what you told us ' - Oil boss rounds on Seely over objection <p> THE boss of UK Oil and Gas ( UKOG ) says he is ' surprised ' by the Isle of Wight MP Bob Seely 's objection to oil drilling plans -- having met him last year to discuss the plans . <p> In a letter to Mr Seely , chief executive of UKOG , Stephen Sanderson , who is seeking permission to drill two exploratory boreholes in Arreton , has rebutted points in Mr Seely 's objection -- saying the development will be truly out of sight , not cause any material traffic disruption and success at the site would bring jobs , generate wealth and tax revenues . <p> However , Mr Sanderson said when the oil company met with the MP in Westminster last August Mr Seely said he would n't object . <p> The letter says : " Your recent objections to our plans at Arreton have come as a great surprise to us . <p> " We recall from our meeting you mentioned you would n't object @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ much-needed funds to the island , ensure the proposed site was visually unobtrusive and complied with all environmental safeguarding policies . " <p> After receiving the letter , Mr Seely said he never agreed to support the application . <p> He said : " I have never said I would support the application . I listened to the applicant 's argument as I said I would . It is my job to represent the interests of my constituency , not those of applicants . <p> " Having now seen the submitted application , I consider that the interests of the Isle of Wight are best served by this application being turned down . I am not bound by any pre-application discussions . " <p> Mr Sanderson argues the site on the A3056 , the main road from Newport to Sandown , will not disrupt the tourism industry on the Island as UKOG plans to conduct drilling operations between October and April . <p> He said UKOG will share profit with the community . <p> He said : " UKOG is very aware that tourism is a key commercial and social @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ additional traffic onto the critical road network during peak season . <p> " We will not industrialise where we operate .
