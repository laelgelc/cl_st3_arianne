<p> The COVID-19 pandemic will ensure summer 2020 is a washout for most . With international travel restrictions limiting holidays abroad , many people in the UK have opted to stay somewhere closer to home . As a result , there have been remarkable increases in the number of visitors to beaches across the UK . Thousands flocked to a beach in Bournemouth on a single day in June , causing the local council to declare a major incident . <p> Will we commemorate the old coastal boundaries with forlorn sojourns above the sunken land ? Will we recreate the beach in the heart of our cities ? Rewilding the coast <p> Perhaps the most pragmatic solution is to accept nature taking its course and relinquish control as rising seas reshape the terrain . Allowing the new coastline to rewild could create millions of acres of new wetlands -- habitats that are very good at storing carbon and that have deteriorated by about 50% since 1900 . <p> Examples from Hong Kong , Spain , and Wallasea Island in the UK demonstrate how turning heavily managed coastal areas into new habitats can create new opportunities for wildlife and people . <p> So does the Mexican island , Mayakoba . Its unique mangrove forests were damaged and polluted by the building of numerous hotel chains on the seafront , but today , only 10% of these hotels remain on the coast . <p> The local community abandoned their high-density model of tourism and protected the dunes and mangroves , which were being eroded by excessive development . New canal networks were dug to create an estuary , attracting birds and amphibians .
