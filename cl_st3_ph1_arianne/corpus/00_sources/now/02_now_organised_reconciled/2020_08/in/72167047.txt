<p> New Delhi : Climate change is one of humanity 's defining challenges with severe economic and social consequences . India has made good progress in decarbonization growing the share of renewable energy , energy efficiency &amp; fuel transition . In this backdrop , there is growing interest and hype for using hydrogen in multiple applications -- Hydrogen-based Agro vehicles , Hydrogen-powered passenger trains , Hydrogen in aviation , and so on . <p> While these applications are indeed interesting , the near-term promise of hydrogen lies in " hard-to-abate " industrial sectors like steel , refining , fertilizer &amp; methanol . These industries contribute to 11 per cent of India 's total emissions and have limited opportunities to decarbonize . This is because fossil fuels play an integral role in their core processes . For example , fertilizer plants -- India 's largest natural gas consumer -- consume natural gas to generate Hydrogen which is a key input for ammonia production . We have a large and growing list of hard-to-abate sectors ( Eg. refining , steel , fertilizer &amp; methanol ) . Our growing natural gas demand is expected to remain dependent on imported LNG . Indian renewable energy sector is both innovative and affordable -- leading the world in integrating multiple sources to create round-the-clock RE power . <p> Today the " hard-to-abate " sectors primarily generate Hydrogen through fossil fuels like natural gas , coal and naphtha ( Grey Hydrogen ) . Adoption of Green Hydrogen in these sectors alone could save around 50-55 M tCO2 per year and create a market for 50 GW of electrolysis capacity and renewable power . It will also reduce both import dependency and exposure to foreign exchange volatility .
