The original NAC died prematurely because Ms Gandhi quit it in 2006 over the office-of-profit controversy . <p> The law has now been amended to free the NAC of this constraint . The new NAC must counsel the UPA on bringing about inclusive growth , which it had promised before both the 2004 and 2009 elections . The UPA has comprehensively betrayed that promise . Small-holder agriculture has become unviable . Farmers ' suicides between 1997 and 2008 number a staggering , unprecedented 1.99 lakh and have spread from Vidarbha and Andhra even to prosperous Punjab , India 's agriculturally most developed state . Instead of helping the poor cope with 18-per cent food inflation and provide them nutritional security , the UPA has given tax-breaks to the rich . The UPA has failed also in another critical area -- the public healthcare system , whose failure enormously burdens even the not-so-poor . The new NAC must urgently address these two issues and soon pay attention to other matters too like safe drinking-water provision , agrarian infrastructure , education and climate change . An ' Alarming ' Fact : A Food Security Act is urgently required to improve the horrifying nutrition levels among the masses . In the Global Hunger Index constructed by the International Food Policy Research Institute , India ranks 65 ( of 88 nations ) . Four states -- Punjab , Haryana , Kerala and Assam -- fall in the serious category . On a global scale , India 's best-performing state , Punjab , ranks 34th . As a result , 33 per cent of Indian adults are malnourished with a body-mass index of under 18.5 . The Food Security Act is meant to do that . Ms Gandhi proposed last June that the Act ensure a monthly household entitlement of 35 kg of cereals at Rs 3 per kg . The UPA referred the proposal to an Empowered Group of Ministers ( EGoM ) , which instead recommends a monthly quota of 25 kg of rice/wheat per poor family , whose price is indeterminate . This neither recognises the Right to Food , nor creates adequate entitlements for all , as recommended by the Kolkata Group chaired by Professor Amartya Sen. It also falls way below the recommendation of a committee appointed by the Supreme Court , chaired by Justice DP Wadhwa , for 35 kg of grains for those who earn less than Rs 100 a day . Estimates of the number of BPL households vary from 28 to 50 per cent of the population . They are based on convoluted methods and are unreliable . Universalisation of PDS : The more reliable National Sample Survey ( 2004-05 ) found that one-half of the poorest households have no BPL card . Worse , many non-poor people use influence to illegally procure BPL cards and corner PDS grain . There 's a radical solution to the BPL problem : universalise the PDS and not target it at the poor , because targeting is always fraught with exclusion and corruption . It would also mean treating the Right to Food as a fundamental right of all citizens , being part of the Right to Life under Article 21 of the Constitution . Targeting the PDS is arbitrary , exclusionary , divisive , corruption-prone and creates competition among the poor . Universalisation would generate pressure for the PDS to perform well for all , as it has done in Tamil Nadu .
