<h> Last Updated : Wednesday 12 August 2020 <p> But how do we calculate the value of a whale ? Whales can help fight climate change and provide an ecosystem service worth millions of dollars . Let us analyse their role in the ecosystem . <p> Whales combat carbon naturally ; big whales such as filter-feeding baleen and sperm whales help sequester carbon . They hoard carbon in their bodies , stockpiling tonnes of it , like trees ( if they could swim ) . <p> We are aware of the effect of trees on carbon : To put it in quantitative terms , a mature tree absorbs up to 22 kilograms of carbon each year . A single whale , with an average lifespan of 60 years , stores about 33 tonnes of carbon dioxide . This means one whale can do the job of 1,500 trees . <p> When a whale dies , its carcass sinks to the bottom of the sea ; the carbon stored in it circulates in the atmospheric cycle for hundreds of years . It becomes a literal carbon sink . <p> They do @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ eco-service called the ' whale pump ' . In the depths of the ocean , whales feed on tiny marine organisms such as krill or plankton and return to the surface to release huge faecal plumes . <p> These plumes are floating masses of faeces rich in iron , phosphorus and nitrogen . These are exactly the substances that plankton needs to grow . But why is plankton important ? Because it captures about 40 per cent of all carbon dioxide produced worldwide and contributes half of oxygen concentration to the atmosphere . <p> This , in simpler terms , also means that it would take four Amazon rainforests to capture the equivalent amount of carbon dioxide . <p> Scientists have discovered that plankton is blooming along the migratory routes of whales . Travelling between the breeding and feeding grounds , whales excrete nutrition into oceans all over the world : Iron into cold waters and nitrogen and phosphorus into warm seas . <p> So in essence , more whales would mean more plankton . It tried to tell the world how important whales are , so it put it in terms people could understand -- money . <p> The IMF took the per tonne price of carbon and multiplied it by the quantity that a single great whale could store in its body . The result : Each whale weighed out to an average of two million dollars . <p> Ralph Chami , assistant direct at the IMF , argued if one takes the whale value and multiply it by the total great whale population , one would get a value of over a trillion dollars . He calculated the price by figuring the amount of carbon that whales capture directly or indirectly and then compared it to the amount captured by trees , mangroves , salt marshes etc . <p> Before commercial whaling started about 500 years ago , four to five million whales would swim in the oceans across the world . The question is , can they return to their pre-whaling numbers ? <p> The appropriate way to move ahead would be to first double that number , which will take approximately 30 years . Small steps are the key . A one per cent recovery of the population could increase plankton that could help capture millions of tonnes of additional carbon dioxide each year . <p> IMF was able to quantify whales in the language of economics , making them an international public good .
