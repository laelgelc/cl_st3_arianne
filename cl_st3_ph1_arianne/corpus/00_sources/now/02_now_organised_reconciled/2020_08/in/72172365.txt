<h> Pollution data of most thermal power stations is rarely disclosed : CSE analysis <h> Last Updated : Monday 17 August 2020 <p> The current levels of pollutants in a vast majority of coal-based thermal power stations go unreported in the public domain despite the announcement of new emission norms . <p> Emission norms for particulate matter ( PM ) , sulphur dioxide ( SO2 ) , oxides of nitrogen ( NOx ) , specific water consumption and mercury were introduced by the Union Ministry of Environment , Forest and Climate Change ( MoEF&amp;CC ) in December 2015 and have to be met by power stations before 2022 . <p> An analysis by Delhi-based non-profit , Centre for Science and Environment , has revealed that data on particulate matter was unavailable in thermal power stations of 70,045-megawatt capacity . Similarly , no information is available on the current SO2 and NOx levels of power stations in the public domain . <p> Power stations are required to put out the following in the public domain , according to experts : <p> Their compliance status with environmental clearance conditions <p> Sustainability reports @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , these documents and information are available online only in a miniscule number of cases . Only a handful of mostly private companies -- Adani , CLP India and Tata Power -- have reasonable amount of environmental data published on their websites . <p> Tata Power publishes comprehensive records of all three documents mentioned above , on their website , which is not done by the rest of the plants . <p> Some stations like IL&amp;FS Tamil Nadu Power Company Ltd create a tab to list environmental data but leave them blank <p> Webpages created to populate environmental data left blank <p> Source : Centre for Science and Environment , 2020 <p> The Damodar Valley Power Corp and state power station websites are even worse . These crucial data help air quality experts to design mitigation measures and serve @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the quality and health of a citizen 's life . <p> In the digital age , crucial pollutant information from industries , particularly thermal power stations , stands hidden and accessible only by regulators , despite sufficient facilities to share the same on a minute-by-minute basis . <p> Today , more than 400 continuous emission monitoring devices exist in utility / large coal-based thermal power stations alone . These systems measure pollutants such as PM , SO2 and NOx , temperature of the flue gas , its velocity or flow rate , water vapour content , carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide content in flue gas , every five seconds to fifteen minutes , depending on their design . <p> A huge set of data reaches the Central Pollution Control Board and other state pollution control boards . However , none of this data has been put out in the public domain . Only the Madhya Pradesh State Pollution Control Board has made such data public . <p> Companies are also required to measure pollutant levels manually every quarter and submit them to their respective pollution control board and upload the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ set of these pollution monitoring equipment costs around Rs 2.5-3.5 million to install .
