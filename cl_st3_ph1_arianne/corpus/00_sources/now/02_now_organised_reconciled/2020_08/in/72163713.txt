These incidents highlight the need to meaningfully address the growing problem of wildlife-human conflicts . <p> Such conflicts are rooted in a complex suite of biological , ecological , climatic and social factors , though they are often reduced to a simplistic narrative in the popular imagination . <p> The consequences are tragic for both people and wildlife . The wild pig is arguably the most ubiquitous mammalian predator of agricultural crops across Eurasia , from Sweden to India and beyond . <p> Kerala 's plantations and homestead gardens are the ideal habitats for wild pigs to take refuge and feed on fallen coconuts and tuberous plants such as tapioca , colocasia and yam . <p> Paddy and other crops are also damaged when pigs dig up plants to search for grub . A 2018 study by EA Jayson of the Kerala Forest Research Institute emphasised the enormity of losses suffered in Malappuram district . The average annual loss was of Rs 15,000 per hectare , with several people killed in attacks by wild pigs . The recommendations of this study were unambiguous : Wild pig populations had to be controlled through means such as culling , if necessary . <p> Although Kerala and other states tried to bring in legislation to permit the culling of wild pigs outside forest areas , they were largely unsuccessful in implementing control measures . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of indigenous lethal and non-lethal means to keep wild pigs away from their farms .
