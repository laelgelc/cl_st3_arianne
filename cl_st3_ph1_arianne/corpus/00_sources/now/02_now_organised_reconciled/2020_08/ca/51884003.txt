Bringing the beach to you <p> The urban beach is a concept that 's growing in popularity worldwide . It involves creating sandy areas in towns and cities by importing sand onto concrete . There may also be artificial pools and fairground rides . Each one has different features . There are family-friendly options , and those catered to adults , with cocktail bars or restaurants . <p> The opportunities for hedonism are still there , but instead of travelling miles to enjoy it , it 's right on your doorstep . Rewilding the coast <p> Perhaps the most pragmatic solution is to accept nature taking its course and relinquish control as rising seas reshape the terrain . Allowing the new coastline to rewild could create millions of acres of new wetlands -- habitats that are very good at storing carbon and that have deteriorated by about 50 per cent since 1900 . <p> Examples from @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ demonstrate how turning heavily managed coastal areas into new habitats can create new opportunities for wildlife and people . <p> So does the Mexican island , Mayakoba . Its unique mangrove forests were damaged and polluted by the building of numerous hotel chains on the seafront , but today , only 10 per cent of these hotels remain on the coast . <p> The local community abandoned their high-density model of tourism and protected the dunes and mangroves , which were being eroded by excessive development . New canal networks were dug to create an estuary , attracting birds and amphibians . This new wetland was designated as a nature reserve and visitors arrived to enjoy a new kind of tourist experience . <p> Visitor capacity and beach activities were reduced to ensure sensitive coastal environments could remain protected . But allowing the sea back into reclaimed coastal territory allowed a more sustainable model of tourism to flourish -- one which could be replicated elsewhere as sea levels rise . <p> But before that can happen , our views of the coast must change .
