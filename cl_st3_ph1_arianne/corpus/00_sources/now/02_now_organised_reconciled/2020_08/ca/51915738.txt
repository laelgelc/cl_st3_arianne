<h> How Plants Close the Door on Infection <p> Plants have a unique ability to safeguard themselves against pathogens by closing their pores -- but until now , no one knew quite how they did it . Scientists have known that a flood of calcium into the cells surrounding the pores triggers them to close , but how the calcium entered the cells was unclear . <p> A new study by an international team including University of Maryland scientists reveals that a protein called OSCA1.3 forms a channel that leaks calcium into the cells surrounding a plant 's pores , and they determined that a known immune system protein triggers the process . <p> The findings are a major step toward understanding the defense mechanisms plants use to resist infection , which could eventually lead to healthier , more resistant and more productive crops .
