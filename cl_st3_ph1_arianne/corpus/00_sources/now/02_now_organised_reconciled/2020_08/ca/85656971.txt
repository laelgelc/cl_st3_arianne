<p> Conservationists and wildlife experts have expressed grave concern that Australian state governments are continuing to log unburned forests that are home to vulnerable koala populations . <p> Estimates suggest that at least 5,000 koalas were killed and over 2 million hectares of habitat was destroyed in the state of New South Wales during the 2019/20 bushfires -- a devastating blow to a species that is already facing the compounding risks of climate change , urban development and deforestation . <p> In light of these threats , a recent government inquiry found that the state 's koalas could become extinct by 2050 unless there is urgent government intervention to prevent habitat loss . <p> Yet despite a number of clear recommendations from that same inquiry -- that the NSW government urgently prioritise the protection of koala habitat in urban planning , for example , and that they ban the opening up of old growth forests to logging -- the state-owned logging agency Forestry Corporation is continuing to cut down trees in increasingly rare koala habitats . <p> " It 's a scandal that the government is n't doing what 's @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ iconic species , " James Tremain , from the Nature Conservation Council of NSW , told Vice News over the phone .
