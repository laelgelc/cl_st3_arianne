<p> Mr. Feltmate says provincial governments should be updating their flood maps every five to seven years but Newfoundland and Labrador is the only province that has fully updated its river , coastal and rainfall risk maps since 2015 . <p> British Columbia and Saskatchewan have n't updated their flood maps in more than two decades . <p> In contrast , the report notes that Alberta is the only province in Western and Central Canada that manages floodplain mapping and produces flood hazard maps for the province . These maps are updated on a 10-year cycle . <p> However , the authors also said Alberta only produces flood hazard studies for ice-related flooding for some parts of the province . In April , Fort McMurray experienced severe flooding after a 25-kilometre long ice jam formed on the Athabasca river and forced 13,000 people from their homes . The Insurance Bureau of Canada has estimated the insured damage from that flood was $522-million . <p> " We need to do more work almost universally across the country to update floodplain maps on a regular basis , " he said . The bad news is we are n't deploying those measures quickly enough , " he said . <p> The report also shows most provinces do n't have any regulations preventing new developments from going up in high flood-risk spots , and several also have n't done much to assess and protect critical buildings like hospitals and schools . <p> Flooding is Canada 's most expensive type of natural disaster , and the number and frequency of floods has increased in recent years , largely attributed to climate change @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ rains . <p> A report by the World Resources Institute earlier this year warned the yearly cost of overflowing rivers and rising seas could triple by 2030 without more effort on flood mitigation .
