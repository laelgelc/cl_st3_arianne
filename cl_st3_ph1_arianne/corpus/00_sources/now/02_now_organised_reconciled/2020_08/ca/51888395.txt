Turns out , fuel additives are often a waste of money.Sean Gallup <p> With the ever increasing desire for provincial and federal governments to take action on climate change , vehicles and the fuel that they burn are always under someone 's microscope . This has brought a lot of changes to the industry over the years . <p> One change that might be better for the air we breathe is definitely causing problems with some of the power equipment we use . We 're talking about ethanol or ethyl alcohol as it 's used as an additive to gasoline . With the exception of a few extreme north jurisdictions , just about every gas station in the country sells gasoline with anywhere from five per cent to 15 per cent @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ proven to reduce greenhouse gases in tailpipe emissions but it does have its drawbacks . <p> While few problems exist with using this mix in almost any fuel-injected passenger car or light truck on the roads today , the same certainly ca n't be said for just about anything that still uses a carburetor . Collector and antique vehicle owners as well as most power-boating enthusiasts have known for years of the havoc that any amount of ethanol can do to their treasures . <p> Old mechanical style fuel pumps can fail in as little as eight hours of running time when ethanol-laced gasoline is added to the tank . Ethanol is a strange breed as it is both a drying agent and a water collector at the same time . The water that it attracts makes it corrosive and the dryness that it brings can remove resins from older styles of fuel tanks , lines , and gaskets , causing leaks and a host of running problems . <p> While anything that cleans deposits or varnish build-ups in fuel tanks and lines might sound great , these sediments usually @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ deposits in their float bowls . Ethanol will also degrade the various gaskets between different components on a carb leading to air and fuel leaks . And as many powered implements use resins in the manufacturing of their fuel tanks , ethanol is a proven method of shortening their life-spans . <p> So who cares about carbureted engines anymore ? Ever wonder why your lawnmower and snow-blower is getting harder to start each season and do n't seem to have the power they used to ? The most likely culprit is ethanol in your gasoline . When filling up the garden shed fuel can for the season , use high octane fuel from a pump that does n't have an ethanol label on it . Fuel retailers are required to label all pumps that deliver gasoline with any amount of ethanol content . <p> Shell 's V-Power blend is guaranteed to not have any amount whatsoever . At the end of each season for powering your lawn or recreational equipment , dump any remaining fuel into your vehicle and start with a fresh supply .
