<h> DEEP DIVE : Building back better with Canadian biosphere reserves <p> This brief is one of a series produced by the Building Back Better Post-COVID-19 Task Force , a group of experts and partners affiliated with the Canadian Commission for UNESCO and its UNESCO Chairs Network . Their goal is to bring together sustainable economic recovery ideas to make our communities stronger in a post-COVID-19 world . <p> The series highlights how responding to the COVID-19 crisis through adaptive and strategic infrastructure investments can preserve critical ecosystems , increase the use of green infrastructure , and protect regional resources and distribution systems . These infrastructures can meet basic human needs and improve human health , while fostering long-term community resilience , well-being and sustainable employment . <p> The suggestions offered in this series support Canada 's commitments to the UN Agenda 2030 Sustainable Development Goals and position the country as a world leader in developing new economies based on environmental sustainability . <p> Building back better with Canadian biosphere reserves <p> The COVID-19 crisis has changed the day-to-day lives of people all around the globe . As many stay home , work remotely , and commute less , the resulting decreases in air and noise pollution have already positively affected our surroundings . It is crucial that the resulting impacts are not quickly turned aside , and that we seize the opportunity to promote a greener , more resilient , and more sustainable society . The 18 biosphere reserves in Canada that have been designated by UNESCO help to preserve the unique ecosystems and biodiversity of our country for present and future generations . These biosphere reserves are ideally positioned to build more resilient and sustainable communities in the post-COVID-19 era . Because they play a key role in mobilizing local partners , biosphere reserves can be used to initiate and pilot sustainable solutions and support a wide range of activities through engagement , collaboration and project development . The mobilization of partnerships and their sustainable activities can be leveraged in future . <p> Context <p> Nearly 2.5 million people live in Canada 's 18 biosphere reserves . They work to promote the conservation of biological and cultural diversity while promoting economic and social development . In each biosphere reserve , community partners work together to find innovative ways to achieve a balance between the needs of humans and nature . This is accomplished by working together to develop and implement new projects , create new resources , and facilitate positive change in conserving biodiversity , sustainable development , sustainable tourism , research and education , adaptation to climate change , and reconciliation . The relationships and partnerships they form within their regions builds a sense of place and community . <p> Through engagement with key local and regional stakeholders , biosphere reserves play an essential role in accelerating sustainable , community-led transformations .
