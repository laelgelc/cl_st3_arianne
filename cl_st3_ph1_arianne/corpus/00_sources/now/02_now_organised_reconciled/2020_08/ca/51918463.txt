<p> Picture an ideal dinner plate . If you 're like most Americans , it features a hearty portion of meat , from animals fattened on midwestern corn and soybeans , and a helping of vegetables , largely trucked in from California . The unique landscapes we rely on to deliver this bounty -- the twin jewels of the US food system -- are locked in a state of slow-motion ecological unravelling . <p> California 's agricultural sector has flourished from decades of easy access to water in one of the globe 's biggest swaths of Mediterranean climate . The Sierra Nevada , the spine of mountains that runs along California 's eastern flank , captures an annual cache of snow that , when it melts , cascades into a network of government-built dams , canals and aqueducts that deliver irrigation water to farmers in the adjoining Central Valley .
