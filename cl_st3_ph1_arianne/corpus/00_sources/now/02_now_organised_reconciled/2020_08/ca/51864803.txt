<p> Last week , Doug Ford passed Bill 195 , a piece of sweeping legislation that , among other things , is designed to extend the premier 's emergency powers far into the future , well after the end of the official state of emergency . Under the current framework , every 28 days the members of the legislature revisit the state of emergency to reassess whether risks of the state of emergency warrant an extension . Bill 195 would eliminate this requirement , giving the government the power to keep extending COVID-19 emergency orders without the need for legislative approval or debate . It will make it all the more difficult for our duly elected representatives to hold the government to account . <p> Bill 195 is an egregious example of a power grab by a government that has repeatedly shown its disdain for process and its own commitment to partisanship at all costs . It consolidates power in the offices of the premiers and the prime minister and , in doing so , undermines the effectiveness of cabinet and other elected members . Bill 195 itself caused MPP Belinda Karahalios to be removed from the PC caucus because she dared vote against her own party in what she felt was an overreach of power . <p> Minority voter support gives us majority governments , which means that small changes in voter sentiment can lead to massive changes in policy . When successive majorities dedicate significant time and resources to " undoing " what the previous government did , it has a debilitating effect on societal progress . Instead of solving the problems we are facing , parties focus on holding power for long enough that their policies become entrenched regardless of their effectiveness . Pursuing lasting and effective programs is a slow and methodical process . <p> Today , the problems we face are monumental : safely restarting the economy in the shadow of a global pandemic ; addressing the growing environmental and fiscal disaster that is climate change ; dealing with the massive housing shortfall in our province ; and rebuilding a long-neglected healthcare system whose cracks have been exposed to name a few . The solutions are complex and nuanced . The solutions will take time , and more than four years . They are the sort of undertakings that are going to need new approaches to government if we want to truly serve Ontarians . <p> If we are to rebuild a province that works for all Ontarians , we must begin from the ground up .
