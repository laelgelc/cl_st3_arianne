For the last 20 years he has focused on climate change politics . He is co-editor of Global Environmental Politics and co-authored ( with Peter Newell ) a book entitled " Climate capitalism : global warming and the transformation of the global economy " . <p> One interesting aspect of the discussion last week of Alberta Premier Alison Redford 's proposed Canadian energy strategy at the Western premier 's meeting has been how little controversy it seems to have provoked . It has been welcomed variously by the Western premiers and the federal government , but also by environmental groups like the Pembina Institute . <p> Partly this is because at present the idea is sufficiently vague that many feel that they can put whatever they want into it . But this is n't enough to understand why , since it was the very prospect of national-level coordination that often triggered hostility in the past . " <p> The real question is of course whether such a strategy can in fact do both of these things -- pursue the agenda of Canada as an " energy superpower " and simultaneously pursue sustainability . <p> Central here is the question of climate change . Virtually nowhere in the debate about this so far has climate policy appeared , either at the federal or provincial levels . It means to decarbonise the economy . <p> It is worth reminding ourselves what a world without decarbonisation might look like . A consensus has emerged that a warming of 2 ? If it is simply about legitimising unrestricted development of the oil sands , then this would in fact represent an acceleration of greenhouse gas emissions growth , heading in exactly the opposite direction . It would certainly mean that the Canadian target of a 17% reduction over 2005 levels by 2020 -- the weakest target of any industrialised country -- is more or less dead in the water . It would take heroic actions in other areas of policy to achieve it . <p> But there is definitely an opportunity to take such a strategy in a different direction . Redford is certainly prepared to talk the talk about making energy strategy " sustainable " . Her effort to open up this debate creates an opportunity for the provinces generally to fill the hole left by the gaping inaction on climate change at the federal level .
