<h> DEEP DIVE : The role of geoparks in Canada 's sustainability <p> This brief is one of a series produced by the Building Back Better Post-COVID-19 Task Force , a group of experts and partners affiliated with the Canadian Commission for UNESCO and its UNESCO Chairs Network . Their goal is to bring together sustainable economic recovery ideas to make our communities stronger in a post-COVID-19 world . <p> The series highlights how responding to the COVID-19 crisis through adaptive and strategic infrastructure investments can preserve critical ecosystems , increase the use of green infrastructure , and protect regional resources and distribution systems . These infrastructures can meet basic human needs and improve human health , while fostering long-term community resilience , well-being and sustainable employment . <p> The suggestions offered in this series support Canada 's commitments to the UN Agenda 2030 Sustainable Development Goals and position the country as a world leader in developing new economies based on environmental sustainability . <p> The COVID-19 global public health crisis has affected Canadians on all levels . Indigenous peoples have a long and storied history of overcoming overwhelming adversity and systemic injustice . As the current pandemic has permanently altered the economic landscape , Canadian Geoparks , with their meaningful partnerships with local Indigenous communities , are well-positioned to facilitate the transition to a new tourism economy , creating local jobs that preserve and share culture . The Indigenous tourism industry in Canada is growing faster than the average , with a 23.2 per cent gain between 2014 and 2017 , outpacing the overall Canadian tourism industry growth of 14.5 per cent over the same period . With one in seven visitors wanting to take part in an authentic Indigenous experience while on vacation , there is a wide variety of potential ways to increase operators , experiences , content , and retail goods in Canada 's Geoparks . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ First Nations of B.C. have established the Twin Sisters Native Plant Nursery , which reclaims areas of industrial disturbance . Seeds are harvested directly from sites before impact , then germinated and replanted when resource industries complete their activities . It has had industry-leading results , generating new interest in community-led initiatives and place-based seed harvesting . There is now a waiting list for industrial clients to purchase plants from the nursery for their projects . The nursery also provides traditional plants to elders and community members for use in their own homes , supporting ongoing cultural practice for all ages . The Twin Sisters Nursery is exploring tourism opportunities where visitors can make baskets of plants with medicinal and/or food properties while learning about the traditional cultures of the area . These initiatives , and others from local Indigenous communities , connect with the Geopark through their Indigenous Advisory Council . <p> The Mi'gmaq people of the Gespeg First Nation have an active collaboration with Perc ?
