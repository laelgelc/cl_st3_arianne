<h> Dr Siddhi Joshi : ' The world needs a good lawyer to look after the planet ' <p> Dr Siddhi Joshi is a marine biogeoscientist and human rights law researcher based in Galway . Here , she talks about her career journey and the importance of gender diversity in STEM . <p> When we think of scientists , engineers , software developers or researchers , it can be easy to focus on one main strand of their career . But many STEM professionals have several strings to their bow and each element can complement the other . <p> Dr Siddhi Joshi is a marine biogeoscientist based in Galway , having studied oceanography at University of Southampton , followed by a master 's in hydrographic surveying and a PhD in earth and ocean science at NUI Galway . <p> But Joshi is also an international human rights law researcher , having completed her Master of Laws at the Irish Centre for Human Rights . " <h> ' A holistic understanding of natural systems ' <p> Aside from her law research , Joshi 's current marine science work involves the study of maerl or rhodolith coralline algae habitats and their sediment dynamics . " Maerl beds are free-living coralline red algae , which form biogenic gravel beaches of maerl debris , often known as coral beaches . " <p> Along with her study of maerl habitats , Joshi is passionate about marine conservation . " <p> Joshi said one of the most interesting parts of her work is observing natural phenomena for the first time in the field , " be it observing the living cold water corals in the remotely operated underwater vehicle camera for the first time , or understanding how the ocean waves , tides and currents are driven on the Irish continental shelf " . <p> She added that reproducing these natural oceanographic phenomena in lab experiments , applying mathematical models to explain them and applying these models to other areas , is another interesting part of the job .
