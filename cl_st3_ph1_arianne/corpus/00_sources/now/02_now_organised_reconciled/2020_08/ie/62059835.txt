<p> Climate change rather than overhunting may have caused the woolly rhinoceros to become extinct around 14,000 years ago , scientists have said . <p> The giant herbivorous creatures are thought to have roamed northern Asia and Europe during the ice ages around 350,000 years ago and genetic analysis suggests they were well adapted to living in colder climates . <p> While the arrival of humans has been proposed as a potential cause of extinction , evidence on this is limited . <p> The findings , published in the journal Current Biology , showed the megaherbivores ' populations remained " stable and diverse " for more than 13,000 years following the arrival of humans . <p> Senior study author Love Dalen , a professor of evolutionary genetics at the Centre for Palaeogenetics , a joint venture between Stockholm University and the Swedish Museum of Natural History , said : " It was initially thought that humans appeared in north-eastern Siberia 14,000 or 15,000 years ago , around when the woolly rhinoceros went extinct . <p> " But recently there have been several discoveries of much older human occupation sites , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> " So , the decline towards extinction of the woolly rhinoceros does n't coincide so much with the first appearance of humans in the region . <p> " If anything , we actually see something looking a bit like an increase in population size during this period .
