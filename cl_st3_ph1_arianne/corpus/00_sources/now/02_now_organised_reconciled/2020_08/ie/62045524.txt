<h> Oxford professor calls on EU to change methane calculation definition <p> The European Commission has been called on by an Oxford University professor to change its methods of calculating methane emissions in order to " clarify the definition of climate neutrality " . <p> Prof. Myles Allen , professor of geosystem science and head of the Climate Dynamics Group in Oxford University 's Department of Physics , made a public submission made to the commission on Tuesday , August 4 . <p> In the submission , he warned that achieving climate neutrality in terms of metric-equivalent emissions " could mean eliminating practices -- such as ruminant agriculture -- that are not actually causing global warming " . <h> Definition of climate neutrality <p> Europe aspires to ' climate neutrality ' by mid-century but has yet to specify exactly what this means . The definition is particularly important for methane policy . <p> " Under the UNFCCC United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change , emissions are reported as CO2-equivalent using 100-year Global Warming Potentials CO2-e100 -- but how emissions are reported does not dictate the metric used to define climate neutrality as a policy objective . " <p> Prof. Allen explained that CO2-e100 understates the warming impact of any new methane source by a factor of four over the first 20 years after the increase , while overstating the warming impact of constant methane emissions , also by a factor of four . <p> This , he said , meant that constant CO2-e100 emissions of methane balanced by ' equivalent ' CO2 removal would lead to a steady reduction in global temperatures " back to pre-industrial levels and beyond " . <p> This conflicts with public and policy-makers ' understanding of ' climate neutrality ' , he added . <p> Advertisement <h> ' Warming-equivalent ' emissions <p> To get around this problem , the professor said that climate neutrality should be defined in terms of ' warming-equivalent ' emissions . <p> " These have , by construction , the same warming impact as CO2 emissions on all timescales , " he added . <p> An activity generating sustained net zero warming-equivalent emissions is not increasing global temperatures , but also not undoing the impact of past contributions to warming , consistent with plausible interpretations of ' climate neutrality ' . <p> " Warming-equivalent emissions of methane are calculated by multiplying present-day CO2-e100 emissions by a factor of four and then subtracting CO2-e100 emissions from 20 years ago multiplied by 3.75 . <p> " Emissions of methane that are declining at approximately 3% per decade are consistent with net zero warming-equivalent emissions , " the professor said . <h> ' Profound implications for agriculture ' <p> " Having decided to aim for climate neutrality , Europe has a simple choice : to define climate neutrality in terms of metric-equivalent emissions which also means deciding which metric to use , also not pre-determined by the metric used for reporting ; or in terms of warming-equivalent emissions . <p> " This should be an open and public discussion -- because the implications , particularly for agriculture , are profound . <p> Achieving climate neutrality in terms of metric-equivalent emissions could mean eliminating practices , such as ruminant agriculture , that are not actually causing global warming .
