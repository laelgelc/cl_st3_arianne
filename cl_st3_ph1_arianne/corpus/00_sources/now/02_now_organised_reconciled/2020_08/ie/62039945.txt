<p> For well over a decade , the EU has set itself a target to increase R&amp;D investment from under two percent ten years ago to three percent of GDP , now aimed for by 2020 , reflecting growing recognition that Europe must compete and excel in the new global knowledge economy . <p> More investment would lead to jobs , growth , and , importantly , to improved sustainability . From climate change and improved health to delivering a truly circular bioeconomy , R&amp;D is and will be critical for delivering innovation for the benefit of people and the planet . <p> But a decade later , insufficient progress has been made on this goal , and Europe is lagging behind on several breakthrough technologies , especially in the field of agricultural biotechnology . <p> Today , 17 million farmers in 24 countries grow genetically modified ( GM ) crops on over 12 percent of the world 's fields .
