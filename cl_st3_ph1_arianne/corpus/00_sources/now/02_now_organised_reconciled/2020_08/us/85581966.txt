<h> Why are n't sea trout thriving anymore ? <p> Researchers in Norway will be checking large numbers of trout to figure out what factors are the greatest disrupters of the wild fish . With this information , researchers might be able take action to help increase the sea trout population again . Credit : Sindre H ? vardstein Eld ? y / NTNU <p> Sea trout get no peace . They 're constantly exposed to new diseases and ailments , many of which are due to climate change and human activity . <p> Now researchers want to find out which diseases are affecting the fish so they can know how to help them . The researchers plan to explore the entire coast of Norway , but have started on a smaller scale . <p> " We 've investigated viruses , bacteria and parasites on 160 sea trout in the Skjerstadfjord and the Tosenfjord in Nordland county , " says associate professor Jan Grimsrud Davidsen in the Department of Natural History at the NTNU University Museum . <p> The research group has looked at what diseases the fish have and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ what is afflicting them <p> The research group searched for 46 different disease-causing factors , so-called pathogens , and found 11 of them . <p> " The most important thing was that we found out the method works . But we did n't really find much disease in the fish in either the Skjerstadfjord or the Tosenfjord , " says Davidsen . <p> The researchers had to use an analysis method developed for fish in the Pacific Ocean -- because that was the only existing option . It was n't an ideal solution . <p> The researchers have looked at what diseases the fish have and how they affect fish behaviour . Credit : Sindre H ? vardstein Eld ? y / NTNU <p> " We think the analysis results were n't completely accurate , because we did n't screen for the correct pathogens . The problem is that we do n't quite know what diseases we 're looking for . Currently we still know very little about which pathogens affect sea trout on the Norwegian coast . But we hope to learn more about that in the follow-up project @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> The studies of the two rivers in Nordland were a pilot project . The researchers have now received 1.1 million Euros from the Research Council of Norway to continue and expand the project , starting this summer . <p> Investigating the entire Norwegian coast <p> In the next few years , the research institute NORCE , the Institute of Marine Research , NINA , fisheries authorities in Canada and the NTNU University Museum will map the spread of sea trout diseases and parasites along the entire Norwegian coast . <p> " Using a new DNA method , the researchers can check the samples for traces of 90 different viruses , parasites and bacteria . " <p> " So far , we 've looked at data that we had already collected . Now we 'll be collecting the first new data from the Beiarfjord in Nordland and a fjord in Western Norway , " says Davidsen . <p> The researchers plan to tag 150 fish with traceable electronic tags . <p> " Then we 'll be able to examine both the activity level and the migration pattern of the fish @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ consumption , " he says . <p> In practical terms , the researchers take a cell sample from the gills of the sea trout , which are fitted with electronic ID tags . The gill sample is only the size of a pinhead , but yields a lot of information that is quite fascinating . <p> " Using a completely new DNA method , we can screen this sample for traces of 90 different viruses , parasites and bacteria , " says Davidsen . <p> These analysis methods are the most advanced available and will enable the researchers to detect whether the sea trout is just a carrier of the disease or is actually sick . <p> " We can link this information to sea trout behavior . We register their behavior using the electronic ID tags and listening stations that we set up in the fjords . Then we can see if sea trout with various diseases or parasites behave differently from other sea trout . <p> Important for the fish <p> So why is this research important ? We know very little about the effect of pathogens , and it 's important to find out the role they play , " says Davidsen . <p> The aquaculture industry in Norway is growing . We know that this also contributes to an increase of pathogens in wild fish , and so it is important to find out what effect they have on sea trout . Researchers have already ascertained that salmon lice have an effect on wild fish . Now it is high time to look at other diseases and parasites as well . <p> " If we figure out what is afflicting the wild fish the most , we may be able to do something about it .
