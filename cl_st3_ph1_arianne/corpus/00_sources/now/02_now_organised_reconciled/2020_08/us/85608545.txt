<h> New Zealand 's Southern Alps glacier melt has doubled <p> View from December 2018 across Hooker Lake to Aoraki - Mt Cook with Little Ice Age moraines and trimlines encircling the lake . Credit : Jonathan Carrivick , University of Leeds <p> Glaciers in the Southern Alps of New Zealand have lost more ice mass since pre-industrial times than remains today , according to a new study . <p> Research led by the University of Leeds , in collaboration with the National Institute of Water and Atmospheric Research ( NIWA ) in New Zealand , mapped Southern Alps ice loss from the end of the Little Ice Age -- roughly 400 years ago -- to 2019 . <p> The study found that the rate of ice loss has doubled since glaciers were at their Little Ice Age peak extent . Relative to recent decades , the Southern Alps lost up to 77% of their total Little Ice Age glacier volume . <p> Climate change has had a significant impact on ice loss around the world . Moraines and trimlines can indicate former ice margin extent and ice thickness changes through time . <p> By comparing changes in the glacier surface reconstructed during the Little Ice Age peak and the glacier surface in more recent digital elevation models , the study found that ice loss has increased two-fold since the Little Ice Age with a rapid increase in ice volume loss in the last 40 years . <p> Up to 17% of the volume that was present at the Little Ice Age was lost between 1978 and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ mass remained in what was formerly the low altitude part of the Little Ice Age glacier region -- also called the ablation zone -- and much of the what used to be ice-covered in the Little Ice Age ablation zone is now completely ice free . <p> Study lead author Dr. Jonathan Carrivick , from the School of Geography , said : " These findings quantify a trend in New Zealand 's ice loss .
