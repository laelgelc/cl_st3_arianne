<h> Black business owners say now is not the time to get rid of natural gas Opinion <p> Posted Aug 31 , 2020 <p> New Jerseyans rely on affordable and reliable natural gas for heating and cooking . Replacing natural gas with electricity would mean pushing New Jersey away from the lowest-cost form of energy ( natural gas ) to the highest ( electricity ) , says John Harmon , president of the African American Chamber of Commerce . <p> The COVID-19 pandemic has brought a world of financial pain and uncertainty to the Garden State . Amid the unprecedented challenges facing public health over the last five months , reasonable people can agree that Gov . Phil Murphy 's response has been measured , reliant on health experts and conducted in the best interest of keeping people safe to defeat the virus . <p> As we know , COVID 's reach stretches beyond immediate health impacts , from cities and towns weighing massive budget deficits to businesses assessing whether remaining open is even a viable option . Families are seeing reduced hours and lost jobs , too . Economic @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and dealing with the crisis requires flexibility and creative thinking , including in the area of energy policy . <p> In January 2020 , prior to the onset of the pandemic , the state unveiled the 2020 Energy Master Plan , which sets forth a number of compelling and aggressive strategies to reduce emissions and combat climate change . As the state moves forward with implementing this plan , however , it 's pivotal that New Jersey approaches energy policy in an appropriate way that acknowledges the economic realities we now face . Responsible energy policy means ensuring affordable energy for New Jersey residents and maintaining a reliable energy supply . <p> If this approach is taken , bills will grow higher in the short- and long-term , saddling residents and businesses with more out-of-pocket expenses , which few can afford . Imposing these new costs would also discourage investment and economic development at a time when we need it most . <p> Lack of adequate natural gas also has serious impacts on electric system reliability , which millions of California residents recently experienced during an intense heatwave where @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ could n't keep up with demand . Imagine that scenario here , only in the dead of winter . <p> The Energy Master Plan itself acknowledges that current electric heating costs are nearly twice that of heating with natural gas . Expecting working families to bear these additional costs is unfair . This is particularly true for Black households . An EIA Residential Energy Consumption Survey releasedduring the Obama administration found that Black families were twice as likely to be energy insecure -- foregoing necessities like medicines and food just to pay energy bills . The median incomes for Black families in New Jersey are half that of their white counterparts . Further , the median net worth of Black families is just $17,150 , compared to a median net worth of $171,000 for white families , according to the Tax Policy Network . <p> At a recent virtual summit hosted by the African American Chamber of Commerce of New Jersey , " The State of Black NJ , " we highlighted how African-American businesses experienced a 41% drop in activity during the first month of the pandemic . The @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of Black-owned businesses survived the last recession , compared to 60% of white-owned businesses . These are present real , long-term economic challenges . Asking those who are barely holding on as it is , to absorb more state-mandated costs down the road through higher energy bills , equipment and building upgrades is illogical and impractical . <p> In sum , now is not the time to push forward on state policies that will drive up energy bills . Now , more than ever , we must recognize the vital role that natural gas plays in our state and how it can support an expansion of cleaner energy technologies as they come online . <p> Natural gas is clean , it 's reliable , and it 's lowering costs -- let 's continue relying on it as a foundation to rebuild and recover from COVID-19.
