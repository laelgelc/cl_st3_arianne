Photo by UPI/Matthew Healey License Photo <p> July 30 ( UPI ) -- The amount of coastal land at risk of extreme flooding due to climate change is expected to double in size over the next 80 years , according to a new study . <p> Models have previously showed that the combination of sea level rise and bigger ocean storms -- trends fueled by climate change -- is likely to yield more frequent and extreme flooding events in the decades ahead . <p> Advertisement <p> The latest research , published Thursday in the journal Scientific Reports , determined the increase in coastal flooding will put new , previously insulated communities at risk . <p> By the end of the century , 77 million more people will be exposed to the risk of extreme flooding . " What the data and our model is saying is that compared with now , what we see as a 1-in-100-year extreme flood event will be 10 times more frequent because of climate change . " <p> Authors of the new study claim that at least some growth in flooding risk is unavoidable , but that communities can help minimize flooding risk by working to slow climate change and proactively building protective coastal barriers . <p> Previous studies have found that protecting coastal reefs , sea grasses , mangroves and other types of coastal estuaries can help minimize the damages caused by flooding . <p> The new research showed that some places will experience a larger increase in flood risk , including northwest Europe , as well as a variety low-lying hotspots in Australia @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , southeast Africa and North America . <p> " This is critical research from a policy point of view because it provides politicians with a credible estimate of the risks and costs we are facing , and a basis or taking action , " said Ian Young , study co-author and professor at the University of Melbourne .
