<h> Fossils reveal diversity of animal species roaming Europe 2 million years ago <p> A re-analysis of fossils from one of Europe 's most significant paleontological sites reveals a wide diversity of animal species , including a large terrestrial monkey , short-necked giraffe , rhinos and saber-toothed cats . <p> These and other species roamed the open grasslands of Eastern Europe during the early Pleistocene , approximately 2 million years ago . Ultimately , the researchers hope the fossils will provide clues about how and when early humans migrated to Eurasia from Africa . Reconstructions of past environments like this also could help researchers better understand future climate change . <p> " My colleagues and I are excited to draw attention back to the fossil site of Graunceanu and the fossil potential of the Oltet River Valley of Romania , " said Claire Terhune , associate professor of anthropology at the University of Arkansas . " It 's such a diverse faunal community . Many Oltet Valley fossil sites , including Graunceanu , were discovered in the 1960s after landslides caused in part by deforestation due to increased agricultural activity in the area . <p> Archeologists and paleontologists from the Emil Racovita Institute of Speleology in Bucharest excavated the sites soon after they were discovered . Fossils were recovered and stored at the institute , and scholarly publications about the sites flourished in the 1970s and 1980s . But interest in these fossils and sites waned over the past 20 to 30 years , in part because many records of the excavations and fossils were lost . <p> Since 2012 , the international team , including Terhune and researchers from Romania , the United States , Sweden and France , has focused on this important fossil region .
