Nye Jr . <p> Opinion contributor <p> Relations with China are now at their lowest point in nearly 50 years . Some blame this on President Donald Trump , but he is more like a man who poured gasoline on a fire . Chinese leaders built the fire with their actions such as mercantilist manipulation of the trading system , theft and coercive transfer of intellectual property , and militarization of artificially constructed islands in the South China Sea . The U.S. reaction has been bipartisan , but thus far with little articulation of a strategy . <p> Pushing back is useful but not a strategy . " <p> But demanding that China buy more American goods without solving the structural issues is not a strategy . Nor is talking about the " China virus " while withdrawing from the World Health Organization and leaving the field open for China . And recent administration speeches that squeeze China into a Cold War ideological framework misrepresent the real strategic challenge we face . <h> Not another US-Soviet Cold War <p> Cold War metaphors can mislead us . The United States and the Soviet Union had little commerce or social contact . We and our allies trade heavily with China , and hundreds of thousands of Chinese students attend our universities . Xi Jinping is not Stalin , and the Chinese system is not Marxist-Leninist but " market Leninist " -- a form of state capitalism based on a hybrid of public and private companies subservient to an authoritarian party elite . We and our allies are more deeply intertwined with this Chinese economy than we ever were with the Soviet Union . It makes sense to decouple security risks by excluding Chinese @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , but it would be too costly to curtail all trade . <p> Moreover , even if we could break apart economic globalization , we remain interdependent with China in ecological globalization such as pandemics and climate change , which obey the laws of biology and physics , not politics . <p> Since we can not solve these problems alone , we must learn to distinguish power with others from power over others . Coping with climate change or pandemics will require us to work with China at the same time that we use our Navy to defend freedom of navigation in the South China Sea , or use sanctions to protest human rights violations in Xinjiang or Hong Kong . The Trump administration 's strategy of simple " great power competition " has failed to make us secure . More Americans have been killed by COVID-19 than in all our wars since World War II . <p> Strategy requires careful net assessment . Underestimation breeds complacency while overestimation creates fear -- either of which can lead to miscalculation . Beijing invests about a third of what we do in military power and remains behind in its global projection . <p> As for the soft power of attraction , international polls show that the United States outstrips China . Moreover , we are bordered by oceans and friendly neighbors , while China has territorial disputes with several neighbors , including India . <h> Alliances , immigration are our aces <p> Of course , we could still fail . A misguided strategy like Trump 's could lead us to discard our aces of alliances and international institutions , or to severely restrict immigration . I once asked former Singaporean Prime Minister Lee Kuan Yew why he thought China would not achieve its goal of surpassing the United States as a global power . He pointed to America 's ability to draw on the talents of the entire world and to recombine them in diversity and creativity that is not possible with China 's ethnic nationalism . <p> Our strategy toward China should aim to avoid either a hot @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ when necessary . Our strategic objective should be to shape China 's external behavior by strengthening our alliances and international institutions , and by bolstering our economy and technological advantages in areas like biotech and artificial intelligence . <p> While China derives power from a vast market , so do we -- but we also offer openness and values that greatly increase our soft power . In addition , our military power of deterrence is welcomed by the many countries that want to maintain friendly relations with China but do not want to be dominated by it . Our strategy should regard our China relationship as a cooperative rivalry where we pay equal heed to both aspects of the term . If we avoid demonization and misleading Cold War analogies , we can succeed with such a strategy .
