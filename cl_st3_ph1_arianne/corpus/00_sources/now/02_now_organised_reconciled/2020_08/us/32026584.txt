<p> Despite being located in the hot and dry Mojave Desert , Nevada is home to 52 fish species found nowhere else in the world . <p> These species are endemic , meaning native to a single geographic location . <p> " Mojave actually means by the water . And this entire area was a bunch of interconnected streams and little lakes all over the place . And it dried up very quickly , " said Frank Van Breukelen , UNLV 's director of the School of Life Sciences . <p> Over the last 35,000 years , as the region became drier , the fish were stranded and isolated into the remaining springs and wetlands scattered across the state . In isolation , they evolved into new and unique species seen only in Nevada . <p> " We think about them ( endemic fishes ) being adapted to live there . But the reality of it is that they spent most of our evolutionary history in cooler waters , which means that they are n't very well adapted to live there , " he said . <p> Nevadans can visit @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ at Ash Meadows National Wildlife Refuge . While studying the Devils Hole pupfish , he found that they can survive without oxygen for more than two hours . They use an alternate method of producing energy . <p> A byproduct of that method is alcohol . The alcohol affects the pupfish similarly to the way it does a human liver . <p> " We might actually be able to get some insight into what life would be like in a natural setting as a chronic alcoholic , " Van Breukelen said . <p> At the Center for Biological Diversity , Donnelly works to conserve the wild things and wild places of Nevada .
