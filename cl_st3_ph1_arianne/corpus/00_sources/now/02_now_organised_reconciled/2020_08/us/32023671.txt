<h> Blanket of rock debris offers glaciers more protection from climate change than previously known <p> A new study which provides a global estimate of rock cover on the Earth 's glaciers has revealed that the expanse of rock debris on glaciers , a factor that has been ignored in models of glacier melt and sea level rise , could be significant . <p> The Northumbria University study , which has been published in Nature Geoscience this week , is the first to manually verify the rockdebris cover on every one of the Earth 's glaciers . <p> As glaciers shrink , their surrounding mountain slopes become exposed and eroded rock debris slides down and accumulates on glacier surfaces . This debris forms a protective layer that can be many metres thick , reducing the rate at which the ice below melts . Although the effects of this protective cover are known , it has never been carefully mapped until now , and so has not been included in global glacier models . <p> As well as revealing where rock verifying more than 923,000 square kilometres of glacier worldwide . <p> The exercise allowed them to analyse the debris cover on a global- , regional- , as well as individual glacier-scale and created the world 's first baseline dataset of glaciers in their current state . <p> They found more than 29,000 square kilometres of the world 's mountain glacier area is covered in rock debris -- an area equivalent to almost 500 Manhattan Islands . <p> Lead researcher Sam Herreid undertook the study for his Ph.D . at Northumbria University and is now believed to be the only person who has One of their findings revealed that 10,000 square kilometres of mapped glacier area was not actually glacier , but rather bedrock or vegetated ground that was either incorrectly mapped previously or glacier area that has since melted away . <p> This , combined with the melt reduction from debris insulating the ice below , means that all past global glacier models based on the Inventory are likely to have overestimated the true volume of glacier melt , run off and subsequent contribution to global sea level rise . <p> They described the 10.6% of glacier area that requires an updated approach to estimating melt as " an alarmingly high number " and said that their work provides a key dataset for revising , and likely lowering , the glacier contribution to sea level rise . <p> The team also devised a way to analyse how the world 's debris-covered glaciers will evolve over the coming centuries . <p> By comparing the many states of glaciers present on Earth today , from @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Greenland , to ' old ' and rock covered in the Himalaya , they were able to piece together a conceptual timeline which they believe outlines how a glacier might evolve in the future . <p> Their timeline reveals that many glaciers are at the older end of the spectrum and can therefore be considered to be on the decline . <p> Co-author Francesca Pellicciotti of the Swiss Federal Research Institute WSL and an Associate Professor at Northumbria University , explained : " The upper levels of the glaciers are constantly accumulating snow and will always be debris free , so we looked only at the lower levels of glaciers which is where rock debris can accumulate . <p> " Ice melts and flows away as water , but the rocks do not , and accumulate at the surface .
