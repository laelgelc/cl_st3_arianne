<h> Letter to the editor : ATVs make it harder to connect with outdoors <p> Share <p> I was shocked and dismayed to read Staff Writer Deirdre Fleming 's article " ATV sales riding high as Mainers long to get outdoors " ( July 26 , Page A1 ) . <p> If the pandemic has taught us nothing else , the one sure lesson is that the scaling back of human activity has lowered pollution levels . All-terrain vehicles are not good for the environment . The principal emissions from motor vehicles are greenhouse gases , which contribute to climate change . The principal greenhouse gas is carbon dioxide , but vehicles also produce the greenhouse gases nitrous oxide and methane . <p> Not to mention that " ATVs and wildlife do not mix , " as the Adirondack Council has noted , and that " ATV impacts include noise disturbance , damage to vegetation , increased runoff , soil erosion and degradation of water quality . Wildlife also suffer from all of these impacts . " <p> I 've seen all of this firsthand while trail riding with my horse . I know my dogs and horses are frightened by ATVs . I ca n't imagine what wild critters are experiencing when the noisy , smelly machines go tearing through the woods , even if they go slowly . <p> Damaging habitats , degrading water quality and scaring wildlife are not letting riders be " able to connect with the outdoors " ( as a new owner of an ATV told the Telegram ) . To connect with the outdoors , take a walk , smell the smells , feel the peace and quiet and know that you are in harmony with nature . <p> I recall a remark from author and outdoorsman Edward Abbey that goes something like this : You ca n't experience wilderness from atop a machine . <p> Doris LutherHollis <p> Invalid username/password . <p> Success .
