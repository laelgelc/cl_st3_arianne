<h> Crude oil or cooking oil ? For some U.S. refiners , it 's now a choice <p> NEW YORK ( Reuters ) - Refiners have always produced fuel using crude oil . Now some are using grease . <p> A slump in demand for gasoline since the onset of the coronavirus pandemic has several refining companies accelerating their plans to retrofit facilities to produce so-called renewable diesel made from , among other things , used cooking oil from fast-food restaurants . <p> The shift helps , they say , because it allows them to tap into lucrative federal and state incentives for production of low carbon fuels at a time when slumping fuel demand has squeezed profit margins for conventional fuels like gasoline . <p> Companies that have recently announced moves to produce renewable diesel include CVR Energy , Marathon Petroleum Corp , Phillips 66 and HollyFrontier Corp , according to a Reuters review of company earnings statements in the first half of 2020 . <p> The planned conversion of Phillips 66 's refinery in Rodeo , California , for example , is " expected to deliver strong returns @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ plant 's operating costs , " the company said in a statement . <p> The project is not expected to come online until 2024 . <p> Renewable diesel fuel burns cleaner than conventional diesel and can run without blending . Refiners can produce it by converting gasoline-making units to hydrotreaters that can process soybean oil or used cooking grease . <p> In California , such a conversion can mean big money . Under the state 's Low Carbon Fuel Standard , refiners can generate tradable credits by producing renewable diesel because it has a lower carbon intensity than fossil fuels , and sell them to other fuel producers for profit . <p> Oregon has a similar credit program , and several other U.S. states are also developing standards . <p> " California 's LCFS ( Low Carbon Fuel Standard ) program is currently a key driver of renewable diesel production , " said Charles Kemp , refining consultant at Baker O'Brien , which estimates that renewable diesel will make up 20% of California 's diesel pool in 2020 and that the percentage will double by 2030 . <p> Federal policy @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ such as a blender 's credit of $1 per gallon expiring in 2022 .
