<h> Researchers find link between Atlantic hurricanes and weather system in East Asia <p> Graphic illustrating the relationship between the East Asian Subtropical Jet and storms in the North Atlantic . Credit : Contributed image . <p> With a new Atlantic hurricane season in full swing , scientists may have found a new influence on how tropical cyclones develop . <p> Researchers led by the University of Iowa have identified a connection between a climate system in East Asia and the frequency of tropical storms that develop in the Atlantic Ocean -- which can strengthen into hurricanes that threaten the United States . <p> In a new study , the researchers say the East Asian Subtropical Jet Stream ( EASJ ) an upper-level river of wind that originates in East Asia and moves west to east across the globe , carries with it an atmospheric phenomenon called a Rossby wave . <p> Rossby waves occur naturally within the Earth 's oceans and atmosphere , forming because of the planet 's rotation . The researchers say Rossby waves hitch a ride on the EASJ to the North Atlantic when tropical cyclones @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ waves affect wind shear , a key element in the formation of tropical storms . <p> " When the EASJ is stronger , it can enhance this pattern , which leads to stronger teleconnections and stronger wind shear in the North Atlantic , " explains says Wei Zhang , a climate scientist at IIHR-Hydroscience &amp; Engineering at Iowa . " That can suppress Atlantic tropical cyclone formation . " <p> The scientists observed nearly 40 years of Atlantic tropical cyclones during prime formation season , from August to November , and their connection during the same time period with EASJ activity between July to October . <p> " What we found was there is a signal ( Rossby waves ) in terms of wind shear and that this signal is coming from the west , being Asia , over the Atlantic , via the East Asian Subtropical Jet Stream , " says Zhang , who is corresponding author on the study , published online in the journal Geophysical Research Letters . They determined based on that information that a stronger EASJ is associated with fewer Atlantic tropical cyclones . <p> The study comes as Hurricane Isiaias became the fifth named storm to make landfall in the continental U.S. -- and already the second hurricane to swipe land -- when it swept across the U.S. East Coast last week . <p> The researchers previously found a connection between the EASJ and storms affecting the western U.S. After that study , they looked for other associations . <p> " We said , ' OK let 's see whether this subtropical jet can influence other weather systems , " says Gabriele Villarini , IIHR 's director and a co-author on the study . <p> " We found a physical mechanism that can provide a basic understanding in the context of tropical cyclone formation , " Villarini says .
