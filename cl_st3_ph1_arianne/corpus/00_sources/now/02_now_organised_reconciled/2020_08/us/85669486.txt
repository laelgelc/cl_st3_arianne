( Photo by Robin Utrecht/SOPA Images/LightRocket via Getty Images ) <p> SOPA Images/LightRocket via Getty Images <p> A new study published today in PLOS Pathogens shows a promising alternative for those taking the currently available antiretroviral therapies ( ART ) that controls human immunodeficiency virus 's ( HIV ) viral load . Researchers at The George Washington University in D.C. and their colleagues found that a drug called Fingolimod , that is approved for multiple sclerosis treatment , also blocks HIV transmission and infection in human cells . <p> Since the HIV virus has the ability to establish latency by integrating its genome into that of a human 's host cells , people with HIV have to remain on ART throughout their lives . Globally , around 40 million people live with HIV . <p> Taking these hurdles into consideration , scientists are looking for ways to discover novel strategies to specifically target HIV latency and infection . Albert Bosque , lead author of the study and an assistant professor at The George Washington University , took a different approach by targeting what is known as phingosine-1-phosphate ( S1P ) receptors . <p> These receptors are a vital component of the immune system that is specifically involved in the progression of infection . The researchers decided to focus on U.S. FDA approved drug , Fingolimod , that has been found to block S1P receptors . <p> By using fingolimod , they further found that the drugs works by blocking the infection of human immune cells known as CD4+ T cells and interrupting multiple steps in the HIV lifecycle . The drug has the ability to diminish the density of CD4 , the protein found on the surface of T cells , and simultaneously preventing viral binding and fusion . <p> The researchers observed that the drug , fingolimod , was not only able @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ reduced any detectable latent virus by 65.85% . Furthermore , PCR testing proved that this decrease in infection level also revealed a 60.4% reduction in integrated DNA . <p> The study 's authors noted , " These results indicate that Fingolimod merits further investigation as an exciting novel therapy for HIV . " <p> They further claimed that this was the first study the potential role of S1P signaling in the process of the HIV infection getting established . The study also delves into the potential to modulate this pathway to alter the course of infection or prevent establishment of the latent reservoir in CD4+ T cells . <p> This compound is clinically approved for the treatment of multiple sclerosis and does n't have major side-effects if taken orally on a daily basis .
