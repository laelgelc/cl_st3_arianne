Widder / Max Planck Institute of Immunobiology and Epigetics <p> Deep-sea anglerfish are unique and unusual for many reasons . They have strangely shaped bodies with horrifying teeth , attract prey using a lure attached to their snouts , and reproduce by fusing their bodies with their mates . Now , a new study suggests that these anglerfish have modified immunity genes that make this form of " sexual parasitism " possible . <p> Deep-sea anglerfish are commonly found below depths of 1,000 feet . Light , food , and mates can be hard to come by in these environments , so anglerfish have adopted a unique strategy to produce offspring . Tiny male anglerfish , which can top out at 1 centimeter in length , will attach to female anglerfish that are many times larger than they are . This arrangement can be temporary or permanent , depending on the species .
