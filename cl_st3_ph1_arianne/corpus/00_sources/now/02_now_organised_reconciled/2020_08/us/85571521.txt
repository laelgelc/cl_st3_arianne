<h> ' Food is more important right now ' : COVID-19 forces some to struggle paying energy bills <p> While the the COVID-19 pandemic resulted in job losses left and right , Evansville resident Aerial Jones was thankful she and her husband kept working . Then the bills started coming in . <p> With two kids at home eating more food and using more energy , her electricity bill doubled -- costing as much as $400 a month to run her three-bedroom home . <p> Jones , an assistant manager at an apartment complex , had never before worried about paying for her utility bill . <p> But now she turned to a payment plan from her utility , Vectren , to make ends meet without sacrificing other necessities . <p> " Expenses have gone up ... groceries have gone up , " Jones said . " Food is more important right now .
