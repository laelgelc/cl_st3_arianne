<p> As triple-digit heat tests the limits of California 's electrical grid to keep millions of people cool , it is clear the effects of human-caused global warming are already here . <p> But the extreme heat baking the Western US is a mere preview of what could be coming : A new study finds that in the future , the heat risk facing the country 's biggest cities could be far greater than previously thought . <p> Without cuts to greenhouse gas emissions , major US cities could see roughly between 13 and 30 times more population-adjusted exposure to extreme heat by 2100 as compared to the beginning of this century , the study found . <p> The study was published Monday in the journal Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences , and adds to the abundant evidence that mankind 's ability to halt global warming and adapt to rising temperatures will be two of the defining challenges of this century . <p> The researchers ' projections are based on a high greenhouse gas emissions scenario , where concentrations of heat-trapping gases continue to climb in the atmosphere @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ decades of this century . <p> Along with the role of greenhouse gas emissions , the scientists also factored in how population growth and urban development can influence a city 's heat risk . <p> The study focused on 47 of the country 's biggest cities . <p> Each hour that a person is exposed to extreme temperatures equals one " person hour , " which is one of the key metrics the researchers used to quantify the projected growth in heat risk for each city . <p> They found that the New York City region could see an increase of 24.6 billion person hours of heat exposure , the largest of any metro region examined and more than twice the increase projected in any other city .
