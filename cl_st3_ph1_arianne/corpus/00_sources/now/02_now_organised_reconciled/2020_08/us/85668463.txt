Yet , the exact potential of regionalized food systems to mitigate climate change and ensure food security remains unknown . <p> Prajal Pradhan , from the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research , and colleagues have quantified greenhouse gas emissions from food transport under a regionalized and a globalized model . In the first case , demand was set to be met by peripheral food production ( modelled as 10 km concentric rings ) ; in the second , food could be supplied from any location . Each UAU 's ' foodshed ' -- that is , the area required to produce enough food for the local population and enough feed for its livestock -- was multiplied by emission factors for land and sea to calculate the total emissions from food transport . Projections for 2050 were also obtained considering demographic growth , dietary changes , feed conversion efficiency , food waste reduction , and crop yields under moderate and severe climate change with and without carbon fertilization . All scenarios assumed potential crop yields and no agricultural expansion . <p> Results showed that 80% of the food demand of the UAUs could be met within a 500 km distance . Additionally , elements such as transport infrastructure , country-specific emission factors , comparative advantages in food production , trade barriers and a more nuanced representation of food nutritional quality would ideally be included in future research . Yet , this study illustrates very clearly how regionalization can affect food transport emissions , and highlights the need for integrated policies to achieve emission reduction .
