<h> David Holt-Biddle looks at wildlife issues and the Summit of the Forum for China-Africa Co-operation . <p> INTERNATIONAL conservation groups have referred to the issue of wildlife poaching in Africa and the Summit of the Forum for China-Africa Co-operation in Johannesburg last weekend as a perfect example of ' the elephant in the room ' situation . <p> Some 50 African leaders , including President Jacob Zuma , of course , and President Xi Jinping of China , met to discuss a comprehensive array of industrialisation and developmental issues , including major deals on infrastructure and agriculture , without coming close to discussing one of the major crises facing Africa today -- wildlife poaching . <p> The African Wildlife Foundation said that one could not discuss development without talking about natural resources and primarily wildlife . <p> The AWF pointed out that China consumed 75 percent of the ivory poached in Africa and asked the question : if China could bring the giant panda back from the brink of extinction , why could it not do something about the African elephant . <p> The Worldwide Fund for Nature ( @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Welfare have added their voices to the call for China-Africa action , pointing out that up to 40 000 elephants a year are being poached in Africa and that the price of ivory had rocketed to R30 000 a kilogram . <p> It was also pointed out that the current elephant population in Africa was about 470 000 compared to some 20-million a hundred years ago . The WWF urged both China and the African states to " incorporate clear goals " in the Summit 's final declaration . <p> It was pointed out that China already imposes severe penalties for ivory trafficking , such as an average sentence of 15 years ( I carried a story in this column some time ago about a group of poachers caught with a dead elephant in the southern province of Yunnan , bordering on Laos and Vietnam , saying that they were tried on the spot by the authorities , found guilty , sentenced and executed right there and then ) , but none of this is enough , say the conservation groups . <p> In view of the above , it @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the Johannesburg Summit of the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation , issued at the end of the Summit on December 5 by the African and Chinese leaders . It is a 30-point document and there is only one mention of conservation , in Point 25.4 , Paragraph 2 : <p> ' Implement the " Initiative on China-Africa Cooperative Partnership for Peace and Security " , support the building of the collective security mechanism in Africa and jointly manage non-traditional security issues and global challenges such as , but not limited to , food security , energy security , cyber security , climate change , biodiversity conservation , major communicable disease and transnational crimes . ' That 's it . <p> This has been about elephants and their ivory , but there has been no mention of the crisis around rhino horn , or the roaring trade in lion bones and body parts ( tiger bones and other bits are becoming increasingly difficult to find ) , or pangolin scales or any of the many other wildlife products that end up in China and South East Asia . <p> There really is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ at the next ' summit ' they 'll talk about these issues .
