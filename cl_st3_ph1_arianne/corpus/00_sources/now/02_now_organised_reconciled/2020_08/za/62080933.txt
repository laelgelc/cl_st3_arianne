<h> When the President met the Imam -- with Mali 's future hanging in the balance <p> On July 3 , arguably the two most powerful men in Mali greeted each other in a reception room somewhere in Bamako . The room was lined with leather sofas ; as they spoke , the national flag hung on a pole behind them . <p> Ibrahim Boubacar Ke ? ta is the president of Mali . His guest that day was Mahmoud Dicko : the influential imam who represents the greatest threat to Ke ? ta 's presidency . <p> After their meeting , as he left , Dicko addressed the president 's cameraman . He sounded optimistic . " We talked about everything that concerns this crisis and the country in general . I think that with the will of everyone and of all the parties concerned , we will , inshallah , find the solution . In the south , where more than 90% of the country lives , endemic poverty and food insecurity has fuelled widespread anger towards the government , with the Covid-19 pandemic worsening the situation . <p> Multiple corruption scandals have not helped any of these tensions , or instilled confidence in IBK 's government , which has been in power since 2013 . <p> Against this backdrop , Dicko has been able to channel public frustration towards the state with an approach that resonates widely : he advocates for moral values and good governance ; calls for unity and divine assistance ; and encourages reconciliation between ethnic and religious groups . <p> Nor is he afraid to criticise the president . After 60,000 people packed into a Bamako stadium to demand his resignation , Maiga duly resigned in April -- along with his entire cabinet . <p> Just two months later , Dicko launched the June 5 Movement -- an anti-government umbrella group that brings together religious leaders , civil society groups and the coalition of opposition parties .
