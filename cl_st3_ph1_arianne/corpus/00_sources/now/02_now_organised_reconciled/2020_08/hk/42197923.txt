<h> Preserving the Buddha 's Birthplace : A Case Against a Spiritual Disneyland <p> Lumbini , the birthplace of the Buddha , resides as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in present-day Nepal . However , with the effects of mass tourism this sacred pilgrimage destination is quickly becoming less a sacred garden and more a concrete Disneyland . <p> When I visited , I was struck by the juxtaposition of ornate monasteries with expanses of construction between them that made rickshaw or bike transportation almost a necessity . The 4.8- by 1.6-kilometer area is split into Theravada monasteries in the east and Mahayana and Vajrayana monasteries in the west , each of which are paid for and maintained by a different country . Tours on loud motorboats are operated on a brick-lined canal spanned by arched bridges , panning dramatically between the two main monuments : the Eternal Peace Flame and the Peace Pagoda . <p> Eternal Peace Flame , Lumbini . The young Siddhartha Gautama and his cousin Devadatta were exploring the gardens behind the palace when a large , low-flying crane caught their attention . Devadatta took out an arrow and shot the bird . The injured animal then took refuge by the young Buddha-to-be , who calmed the bird by removing the arrow and tending to the wound . <p> The cousins fought over who could claim the bird . Devadatta argued that because he shot it and had the right to hunt , it belonged to him . Siddhartha said that because the bird had not yet died and was seeking protection , it was his right to take care of it . <p> They went to Siddhartha 's father , the king , who took the moral issue to the court . The birds are critically endangered as a result of habitat destruction in Lumbini , pesticides leaching into their food chain , thinner egg shells leading to a lower hatching rate , and electrocution from flying into power transmission lines . <p> Dharmendra and I stood on a watch tower in the middle of the quiet sanctuary , the early morning sun highlighting the bright red bobbing heads of two cranes and their chick as they weaved between the tall grass . " <p> Dharmendra told me that Sarus cranes live in pairs and only have one baby annually . Often , however , their eggs are eaten by jackals or stolen by local children to make omelets . <p> Kenzo Tange , a famous Japanese architect , created the master plan for Lumbini in 1978 . He envisioned leaving 60 per cent of the land as nature preserve to reflect the Buddha 's teachings on the harmony between humans and nature . The International Crane Foundation has a 50-year lease with the Lumbini Development Trust ( LDT ) for a 254-acre bird sanctuary on the northern edge of the Lumbini Master Plan . <p> Finding a delicate balance between tourist accommodation and nature preservation is a major challenge .
