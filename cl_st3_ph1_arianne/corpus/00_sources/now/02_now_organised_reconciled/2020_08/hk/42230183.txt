Photo : iStock <p> There is a good chance the device on which you are reading this contains cobalt , a metal essential to the production of batteries in phones and laptops . There is also a serious likelihood that the cobalt in your device was mined by slaves . <p> Almost two-thirds of the cobalt mined around the world comes from the Democratic Republic of the Congo ( DRC ) . The central African country has a notorious reputation for human rights abuses , including slave labor . <p> It is all but impossible to know if cobalt from the DRC is slave-free . The same goes for many other products and commodities around the world , from tuna to coffee . <p> Some businesses see a solution in blockchain , the technology behind bitcoin , to verify global supply chains . <p> This is the latest promise @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for unregulated prison economies , climate change and counterfeiting . Even if we can not put all our hopes on technology solving complex social problems , maybe this time it will prove part of the solution . <p> In the case of cobalt , the supply chain can consist of countless middlemen who buy and mix cobalt from countless different mines . This means it is almost impossible for a cobalt buyer such as a battery maker to trace where the metal comes from . <p> The DRC 's cobalt industry encompasses a wide range of working conditions . Some miners are paid relatively well and work in safe conditions , but around a fifth of all cobalt is dug up by about 110,000 to 150,000 workers in small " artisanal " mines . Those who work in this unregulated sector often earn a pittance and toil in unsafe conditions . <p> As sales of electric cars swells demand for cobalt , these conditions are worsening . <p> It is difficult to know exactly what proportion of the DRC 's cobalt industry uses slave labor . A 2014 report by UNICEF estimated 40,000 children were working in mines in DRC 's south , most of them digging cobalt . <p> Blockchain 's promise <p> It is not just cobalt . The same is true of everything from copper to cocoa . It is hard to know how products are made or where they are sourced . <p> So how can we ensure supply chains are not tainted by modern slavery ? <p> This is where companies are experimenting with blockchain technology . To understand their interest , let 's recap the basics of this technology . <p> Think of blockchain as an online public ledger . Once a transaction occurs , a permanent and unchangeable record of that transaction is created and has to be validated by others in the blockchain . Mining and resources giant BHP wants to use the same technology to verify copper supplies . <p> Ford and IBM are part of a consortium looking to use the technology to monitor cobalt supplies . It would mean the ability to track the metal from mine to battery . Ethically mined cobalt can be recorded in the blockchain and followed as it moves around the supply chain . <p> Challenges remain <p> While blockchain is promising , we need to address several challenges if it is going to work . <p> A crucial element in any blockchain is the " consensus protocol " . This determines who gets to validate a transaction , whether it be all participants , a majority , a select few or a random selection . There is no guarantee this will happen , especially for marginalized or oppressed workers . <p> Second , it is important to know what standard for ethical sourcing a blockchain upholds . There are several blockchain platforms , so different , potentially less robust , standards could easily develop . This is an issue for other areas of ethical certification , where competing schemes for goods such as coffee exist . <p> Third , we should always question the link between a " block " and its material reality . Finding a way to insert goods made using slave labor into the blockchain would be highly lucrative . Since the integrity of blockchain data depends on humans , it is vulnerable to inaccuracies or fraud . <p> Fourth , blockchain may create a " digital divide " . Larger suppliers with technical experience will have less trouble using this technology , while smaller suppliers may be left out . We need to guard against blockchain becoming a barrier to small suppliers entering the market . <p> No technological fix <p> As a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ into where goods came from .
