<p> The private sector plays a key role in providing access to water , sanitation and hygiene ( WASH ) products and services in rural and peri-urban communities of Tanzania . <p> Secondary school students at Iringa girls learning to use hedhi cup during training session conducted at their school by Anuflo Industries . <p> Most people living in these areas rely on hardware shops and pharmacies to supply them with latrine construction materials , and personal hygiene and water treatment products . However , WASH businesses face many constraints . <p> Challenges include high transaction costs , small marginal profit from sales of WASH products , and lack of consumer awareness on the availability and importance of the products . As a result , many communities have limited access to quality products and services as compared to communities in urban areas . <p> To broaden access to affordable WASH products , WARIDI works with suppliers , wholesalers , and retailers to build strong distribution networks that reach into underserved communities . <p> WARIDI has provided business training in marketing , basic accounting and business planning to more than 480 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ peri-urban areas such as hardware and farm input stores , and pharmacies to 20 local government authorities ( LGAs ) in Pwani , Morogoro , Iringa , Njombe and Mbeya regions . <p> On the supplier side , WARIDI 's enterprise development team supports companies to strengthen their supply chains , customer relationship management , and to market products outside of urban centers . <p> WARIDI is linking WASH product producers to shops through Business-to-Business ( B2B ) meetings , events that bring together all members of the supply chain in one room , to build understanding of the products , awareness of potential business opportunities , and lay a foundation of trust for future business relationships . <p> Through these B2B meetings more than 70 contracts have been signed between products retailers and wholesalers in 20 LGAs which will support long-term market development for sanitation and hygiene products . In collaboration with five ( 5 ) Local Government Authorities ( LGAs ) , WARIDI has supported construction of improved sanitation facilities for 25 public schools enabling over 14,000 pupils from primary schools and one secondary school to use high-quality latrines . The pour flush latrines use onsite treatment consisting of a septic tank and soak-away pit and have a washable floor . These latrines provide a much higher level of service than what schools previously had and longer useful life than a standard pit latrine . The latrines are also designed to meet the needs of girls and children with physical disabilities . Girls toilets have a room dedicated to menstrual hygiene management and a burning chamber for sanitary pad disposal . WARIDI ensure a reliable water supply , water storage and hand-washing facilities are also in place whereby students can easily use the facilities and learn healthy sanitation practices .
