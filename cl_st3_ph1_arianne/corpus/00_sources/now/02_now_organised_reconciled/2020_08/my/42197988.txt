Photo : AFP/Arun Sankar <p> By Abhaya Srivastava <p> THRISSUR , India : Green , spiky and with a strong , sweet smell , the bulky jackfruit has morphed from a backyard nuisance in India 's south coast into the meat-substitute darling of vegans and vegetarians in the West . <p> Part of the South Asia 's diet for centuries , jackfruit was so abundant that tonnes of it went to waste every year . <p> But now India , the world 's biggest producer of jackfruit , is capitalising on its growing popularity as a " superfood " meat alternative -- touted by chefs from San Francisco to London and Delhi for its pork-like texture when unripe . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the international level , the interest in jackfruit has grown manifold , " Varghese Tharakkan tells AFP from his orchard in Kerala 's Thrissur district . <p> The fruit , which weighs five kilogrammes on average , has a waxy yellow flesh when ripe and is eaten fresh , or used to make cakes , juices , ice creams and crisps . <p> When unripe , it is added to curries or fried , minced and sauted . In the West , shredded jackfruit has become a popular alternative to pulled pork and is even used as a pizza topping . <p> " People love it , " Anu Bhambri , who owns a chain of restaurants in the US and India , explains . <p> " The jackfruit tacos have been a hit at each and every location .
