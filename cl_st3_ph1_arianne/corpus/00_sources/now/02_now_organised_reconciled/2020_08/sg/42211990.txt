<h> New guidelines designed to give poor and isolated communities more of a say in how tropical forests are used and preserved around the world were published on Thursday , by the Food and Agriculture Organization ( FAO ) . <p> The guidelines aim to support countries in tropical regions to manage forest lands and provide sustainable development for local communities . Image : Jonathan Hull , CC BY-NC-ND 2.0 <h> Share <p> The FAO is the lead United Nations agency on the sustainable use of forests , and the voluntary guidelines refer to so-called forest concessions ; laws and policies that allow local communities and private individuals or businesses to use forested land in return for payment or services . <p> It is hoped that by adopting the guidelines , countries in tropical regions will be able to curb abuse of forest land due to poor management ; corruption and bribery ; tenure disputes , and the harassment of local communities . <p> " If well managed , forest concessions can have multiple socio-economic and environmental benefits and increase the value of standing forests for present and future generations @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Policy and Resources Division at FAO . <p> " All in all , they can improve the lives of rural communities in some of the poorest and most isolated parts of the world . They can also reduce carbon footprint and combat climate change , while increasing employment opportunities and services in local communities . <p> Proper concessions also add to local and national revenues , which can in turn be invested in development , health and services . <p> A good example is the Borneo Initiative , a foundation established in 2008 , which has provided financial and technical assistance to concession-holders linking them to a professional network of experts to guide them through the process .
