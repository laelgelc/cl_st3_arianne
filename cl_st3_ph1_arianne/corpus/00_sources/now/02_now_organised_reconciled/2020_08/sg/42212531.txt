<h> Millions in India face eviction amid coronavirus recovery push <h> In the last three years , 22 people were evicted every hour , with only about a fourth being resettled . <p> A homeless man and his son cover themselves with a plastic sheet to protect themselves from rain as they walk to a shelter , during a nationwide lockdown to slow the spreading of the coronavirus disease , in Kolkata , India . This is indefensible , " she said . <p> Nearly half of all evictions were for so-called city beautification drives , with infrastructure projects , conservation schemes and disaster management also being reasons for displacement , according to HLRN . <p> In nearly all cases , authorities did not follow due process including giving sufficient notice , and only about a quarter of those removed were resettled , it said . <p> There is no official data on evictions in India . <p> Officials in Delhi and Mumbai , which had evictions during the lockdown , did not respond to requests for comment , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ had an eviction , said none took place then , and that notice is generally given . <p> " These are illegal structures on public land . But implementation has been slow , and campaigners say it does not address the issue of homelessness . <p> Last month , federal authorities approved a plan to develop affordable urban rental housing for migrant workers after millions of labourers fled lockdowns that left many without jobs and homes . <p> Yet nearly 15 million people live under the threat of displacement from infrastructure and development projects , including about 2 million people whose rights to forest land have been rejected , according to HLRN . <p> With the government keen to boost economic growth in the wake of the pandemic , people living at @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ are likely to be removed , Chaudhry said . <p> " With every house demolished , the state backtracks on its commitment of housing for all , " she told the Thomson Reuters Foundation . <p> " It is important for the government to recognise forced evictions as gross violation of human rights .
