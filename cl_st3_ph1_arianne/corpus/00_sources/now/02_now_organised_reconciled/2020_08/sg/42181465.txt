But this process involved trade-offs . <p> Driven by the desire for wealth , firms and governments sought to reduce costs and boost productivity and profits , which led to disruptions that sometimes left hundreds of millions of people impoverished and unemployed . <p> For decades , workers in mines and factories were brutally coerced to eke out ever more output , until they managed to organise and secure some political power for themselves . <p> Thousands of people flock to the Myanmar jade mines in the hoping of finding an @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ : AFP/Ye Aung Thu ) <p> And , of course , the early industrial age encouraged slavery and the quest for access to natural resources , which led to massive wars and brutal forms of imperialist rule . <p> These excesses were neither an aberration nor inevitable . Many have since been corrected through the market economy , labour-relations reforms , state regulation , and new institutions . <p> But other significant unintended consequences of industrialisation have yet to be addressed , because no organised political constituency emerged to address them . The most pressing concern is catastrophic global risks , the most obvious being anthropogenic climate change -- a prime example of how a process of enrichment can create an existential threat . <p> A second , somewhat related problem is biodiversity loss . <p> The estimated rate of species extinction today is anywhere from 100 to 1,000 times that of the pre-industrial era , yet there is still very little recognition of the risks created by such a radical destabilisation of nature . <p> The third global risk is nuclear war .
