<h> Cash transfer programs , efficient government expenditure and capacity building are ways in which communities across Asia can receive adequate social protection and meet their basic needs . <h> Share <p> Social protection is crucial to achieve the Sustainable Development Goals ( SDGs ) . SDG Target 1.3 emphasises the importance of a social protection floor covering the nutritional , health , and educational needs of children ; income security for the working-age population ; pensions for the elderly ; and universal health care coverage . <p> In recent years , countries in Asia and the Pacific have taken serious steps to expand various aspects of their social protection systems with increased spending . Nearly 60 per cent of the region 's population now has access to health care . <p> The People 's Republic of China ( PRC ) , Mongolia , Thailand , and Viet Nam have established comprehensive social protection programs that cover not only social pensions but also health insurance . But many gaps remain . Benefits are generally low , and informal workers are not fully covered by pension and health insurance . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ people in Asia and the Pacific have easy access to healthcare , and that access is limited to those who are already covered . Only 30 per cent of people in Asia enjoy some type of social assistance , and less than 20 per cent have social insurance . Just over half of all older people in the region receive a pension . <p> This rather gloomy picture is closely linked to inadequate financing of social protection systems . An ADB study of 16 Asian countries to be published later this year shows the weighted average public social expenditure was 4.4 per cent of GDP in 2015 , barely half of the global average . <p> To close the region 's social protection gap , countries would need to spend at least 3 per cent more of GDP . Expanded fiscal burdens from socio-economic changes -- namely rapidly aging populations , rapid urbanisation , increasing costs of social services , and rising exposure to natural disasters and climate change -- make it even harder to come up with the funds . <p> So , how can countries find the financing they need ? But there is room for improvement . <h> Indonesia cash transfers is a good example of targeted social protection <p> Arguably the most significant transformation would be to shift toward a more targeted approach , away from general subsidies , particularly in response to economic shocks and natural disasters . Enhanced targeting , coupled with strengthening the productive aspects of programs , can have a greater impact on social protection for a given level of resources . <p> A good example is Indonesia , which has implemented a series of cash transfer programs over the past decade .
