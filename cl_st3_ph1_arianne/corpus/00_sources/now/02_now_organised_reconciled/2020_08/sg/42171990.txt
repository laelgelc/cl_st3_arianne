<p> In the 1980s , the then Prime Minister Indira Gandhi took a strong position against the construction of a controversial hydel power project that would jeopardise the Silent Valley 's rich biodiversity . What forced Indira Gandhi to take such a strong stand was India 's first environmental protection campaign -- the Silent Valley Movement -- that was organised in the 1970s against the repeated attempts of Kerala state electricity board to establish the project at any cost . <p> Now , years after the historical struggle that turned Silent Valley as one among the well-protected biodiversity-rich regions of the country , Kerala government is in the process of converting its buffer zone as a wildlife sanctuary to ensure foolproof protection . The proposed Bhavani Wildlife Sanctuary comprising the entire buffer zone of Silent Valley would soon turn to be Kerala 's 25th protected area . <p> " We are all set to declare the 148 square kilometres buffer zone of Silent Valley National Park as Bhavani wildlife sanctuary with the objective of further cementing the on-going conservation efforts . The topographical isolation facilitated by the surrounding steep ridges , hills and escarpments has helped these forests to develop into a rich biodiversity reserve untouched hitherto by humans . It has over 960 species of flora with 17 among them coming under the International Union for Conservation of Nature 's ( IUCN ) Red List . <p> Though the legends are aplenty , what makes this largest habitat of highly endangered lion-tailed macaques is the ecological importance it holds . <p> The lion-tailed macaque . As per a rough estimate , over half of the global population of these macaques can be found inside these forests .
