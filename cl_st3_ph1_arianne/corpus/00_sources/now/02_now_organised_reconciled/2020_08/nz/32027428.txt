<h> Pacific Dance NZ shines the spotlight on Tuvalu for 2020 <p> A Tuvaluan choreographer who has been dancing the traditional fatele since she was young , will now be able to showcase her culture to people of all ages in Auckland and abroad . <p> Dancers from Tuvalu . Photo : RNZ/ Koroi Hawkins <p> Molia Alama Tulafono has been named the Artist in Residence for 2020 for Pacific Dance New Zealand , as it celebrates its 10 year anniversary . <p> Tulafono started dancing in her Fale ote Kaiga , ( family home ) , and observed and learnt the basic dance forms from her aunts . <p> She also learnt at the faleumu , ( working/cooking hut ) , the practicalities of the dance artforms , including lakei , ( costuming ) , kupu o fatele , ( fatele lyrics ) , choreography and movement and performed in the falekaupule , ( island meeting house ) . <p> " I have been working with our elders and knowledge holders to make sure I 'm telling our stories in a way that is culturally appropriate and our protocols @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that level . " <p> Tulafono explained that Tuvaluans told their stories through the art of dancing , and empowered the young to embrace their language , culture and heritage . <p> " We develop creative , confident and resilient leaders through the art of dance and costume making . <p> " The vision is to continue to invest in our cultural dance practices and progress into prominent leadership to identify and address issues and be a beacon of light for our future , " she said . <p> Director of Pacific Dance NZ Iosefa Enari said the decision was based on Tulafono 's rich knowledge of Tuvalu 's heritage , language , culture and costume making . <p> " Tuvalu as a nation and as a culture deserves recognition alongside our other Pacific countries , " he said . <p> The residency will run for six-weeks starting on 22 August and will conclude during Tuvalu Language Week on 2 October . <p> For the residency , the artist has prepared workshops that have incorporated unique themes : the Laeva effect , ( a concept that empowers people to strive @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ strands of love ) , Te Niu Fakamauganiu , ( climate change using coconut shells to convey stories and message ) , Taku Fatele , ( Cultural instruments and storytelling ) , Tou Fou , ( garland and fatele ) and Te Fenua , ( the final showcase ) . <p> These themes will cover myths and legends , dance costumes and the art of dancing ' fatele ' .
