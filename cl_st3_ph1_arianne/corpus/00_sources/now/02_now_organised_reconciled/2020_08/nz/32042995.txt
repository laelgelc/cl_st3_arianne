<h> New Marlborough District Council grant puts environment on par with the likes of sports and arts <p> Councillors in Marlborough have put their " money where their mouths are " creating a new kitty for protecting the region 's environment . <p> The Marlborough District Council is looking at banning non-self-contained vehicles from its freedom camping sites . Photo : LDR / Chloe Ranford <p> The new ' Working for Nature/Mahi mo te Taiao ' grant will see the environment treated on par with the likes of sport , arts and culture , heritage , and youth projects , which have been receiving council handouts for years . <p> The $70,000 funding will be available to landowners , businesses and community groups looking to protect or restore the environment . <p> A council report said environmental projects had been left to battle for attention during the Marlborough District Council 's annual plan process each year , leading to " ad hoc decision-making " and community uncertainty . <p> Council environmental scientist Alan Johnson unveiled plans to change this at an environment committee meeting on Thursday , saying the new @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ October . <p> The grant would be split across two categories : $25,000 under ' Habitat Marlborough ' , for projects that restored or improved native habitats or biodiversity ; and $45,000 under ' Protecting Marlborough ' , for projects around pest control . <p> Habitat Marlborough projects could receive up to $5000 , while Protecting Marlborough projects could receive up to $15,000 . <p> Funds could be used to cover professional advice , project co-ordinators , pest management devices , weed control equipment , and the cost of buying new native plants . <p> In doing so , the council would fulfil its national and regional obligations to improve biodiversity , freshwater quality and pest control , and reduce the impacts of climate change . <p> Councillor Gerald Hope said it was the first time in his decades at the council that environmental protection had been incentivised , " providing teeth to our policies " . <p> " It puts the money where our mouths are , " he said . <p> Councillor Francis Maher said he supported the grant . <p> " These are very real issues that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ make sure that we leave a legacy for the even younger people . " <p> Councillor David Oddie said it was a " positive move " . <p> " There 's going to be a massive rush for the funds we 've got available , but we 've got to work through that , " Oddie said . <p> Applications would be judged on how they benefited the environment , their likelihood of success and securing other funding , and how much future maintenance was required . <p> Successful applicants had to report how money was spent . <p> Funds for the grant would be pulled from the Tui to Town project , which encouraged people to plant natives , and the Marlborough Landscape Group , in charge of land issues . <p> More money could be thrown at the grant in the future , depending on demand and the outcomes it achieved . <p> If adopted at full council next month , it was expected funding applications would open from 1 October . <p> Local Democracy Reporting is a public interest news service supported by RNZ , the News @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @
