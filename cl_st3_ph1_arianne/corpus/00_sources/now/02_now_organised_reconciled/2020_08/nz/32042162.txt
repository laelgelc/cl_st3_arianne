<h> New plan gives Pacific people chance to stay home <p> The Pacific is at the forefront of climate change , and some countries are facing the displacement of their people . Laura Walters travelled to Kiribati and Fiji for this Newsroom three-part series on climate migration . In the third instalment , she talks to the Kiwis behind a project that might change the game for low-lying Pacific nations . <p> For Pacific countries like Kiribati , the threat of climate change means an uncertain future for the islands , and the people that occupy them . <p> Until recently , there has been talk of climate refugees , and special humanitarian visas , but Pacific leaders are telling New Zealand politicians they want to stay on their land , and fight against climate change . <p> This has led to a change of thinking towards mitigation and adaptation measures . <p> One of the prime objectives of New Zealand 's Pacific Reset , launched just over a year ago , is to assist with these projects . <p> But the Pacific is going to need a lot more @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ some willpower if countries like Kiribati -- often referred to as a ' sinking ' and ' disappearing ' nation -- are going to keep their growing populations on a shrinking island . <p> This resolve to try and adapt -- rather than migrate - has presented a challenge on an unprecedented scale , but some see this as an opportunity to change the way the world deals with climate change . <p> Initial designs to sustain Kiribati 's population into the future were bold and futuristic . <p> The former Kiribati government believed looking at futuristic underwater villages , or floating oil rigs , was one of the few choices its people had left .
