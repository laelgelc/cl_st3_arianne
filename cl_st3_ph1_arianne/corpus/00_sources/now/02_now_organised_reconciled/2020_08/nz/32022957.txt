On the flip side , rising interest rates typically mean prior monetary policy was too loose . <p> What Milton Friedman said <p> Milton Friedman drew an analogy between monetary policy and a thermostat . <p> If monetary policy is working perfectly , it accommodates anticipated changes in economic activity to keep inflation around the midpoint of the target band . A very good thermostat might link to the MetService hourly weather forecast and start the furnace as the temperature outside begins to drop , so the indoor temperature never changes . That does not mean that the thermostat 's policy has become accommodative and is now targeting a higher temperature , or ( worse ! ) that the furnace makes the room cold . It just means that it is reacting to a past error . <p> For much of the past decade , the RBNZ has undershot the midpoint of its CPI target -- with several quarters ' inflation figures below the lower bound of the target range .
