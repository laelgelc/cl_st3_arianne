" <p> In the next 80 years , flooding around the planet 's landmasses is likely to rise by almost 50% , endangering many millions of coastal dwellers . If people go on burning ever greater volumes of fossil fuels , while destroying ever more natural forest , then another 77 million people could be at risk of flooding , a rise of 52% . <p> And these floods -- increasingly frequent and extending over greater areas -- will put at risk cities , homes , resorts and industries valued at more than $14 trillion . <p> This sum alone is worth 20% of global GDP ( gross domestic product ) , the economist 's preferred indicator of economic health and wealth , according to a new study in the journal Scientific Reports . <p> The researchers built their argument on historic data from 681 tide-gauge stations around the world to model @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Climate News Network . <p> " Compared with now , what we see as a one-in-100-year extreme flood event will be ten times more frequent because of climate change , " researchers said . <p> They conclude that the land area exposed to extreme flood will increase by more than 250,000 sq kms -- an increase of 48% -- to 800,000 sq kms , a threat to 252 million people . <p> " A warming climate is driving sea level rise because water expands as it warms , and glaciers are melting .
