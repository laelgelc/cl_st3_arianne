<p> Well-known brands , such as Mars and Nestle , are buying through global traders cocoa that is grown illegally in dwindling national parks and reserves in Ivory Coast and Ghana , environmental group Mighty Earth said . <p> " Every consumer of chocolate is a part of either the problem or the solution , " Etelle Higonnet , campaign director at Mighty Earth , told the Thomson Reuters Foundation . <p> Deforestation for cocoa happens in sight of authorities and chocolate traders are aware of it , they said . <p> Loss of natural forests is problematic because they act as a home for the region 's wildlife , and a key weapon against climate change , absorbing carbon dioxide - a major driver of climate change - as they grow . <p> Available land for new cocoa plantations in Ivory Coast ran out long ago , so farmers have moved into parks and reserves , taking advantage of a decade of political crisis that ended in 2011 . <p> Ivory Coast 's now has about 2.5 million hectares ( 6 million acres ) of natural forest , a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ according to European Union figures . Most of the losses have been due to expanding agriculture . <p> The government has struggled to evict farmers from forest reserves amid accusations in 2013 of human rights abuses by security forces . <p> Details of the Cocoa and Forests Initiative , which is initially focusing on Ivory Coast and Ghana , will be announced by November 's global climate talks in Bonn .
