<h> Hokusai a wave of inspiration for students <p> Many people will recognise the distinctive blue and white wave patterns that feature in Japanese woodblock artist Hokusai 's famous work The Great Wave off Kanagawa , often known simply as The Wave . <p> Depicting large waves surrounding Mount Fuji , the work is one of a series created around 1829 and is now visiting Australian shores for a brief sojourn at the National Gallery of Victoria in Melbourne . <p> Only revealed to the public for limited periods due to their fragility , the prints were created through a time consuming process of drawing the image , carving a woodblock from cherry or similar timber and then finally taking a print from the woodblock . <h> Inquiry focus <p> The Wave series of prints is a wonderful seed for an inquiry learning project , with students developing their own focus questions and then conducting research and creating an information report based on an area which interests them . <p> Focus areas could include : <p> * Researching which artists and products have used The Wave as inspiration for their @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Aboriginal art and surfwear brands ) . <p> * Finding out about Hokusai 's life and the period in which he was creating his woodblock prints , and how his work influenced others and was influenced by others . <p> * Discovering more about the town of Kanagawa , the location which forms the basis for the prints , as well as Mount Fuji , the highest mountain in Japan , which is shown in many of the prints in the series . <h> Creative design focus <p> Discovering the process by which Hokusai created his stunning artworks is useful as an artistic skill for students , although it is one which should be tackled with some care . <p> The tools which are used for true woodblock printing are necessarily very sharp and require a fair degree of skill to use safely . <p> In the appropriate hands and with good safety instruction and supervision , woodblock printing can be used to create a very wide range of images from a carved block which can be re-used many times over , with different colours and shades used to apply @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ In the wrong hands , however , woodblock printing can be responsible for all manner of injuries , particularly to unwary fingers that are left in the path of wayward blades . <p> For less skilled operators , a good option is to create a print from a block of polystyrene or soft linoleum- style materials that have been specially designed for younger hands to cut more safely . <p> The resulting print design can be recreated onto library bags , T-shirts , gift paper or celebration cards , allowing students to plan , design and create their own unique block prints that can be shared and re-used many times over .
