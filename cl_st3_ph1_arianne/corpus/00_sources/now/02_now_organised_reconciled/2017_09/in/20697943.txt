<p> Artistic interventions raise awareness and provide solutions through this travelling festival that opens in the city today <p> Artist Dayananda 's installation is n't quite exciting at first glance . After all , paddy saplings growing in coconut shells do n't sound worthy of an installation . Or do they ? Bunking the concept of art being synonymous with an object of beauty , this installation will trigger a moot question : ' What exactly is this ' ? <p> The young artist , whose grandfather was a farmer , explains his concerns about disappearing traditional farming practices . Like his grandfather , it was the norm for farmers to plant about 4-5 seed varieties of a particular crop in coconut shells filled with mud about 20 days before the actual planting . Depending on how the saplings grow , the farmer would then select the seed best suited for the season . That practice , says the artist , is long gone . I was fortunate to learn about it in my childhood , which has come into my art practice and I wish to carry on with this legacy by meeting several farmers who are also reviving this technique . " <p> Art interventions such as these are precisely what the curatorial collective ' Artport Making Waves ' looks at , in order to raise awareness about environmental issues with a focus on climate change . The festival at Goeth Institut/Max Mueller , ' ArtportGood Food India ' , is an international interdisciplinary incubator programme that brings together multi-layered projects exploring the centrality of food to human existence . <p> Curators Lina Vincent Sunish and Anne-Marie Melster have brought together 12 artists to comment on the human impact on environment and , importantly , engage audiences in proposals for alternate living . He has fashioned the carcass of a cow entirely out of wires and garbage bags which will be hung as a slaughtered animal , a theme that the artist informs was very common in European art . As Petruccelli writes : " To confront myself with this theme in India , meant to look at it from a completely different perspective . When does food becomes a taboo ; a symbol of mortality become an untouchable deity , and yet is the destiny of the divine giver so different from the brutal sacrifice depicted by European artists ? The destruction of nature is a sacrifice that is not even caused by the need of food : it 's a meaningless process that is actually leading us humans -- the butchers -- to painful consequences . Hence the comparison between slaughtered life and human condition seems to me quite tangible even here , in Bangalore . By depicting the tongue as the most sensitive and sensual muscle in a human 's body , the artist brings into light how we are responsible for some of the worst environmental practices , all thanks to our needs . <p> In the city , as in the rest of the world , there is increasing demand for ' free ' food as in oil-free , fat-free , gluten-free , sugar-free and so on . Artist Aishwaryan K 's text-based installation dwells on the traditional wholesome food that caters to the nutritional needs of the family and points at the capitalist model that society is following . <p> The festival will also include a ' City Charche ' -- a panel discussion to come up with collective action towards climate change .
