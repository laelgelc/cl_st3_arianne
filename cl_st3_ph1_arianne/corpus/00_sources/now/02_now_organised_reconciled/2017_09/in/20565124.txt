<p> Asia 's greatest rivers , including the Ganga , are fed by large stores of frozen water only dwarfed by glaciers at the poles . But in the next 100 years , those glaciers , and the rivers from them will lose a third of their water supply . <p> Reuters <p> The dire circumstances have been predicted by a study from Utrecht University in the Netherlands , which states that these frozen storehouses will melt away and lose a third of their mass by the year 2100 , thanks to global warming . That 's not even the worst part . What 's worse is that this is the best-case scenario prediction , if the Earth manages to limit global warming to only 1.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels . <p> More than a billion people across Asia depend on rivers like the Ganga , Yangtze , and Mekong , which are fed by glaciers in the Himalayas . These rivers are crucial as a source of fresh water , for farming , and hydro-electric power , all of which will suffer if their flows reduce .
