<h> Limiting global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius possible : study <p> Significant emission reductions are required if the key goal of the Paris agreement is to be achieved . Photo : Getty Images <p> London : Limiting the increase in global average temperatures to 1.5 degrees Celsius -- the key goal of the Paris Climate Agreement -- is still possible , but likely requires more ambitious emission reductions than those pledged so far , according to a study . <p> Researchers , including those from the University of Oxford in the UK , investigated the geophysical likelihood of limiting global warming to " well below two degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels and pursuing efforts to limit the temperature increase to 1.5 degrees Celsius " . They found that significant emission reductions are required if we are to achieve one of the key goals of the Paris agreement , and limit the increase in global average temperatures to 1.5 degrees Celsius . <p> Three approaches were used to evaluate the outstanding " carbon budget " -- the total amount of CO2 emissions compatible with a given global average warming - @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ provided by complex Earth System Models , new experiments with an intermediate-complexity model , and evaluating the implications of current ranges of uncertainty in climate system properties using a simple model . <p> In all cases , the level of emissions and warming to date were taken into account . " Limiting total CO2 emissions from the start of 2015 to beneath 240 billion tonnes of carbon ( 880 billion tonnes of CO2 ) , or about 20 years ' of current emissions , would likely achieve the Paris goal of limiting warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels , " said Richard Millar , post-doctoral research fellow at Oxford University . <p> " Previous estimates of the remaining 1.5 degrees Celsius carbon budget based on the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) 5th Assessment were around four times lower , so this is very good news for the achievability of the Paris targets , " said professor Pierre Friedlingstein of the University of Exeter in the UK . <p> " The 5th Assessment did not specifically address the implications of the very ambitious 1.5 degrees Celsius @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . The ambition of Paris caught much of the science community by surprise , " said Friedlingstein , co-author of the study published in the journal Nature Geoscience.
