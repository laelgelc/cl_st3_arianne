<h> Sooner or later Labour will retake the Beehive - then what ? <p> OPINION : By this time next week , we may have elected a Labour Government . <p> Who would be hardest hit by this ? Farmers ? Business owners ? Perhaps . It 's hard to say , given how little we know about the alternative Government 's agenda . One thing that should be clear , though , is that earnest and serious Leftwingers may be in for a rough time . <p> In the past nine years , there has been a large degree of alignment on the Left . Those with a tribal loyalty to Labour have tended to be firm allies of those with radical views -- and vice versa . The fact that Labour has had no realistic path to power is a factor in that . When you are not expected to deliver yourself , it is easy to pile on with criticism . <p> But now that Labour looks like it might win , the responsibilities of government have come to the fore . The party seems to be @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ground and its head out of the clouds . Gone are the directionless musings about a " universal basic income " , for example . <p> If Labour gets into power , the disappointments of reality will soon set in . <p> Governments are n't omnipotent . Things that seemed like no-brainers in opposition soon run into real-world difficulties . For those in power and with eyes on the next election , the path of least resistance is tempting indeed . <p> Labour has n't even won the election yet and it 's already had to make a big climb down on taxation . <p> One articulate voice on the Left , who blogs as Idiot/Savant , summed it up well saying it was " Labour in a nutshell " and that the pattern for the party is to " promise change , " but " do nothing to make it happen " . It " talks big about benefiting the many , not the few , then promises to preserve the status quo " . <p> But while some Left-wingers will be disappointed , others will pivot to making @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ will seem less important to press matters like inequality , climate change and river quality . It 's not that they wo n't care about those principles , but they will be subordinate to the goal of keeping Labour in power -- and National out of it . <p> And the Left may also find that it has to work harder to keep the mainstream press interested in those subjects as well . <p> Consider the plight of the homelessness in the United States . This was a perennial story during the George HW Bush years . According to the Media Research Council , there was an average of 52.5 network news stories on the subject a year during his presidency . Then Bill Clinton became president and , for his time in office , the average number of stories dropped to 16.5 a year . <p> Was this because Clinton immediately resolved the problem ? Hardly . Homelessness was a problem throughout this time . Given the increases in property prices and business outsourcing , this is not surprising . So why , as a matter of media attention @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ moved into the White House ? <p> In the same way , media interest in the subject of inequality has accelerated under National . Much more so than actual inequality has increased . I ca n't see this continuing under Labour -- even if there is no material improvement in the numbers . The lazy critique of this is to put it all down to partisan bias on the part of the media . <p> Some of this probably exists . The average journalist is much more likely to be a Labour or Green voter than the average voter . To deny that this has an effect on coverage is to deny human nature . <p> But few professional reporters wake up with pushing a partisan agenda on their mind . Talk with a reporter one on one and you will soon see they are quite dedicated to the ideals of their vocation . The idea that there is some kind of conspiracy to manipulate voters really does not stack up . <p> But at an elemental level , we are all primed to see the various types of government @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Left-wing bent , we take it for granted that concern for the poor is a priority for it . We are more likely to assume that what can be done , is being done . <p> The Centre-Right benefits from this too . The media used to give Helen Clark a hard time over not cutting taxes , but it does n't do this to National . We assume National is hawkish on the subject so it gets a pass , despite its limited record in delivering relief over the past nine years . <p> Sooner or later , Labour will retake the Beehive . When that happens , those who have found a ready audience for their cause will find it harder to be heard . And the longer the new government lasts , the worse it will get .
