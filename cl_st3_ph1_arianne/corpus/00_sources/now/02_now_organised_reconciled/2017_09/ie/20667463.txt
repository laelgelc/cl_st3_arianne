<h> Galway schools urged to join revolt against food waste <p> Galway schools are being asked to join a nationwide campaign against food waste as world hunger levels rise to new alarming levels . <p> Hunger Heroes , run by Irish aid agency Concern Worldwide , raises awareness of the global hunger crisis and teaches children how they can help solve it . <p> " Hunger Heroes is about fighting hunger and food waste locally , in our schools and in our kitchens , " said Lauren Wright , Concern 's schools and youth programme officer . <p> " This is a fun campaign for schools about very serious issues that affect us all . " <p> As part of the campaign , schools are asked to mark Hunger Heroes Day on Friday , October 27 , when students can leave their uniforms at home and go to school in fun costumes while learning how they can help the 815 million people in the world who do not have enough food to eat . <p> The stark figure , which amounts to 11 per cent of the world 's population @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and is up 38 million from the 777 million people estimated to be chronically undernourished in 2015 . <p> According to Concern Worldwide 30 per cent of all food in the world ( 1.3 billion tonnes ) is being wasted , which would be enough to feed all the world 's hungry four times over . <p> In Ireland , it is estimated that one million tonnes of food is let go to waste and that this costs each household an average of ? 700 a year . <p> The campaign was launched recently at Our Lady Queen of the Apostles Clonburris National School in Clondalkin , Dublin , where pupils dressed up as fruit and vegetables and marched in their yard chanting " stop wasting food " while holding placards with demands like " do n't let me decompose .
