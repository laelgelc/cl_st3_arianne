A Democratic victory would be as much an affirmation of New Jersey 's blue reflex -- Hillary Clinton thumped part-time resident Donald Trump there by 14 percentage points -- as it would be a final repudiation of its friendless departing Republican governor , Chris Christie . <p> This means that Virginia -- considered two-party competitive , despite mounting Democratic strength -- will be this year 's main event . And the main measure of that will be money ; in particular , out-of-state money . <p> We are seeing early bursts of big spending in the Ed Gillespie-Ralph Northam race by non-Virginia individuals and entities . So far , at least $16.5 million has been directed by only seven organizations to the candidates or is being spent on behalf of Gillespie or Northam , who has single-digit leads in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ mid-September . <p> Virginia , which comfortably tipped to Clinton and where distaste for President Trump has deepened , is expected to be awash in political money because the parties view the gubernatorial election as their best chance to telegraph very different messages to the nation . <p> For Republicans , it 's that the party is advancing -- despite setbacks and stigmas synonymous with Trump . For Democrats , it 's that the party is advancing -- because of setbacks and stigmas synonymous with Trump . <p> When it comes to political spending , New Jersey and Virginia are apples and oranges . New Jersey has public financing of elections . Virginia campaigns are bankrolled with unlimited private dollars . But public financing does n't mean eye-popping sums are n't spent in New Jersey . <p> In 2005 , New Jersey 's gubernatorial candidates -- both wealthy men -- spent a total of $90 million , drawing on their personal fortunes . That was about a third more than the $60 million that the Virginia nominees spent in 2013 . <p> A huge cost in New Jersey campaigns is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ this year -- Democrat Phil Murphy and Republican Kim Guadagno -- must purchase air time in two of the nation 's priciest broadcast markets : New York and Philadelphia , which bracket the state . <p> That 's not as big a problem in Virginia . It has high-dollar Washington and about a half-dozen smaller , lower-priced markets , including Richmond . <p> Murphy , a multimillionaire , ex-Goldman Sachs guy , loaned his campaign nearly $20 million during the primary . In the general election , he is deferring to a state-set cap on spending of $13.8 million . However , Murphy 's early investment in himself continues to yield dividends . <p> A recent poll showed him 21 percentage points ahead of Guadagno , who -- as Christie 's lieutenant governor -- has to answer for her unpopular boss , a vocal supporter of Trump . <p> Because Virginia has no restrictions on fundraising and spending in state elections , campaigns for governor can be the financial equivalent of a nuclear arms race , an ever-accelerating scramble by both sides -- and their allies -- to harvest more @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ social media , more telephone banks and more operatives . <p> That is an open invitation to politically muscular organizations with vast cash troves to throw their weight around , amassing chits with the prospective governor of the nation 's 12th-largest state and testing themes and technology for future campaigns . <p> The National Rifle Association , backing Gillespie and his running mates for lieutenant governor and attorney general , is renowned for the frequency and speed with which it can communicate with its members . It spent $500,000 four years ago , backing Republican Ken Cuccinelli over the winner for governor , Democrat Terry McAuliffe . <p> Two gun-control groups , Mike Bloomberg-led Everytown for Gun Safety and Gabby Giffords ' Americans for Responsible Solutions , combined to spend more than $2 million in behalf of McAuliffe and other Democrats .
