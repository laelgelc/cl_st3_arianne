It turns out that how often someone trusts their intuition and how important they think it is to have evidence are two separate things . Both make a big difference in what we believe . <p> What we learned offers some hope for people 's ability to tell truth from fiction , despite the fact that so many trust their gut . <p> Many incorrect beliefs have political foundations . And certain conspiracy theories -- like the belief that President Kennedy 's assassination was orchestrated by a powerful secret organization -- are remarkably persistent . <p> With all the talk about political bias , it 's easy to lose track of the fact that politics are n't the only thing shaping people 's beliefs . Other factors play a role too . <p> For example , people are more likely to believe something the more often they 've heard it said -- commonly known as the illusory truth effect .
