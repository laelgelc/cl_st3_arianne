<p> JOHANNESBURG - Government interference in the critical agriculture industry poses a serious threat to global food security . The contentious issue of climate change was also identified as among factors threatening food production . <p> Independent agricultural economist Fanie Brink made the remarks in Pretoria on Tuesday , at the congress of the Transvaal Agricultural Union of SA , where he delivered a paper on the " economic rules for the production of food " . <p> He told delegates that climate change " is one of the biggest threats " and said government 's political and economic policies in agriculture were another . Brink bemoaned the policies , saying in many cases they caused " negative consequences for the industry " regarding taxes , increases in electricity tariffs and regulation of minimum wages . <p> " The agricultural industry in the world displays specific characteristics and realities that seriously endanger the sustainability of agricultural production and food security , " he said . <p> He further criticised the ANC-led government which does " not always acknowledge the importance of the commercial agriculture as the actual creator of food @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ no support for the sustainability of food production , even during the very severe drought experienced last year , while it also does not have a long term plan for the future of commercial agriculture " . <p> Seventy percent of the world 's poor live in rural areas where land is a fundamental asset and a primary source of income , security , opportunity and status . <p> Statistics South Africa commended the agriculture industry for helping get the country out of technical recession as the gross domestic product grew 2.5% in the second quarter of the year . Statistician general Pali Lehohla described growth in the sector as " shooting through the roof " by 33.6% , adding that it contributed 0.7 of a percentage point to overall GDP growth . <p> The government 's National Development Plan , a blueprint to address the country 's socio-economic challenges of unemployment , inequality and poverty , has identified the agriculture industry has having the potential to create one million jobs by 2030.
