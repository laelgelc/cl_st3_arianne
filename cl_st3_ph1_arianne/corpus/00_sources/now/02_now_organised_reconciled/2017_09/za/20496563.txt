<h> Climate change threatens Latin America coffee producers <h> Climate change is threatening the Latin American zones most favorable for growing coffee , according to a study out Monday that warns seed production could drop by nearly 90 percent by 2050 . <p> The study suggests high quality coffees are most at risk -- with Arabica coffee unable to withstand even slight fluctuations in temperature , humidity and sunlight . <p> Robusta coffee , mostly grown in Africa to be made into instant coffee , is slightly more resistant . <p> " Coffee is one of the most valuable commodities on Earth , and needs a suitable climate and pollinating bees to produce well , " said study co-author Taylor Ricketts , director of the University of Vermont 's Gund Institute for Environment . <p> " This is the first study to show how both will likely change under global warming -- in ways that will hit coffee producers hard . " <p> During the study , published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America ( PNAS ) , researchers estimated changes @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of pollinating bees . <p> They concluded a temperature increase of over 3.6 degrees Fahrenheit ( two degrees Celsius ) by 2050 will reduce seed production by between 73 and 88 percent in locations best known for coffee production today . <p> A fall in the bee population of eight to 18 percent will also contribute to this decline . <p> The largest production losses are expected in Honduras , Nicaragua and Venezuela . <p> But it 's not all bad news -- as researchers suggested bee population and diversity in other areas is in fact likely to increase . <p> This would contribute to a favorable coffee-growing environment in the likes of Colombia , Costa Rica , Guatemala and Mexico , where temperatures in mountainous regions are expected to be more suitable . <p> The study also highlights the importance of tropical forests for bees and other key pollinators . <p> Around 91 percent of Latin America 's most fertile areas for coffee production are currently less than 1.2 miles ( 1.9 kilometers ) from a rainforest . <p> To comment you need to be signed in to Facebook @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ We reserve the right to remove offensive comments .
