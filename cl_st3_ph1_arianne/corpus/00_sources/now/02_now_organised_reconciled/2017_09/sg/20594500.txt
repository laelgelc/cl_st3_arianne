<p> Hospitals in India are starting to tag newborns , mothers and medics as well as installing extra security cameras and educating staff to spot baby thieves amid fears that baby trafficking is becoming an organised crime nationwide . <p> Officials said this was part of a drive starting at government-run hospitals in southern Tamil Nadu state to ensure nurses , doctors , and visitors know of the threat of babies being stolen from maternity wards and sold illegally for adoption . <p> At the Rajaji government hospital in Madurai , the first in Tamil Nadu to introduce the programme , laser beams at exit points trigger alarms if untagged adults take babies out . <p> " We just want to prevent the theft of babies , " NK Mahalakshmi , the doctor is in charge of laser tagging at the hospital , told the Thomson Reuters Foundation . <p> " It is not fool proof but a deterrent ... Our hospital staff has also been told to be extra vigilant .
