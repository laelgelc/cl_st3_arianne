<h> Sustainability research that aims to change the world <h> Pursuing knowledge can build expertise in a field , or it can change the world . Professor Manfred Lenzen explains how the University of Sydney 's purpose-first approach to sustainability research helps students tackle tough global challenges . <h> Share this story <p> Sydney University professor Manfred Lenzen earned his PhD in nuclear physics from the University of Bonn in 1995 , but these days , his research interests are a long way off from the world of atoms , protons and neutrons . <p> As professor of sustainability research at the University of Sydney 's Integrated Sustainability Analysis ( ISA ) research cluster , Lenzen 's work today involves crunching numbers with the help of high-performance computers in order to examine the link between environmental , social and economic issues . <p> His research touches on issues such the links between inequality , responsiblity for carbon emissions and vulnerability to climate impacts . " <p> Lenzen says this advice had a heavy hand in guiding his own shift towards exploring renewable energy , climate change , and socio-economic inequality issues . He also sees a strong sense of purpose reflected in much of the sustainability research conducted by his colleagues at the university . <p> This mindset empowers students enrolled in the university 's postgraduate Sydney Sustainability Programme -- for which Lenzen is the course co-ordinator -- with the motivation to confront the tough , complex challenges that the future holds , he adds . <p> Taught as an 18-month full-time Master 's degree or shorter Graduate Diploma and Graduate Certificate programmes , the Sydney Sustainability Programme 's core curriculum spans seven disciplines , including Life @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ .
