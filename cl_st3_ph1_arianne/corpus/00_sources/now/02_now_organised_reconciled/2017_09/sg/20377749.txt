Rising temperatures caused by climate change not only threaten to destroy many of the world 's ecosystems on which animal and human life depend , but heat increases in the workplace will also lead to a significant drop in economic productivity . <p> Ongoing studies into the impact of rising temperatures on various economic sectors suggest that the productivity reduction could be up to 25 per cent in some cases . <p> The project is examining the way increases in temperature will affect the health and productivity of workers in agriculture , transport , construction , manufacturing and tourism across Europe . <h> " <p> If you want politicians and others with influence to do something about the problem of rising temperatures , you also need to understand its economic aspects . <p> Lars Nybo , deputy head , Department of Sport and Nutrition , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ talk about climate change , the focus is often on the " green " aspect of what will happen in future , " Nybo says in the online ScienceNordic journal . <p> " But if you want politicians and others with influence to do something about the problem of rising temperatures , you also need to understand its economic aspects . <p> " This is what our research focuses on , and our initial results show that climate change could cost up to 25 per cent of the productivity of some companies . " <p> Economic productivity will drop not only in parts of the world already experiencing extreme heat -- such as southern Europe , the Middle East and the southern states of the US -- but also in countries and companies in northern Europe , which could see big drops in economic output . <p> Not surprisingly , results from Heat-Shield studies show the sectors hardest hit by rising temperatures will be those that involve outdoor manual work , as well as indoor work where there is little or no access to air conditioning . <p> " In @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 40 per cent to 50 per cent of both the workforce and gross domestic product , " Nybo says . <p> " If productivity in these sectors falls by five per cent it will have a big impact .
