<h> Sea turtles are coming back from the brink of extinction following extensive conservation efforts <p> Sea turtle numbers are growing thanks to conservation efforts , a study has found . <p> Researchers found populations of the large turtles to be improving when they looked at nearly 60 regions across the globe . <p> Sea turtles are estimated to live up to 80 years , but habitat loss , hunting , accidentally being caught in fishing nets , plastics pollution , egg harvesting and climate change pushed them to endangered levels . <p> Hawaiian green sea turtles have seen a jump in numbers ( WhitcombeRD/Getty Images ) <p> " There 's a positive sign at the end of the story , " said study lead author Antonios Mazaris , ecology professor at Aristotle University of Thessaloniki in Greece . " We should be more optimistic about our efforts in society . " <p> Mazaris said Hawaiian green sea turtles , in trouble 40 years ago , have seen a jump in numbers . <p> " They have more turtles than they know what to do with , " said Roderic @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ International Union for Conservation of Nature , who was not involved in the study . <p> He added : " It 's a good problem to have . The rest did n't change or there was n't enough data . <p> There were increases in North and South America on the Atlantic coast but setbacks in the Asia-Pacific region . <p> The US and Mexican governments changed laws and fishing practices and set aside dark , quiet areas for turtles to nest as part of the programme . <p> However , six out of seven species of sea turtles remain endangered . <p> In the paper , Mazaris and his team noted that the " positive trends in abundance " should be seen as a tale of cautionary optimism . <p> Leatherback turtles are declining in the eastern and western Pacific ( Owen Humphreys/PA ) <p> They praise conservationists working to save turtles over the past seven decades , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> " Conservation concerns remain , such as the decline in leatherback turtles in the eastern and western Pacific . <p> " Furthermore , we also show that , often , time series are too short to identify trends in abundance . <p> " Our findings highlight the importance of continued conservation and monitoring efforts that underpin this global conservation success story .
