<h> Slicks , scum , odour " virtually disappeared " from Thunder Bay harbour <p> Thunder Bay Harbour has benefited from mill closures and $200 million in expenditures ( Supplied ) <p> THUNDER BAY -- Participants in a program that oversees the long-term cleanup of Thunder Bay harbour have determined that many of the noticeable signs of contamination have been eradicated . <p> As a result , the Thunder Bay Remedial Action Plan ( RAP ) is now recommending that " Degradation of Aesthetics " be removed from the list of environmental issues identified when the harbour was named one of 43 Great Lakes areas of concern in 1987 . <p> Cleanup plans for harbours on both the Canadian and American sides defined degraded aesthetics as including " slicks , scums , odours , foam , unnatural deposits , colour or turbidity . " <p> According to RAP spokesperson Jim Bailey of Lakehead University 's Department of Geography and the Environment , documentation at the time noted that " Degraded water quality has impaired river and harbour front aesthetics , thereby affecting recreational use of associated lands and water resources @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ less attractive to recreational boaters , fishermen and the public . " <p> Bailey said that , more recently , Thunder Bay harbour water quality has improved to the point that slicks , scums and odours have virtually disappeared due to a number of corrective measures including : <p> cleanup of creosote contamination at the former Northern Wood Preservers facility at a cost of $25 million <p> expenditure of over $100 million by Resolute Forest Products and earlier operators of the mill to improve industrial effluent treatment <p> implementation of secondary treatment at the Thunder Bay Water Pollution Control Plant at a cost of $74 million <p> closure of three paper mills <p> improved standards for effluent entering Lake Superior <p> The Thunder Bay RAP is supported by Lakehead University , Environment and Climate Change Canada , the Ontario Ministry of Environment and Climate Change , and the Ontario Ministry of Natural Resources and Forestry . <p> Anyone wishing to comment on the proposal to change the status of " Degradation of Aesthetics " to unimpaired can submit their feedback to Bailey via email : jfbailey@lakeheadu.ca
