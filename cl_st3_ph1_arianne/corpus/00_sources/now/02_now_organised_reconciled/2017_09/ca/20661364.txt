Photos by Riley Brandt , University of Calgary <p> Carter Dzuiba 's research is part of a new partnership between the University of Calgary and the Norwegian University of Science and Technology for advanced research and education on carbon storage and enhanced oil recovery . <p> Carter Dziuba conducted energy research this summer in Norway after completing his graduate degree at the University of Calgary , under a new partnership between the university and the Norwegian University of Science and Technology ( NTNU ) . He worked with Reidun Aadland , a PhD student at NTNU who has twice come to UCalgary , where she collaborated with Dziuba on @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> " My research exchange for three months with NTNU allowed me broader technical and cultural exposure , " Dziuba says . " I received a lot of input from many international colleagues that greatly helped to increase the quality of my research . " <p> The three-year , $850,000 partnership between the two universities , for advanced research and education focused on carbon storage and enhanced oil recovery , is called CARBEOR . <p> " The problems are so big that we need to be doing our research with other centres of expertise in energy around the world , " says Bryant , Canada Excellence Research Chair in Materials Engineering for Unconventional Oil Reservoirs . <p> Real-world field training for students <p> Through CARBEOR , Calgary-based CMC Research Institutes will host a summer field school in 2018 at the Containment Monitoring Institute ( CaMI ) field research station . CaMI , owned and operated by CMC Research Institutes with operational funding from UCalgary , is located in Newell County in southern Alberta . <p> About 15 to 20 graduate students from UCalgary , Norway and the Netherlands will do @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ monitoring carbon dioxide injected underground at the facility . The research station has state-of-the-art geophysical equipment for tracking CO2 in an underground geological formation and testing new monitoring methods and technologies . <p> Safe and permanent geological storage is a key technology to keep the greenhouse gas of the atmosphere , where it contributes to global warming and climate change . <p> " The CARBEOR project is to enhance training opportunities and give graduate students an experience in a different environment , " says Don Lawton , project co-manager and professor of geophysics in the Department of Geoscience in the Faculty of Science . <p> The project " has given rise to a lot of interaction with the Norwegians , because their carbon capture and storage activity is really advancing , " says Lawton , who 's also director of the CaMI research station . <p> More environmentally friendly oil extraction <p> Dziuba , for his master 's degree in chemical and petroleum engineering , investigated the potential of nano-sized ( about 10,000 times smaller than a strand of human hair ) nanocellulose -- a forest industry product -- as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> " The major benefit of nanocellulose is that it could be an environmentally friendly alternative to some of the current chemicals used in petroleum extraction , " he says . " It 's essentially little bits of wood that poses minimal environmental impact . " <p> His experiment at UCalgary involved building a sand pack flooding apparatus containing porous silica sand , like that found in a sandstone petroleum reservoir , into which a solution of water and nanocellulose was pumped . Dziuba measured the drop in pressure across the sand pack and the concentration of nanocellulose coming out of the apparatus . That told him how well the nanocellulose was flowing through the sand pack and whether it was getting stuck against the sand . <p> Nanocellulose solutions that flow long distances though an actual petroleum reservoir could reach more oil and increase recovery rates . On the other hand , nanocellulose solutions that get stuck in a reservoir could also be useful in well-drilling operations using drilling mud , for creating a ' plug , ' called a filter cake , to prevent drilling fluid losses @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ research at UCalgary , Dziuba says nanocellulose looks promising as a drilling mud additive . " From the technical side , it 's too early to say if it 's showing promise for enhanced oil recovery . " <p> In Norway , Aadland is still continuing her experiments on nanocellulose 's potential for EOR , working with larger core-flooding apparatus that uses actual reservoir rock cores saturated with oil from the North Sea . <p> Dziuba completed his degree in May and started work this month with Cenovus Energy on a four-year graduate engineering program .
