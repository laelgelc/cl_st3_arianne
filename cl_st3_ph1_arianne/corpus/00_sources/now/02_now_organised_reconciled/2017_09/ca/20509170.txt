<p> September brings the Days of Awe , the period between the Rosh Hashanah and the Day of Atonement . For Jews , it is a time of repentance , prayer and charity . <p> This year the Days of Awe are preceded by a new , secular interlude of anxiety . You do n't have to be Jewish to see it . You do n't have to read the Bible . <p> Just tune in to CNN . Listen to the warnings from officials around Hurricane Irma . To them this was the End of Days , an unprecedented , apocalyptic disaster . <p> Watching the wall-to-wall television coverage , I found most particularly striking the talk of Doomsday from the authorities , principally Rick Scott , the governor of Florida . <p> In the daily tableau for the cameras , there was Scott , sporting a baseball cap emblazoned with " NAVY , " flanked by sombre uniformed officials , issuing the latest threat from the storm that would " devastate the United States . " <p> Worried that Floridians would refuse to flee , Scott became the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . " Not tonight . Not in an hour . You need to go now . " <p> With every passing hour , he was one of the Horsemen of the Apocalypse : " It ( the water ) flows in very fast . It is going to be faster ... than you are . " <p> The governor 's language was strong because of traditional skepticism of these warnings among weather-weary residents who insist they can survive . During Hurricane Sandy , in the northeast , this ilk thought they could hole up in their homes . Many died . <p> Sounding the alarm was responsible ; the governor tried to save Floridians from themselves . But as the waters recede , it 's now time to ask : Who can save them from the governor ? <p> After all , it is antediluvians such as Scott who brazenly deny climate change . " Global warming " is so anathema to him that he will not utter its name . State employees say he has asked them to do the same . <p> This is n't to say that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Harvey . Or that it brought this summer 's scourge of wildfires , earthquakes , tornadoes and floods . <p> But if Scott was Cassandra-in-Chief before the storm , where is he after the storm ? Why can not he acknowledge the warming air and water , the melting glaciers , the rising oceans and the increasingly frequent " once-in-100-years " floods and storms ? Global warming may not create them , but it intensifies their power and magnifies their size . <p> Scientists overwhelmingly agree . But Rick Scott -- and Scott Pruitt , head of the Environmental Protection Agency in Washington -- are in denial . So is Donald Trump , who has withdrawn the United States from the Paris Agreement . <p> During Hurricane Irma , Pruitt said that evoking climate change was " insensitive , " as if it were raising incest at a family wedding . This would " politicize " the weather , cried conservatives . <p> Pruitt , meanwhile , is gleefully dismantling the EPA . Christine Todd Whitman , a former head of the agency ( and a Republican ) , calls @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ scientists " to challenge the consensus of thousands of scientists on climate change " a slow-rolling catastrophe in the making . " <p> She writes in the New York Times that the relationship between burning fossil fuels ( which emit carbon dioxide ) and the warming atmosphere is " as certain as the link between smoking and cancer . " <p> But not to Rick Scott , whose densely populated peninsular state is low , flat and washed by the ocean , making it the most vulnerable to climate change in America . <p> Think about it : Rather than warning about the threat of Hurricane Irma , what if he were to stop development along the state 's beaches ? What if he were to make Irma a teachable moment ? <p> Of course not . Scott , Pruitt and other deniers are prisoners to ideology , in thrall to fossil fuel producers who have financed their political careers .
