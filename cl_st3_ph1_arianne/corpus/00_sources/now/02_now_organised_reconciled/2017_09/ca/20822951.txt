<h> U of A-discovered microbe could be key to combating climate change <p> Researchers from the University of Alberta and the University of Vienna , have identified a new microbe to join the fight against nitrogen pollution . <p> The newly characterized bacteria , Nitrospiral inopinata , have the unique ability to recycle ammonia into nitrate without the usual production of toxic greenhouse gases . Lisa Stein , a co-author of the study and professor of microbiology at the U of A , thinks this discovery is the first step of many in correcting our planet 's nitrogen problem . <p> " Humans add more active nitrogen to the biosphere than all natural sources combined , " she explained . " Because of that , the nitrogen cycle has been identified as the most unbalanced biogeochemical cycle on the planet . " <p> The demand for agriculture has led to the overuse of fertilizers , leading to a buildup of ammonia in soils , rivers and oceans . " It 's already in an overload and in a runaway . " <p> However , the newly characterized ammonia-eating microbe could help rebalance the planet 's nitrogen cycle . Stein 's former graduate student and lead author of the study , Dimitri Kits , demonstrated how the bacteria could convert ammonia directly to nitrate , in a way that could bypass nitrous oxide production .
