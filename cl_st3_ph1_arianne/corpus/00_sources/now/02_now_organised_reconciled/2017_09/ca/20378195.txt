<h> READER 'S CORNER : Harbour water safe only on the surface <p> A group of tourists kayak near George 's Island in Halifax Harbour ( The Chronicle Herald ) <p> Not surprisingly , there is renewed interest in Halifax Harbour for recreational fishing and swimming , as recent articles in The Chronicle Herald attest . Sewage and other household effluents produced by HRM residents undergo advanced primary treatment before entering the harbour . Storm water overflows after severe rain events are not always treated . But overall , harbour water quality appears to have improved -- waters are clearer and visible surface sludge is rare . <p> But a word of caution is apt . Despite earlier studies , there is no ongoing monitoring of harbour water , sediment and fish tissue(s) to ensure the safety of citizens . The data publicly available for comprehensive health risk assessments of fishing and swimming are severely limited or out of date . <p> Federal departments ( Fisheries and Oceans , Canadian Food Inspection Agency , Health Canada , Environment and Climate Change Canada ) should be conducting such monitoring and assessments @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . As well , the province and HRM do cursory monitoring , if at all . <p> Occasional single-point monitoring of fecal coliform bacteria is inadequate for meaningful health-risk assessments . Water masses are highly variable in space and time , and contaminated bottom sediments sometimes mix with sub-surface waters that contact fish and swimmers . In the absence of ongoing reliable data on levels of chemical contaminants in local fish tissue ( e.g. , mercury , lead , plasticizers , oil constituents ) and levels of water-borne pathogens ( e.g. , bacteria , viruses , parasites ) , safe conditions may be illusory.
