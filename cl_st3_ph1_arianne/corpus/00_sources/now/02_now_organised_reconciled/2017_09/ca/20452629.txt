<p> With Florida bracing for a monster Hurricane Irma after Harvey devastated Texas , money for the U.S. Federal Emergency Management Agency could be depleted before month 's end or sooner , despite a government top-up of $7.4 billion that was approved on Friday . <p> Based on the " burn rate " of relief funds , cash that 's used for rebuilding roads and bridges and for distributing bottled water , medical supplies , food and baby formula should be gone before Sept. 30 , when government funding expires and a new fiscal year begins . <p> Compounding the problem is the fact Irma is forecast to batter highly developed parts of Florida , while Hurricane Jose gathers strength over the Atlantic Ocean . <p> " FEMA can easily go through $200 million a day , just co-ordinating and doing relief , " said Elizabeth Zimmerman , a former associate administrator at the agency during the Obama administration . <p> " They were burning through that amount just doing that with Harvey .
