<p> MONCTON -- Sea stars , rocky cliffs covered with sea anemone and many other rare sights came into view during a recent video and sampling exploration in the Gulf of St. Lawrence . <p> Mireille Chiasson , Oceans biologist and manager of the Oceans Division with the Department of Fisheries and Oceans in Moncton was onboard for the August 23 to 30 expedition . <p> " We did water , sediment sampling and we also collected sponge samples for DNA analysis and also for identification , " Chiasson reported . She suggested there is a possibility some of the sponges they collected might never have been identified previously . <p> DFO partnered with Oceana Canada to bring a submersible robot , ROPOS ( Remotely-operated Platform for Ocean Science ) , into the Gulf for the first time . <p> " There are a multitude of tools we can use and add on to satisfy the requirement of the testing we want , " Chiasson described the ROPOS . <p> Planning for the expedition using the ROPOS , which is normally based in BC , started a year and a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ various depths , down to 450 meters . <p> Water samples were collected at the ocean floor , mid-water and at 25 meters down and will be analyzed by a DFO scientist in Moncton who is studying the impact of climate change in the Gulf of St. Lawrence . <p> " Data on water quality will help us determine , and see a bit further , how the Gulf of St. Lawrence is changing , " she commented . <p> " There were sights , it looked like , basically , the Caribbean , " Chiasson said of the images the ROPOS captured in the Cape Breton trough .
