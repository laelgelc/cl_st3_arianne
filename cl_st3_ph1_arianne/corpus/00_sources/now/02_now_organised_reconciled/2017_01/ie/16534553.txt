<p> As Donald Trump assumed the US presidency , 35 international business leaders , led by Unilever CEO Paul Polman and me ( I 'm chair of the Business and Sustainable Development Commission ) , are defending open markets , endorsing the fight against climate change , and demanding a massive push against global inequality . <p> These are the core elements of what we believe is the only viable economic strategy for the US and the world . <p> Recent electoral outcomes , including Mr Trump 's US win , highlight the intensifying economic grievances of many households across the developed world . In the 20 years before the 2008 financial crisis , unprecedented globalisation raised incomes for just about everyone . <p> The incomes of the poorest third of humanity rose by 40%-70% , and those of the middle third increased by 80% . The top 1% did even better -- so much better that the business elite is now facing a powerful backlash . <p> And , yet , the incomes of a crucial group -- lower middle-income households -- barely rose .
