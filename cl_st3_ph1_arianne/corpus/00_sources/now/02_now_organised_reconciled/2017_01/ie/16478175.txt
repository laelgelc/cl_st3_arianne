<h> Primates disappearing at alarming rate , 60pc of species under threat <p> The plethora of research into disastrous species population decline due to human behaviour is reaching a crescendo , with 60pc of primate species now threatened with extinction . <p> It 's not a good time to be a primate . Three-quarters of species are suffering from a decline in numbers , and with 60pc now threatened with extinction , the future is n't painting too colourful a picture . <p> That 's according to a new piece of research compiled from 31 authors , published in Nature Communications , which draws on information from previous reports , years of research and the International Union for Conservation of Nature and Natural Resources ( IUCN ) red list . <p> Image : Wang LiQiang/Shutterstock <p> The problem is due to certain pressures that are turning many of the 504 species known to humans into a vanishing act . <p> These pressures include global and local market demands , leading to extensive habitat loss through the expansion of industrial agriculture , large-scale cattle ranching , logging , oil and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of new road networks in primate regions . <p> According to the study , bushmeat hunting , the illegal trade of primates as pets , a more grisly illegal trade of primate body parts and the old favourite , climate change , are also pushing many species to the brink . <p> Jo Setchell , co-author of the study , said after completing the research that primates are in " dire trouble " . <p> The decline is mammoth , the extinction threat real . ' This dismal situation is our fault , " she said . <p> " Without action , these numbers will grow and more species will disappear forever . " <p> Image : Nikolay Se/Shutterstock <p> Primates live in around 90 countries , but the vast majority of species live in Brazil , Indonesia , the Democratic Republic of Congo and Madagascar . In the latter , 87pc of its species face extinction . <p> In the two decades after 1990 , 1.5m sq km of primate habitats have been converted into agricultural lands . <p> " The threat to primates is a result of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ policies that favour short-term profit over long-term sustainability . <p> " It is hard to be positive when faced with human-driven extinction of our closest relatives . " <p> The aim of the study was twofold : establish the plight of primates and drum up awareness of the gravity of the situation -- though the latter might not be as effective as people hope . <p> For example , just before Christmas , the IUCN 's most recent red list of species under threat went big , highlighting the plummeting giraffe populations in the wild . <p> Wild tiger populations have plummeted by 97pc in the past 100 or so years , with India 's mangroves , Russia 's snow-covered plains and south-east Asia 's dense rainforests down to their final 3,200 . <p> For this species to be put under such surprising duress is surely the type of call to arms that this scientific field sorely needs -- but it does not appear to be happening . <p> " Biologically , extinction is a normal phenomenon , " said Setchell .
