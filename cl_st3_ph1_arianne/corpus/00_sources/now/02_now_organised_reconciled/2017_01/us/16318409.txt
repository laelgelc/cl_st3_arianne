The 2011 storm was the most recent event to bring area rivers to 100-year-flood levels . <p> Work proceeds on the rehabilitation of the dam and flood gates on the Millers River in Orange Center . August 12 , 2016 photo . <p> By RICHIE DAVIS Recorder Staff <p> Monday , January 09 , 2017 <p> Print <p> As it launches a new effort to help area towns work together to boost resiliency against increasingly severe storm events , the Franklin Regional Council of Governments has launched a survey about the impact of flooding on critical infrastructure and natural resources and habitat . <p> The survey , which has been out for about a week in Survey Monkey format , has already drawn about 40 responses from people who live or work in 14 towns in the Deerfield River Watershed , according to Kimberly Noke MacPhee @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Responses will continue to be collected for at least another month to help Ashfield , Bernardston , Buckland , Charlemont , Colrain , Conway , Deerfield , Greenfield , Hawley , Heath , Leyden , Monroe , Rowe and Shelburne to help with climate resiliency planning . <p> The survey focuses on critical infrastructure like roads , bridges and culverts , natural resources and habitat . It asks about strategies that are important to protect structures from flooding , whether any key town facilities are in areas prone to erosion or flooding and which strategies are key to protecting flood mitigation benefits provided by farmland , forested land and other natural resources . <p> The project , funded by a state Community Compact grant , will help take stock of how climate change could affect the communities ' natural resources and habitat , local economy and government . It also will consider effects on infrastructure like wastewater and stormwater drains , dams , railroads , water supplies as well as sanitation , agriculture and food systems .
