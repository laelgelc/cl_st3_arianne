<h> New study challenges understanding of salinity impacts on freshwater biodiversity <h> January 18 , 2017 by Marea Martlew <p> Mayfly . Credit : Renee Dowse <p> New research exploring the impact of salinity on insects in freshwater environments has led to a discovery that seems to contradict long-held theories about mechanisms for salt regulation in invertebrates . <p> The results of the study , published today in Royal Society Open Science , provide insight into why surprisingly low salinity levels are causing high mortality rates in organisms that are a vital component of freshwater food webs . <p> " Agriculture , mining and climate change are driving rising salinity levels in the world 's freshwater ecosystems , threatening global biodiversity , " said lead author Dr Renee Dowse , a freshwater ecotoxicologist from the School of Life Sciences at the University of Technology Sydney ( UTS ) . <p> " Our study organism , the mayfly nymph Austrophlebioides pusillu , belongs to a group of macroinvertebrates that predominantly live in rivers and streams . They are an important food source for other riverine animals and through their feeding @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ streams clean , " she said . <p> Dr Dowse said recent international field studies have shown the loss of mayfly biodiversity and abundance at " surprisingly low salinities " . <p> " Declining numbers are concerning , especially because conventional wisdom about salt-regulation mechanisms in these types of organisms ca n't explain it , " she said . <p> Dr Dowse said these insects are considered osmoregulators , in that they actively regulate their internal media to remain at a relatively constant osmotic pressure that is different to their external environment .
