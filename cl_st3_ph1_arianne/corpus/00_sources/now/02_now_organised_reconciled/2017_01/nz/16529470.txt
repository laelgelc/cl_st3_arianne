<h> Driving may become favoured recreation for dry river beds <p> I have been reading with interest the concern expressed by people about the low flows at Coes Ford ( Lower Selwyn River ) over Christmas . <p> Could I suggest these people need to be brought into the future . The new recreational past-time is not fishing , nor swimming , but driving your 4WD vehicle up dry riverbeds . They should view the warning signs erected advising them not to swim due to toxic algae is for their own protection so they will not be run over . <p> They need to take off those rose-tinted glasses that show a catchment , classified by Environment Canterbury ( ECan ) as a " red zone " , with an overallocation of water ( Dec 2014 ) of 119 per cent and put on some " green " ones that will help them see this catchment as others do -- a pristine environment with springs flowing all year , healthy streams , creeks and drains fenced for stock exclusion , providing habitants for native fish , trout , and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ impact of no aquifer recharge for the past three years in the foothills of the Upper Selwyn on this resilient catchment . <p> ALDEN WILLIAMS/FAIRFAX NZ <p> German tourists Charlotte Mahrenholtz , left , and Imke Bruchhaeuser heed warnings not to swim in the Selwyn River near the Coes Ford Recreation Reserve . <p> So , how do I know there is still lots of water ?
