<h> Government help needed on rising sea levels <p> From threats of court action , to uncertainty of how high sea levels will rise and how soon . <p> Councils around the country face a myriad of problems when looking at dealing with climate change related sea level rise . <p> While the government has allocated itself the role of mitigating the country 's green house emissions , local councils were given the role of adapting to climate change consequences including rising sea levels . <p> Delwyn Dickey <p> Orewa beach is the first in the Auckland area to have serious and immediate inundation issues . <p> This has made us more vulnerable to the effects of climate change the International Panel on Climate Change has noted in its reports . <p> There is still uncertainty on a timeline for sea level rises , including how much impact melting of ice sheets in Antarctica and Greenland will have on sea levels . <p> Since the fifth International Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) report in 2014 , with upper projected sea level rise of 0.98 metres by 2100 , there has been ongoing research on the massive land based ice sheets in both Antarctica and Greenland which has shown them to be more unstable than originally thought . <p> They have the potential to slide into the sea , raising sea levels significantly , with rises likely to continue for centuries to come . <p> Though there is still uncertainty , more reports coming in suggest actual levels are likely to be about 1.25 metres higher by 2100 , professor Martin Manning from the Climate Research Institute at Victoria University says . Manning has also been involved with New Zealand 's reports for the IPCC . <p> From a risk management perspective , councils should be looking at 2 metres by 2100 , he says . <p> Councils have a statutory duty to use information about natural hazards like sea level rise , and may be found liable for damages or loss if it does n't use the best information available . But those that do risk community backlash and challenges through the courts . <p> This has seen councils opt for regulations that take the lower end of possible sea level rises into account as they are easier to defend in court . <p> Supplied <p> 2m sea level rise : Pt Wells and Omaha Beach <p> One of the key tools councils use to accurately gauge which areas will be affected by inundation and storm surges is Lidar radar mapping - which is expensive . <p> While Auckland Council has been able to map all of the city 's 3100 kilometres of coastline using Lidar mapping , costs have seen many other councils , like Northland Regional Council stick to only Lidar mapping residential coastal villages and known flood zones . Less exact , cheaper or freely available mapping for other areas . <p> This has seen councils come up with a piecemeal of standards around the country dealing with building regulations and zoning and leaves much of the country under-protected from the extremes of sea level rises and storm inundation , including agricultural and horticultural areas . <p> Yule is n't prepared to go into details on what is in position paper on climate change going to government but it seems likely government intervention and legislation will be called for . <p> Climate Change Minister Paula Bennett says it was a matter for councils and insurers to decide what areas could be built on .
