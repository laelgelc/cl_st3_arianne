<h> Andy Maciver : Put road and rail on equal footing <p> If the Government took a wider view of rail funding , electrifiying the North Island Main Trunk line would be a " no-brainer " , says Andy Maciver . <h> Relevant offers <p> OPINION : KiwiRail 's decision to retire its small fleet of electric locomotives used on freight trains between Hamilton and Palmerston North raises questions about the rail company and its place in New Zealand . <p> Although the electrified section is a relatively small section of the KiwiRail system , the company 's decision to move to an all-diesel operation flies in the face of moves elsewhere , such as Transport Minister Simon Bridges ' promotion of plug-in electric cars , to meet our obligations under the Paris Agreement on climate change . <p> It was ironic that shortly after KiwiRail chief executive Peter Reidy made the announcement , Dutch Railways said all their electric trains were powered entirely by wind generation . <p> The Government 's hands-off attitude contrasts with its direction of motorway building . It also shows a lack of long-term @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ future change of policy by keeping the power to the system 's wires switched on . " This is not a forever decision , " Reidy said . <p> The electrified section of railway between the two cities is a legacy of National 's Think Big programme of the 1980s . It was conceived as a means of using locally sourced energy as part of insulating the country from the vagaries of international oil supplies . <p> As well as the high voltage wiring and the supply of 22 locomotives -- 15 are still operational -- to operate the system , numerous deviations and bypasses were built to straighten some of the original route built in the late 19th and early 20th centuries . <p> In the early 1980s , the then New Zealand Railways was operating in a very different environment from 2017 . Limits on road freight were still in place and the railways were still expected to provide a significant amount of short-haul capacity . Trains made frequent stops to pick up and set down freight at towns where industries and stations are now long-gone . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ serviced by trucking . <p> Arguably , the missing links to Auckland and Wellington should have been electrified at the time , with locomotives equipped to operate on the lower voltage direct current system used in the Wellington region . With short-term thinking prevailing , the move to drop electric haulage for one sector and the resulting deletion of locomotive changes at Hamilton and Palmerston North , with its resulting staff cuts , is understandable . <p> In the intervening years , KiwiRail , and before that as Toll Rail , Tranz Rail and NZR , the company has faced the challenges of unrestrained -- some would say heavily assisted -- road haulage , rural decline and asset-stripping . <p> The direction of much of the company 's spending of government financial support is questionable . <p> Just as much of the trucking industry leases vehicles , rather than buying retrograde wagons and locomotives from China , KiwiRail could have leased more satisfactory vehicles that would have improved its operating economics , as Tranz Rail did and many other rail operators around the world still do . <p> Instead @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ electrifying the busy line between South Auckland and Tauranga , in the process bridging the gap between Hamilton and Auckland . <p> Such judicious Government investment makes sense because it reduces the peak demand on the highway network , which can result in lower overall costs for a given transport capacity . <p> Ideally we would have an integrated land transport strategy and investment system that weighed the relative merits of projects to enhance rail against the increased highway building and maintenance that will otherwise be necessary . This thinking is already applied in the case of the metro rail systems in Auckland and Wellington -- the Land Transport Fund is used to support these rail operations simply because to provide a satisfactory all-road commuter system would be unaffordable . <p> So why do we not apply it to freight transport ? NZTA builds and maintains highways while in the other corner KiwiRail relies on drip-feed funding to sustain a minimal rail network . <p> It is time for the NLTF to be used to maintain and develop the rail as well as the highway network according to rational @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ rail-user charges into it exactly as road-users do . Then we could have rapid progress on such no-brain rail developments as completing the electrification of the Main Trunk and Bay of Plenty lines and buying a fleet of heavier , modern dual-voltage electric locomotives , along with the many other rail productivity and service improvements that are currently passing us by . <p> With more freight shippers choosing the more attractive rail services , our highways would become safer and more satisfactory for all road users , without any overall spending increase . <p> And the reduction in carbon emissions ( further reduced by electric rail operation ) , combined with lower highway accident rates and less land required for roading purposes , are just some of the other pay-offs to the nation . <p> Andy Maciver is a former senior Dominion Post sub-editor and occasional commentator on the rail industry .
