Photo : RNZ / Tracy Neal <p> Erosion within the Waimea Inlet in Nelson has recently revealed human bones . <p> Local iwi said a human jawbone found recently in the Nelson inlet , followed soon after by the discovery of a legbone in the tidal zone below a sandbank , most likely belonged to a member of one of the many tribes who once inhabited the area . <p> Police were confident the bones were not linked to any open investigation or missing person case . <p> Department of Conservation historic adviser Steve Bagley said such discoveries could have a major impact on historic records , and they were likely to increase with the effects of climate change on sea level rise and erosion . <p> " Coastal erosion over the years has frequently turned up koiwi and bones in sites like this . It 's also important we know exactly where it is . " <p> The jawbone found in the inlet on New Year 's Day was fully exposed in the sand . The person who found it phoned the police non-emergency number , who asked her to bring it into the Nelson station . <p> A police spokesperson told RNZ they would generally advise people who found an object or remnant like this to leave it where it was and report it to them . <p> It was not the first human bone to be found in the area , police said .
