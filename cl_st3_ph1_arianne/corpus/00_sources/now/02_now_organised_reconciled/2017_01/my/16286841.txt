<h> DRIVEN : BMW 330e -- it 's the future , but with a catch <p> For years , carmakers have seemed to be stuck in the same cycle of producing slightly better iterations of cars that came before . Lighter , bigger , faster and more efficient , but the recipe stays the same -- an internal combustion engine burns fuel to propel a crankshaft , spinning a transmission that sends power to the road . Niche hybrids aside , the car as a piece of technology has stayed stagnant for over a century . <p> We 've long looked at electric vehicles as the true car of the future , one that you plug into your home just like a smartphone and produces zero emissions . Just like your smartphone , however , an EV 's battery life is unpredictable , and when you do deplete it , it takes ages to charge it back up again . Fine if you 're just browsing Facebook , not so fine if you 're on your way to Penang and the low battery warning just came on . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ perfect solution , at least in the short term . With a more powerful electric motor and a bigger battery compared to a conventional hybrid , it provides all of the benefits of an electric car , but the presence of a regular engine means that it does away with the usual EV issues of range anxiety , long charge time and steep pricing . <p> Premium carmakers have been the biggest investors in these vehicles , and Malaysians have been a strange beneficiary thanks to Energy Efficient Vehicle ( EEV ) rebates , with cars like the Volvo XC90 T8 Twin Engine and BMW X5 xDrive40e . But the new BMW 330e will be the real litmus test for this technology , with its price putting it firmly within the grasp of the entry-level premium car buyer . So , could this car really be a good fit for the typical 3 Series buyer ? We take it for a spin on local roads and conditions to find out . <p> Malaysians have been able to buy the F30 BMW 330e iPerformance since late August , just five @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a surprisingly short lead time . What 's more , like other plug-in hybrid sold in Malaysia , the car is locally-assembled ( CKD ) to take advantage of the aforementioned incentives . <p> As such , it comes into the market priced at just RM248,800 on-the-road without insurance , slotting in just above the less powerful , less well-equipped 320i Sport . This puts it pretty much smack in the middle of the local 3 Series range in terms of price , although with the recent discontinuation of the RM297,800 330i M Sport , it now sits at the top of that range . <p> The 330e is also significantly less expensive than its closest rival , the Mercedes-Benz C 350 e -- Stuttgart 's own take on the plug-in hybrid sports sedan recipe is priced between RM288,888 and RM298,888 . However , the BMW does n't come with quite the level of kit offered on the Mercedes . <p> Locally , the car is specced in Sport trim , losing out on the 330i 's M Sport package . Whether that 's a good thing or not @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Malaysians would probably view it as the latter . I , however , do like the more subtle look that this car has -- with the sharp front end and well-balanced proportions , there 's a reserved athleticism about it , but it 's never too showy . <p> Attractive full-LED head- and tail lights are standard across the range , but the 18-inch Style 658 V-spoke alloy wheels mark the 330e out as the top-spec model . What it does n't get is the iPerformance styling package that adds blue highlights to the grille and wheel badges -- for the more conservative Malaysian market , that 's probably a good thing . Also missing is the BMW i front fender badging found on other markets , although the eDrive badges on the C-pillars remain . The charging port is on the left front fender . <p> Inside , it 's standard F30 3 Series fare , with a no-frills , driver-oriented design that hides some questionable materials and trim choices , especially compared to the outstanding interiors of the C-Class and the recently-launched Audi A4 . Still , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ has a clarity and simple logic to its switchgear layout and instrumentation that the others still have yet to master . <p> As is the case with the regular F30 , the 330e exhibits impressive packaging , with plenty of head- and legroom to spare despite being smaller than the C-Class and A4 . What has been compromised by the new-age powertrain is boot space , which has dropped from 480 litres to 370 litres . <p> Mind you , that 's still more than the C 350 e 's boot , which can house just 335 litres . Here , it 's in the same tune as the 320i , and produces 184 hp from 5,000 to 6,500 rpm , as well as 290 Nm of torque between 1,350 and 4,250 rpm . <p> Sandwiched between it and the eight-speed ZF automatic transmission is an 87 hp/250 Nm electric motor , boosting total system output @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ battery juices the motor . The top speed is also a slightly more modest 225 km/h rather than the 330i 's electronically-limited 250 km/h . <p> At the other end of the spectrum , the 330e is capable of achieving a claimed combined fuel consumption figure of just 2.1 litres per 100 km , produces carbon dioxide emissions of just 49 grams per kilometre and has an all-electric range of 35 km , with a maximum speed of 120 km/h in this mode . <p> Drivers can choose between three dedicated eDrive powertrain settings in addition to BMW 's usual Eco Pro , Comfort , Sport and Sport Plus driving modes . Even under moderate acceleration , the car will go all the way up to 80 km/h on electric power alone -- above which the petrol engine , being more efficient at these speeds , takes over propulsion duties . <p> It takes a determined stab on the throttle to force the four-pot into play , but it 's not the cleanest of transitions . In the right conditions , the 330e will fling itself towards the horizon at a considerable clip , and it does n't feel too far off the more powerful 330i in terms of outright pace . <p> This impressive performance is readily felt even in Max eDrive -- assuming , of course , you keep the battery charged .
