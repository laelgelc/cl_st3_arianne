<h> Daniel Andrews ' Alcoa secrecy an insult <p> After 30 years of secrecy , it 's time governments came clean on the rationale for and details of taxpayer support to the US multinational , Alcoa . <p> A new $240 million assistance package - this is an estimate because state figures have not been officially released - has helped Alcoa strike a power deal with AGL , saving hundreds of jobs , temporarily , at Alcoa 's smelter in Portland . <p> First , why is an avowed free marketeer and climate change believer such as the PM so avidly supporting a taxpayer bailout of a single employer , and a massive guzzler of brown coal-fired electricity at that , especially when this means higher energy prices for others ? <p> Former Treasurer Joe Hockey effectively turned the public tap off to car manufacturers in Australia . Before him conservative and Labor governments going back to Whitlam reduced protection . Entire industries and countless jobs have been wiped out in the name of the marketplace . <p> Advertisement <p> So what 's so special about Alcoa ? <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Victoria 's smelter had already enjoyed a massive public chop out over 30 years . <p> Portland has been controversial since the Hamer government in the 1970s opted to build it 500 kilometres from the state 's electricity hub in the Latrobe Valley . It became even more so when the Cain government struck a power contract that turned into a massive annual subsidy . <p> The subsidies continued until the then current contract ended in 2016 . <p> Alcoa has milked the power outage from late last year that reduced its plant 's capacity to about a third . <p> Not for the first time , the global metals giant refused to commit to a future in Victoria . Governments panicked . <p> Yet the outage was due to a failure in a privately-owned electricity distribution network . Strictly speaking , the smelter meltdown should be a matter between two private parties - Alcoa and AusNet , the owner of the electricity distribution network . <p> It beggars belief , given aluminium 's reliance on electricity , that Alcoa would not have been insured against such an eventuality @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ nor Alcoa will answer the insurance question . Under the circumstances I could n't disagree more . <p> As long as Alcoa hangs on - ever so ambivalently - in Portland , governments will fail to plan for a future for the region beyond it . And for as long as it remains , Alcoa will demand subsidy . <p> At least Turnbull 's $30 million in support comes with strings . It is conditional on operations at the smelter continuing until at least June 30 , 2021 and maintaining production to at least 90 per cent of pre-outage levels . <p> As for the state assistance , which Treasurer Tim Pallas said on ABC Melbourne and 3AW would be up to be $200 million over four years , there is no real detail . Is it the last hand-out ? What are the conditions ? What is required of Alcoa ? Are there any obligations on it for even a medium-term commitment to Victoria ? To renewable energy ? Anything ? <p> Remarkably , we the taxpayers @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the hand-out . It 's all confidential . On Friday morning Daniel Andrews said " this deal is worth every cent " . What deal ? <p> In a different era when politicians had authority and citizens had some trust , secrecy around a major new enterprise may have made some sense , even if the original power contract with Alcoa is believed to be somewhere around $4 billion . <p> But at a time when such deals are at odds with politicians ' philosophies , policies and promises , and when public trust is all but gone , such confidentiality is an insult to those picking up the bill .
