<h> Scientist claims Pakistan reduced ozone-depleting pollutants by 35% <p> LAHORE : Pakistan has made more progress than it had committed to protect the ozone layer , a seminar organised by the Punjab University revealed on Friday . <p> The seminar was organised by the PU 's College of Earth and Environmental Sciences ( CEES ) in collaboration with the Ministry of Climate Change . The one-day seminar titled ' Hydrochlorofluoro carbons ( HCFCs ) Phase Out in Pakistan ' was held at its seminar hall . <p> In the opening address , CEES Principal Dr Sajid and Dr Faiza Mazhar gave a presentation on the topic " HCFCs phase out in Pakistan " . <p> He said according to the commitment , Pakistan had to reduce HCFCs by 10% . <p> " The findings showed that Pakistan has reduced the these by 35% , which is a huge reduction , " he added . <p> " We used remote sensing with a Geographic Information System ( GIS ) and gathered data from Karachi and Lahore . This is the first of its kind study carried out in Pakistan @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ journal , " he said , while elaborating on the methods adopted by researchers . <p> While sharing his research findings , Dr Sajid said Pakistan was a signatory of the Montreal Protocol to reduce ozone depletion and the research focused on the results of the efforts made by the country . <p> Ministry of Climate Change Joint Secretary Iftikharul Hassan Shah Gilani briefed the participants on the role played by the ministry in this scenario . He also pledged to help CEES in two projects dealing with mercury reduction and persistent organic pollutants ( POPs ) reduction . He said the ministry would jointly research and provide funding to CEES . <p> Agreed on August 26 , 1987 , the Montreal Protocol on substances that deplete the ozone layer ' is a protocol of the Vienna Convention . It is an international treaty designed to protect the ozone layer by phasing out the production of numerous substances that are responsible for ozone depletion .
