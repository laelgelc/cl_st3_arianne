<h> Bucket baths , fewer flushes and cooking tips to beat the water crisis <p> Thousands of people are facing a water crisis so severe that households are going for days without running water in their taps . <p> This comes after drastic climate change , characterised by rainfall deficit and unusually high temperatures , resulting in prolonged drought . <p> NATIONAL SHORTAGE <p> Currently , 23 counties and counting are facing a water shortage crisis , as the country faces the 12th drought since 1975 . <p> South B resident Valerie Olungah says they are receiving water once a week , which is not enough . They have to supplement with water storage tanks until the next supply from City Council . <p> " I am a mother of three teenagers and I run a household of six people , " she says . " We require a lot of water just to run the house . " <p> But with the showers and constant flushing , coupled with poor water consumption habits , Olungah has realised that the water runs out in just two days . <p> " We have even had instances where the water ran out in just a day , especially on weekends , because we spend most of our time at home , " she says . <p> ECONOMY IN THE BATHROOM <p> To escape the exorbitant water prices from water vendors who , according to the Water ministry , are charging up to 17 times the normal rates , Olungah has introduced a few drastic measures . <p> " No one is allowed to take a shower or even flush the toilet . We are now using buckets to take a bath . We also have another bucket full of water that we now keep in the toilet , " she says . <p> " The toilet is only to be flushed when it is absolutely necessary and we have to use the bucket water to save water , " Olungah says . <p> According to experts on water conservation such as Edith Mukui , Olungah is on the right track . <p> " Showers release a lot of water . Up to 100 litres can be used by just one person taking a shower , " she says . <p> This is compared to the 10 to 20 litres the same person can use when bathing from a bucket . <p> But for those who must use the shower , Mukui advises them to learn to take five minutes or less to minimise usage . <p> She adds , though , that even a five-minute shower can require " about 40 to 80 litres of water , so it is still more water than using a bucket . " <p> Those using showers can save water by not only cutting down the amount of time spent showering , but by using a few other tricks . <p> " Water your entire body in just under a minute , turn off the water , lather and scrub your entire body , and then rinse in just one minute , " says Cathy Ng'endo , a resident of Imara Daima , where the water crisis has reached new proportions . <p> There should be a bucket on the bathroom at all times , she says . " There are those few seconds when cold water runs before the water heats up , as you adjust the temperature . Collect the water in a bucket . It can be used to mop the floor later , " she says . <p> As for the toilet , though many consider it an inconvenience , flushing the toilet using a bucket in just one swoop is enough to keep the toilet clean . <p> Experts say that an average person flushes the toilet five times a day , meaning it is in the toilet that most domestic water is consumed and it is also here that water can be most conserved . <p> " One toilet flush consumes about 14 litres . Older toilets require more water to serve the same purpose that five litres of water in a bucket does , " Mukui says . <p> Ng'endo says another way to ensure the toilet does not consume a lot of water is to ensure users do not throw dirt into the toilet . <p> " If it did not come from the human body , it should not go inside the toilet . When the toilet is stuffed with dirt , it clogs up , requiring a lot of water , " she says . <p> Robert Wanjala , a plumber , offers another trick to minimise water usage when flushing the loo . <p> " Put a rock inside a plastic bag and place it in the water bowl , this will ensure the toilet gets the same amount of water pressure when flushed , but without using a lot of water , " he says . <p> Ng'endo says many people brush their teeth while taking a shower . <p> " A lot of water gets wasted this way . I have two children who used to do that , but I stopped the habit , " she says . <p> Using a container to brush teeth comes highly recommended , with experts saying one should turn off the tap after wetting the toothbrush and to rinse using a container . <p> Research shows one person can save up to 30 litres a day by turning off the tap when brushing teeth . <p> ECONOMY IN THE KITCHEN <p> The kitchen is the heart of the household , where a lot of water is also consumed . Ng'endo cautions against " rinsing the dishes before actually washing them , for this is a waste of water " . <p> She says what remains in plates and pots can be removed by hand and the dishes washed from a basin instead of running water . <p> " In most instances , cooking ingredients are stored in the freezer and removed just before cooking , which requires water to defrost . This is another way of wasting water , " Ng'endo says . <p> She adds that people should decide early enough on what is to be cooked so that what is frozen can be removed in good time and allowed to defrost naturally . <p> Using the right size of cooking pot is yet another brilliant idea , as the cook will end up using only the right amount of water needed to ensure the meal is ready . <p> " I tell my children not to boil more water than they need . It used to be the habit that even where only two cups are needed , the entire kettle is filled with water that no one needs , " Olungah says . <p> " Even better , the water can be boiled and stored in a flask to be used on a needs basis . " <p> Wanjala advises people to fix leaking taps . " This is very common in our households and a lot of water gets wasted in this way . " <p> Experts say one drip per second from just one tap can waste at least 11,000 litres of water in a year . <p> " This is also the time to rewind clothes with dark colours . A black trouser does not need to be washed just because you wore it once , " Olungah says . <p> She adds that clothes should be washed in a bunch and the water used to wipe the floor and clean the toilet . <p> As for the verandah , she says sweeping the floor first ensures less water is used while moping , " and this goes for the house too " . <p> Whatever tricks can be employed will certainly come in handy , as the ongoing dry spell does not appear to let up anytime soon . <p> Thank you for participating in discussions on The Star , Kenya .
