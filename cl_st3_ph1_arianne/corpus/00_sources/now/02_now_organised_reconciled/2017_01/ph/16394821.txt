<h> Florida researchers find 2 new invasive mosquitoes in state <p> MIAMI -- Two more tropical disease-carrying mosquitoes have been found on the US mainland for the first time , caught in traps near Florida 's Everglades . <p> The scientists involved say this could raise the risk of mosquito-borne viruses reaching people and birds , but health officials say it 's too early to sound an alarm . <p> The new arrivals from Latin America and the Caribbean -- Culex panocossa and Aedeomyia squamipennis -- were trapped last October in rural areas bordering Everglades National Park by University of Florida Institute of Food and Agricultural Sciences entomologist Nathan Burkett-Cadena and Florida Medical Entomology Laboratory researcher Erik Blosser . <p> Their research is being published in the Journal of Medical Entomology and the journal Acta Tropica . <p> In the traps , they discovered that native species were crowded out by thousands of Culex panocossa mosquitoes and hundreds of Aedeomyia squamipennis mosquitoes . <p> Both species can be found on a few Caribbean islands , as well as from Mexico into South America . <p> They lay their eggs @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , drainage ditches and other waterways crisscrossing Florida neighborhoods . <p> " ' Hundreds ' is substantial , particularly when you get a hundred from a single trap . One , Aedes aegypti , is blamed for spreading the Zika virus , along with dengue fever and chikungunya . <p> The new arrivals are another sign that climate change , along with increased tourism and global trade , has made Florida more hospitable to exotic species , Burkett-Cadena said . <p> Health officials downplayed the immediate cause for concern , saying more research was needed . <p> " We have seen in Florida some invasive mosquito species that have become significant and others that have not , " Florida Department of Health Spokesman Mara Gambineri said in an e-mail on Tuesday . <p> One of roughly 200 mosquito species worldwide known to transmit diseases to humans @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of viruses that includes the Everglades virus . <p> The native Florida mosquito that Burkett-Cadena and Blosser were hoping to trap also can infect humans with Everglades virus , but it rarely has the opportunity to do so because humans rarely venture into its remote wetlands habitat . <p> The new Culex species in Florida lives closer to civilization , potentially increasing the risk of Everglades virus exposure , Burkett-Cadena said . <p> Aedeomyia squamipennis spreads bird malarias , including the kinds that have devastated Hawaii 's native bird populations .
