We tend to underestimate the extent to which we are motivated by social norms , basing much of our individual behaviour on how other people act , or on how we perceive they act . <p> An often attributed example of this tactic is when Obama was looking to increase voter turnout in 2008 . His team issued a simple press release stating that there had been " a record turnout expected " at the polls that year . Having a mini waste-bin acts as a visual sign for employees when they discard something , indicating that the social norm is for rubbish to be put in the recycling bin . <h> Amplify responses <p> In one six-week exercise , 140,000 people were sent a variety of letters by HMRC . One stressed the need to pay outstanding taxes , while others contained statements such as " nine out of 10 people in Britain pay their tax on time " . Smiley faces were used to rate conservation levels and resulted in a 2.8 per cent reduction in energy use . <p> Offices can use this insight within their email and other communication , rating departments ' ecological performance using smiley faces instead of words , as this will tap into social norms more effectively . <p> The key lesson here for offices is that they should n't only try to encourage green behaviour by telling their employees of the benefits , but by using implicit and explicit tactics to show that others are already taking steps to reduce waste .
