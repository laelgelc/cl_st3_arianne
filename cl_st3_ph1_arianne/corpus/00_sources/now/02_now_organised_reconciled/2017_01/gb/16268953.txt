CAROL TURNER explains why the conference is not to be missed <p> W HAT exactly does the prospect of four years of a Donald Trump presidency hold ? As his inauguration approaches , that question 's on all our lips . <p> With no past political offices to judge him by , it 's difficult to get a handle on what 's in store . Trump 's racism , misogyny and homophobia situate him squarely in the camp of social conservatism ; his business interests permit no doubts about where he stands on the economy . <p> But his apparently contradictory views on international affairs leave many of us a bit perplexed . He might be a head-in-the-sand climate change denier , but whatever else he is , Trump 's no idiot . That 's not how you get to be president of the USA . And the pundits , who lined up throughout his campaign to tell us he was , have done no-one any @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ bigot or a moron , they diverted attention away from the real dangers Trump poses . <p> By reinforcing a view that his bid for the world 's top political spot could never succeed , they helped disarm his opponents -- leaving Trump a free run to speak to voters over the heads of a failed political Establishment . <p> Amid all the election chatter , one of the most sensible comments I heard came from a US analyst on the day after the results were out . He argued that Trump had won because he reinforced what his supporters already believed . <p> Disregarding truth or reason , Trump told them what they wanted to hear . Preferring rational debate to the politics of the Twittersphere , I too found Trump offensive . His message was n't one I wanted to hear . <p> An American friend told me she 'd blocked every one of her lifelong friends whose Facebook page gave the slightest hint of sympathy with his views . <p> Nevertheless , his campaign strategy served him well . Is praise for Vladimir Putin the admiration of a wannabe strong leader ? And why court Russia but confront China ? Over Taiwan too , the issue most likely to prompt a breach in Sino-US relations . <p> Russia remains the nearest thing to a military challenge the US faces . But strategically speaking , it 's a declining power with a weak economy skewed by an overreliance on petroleum . <p> China on the other hand , a rising power , is the United States ' real rival in the years ahead . Perhaps Trump 's not so lacking in political nous as not to see this . Is he after driving a wedge between China and Russia ? Their relations have progressed apace since the turn of the new century with regional initiatives like the Shanghai Co-operation Organisation . <p> But if so , how likely is Trump to succeed ?
