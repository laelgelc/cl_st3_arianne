<p> Goundo Wandianga is the only young man left in his village . Or at least , that 's what it feels like . <p> He spends his days lounging under mango trees in his family 's small village in Casamance , the southern region of Senegal , playing on his phone , doing odd jobs for his mother , and dreaming about Europe . <p> Everyone else carries on as normal in Sare Bakary , his village . Old men sit on benches cracking peanuts and chatting about cattle , the old days , and past attempts to get to Europe . Their wives and daughters work constantly , watering the garden , cooking lunch , and sweeping up the peanut shells , while children dart in and out between the huts . <p> But in Sare Bakary , there is no group of surly male adolescents , or strapping young men in their 20s . There is just Goundo . <p> He is only 21 , but all of his friends and even his twin brother have left to seek their fortune elsewhere .
