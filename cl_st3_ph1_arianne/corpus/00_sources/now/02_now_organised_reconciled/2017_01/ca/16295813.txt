<p> -- image credit : Michael Klotz/Special to the Langley Advance <p> by Roxanne Hooper - Langley Advance <p> Langley posted Jan 8 , 2017 at 1:00 PM <p> Michael Klotz got started birding when his grandpa , Jack , gave him his first field guide and a pair of binoculars at the age of eight . <p> Blue jays and American goldfinch are still burnt into his memory from his days visiting his grandparents in Winnipeg . <p> Fast forward a few decades , and the 46-year-old Langley birdwatcher has since travelled extensively through North and Central America -- along with the Caribbean and a sprinkle of Africa thrown in -- in search of new and exotic species of birds . <p> He 's currently sighted more than 850 different species , and that number is always growing . <p> His quest to learn more about his fine-feathered friends and neighbours began in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ college in Santa Barbara . <p> It 's " born out of a passion for birding and the need to protect the wild areas of the world that promote healthy diversity and protection of the anchor species of that region , " Klotz explained of his near obsession with birding . <p> That same motivation is part of what drives him to participate every year in the annual Audobon Christmas bird count -- and what motivated him to take on the organizing role of this year 's event . <p> This year , the count was held on Jan. 2 and he joined 20 other birders and field naturalists in mapping sightings of birds spotted between 200th and 232nd Streets , and from Springbrook Road south to 48th Avenue in Langley . <p> Langley is just part of what is designated nationally as the White Rock zone for the count , which actually takes in birds spotted in Blaine , Surrey , and part of Langley , he explained . <p> It may have been beautiful and sunny during last week 's count , he said , but temperatures @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ was covered with snow and ice -- making it difficult for participants to access some of the traditional 4,417 individual birds . <p> That was a drop , he said , because of weather compared to 2015 , when they spotted 67 species and 7,934 individual birds . <p> Numbers , he said , are still not tallied for the complete White Rock bird count , but he expects there will be a drop across the board not only because it was more difficult for people to get around , but also because many of the birds sought shelter due to the colder temperatures . <p> Nevertheless , for more than seven hours , teams of four to five people took notes and pictures of all the birds they could see . This prompted his purchase of a used camera and long lens from a local Internet classified site . Five cameras later and a tad more experience ( the early pictures prove it ) and he has a website -- www.thebirdblogger.com . <p> While Klotz revels in wandering the flood plains , mountains , lakes , backroads , ocean shores , rivers , forests , and even farmers fields hunting for different birds , and taking thousands of pictures , there 's much more to his @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as he calls it , and the compilation of hands-on data during ventures such as the bird counts , that he feels is imperative to conservation and preservation of birds . <p> Understanding how everything from weather , and development , to climate change and human patterns impact on the bird population is key , he said . <p> For instance , the song bird population in North America is declining , Klotz explained . And , to prevent that from worsening , society has to understand where and why it is happening . Counts like this give a real-time picture of what 's going on . <p> Klotz 's goal , ultimately , is to promote conservation by saving tracts of land in crucial areas where birds are critically endangered . <p> So , his hope is to be able to purchase land in and around important bird habitat throughout the world allowing corridors to be built between green spaces -- which allow for safe transport from one to another of the birds . <p> Identifying problem areas and dramatic changes in patterns , he said moves him @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ For more information about the annual Christmas bird count or two other counts this spring ( Derby Reach/Brae Island on Jan. 31 and Glen Valley Spring Bird Count May 30 ) , visit the Langley Field Naturalists website at : **31;931;TOOLONG . <p> CAPTION : Michael Klotz/Special to the Langley Advance <p> Some of the only Mourning Doves in the Lower Mainland . <p> CAPTION : Michael Klotz/Special to the Langley Advance <p> A juvenile red-tailed hawk on the fence at Springbrook Road near Glover Road . <p> CAPTION : Michael Klotz/Special to the Langley Advance <p> Taken from the grounds of the MIlner Downs , Michael Klotz captures this view of the Golden Ears Mountains . <p> We encourage an open exchange of ideas on this story 's topic , but we ask you to follow our guidelines for respecting community standards .
