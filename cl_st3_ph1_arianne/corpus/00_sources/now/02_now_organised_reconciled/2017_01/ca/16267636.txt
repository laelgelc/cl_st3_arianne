A recent rise in fuel prices in the territory is probably due to refinery problems in the American Midwest , not Alberta 's carbon tax , according to a petroleum analyst . <p> Yukon drivers may have noticed they 're paying a little more at the pump these days . <p> Gasoline prices in Whitehorse were hovering around $1.20 per litre as of Friday morning , up from about $1.12 on Dec. 21 , according to the Yukon Bureau of Statistics . <p> So does the price hike have anything to do with Alberta 's new carbon tax , which came into effect on Jan. 1 ? <p> As of this week , Albertans are paying a carbon tax of 4.49 cents per litre on gasoline . The provincial carbon tax was implemented at $20 per tonne in the new year . <p> Yukon purchases most of its gasoline from Alberta @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ carbon tax at the pump . <p> " In our tax plan , only gas that 's consumed in Alberta , burnt in Alberta , would be subject to the levy , " said Mike Brown , press secretary for Alberta 's finance minister . " So there should n't be an impact on Yukon gas prices . " <p> Alberta 's carbon tax is applied to consumer purchases . So if Yukoners drove down to Alberta and filled up at gas stations there , they would pay the levy . But there 's no carbon tax on the gasoline imported for sale in the Yukon . <p> Alberta 's 2016 budget , which lays out the province 's carbon tax plan , includes a section on exported fuel . <p> " Fuel sold for export will be exempt from the levy as it is not consumed in Alberta and the related emissions are not released here , " it reads . <p> There are a couple of other ways that gas prices in the Yukon could be affected by the carbon price in Alberta .
