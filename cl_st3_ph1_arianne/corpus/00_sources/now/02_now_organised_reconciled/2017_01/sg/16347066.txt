<h> Rising inequality among biggest global risks : WEF <h> Forum also flags climate change and social polarisation as major trends in next decade <p> LONDON * Rising inequality and social polarisation are set to shape world developments for the next decade after contributing to Britain 's decision to leave the European Union and the ballot-box success of Mr Donald Trump , the World Economic Forum ( WEF ) said yesterday . <p> Climate change was underlined as the third major global trend in the WEF 's annual assessment of global risks , released at an event at Bloomberg 's European headquarters in London . <p> The report will be discussed at the WEF 's annual gathering of political and business leaders in Davos , Switzerland , beginning on Jan 17 . <p> A weak economic recovery following the global financial crisis has widened the gap between rich and poor , fuelling a sense of " economic malaise " that has led to the rise of populist parties , the report said . While Brexit and Mr Trump were the highest-profile signs of an anti-establishment backlash in Western democracies @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ far-right parties in Germany , France , Italy and the Netherlands , among others . <p> The poll involved 750 experts assessing 30 global risks ranging from deflation to extreme weather events and cyber attacks . They identified trends such as an ageing population , climate change , social polarisation and income inequality as amplifying those risks . <p> The WEF ranked risks by impact and likelihood . While weapons of mass destruction were deemed the most impactful risk , they were also judged unlikely . Extreme weather events were the next most impactful , and were also rated the most likely risk . <p> The next four most likely risks were large-scale involuntary migration , major natural disasters , large-scale terror attacks and a big incident of data theft or fraud . <p> Rising income inequality was identified as the most important underlying trend determining global events , followed by climate change and societal polarisation . <p> The report 's authors pointed to a " deteriorating commitment to global cooperation " , with China rejecting the verdict of an international tribunal over disputed waters in the South China @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ considering pulling the US out of the Paris Agreement , the first truly global deal to combat climate change . <p> The WEF urged the creation of new policies to help more people benefit from new technologies . It examined 12 emerging technologies , ranging from 3D printing to renewable energy . <p> Advances in artificial intelligence and robotics yielded the greatest potential benefits , but also the biggest potential negative effects , it said . <p> BLOOMBERG <p> A version of this article appeared in the print edition of The Straits Times on January 12 , 2017 , with the headline ' Rising inequality among biggest global risks : WEF ' .
