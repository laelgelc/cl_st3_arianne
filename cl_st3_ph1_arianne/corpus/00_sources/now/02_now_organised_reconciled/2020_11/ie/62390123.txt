<p> Austrian Pensionskassen are allocating 31.3% of their assets to sectors that have a negative impact on climate , according to a report by the Financial Market Authority ( FMA ) . <p> The regulator conducted a stress test to assess risk management activities of various pension funds ' business models in the field of sustainability and impact on climate . It found that Pensionskassen are particularly exposed to energy-intensive ( 12.1% ) and real estate ( 9.9% ) segments with an impact on climate . <p> The FMA considered utilities , transport , real estate and energy-intensive segments as so-called " climate-relevant " sectors , based on their contribution to CO2 emissions , while fossil fuels contribute indirectly to high CO2 emissions , it added . <p> The report noted that the share of assets invested in activities with an impact on climate varies for individual Pensionskassen between 28.6% and 38.6% , but seven out of eight funds show values above 30% . <p> By using the PACTA tool , which measures equity and bonds portfolios with regards to CO2 emissions , the regulator found that $2.1bn ( ? @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ total investments , is allocated in segments with an impact on climate change , mostly fossil fuels and energy . <p> PACTA has identified $3.81bn , which equals to 13.7% of total assets , as climate-relevant . <p> Six out of eight Pensionskassen in Austria take into account ESG criteria if returns do not suffer , while five funds stated that for certain types of investments ESG policies are not relevant . <p> Changes in asset mix <p> Austrian Pensionskassen assets under management reached ? 23.2bn at the end of June .
