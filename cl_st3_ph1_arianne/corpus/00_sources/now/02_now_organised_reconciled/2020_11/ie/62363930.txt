<h> FCA develops principles to tackle greenwashing concerns <p> The UK 's Financial Conduct Authority ( FCA ) has developed a set of principles as part of its efforts to address potential greenwashing , the regulator 's CEO revealed yesterday . <p> Speaking during a green finance conference broadcast from London , Nikhil Rathi said the principles were intended to help fund management firms " interpret existing rules requiring that disclosures are ' fair , clear and not misleading ' , including when they wanted to submit new products for regulatory authorisation . <p> " Better disclosures will , in turn , help consumers understand and compare the products they are offered , " Rathi added . " We will shortly start discussing these principles with industry with a view to finalising them in the new year . " <p> In a feedback statement on climate change and green finance last year , the FCA said it would carry out further policy analysis on greenwashing and take action , for example guidance , " to address concerns as appropriate " . <p> Yesterday Rathi , who took up his post @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ said the regulator was planning to run some consumer experiments to help the regulator better understand what information influenced consumers ' choices in sustainable products . <p> The FCA has so far defined greenwashing as " marketing that portrays an organisation 's products , activities or policies as producing positive environmental outcomes when this is not the case " . <p> Referencing two investment product categories introduced by new EU sustainable finance regulation , ESMA said : " The sustainability-related information of a marketing communication should be commensurate with the extent to which the investment strategy of the fund promotes environmental or social characteristics , or sustainable investment objectives " . <p> " For example , if the investment strategy of a fund is primarily pursuing financial performance , any sustainability-aspects of the investment in the promoted fund should not be the main information of a marketing communication , " it added . <p> The supervisor has said greenwashing " consists in gaining an unfair competitive advantage by marketing a financial product as environmentally friendly , when in fact basic environmental standards have not been met " .
