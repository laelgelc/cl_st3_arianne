<p> The UK government has today ( 30 November ) launched its post-Brexit " path to sustainable farming " roadmap , changes which will be brought in over a period of seven years . <p> The plan means the UK will transition away from the ? 3bn European Common Agricultural Policy system of direct payments in favour of incentives for sustainable production . <p> Changes will only apply to farmers in England , as the Welsh and Scottish devolved administrations will decide separately how to allocate their own farm funding . <p> Under the proposals , the government will introduce the Environmental Land Management ( ELM ) scheme to incentivise sustainable farming practices , create habitats for nature recovery and establish new woodland to help tackle climate change . <p> The ELMS will consist of three components . The Sustainable Farming Incentive will support approaches to farm husbandry that deliver for the environment , such as actions to improve soil health and integrated pest management . <p> The Local Nature Recovery will pay for actions such as creating , managing or restoring habitats , natural flood management and species management @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ will focus on landscape and ecosystem recovery through projects looking to achieve large-scale woodland creation , peatland restoration , or coastal restoration . <p> A new Farming Investment Fund is also set to open for applications next year , providing support to businesses so that they can invest in equipment , technology and infrastructure . <p> It will include two levels . The Farming Equipment Technology Fund will offer small grants toward the purchase of a list of specified items , and the Farming Transformation Fund will provide larger sums towards more substantial investments . <p> The new system announced today represents the most significant change to farming and land management in England in 70 years . <p> But farming groups have reacted with concern over the move toward cuts to direct payments , which will be reduced starting from the 2021 Basic Payment Scheme ( BPS ) year . <p> Businesses who receive ? 30,000 or less per year will see their subsidy fall by 5 per cent in 2021 , followed by a gradual increase to 50 per cent by 2024 , farming groups say . <p> Farmers @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in BPS payments are on course to face reductions even higher than that .
