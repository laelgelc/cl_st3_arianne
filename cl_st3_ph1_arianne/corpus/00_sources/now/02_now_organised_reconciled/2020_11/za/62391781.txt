<h> When it comes to clean energy , SA still has a long way to go <p> South Africa 's energy sector remains one of the most coal-dependent and largest per capita polluters in the G20 despite being at greater risk of climate-related impacts . <p> This is according to the latest report from Climate Transparency International that cautions G20 countries against quick-fix Covid-19 stimulus packages that favour fossil fuel industries . <p> The Climate Transparency Report is the world 's most comprehensive annual review of climate action by G20 countries . The report draws on the latest emissions data from 2019 and covers 100 indicators on decarbonisation , climate policies , finance and vulnerability to the impacts of climate change . <p> According to the report , fossil fuels still account for 92% of South Africa 's total energy mix , with more than 85% of electricity coming from coal . As a result , the report found that South Africa 's per capita greenhouse gas ( GHG ) emissions are some of the highest among the G20 nations . <p> By comparison , renewable energy only accounts for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ G20 average of 27% . <p> " Right now we have an opportunity to make decisions about our energy mix that will benefit us both in the short term and in the long term , " says lead author of the report , Bryce McCall of the University of Cape Town 's Energy Systems Research Group . <p> " The impacts of climate , change are already being felt in South Africa yet we have made little progress in addressing domestic emissions intensity or our contribution to greenhouse gases . If the government is serious about economic recovery we need to invest in technology that will provide affordable and sustainable energy solutions , " says McCall . <p> Despite South Africa being at greater risk of climate-related impacts than other G20 countries -- and being one of its biggest emitters -- the South African government has yet to make a firm commitment to phase out coal power by 2050 .
