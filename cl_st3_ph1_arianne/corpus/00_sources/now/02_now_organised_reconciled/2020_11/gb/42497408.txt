<h> Competitions launched to conserve world-famous Highgate Cemetery <h> Competitions launched to conserve world-famous Highgate Cemetery <p> A search is being launched for designers and architects to help conserve the world-famous Highgate Cemetery in London in the face of climate change . <p> Monuments in the burial ground , the resting place of well known people including Karl Marx and Lucian Freud , are under threat from long-term decay and maturing self-seeded trees , Friends of Highgate Cemetery Trust said . <p> The trees , such as ash , are increasingly stressed by climate change and afflicted by new pests and diseases . <p> And more extreme weather brought by a changing climate is eroding the cemetery 's gravel paths and overwhelming its historic drainage systems . <p> The trust , which owns and cares for the cemetery , said these impacts were undermining the historic landscape design and reducing the wildlife in the Grade I-listed north London site . <p> Conservation action Friends of Highgate Cemetery Trust are launching two open competitions , the first of which is to find the best landscape designers to draw up a masterplan for the site . <p> The second competition is to find an architect-led design team to investigate projects to preserve and enhance historic structures and improve facilities for staff and visitors . <p> Martin Adeney , chairman of the Friends of Highgate Cemetery Trust , said : " We will be working with the local community , cemetery volunteers and experts so that the trees , paths , monuments and buildings will be better looked after , the cemetery will continue to function as an active burial ground and visiting will be easier and more rewarding . <p> " Having saved the cemetery from dereliction in the 1970s , and secured the major monuments , the Friends of Highgate Cemetery Trust is now looking at how this world-famous resting place of so many distinguished people can be conserved and enhanced for future generations . <p> " We
