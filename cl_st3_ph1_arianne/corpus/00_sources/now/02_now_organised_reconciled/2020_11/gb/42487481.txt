<h> ? 15m home for Concorde thrown out - to save trees from the chop <h> National Museums of Scotland wanted 60ft hangar for jets <p> A BID to build a new ? 15m home for historic planes including Concorde has been thrown out after it was ruled ancient trees should not be chopped down . <p> National Museums of Scotland ( NMS ) wanted to build a 60ft tall hangar for the world famous supersonic jet and two other aircraft . <p> The proposal would also have seen construction of a new visitor centre , cafe and shop at the National Museum of Flight which would have seen around ? 7.3m pumped into the local economy and provided 40 jobs . <p> However fury erupted when it emerged the plans involved the felling of 299 trees in woodland near to the East Lothian site . <p> More than 200 people wrote letters of complaint while over 6,500 people signed a petition against the move . <p> Bosses of the popular museum pledged to replace the woodland in a new area to the south of the site with 1,000 plants @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Lothian Council rejected their application earlier this year after ruling it breached the council 's strategy on climate change . <p> It was argued it would take 15 years for any new trees to equal the current impact on the area 's carbon footprint being provided by the existing trees . <p> Museum chiefs appealed to the Scottish Government in a bid to have it overturned but have been left ' extremely disappointed ' by the decision to uphold the original decision . <p> A petition against the plans set up by The Woodland Trust and Rewilding East Lothian said : " At a time of climate emergency and with wildlife habitats already in rapid decline it is extremely shortsighted to chop down a mature swathe of wood . <p> " Planting young trees as a ' replacement ' is not sufficient .
