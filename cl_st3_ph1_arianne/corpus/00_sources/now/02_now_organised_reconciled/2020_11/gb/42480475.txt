Photo modes are absolutely wonderful , and they 're also about you , the player . They provide a lot of cool options for framing the main character in really interesting ways . Each level of Umurangi Generation is , to some extent , open ; you have the freedom to move around it however you 'd like , getting whatever angles you can concoct for your shots . <p> The worlds then , at least in the base game , are sort of like still-lifes themselves . Things are certainly happening -- teens are dancing , officers are moving people along designated routes , and bystanders are milling about . There are also some instant post-processing tools , where you can fiddle with settings like exposure or contrast . <p> It 's a DSLR simulator in the truest sense , because you 're essentially given a camera and let loose .
