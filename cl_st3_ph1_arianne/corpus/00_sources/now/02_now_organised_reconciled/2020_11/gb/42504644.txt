<h> Letter about Portland incinerator <p> Clearly everyone would like Portland Port to survive . It has a long and illustrious history which , unbelievably , is not commemorated in the Port itself . <p> However , its future does not have to depend , as claimed , on receiving shore-power from an ERF incinerator . The only other port planning to have the facility is Southampton . However , they will run it from the national grid , not ERF incineration . This makes complete sense environmentally because , to quote a recent report : <p> " The average carbon intensity of the EU 28 ( 2018 ) electricity grid has constantly been falling thanks to the increasing uptake of renewable wind and solar power , and stands currently at 296g CO2eq/KWh . This means that the carbon intensity of energy produced through waste incineration ( 580g CO2eq/kWh ) is already about two-times greater than the current EU average electricity grid carbon intensity . " September 2019 : Zero Waste Europe . <p> The reduction in CO2 emissions from the national grid will only increase given the Government 's @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ net zero carbon future . According to the Prime Minister , if the UK is to become a leader in the race to become zero carbon , this will be achieved through off-shore wind power , not the paltry 2 % of the national grid that the 40 plus CO2 and PM 2.5-PM 1 emitting ERF incinerators provide . <p> In his usual colourful way the P.M. stated in a BBC news report : <p> " Your kettle , your washing machine , your cooker , your heating , your plug-in electric vehicle - the whole lot of them will get their juice cleanly and without guilt from the breezes that blow around these islands . Far out in the deepest waters we will harvest the gusts , and by upgrading infrastructure in places like Teesside and Humber and Scotland and Wales , we will increase an offshore wind capacity that is already the biggest in the world . " <p> The UK is set to host the postponed COP 26 international conference on Climate Change in 2021 . Boris Johnson will be advised by the ex-head of the Bank @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ opinion that those businesses that do n't aim for zero carbon will go bankrupt . This is an extremely serious prediction made by a man who knows what he is talking about . What he says is in line with the EU 's removal of ERF incineration from its list of sustainable activities , with severe financial repercussions for the waste industry . <p> So why given the environmentally better national grid or zero-carbon off-shore wind power options , would it make sense to have all the disruption , unavoidable damaging to health and the environment of an ERF incinerator blighting the UNESCO protected Jurassic Coast ? <p> The developers estimate " up to 15 MW will be available to moored ships " out of total of 15.2 MW . This illustrates the impossibility of simultaneously providing shore-power , heating and electricity , as well as heating to the Verne and local businesses , so why is their proposal based on this claim ? Despite this question having been raised several times , an answer to this vital question has never been provided . <p> In any case , what @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ lose their future bookings due to a lack of shore-power ? Providing shore power to ships is extremely expensive - so will cash-strapped cruise companies pay for the service ? Cruise companies , already aware of up-coming environmental marine legislation are already investing in ships powered by liquefied natural gas ( LNG ) . The fuel is far from environmentally ideal and their focus is now on researching bio-LNG , which is by all accounts is virtually zero-carbon . This would seem to remove any need for shore power , and in any case many cruise liners are not equipped for shore power . <p> Given the number of cruise ships already visiting pre-COVID-19 Portland Port , if cruise ships benefited the local economy , Portland and Weymouth would not be among the most deprived areas in the UK . If cruise ships have indeed generated a vast income why has n't it been invested in the local economy ?
