<h> 12-year-old Liz spoke at a Combating Climate Change debate <p> * Manningtree High School student Liz Garnham spoke at a Combating Climate Change debate . This is her review of the event . <p> How can a 12-Year-old girl be expected to influence national climate policy ? <p> That 's what I thought when I was offered the chance to speak alongside Sir Bernard Jenkin , our MP , Philip Dunne , the chair of the environmental audit committee , and Gareth Redmond King , the head of climate change at WWF . <p> It seemed a little ridiculous . <p> But in October I put my nerves behind me and appeared as part of a panel to discuss the environment . <p> Coming from an Essex farming family , I tried to explain the " perfect storm " which farmers find themselves facing at the moment . <p> Brexit , coronavirus and climate change are all placing different stresses and strains on the people who manage most of this country 's land . <p> Coronavirus has shown how important it is to maintain food supply lines . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , such as pasta and tinned tomatoes . <p> I asked Sir Bernard whether the Government was going to prioritise food production in their new agriculture bill , which is going through Parliament . <p> I also tried to explain that food production needs to work alongside environmental initiatives . <p> On our farm we are working with the Essex Wildlife Trust to try to create safe corridors for the rare Hazel Dormouse which has a healthy population in Tendring . <p> This sort of initiative -- involving planting trees and encouraging hedgerows -- may reduce food production but increase biodiversity . <p> Finally I discussed the importance of protecting our marshes and saltings . <p> They are amazing stores for carbon as well as nesting sites for rare and fascinating birds . <p> I love to think about the godwit which recently flew from Alaska to New Zealand without stopping . <p> We have important numbers of godwits in Essex but if we do n't protect our wetlands from development and human disruption , we risk losing wading birds like these including beautiful curlews , lapwings and whimbrels . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ I had rehearsed my speech , I think it went ok . <p> Then it was time to answer questions . <p> I was asked what farmers would say to food consumers . I tried to explain that everyone needs to do their best to buy local food which is in season . <p> Importing food which has come thousands of miles is not good for the environment and we have so many brilliant food producers in East Anglia that it should be fun . <p> It was actually a really fun evening and maybe - just maybe - I have made a little bit of a difference . <p> This website and associated newspapers adhere to the Independent Press Standards Organisation 's Editors ' Code of Practice .
