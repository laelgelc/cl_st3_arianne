<p> Governments have rejected calls for tougher regulation of international shipping , settling instead for new rules on reducing greenhouse gas emissions that campaigners say will imperil the Paris climate goals . <p> The International Maritime Organization ( IMO ) , the UN body that regulates international shipping , agreed on Friday after a week-long online meeting to make an existing target legally binding : to reduce the carbon intensity of shipping by 40% compared with 2008 levels in the next 10 years . <p> The conclusions , reached by ministers from around the world despite calls from the UK , Ireland , New Zealand and others for more stringent curbs on emissions , will go forward to the IMO 's marine environment protection committee for acceptance next month . <p> Campaigners said the deal would allow carbon dioxide emissions from shipping to continue to rise in the next decade , despite warnings from scientists that global emissions need to be brought down sharply over that period . <p> International shipping accounts for about 2.5% of global emissions , or about 1bn tonnes of CO2 equivalent per year -- more @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ .
