A new study reports that ants are pros at cultivating climate-resilient crops . <p> Share : <p> FULL STORY <p> Fungus-farming ants are an insect lineage that relies on farmed fungus for their survival . In return for tending to their fungal crops -- protecting them against pests and pathogens , providing them with stable growth conditions in underground nests , and provisioning them with nutritional ' fertilizers ' -- the ants gain a stable food supply . <p> advertisement <p> These fungus farming systems are an expression of striking collective organization honed over 60 million years of fungus crop domestication . The farming systems of humans thus pale in comparison , since they emerged only ca. 10,000 years ago . <p> A new study from the University @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , demonstrates that these ants might be one up on us as far as farming skills go . Long ago , they managed to appear to have overcome key domestication challenges that we have yet to solve . <p> " Ants have managed to retain a farming lifestyle across 60 million years of climate change , and Leafcutter ants appear able to grow a single cultivar species across diverse habitats , from grasslands to tropical rainforest " explains Jonathan Z. Shik , one of the study 's authors and an assistant professor at the University of Copenhagen 's Department of Biology . <p> Through fieldwork in the rainforests of Panama , he and researchers from the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute studied how fungus-farming ants use nutrition to manage a tradeoff between the cultivar 's increasingly specialized production benefits , and it 's rising vulnerability to environmental variation . <p> But these benefits of crop domestication can also result in greater sensitivity to environmental threats from weather and pests , requiring increasing pesticide use and irrigation . Armed only with forceps , they stole tiny pieces of leaves and other substrates from the jaws of ants as they returned from foraging trips . <p> They did this while snakes slithered through the leaf litter and monkeys peered down at him from the treetops . <p> advertisement <p> " For instance , our nutritional analyses of the plant substrates foraged by leafcutter ants show that they collect leaves , fruit , and flowers from hundreds of different rainforest trees . " <p> What can we learn from ants ? <p> Over the years , the ants have adapted their leaf collecting to the needs of the fungus -- a kind of organic farming , without the benefits of the technological advances that have helped human farmers over the millenia , one might say . <p> One might wonder , is it possible to simply copy their ingenious methods ? <p> " Because our plant crops require sunlight and must thus be grown above ground , we ca n't directly transfer the ants ' methods to our own agricultural practices .
