<p> LONDON/BRUSSELS ( Reuters ) - The International Development Finance Club , a group of 26 national and regional development banks , said its members supplied $197 billion in green finance in 2019 , representing a quarter of their total lending . <p> While a 47% increase on 2018 , the figure for 2019 remains below a high reached in 2017 , the IDFC said in a report published on Friday . <p> In total , $867 billion has been invested since 2015 , when the Paris Agreement was struck to limit global warming . <p> The role of state-backed lenders in driving the global response to climate change is increasingly under the spotlight given their deep pockets and ability to pave the way for increased private-sector investment by sharing risk . <p> Their willingness to do more is set to be put to the test next week when the world 's 450 development banks meet for the first time in Paris , with a number expected to sign a joint commitment to accelerate their climate efforts . <p> Of the finance provided in 2019 by the group , which @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Korea and France , 95% went towards climate finance with the rest , some $10 billion , towards other environmental objectives such as biodiversity projects and industrial pollution control . <p> The group did not specify how much it lent towards fossil fuel projects . A total of $5 billion was spent on " lower carbon and efficient energy generation " , which can include fuel switch projects such as funding gas in favour of coal . <p> Climate change adaptation , meanwhile , accounted for 10% of total climate financing , up a quarter on the prior year and more than three times that invested in 2015 , the report said . <p> The vast majority of the green finance expenditure - $135.8 billion - was aimed at projects in east Asia and the Pacific region , in line with the geographical make-up of the group and its spread of assets . <p> Most of the total spend , 87% , was invested in the banks ' home countries and almost all of the financing , 97% , was provided in the form of loans .
