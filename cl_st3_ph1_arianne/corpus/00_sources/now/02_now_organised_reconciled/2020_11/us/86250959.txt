With that in mind , Orlando got an early Christmas present Oct. 14 . <p> Actually , the gift is more like a promissory note . The Orlando Utilities Commission unveiled its plan to convert from fossil fuels to solar and other renewable sources . <p> That 's the good news . The $1 million study calls for the two coal-fired Stanton Energy Center plants in east Orange County to be converted to natural gas between 2025 and 2027 . They will be completely shut down in 2040 . <p> That 's about 20 years too late for environmental groups . Coal-fired power plants release more greenhouse gases , such as carbon dioxide per unit , than any other electricity source . <p> With climate change becoming more of a concern every day , the thought of CO2 spewing into the atmosphere for at least another five years is depressing . What 's worse , the sun never shines at night . <p> That means solar power must be stored . Battery technology is improving fast but it wo n't be cost-effective anytime soon . <p> As for wind , Florida is n't breezy enough to depend on wind turbines . And since it 's a peninsula , clean energy can only be imported from the north .
