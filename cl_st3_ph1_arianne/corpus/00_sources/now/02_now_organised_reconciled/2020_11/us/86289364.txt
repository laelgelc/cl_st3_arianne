<p> Edelman , after becoming the biggest public-relations firm , has long boasted that it could snatch share from advertising agencies . <p> It says those ambitions are starting to bear fruit now that the company has hired a slew of prominent advertising vets , like the ex-Leo Burnett exec Judy John and the McCann vet Lee Maicon , and built a 600-person creative and planning staff . <p> Business Insider spoke with John , the company 's global chief creative officer ; Maicon , its global chief innovation and strategy officer ; and Mattias Ronge , Edelman 's chief creative officer for Europe , the Middle East , and Africa . They laid out how the firm was going after that business . <p> The execs said Edelman was well-positioned to win accounts from a traditional ad industry that 's been hobbled by downsizing . <p> According to John , holding companies have been prioritizing technology over creative , and as a result , their creativity is suffering . <p> " If you 're using the old paid model , which only lives in the lane where you create @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to be interesting , then you 're going to continue to lose clients , " John said . <p> Edelman is n't trying to make big-budget TV commercials that are traditional agencies ' bread and butter , and it would n't specify how big the company believed ad services could become for the firm . <p> Instead , its goal is to apply creativity 's lessons to solve companies ' PR and reputational problems .
