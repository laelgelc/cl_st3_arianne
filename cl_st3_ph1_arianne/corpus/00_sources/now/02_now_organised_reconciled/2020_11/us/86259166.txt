<h> Protecting Investors From Politicized Pension Portfolios <p> The pitched political battles of the last few years may see a brief respite after the election officially concludes , but it will only be a pause . These fights have metastasized well beyond the political realm and now seem an inescapable facet of daily life -- even the sports pages in most papers feel obligated to weigh in on the election and a plethora of other issues irrelevant to actual sports competition . <p> Part of this has to do with the passion that people feel both for and against Donald Trump , of course . But in some situations this change is more strategic , and potentially harmful for millions of retirement savers unaware of what is transpiring . <p> For instance , financial markets in particular have become more politicized in the last four years , as investment managers have encountered increasing pressure to hew to norms concerning climate change , diversity , and a variety of other social goals as well . Since it is relatively easy and inexpensive to put forth a resolution , companies have been faced with a greatly increased number of resolutions for their annual meetings , most of which advocate an agenda that includes forcing firms to take steps above and beyond what is legally required to reduce carbon emissions , further diversifying their hiring practices and their board , and hewing to a variety of other social agenda . <p> These efforts have been abetted by proxy advisory firms , which help investment management firms decide how to vote on their proxies . And pretending that political choices do n't impact returns , or that the reduction in returns would be minuscule and therefore irrelevant , does not hold water .
