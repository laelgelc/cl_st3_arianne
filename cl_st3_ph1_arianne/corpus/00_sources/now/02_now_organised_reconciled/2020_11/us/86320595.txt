<p> In a new Department of Labor ( DOL ) rule issued on October 30 , the DOL essentially prohibits the use of widely used environmental , social and governance ( ESG ) factors in selecting investments for employee retirement plans . However , it is considered unlikely that the rule will remain in place , as it has faced much backlash . Let 's take a closer look . <h> ESG investing <p> ESG or " sustainable " investing involves looking at non-financial company factors , such as diversity , climate change and sustainability , in deciding whether to invest in the shares of that company . By doing so , ESG investing encourages corporate boards and management to make ESG goals a substantial consideration in the operation of their company . As David Gardner , co-founder of Motley Fool , says , in championing ESG investing , a portfolio should " reflect your best vision for the future " and work to build the world you want your investment dollars to create . <p> It should be noted that investors are acting on that concept . The potential long-term benefits of ESG investing are significant and , according to NASDAQ NDAQ Board member Betsy Atkins , should be a priority for corporate management and boards , as well as for investors . Even legendary investment guru Warren Buffet says that investing in the community and the environment is a " key ingredient of profitability . " <p> The question really becomes , then , what are the " best " interests of retirement plan investors ? Are they simply the best immediate financial returns ( which ESG may or may not provide ) , or is it in the best interest of these investors to have a healthier and better-run world to spend their retirement in ? Of course , a popular , narrow definition of " best interests " is purely financial , so it is particularly important to re-define that interest to include the broader meaning . <h> What is the real issue ? <p> ESG investing has long been recognized as offering long-term financial and societal benefits , including increased returns and reduced costs for businesses . Moreover , Morningstar MORN reports that 72% of the US population has at least some interest in sustainable investing , and this group includes all ages and genders . Not only has the public turned to ESG , but the industry is also recognizing its importance ; S&amp;P Global Ratings has been exploring the links between ESG and corporate financial performance and finds them important . <p> For 30 years , however , the US Department of Labor has been considering whether recommending these kinds of investments in employee retirement accounts - that is , those factors which look at other than financial objectives - might be problematic . Their concern was that by focusing on the ESG needs , a manager using ESG products was not focusing attention on the client 's financial goals - which is the basic fiduciary requirement under DOL rules . <h> Fiduciary duty <p> Under the Employee Retirement Income Security Act ( ERISA ) , which is the law underlying the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ must run the plan solely in the interest of plan participants and beneficiaries , for the exclusive purpose of paying employee benefits and plan expenses . Failure to meet these standards can result in litigation and regulatory action for plan executives and management and can include significant personal liability for such individuals . <h> The new rule <p> The new rule , according to the DOL , is not explicitly focused on prohibiting ESG investing ; rather it requires that plan fiduciaries select investments and investment strategies based solely on factors that are likely to have a material effect on the risk or return of an investment . Opponents of the rule , of which there are many ( more than 96% of the 8,730 comments ) , say that the rule will discourage plans subject to ERISA from offering ESG investments or using ESG factors in making investment decisions for plans they manage .
