<p> There 's a whole genre of dystopian fiction that imagines what nature would be like if humans disappeared from it . In The New Wilderness , a book by Diane Cook that made the 2020 Booker Prize Shortlist , twenty humans are given permission to live without any modern technology in The Wilderness , the last tract of land on earth not to be corrupted by a sprawling , polluting human population . It 's easy to see how Cook , in imagining this pristine , preserved and precious place , could have looked at Joan Nelson 's landscape paintings . Nelson 's paintings are of our world , but also , better than it . <p> " I think of a world in which humans do n't exist , and I 'm a little gleeful about it , " Nelson told me . Her paintings , which are often copies of her earlier paintings , originate from various sources , including photographs of landscapes Nelson finds through Google searches . Her family would sometimes take day trips into the mountains . " That was where the magic was , " she says . " The intense beauty , the smell of pine trees . I still have strong emotional ties to those places . " <p> Nelson loves looking at a landscape , and testing what is possible . " If you want a green sky , there can be a green sky , " she says . " In a painting , you can do things that defy gravity . Nelson uses materials like glitter and mascara , and techniques like stippling , to create effects that suggest atmosphere . <p> Noticeably absent are human beings , and their structures -- bridges , lookouts , retreats , country houses . Nelson is deeply concerned about the state of the environment due to climate change . Her paintings are a sort of consolation prize in case we make our own world uninhabitable .
