<p> Half the corals on Australia 's Great Barrier Reef have died over the past 25 years , scientists said Wednesday , warning that climate change is irreversibly destroying the World Heritage-listed underwater ecosystem . <p> A study published in the Proceedings of the Royal Society Journal found an alarming rate of decline across all sizes of corals since the mid-1990s on the vast reef that lies off the country 's northeastern coast . <p> Larger species , such as branching and table-shaped corals , have been worst affected -- almost disappearing from the far northern reaches of the reef , researchers found . <p> " They make the nooks and crannies that fish and other creatures depend on , so losing big three-dimensional corals changes the broader ecosystem .
