<p> When Susan Cook-Patton was doing a postdoc in forest restoration at the Smithsonian Environmental Research Center in Maryland seven years ago , she says , she helped plant 20,000 trees along Chesapeake Bay . It was a salutary lesson . " The ones that grew best were mostly ones we did n't plant , " she remembers . " They just grew naturally on the ground we had set aside for planting . Lots popped up all around . It was a good reminder that nature knows what it is doing . " <p> What is true for Chesapeake Bay is probably true in many other places , says Cook-Patton , now at the Nature Conservancy . Sometimes , we just need to give nature room to grow back naturally . Her conclusion follows a new global study that finds the potential for natural forest regrowth to absorb atmospheric carbon and fight climate change has been seriously underestimated . <p> Tree planting is all the rage right now .
