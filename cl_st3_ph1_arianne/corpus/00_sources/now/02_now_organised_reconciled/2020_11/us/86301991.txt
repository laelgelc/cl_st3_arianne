<p> Researchers at the Cornell Lab of Ornithology found evidence that suggests many species of birds follow green vegetation when they migrate . If birds use the vegetation to guide migration , climate change that impacts plants could also affect birds ' migratory patterns . <p> The new study , which is published in the Journal of Animal Ecology , would not have been possible without birdwatchers across North America . The researchers used over a decade of bird sighting data reported by volunteers through eBird , the lab 's worldwide bird observation reporting platform , and compared the bird data with vegetation data from satellites . <p> Lead author Frank La Sorte said eBird is the largest citizen scientist database in the world . <p> La Sorte said migration is risky because it means going into a region with new threats while expending a lot of energy . Animals want to migrate in a way that allows them to get more resources as they go .
