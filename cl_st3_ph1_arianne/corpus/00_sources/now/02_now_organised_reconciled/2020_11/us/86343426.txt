But when the federal government -- even under Barack Obama -- has tried to calculate the true costs of global warming , it has implicitly assumed that future generations are not worth very much . It has also failed to assign value to iconic historical and cultural resources . The Biden administration has the opportunity to fix that . <p> Obama 's Environmental Protection Agency tried to calculate what it called the " social cost of carbon , " or " SCC , " described as " a measure , in dollars , of the long-term damage done by a ton of carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) emissions in a given year . " They recognized that it would be important to have such a metric when developing regulations designed to reduce carbon emissions . Economists assume that future generations will be richer , so a dollar will be worth less to the people of the future than to us today . Even in the short term , most people would rather have a dollar today than , say , $1.15 in ten years . Economists have developed discount rates to reflect these preferences . <p> But carbon emissions cause ongoing damage for thousands of years . And when you apply discount rates to such multigenerational issues , you are essentially saying that the lives and experience of people in the future are worth less than those of people today . <p> The Obama administration applied a 3 percent discount rate in developing the SCC , assuming that each year , a dollar in the future is worth 3 percent less than the year before . The Trump administration has used a 7 percent discount rate . The difference is dramatic .
