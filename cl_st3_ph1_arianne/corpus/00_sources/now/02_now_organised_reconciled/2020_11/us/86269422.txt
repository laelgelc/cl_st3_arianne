<h> COLUMN : We have a choice to be compassionate <p> America has a momentous choice to make in the next few days . No , I am not talking about the election . I am talking about how we will treat each other after the election . <p> Any election is cause for some angst and anxiety . But , as with everything else in 2020 , the negative mental health effects of this election cycle are on some form of steroids unknown to even the Russian Olympic squad . <p> America , on the eve of this election , is a nation on the brink of tearing itself apart . <p> Polarization of our politics , which has been on the uptick since the 1990s , made a sharp turn toward self-destructive levels in the run-up to the 2016 election , and has redoubled in 2020 . But , place that election into the unique sewer tornado that is 2020 , and , well , the effects are multiplied . <p> Perhaps the most contentious and consequential election since the Civil War also happens to be set in the midst of our nation 's cataclysmically impotent response to a worldwide pandemic . <p> More than 228,000 Americans already have died , and many families , including Clan Neal , are mourning a loved one who lost their lives to the pandemic . <p> Economic uncertainty , disrupted routines , social isolation , fear of the virus and an increase of substance abuse compounds all of this . <p> Racial strife has rocked our nation this year , and many Americans continue to carry unseen and unhealed wounds of injustice and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ about the effects of climate change , which call into question the survivability of society , if not humanity itself . <p> Here in Oklahoma , you can tie all that up with the ugly bow of an ice storm , power outages and property damage . <p> Take all that together , and it should be no surprise Americans are suffering addiction and mental illness at unprecedented levels . <p> According to the CDC , as of August , mental illness has seen a threefold increase in America in 2020 .
