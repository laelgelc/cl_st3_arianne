<h> California catches a break from worst-ever year for wildfires with cooler weather , but the threat remains <p> LOS ANGELES -- After being ravaged by wildfires , California has won a temporary reprieve . <p> Temperatures plummeted , rain pelted much of the state , and snow dusted the mountains in the past week . Cooler weather , however , is little comfort to firefighters who know the state is still in the prime of what many still call fire season . <p> Firefighters , however , are determined not to call it that . <p> " We are trying to break away from the terminology , " said Battalion Chief Issac Sanchez , spokesman for the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection , or Cal Fire . " There really was n't a flip of the switch . " <p> In the past , Southern California 's fire season ran from about May or June to November , he said . Now , it seems as if wildfires can break out just about any time of the year . Five of the six of the largest wildfires in California history started in August and September . Collectively they burned more than 2,500 square miles , an area about the size of Delaware . The fires killed 22 people . <p> While fire departments may not refer to fire season per se in their public declarations , their leases of firefighting aircraft and other contract services still focus on the most dangerous months of the year . <p> For more than two decades , for example , Los Angeles County has leased a pair of Bombardier CL-415 SuperScooper aircraft for 90 days at a time from Quebec , Canada , to augment its own fleet of water-dropping helicopters .
