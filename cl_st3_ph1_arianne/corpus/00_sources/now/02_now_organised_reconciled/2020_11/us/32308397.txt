<h> Opinion : Degradation of Quebec forests is costly to allBack to video <p> The author , Miguel Ouellette of the Montreal Economic Institute , argued that we are not cutting enough to take advantage of the forests ' regeneration capacity . He said that this supposed policy failure compounds the economic suffering of the regions , which are hard hit by COVID-19 . Further , he said , the Quebec forest industry actually removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere , helping combat climate change . <p> These perspectives appear to be based on misunderstandings about the carbon storage and regeneration capacity of Quebec 's forests . <p> Since the 1980s , Quebec 's forest industry has expanded rapidly northward to tap into the high-quality timbers of the boreal zone . Since 1990 , nearly one-quarter of the boreal forest has been cut , especially the highest-quality stands . The boreal forest may be vast , but it is also very slow-growing : in a 25-year-old cut we recently visited , the tallest trees were barely the size of a person and not much thicker than a toonie . As logging pushes north , it leaves behind degraded forests on a scale never before known to the landscape . With compacted and disturbed soils , these forests often have diminished growth potential and lowered resilience to shocks . And the science is clear that forest degradation is pushing woodland caribou to extinction . Quebec is banking the future of the industry and the regions on a risky experiment on our forests . <p> Degraded forests are less efficient at absorbing carbon from the atmosphere . Canada 's forests have been a net carbon source for the past 20 years , our third-biggest source of emissions ahead of heavy industry . This period coincides with the accelerating logging in the boreal forest . <p> In short , the ongoing rapid degradation of vast areas of boreal forest has many consequences . It raises questions about whether the industry will exhaust the high-quality old-growth timbers it depends on before old cuts sufficiently regrow , which would devastate the regions ' economies . It worsens Canada 's already-poor performance on climate change .
