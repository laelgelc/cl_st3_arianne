<h> Trees are losing leaves earlier because of climate change <p> Trees will start to shed their leaves earlier as the planet warms , a new study has suggested , contradicting previous assumptions that warming temperatures are delaying the onset of fall . <p> Every year , in a process known as senescence , the leaves of deciduous trees turn yellow , orange and red as they suspend growth and extract nutrients from foliage , before falling from the tree ahead of winter . Leaf senescence also marks the end of the period during which plants absorb carbon dioxide through photosynthesis . <p> Global warming has resulted in longer growing seasons -- spring leaves are emerging in European trees about two weeks earlier , compared with 100 years ago , researchers said . <p> " Previous models assumed that because autumns will get warmer and warmer over the coming century , autumn will get delayed -- growing seasons will overall get longer , and autumn will get delayed by two to three weeks , " ecosystem ecologist Constantin Zohner said . <p> However , Zohner and a team of researchers @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ We actually predict by the end of the century , leaves might even fall off three to six days earlier , " Zohner , a corresponding author on the paper published Friday in the journal Science , added . <p> Using a combination of field observations , laboratory tests and modeling , experts studied data that tracked six European deciduous tree species -- European horse chestnut , silver birch , European beech , European larch , English oak and rowan -- over the last six decades . <p> Increases in spring and summer productivity that come as a result of elevated carbon dioxide , temperature and light levels actually drive trees to lose their leaves earlier , the experts found . <p> It had been assumed , Zohner said , that fall temperatures and day length were the main environmental factors that cause trees to lose their leaves . Now , researchers have identified a third factor -- a " self-constraining " productivity . <p> " What we now see is there is this third huge mechanism that is going on -- the ( tree ) productivity is self constraining @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ spring and summer -- if the plant absorbs more CO2 in synthesis through spring and summer , they lose their leaves earlier , " he said . <p> " This is a mechanism we also see in humans -- if you start eating earlier , you will be full earlier , " he said . <p> The findings , Zohner said , have shown that trees have productivity constraints . <p> " We can not just put more and more CO2 in the atmosphere and ( expect ) trees will just do so much more -- there are limits , " he said .
