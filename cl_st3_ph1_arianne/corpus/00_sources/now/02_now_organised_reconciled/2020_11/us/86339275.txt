( AP Photo/Charlie Riedel , file ) ASSOCIATED PRESS <p> By Guest Columnist , cleveland.com <p> CLEVELAND -- Our current divide between Democrats and Republicans may feel insurmountable . Yet in Britain in 1710 , the split between Whigs and Tories was so intense that , while only 3% of the population had the right to vote , many encountered violence as they tried " to come and give their votes for a whig . This era in British history also shows us how we can move forward today in addressing urgent contemporary issues such as climate change by using the language of political moderation and pragmatism . <p> The split between Whigs and Tories in November 1710 was heightened by the impeachment of Henry Sacheverell , a Tory preacher in the Church of England , in March that year . The Whig majority in parliament voted to impeach Sacheverell ( who had preached against the Glorious Revolution of 1688 , which made parliament more powerful than the monarch and introduced religious toleration ) but lost the war of public opinion . Mobs rose up in support of the preacher and against the Whigs , many of whom lost their seats in parliament in the November 1710 election , which the Tories won by a margin of almost two to one . <p> High Church Tories like Sacheverell were opposed to changing traditional hierarchical government and resentful of religious toleration @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Maryland has described the phenomenon of people wanting " tighter social norms " when they " feel threatened . " <p> In 1710 , many Tories , even those could not vote , felt resentful of wealthy Presbyterians who were not members of the Church of England ( but now had freedom to practice their religion ) . They also felt threatened by German immigrants and fearful of starvation after a terrible harvest . Sacheverell 's supporters broke into public rioting ( somewhat paradoxically ) in their demand for stronger monarchical control and less toleration of those who were marked by difference . <p> Rachel Carnell , a professor of English at Cleveland State University , is the author of " Backlash : Libel , Impeachment , and Populism in the Reign of Queen Anne , " just published by University of Virginia Press . <p> After the death of Queen Anne in 1714 , the Whigs changed their tone . They regained power under George I and held it until the late 18th century using moderate rather than revolutionary language . While during the impeachment trial of Henry Sacheverell @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 1688 , by 1715 they were promoting the Revolution of 1688 as more " Glorious " than revolutionary . <p> The language of moderation , as the Whigs learned after 1710 , can be the best tool for long-term progress . President-elect Joe Biden , of course , has been speaking that language for his entire political career . Many of his supporters hope he will lead by appealing to the broad center of America . <p> For those who are impatient for faster change , however , recent events demonstrate that using less radical language can also be the mechanism for swift political transformation . <p> After the devastating tornado of 2007 , the Republican town of Greensburg , Kansas , was rebuilt to strict codes of environmental sustainability . Those responsible for effecting this change were , notably , careful to avoid the radical language of climate activists . <p> The initial attitude of some Greensburg natives was summed up by a bumper sticker that read , " May you ecological bastards freeze to death in the dark . " The visionaries who brought in energy efficiency were @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ not tree-hugger green , but economic green , " as John Janssen , city council president at the time the tornado hit , explained recently to The Washington Post . He added , " Ramming stuff down people 's throats -- especially in this part of the world -- does n't work . " <p> As the incoming Biden administration seeks to address the multipronged crises of a global pandemic , an economic downturn , systemic racial injustice , and a ticking clock on the climate crisis , it has every reason to plan comprehensive legislation on par with FDR 's New Deal . In order to achieve such change in a nation as divided as ours , however , the Biden administration must couch its goals as practical , not as a cultural revolution that sparks resistance in the streets .
