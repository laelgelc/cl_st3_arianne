No matter where you look , it seems like Trump and Biden voters really do n't understand each other . <p> Listening to what the other side has to say , whether you 're talking about immigration or the environment , can seem like peering into an alternate universe . " Democrats speak of empathy , protests , and the " climate crisis . " <p> And where their vocabulary does overlap , definitions have parted ways . One part of the answer : Words have simply failed us . <h> Poles apart <p> The polarization of our language seems to be a relatively new phenomenon . Last year , a study led by researchers from Stanford and Brown analyzed congressional speeches from 1873 , not long after 2 percent of the population died in the Civil War , until 2016 , when Trump was elected president . What they found was that polarized language had increased sharply in the 1990s , following a century of remaining relatively steady . " Democrats and Republicans now speak different languages to a far greater degree than ever before , " the authors wrote . <p> So what happened ?
