<p> Last Monday , Quebec Premier Francois Legault announced a ban on new gas-powered cars and SUVs by 2035 . On Thursday , Prime Minister Justin Trudeau 's government introduced Bill C-12 , aiming for net zero carbon emissions in Canada by 2050 . The policies , Legault and Trudeau stated , were born out of a need to address climate change . Legault said , " We have a duty to the next generation . " Trudeau echoed , " Climate change remains one of the greatest challenges of our times . " <p> These words rang hollow . According to the world 's leading scientific body , the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) , we have only a decade to substantially shift course if we want to have a shot at a sustainable future . I am a climate scientist , researching the global carbon cycle and greenhouse gas emissions policy . I am fully aware of the actions governments must take if they are serious about the climate crisis . And neither Legault nor Trudeau 's recent announcements are sufficient . <p> Bill C-12 is too little , too late . It does n't illustrate how Canada will meet its already woefully inadequate emissions-reductions goals . As someone who studies how greenhouse gas emissions are counted , I am wary of a plan to have " net zero " emissions without numbers to back those assertions . I am also skeptical of the advisory body that Bill C-12 will establish , as it is currently proposed , because Trudeau has not committed to fossil-free energy representation . Many of the world 's largest oil companies knew of climate change risks decades ago , yet continued to fund climate-denier lobbies . First Nations communities are among the most affected by climate change , and many of them have repeatedly opposed the construction of more pipelines that harm their communities . Bill C-12 has important potential , but it needs to be informed by science , respectful of Indigenous rights and grounded in justice . If Trudeau is serious about climate action , he must amend it accordingly . <p> Quebec 's announcement similarly fell short . Banning the sale of new gas-powered cars is an important step , but it is n't enough . For starters , the policy does n't address used cars . And vehicle-based fossil fuel emissions are only the tip of the iceberg : Where are Legault 's plans for the energy transition we need , one that will extend to all aspects of society ?
