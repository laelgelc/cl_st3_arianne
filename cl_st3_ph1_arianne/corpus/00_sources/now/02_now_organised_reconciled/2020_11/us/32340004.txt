<h> Rubenstein : Catching our collective breath <p> As a rabbi and spiritual leader , I operate according to time cycles . The seven-day cycle of weekday and Sabbath . The monthly cycle of the waxing and waning of the moon . The yearly cycle of holidays . The seven-year cycle , the Sabbatical year , described in the Bible . Each of these cycles has a built-in ebb and flow between work and rest , action and contemplation , striving and gratitude . These cycles allow me to periodically take stock of where I am and approach my time mindfully and proactively . <p> Without time markers , we can easily be overtaken by the 24-hour news cycle , which allows no room for rest or correcting course . In the past four years , the pressures of the constant news cycle became even more acute as the president 's tweets and frequently outlandish claims ( themselves " news " ) grabbed our attention . Something from the president always demanded immediate reaction . <p> There are many things to be said about the election of a new administration @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ particularly note one thing that is likely to be overlooked by many of the pundits . The new president-elect 's daily behavior and every turn of phrase is certain to be less newsworthy than that of the incumbent he defeated . <p> This presents a challenge and an opportunity . On the one hand , America can not just " get back to brunch . " We can not even take for granted that the transition of power will occur smoothly ; we have to be vigilant . And once the president- and vice-president-elect are in office , we do not get to disengage from the glaring issues that were present before and during this waning administration . We will need significant public activism to motivate them to aggressively address the major issues they named in their acceptance speeches : pandemic , economy , structural racism and climate change , not to mention the priorities that they did n't name . Vast inequalities of health , housing and educational access , our country 's shameful treatment of veterans and the underservicing of rural communities , among many other issues . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ about the president 's latest tweets , hopefully we will now have an opportunity to pause , breathe and mindfully relate to time . We can choose what our priorities will be . In Seven Habits of Highly Effective People , Stephen Covey wrote about the necessity of distinguishing between what is important and what is merely urgent . After four years of constant urgency , we get to reclaim and redefine what is important . <p> I hope that the people of Lane County will continue to organize together , to build coalitions , to set goals and work to achieve them . We 've done it during this presidency , when every tweet demanded a response . The challenge will be : Can we transition from a constant , reactive energy to a more proactive approach ? Can we stay engaged , but from a place of mindful prioritizing ? <p> The Jewish Bible ( Exodus 34:21 ) teaches , " Six days shall you labor , and the seventh day you shall rest . " Both the work and the rest are commanded ; we need both @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and months to come . But throughout it , we should take moments to rest and catch our breath . <p> Ruhi Sophia Motzkin Rubenstein is the rabbi of Temple Beth Israel in Eugene and writes a monthly column for The Register-Guard.
