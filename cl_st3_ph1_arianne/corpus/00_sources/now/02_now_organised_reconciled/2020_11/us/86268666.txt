<p> Financial advisors are engaging clients as they navigate a year of political turmoil , a medical pandemic , social unrest as a result of race relations and a volatile stock market . Dr. Joseph Coughlin , the director of the AgeLab at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology , says that it is important to engage clients on these important issues . <p> " If you do n't have conversations about this , you do n't have to worry about having conversations at all with those clients after the pandemic , " Coughlin said during the Forbes SHOOK Wealth Management Virtual Summit . <p> Coughlin and his counterparts at MIT have done research finding that clients want to know whether their financial advisor " gets " them and cares about them . He warns that advisors need to address issues that may seem tangential to them but can be paramount and personal to clients . For many clients , it is important to get personal before getting to portfolios . When approaching these issues it is important to note the fact that they are interconnected , from climate change to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to Coughlin . <p> The pandemic has accelerated an emerging trend of nesting behavior , Coughlin adds . This has led to spending focused on homes as well as things that are local , personal and trustworthy , and creates another important topic of conversation to address with clients , around how and where they want to live in retirement , an important dynamic of how they see their future . <p> " If you have n't had these conversations , you have not displayed that you care about these clients and , by the way , this is the new normal , " Coughlin adds . <p> An advisor also has to be prepared for the new reality in which clients are constantly " on . " Clients now want more around planning for longevity and constant contact through e-mail , on the phone and face to face . This all comes down to making an effort to show clients you care about them as much as their portfolio , Coughlin says . <p> While older clients accept the more classic paradigm of quarterly or biannual portfolio reviews , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ X and younger baby boomers , those in their 40s and 50s , are looking for advisors to be " more of a coach , educator and agenda-setter . " This boils down to more frequent , shorter conversations about their money as well as other aspects of their life from home care to caregiving . <p> Coming out of the remote working reality of the present , Coughlin says that there will be new expectations but that high technology will never replace high touch service .
