<h> Millennials get their political say during week-long UnConvention in NYC <p> Women from St. Louis , MO area protest Trump remarks about women outside Washington University campus where second presidential debate occurred on October 9 , 2016 . <p> Credit : <p> Dan Waldman <p> Share <p> Do n't expect balloons or confetti in New York October 17-21 when The UnConvention comes to town . Instead there will be comic relief , straight talk and a focus on issues that young voters care about . <p> Young adults have the most at risk in the coming US election . They will live for more than half a century with the consequences of today 's policies on jobs , climate , immigration , education and security . Yet their voices , views and issues have barely been heard in this campaign . That is changing . <p> This article is part of The UnConvention , coverage and conversation that highlights the issues and voices of young voters , in partnership with 92Y and Mic . <p> The UnConvention -- a partnership of PRI , Mic and the 92nd Street Y @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ election through reporting , quizzes , polls , social videos and conversations . Now the UnConvention comes to New York City , with a week of live events . <p> On Monday , October 17 , actor and former Daily Show comedian Aasif Mandvi tackles Immigration , Identity &amp; the 2016 Election at the 92nd Street Y. His guests include Israel Ortega , national commentator on conservative issues , especially immigration ; Eric K Ward , veteran civil rights organizer focusing on immigration and equality ; and Negin Farsad , stand-up comic , filmmaker and author of " How To Make White People Laugh . " <p> On Tuesday , October 18 , a panel explores Political Correctness From Left to Right at The Greene Space at WNYC . Todd Zwillich , Washington correspondent for The Takeaway , will lead a candid conversation challenging the notion of what is ' politically correct ' on both sides of the political spectrum . Helping him will be CNN political commentator and The Daily Beast contributor Sally Kohn , Mic senior staff writer Jamila King and associate editor at Reason Robby Soave . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ on Wednesday , October 19 will be at the UnConvention Debate Watching Party at Housing Works . An all-star line-up of New York City stand-up comics will square off over the issues in a mock debate , followed by livestreaming of the final Clinton/Trump showdown from Las Vegas . Participants include comedians Aparna Nancherla , Connor Raitliff , Jo Firestone and Keisha Zollar . <p> Thursday , October 20 brings a more serious topic to the UnConvention . A World without Borders : Data in the 21st Century is a conversation with White House Chief Data Scientist DJ Patil . He 'll talk with Zwillich about the purpose of all of this information and how is it revolutionizing everything from healthcare to civil rights to security . <p> All the events will be live-streamed from PRI 's Facebook page . Join us live or virtually . Balloons and confetti optional . <p> The Millennial generation has a lot of power to determine its future . In fact , Millennials have as much voting power as the Boomers and will soon surpass them . That is , if they vote . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the last presidential election , just 46 percent of eligible Millennials voted compared to 69 percent of Boomers , according to the Pew Research Center . Even when Barack Obama was first elected , a high watermark for voter participation , only half of eligible Millennials voted . <p> Many young voters say they want nothing to do with polarized politics and stage-managed conventions . They tend to support movements -- from Occupy to Black Lives Matter to Bernie Sanders -- rather than parties . And 50 percent called themselves Independents in 2014 , according to Pew . <p> And this generation is the first to see themselves as global citizens , connected with their peers around the world when it comes to jobs , immigration , human rights or climate change . Conventional politics is a non-starter . Party conventions are a relic of the past .
