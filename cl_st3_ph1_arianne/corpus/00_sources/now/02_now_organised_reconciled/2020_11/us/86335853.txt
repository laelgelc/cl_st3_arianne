<p> Petrol driven cars are set to disappear from Britain 's roads rather sooner than expected . Under the government 's updated " Green Industrial Revolution " plan , every new car sold from 2030 onwards must be either electric or an approved hybrid . This replaces a previous deadline of 2040 . <p> All except the most hardened climate change skeptics will see this as a good thing , but a rapid transition to battery-powered mobility does pose certain short-term problems , not least in terms of providing a workable and user-friendly charging infrastructure . <p> As things stand , even a rapid charging point will take around thirty minutes to pump up a battery from empty to full . Arguably that 's not too bad , but it compares unfavorably with the two or three minutes required to fill a petrol tank . Yes , you 're helping to save the planet but a half-hour wait is less than convenient . <p> The alternative is to charge at home . Not an appealing prospect . <p> One answer is an extensive network of on-street charging points that anyone can use . You park at home or close to a place of work , find the nearest point on the street , plug in and leave your car there until the battery is full . Simplicity itself . But here 's the problem . Providing enough street charging points will be a massive undertaking . We are talking about serious infrastructure and infrastructure is notoriously expensive to build . <p> Founded in 2017 , Connected Kerb aims to be a key enabler in the transition to electric cars in Britain .
