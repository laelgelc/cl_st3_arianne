<h> Column : When will children become central to our concerns ? <p> Kent Johnson <p> Eight-year-old Ben 's dad lost his job . " Though we have enough money , I do n't know if we have enough to last , " he wrote the editors of Highlights magazine . " My world is turning upside down , " 10-year-old Allison wrote from the path of a California wildfire . From Alaska , 8-year-old Anna wanted to know how she could stop worrying about COVID-19 . Another reader plaintively requested " an honest answer " about when schools would open . " I 'm 11 , " they wrote , " and I really want to finish fifth grade ! " <p> News flash for anyone who 's ever whispered , " Do n't let the kids hear " : The kids know . Throughout three debates , the candidates who aspire to our nation 's highest office sparred over the economy , health care , climate change , the Supreme Court and , of course , the coronavirus pandemic . Why do n't our leaders care about them ? <p> More than any other group , children -- in particular , children of color -- have suffered at the hands of the coronavirus pandemic and its economic fallout . Roughly 60% of households with children are reporting serious financial hardship -- with the greatest burden landing on our Black and Latino kids . A record 14 million are not getting enough food . <p> Child poverty , already higher than in other developed nations , threatens to be worse than during the Great Recession . Which means that the politicians who actually follow through do so because they understand it as a moral obligation and as the single most effective way to ensure our country 's economic future . <p> The advocacy group First Focus Campaign for Children names just 20 senators and 40 representatives as Champions for Children each year , a distinction won by fighting -- sometimes against their colleagues -- to prioritize children 's interests .
