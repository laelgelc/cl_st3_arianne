<h> Guest View : Why trust science ? It 's the method , man <p> As the COVID-19 pandemic has unfolded and climate change is upon us we often hear or read that we should " trust science " or " use science " to make good decisions . <p> But why should we trust science ? <p> There are several reasons , one being that science is based on a formal process to develop valid and useful information pertinent to the issue at hand . The scientific method involves observing a phenomenon , speculating as to its cause ( forming a hypothesis ) and testing it with experiments , additional observations or models ( analytical representations of the components and processes of the phenomenon ) . <p> Scientists then use statistics to determine the probability that their results support -- or refute -- the hypothesis . We all use this approach unconsciously , minus the models and statistics , when , for example , we adjust a recipe . We might speculate that adding basil will make the dish taste better , and then we do the experiment . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is self-correcting . That is , errors are corrected , weaknesses are exposed and filled in , and rough edges are smoothed out as more information ( data ) is gathered . We 've seen this regarding the efficacy of wearing masks during the pandemic . Initially it was thought that masks would provide little protection , but as data accumulated from various sources , it became clear that wearing masks protected the wearer and those nearby . <p> Dr. Anthony Fauci has been questioned and even criticized for changing his assessment of masks , but this is exactly the trajectory that most science takes : from original speculation through data and analysis , honing the results to yield consensus ( but not necessarily to adoption ) on the outcomes <p> A component of self-correction is peer review . Throughout the process of the scientific method , ideas , experimental designs , analyses and publication of the results ( and more recently , the publication of the actual raw data from the project ) are verified , corrected or refuted by others in the field who are knowledgeable about the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ every manuscript and grant proposal I wrote came back dripping with red ink pointing to inconsistencies and errors , in all components of the process ( as well as grammatical flaws ) . An additional component of self-correction is repeatability . If the results can be repeated by other scientists in other locations , confidence in the outcomes increase . <p> Another reason to trust science is that scientists are rewarded explicitly for the accuracy and validity of their ideas and results . There is no reward , and maybe even ridicule , for sloppy reasoning or propagating misinformation . Rewards come from personal satisfaction for contributing to our knowledge base , accolades by colleagues ( e.g. , Nobel Prize ) , publications in respected journals , grants to continue their research , jobs in their favored institutions , and , in some cases , patents on inventions . <p> If what I 've written is legitimate , we must then ask , " why trust scientists ? " Scientists are human so , like all people , we make mistakes and unconsciously exhibit confirmation bias , the tendency to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ evidence that challenges it . Unsurprisingly , some scientists fudge their data purposely , or even lie when they view the reward to be greater than telling the truth . <p> Think of the scientists working for tobacco companies who misrepresented information about the dangers of smoking . But the realm of science is filled with skeptics willing and capable of refuting misinformation , thereby stoking the self-correcting mechanisms of the discipline . Not only does the practice of science put a premium on seeking truth but it is actually a system that identifies and rewards accurate results based on the predominance of evidence rather than agendas or idiosyncratic beliefs . <p> Decisions based on the scientific approach are sometimes described as evidence-based . That is , based on what has been determined to be accurate and reliable . Evidence , in the form of data , can be complied into information which can be integrated into knowledge . <p> Simply increasing our understanding of natural systems is valuable . But knowledge is a critically important resource used to educate and the improve our health ( clean environments , medicines @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ such as buildings , bridges , airplanes , etc . ) . <p> Thus , it is important that the information be accurate and reliable . This is precisely why science , which provides a path to the veracity , is so valuable . <p> Jim Reichman is a retired ecology professor living in Eugene . He can be reached at ojreichman@gmail.com.
