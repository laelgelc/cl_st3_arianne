<p> Petition had accused Prayeja Realty of multiple eco violations in building a project near Sinhagad Road <p> The principal bench of the National Green Tribunal ( NGT ) on Thursday directed the developer of a mega housing project in the city to deposit interim compensation of Rs 5 crore with the Maharashtra Pollution Control Board ( MPCB ) within a deadline of two months . <p> The developer , Prayeja Realty Private Limited Company , is engaged in constructing Prayeja City , a 12-wing complex housing over 600 flats , at Vadgaon Budruk near Sinhagad Road . While doing so , several trees were allegedly hacked for the project between 2017 and 2019 , but the developer failed to replant t hem as per the norms , causing damage to the environment . It has further been alleged that groundwater was extracted without prior permission from the relevant authorities , and sewage generated from the project was also not treated , which is another mandate as per environment rules . <p> While issuing an order for the fine , the green court also directed that no further construction on @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> The bench took this strict action after receiving a report from an expert committee , comprising officials of the State Environment Impact Assessment Authority ( SEIAA ) , MPCB , and the Pune Municipal Corporation 's ( PMC ) commissioner . <p> It was in June this year that a resident of Pune , civil engineer Tanaji Balasaheb Gambhire , had filed the petition in this regard before the NGT . He had demanded compensation from the developer for irreparable losses to the environment due to flaws committed while constructing Prayeja City . <p> As per petitioner Gambhire ; ( inset ) , the developer hacked many trees while constructing Prayeja City , but did not replant any in their stead ; there was also allegedly no treatment of sewage generated in the project and groundwater was extracted without permission , amid other lack of compliance ; PHOTO : NIKHIL GHORPADE <p> In his petition , Gambhire had claimed that the developer violated eco norms by not obtaining mandatory prior environmental clearance . He pointed out that although trees were felled , there was no replacement of them @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as per the mandate . The petitioner also mentioned that there is non-installation of a sewage treatment plant ( STP ) or solid waste treatment unit at the location , among other wrongdoings . All these violations , he said , have wreaked irreparable havoc on the environment . <p> During the hearing , advocate Nitin Lonkar -- who represents Gambhire -- submitted that the developer constructed the project without prior environmental clearance from the SEIAA or Union ministry of environment , forest and climate change ( MoEF&amp;CC ) . " Replantation was never done in lieu of the axed trees , which is mandatory to compensate for loss of greenery . The soil extracted from the land was also not preserved , which is a mandate , " he added . <p> During a previous hearing in this case in August this year , the bench had directed to constitute an expert committee , which subsequently submitted its report . The report states that the developer has not taken prior environmental clearance , or consent to establish and consent to operate under the Water ( Prevention and Control of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Prevention and Control of Pollution ) Act , 1981 . Further , a no-objection certificate ( NOC ) from the Central Ground Water Authority ( CGWA ) was also not obtained for groundwater extraction . <p> In the latest order , the bench of judicial members Adarsh Kumar Goel and SK Singh , and expert members including Dr SS Garbyal and Dr Nagin Nanda , has said , " In view of the facts , we constitute another joint committee comprising the Central PCB , MPCB and SEIAA to take further remedial action by way of removing the illegal construction and/or recovering compensations for violations , following due process of law . We further direct that the committee ensure that no further construction takes place without compliance to statutory norms . We direct the developer to deposit of interim compensation of Rs 5 crore with MPCB within two months to be used for restoration of environment , failing which the state PCB will be at liberty to take coercive measures . " <p> Contacted about this order , Sandeep Jani , director of Prayeja Realty , said , " The @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have so far complied with over 70 per cent environmental conditions and the remaining are underway .
