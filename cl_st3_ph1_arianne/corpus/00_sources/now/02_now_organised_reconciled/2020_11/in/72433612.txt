<h> Why the Indian Ocean is Earth 's Clearing-house for Ocean Warming <p> A composite satellite image of Earth centred on the Indian Ocean . Photo : NASA . <p> The world 's oceans take up more than 90% of the excess heat being trapped by the increasing volumes of greenhouse gases and other human activities such as deforestation and urbanisation . And the evidence thus far of the distribution of this heat in the oceans seems to indicate that the Indian Ocean in particular may be acting as the clearinghouse . <p> The Indian subcontinent has been a special place since it drifted northward across the equator and smashed into the Asian plate to create the Himalaya . The jutting of the Indian subcontinent down into the deep tropics created the monsoon circulation and abundant rainfall . These rains in turn affected the evolutionary pathways of flora and fauna over parts of East Africa and India . This transport , through the Indonesian throughflow , transports fresh , warm waters from the western Pacific into the eastern Indian Ocean , between Java and Australia . <p> The inflow from the Southern Ocean is part of the so-called global conveyor belt , which is a grand circulation of dense waters that form at the surface at just a few locations . They flow back to the North Atlantic via the Indonesian throughflow , down around the southern tip of Africa and up across the equator in the GIN Seas . <p> The unique monsoonal circulation also directly warms the Indian Ocean at the surface by atmospheric heating . <p> The resulting confluence of heat from the Pacific and Southern Oceans and from the atmosphere is what makes the Indian Ocean a bathtub of warm waters -- this warm is also what fuels the monsoon circulation and supplies the 200 lakh crore buckets of water dumped on the subcontinent @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ it , the singular nature of the Indian Ocean seems to extend into the global warming process as well . As the Pacific has warmed and the trade winds have strengthened , excess heat is being pumped into the Indian Ocean via the Indonesian throughflow . At the same time , the winds are pushing warmer waters on the surface of the Southern Ocean all the way down below , to be piped into the Indian Ocean as part of the global conveyor belt . <p> As it happens , a stunning new finding has also demonstrated that the Southern Ocean warming is able to reach the tropical Indian Ocean in only about 10 years . These results are set to be published by the Journal of Geophysical Research . <p> The Indian Ocean is thus gathering additional heat from the Pacific and the Southern Oceans and exerting an influence in turn on the Pacific Ocean and the global conveyor belt .
