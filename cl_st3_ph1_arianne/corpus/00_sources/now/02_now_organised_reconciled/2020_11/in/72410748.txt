<h> Trump may be out , but not Trumpism <p> Gyan Prakash on why lessons from the US polls resonate around the world . <p> Phew ! With Biden squeaking through , people anxious about the outcome of the election can re-shelve their razors . The past four Trump years have been exhausting , with daily scandals of corruption , racist outrages , the bending of the law , violation of norms , and attacks on just common decency . The Trump nightmare is over and US citizens can finally exhale . But , given the nail-biting finish , many frustrated observers will want the pollsters to be tarred and feathered . <p> We will have to wait for the granular data on voting demographics from each state to fully interpret the meaning of the election , but we can arrive at certain broad conclusions . But the nail-biting election suggests that Americans are almost evenly and stubbornly divided . <p> Trump successfully incited divisions and forged a cult-like following . The voters in the Democratic primaries , particularly the Blacks in the South chose Biden decisively , tipping the scale against Bernie Sanders . As Biden began building a platform uniting different factions in the party , protests exploded on the streets during the summer over the police murder of George Floyd . Trump may have brought white supremacy out in the open , but the Republicans since Richard Nixon have deployed it strategically . It all came to a head with Obama 's election in 2008 .
