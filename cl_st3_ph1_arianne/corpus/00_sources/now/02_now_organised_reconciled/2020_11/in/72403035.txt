<h> Remember , remember the fifth of November <p> Earlier this year I was reunited with a childhood friend I 'd never expected to meet again . I bring it up for two reasons . The first is to spread the word that anyone can have some unexpected good luck even in this loveless pandemic year . The second is that this friend loves graphic novels , of which I 've read very few . So this column is inspired by her . <p> Fifteen years ago , a minor controversy erupted over the hit movie V for Vendetta , a story about a romantic freedom fighter in a mask who goes around bombing public institutions and murdering political leaders in a hellish dystopian state . The film was political , in that vividly optimistic way Hollywood comicbook movies always are . A powerful nation has been taken over by a white supremacist police state that exterminates minorities and dissenters in concentration camps . They are kept in power by an apathetic public drip-fed on propaganda and religious ritual . V is a victim of their brutality . V not only wants revenge , but an awakening . V believes in poetic , theatrical violence , but also in love , poetry , music and flowers -- all things outlawed or carefully controlled by the regime . <p> Moore and Lloyd created their story over the 1980s , a contentious time in the history of their country .
