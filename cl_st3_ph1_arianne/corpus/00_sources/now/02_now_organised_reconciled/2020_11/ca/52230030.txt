<h> Paving over Ontario 's wetlands paradise <p> Recently a " save our wetlands " petition from the Green Party of Ontario ( GPO ) found its way to my inbox . Southern Ontario has lost 75 per cent of its original wetlands , the GPO said , and Premier Doug Ford is selling off what 's left to deep-pocketed developers . <p> Apparently , with Ford 's latest giveaway , a prominent Progressive Conservative party donor will turn a 54-acre wetland in Durham Region into a warehouse . Even though the wetlands are protected , " Ford is abusing special zoning orders to sell them off , " the GPO says . <p> I signed the petition . Not because it came from the Green Party . But because I 'm a lifelong wetlands enthusiast . <p> As a young boy I spent countless hours exploring neighbourhood streams , ponds and marshes . Terms like " biodiversity " and " ecosystem " were n't in common usage then , but I think I intuited the value and necessity of the multitude of benefits wetlands provide . <p> Wetlands , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Throughout history they 've been misunderstood , underappreciated and maligned . <p> Folklore , legends , mythology and modern cinema , for example , are chockfull of images of wetlands , swamps especially , as malevolent places where evil lurks and death stalks . <p> For its part , the English language is fraught with idioms that disparage wetlands . He 's a " stick in the mud . " I 'm just " swamped . " Her name was " dragged through the mire . " <p> They also make landscapes more resilient to the anticipated impacts of climate change including flooding , drought and biodiversity loss . And they possess an enormous capacity to store carbon . <p> Wetlands have even been called " biological supermarkets . " They produce vast volumes of food that support a remarkable degree of biodiversity . <p> According to the Chicago-based The Wetlands Initiative ( TWI ) , " in terms of number and variety of species supported , ( globally ) wetlands are as rich as rainforests and coral reefs . " <p> Wetlands also contribute $14 billion annually in economic @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ including fish , blueberries , cranberries , timber and wild rice -- all are harvested from wetlands . <p> With attributes like these , surely wetlands should be protected and preserved , not destroyed . <p> Yet , under cover of the praise Premier Ford has received from many for his impassioned handling of Ontario 's COVID-19 response , his government has quietly continued the assault on wetlands ( and on the natural environment generally ) that it launched after coming to power in June 2018 . <p> Most recently -- on Nov. 5 -- the Ford government introduced new legislation proposing numerous changes to the Conservation Authorities Act . Ontario Nature , a conservation organization that protects wild species and spaces through conservation , education and public engagement , says the alterations will " severely curtail the role of Conservation Authorities in watershed planning and management . " <p> The legislation will " undermine efforts to conserve biodiversity and build community resilience to climate change , " Ontario Nature said . <p> The proposed changes are buried out of ready sight in Bill 229 , an omnibus budget bill @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the Environmental Bill of Rights is overridden . <p> Conservation authorities -- there are 36 province-wide -- are understandably alarmed . Kim Gavine , general manager of Conservation Ontario , the umbrella organization for conservation authorities , told The Globe and Mail that the legislation takes away the role of the authorities to provide science-based input on development applications . <p> It also puts the power to issue permits for contentious developments directly into the hands of the minister of natural resources and forestry . The minister would have new power to overturn a conservation authority 's decision to refuse to issue a permit for development . <p> All of which means that important decisions could potentially be driven by commercial and political interests rather than what 's best for the natural environment . <p> Tim Grey , executive director of Environmental Defense , said the new legislation " strips the conservation authorities of any powers they had to protect communities from flooding , or protect wetlands or forests . " <p> " They can still give advice , " Grey said of the authorities , " but a developer @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have to listen to you . ' " <p> Ford may be worthy of commendation for his handling of the novel coronavirus , although an increasing number of commentators dispute this . But on wetlands and other natural infrastructure in Ontario , the premier seems either blind to or dismissive of their inherent value . <p> The lyrics of a famous song by iconic Canadian singer Joni Mitchell still ring true today : " They paved paradise , and put up a parking lot .
