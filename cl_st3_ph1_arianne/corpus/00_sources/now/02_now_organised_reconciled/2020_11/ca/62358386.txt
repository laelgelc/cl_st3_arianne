They want the school to stop funding rich corporations that are wrecking the planet . And many of them are bitterly disappointed UW 's board of governors refused to do this when it met last week . <p> But hold on a moment : The board deserves credit , not criticism , for taking its time on the contentious issue of divestment . You ca n't turn an ocean liner around in a couple of minutes , particularly if you want to shift it to wind power . <p> There 's a lot of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ $44.8 million worth of investments in fossil fuel and associated industry holdings . Some of that money is part of UW 's pension plan ; some is tied up with endowment funds . The bottom line is a lot of lives depend to a greater or lesser extent on how the university handles these investments . <p> At the same time , inaction should not be confused with necessary action born of careful consideration . Given UW 's strong commitment to fighting climate change -- including a vow to be carbon neutral by 2050 -- we think it 's inevitable that the university will eventually cut its ties with the fossil fuel industry . After all , UW signed the Responsible Investment Charter for universities in June , confirming its commitment to considering environmental and social factors when it invests . <p> So let the consideration begin : The first argument for divestment is moral and based on overwhelming scientific evidence . The carbon emissions that come from burning fossil fuels are the major reason the planet 's atmosphere is overheating , sea levels are rising and we 're witnessing @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ The only way to avoid global catastrophe , climate scientists agree , is by keeping between two-thirds and four-fifths of fossil fuels in the ground . That wo n't happen if investors keep paying to take it out . <p> The second argument for divestment is , perhaps surprisingly , economic . If Canada and the 194 other countries that signed the 2016 Paris Agreement on climate change keep their word and rapidly embrace alternative energy sources , the petroleum industry will become a shadow of its former self . <p> So if UW 's governors truly want to protect the school 's endowment and pension funds , they should realize investing in fossil fuels is becoming a risky business . Indeed , a recent study from Truzaar Dordi , a UW doctoral candidate in sustainable management , concluded the university potentially lost upwards of $20 million between 2011 and 2015 through its fossil fuel investments . <p> No one should expect a cold-turkey divestment from fossil fuels . On the plus side , perhaps divestment could spur more profitable investments in more environmentally friendly businesses . But this could take months , even years . <p> The divestment file will be back on the board of governor 's agenda when it meets next in February . The board should use the coming months to explore and weigh this important issue . And it should remember that in a ranking of more than 760 of the world 's top universities , based on the United Nations ' Sustainable Development Goals , UW is currently No. 16 . <p> It has a reputation to uphold .
