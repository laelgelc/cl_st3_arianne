<h> Let 's point Chrystia Freeland 's compass to nature <p> Finance Minister Chrystia Freeland recently used the reassuring metaphor of a government compass to guide Canadians through the uncharted waters of the pandemic to a safe harbour . <p> A compass works by detecting signals that nature sends from the Earth 's magnetic fields . Nature is indeed sending us strong signals , and , if we pay attention , the direction to a sustainable and healthy future for all Canadians is clear . <p> Globally , one million animal and plant species are threatened with extinction , many within decades , and more than ever before in human history . In Canada , which is the custodian of much of the Earth 's lands , forests , fresh water and ocean shorelines , more than half of wildlife populations are in decline . <p> What is also increasingly evident is how the destruction of nature imperils our economy and our shared future . <p> According to the recent World Economic Forum report on global risks , how humans grow and harvest food , produce energy , dispose of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ balance of clean air , water and life that we and all other species need to survive . Biodiversity loss is listed in the Forum 's report as among the Top 5 risks to the world economy in likelihood and in severity of impact . <p> Indeed , the emergence of COVID-19 itself , like all global health pandemics before it since the deadly influenza of 1918 , was driven by the disruption of the natural environment through human activity . <p> It 's important to note that our actions can also halt and reverse this destruction . Intact habitat can be protected , and degraded habitat restored . It will take time and cost money , but the costs of doing nothing are far greater . <p> And there is no better time to focus on the benefits of nature than now , when authorities are deciding how to invest in the time of COVID and its aftermath . <p> In her speech to the Toronto Global Forum in late October , Freeland said her government plans to lay " a foundation for a green economy , an innovation @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for all Canadians . " <p> Each of these elements can be strengthened through investments in nature , our ultimate shared foundation . Bold investments in nature will therefore be critical for Freeland to consider for the federal fiscal and economic update expected in the next few weeks . <p> Think land conservation and ocean protection , mass tree planting and restoring natural habitat , and investing in Indigenous Guardian programs so that traditional knowledge can help us monitor ecological health , maintain cultural sites , and protect sensitive areas and species . Investments in nature can help halt biodiversity loss , provide nature-based solutions to climate change , and generate economic opportunity , good jobs , and significant financial returns . <p> Just as families count their income and debt -- and governments calculate benchmarks for fiscal responsibility -- the powerful economic value of nature should also be counted . <p> For example , in 2017-18 alone , visitor spending at Parks Canada sites contributed $2.6 billion to Canada 's GDP and $449 million in tax revenues across multiple levels of government .
