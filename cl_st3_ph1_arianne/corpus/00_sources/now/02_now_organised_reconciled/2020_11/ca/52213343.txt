<h> PETER MCKENNA : Beware of what the Biden administration will bring <p> U.S. president-elect Joe Biden will be keen to work collaboratively with Canada on a wide range of international issues but may also punish Canadians for their failure to meet climate goals . - REUTERS file <p> Peter McKenna <p> Guest opinion <p> Well , it 's ( almost ) official now . Canada can start planning for the new Biden administration south of the border . <p> Ottawa will arguably begin with a few critical advantages at its disposal . First , the Biden White House will be eager to repair the damage to Canada-U.S. relations inflicted during the ugly Trump years . Since the overall relationship has practically hit rock-bottom -- unquestionably the worst it has been in several decades -- the Joe Biden team will be anxious to consider any " irritants " that the Justin Trudeau government might have . <p> Secondly , the Biden administration will no doubt look to Canada as a possible bridge to its European friends and partners , who were unceremoniously sidelined and maligned by the Trump White House @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to the Europeans that there is a new sheriff in town , and one that values allies as assets and not absconders . <p> Thirdly , Biden will be keen to work collaboratively with Canada on a wide range of international issues -- from containing China to curtailing Iran 's nuclear program . The two sides can also work together on dealing with global pandemic responses , strengthening rules-based international institutions and confronting the world 's climate emergency . <p> But the thorny issue of climate change could potentially pose a serious challenge to Canada . The problem starts with reading the fine print of Joe Biden 's " Plan for Climate Change . " <p> When you set to one side his pledges on a clean energy economy , net-zero carbon emissions by 2050 and re-committing to the 2015 Paris Agreement , there is still the key issue of getting the rest of the world to do its part . The one thing that Biden is cognizant of is that he knows that the U.S. , acting alone to save the planet , is not going to be enough @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ) . <p> What Biden is proposing , then , is a mechanism to not so subtly encourage other countries to reduce their domestic production of greenhouse gases by applying carbon pricing to imports . As his campaign document states pointedly : " He will fully integrate climate change into our foreign policy and national security strategies , as well as our approach to trade . " And therein lies the rub . <p> The United States , as most Canadians know , is Canada 's best customer . We export fully 75 per cent of everything that we make to our American friends . That means that we could be vulnerable on the bilateral trade front ( in addition to " Buy America " provisions ) . <p> In a bid to hold countries to their climate commitments , a Biden White House will " not allow other nations , including China , to game the system by becoming destination economies for polluters , undermining our climate efforts and exploiting American workers and businesses . " The campaign platform then goes on to state : " As the U.S. @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of their carbon pollution , the Biden administration will impose carbon adjustment fees or quotas on carbon-intensive goods from countries that are failing to meet their climate and environmental obligations . " <p> Significantly , there is no disputing the fact that Canada is absolutely failing to meet its current carbon reduction targets . And we certainly wo n't get there , as provinces like Alberta expand their carbon footprints , in the short to near term . <p> There were , however , no specifics on what those U.S. carbon border fees would be or even how they would be calculated . Nor was there any singling out of specific trade sectors or industrial activities that would be subject to the adjustment tariffs . <p> As I mentioned at the outset , Canada may be able to play one of its diplomatic cards to secure an exemption from these carbon tariffs . It would clearly be an odd measure to put into place if you were trying to mend fences with your Canadian neighbour . <p> But it 's hard to know for sure just how committed a Biden @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ assumes office in January 2021 . It may slow-walk the punitive measures out of deference to U.S. allies and friends . Or , it could simply push the whole border adjustment file over to the legislative branch and leave it for members of Congress to thrash out . <p> In an effort to placate the progressive wing of the Democratic Party in the House , Biden may have to go all-out to impose those adjustment fees on foreign imports . However , losing their bid to gain a majority in the U.S. Senate will be a serious impediment to a Biden White House . <p> Look , I 'm not for a second suggesting here that Canada would be better off if we still had Trump in the White House . But Ottawa should go into this new period in Canada-U.S. relations with its eyes wide open and recognize that it will not always be smooth sailing with Biden at the U.S. helm . <p> Peter McKenna is professor of political science at the University of Prince Edward Island .
