<h> Euro zone banks fall short of ECB 's climate demands <p> Reuters <p> Published : 16 hours ago <p> Sorry you must be at least 19 years of age to consume this content . <p> FRANKFURT ( Reuters ) - Euro zone banks are failing to disclose even the most basic information about their exposure to climate change , the European Central Bank said on Friday , as it set out its expectations for banks on its watch . <p> Banks will be expected to take climate change into account when making loans or investing , considering aspects such as the carbon footprint of a business and the risk that it is affected by extreme weather or changes in regulation . <p> The ECB has given banks a year to gather the information and comply with its guidelines , although it does not plan to raise capital requirements for those that fail to do so , at least initially . <p> Currently , only 3% of the euro zone 's top banks disclose all basic climate-related information , 58% disclose less than half and 16% none at all , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ virtually none of the institutions in the scope of the assessment would meet a minimum level of disclosures , " the ECB said . <p> " There is a general lack of articulation among climate-related topics and statements are too rarely supported by quantitative information . " <p> The ECB was also rolling out the final versions of its guidelines , which were put up for consultation in the spring . <p> Among a number of requirements , banks will have to quantify the climate-related impact and vulnerability of their clients , such as their water use , pollution , waste production and impact on biodiversity . <p> The ECB will ask banks to conduct a " self-assessment " early next year . It will then review the sector 's progress , carry out a stress test and take any " follow-up measures " in 2022 . <p> Some banks have already started mapping climate-related risks , such as the depreciation of assets in carbon-intensive businesses , a shift to electric vehicles and the cost of repairing assets damaged by extreme weather , the ECB said .
