<p> In this year of the COVID-19 pandemic , the proportion of British Columbians who refer to the environment as the most important issue facing the province has remained in single digits . <p> Concerns about health care , housing and the economy have dominated the landscape before , during and after voters cast their ballots in the provincial election . <p> That different generations are primarily worried about other matters does not mean that our appetite for environmental protection is nonexistent . When Research Co. and Glacier Media asked the province 's residents about their personal environmental concerns , majorities of residents expressed an interest in preservation . <p> About two-thirds of British Columbians say they are personally concerned about three specific problems : the pollution of rivers , lakes and reservoirs ( 66% ) , air pollution ( 65% ) and the pollution of drinking water ( also 65% ) . <p> More than three in five British Columbians ( 63% ) are personally worried about global warming or climate change -- including 67% of women and 67% of those aged 55 and over . <p> There is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ reviewed . The proportion of voters who are personally concerned about global warming or climate change climbs to 72% among those who supported the BC New Democratic Party ( NDP ) and the BC Green Party in last month 's election . Among those who cast ballots for the BC Liberals -- the party that implemented the first carbon tax in North America in 2008 -- it drops to 52% . <p> Six other issues are also identified as personal concerns by more than half of British Columbians : the contamination of soil and water by toxic waste ( 60% ) , deforestation ( 58% ) , the extinction of plant and animal species ( also 58% ) , the depletion of fish stocks through overfishing ( also 58% ) , the loss of tropical rain forests ( 55% ) and the maintenance of the supply of fresh water for household needs ( also 55% ) .
