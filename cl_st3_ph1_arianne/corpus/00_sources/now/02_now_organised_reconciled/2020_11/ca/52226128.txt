<h> Feds give nearly $10M to Horsefly spillway in Municipal District of Taber <p> TABER , AB -- Enlarging 14 kilometers of existing irrigation canals to divert stormwater from the St. Mary River Irrigation District Main Canal to the Oldman River during storm events will better protect properties , crops and other infrastructure in the Municipal District of Taber . <p> Federal funding of more than $9.8 million for Phase 2 and Phase 3 of the Horsefly Regional Emergency Spillway was announced today by Infrastructure Minister Catherine McKenna and Reeve Merrill Harris of the MD of Taber . <p> " The Municipal District of Taber underwent catastrophic flooding in 2018 , " said McKenna via videoconference from Ottawa . " Flooding of home but also of prime agricultural land that put people but also your community at risk . " <p> McKenna said the project will help make more than 60,000 people across the region more resilient to flooding , by protecting residents , their properties and crops . <p> " In the last 10 years our region has been hit with five of what could be called one-in-a-hundred-year flood @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , farmland and infrastructure damage while forcing some people to be evacuated from their homes causing emotional devastation for several residents across southern Alberta , " said Harris . <p> Past events created the possibility of overwhelming the main canal . If any part of the system was broken , says Harris , " the result would be six months to a year where vital irrigation water would be unavailable . Water that is vital and needed to support the production of the high-value specialty scops that are grown in this region such as potatoes , sugar beets , corn , seed canola , onions alfalfa , Timothy ha , dry beans and sunflowers . " <p> The water delivered by the SMRID supplies over 500,000 acres of irrigated farmland in southern Alberta , he said . <p> McKenna said it will divert stormwater at a rate of 55 cubic metres per second from the main canal to the Oldman River during snowmelts and heavy rainfalls . Wetlands will be added to reduce pollutants entering the Oldman River . <p> " All too often now our communities are seeing worse @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Disaster mitigation projects like this one have a real and positive impact on communities by helping to ensure that everyone is better protected .
