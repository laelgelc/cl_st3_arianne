<h> Which of these seven startup founder mindsets are you ? <p> Recessions can spark startups . People laid off from work may have no other option in an economy with many downsizing companies . And with everything in flux , opportunities can arise . <p> Usually a startup begins with an idea , and a dream . But venture capitalist Rizwan Virk , who specializes in tech , believes it 's also important early on to examine your motivations and that of your team . Usually he finds it 's one of these seven : <p> Money-driven : This seems to be on the upswing -- what he calls " coin-driven " individuals , often who want to get very rich , building a billion-dollar company . Indeed , at some point in the past two decades entrepreneurs and investors became obsessed with the word " billion . " Every tech startup is supposed to scale to that size or otherwise it 's a bust . " If you really want to build a billion-dollar company , do n't try to build a billion-dollar company , " he advises @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ small , emerging market opportunity where you can be a key or dominant player , and grow with it . <p> Creativity-driven : Some entrepreneurs want to build neat products . When the company has a product that is accepted by the market they get bored as the focus should become building an organization and that 's not their particular desire . <p> Technology- or market-driven : Some entrepreneurs are deeply immersed in a certain field and would never get into an enterprise in a different market . That contrasts with the money-driven entrepreneurs who almost do n't care which market they are in as long as they can ride a hot startup . <p> **26;0;TOOLONG : Some entrepreneurs want to be famous , known for their success with a particular startup or as a guru who created a series of them . " Some of these founders can get what they want even if the company goes out of business , as long as they are seen as experts in the field and their startups get lots of press coverage , " he says . <p> Independence-driven : These @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ do n't really care what anyone else thinks or whether they are getting any accolades in the press , or whether the company is growing faster ( or slower ) than anyone else 's . They also do n't care so much about the money they are making ; they just do n't want anyone telling them what to do , " he writes . Recession startups often begin with this impulse , the founder tired feeling cast off by their employer and wanting control . <p> Empire-driven ( or competition-driven ) : These entrepreneurs like to win and want to build something big and dominant . That instinct applies to many entrepreneurs , of course , but this subgroup ca n't stand losing even a single deal to a competitor . <p> Mission-driven : These folks want to help a particular community or further a cause . " The hard part of this exercise is being honest with yourself , " he warns , suggesting that if you have a co-founder you each might have a more accurate sense of each other 's motivation than of your own . Keep in mind that the motivations might change over time , so repeat the exercise annually . <p> If this exercise seems trivial , he would differ . If expectations are misaligned one of the co-founders is likely to leave early .
