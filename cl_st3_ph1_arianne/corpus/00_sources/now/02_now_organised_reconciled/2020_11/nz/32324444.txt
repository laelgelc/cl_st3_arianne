<h> Removing the stain of climate change from washing products <p> Unilever is on a mission to eliminate fossil-derived carbon from all of its cleaning products by 2030 . Russell Brown learns how the company is cleaning the carbon out of its cleaning products . <p> There 's a train coming and we 're all on the tracks . We know it 's coming , everybody 's talking about the train and how much it 's going to hurt when it arrives . But no one 's actually doing anything to stop it , or even slow it down . That 's how facing down climate change can often feel . <p> Governments convene , agree there 's a problem and that it 's increasingly urgent -- and then , most of the time , shy away from the short-term economic and political costs of acting . But what if someone did take a big , bold stride off the track we 're all on ? <p> That 's how Unilever sees its new Clean Future project . That was an undertaking to support the UN Sustainable Development Goals by taking action on health and wellbeing ( including by sponsoring handwashing and oral health programmes ) , enhancing livelihoods ( introducing new employment practices and creating more opportunities for women ) -- and halving the company 's environmental impact . Clean Future is easily the largest stride so far towards that third commitment . <p> The role of carbon in cleaning products is bigger than you might think . Stain removers in laundry products , for example , have long been petrochemical derived . <p> Like many homecare manufacturers around the world , the ingredients in Unilever 's cleaning products account for a significant proportion of its carbon footprint -- 40% , in fact . And yet those ingredients are currently key to the products ' effectiveness . No one wants cleaning products that do n't clean . <p> The solution , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Kulve put it recently , is to " stop pumping carbon from under the ground when there is ample carbon on and above the ground if we can learn to utilise it at scale . The science for making some of those substitutions at scale does n't exist yet , which is why Unilever has put up a NZD$1.77 billion research fund to make it work . <p> The first of the reformulation is already on the shelves in New Zealand .
