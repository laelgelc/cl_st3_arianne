<h> More plant diversity means less pesticide - study <p> Increasing plant diversity naturally controls plant-eating insects in grasslands , according to a studyled by University of Waikato senior lecturer Dr Andrew Barnes and the German Centre forIntegrative Biodiversity Research . <p> Plant diversity provides less favoured food for insects and supports their natural predators likespiders and beetles . <p> The study , published in Science Advances , shows increasing plantbiodiversity could help reduce the need for pesticides in agriculture . <p> The team of researchers used two long-running grassland biodiversity experiments in Germany and the US . <p> They collected data from these areas over two years , studying the natural food webs in monocultures ( areas with a single plant species ) and biodiverse grasslands . <p> Andrew says the news is very relevant for New Zealand agriculture . <p> " Our findings demonstrate how conserving biodiversity in production landscapes could help balance environmental and economic benefits through natural biological control of pests , " he says . <p> " Supporting agricultural production by enhancing natural pest control could be one way to help achieve favourable biodiversity outcomes @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ -- the biological diversity of all species on Earth -- is crucial for maintaining ecosystems in planted and natural grasslands . With agriculture intensifying to feed the world 's growing population , grasslands are under pressure to support more crop production and livestock . <p> Insect herbivores cause an estimated 18-26 per cent loss in global crop production , which has driven significant growth in the use of environmentally harmful pesticides . The intensification of agriculture and pesticide use is reducing biodiversity in many parts of the world . <p> Plant-eating insects prefer monocultures <p> The researchers found insects consume significantly less plant matter in areas with high diversity . <p> Their feeding rate ( per gram of plant biomass ) was 44 per cent lower than in monocultures . <p> In diverse plant communities , specialised insect herbivores are less likely to find their preferred plant species and may move on to other areas . Previous research has shown lower levels of nitrogen in these plant communities , making them less nutritious for insects . <p> " That ultimately means that where multiple species are planted together , this @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and each individual plant in diverse mixtures will receive lower damage from herbivores , " says Andrew . <p> Plant diversity can limit pest outbreaks <p> Heavy use of insecticides can lead to rebounds of herbivore pests by destabilising their natural enemies . <p> " Our experiments show that conserving plant diversity provides multiple benefits for controlling herbivore pests which could play a key role in reducing inputs of agrochemicals and enhancing plant productivity , " says Andrew . <p> " Moving forward , we will be focusing on understanding how biodiversity enrichment in New Zealand agricultural systems could enhance herbivore pest control under future climate change scenarios .
