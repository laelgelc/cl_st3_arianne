<h> Maori research projects land Marsden Fund support <p> University of Otago researchers this week learned they have secured $17.5 million in funding for 30 projects , including three with a unique Maori perspective . <p> University of Otago Source : istock.com <p> The Marsden funding will go towards projects across the university 's disciplines from Commerce and Health to the Humanities and Sciences . <p> Fourteen projects received grants ranging from $729,000 to $960,000 , and a further 16 researchers received kick-start $300,000 grants . <p> FUNDING FOR CLIMATE CHANGE RESEARCH <p> The newly-formed Climate and Energy Finance Group received $869,000 to explore to what extent , and when , increasing floods would affect property values in coastal cities . <p> The research , led by Otago Business School 's Associate Professor Ivan Diaz-Rainey , had immense value for the future . <p> " This is an extremely important topic because 65 per cent of the population in New Zealand live within 5km of the sea and 85 per cent of individual wealth is concentrated in real estate , " associate professor Diaz-Rainey said . <p> " It also @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ over 60 percent of the value of the four largest New Zealand banks loan portfolios are to housing . " <p> The research drew on a unique framework of Maori traditions and principles . <p> " Our research framework learns from Maori traditions of considering multiple generations in decision-making and Maori investment principles where environment and people are integral to a systemic view of investment decisions , " he said . <p> " Accordingly , this research will explore to what extent , and when , increasing flood frequencies will impact property values in New Zealand 's coastal cities given that markets are forward looking and have imperfect information . <p> " Further , we will examine if there are flow-on effects of these losses on the stability of domestic banking system . <p> " New approaches to answering these questions will be adopted by an interdisciplinary team of experts in geographical information systems , geology , climate change , real-estate and banking . " <p> FUNDING TO EXAMINE ADOPTION ACT <p> Researcher Dr Erica Newman received $300,000 to examine the impact of the 1955 Adoption Act on the identity @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ taha Maori . <p> The work held personal significance , she said . <p> " I am the daughter of a Maori adoptee and do not yet know my taha Maori , " she said . <p> " Although I know I am of Maori descent I do not know my whakapapa , my hapu or iwi , my pepeha or my turangawaewae and this is because my mother was adopted into a non-Maori family . I know I am not the only person of Maori descent who is in this situation and it is important that these stories and journeys are explored . <p> " Absence of turangawaewae for descendants of Maori adoptees has an effect on their identity and their health and wellbeing . Finding whakapapa connections can only benefit the participant in strengthening their Maori identity by having the knowledge of who they are and where they come from . This journey could also connect the adoptee , if they are ready .
