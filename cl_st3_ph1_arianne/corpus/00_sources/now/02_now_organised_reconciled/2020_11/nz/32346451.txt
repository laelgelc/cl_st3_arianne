<h> Staying ahead of the game <p> Measuring and recording performance is critical to driving performance on sheep and beef farms <p> High-performing farmers are front-footing challenges facing the sheep and beef sector and adapting their businesses , according to new research by the Red Meat Profit Partnership ( RMPP ) . <p> According to the study , consistency of execution , attention to detail and measuring and recording performance were all critical to driving performance on sheep and beef farms . <p> RMPP commissioned the study to ask a group of New Zealand 's highest performing sheep and beef farmers to identify the main drivers of their performance . This year 's research , which followed a similar study in 2015 , identified the critical characteristics that enabled them to consistently achieve good results . <p> It also focused on what had changed for these farmers , particularly the challenges they were facing and how they were responding to the issues compared to five years ago . <p> The research found there was a stronger focus among high performing farmers on addressing environmental challenges through quality assurance programmes . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ had led to an increasingly negative public perception towards farming . <p> Many high performing farmers want to take ownership of this issue with some wishing to help show the public how they farm with integrity , especially regarding animal welfare and the environment . <p> Facing droughts , dealing with climate change , and the need to map pathways towards becoming carbon neutral were also more prominent issues compared to 2015 . <p> Meanwhile , the farmers recognised the changing consumer preferences and what this meant for the long-term viability of their businesses . Covid-19 also presented a range of challenges and opportunities . <p> The research shows most high-performing farmers are continuing to do what they have already been successfully focusing on for many years . <p> " These farmers do not blindly follow trends , rather when they introduce new practices , they tweak them to suit both their style of farming and the environment they operate in .
