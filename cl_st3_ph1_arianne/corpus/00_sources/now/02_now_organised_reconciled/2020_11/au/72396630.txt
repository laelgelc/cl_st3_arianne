<h> Biden 's victory in the U.S. gives the world hope on climate <p> Joe Biden 's climate policy is likely to leave Australia under pressure to revise its own stance ( image by Gage Skidmore via Wikimedia Commons ) <p> Joe Biden 's win in the U.S. Presidential Election is part of a run of good news for the global climate . <p> Biden has promised to rejoin the Paris Agreement on his first day in office . <p> The U.S. will then be required to commit a Nationally Determined Contributions ( NDCs ) to reductions in carbon emissions . Biden has already announced that he is committed to achieving zero net emissions by 2050 , along with an interim 2030 target will presumably be the basis of the US NDC . <p> Net-zero by 2050 is now the international norm . South Korea and Japan , both holdouts until relatively recently , announced commitments in October . Only a handful of OECD countries , including Australia , are now holding out . Middle-income countries with big emissions are also moving . <p> China set its target for 2060 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and South Africa has expressed an aspiration for net-zero by 2050 . <p> There are also more immediately positive developments . Bangladesh , the Philippines and Vietnam have all cancelled or greatly modified plans which involved a big expansion of coal-fired power . The same is true in Poland which has been , along with Turkey , the last big holdout . <p> One of the reasons for these moves is that it is now almost impossible to get finance for projects related to coal . The vast majority of both government and private lending agencies now have divestiture policies excluding , which are now being extended to cover the most destructive oil and gas projects , such as tar sands . There are still loopholes through which some coal projects can squeeze , but they are tightening all the time . <p> The final stage of the process is the withdrawal of big engineering companies from the business of building coal plants . General Electric announced this recently . Getting to zero emissions by 2050 is a huge improvement on the scenarios that were being considered until recently , but not enough to prevent severe climate damage , some of which is already happening . An earlier decarbonization target and a shift to negative emissions are needed . <p> And targets are one thing , delivery another . China 's announcement wo n't have much effect unless Xi Jinping can rein in provincial governments that are still eager to develop coal . And Biden probably wo n't have much capacity to pass legislative action . <p> As the law stands at present , he can do a lot through executive action and regulation , but the Republican majority on the Supreme Court may try to stop this . But simply reversing actions taken by Trump would be sufficient . <p> Where does this leave Australia ? Until now , climate policy has been driven almost entirely by domestic politics , in particular the climate denialism of the far right .
