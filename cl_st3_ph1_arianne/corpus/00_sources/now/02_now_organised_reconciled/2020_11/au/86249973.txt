Preliminary findings from our recent research show many young people are worried about the future . It involved 740 young people in New South Wales between the ages of 16 and 25 completing a series of standardised questionnaires about their current emotional state , and their concerns about climate change . <p> Our early findings were presented at the International Association of People-Environment Studies ( IAPS ) conference online earlier this year . <p> Some 57% of respondents lived in metropolitan areas and 43% in rural or regional areas . They also reported drug and alcohol use at levels that , according to the UNCOPE substance use screening tool , suggested cause for concern . <h> What does this mean ? <p> We are still analysing the data we collected , but our preliminary results strongly suggest climate change is linked to how hopeful young people feel about the future . <p> We are already locked into a significant degree of warming -- the only questions are just how bad will it get and how quickly . <p> Young people need better access to mental health services and support . It 's clear we must find effective ways to help young people build psychological resilience to bushfires , and other challenges climate change will bring . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , Belinda Craig and Clara Murray contributed to this research .
