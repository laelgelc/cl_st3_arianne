Eric Thoo looks at what is at stake . <h> The Public Forum -- Pulau Jerejak <p> Year : 2018 <p> " Father told me that he would visit me once a week . " <p> Lim Boon Nya was only seven when she was diagnosed with leprosy , a contagious disease that affects the skin , mucous membranes and nerves , that spread in Malaya in the early 20th Century . <p> Lim Boon Nya <p> " When I saw a boat pass by ( Jerejak Island ) , I ran to the beach . I could see my father leaving in that boat and he did n't bring me along to go home ... Everyday , I cried . " <p> Back in the old days in Penang , leprosy patients were often segregated in quarantine camps on Pulau Jerejak , which means Jerejak Island . <p> Lim was one of them . " When I saw a boat passing by ( Jerejak Island ) , I ran to the beach . I could see my father leaving in that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ home ... Every day I cried . " <p> Also sharing the same fate with Lim was Goh Sooi Seong . Unlike Lim , however , Goh was a teenager when she was forced to leave the only life she knew behind . <p> Goh Sooi Seong <p> At the age of 17 , when life was unfolding and offering infinite possibilities to others , Goh was left with no option but to be sent away to Jerejak Island <p> The Jerejak journeys narrated by Lim and Goh share a similar pattern : the initial grievances or confusion over the loss of their previous life , followed by a sense of rejection and abandonment on Pulau Jerejak , to eventual acceptance of their new reality . <p> Later , they even managed to have a somewhat good time on the island , from participating in community activities such as movie-screenings and festival celebrations , to carrying out simple chore such as raising poultry and growing vegetables for money . <p> In the public forum hosted by Penang 's Think City , it felt as though Lim and Goh were there @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ life accounts on Pulau Jerejak . In reality , however , it was a presentation by Mike Gibby , a historian who has the ability to take his audiences backwards in time . <h> The forgotten prime of Jerejak Island <p> Year : 1969 <p> Mike introduced us to Camp A on Pulau Jerejak in 1969 . With faculties such as the main leprosy camp , leprosy hospital and Catholic Church , the south-east part of the island was easily the busiest part of the island . <p> Further south of the camp , we saw two young men in khaki uniform on watch duty . Recalling an earlier presentation by environmentalist and Penang Hills Watch co-founder Rexy Prakash , we concluded they were prison guards for a maximum security prison nearby that detained inmates detained after the riots of 1969 . <p> It was then Mike directed our attention to a Chinese tombstone sitting quietly near them . It was -- and is -- the tradition for the Chinese to practise the order of ' man on the left , woman on the right " . Upon close inspection @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ stone read " Mr Lee You Fu " and on the right was " Madam Chen Jin " . <p> A mysterious Chinese tombstone that , according to its vague carvings , belonged to a couple from the Fujian Province of China . <p> Although this type of Chinese tombstone was not an uncommon sight -- it is still widely available in Malaysian Chinese cemeteries today -- there was something odd about this particular tombstone . <p> " The date of death is missing " , remarked Mike , as if reading our minds . <p> He was right . <p> " So what happened here ? " Mike continued . " Who were they ? Did they die without proper burial ? Or did they flee from an unknown danger on the island ? " <p> The fact that none of us had the answer just added another layer to the veil of mystery shrouding this largely forgotten island in Penang today . <p> We were still pondering the question when the Penang Heritage Trust secretary Ben Wismen took the stage and zapped us into the future . <h> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 2030 <p> Our sensory receptors were instantly sent into overdrive . <p> The scenery of lush greenery with sunlight filtering through the tree canopy was replaced by bright neon lights with sharp colours . The music of birds chirping and wind gusting through the branches of trees was replaced by the noise of agitated car honks and an excited chattering crowd of people . <p> Perhaps worst of all , the fragrance of a combination of fruits and flowers was replaced by thick exhaust fumes . <p> Block upon block of buildings filled the flat land on the island . Modern restaurants , souvenir shops , and shopping outlets were packed with people . Perhaps the most puzzling part was the endless stream of traffic on the small island , leading to what looked like a commercial theme park ahead . <p> We were drenched in sweat under the unfiltered sunlight as we paced aimlessly on the scorching tarred road . <p> Ben then revealed that back in 2007 , much of Pulau Jerejak was approved by the Penang State Assembly to be permanent forest reserve . But plans for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . " The development includes a theme park , hotels , 1,200 residential units , and a mega bridge that would connect Pulau Jerejak with Penang Island . " <p> In this future that we were in , the bridge construction had successfully wiped out the rich marine life and replaced it with lifeless concrete . That explained the traffic we witnessed earlier . Once an essential green lung of Penang that absorbed carbon dioxide , Pulau Jerejak was turned into one of the sites that emit heat-trapping pollutants into the air . On the ground , over 130 species of plants and 24 species of land animals were also under threat and at risk of extinction . <p> The historical site of leprosy hospitals , maximum security prisons , and many more will not be spared from the urbanisation either , said Ben . " This is because , although the development only involves 15% of the island , that little land was exactly where the historically significant sites are located . The aim was simple ( but not easy ) -- to raise awareness among Penangites of the forgotten history of Pulau jerejak . The crucial part , however , is what followed next . <p> What is our collective decision on what to do with Pulau Jerejak ? <p> Do we watch by idly and let the future of our state be decided by an elite few ?
