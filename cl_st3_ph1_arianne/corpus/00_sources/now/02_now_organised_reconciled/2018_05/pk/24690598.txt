<p> Climate change and global warming put some forms of life at risk , but researchers found one instance that might not feel the heat -- corn . <p> Contrary to previous analyses , research published by Michigan State University shows that projected changes in temperature and humidity will not lead to greater water use in corn . This means that while changes in temperatures and humidity trend as they have in the past 50 years , crop yields can not only survive -- but thrive . <p> " There is a lot of optimism looking at the future for farmers , especially in the Midwest , " said Bruno Basso , lead author of the study and University Distinguished professor . <p> Basso and his colleague Joe Ritchie , co-author on the study , calculated how much energy crops receive from the sun and how it is converted to evaporative loss from the crop , known as evapotranspiration . <p> " Think of the energy balance like a bank account . There are additions and subtractions , " Basso said . They found that the water loss was the same as it was for lower yielding crops because the energy balance was about the same . <p> The trend for the past 50 years of a slightly more humid environment decreases the energy for the crops ' water use . <p> " Our analysis , and that of other climate researchers , shows that the amount of water vapor in the air is gradually increasing in the summers because the daily low temperatures are getting gradually warmer , but the daily high temperatures are cooling -- or staying the same -- in many areas of the Midwest , " Basso said . " This causes more humidity and slightly decreases how much energy is used for evaporation .
