<p> As technology makes flights cheaper and affluence grows in countries like China and India , the global demand for travel continues to outstrip the de-carbonisation efforts of tourism operations , researchers have revealed . <p> The study , published in Nature Climate Change on Tuesday , found the tourism sector accounts for eight per cent of global greenhouse gas emissions with the true environmental cost of the industry quantified for the first time . <p> Unlike previous studies , the research led by Integrated Sustainability Analysis took into account the carbon emitted directly during tourism activities , from shopping and food to accommodation and transport , across 160 countries . <p> The study found tourism 's global footprint has increased from 3.9 to 4.5 gigatonnes of carbon emissions between 2009 and 2013 - nearly four times more than previously estimated . <p> The increase in global tourism and its emissions has " cancelled out all carbon intensity reductions " , the study said . <p> Affluence in countries such as the United States and growing middle-classes in China and India has turned tourism into a more carbon-intensive activity , with travellers spending their money on longer flights to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ through shopping and hospitality . <p> Meanwhile , visitors from low-income countries consume a high proportion of unprocessed food and use road transport with little commercial hospitality services , which translates to a smaller carbon footprint . <p> Dr Arunima Malik , researcher and University of Sydney lecturer , said travellers had to look at their whole trip , and not just their flight , to consider their carbon footprint . <p> " Whether it 's public transport or a more carbon intensive mode ( of transport ) , that all comes together and is part of your carbon footprint , " Dr Malik told AAP . <p> Dr Malik said the tourism industry could contribute to its own eventual decline if it does not take measures to offset emissions , with the industry 's global footprint forecast to reach 6.5 gigatonnes of carbon emissions by 2025 . <p> The researcher hoped the " eye-opening " research would inform conversations ahead of the next meeting of the Paris Agreement in 2023 . <p> " Our holidays destinations that we love going to , such as coastal areas , winter locations @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to the effects of climate change , " she said . <p> " It 's likely that these destinations that we love are going to undergo drastic changes in coming years if we do n't protect them .
