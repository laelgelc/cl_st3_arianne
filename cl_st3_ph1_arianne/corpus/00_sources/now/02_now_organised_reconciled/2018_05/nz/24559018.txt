<h> Farmers doing a ' great job ' - Shaw <p> Climate Change Minister James Shaw says all of NZ , not just farmers , have a role to play in reducing climate-warming emissions . Photo : Peter McIntosh <p> Climate Change Minister James Shaw has stepped into the lions ' den in the last week . <p> Shaw spoke in deepest blue territory on Thursday at a Federated Farmers meeting in Southland , and today was at a farmers forum hosted by DairyNZ in the Waikato . <p> Those appearances came immediately after the Greens-supported Government made announcements which directly affect dairy farmers , including an end to irrigation subsidies and proposals to effectively limit cow numbers by setting a cap on farm run-off . <p> Shaw , the Green Party co-leader , took a conciliatory approach today in a half-hour speech which did not criticise the previous government or the agricultural sector 's environmental record once . <p> There was a notable contrast in tone to Environment Minister David Parker , who on the weekend threatened regulations for polluting farmers who did not get into line . <p> At times , Shaw 's speech veered towards flattery @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ me -- I think you 're doing a great job , " he told the farmers ' forum . <p> " That may come as a bit of a surprise to people given some of the headlines over the last few years . " <p> He offered an olive branch to farmers , saying he wanted to destroy the myth that the Government 's approach to improving the environment was " all about the agricultural sector " . <p> The National Party and its farming base accused Labour and the Greens of creating an urban and rural divide during the election campaign , in particular through the now abandoned promise to tax rural water use . <p> That theme has been revived after the Government 's announcements on oil and gas exploration and regional fuel taxes . <p> " That really distresses me , " Shaw said . " <p> Shaw went as far as echoing some of agricultural sector 's complaints that urban areas were responsible for a large part of the country 's environmental problems . <p> Auckland City was the source of 20 per cent of the country 's emissions , he said . <p> " We 've got to bend the curve in Auckland . If we do n't , nothing 's going to happen in terms of making progress on our targets . " <p> There was only a brief reference to making agriculture pay for its greenhouse gas emissions or other regulations which will affect farmers . <p> Instead , the speech focused on the need to change the way climate change was discussed in this country . <p> To date , adapting to a low carbon economy has been thought about as a sunk cost rather than an economic opportunity , he said . <p> " It is the nature of thinking about it as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that we have n't been able to make progress on this .
