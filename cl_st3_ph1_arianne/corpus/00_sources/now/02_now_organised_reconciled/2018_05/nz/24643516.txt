<h> Climate change and health report launched <p> A new report shows how climate change will impact on New Zealander 's health over the next 50-100 years , and makes the case for better preparation . <p> " Our climate is changing , and how this impacts on people 's health will also change . We need our health system to be better prepared to deal with increased temperatures and more extreme weather events , " Associate Minister for Health Julie Anne Genter said today . <p> The report was commissioned by the Ministry of Health and published by the Institute of Environmental Science and Research ( ESR ) . <p> " The risks outlined in this report show why we need to act to reduce climate pollution now , as well as prepare for the level of climate change that is already set to happen . <p> " The flooding and evacuation of Edgecumbe caused serious disruption to people 's lives . Already this year we have seen how a storm like cyclone Fehi caused a state of emergency in Buller and Dunedin . <p> " Today 's report maps out where the problems will be . Allergens @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ solar radiation , and vector-borne , water-borne and infectious diseases might all increase in the coming decades and they have the potential to impact on our health and the health of our loved ones . <p> " The spread of infectious disease , particularly in our water sources , is of concern and needs greater attention . <p> " The Ministry of Health will be working with DHBs , many of which are already doing a lot of work on this , to become more sustainable and reduce their carbon footprints . The Ministry has asked ESR to provide scientific advice on how the health sector can adapt to climate change . <p> " I 'm really encouraged to see greater work happening on preparing for how climate change will affect us . Later this year the Heat Health Plan Guide will be published . <p> " Climate change is one of the biggest threats to society this century but reports like todays will ensure New Zealand is better prepared for dealing with future challenges , " Ms Genter said .
