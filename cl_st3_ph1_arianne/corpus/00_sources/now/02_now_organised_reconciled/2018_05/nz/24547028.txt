<h> NZ ' internationally obliged ' to respond to climate change <p> OPINION : The climate change debate came to Southland last week in the form of Minister for Climate Change James Shaw addressing the Southland Federated Farmers ' annual meeting . <p> Obviously how agriculture is treated in regard to New Zealand meeting its international obligations is of keen interest to farmers and I commend the Southland Feds for being proactive in inviting the minister . <p> Shaw outlined the process from here of fully establishing his independent climate commission , which will be the body responsible for setting a series of interim targets to ensure we meet our Paris Agreement commitments and overarching goal of New Zealand being carbon neutral by 2050 . <p> Of course the reason why we farmers are so keenly interested in this issue is because 48 per cent of New Zealand 's emissions profile comes from ruminants for which we have no current method of reducing their output . The potential for a tax which acts as an increase on the cost of production , and potentially pushes that production towards less efficient foreign farming systems , is a valid concern . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is why we have negotiated within the coalition agreement that any inclusion of agriculture into an Emission Trading Scheme would initially only be at 5 per cent to allow appropriate phasing . It is also the reason we have advocated for the billion tree strategy as it is a practical method of increasing New Zealand 's carbon absorption capacity rate with a known outcome . <p> Of course it is possible the commission will not recommend including agriculture at all but I would n't be advising anyone on betting the farm on that . <p> What Shaw also articulated was that this situation also presents opportunity . <p> The rest of the world is looking for New Zealand to lead in this space . Indeed , work going on at Massey University as part of an international response led by AgResearch comprising 29 countries is showing promising results with vaccines that change gut reactions . <p> They are also finding that as they measure individual animal emissions there is quite a variance and crucially this variance is highly heritable and tends to be from higher production animals . <p> We @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ actual tools to mitigate our emissions short of destocking . It also presents a marketing opportunity for our products in the eyes of the very high end consumer we are seeking to target . <p> Whatever we think about climate change the facts are that we are internationally obligated to respond . Any correspondence on that should be directed at Paula Bennett who signed us up to the Paris climate accord . <p> The recent productivity commission report indicated just how hard it is going to be to meet those commitments . Encouragingly , Shaw acknowledged that the lessons of the 1980s have been noted and that he is conscious of not inflicting abrupt change . Finding an acceptable pathway will be challenging but he is to be commended for fronting the Feds and getting those conversations going . <p> - Mark Patterson is a New Zealand First List MP and Lawrence sheep and beef farmer .
