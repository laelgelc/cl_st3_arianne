<p> Three prominent Quebec-area Indigenous chiefs were among the hundreds of people who gathered in Montreal on Sunday to protest the Kinder Morgan pipeline expansion . <p> Assembly of First Nations regional Chief Ghislain Picard , Mohawk Chief Serge Simon and Innu Chief Jean-Charles Pietacho spoke out against the project , citing the need to show solidarity with First Nations and other groups in British Columbia who are fighting against it . <p> A large crowd , many wearing raincoats carrying bright umbrellas , gathered under rainy skies as Indigenous singers beat a drum and sang of the importance of protecting water . <p> Picard , who is the Assembly of First Nations ' regional chief for Quebec and Labrador , told the crowds that the debate over the pipeline can not be restricted to B.C. and Alberta because climate change knows no borders . <p> " We respect the rights of our brothers and sisters in British Columbia to have their own debates , " he told the crowd . <p> " But we also say climate is indivisible , and we 'll have our word to say on any project that tries to diminish that reality . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for the benefit of those watching in British Columbia , stressed the importance of solidarity with those fighting the project in western Canada . <p> He urged governments to divert the money that is spent on oil towards developing clean energy instead . <p> " We have to band together , and we have to force the governments to become a little bolder when it comes to investing in the future , " he said . <p> " Oil is n't the future , it 's the past .
