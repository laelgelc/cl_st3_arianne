<h> Increasingly at risk <h> Paper reports marine protected areas vulnerable to climate change <p> A new paper is warning marine protected areas ( MPAs ) are increasingly at risk from climate change . <p> Dr. Amanda Bates is the Canada Research Chair in Marine Physiological Ecology with the Department of Ocean Sciences . She co-authored the paper , which was published in the journal Nature Climate Change this week . <h> Threat to biodiversity <p> Although MPAs are a primary management tool for mitigating threats to marine biodiversity , she says the warming associated with continued " business as usual " emissions will likely result in further habitat and species loss . <p> " Climate change is not going to stay out of our MPA borders -- it 's there , " said Dr. Bates . <p> The team behind the paper pulled data from MPA sites around the world to create a global framework to examine environmental conditions in MPAs . We also found if we rearrange MPAs to reduce exposure to one stressor , we may well increase exposure to another . " <h> Change is coming <p> Dr. Bates says the effects of ocean warming on marine species and ecosystems are well documented . <p> Changes to species distribution and composition are expected as populations track the geographic movement of their thermal niches and shift to higher latitudes . <p> " We believe that within our children 's lifetime there are going to be major changes in our MPAs. By preventing and reducing our carbon output . <p> " Locally you can reduce stressors to MPAs , but that takes a lot of effort , " said Dr. Bates . <p> " It also removes resources and changes allocations in human systems .
