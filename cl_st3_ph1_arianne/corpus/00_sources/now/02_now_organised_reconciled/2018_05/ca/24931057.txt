<p> After 15 years of Liberal rule , it is time for a change , and change is what Northern Life is endorsing this election . <p> We will not be endorsing any one candidate . This is not because we do n't like the local candidates -- the slate offers some solid choices , even if no one candidate stands out as singularly impressive . What we can not support are the election platforms of the major contenders . Each one goes off the rails in significant ways . <p> Allow us to explain . <p> The Liberals began their impressive 15-year run promisingly enough , offering a kinder-gentler refuge from the startling pragmatism of Mike Harris ' Common Sense Revolution ( itself a backlash to the five-year reign of the NDP 's Bob Rae ) . They repaired the province 's relationship with teachers , enacted important ( sometimes misguided ) environmental regulations and took a more humanistic approach to social services . <p> But the older a government gets , the worse it gets ... at policy , at communication and at management . <p> As premier , Kathleen Wynne took that kinder-gentler policy to new @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . All she had to do was stand by as Tim Hudak self-destructed with his promise to lay off 100,000 civil servants , helping Wynne to a surprise majority government . <p> But the baggage piled up . Electricity costs were rising , and the gas plant scandal would n't go away . The sell-off of 52 per cent of Hydro One funded infrastructure spending , but resulted in an enormous backlash . The Liberals froze hospital funding , but did n't change Dalton McGuinty-era legislation preventing hospitals from running deficits . When the Liberals raised the minimum wage , small business took it as a sucker-punch . <p> In Ontario , we are seeing some of the left -- right polarization that we 're seeing in other parts of the world . It 's a gentler Canadian version , to be sure , but it 's there . That they have no climate change plan -- either carbon fee and dividend or cap and trade -- sends the wrong message . Their ideological rejection of sex-ed courses is unwise and dogmatic . <p> On the other hand , the NDP , who believe in fighting climate change with a carbon tax and putting the proceeds to work , want to spend billions to buy back Hydro One for no discernible purpose .
