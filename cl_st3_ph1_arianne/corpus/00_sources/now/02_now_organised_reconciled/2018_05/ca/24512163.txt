<h> Artist Susan Tooke wants ' people to imagine the basic elements of life ' in her Ephemerals exhibit at Halifax 's Secord Gallery <p> Artist Susan Tooke poses with some of her work at the Secord Gallery on Quinpool Road in Halifax on May 1 . ( RYAN TAPLIN Staff ) <p> Susan Tooke 's love of nature goes back to a childhood spent on a New Jersey farm . Now in her 60s , the award-winning illustrator , painter and animator is leaving Halifax to return to the country . <p> She and her husband , artist Richard Rudnicki , have purchased a property with more than 100 acres of land on the south side of the North Mountain in Port Royal . <p> It 'll take her closer to nature and the environmental themes she explores in her art , now on exhibit in Ephemerals at Secord Gallery , 6301 Quinpool Rd. , until May 19 . <p> Tooke holds the ecosystem in a tenuous , ideal balance of kinetic , interacting patterns in landscapes of jagged trees , rocks and waterways , and in her new Groundscapes series looking down at teeming biological life . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ selected by curator Sarah Fillmore for the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia 's 2016 Terroir : A Nova Scotia Survey . <p> " I feel this is a major breakthrough of a piece , " says Tooke , standing before the painting 's swirling , pulsating , mud brown and chalky blue patterns suggesting creatures ranging from fish spines to ancient proterozoic forms . <p> It was inspired by a beach walk during an artist residency at White Rabbit in Economy on the Bay of Fundy . <p> Her mind was already expanded by sound artist Helmut Lemke , who had his resident artists drawing sound , and by Indigenous , mixed ancestry , artist-musician Lindsay Dobbin , who had drummed with the ocean and thrown cymbals down to the beach . <p> " One morning I got up , " says Tooke , " and walked along the beach and saw all the debris left behind in the swash zone and that was the inspiration for the symbols you see .
