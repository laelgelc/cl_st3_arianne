Reliable productivity from cacao plants is essential to the multibillion-dollar chocolate industry , the economies of producing countries and the livelihoods of millions of smallholder cacao farmers . <p> " In West Africa , severe outbreaks of fungal diseases can destroy all cacao fruit on a single farm , " said Fister . It is a way to modify an organism 's genome by precisely delivering a DNA-cutting enzyme , Cas9 , to a targeted region of DNA . The resulting change can delete or replace specific DNA pieces , thereby promoting or disabling certain traits . <p> Previous work in cacao identified a gene , known as TcNPR3 , that suppresses the plant 's disease response . The researchers hypothesized that using CRISPR-Cas9 to knock out this gene would result in enhanced disease resistance . <p> To test their hypothesis , they used Agrobacterium -- a plant pathogen modified to remove its ability to cause disease -- to introduce CRISPR-Cas9 components into detached cacao leaves .
