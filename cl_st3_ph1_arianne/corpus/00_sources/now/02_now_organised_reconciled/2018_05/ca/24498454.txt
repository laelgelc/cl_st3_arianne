<h> LETTER OF THE WEEK : End run on environment <p> It is not surprising the audit by the federal Commissioner of the Environment and Sustainable Development ( April 25 story ) has found that the federal government , primarily Fisheries and Oceans Canada , " did not adequately manage the risks associated with salmon aquaculture consistent with its mandate to protect wild fish . " <p> I 'm a former federal civil servant who was involved with determining and mitigating some of those risks . I can attest to the fact that , up to the time that I left , the culture in the federal government was to ensure that environmental control " obstacles " were not placed in the path of salmon aquaculture development . It would appear from the audit that the culture still exists . <p> Most of the commissioner 's criticisms focus on DFO 's lack of follow-through on the requirements of the aquaculture activity regulations , implemented in 2015 . In my experience , those regulations were simply intended to remove the responsibility for aquaculture under the Fisheries Act from Environment Canada -- an agency with a clear and demonstrated environmental protection @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ agency with a dominant fisheries-promotion mandate . Once that was done , DFO placed little effort into ensuring the provisions were implemented . <p> The audit indicated DFO fails to determine whether the regulations minimize the harm to wild fish from drugs and pesticides , which I can attest to because of my involvement . The monitoring protocol promised by the aquaculture activity regulations has not yet been developed and there is therefore no effort going into determining whether operational use of those chemicals is being adequately controlled . Furthermore , as pointed out , the use of such chemicals is at the discretion of a veterinarian , usually employed by the aquaculture operator . <p> Although the regulations require DFO to collect information on the use of aquaculture chemicals , which should be made public , they are not doing so . I am still waiting for information I requested over five months ago . <p> Although DFO was singled out in this audit , it should also be recognized that a reluctance to enforce environmental protection legislation currently exists in other departments . Environment and Climate Change Canada @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ violates the Canadian Environmental Protection Act , yet it chooses not to take industry-wide action . <p> Subverting environmental protection objectives established by Parliament seems to be pervasive in this sector .
