<p> This is the time of the year when the heat in the plains of India , the Deccan plateau , and beyond in the peninsula becomes so excruciating that people pack their bags and head off to the hill stations such as Mussoorie , Nainital and Shimla . It 's the most popular summer pastime in the country . But anyone who has visited these or many of the other famous hill stations of the Himalayas , all established by the British during the Raj and therefore made more charming with their colonial-era appearance , would know what a trauma it is to be in there during this summer . <p> Instead of a quiet walk in the hills , one has to wade through crowds , the silence of the hills is replaced by constantly blaring horns of innumerable vehicles at the service of the tourists , and the rhododendrons on the face of the hills long disappeared to make way for the ugly hotel facades with their names flashing like a bad dream through the mountain air . In England , many of our coastal towns have become victims to ' Faded Seaside Glamour ' in the last 30 years .
