Environmental and Native American groups are challenging the U.S. government 's approval of Keystone XL in court in Montana . ( Nati Harnik/Associated Press/Canadian Press ) <p> Trump administration attorneys defended the disputed Keystone XL oil sands pipeline in federal court on Thursday against environmentalists and Native American groups that want to derail the project . <p> President Barack Obama rejected the 1,800-kilometre line proposed by TransCanada Corporation in 2015 because of its potential to exacerbate climate change . <p> President Donald Trump revived the project soon after taking office last year , citing its potential to create jobs and advance energy independence . <p> Environmentalists and Native American groups sued to stop the line and asked U.S. District Judge Brian Morris to halt the project . They and others , including landowners , are worried about spills that could foul groundwater and the pipeline 's impacts to their property rights . <p> Morris did not immediately rule following a four-hour Thursday hearing in federal court @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Trump 's change in course from Obama 's focus on climate change reflected a legitimate shift in policy , not an arbitrary rejection of previous studies of the project . <p> " While the importance of climate change was considered , the interests of energy security and economic development outweighed those concerns , " the attorneys recently wrote . <p> Morris previously rejected a bid by the administration to dismiss the lawsuit on the grounds that Trump had constitutional authority over the pipeline as a matter of national security . <p> Keystone XL would cost an estimated $8 billion US . It would begin in Alberta and transport up to 830,000 barrels a day of crude through Montana and South Dakota to Nebraska , where it would connect with lines to carry oil to Gulf Coast refineries . <p> Federal approval is required because the route crosses an international border .
