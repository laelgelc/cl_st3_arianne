<h> Biofuels key to success of Canada 's emerging Clean Fuel Standard <p> By RICanada and Advanced Biofuels Canada May 02 , 2018 <p> Canada 's two largest biofuels stakeholders -- Renewable Industries Canada ( RICanada ) and Advanced Biofuels Canada ( ABFC ) , together representing almost 50 member companies -- have released the results of a new study on the economic impact of Canada 's upcoming Clean Fuel Standard . <p> The study demonstrates that biofuels could be responsible for as much as 21.3 megatons ( MT ) per year of greenhouse gas ( GHG ) emission reductions by 2030 -- approximately 70 percent of the 30 MT target set by the federal government . On this basis , study author , Doyletech Corp. , concludes that meeting the increased demand for biofuels with domestic production would have a one-time construction phase impact of $9.6 billion and 47,100 job-years during the build-out period ; and bring an increase of $21.3 billion per year in economic activity and 12,614 permanent jobs . <p> All levels of government would benefit from increased tax revenues , including : $1.69 billion per year for the federal government ; $1.47 billion per @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for municipal governments . <p> " This objective report provides yet more evidence that the Canadian biofuels industry can play a major role in further reducing greenhouse gas emissions from transportation , while creating jobs and economic growth , " said Jim Grey , RICanada chair . " Our biofuels sector is poised to grow and build upon our past successes . " <p> Ian Thomson , ABFC president , said , " The drive to low carbon fuels is on , with Canada in a race to attract global capital to expand domestic clean fuels . This study shows how a robust CFS can benefit Canadians in agriculture and forestry , as well as provide new , economical solutions for urban and industrial wastes . If we had tallied lower health bills from cleaner air in our cities , the benefits would be even larger . " <p> Since 2010 , Canada 's biofuels industry has been removing the CO2 equivalent of 1 million cars from our roads each year .
