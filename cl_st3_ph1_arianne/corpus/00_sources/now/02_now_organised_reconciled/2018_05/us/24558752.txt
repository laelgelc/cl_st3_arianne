<p> A new study has found that US metropolitan areas are losing about 36 million trees every year across the entire country . That 's equivalent to 175,000 acres of tree cover in central , suburban and exurban fringes . <p> Just as a growing number of Americans are choosing to live in cities , scientists have begun to discover the importance of living near trees for our health and wellbeing . <p> The problem is obvious , and only made worse when urban forests and green spaces in the US are being decimated at such an alarming rate . <p> To give some context , Central Park in New York City is about 840 acres . This means that every year , US cities are losing over 208 Central Parks . <p> The value of this loss is roughly equivalent to about US$96 million in benefits , according to lead author David Nowak of the US Forest Service ( USFS ) . <p> And that number is quite conservative .
