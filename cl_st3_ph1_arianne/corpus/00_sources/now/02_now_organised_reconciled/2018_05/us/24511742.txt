It is the first wolverine to be documented south of I-90 in decades , according to the Cascades Carnivore Project and the U.S. Forest Service . <p> A breeding female wolverine has been documented east of Mount Rainier by researchers from the Cascades Carnivore Project and the U.S. Forest Service , according to a news release . <p> The female wolverine , nicknamed Pepper , is the first female wolverine to be documented south of I-90 in the Washington Cascade Range in decades , the group said . <p> The wolverine was first detected in May 2016 at baited camera stations at the Naches Ranger District . Her DNA was collected and genotyped from a hair sample collected in 2017 as part of the Western Wolverine Conservation Project . <p> Recently , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to identify individual wolverines from photographs based on their unique chest blaze and determine the wolverine 's sex and its reproductive status . <p> Wolverine experts confirmed she is lactating , suggesting she has kits . <p> Wolverines are native to Washington 's Cascade Range but were believed to have disappeared by the mid-1900s , most likely due to unregulated trapping , shooting and poisoning associated with predator control efforts . <p> Individual wolverines moved south out of Canada to recolonize the North Cascades ecosystem , north of I-90 and a small population has been documented there . In 2006 , the Yakama Nation detected a wolverine south of I-90 , on the east slopes of Mount Adams . <p> Wolverines are a hard species to track , said Dr. Jocelyn Akins , conservation director of the Cascades Carnivore Project . <p> " They live in really remote terrain , so just getting to where a wolverine might be hanging out can take the better part of the day , " she said , adding that winter conditions can make getting to these areas even harder .
