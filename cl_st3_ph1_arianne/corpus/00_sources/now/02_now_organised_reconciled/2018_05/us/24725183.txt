<h> The crowded field of candidates means V.I . voters need straight talk <p> There are 119 persons who have registered to contest for elected office in the USVI this coming November . <p> There are nine teams vying for governor and lieutenant governor against incumbents Gov . Kenneth Mapp and Lt. Gov . Osbert Potter . Most are running as independents , one as ICM , and two as Democrats . The Republican Party , with just 1,816 registered members , did not even field candidates . The first and last time the Republican Party won the governorship was in 1970 , when Dr. Melvin Evans became the first elected governor . <p> In this very crowded field , runoffs will be likely . In the first round , no team is likely to clear the hurdle of 50-plus percent of votes needed to be declared an outright winner . <p> This state of affairs says three things : <p> * First , formal political parties seem to have little or no relevance in the USVI . They have no common unifying vision or set of values . They do not serve to winnow candidates down to a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ source of campaign finance . The formal political parties seem to have lost the trust and respect of the majority of people . <p> In a territory with 103,000 people and 48,713 registered voters ( Note : 30 percent of the territory 's population is foreign born ) , there is one gubernatorial candidate for every 5,413 eligible voters . <p> By comparison , in the states , where the population is 325.7 million ( 235.2 million people of voting age ) , there were four candidates for president in 2016 after the primaries meaning 1 candidate for every 58.8 million eligible voters . <p> The U.S. state with the smallest population is Wyoming , which has a population of 582,658 . It had four people running for governor in the last election ( 2014 ) . <p> Of the five U.S. territories , Puerto Rico and Guam have populations larger than the U.S. Virgin Islands , but the Northern Mariana Islands and American Samoa have smaller populations . In the Northern Marianas , four teams contested the governorship in 2014 . The population of the Northern Marianas is 53,467 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ population of 56,000 , three teams contested the gubernatorial elections . <p> These other U.S. possessions have much smaller populations of foreign born , so their eligible voting population is relatively larger than the USVI . <p> Thus , politics in the USVI has been reduced to hyper individualism . It is who has the best fish fry , who hands out the most swag , who was the most popular in school and who has the most friends . <p> * Second , the candidates are not articulating clear , concrete , and realistic plans of actions and stances on the issues of the day . Flowery and aspirational language abounds . The major issues are : <p> ( 3 ) How to have a more efficient , effective , and accountable public sector . <p> ( 4 ) How to diversify and grow the economy . <p> ( 5 ) How to control crime and violence . <p> ( 6 ) How to build a cohesive community . <p> ( 7 ) How to engage in climate change adaptation and diversify the energy matrix . <p> In the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ stouthearted leader faint , does the high number of aspirants for governor of the USVI reflect more a motivation of getting a good salary and attractive set of perks rather than a motivation of service to others and trying to make a difference ? <p> * Third , the biggest hurdle in V.I . politics is the lack of cooperation and comity . For years , the executive and legislative branches have been at odds , stymieing reform efforts and new initiatives . <p> Elected politicians have to forge alliances and work with each other . How do 10 persons running for governor and a third of the incumbent senators running for higher office speak to finding common ground ? <p> The territory needs a critical mass of like-minded , highly committed , highly competent persons in the executive and legislative branches to advance an agenda of prosperity , inclusion , and good governance . <p> How will the gubernatorial candidates be able to work with others , listen to others and build consensus when everyone wants to be " De boss " ? <p> -- Mark D.
