<h> Durban 's beaches will self-recover , but it may take up to two years <p> A grader pushes sand on North Beach . Sand is being pumped on to the city 's beaches to reverse the effects of erosion . <p> OPINION - The visible damage to Durban 's central and northern beaches has brought into sharp focus the impact of human activity and climate change on our fragile coastline . <p> Human activities such as sand mining , dams and harbour construction , coupled with rising sea levels resulting from changing weather patterns , are having increasingly destructive and costly impacts on our coastline . <p> Degradation of our beaches will not only impact the liveability of our city , but since sun , sea , sand and surf are the main economic drivers in our city , the poor state of our beaches will severely restrict economic growth due to declines in tourism . Hence understanding how to preserve or sustainably manage our coastline should be foremost in the minds of planners and developers . <p> Many factors affect shoreline change . These include changes in sand supply , wave characteristics and sea-level rise . The @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is difficult to isolate one main driver of shoreline change . <p> Therefore , it is important to have a systemic understanding of the physical processes that drive shoreline change and what we can do to mitigate the effects . <p> Waves are generated by ? interaction between winds and the ocean surface . Major wave events are typically associated with low pressure weather systems that generate waves and swell that travel long distances before arriving at our beaches . <p> Most of our waves tend to come from the south-easterly direction . One consequence of this is that the waves drive a dominant alongshore current from south to north along our coastline . <p> This , in turn , transports a river of sand in the near-shore zone where the waves are breaking . Any obstruction to the flow within this zone will disrupt the sediment movement . <p> This includes the extension of the Durban Port 's southern breakwater , which blocks sediment moving up the coast and results in the build-up of sand at the southern side of this breakwater . Waves that arrive perpendicular to the shoreline do not drive alongshore currents , and since waves can come from many directions the alongshore current can ? reverse direction . <p> Sand eroded from the beach face is deposited offshore just beyond where the waves start breaking , but over time most of this sand is gradually moved back onshore during ? periods of smaller waves .
