<p> A gap that 's trapping people in poverty and if not tackled urgently could thwart our ambition to achieve sustainable development . <p> This is the central challenge heads of state and government considered at the recent Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific ( ESCAP ) . <p> A strengthened regional approach to more sustainable , inclusive growth must be the outcome . <p> It 's imperative , because ESCAP 's " Sustainable Development Goal ( SGD ) Progress Report " shows that at the current rate of progress , Asia and the Pacific will fall short of achieving the UN 's 2030 Agenda . <p> There has been some welcome progress , including in some of the least developed countries of our region . <p> Healthier lives are being led and well being has increased . Poverty levels are declining , albeit too slowly . But only one SDG , focused on achieving quality education and lifelong learning , is on track to be met . <p> In several critical areas , the region 's heading in the wrong direction . <p> Environmental stewardship has fallen seriously short . <p> The health of our @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ our ecosystems ' biodiversity is threatened . Forest conservation and the protection of natural habitats has weakened . Greenhouse gas emissions are still too high . <p> But it 's the widening inequalities during a period of robust growth that are particularly striking . <p> Wealth has become increasingly concentrated . <p> Inequalities have increased both within and between countries . <p> Over 30 years , the Gini coefficient , measuring the distribution of wealth , increased in four of our most populous countries , home to over 70 per cent of the region 's population . Human , societal and economic costs are real . <p> Had income inequality not increased over the past decade , close to 140 million more people could have been lifted out of poverty . <p> More women would have had the opportunity to attend school and complete their secondary education . <p> Access to healthcare , to basic sanitation or even bank accounts would have been denied to fewer citizens . <p> Fewer people would have died from diseases caused by the fuels they cook with . <p> Natural disasters would have wrought @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is that inequality runs deep in many parts of Asia and the Pacific . <p> There 's no silver bullet , no handy lever we can reach for to reduce it overnight . But an integrated , coordinated approach can , over time , return our economies and our societies to a sustainable footing . Recent ESCAP analysis provides recommendations on how to do just that . <p> At their heart is a call to invest in our people : to improve access to healthcare and education . <p> Only a healthy population can study , work and become more prosperous . The universal basic healthcare schemes established by Bhutan and Thailand are success stories to build on . Expanding social protection to low income families through cash transfers can also help underpin a healthy society . <p> Increasing investment in education is fundamental to both development and equality . <p> Here the key to success is making secondary education genuinely accessible and affordable , including for those living in rural areas . Where universal access has been achieved , the focus must be on improving quality . It can quicken the pace of development . But it is also creating a digital divide that must be bridged . <p> So investment in ICT infrastructure is key , to support innovative technologies and ensure no one is left behind . Put simply , we need better broadband access across our region . Geography ca n't determine opportunity . <p> This is also true when it comes to tackling climate change , disasters and environmental degradation . Public expenditure could then be made more efficient and progressive , the proceeds of growth shared more widely , and inequalities reduced . <p> My hope is that leaders will seize the moment , strengthen our commitment to fighting inequality on all fronts and put us back on track to sustainable development in Asia and the Pacific . <p> The writer is the under-secretary-general of the United Nations and executive secretary of Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific ( ESCAP ) .
