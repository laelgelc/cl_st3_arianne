<h> Share this story <p> The carbon footprint of tourism is about four times larger than previously thought , according to a world-first study published today in Nature Climate Change . <p> Researchers from the University of Sydney , University of Queensland and National Cheng Kung University -- including ourselves -- worked together to assess the entire supply chain of tourism . This includes transportation , accommodation , food and beverages , souvenirs , clothing , cosmetics and other goods . <p> Put together , global tourism produces about 8 per cent of global greenhouse gas emissions , much more than previous estimates . <h> Adding it all up <p> To determine the true emissions produced by tourism , we scanned over a billion supply chains of a range of commodities consumed by tourists . By combining a detailed international trade database with accounts tracking what goods and services tourists bought , we identified carbon flows between 160 countries from 2009 to 2013 . <p> Our results show that tourism-related emissions increased by around 15 per cent over that period , from 3.9 gigatonnes ( Gt ) of carbon-dioxide equivalent ( CO2-e ) to 4.5Gt . The former perspective allocates emissions to the country of residence of tourists , the latter to the country of destination . Put simply , are tourism-related carbon emissions the responsibility of travellers or tourist destinations ? <p> If responsibility lies with the travellers , then we should identify the countries that send the most tourists out into the world , and find ways to reduce the carbon footprint of their travel . <p> On the other hand , destination-based accounting can offer insights into tourism @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ from technology improvements and regulations for reducing the carbon footprint of tourism . <p> Tracking emissions under destination-based accounting over a specific period could help researchers and policymakers to answer questions about the success of incentive schemes and regulations , and to assess the speed of decarbonisation of tourism-related sectors . <p> So how do countries rank under the two accounting perspectives ?
