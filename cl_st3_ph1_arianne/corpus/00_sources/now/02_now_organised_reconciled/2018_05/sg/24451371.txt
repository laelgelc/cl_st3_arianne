<h> Diseases from ticks , fleas , mosquitoes soar in US <p> A pamphlet educating people about mosquitos hangs on a fence in Miami in 2017 <p> Diseases from mosquitoes , ticks and flea bites tripled in the United States from 2004 to 2016 , and officials said Tuesday rising temperatures and an increasingly connected global society are to blame . <p> More than 642,000 cases of these illnesses were reported during the 13 years studied in the Vital Signs report from the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention . <p> Cases of Lyme disease , Rocky Mountain spotted fever , West Nile and Zika numbered more than 27,000 in 2004 , and soared to over 96,000 in 2016 , it said , warning that since many cases go unreported , these numbers are likely " substantially " lower than the true amount . <p> These diseases pose " an increasing risk " and the " nation needs to be better prepared to face this public health threat , " said the report . <p> " Zika , West Nile , Lyme , and chikungunya -- a growing list of diseases caused by the bite of an infected @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ US in recent years , making a lot of people sick , " said CDC director Robert Redfield . <p> Currently , the most common tick-borne disease in the United States is Lyme disease . <p> The most common mosquito-borne viruses are West Nile , dengue , and Zika , while the most common disease resulting from the bite of an infected flea is the plague . <p> Lyle Petersen , director of the CDC division of vector-borne diseases , said increasing temperatures can help such diseases spread , though he declined to say specifically if climate change is the cause . <p> " Many of these diseases are sensitive to increasing temperatures , " he told reporters on a conference call . <p> " Increasing temperatures will tend to expand the ranges of these ticks further north as well as increasing the length of tick season .
