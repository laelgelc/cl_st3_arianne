<p> There are 8,236 MPAs around the world , covering 4% of the total surface of the oceans . <p> They were set up to provide safe havens for wildlife and conserve endangered habitats by restricting human activities such as fishing , mining and tourism . <p> The new study published in the journal Nature Climate Change suggests that without drastic action MPAs will be " devastated " by rapid global warming . <p> Lead scientist Professor John Bruno , from the University of North Carolina in the US , said : " With warming of this magnitude , we expect to lose many , if not most , animal species from Marine Protected Areas by the turn of the century . <p> " To avoid the worst outcomes , we need to immediately adopt an emission reduction scenario in which emissions peak within the next two decades and then decrease very significantly , replacing fossil fuels with cleaner energy sources like solar and wind . " <p> The scientists carried out simulations to model sea surface temperatures and oxygen concentrations in MPAs around the world , including those where fishing is banned . <p> They found that even @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " business-as-usual " emissions scenario , MPAs were expected to warm by 0.034C per year . <p> By the end of the century ocean temperatures in MPAs will have increased by an average of 2.8C , they said . <p> For each MPA the scientists calculated a " community thermal safety margin " ( CTSM ) , the critical point after which most species would not be able to tolerate further change in their living conditions . <p> In tropical areas , this threshold was expected to be reached by about 2050 . <p> MPAs in the Arctic and Antarctic were among those at greatest risk , said the scientists .
