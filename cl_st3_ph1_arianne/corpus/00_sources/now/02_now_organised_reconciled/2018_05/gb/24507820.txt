<h> Scottish university team makes climate change breakthrough <p> WITH climate change affecting many of the world 's estimated 200,000 glaciers , Edinburgh scientists have made a breakthrough in charting the loss of ice which is increasing sea levels worldwide . <p> The researchers from Edinburgh University are using satellite data to map complex changes in ice cover on glaciers in Patagonia , the area shared between Chile and Argentina in South America . <p> The Edinburgh scientists are part of the European Space Agency 's CryoSat mission . CryoSat has been monitoring sea ice levels since it was launched in 2010 and is now examining glaciers . <p> The study , published in the journal Remote Sensing of Environment , has revealed that widespread thinning of the region 's glaciers is causing global sea levels to rise by around 0.06 mm each year . <p> The team from the University 's School of GeoSciences has developed a new way of analysing satellite radar data which allows them to map glaciers in greater detail than ever before . This was an increase of almost 25 per cent compared with the amount of ice lost between 2000 and 2014 . <p> The Jorge Montt glacier retreated 2.5 kilometres and lost around 2.2 Gt each year . By contrast , Pio XI -- the largest glacier in South America -- advanced and gained mass at a rate of around 0.67 Gt per year . <p> It has until now proven difficult to monitor the speed at which glaciers in Patagonia are changing . Satellite radar readings have been used to map large ice sheets for the past 25 years .
