<h> RBS updates lending policy in bid to tackle climate change <p> ROYAL Bank of Scotland is taking a harder line on climate change and has updated its energy policy , pledging to cut lending to firms profiting from coal and no longer funding Arctic oil projects . <p> The changes covering the mining , power , and oil and gas sectors , mean the bank will not provide " project-specific finance " to new coal-fired power stations , new thermal coal mines , oil sands or Arctic oil projects , or those involved in " unsustainable " vegetation or peatland clearing . <p> RBS will also tighten restrictions on general lending to mining firms that source more than 40% of their revenues from thermal coal , and power companies that generate over 40% of their electricity from coal . This marks a reduction from the former threshold of 65% . <p> The new lending restrictions come ahead of the bank 's AGM in Edinburgh today , and may spare it from the kind of protests that disrupted the Barclays annual meeting earlier this month , when climate change activists called on it to stop financing firms involved in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ where Barclays executives were seated and had to be forcibly removed . Barclays chairman John McFarlane said his bank is in the process of preparing a formal review of the fossil fuel sector that it hopes to publish later this year . <p> RBS director of sustainable banking , Kirsty Britz , said : " If we 're going to support our customers in the long run , then it means addressing the challenge of climate change and the risks and opportunities it presents . <p> " We want to help build a cleaner , more sustainable economy for the future , and these policy changes form part of our broader approach to this major issue . " <p> The bank 's efforts have been commended by responsible investment group ShareAction , which has been pushing a number of firms to ramp up their environmental sustainability policies . <p> ShareAction project manager Sonia Hierzig said : " The strengthened energy financing policies of RBS implement many of ShareAction 's recommendations for more robust management of climate-related risks .
