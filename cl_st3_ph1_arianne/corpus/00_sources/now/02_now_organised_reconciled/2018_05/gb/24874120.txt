Even after decades of sustained research , no one knows how many of these big and extremely elusive cats remain . Researchers from all 12 Asian nations that these cats call home are trying to remedy that with a five-year census using camera traps set up on remote mountains , radio collaring , and hair and scat identification . <p> The survey comes on the heels of a controversial decision last year by the International Union for Conservation of Nature ( IUCN ) to remove the snow leopard from its list of " endangered " species after 45 years . But getting results wo n't be easy . <p> That 's because unlike other big cats , snow leopards are specially evolved for cold heights . Snow leopards have the longest and thickest fur of any big cat , and a long , thick tail that they can wrap around themselves to stay warm . <p> A large nasal cavity and broad facial bones allow the leopards to breathe in very cold air and warm it for breathing , and a high concentration of red blood cells maximises oxygen intake , meaning they do n't easily get tired while climbing mountains . In fact , they 're comfortable at heights reaching 18,000 feet . All these features are essential for their survival and make them especially hard to track , even for the hardiest researchers . <p> But leopards are , to a degree , predictable -- frequenting game trails and ridges and often spraying rocks to mark their territory .
