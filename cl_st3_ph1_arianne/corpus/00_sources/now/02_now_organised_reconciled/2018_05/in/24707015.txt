<h> Forest loss in one part of US can harm trees on the opposite coast <p> 18 May 2018 <p> Large swaths of US forests are vulnerable to drought , forest fires and disease . Many local impacts of forest loss are well known : drier soils , stronger winds , increased erosion , loss of shade and habitat . But if a whole forest disappears , new research shows , this has ricocheting effects in the atmosphere that can affect vegetation on the other side of the country . <p> A University of Washington-led study published 16 May in Environmental Research Letters shows that forest die-offs in specific regions of the United States can influence plant growth in other parts of the country . The largest impacts seen were from losing forest cover in California , a region that is currently experiencing dramatic tree mortality . <p> " These smaller areas of forest can have continental-scale impacts , and we really need to be considering this when we 're thinking about ecological changes , " says first author Abigail Swann , a University of Washington ( UW ) assistant professor of atmospheric sciences and of biology .
