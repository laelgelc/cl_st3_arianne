<p> Prime Minister Scott Morrison visited firefighters battling Australia 's bushfire crisis on Sunday as he apologised for a Hawaiian holiday that ended early after public outrage . <p> Morrison toured the headquarters of the New South Wales Rural Fire Service -- whose exhausted volunteers have been struggling to contain deadly , out-of-control-blazes for months on end -- where he admitted he had erred in travelling overseas . <p> Morrison had departed for a family holiday amid record bushfires that have destroyed an area the size of Belgium and cloaked major cities from Brisbane to Sydney to Canberra to Melbourne in choking toxic fumes . <p> " I get it that people would have been upset to know that I was holidaying with my family while their families were under great stress , " he said . <p> " If you had your time over again and the benefit of hindsight we would have made different decisions . " <p> News of his holiday prompted street protests and widespread criticism on social media , with Australians deploying the hashtag #WhereTheBloodyHellAreYa ? <p> " I apologise , " he said . " There have been lessons learned this week , " adding that he believed it was time to move on from the controversy . <p> " I 'm sure Australians are fair-minded and understand that when you make a promise to your kids , you try and keep it , " the conservative leader said by way of explanation . <p> The embattled prime minister again acknowledged some link between climate change and weather patterns that scientists say has fuelled the fire crisis , but he indicated there would be no change in pro-coal policies . <p> -- Heavy rain needed -- Morrison also praised volunteer firefighters , who in the last 24 hours have faced catastrophic conditions brought by a record heatwave , gale-force winds and prolonged drought . <p> Australia 's firefighting force is overwhelmingly made up of volunteers who have been strained by the intensity and the length of this year 's fire season . <p> Conditions eased markedly on Sunday , giving them time to try to contain massive blazes near Sydney that are only likely to be extinguished with heavy rainfall . <p> Large scale back-burning is planned over the next few days before conditions are expected to worsen again in a week 's time . <p> Rain is expected in some fire-hit areas of New South Wales on Tuesday and Wednesday , a welcome Christmas gift for many . <p> But firefighters are still measuring the toll of Saturday 's destruction . <p> " We 've seen widespread damage and destruction being reported across a number of these fire grounds , " said New South Wales Rural Fire Services boss Shane Fitzsimmons , who earlier described Saturday as " an awful day " . <p> " The devastation is shocking , " said New South Wales premier Gladys Berejiklian . " We have got the devastating news that there 's not much left in the town of Balmoral . " <p> The state of South Australia has also been hit hard . Two people died in fires there over the past two days , and dozens of firefighters and residents have been treated for injuries and smoke inhalation . <p> State premier Steven Marshall said that 72 homes had been destroyed in the Adelaide Hills alone , according to public broadcaster ABC . <p> -- Public health emergency -- Officials said two fires -- already big enough to create their own thunderstorms -- had joined to form another " megafire " to the southwest of Sydney . <p> A fire-generated thunderstorm can occur when a smoke plume is cooled as it meets pressure in the atmosphere , creating a cloud capable of producing its own lightning and powerful winds . <p> The fires have torched at least three million hectares ( 7.4 million acres ) of land with at least 10 people killed and more than 800 homes destroyed . <p> Leading doctors have warned of a public health emergency given the unprecedented toxic smoke choking Sydney , which again became a dense haze on Saturday . <p> " It is pretty much the whole New South Wales population being exposed to prolonged smoke and because we have never experienced this before , we do n't know what the eventual outcome will be , " doctor Kim Loo told AFP . <p> " It probably wo n't be obvious for months , or even years , " added Loo , who is also a member of advocacy group Doctors for the Environment . <p> Hospitals have recorded sharp increases in emergency room visits for heat exhaustion and respiratory problems . <p> The comments posted on this site do not necessarily represent or reflect the views of management and owner of POLITICS.com.ph .
