<p> When Barack Obama 's White House Chief of Staff remarked : " You never want a serious crisis to go to waste " , he predictably came in for some flak . Opponents presented it as being typical of cynical politicians who would happily manipulate events - even tragedies - to suit their own agendas and interests . Yet Rahm Emanuel was hardly the first person to see in a crisis " an opportunity to do things that you had never considered , or that you did n't think were possible " . John F Kennedy liked to say ( wrongly ) that the word " crisis " is formed in the Chinese language using two characters ; one representing danger and the other opportunity . A world away from American politics , much of Australia is currently in the grip of a bushfire crisis . And our Prime Minister seized the opportunity to go on holiday . If the smoke suffocating Sydney was not troubling Scott Morrison as he sunned himself on a beach in , reputedly , Hawaii , the storm his absence provoked did . Twitter is far from a good sample of Australian voters , yet it did not escape attention that one of the top trending hashtags this week was whereisScoMo . Cartoonist Pope may have captured the mood beautifully in a work published in our Thursday edition evoking Saville Lumley 's famous wartime recruiting poster . The PM is pictured in a lounge chair - colourful lei contrasting a brown suit , with a child on his lap who looks up from her book to ask : " Daddy , what did you do in the climate emergency ? " Aside from the furore over his ill-timed holiday , much of the criticism has been aimed at the idea that all Mr Morrison can offer are " thoughts and prayers " . It 's almost like the nation is channeling the famously impassioned plea of Hawthorn legend John Kennedy Sr : " At least do something ! Do ! Do n't think , do n't hope , do ! " Whether any of this is fair is largely irrelevant because perception is reality in politics . And Mr Morrison garners no sympathy since in 2010 he joined in the public stoning of a former Victorian Police Commissioner who , it had emerged , went out for dinner on Black Saturday . That clip of his waffling commentary about Christine Nixon 's " bad judgment call " came back to haunt him . So it was little surprise that with New South Wales Premier Gladys Berejiklian declaring a state of emergency as fire conditions again worsened , the PM made the decision to cut his holiday short . His " deep regret for any offence caused " showed Mr Morrison well and truly felt the public 's wrath . But what now ? What should he be doing now he 's back in the country ? Many would like Mr Morrison to find in the raging bushfires an opportunity to act - to not let this " serious crisis got to waste " . In their minds , the fires are intrinsically linked to the impact of climate change ; being symptomatic of the " climate emergency " Pope was referencing . Others - heads stuck in the sand opposing the scientific consensus or not - argue that 's not the case by pointing to such natural disasters throughout our history . Heading into summer though , those responsible , past and present , for leading our firefighting effort were stating that climate change is having an effect . It 's been widely reported that a group of more than 20 former fire and emergency leaders tried unsuccessfully for much of the year to get a meeting with the PM to warn about the coming season . The Emergency Leaders for Climate Action believe there is " a denial of the problem " - being climate change - and are planning to hold a national summit after the bushfire crisis . Mr Morrison and his ministers have pointed to the help they have provided to the states , such as boosting the federal contribution to the National Aerial Firefighting Centre , which coordinates the use of aircraft . That 's practical assistance , but it does nothing to address climate change , and that 's where the demand for action is growing in its intensity . When Time magazine chose a Swedish teenager - and climate change activist - to be its Person of the Year earlier this month it showed how prominent the issue has become around the world . On the other side , however , there is also strong opinion against action in the name of climate change , particularly that which would hit our hip pockets . The often churlish backlash against Greta Thunberg , and young people generally who are concerned about their future , demonstrates it 's an incredibly divisive subject . Mr Morrison knows rusted-on Coalition voters would not be impressed by the declaration of a climate emergency . It 's true too that even if Australia magically went carbon neutral overnight , it would do nothing to change our climate and diminish the threat of bushfires . But that 's not an excuse for a failure to act , particularly for failing to work for the international agreement needed to achieve meaningful outcomes .
