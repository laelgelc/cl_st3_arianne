<h> The decade Australia became a dangerous place for prime ministers <p> It was the decade when Australia became a dangerous land for Prime Ministers - with the fall of Kevin Rudd and the end of Malcolm Turnbull 's reign bookending ten years of political chaos and instability . <h> The End of Kevin 07 <p> The clinically planned night-time dispatch of Kevin Rudd as Prime Minister shocked the nation and began Australia 's dismal decade of political chaos , writes Chris Uhlmann . <p> The deed was done in the dark of night on June 23 , 2010 but the assassins had been sharpening their blades for long time . Rudd was never a creature of the Labor Party and although popular with the people , he had few allies in his ranks . His bad-temper , chaotic leadership and sharp tongue helped fuel internal resentment . <p> Rudd won a thumping victory in 2007 and flew high in opinion polls until October 2009 , when his dismantling of the Coalition 's tough border controls led to a spike in asylum seeker boat arrivals . He took another hit when his ambitious plan to impose a price on carbon emissions was defeated in the Senate and Malcolm Turnbull was replaced as Opposition leader by a stone-cold political killer , Tony Abbott . <p> Rudd had declared that climate change " the greatest moral challenge of our time " but as Abbott gained ground in early 2010 , and with an election looming , he shelved his plans to cut carbon and his poll numbers crashed . Rumblings about a leadership change began in earnest . <p> But by Wednesday June 23 , and with Parliament scheduled to rise for the long Winter recess the next day , Rudd appeared safe . That afternoon I was at Aussies , a caf ? inside Parliament House , when a Labor powerbroker invited me to share a coffee . As soon as we sat down he lowered his voice and said " Could we win with Julia Gillard ? " I said , " I did n't think that was an option " and before I could ask a question he bolted , leaving an untouched coffee behind . <p> I was working for the ABC at the time and raced back to office where I began hitting the phones with my colleague Mark Simkin . At 7pm we broke the news that a leadership spill was afoot , driving the plotters into the spotlight . <p> Julia Gillard was leader by the next day and Australia became a dangerous land for Prime Ministers . <h> The fall of Abbott <p> Missing victory by a whisker in 2010 , Tony Abbott presided over a landslide win for the Coalition on September 7 , 2013 , against Rudd , who had just months earlier replaced Gillard after a Labor leadership spill , writes Kerrie Yaxley . <p> By January 2015 , things had started to unravel . <p> On Australia Day that year jaws were dropping throughout Coalition ranks . <p> Prime Minister Abbott -- who had brought back the knights and dames honours system the previous year -- made the Duke of Edinburgh , Prince Philip a knight in the order of Australia . <p> A move so out of touch with modern day Australia , it was incomprehensible to many . To his critics and others , it was proof that he was a voice from the past . <p> It was a captain 's call . A bad one . Questions about his judgement grew louder and the backbench started to feel nervous . <p> The anxiety and chatter grew resulting in a challenge with no challenger . Party members voted 61-39 against opening the leadership positions to a vote . <p> Prime Minister Tony Abbott and fellow MPs arrive for a special Liberal party room meeting at Parliament House in Canberra . ( AAP ) <p> In political terms , the prime minister was mortally wounded . He 'd lost the support of a significant portion of his party . <p> Regardless , the Prime Minister had time -- which he 'd been asking for . <p> Contributing to the angst among his coalition colleagues , was concern about the Prime Minister 's Chief of Staff Peta Credlin and the power she wielded within the Government . <p> Mr Abbott dug in firmly behind her and for the most part refused to act , let alone dismiss her . <p> His loyalty toward the Speaker Bronwyn Bishop also cost him political skin . <p> It was revealed the Member for McKellar blatantly misused her travel entitlements , chartering a helicopter to travel a relatively short distance to a Liberal Party fundraiser . <p> It did n't pass the pub test of any level . Yet for far too long , the Prime Minister stood by her , one of his oldest allies until finally public pressure became too strong and Ms Bishop stood down . <p> Again , concerns about the Prime Minister 's judgement were growing . <p> That resentment was fuelled for some , by the Government 's marathon meeting over what to do about the growing momentum to change the Marriage Act to legalise same sex marriage . <p> There was further splintering between the conservatives and moderates . No one left the meeting satisfied .
