<p> Influential businessman and Orica chairman Malcolm Broomhead has lamented the rise of a culture of " self-righteousness " and a mindset where people are " intolerant " of others . <p> In a speech to Orica shareholders , Mr Broomhead said " the decline we see of reasoned , articulate and productive discussion and debate , at almost every level in our society " , was a challenge to Orica and most companies in western economies . <p> " It 's a great shame that 30 years after the fall of the Berlin Wall , where there was great hope of a future united world , we seem to just be pulling ourselves apart in a frenzy of self-righteousness , " he said at the explosives maker 's annual general meeting on Tuesday . <p> " And there 's an attitude of ' I 'm right and I 'll shout down anyone who disagrees ' , and it 's becoming increasingly prevalent , " he said . <p> Advertisement <p> Mr Broomhead , a director of the world 's biggest miner , BHP , and a widely respected figure in business circles , also lamented the impact of social media on debate . <p> " People tend to listen , as a result of new media , more to their own echo chambers and are becoming more intolerant of the general views of others , " he noted . <p> Widely respected in business circles , Broomhead is the current chairman of Orica and a director at BHP.Credit:Joe Armao <p> Mr Broomhead noted this phenomenon was widespread and occurring in identity politics , the climate change debate and in Australia 's approach to China , where some people focus only on the security aspect of the relationship without considering the wider economic and trading relationship between the countries . <p> The Orica chairman also expressed concern about what he termed an attitude of " extreme pessimism " among some people .
