( AAP Image/Dean Lewins ) <p> Opinion <h> Challenges lay ahead of us <p> Bill HoffmanWhether taking on developers hell-bent on destroying the Coast 's natural appeal or a Prime Minister indifferent to the plight of the poor , Bill Hoffman has never been one to mince his words . Bill 's been a journalist for 32 years , 29 of those on the Coast . Love him or hate him , he 'll get you blogging . <p> AS WE move into the Christmas week whether Christian or not in this secular Australia , rich or poor , we have much to be thankful for . <p> Here on the Sunshine Coast , whether it be through an accident of birth or choice of destination , we live in a place where whatever our circumstance we remain bound and enriched by values that have driven the many acts of generosity and sacrifice this paper has recorded over the past 12 months . <p> As individuals our views on what 's best for the region and which direction we should take as a nation remain at times at great variance to the point of polar opposition . <p> But , a capacity to think beyond our individual needs and to act in the service of others has been retained -- despite seemingly constant change -- as part of region 's DNA . <p> Whether it be through donation or effort , the sacrifice of time , or as we have witnessed over the past four months , the willingness of many to stand in the face of the fires and fight for the homes and properties of others , there is much that should at this time give pause for reflection and gratitude . <p> That this innate good can survive in an environment where our politics have become more divisive to the point of bitterness and where social media has provided an arena in which to swing virtual bats at each other is , if not a miracle , certainly a reminder of just how lucky we are to live here . <p> We will enter the new decade though coming off a year where a federal election has done nothing to build a shared vision for the future , plunging into first local government and then state elections that will dominate the year 's agenda . <p> Whatever individual views may be on which course that future should take , it is to be hoped that voters go to the next two ballot boxes having informed themselves fully of the issues and potential consequences of their choices . <p> We are fast approaching the point where we are in danger of losing grip on the amenity and sense of community that either brought most of us here in the first place or have encouraged us to stay . <p> No number of illusionary promises of more jobs or greater prosperity have the capacity to replace the very simple things that exist here to enjoy for free and that remain gifts of our geography . <p> How precious those gifts and just how fragile they are , have been demonstrated clearly by this already extended bushfire season which has seen some people lose their homes and many others forced from theirs with startling regularity during recent months . <p> With no rain in sight and forecasts firm for hotter and dryer months to come , no protest or political verbiage has better focused minds to the realities of climate change . <p> The challenges it presents ca n't continue to be avoided , dismissed or denied and will require long overdue policy and action . <p> A continuation of the status quo -- whether that be inexcusably blinkered land-use planning , farming practice , waste-ful consumption or damaging energy use -- should no longer be tolerable to anyone with the capacity to think beyond their own immediate self-interest to consider forces that threaten the futures of our children and grandchildren . It is both fanciful and lazy to dismiss the dangers ahead as nothing more than a hoax , some sort of global conspiracy hatched by a united cabal of grant-seeking scientists and opportunists . <p> Just as a shameful indifference to the circumstance of indigenous Australians and poor souls trapped in the statelessness of detention hang over us as a shameful pall , so too does the dismissal of the voices of an increasing number of young people demanding more attention be paid to the science and reality . <p> Whatever the promises that may be made as we head to the polls locally on March 28 and for state elections in late October , if they remain unmeasured by the reality of undeniable fundamental change to come , they are ultimately of no worth . <p> My sincere gratitude to everyone who has provided their time to assist me during the past 12 months with background and information that has helped me produce this column on a near weekly basis and to write about the issues that affect us as individuals and communities . <p> Regardless of claims by some , that we now live in an era of fake news the reality is that people are still turning to newspapers to speak truth to power and to give them a voice they feel lost to the indifference of process . <p> May this Christmas period , whether for you a time for reunion or religious celebration , bring joy and happiness .
