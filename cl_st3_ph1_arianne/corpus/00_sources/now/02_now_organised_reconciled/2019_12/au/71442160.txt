<h> Ardern starts spending spree with schools <p> New Zealand prime minister Jacinda Ardern has cleared the way for an election year cash splash , closing the annual Labour party conference by writing bumper cheques for school repairs . <p> Her government will spend $400 million on improving public school facilities as part of what it calls a major infrastructure investment . <p> Ardern was given a rapturous welcome by the party faithful in Whanganui on Sunday when she announced " the largest increase in maintenance funding for schools in roughly 25 years " . <p> Schools will receive a one-off payment of between NZ$50,000 to NZ$400,000 and will be told to spend it within two years . <p> The government will commit billions more tof spending projects before Christmas . <p> On December 11 at the government 's half-yearly economic and fiscal update , the government will release its spending shackles , announcing new projects aimed at stimulating the economy . <p> New Zealand certainly has the cash to do so . <p> In October , finance minister Grant Robertson announced a NZ$7.5 billion surplus - the country 's best fiscal result in a decade . <p> And so now they 're spending , beginning with schools . <p> " We have the lowest borrowing costs in New Zealand 's history , so it is time to invest , " Robertson said . <p> " In terms of where we are in the economic cycle , in terms of the good shape that the Government 's books are in ; now is the time to take the next step forward in our legacy of building New Zealand . " <p> Ardern said she had n't visited a state school during her time in office that did n't need maintenance work . <p> " The bonus of focusing first on schools and education is that it will affect every community , every city , every town in New Zealand and have a really positive impact , " she said . <p> " It 's for those extra things that schools often defer out of desperation , and now we 're giving them a significant boost . " <p> Ardern 's announcement came at the Royal Whanganui Opera House , a tired but stately centrepiece of the North Island regional city . <p> She was preceded by a comedian and traditional singing performance , where Maori MPs joined artists on stage . <p> The mood among the many Labour members , volunteers and supporters was vibrant and celebratory- just as you 'd expect given Ardern 's saintly reputation in the party . <p> The prime minister was feted during a half-hour address , enjoying her best reception during a recount of climate change legislative achievements and the new education spending . <p> But the conference was n't all beer and skittles . <p> A closed-door session focused on " creating a safe and inclusive party " in response to a damaging sexual assault scandal which drew the resignation of previous president Nigel Haworth . <p> In his place , multi-talented Aucklander Claire Szabo was elected on Saturday to lead the party 's organisational wing in an election year . <p> With a war chest to spend , a popular prime minister and favourable economic indicators , in a country that does n't turf first-term governments - it 's easy to understand the confidence of Labour supporters heading into the poll , expected in Spring 2020.
