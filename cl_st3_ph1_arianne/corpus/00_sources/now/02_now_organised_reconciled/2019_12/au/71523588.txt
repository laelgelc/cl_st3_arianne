<h> Mayoral candidate prepared to do the top job for no pay <p> CHRIS Hooper ran for mayor of the Rockhampton region at the last election on a promise that he didn not want to be paid if he got the job . <p> And he 's prepared to do the same again at the local government elections in March 2020 . <p> As was the case last time , the 67-year-old pensioner intends to base his campaign policies around environmental issues . <p> " I was environmental last time and it 's about environmental stuff again , " Mr Hooper said . <p> " Climate change , or whatever you want to call it - a lot of councils around Australia have come on board with that and acknowledged it , whereas Rockhampton does n't want to acknowledge it . <p> " Locally we could do a lot of things , like I think you 've got to have free public transport . " <p> Mr Hooper said he had suggested this idea previously to the council . <p> " They sort of laughed at me and said ' we 've got nothing to do with that , it 's a state government thing ' . <p> " Rockhampton Regional Council really has got zero input into how the buses run in Rocky . <p> " My angle there is - and it might be a bit simplistic but that 's the way I act - is if you give people free buses then you might be able to change the way they operate and get back to having one-car families . <p> " If people do n't believe there 's something going on with the poor old planet - you know I think people are getting a bit worried now when it 's not raining . " <p> Mr Hooper said he would also like to see local politicians from the three tiers of government working more closely together . <p> " They 're not working for the community basically , they 're working for their side of the fence . <p> " That needs to change . " <p> Despite his age , Mr Hooper said he 'd like to see more younger people run for councillor roles .
