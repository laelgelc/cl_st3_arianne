<h> How 2019 became the year of the climate strike -- and mass demands for action <p> Thousands of students , parents and workers from across Australia united in a mass climate strike in September , demanding urgent action on global warming . <p> At least 2000 businesses allowed employees to take Friday , September 20 , off to join in 110 city and regional locations , as protesters marched in the streets across the country and gathered in parklands , holding signs including " Stop Adani " and " Stop drilling in the Bight " . <p> The climate strikes -- which were mirrored by more mass crowds in cities around the world -- were inspired by teenage Swedish activist Greta Thunberg . In 2019 , the lone protests she had started outside the Swedish parliament sparked a growing global movement that has drawn in millions -- and helped lead to her being named Time magazine 's person of the year . <p> The movement started to gain momentum in Australia In November 2018 , when thousands of school students went on strike . They followed that up with a mass global day of action in March . <p> September 's rally , however , also drew hundreds of thousands of protestors from all walks of life . Office workers , tradies , artists , grandparents , doctors and baby boomers joined in demonstrations calling for urgent action on global warming . <p> Melbourne drew the nation 's biggest crowd -- organisers said about 150,000 people attended the rally in the Victorian capital 's CBD alone . <p> Among the diverse Australians to attend was Koo Wee Rup Secondary College student Ethan Lewis , 14 , who travelled more than 70 kilometres to Melbourne with his family to attend . <p> " I want to see animals thrive and I want to be able to go to the Great Barrier Reef without it being bleached , " Ethan , a member of his school 's environment club , told The New Daily in Melbourne . <p> " I want to be able to have those experiences for future generations and to preserve our Earth . " <p> His brother Hunter , 12 , said he was there because he wanted to " have a better future " and to ensure we have a " bigger range of species " . <p> The Lewis family travelled to Melbourne for the #ClimateStrike protests . Photo : The New Daily <p> Matthew Flinders Girls Secondary College student Annie Stephens , 17 , travelled from Geelong to Melbourne to rally with her friends . <p> " I 'm here because the government is doing nothing about climate change and I just feel like it 's time that we step in and actually do something , " Annie told The New Daily . <p> " I made this sign this morning on my bedroom floor . " <p> Thousands of people aged 50 years and older also turned up en masse to show their support . <p> Janice Miller , 57 , a support worker at sustainable food centre Cultivating Community , said she was at the rally to support Australia 's youth . <p> " I want to get more action on climate change from leaders and I want to support the young people who started this , " Ms Miller said . <p> Long-time climate activist Carol Henderson , 53 , said she was inspired by the large number of student activists pushing for change . <p> Turtles Against Climate Change member John McDonald , 64 , said he was particularly concerned about the effect of global warming on our turtle population . <p> " Turtles are already threatened by climate change , " Mr McDonald told The New Daily . <p> " The sand is getting so hot where they 're hatching that some of them are dying just as they 're getting out of their nests . <p> " The beaches are being washed away and the Great Barrier Reef is being destroyed too . " <p> Cultivating Community members at the rally . Photo : The New Daily <p> However , the mss rallies did n't have everyone 's support . Deputy Prime Minister Michael McCormack slammed them as a " disruption " to schools and businesses , arguing students would learn more at school than at a rally . <p> " I think these sorts of rallies should be held on a weekend where it does n't actually disrupt business , it does n't disrupt schools , it does n't disrupt universities , " Mr McCormack said . <p> But the comments did n't stop the students -- or their parents , grandparents or teachers from marching . <p> Nor has it stopped the momentum . On Wednesday , December 11 , thousands more gathered in Sydney to continue to demand climate change action as the devastating bushfire crisis rolls on across NSW . <p> One police officer estimated there were more that 7500 people outside the Sydney Town Hall . Some wore the face masks many Sydney-siders have donned to cope with weeks of smoke haze , while others brandished signs reading : " Denial is not a policy " , " Less debate more climate action " , " For my grandkids " and " Climate change is a public health emergency " . <p> Marcus James , from Sydney , said climate action was an issue across all sectors of politics and he felt a moral obligation to attend . <p> " I cared about the issue for quite a while but I think having the bushfires reach Sydney is very , very -- I suppose it makes it quite lurid , " he said . <p> " It 's right in your face and you 're breathing it in . <p> " I think it highlights that climate change and bushfires and natural disasters around the world but especially in Australia , does n't discriminate and it sort of affects all sectors of the population . " <p> Elise Vohradsky , from Western Sydney , said she was motivated to attend after seeing smoke shroud the city on Tuesday . <p> " I went outside and could n't even see anything , like it was just smog , " she said . <p> " There 's so much loss and devastation -- and not just the people , the cities but the environment , the animals . <p> " At least I can turn up and be part of a visual representation of how many people are n't cool with this . " <p> Babette Robertson , who grew up in an area of the NSW mid north coast , that has been hit by the bushfires , said her family had been spared but their friends had lost homes . <p> " It 's so devastating . I just find myself crying some days ... and dad just sends photos every day and it 's just so heartbreaking , " she said . <p> She accused the government of ignoring the science of climate change and said she hoped she would be heard and people 's concerns would be acknowledged .
