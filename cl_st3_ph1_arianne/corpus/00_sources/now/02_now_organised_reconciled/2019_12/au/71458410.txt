<h> Super fund members vote with their feet on climate change <h> By Nina Hendy <p> When Sandra Chambers realised she could have control over the type of investments her superannuation fund makes , she jumped at the chance to switch to an ethical investment option . <p> It was an easy decision for the 54-year-old baby boomer from the Yarra Valley , who works in conservation for local government . She is a member of VicSuper and her husband recently made the switch to the fund , too . <p> " I 'm relieved to know our super is n't just helping large companies and that instead , we 're investing in the environment , " she says . " You ca n't expect all the big players to take the steps they should to do the right thing for our kids ' futures . " <p> Chambers is one of a growing number of super fund members that want their retirement savings to have more of a focus on ethical investments . <p> Advertisement <p> The ethical funds segment of the $2.7 trillion super fund industry grew by 13 per cent in past year to $980 billion , up from just $178 billion five years ago . <p> More than 80 per cent of the largest funds have now embedded a formal commitment to responsible investments , up from 70 per cent in 2016 , the Super Fund Benchmark Report 2018 reveals . <p> Consideration of environmental , social , governance and ethical factors is becoming the expected minimum standard of good investment practice <p> " Consideration of environmental , social , governance and ethical factors is becoming the expected minimum standard of good investment practice , " he says . <p> VicSuper has seen a 22 per cent increase in members using the fund 's socially conscious option in the past year , with 13 per cent of its entire investment portfolio focused on sustainable outcomes . <p> " The feedback is that members want to integrate their social beliefs into their investment approach , " says Kim Farrant , VicSuper 's portfolio manager for responsible investments . <h> Part of solution <p> Australian Ethical , which launched in 1986 , has doubled its membership in the past four years , making it the fastest growing fund by members for the five years to 2018 , according to KPMG . <p> It has $3.6 billion in assets under management , up from less than a billion in 2015 . <p> Loading <p> " Blind Freddy could see that consumers no longer want to be investing in companies producing fossil fuels and tobacco , " Australian Ethical chairman Steve Gibbs says . " They want to be part of the climate change solution , not part of the problem . " <p> The fund 's in-house ethics team works with its investment team to filter out unsuitable investments , then looks at the financials of those remaining . <p> " People switch funds when they realise they can invest their super in a positive way and still get good returns , " Gibbs says . <p> Fund manager Ausbil has also put environment , social and governance factors front and centre for investments in all the funds it manages . <p> " We believe it improves the sustainability of earnings , and materially reduces the risks to those earnings , " chief investment officer Paul Xiradis says . <p> Vision Super goes one step further by disclosing carbon emissions footprints of its share portfolios and intends to make its portfolios carbon neutral by 2050 . This will include divesting from companies involved in mining , transportation and consumption of coal , oil and gas . <p> Chambers says she is yet to see if her switch to responsible investing to fund her retirement will pay off financially over the long term . However , she 's impressed by VicSuper 's results so far . <p> VicSuper says its socially conscious investment option was its highest performing multi-asset class portfolio in the 12 months to September , 2019 , generating a 9.08 per cent return for members in accumulation mode , and a 10.51 per cent return for retired members .
