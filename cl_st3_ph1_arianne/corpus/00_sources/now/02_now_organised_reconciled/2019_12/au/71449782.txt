<h> One in five business owners do n't know who Bill Shorten is : Is small business out of touch with politics ? <p> Do you know who the Prime Minister is ? How about the Opposition Leader ? <p> If you could n't answer those questions without the help of Google , you 're not alone . A new survey of Australian small-business owners by accounting software provider Xero has found , despite being in the middle of a fiery election campaign , 15% of SME owners do n't know who the Prime Minister is . <p> A further 20% do n't know who the Opposition Leader is , and a massive 75% could n't name the Small and Family Business Minister if asked . <p> While Michaelia Cash ( the small business minister ) might not be a household name , small-business owners not knowing the who Scott Morrison or Bill Shorten are just 10 days away from an election is worrying , with some believing it could lead to SMEs making a poorly informed choice on polling day . <h> Small business disenfranchised <p> Carlia Ashton is the founder of eco-friendly beauty brand ONNE , and tells SmartCompany she views herself as politically savvy . Ashton was easily able to name all three politicians . <p> However , she admits her knowledge of politics does n't necessarily extend into the world of small-business policy , saying she 's concerned about broader issues , such as climate change . <p> " My brand 's very environmentally conscious , so my focus lies there rather than with small-business policy more broadly . I have n't really followed any of the new small-business announcements , " she says . <p> " I think there are a lot of issues bigger than small business . " <p> Carlia Ashton , ONNE founder . Source : Supplied . <p> Stephanie Oley is the head of creative agency The Offices , and a self-described " ignorant small-business owner " . She tells SmartCompany she was a fan of Turnbull , but feels like she 's in between a rock and a hard place , with " colourless " Morrison on one side , and " crazy idealist " Shorten on the other . <p> She 's a member of many business organisations in inner-western Sydney , but says she has n't seen one politician attend any of the meetings or gatherings all year , something she believes would go a long way towards getting SME owners engaged and informed about policies . <p> " People like Michaelia Cash and other politicians who want to get the small-business vote should headline these events . It 's a huge missed opportunity , " she says . <p> The lack of engagement from small business is the effect of a lack of engagement from politicians themselves , says Oley , who believes platforms and policies should come before " sniping and cutting down " political opponents in media interviews . <p> " They should be focusing on all the exciting things they 're planning to bring to their electorates . They should be attending events , going on social media , doing the occasional paid ad , " she says . <p> " They need to bring trust back , and do something that makes it clear what they stand for . " <p> Stephanie Oley , managing director of The Offices . Source : Supplied <p> Oley also believes many policies are failing to pass the time-honoured " pub test " , which in turn is contributing to the disenfranchisement of small-business owner voters . <p> However , the survey found while business owners might not be able to name key politicians , they were involved with the issues affecting the business community more broadly , such as tax cuts and energy prices . <p> Almost 50% of those surveyed felt the sector was being overlooked by both sides of politics , and 47% stated they would be considering minor parties come polling day . Two out of five small-business owners surveyed said they only " somewhat understand " the differences in policy between Labor and the Coalition . <h> Keeping it local <p> Ashton thinks the ongoing disenfranchisement in politics from the small-business community is " definitely " a problem , and in part blames the short government terms . <p> " We 're always changing governments as we only have short , three-year terms . That means there 's so much to follow , and the parties put these promises out , and we either eventually get them or we do n't , " she says . <p> " We 're disenfranchised because we 're not engaged . If we had longer-term governments , more people would be engaged . With mandatory voting , we have more risk of people casting their vote without being educated . " <p> Ashton is also concerned about the groundswell of support from small-business owners for minor parties , saying while refusing to vote for the two major parties is valid , preference deals from smaller parties and independents can mean your vote can flow in ways you might not be satisfied with . <p> Oley also raises some concerns around this , saying business owners often " think they 're sticking their finger up " at larger parties , but believes smaller parties gaining power only serves to undermine and reduce the authority of the major party that inevitably forms government . <p> According to Xero 's head of industry Matthew Prouse , there are a number of policies announced in the election campaign which will , directly and indirectly , affect small-business owners , and believes there is a " critical " need for more engagement from politicians . <p> " With almost one in two small businesses saying their needs had not received enough attention in the election campaign to date , and some even unable to name the Prime Minister , it 's clear there is a critical need for engagement between politicians and the more than two million small-business owners , who employ five million Australians , " Prouse said in a statement . <p> Ashton agrees , saying local members should make it clear to their constituents that they can pick up the phone and chat to them . <p> " I think a lot of business owners think they ca n't just call their local member , but you really can . Local politicians need to make it clear they 're here to talk , " he says . <p> " My husband runs a business and speaks regularly to his local member . With so many industries dealing with so much red tape , I think we need to talk local .
