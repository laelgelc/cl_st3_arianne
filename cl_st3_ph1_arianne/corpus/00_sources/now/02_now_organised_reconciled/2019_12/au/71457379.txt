<p> If there 's one thing wine lovers love almost as much as drinking wine , it 's reading about wine . Show me someone who has a lot of good bottles in their cellar , and I can guarantee that person will also have a lot of good books on their shelves . Wine is an endlessly fascinating subject , and we wine geeks love to pore over its multifaceted nuances , both in the glass and on the page . <p> This year has seen some impressive books published by a range of experts in the field -- first-time author Jane Lopes , wine director of Attica restaurant in Melbourne ; New York-based Alice Feiring , a leading advocate of natural wine ; and British co-authors Hugh Johnson , veteran wine writer , and Master of Wine Jancis Robinson , described by Decanter magazine as " the most respected wine critic and journalist in the world " . <p> Lopes has come up with wonderfully novel ways to present information that make even already knowledgeable readers see the subject anew . <p> Each book has a lot to recommend it . Lopes ' impressive debut work is as much candid and compelling memoir as wine guide , and deserves to be read cover to cover . <p> Feiring 's book is an indispensable primer for people both perplexed by and passionate about natural wine , while Johnson and Robinson 's book is a new , completely revised edition of a longstanding classic that you should still buy even if you already own the first seven editions . <p> Jancis Robinson with the wine glass she produced in collaboration with UK designer Richard Brendon . <p> If you 'd rather drink wine than read about it , then Robinson also has you covered this Christmas . This year , she launched her own range of glassware , produced in collaboration with London designer Richard Brendon . <p> What makes this range different from many others is that it features just one shape of glass rather than a multitude of styles and sizes . Very fine , very light and with an impossibly thin stem ( there 's also a stemless version for the clumsier drinkers among us ) it 's a joy to drink from , whether you 're sipping champagne , chardonnay or shiraz . It 's also remarkably robust -- that is , you can put it in the dishwasher . <p> The JR x RB wine glass comes in a twin gift pack ( $130 ) , six-pack ( $366 ) and twelve-pack ( $677 ) . The stemless glass comes as a pair ( $99 ) , or a six ( $297 ) . The range also features a wide , squat decanter designed for younger wines but also big enough to take a magnum ( $185 ) ; and a narrower , bottle-shaped decanter designed for older wines ( $230 ) . <h> Wine books of the year <p> This is such a rich and rewarding book , full of insight and ideas . On one level , it 's a list of 100 wines -- and many other drinks , from mezcal to chartreuse to rum -- each with a story attached . But it 's so much more than that . <p> Lopes has come up with wonderfully novel ways to present information that make even already knowledgeable readers see the subject anew . There are cocktail recipes , a mini-graphic novel ( illustrator Robin Cowcher 's brilliant work beautifully complements Lopes ' words throughout the book ) , a crossword , eye-opening food-and-wine matches , playlists to accompany your drinking -- a cornucopia of clever content . <p> Lopes respects the reader 's intelligence and curiosity , and is n't afraid to editorialise as she educates . She 's also remarkably honest and candid as she weaves a narrative of her own life through the book , taking us with her on both her professional journey -- working at some of America 's top bars and restaurants , sitting the gruelling Master Sommelier exams -- and her personal trials with at-times crippling physical and mental health problems . <p> It 's this frankness that is Vignette 's great strength and sets it apart from most drinks books . <p> Natural wine continues to be polarising . Some swear by it and would n't dream of drinking anything else . Others swear about it and would rather drink anything else . People on both sides of the divide should read this book : if you 're already a convert , it 'll teach you even more about the wonderful world of wild and woolly wines you 've fallen in love with ; if you 're yet to be convinced , it 'll help you at least understand what all the fuss is about , and may even change your mind . <p> Advertisement <p> Alice Feiring has been drinking and writing about natural wines for many years -- this is her seventh book -- so it 's no surprise to find this latest work full of particularly clear , concise insights into the topic . The way Feiring outlines the differences between conventional , sustainable , organic , biodynamic and natural , for example , is the most usefully straightforward I 've seen , and she has developed a pithy turn of phrase -- discussing the use of minimal sulphur dioxide preservative , she says " ( sulphur ) may be brimstone , but it 's not the devil " . <p> " In a digital world , " writes Feiring , " analog is needed . In a world where fake dominates , we need real even more . No one had attempted to map all the world 's big wine regions in one book before , and the concept was received enthusiastically by wine lovers around the world , with half a million copies sold over two years . <p> I have a copy of that first edition , and comparing it with this latest instalment , it 's hard to believe that so much has changed in the intervening years . Back then , New Zealand did n't produce enough wine to even merit inclusion , the whole of South America got one double-page spread ( France had more than 70 pages ) , and shiraz was n't deemed important enough to feature in the list of the world 's major grape varieties . <p> If anything , though , it 's more relevant comparing this new , completely revised and redesigned eighth edition with more recent versions of the atlas .
