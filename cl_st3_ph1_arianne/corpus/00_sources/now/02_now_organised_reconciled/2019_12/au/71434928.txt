<h> Macquarie Dictionary names its Word Of The Year <p> As 2019 draws to a close , Macquarie Dictionary has decreed its Word Of The Year , and it 's surely one to spark some conversation . <p> The venerable tome anointed " cancel culture " as the term that led all others in the past 12 months . <p> It defined " cancel culture " as " the attitudes within a community which call for or bring about the withdrawal of support from a public figure , such as cancellation of an acting role , a ban on playing an artist 's music , removal from social media , etc. , usually in response to an accusation of a socially unacceptable action or comment by the figure . " <p> It is no doubt an apt choice , as debate continues to rage over whether the " cancellation " of a public figure for unsavoury acts constitutes censorship , prudishness , or simply well-merited consequences . <p> The Macquarie Dictionary has nominated its Word Of The Year . ( The Age ) <p> " A term that captures an important aspect of the past year 's Zeitgeist ... an attitude which is so pervasive that it now has a name , society 's cancel culture has become , for better or worse , a powerful force , " the choosing committee said in a statement . <p> " Cancellation " has hammered down on various public figures this year , from Roseanne Barr to Game Of Thrones showrunners David Benioff and D.B. Weiss . <p> Macquarie also posited several " honourable mentions " for its word of the year . <p> Leading the way was " ngangkari " - a Pitjantjatjara word denoting an Indigenous Australian healer . <p> Macquarie also picked out " eco-anxiety " ( the distress and fear brought on by the effects of climate change ) and " thicc " ( African-American vernacular celebrating body positivity that does not conform to white Western standards of beauty ) . <p> The Oxford Dictionary also hit on modern anxieties with its word of the year , picking " climate emergency " out of the crowd . <p> Merriam-Webster has not nominated its word that best represents 2019 yet , but its 2018 pick was " justice " . <p> Online word repository Dictionary.com has likewise not yet put forward its 2019 nominee , but in 2018 it chose " misinformation " .
