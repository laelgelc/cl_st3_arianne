<h> More <h> The public service shake-up is n't over <p> The federal public service is set for a further shake-up on top of Thursday 's upheaval when the number of departments was cut from 18 to 14 and five departmental secretaries were given their marching orders . <p> Prime Minister Scott Morrison will demand cultural changes next week as part of his response to a review led by businessman David Thodey and commissioned by Malcolm Turnbull . <p> In August , Mr Morrison rejected the review as too wishy-washy and sent it back with the demand its recommendations be more robust . It is expected to recommend a leaner and more responsive bureaucracy with practices more aligned to those of the private sector . <p> Putting his stamp on the public sector not only involves the realignment of departments but is forcing the sector to be more open and relevant to the people it serves . <p> Loading <p> Mr Morrison likened himself to Bob Hawke , whose Machinery of Government reforms cut the number of departments from 28 to 18 . <p> Advertisement <p> Mr Morrison 's structural changes will culminate in a total of 14 departments , including four new super departments . <p> The changes include separating environment from climate change and lumping it with agriculture and water , a move that has raised concerns among the environmental movement . <p> The Small Business portfolio name has also disappeared from the title of the new super department into which it has been merged , prompting concerns from the Australian Chamber of Commerce and Industry about emphasis . <p> " Small business is a big deal . We are concerned that small businesses , the engine room of the economy that make up 98 per cent of all businesses , are not overlooked in the new mega Department of Industry , Science , Energy and Resources , ' ' ACCI chief executive James Pearson said . <h> ' Fewer departments does n't mean fewer ministers ' <p> Mr Morrison said the shake-up would create a more efficient and responsive bureaucracy . Fewer departments did not mean fewer ministers , he said , adding there would still be ministers for small business and the environment . <p> He said it was not about cutting jobs or making budget savings but making the public sector more " public-facing ' ' , in that it must engage more with the community it serves . <p> " Having fewer departments will allow us to bust bureaucratic congestion , improve decision-making and ultimately deliver better services for the Australian people , ' ' he said . <p> Advertisement <p> It was consistent , he said , with his public service edict of " respect and expect " . That is , the government respects the public service but expects it to deliver the government 's agenda . <p> The new departments will be : Education , Skills and Employment ; Agriculture , Water and the Environment ; Industry , Science , Energy and Resources ; and Infrastructure , Transport , Regional Development , Communications and the Arts . <h> Flaming response <p> Mike Mrdak , who was dumped when his Department of Communications and the Arts was rolled into a new super department , told staff in a farewell email that : " I was told of the government 's decision to abolish the department late yesterday afternoon . <p> Mike Mrdak told staff the changes would end his 32-year career . ' ' <p> " The success of policy is recognised in its delivery and implementation . It 's the execution of the strategy that matters not just having one , " the Prime Minister said . <p> Secretaries to be moved on are Mr Mrdak , Kerri Hartland from the Department of Employment , Renee Leon from the Department of Human Services , Agriculture Department Secretary Daryl Quinlivan and Heather Smith , secretary of the Industry , Innovation and Science Department . <h> Environment movement concerns <p> The new Agriculture , Water and Environment Department will be headed by veteran public servant Andrew Metcalfe , who was removed from Agriculture after Tony Abbott won the 2013 election . <p> Dr Heather Smith , the wife of former Treasury boss Martin Parkinson , was also a casualty of the restructure . Alex Ellinghausen <p> He was one of three mandarins sacked at the time . He had previously led the old immigration department and both Mr Abbott and Mr Morrison were unhappy with him for trying to help Labor with its Malaysia solution proposal to stop the boats . <p> Mr Metcalfe was punted , even though he had devised the Pacific solution for the Howard government . <p> Mr Morrison said the Nationals , who have responsibility for agriculture and water , would not have control of the environment . That would stay with Environment Minister Sussan Ley , a Liberal . <p> Advertisement <p> But there were concerns among the environmental movement . <p> " There are risks associated with the energy and climate change branches of the Department of Environment and Energy being absorbed into a big new industry and resources portfolio if the resources sector is the dominant voice in policy development , " Australian Conservation Foundation campaigns director Paul Sinclair said . <p> Tim Beshara of the Wilderness Society wrote to Ms Ley seeking clarity given the three portfolios " have clearly conflicting mandates " . <p> " In a time where community concern among Australians over the state of our natural world has never been higher , the Prime Minister has chosen to symbolically de-prioritise environmental protection , " Mr Beshara said . <p> Ms Ley , Agriculture Minister Bridget McKenzie and Water Minister David Littleproud issued a joint statement , saying said the merger would " provide greater synergies to the policies and programs that underpin regional Australia and our agriculture , fisheries and forestry industries " . <p> Opposition Leader Anthony Albanese was sceptical of the changes and warned jobs would be cut . <p> " This is about centralising power , " Mr Albanese said . <p> " This Prime Minister has been making cuts to the public service continuously since they were elected in 2013 . " <p> On Thursday afternoon , the secretary of the Department of Prime Minister and Cabinet , Phil Gaetjens , addressed the changes in a speech to the Institute of Public Administration Australia at the National Gallery . <p> The five dumped secretaries , whose terms expire in February , did not turn up . <p> Phillip Coorey is The Australian Financial Review 's Political Editor based in Canberra .
