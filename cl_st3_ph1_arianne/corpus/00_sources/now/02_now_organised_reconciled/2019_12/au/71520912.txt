<h> Climate report casts doubt on Boxing Day Test 's future <p> Cricket Australia says it is working to develop a sustainability strategy , with a new report warning that the MCG Test may need to be shifted from its normal slot as " the number of extreme heat days in Melbourne during December is expected to increase significantly over the next 40-60 years . " <p> An Australian Conservation Foundation report , based on analysis from the Monash Climate Change Communication Research Hub , has also put the onus on CA to act or risk liability under the Corporations Act for failing to adequately address the issue of climate change . <p> The report says the Boxing Day Test could need to be moved in future from its usual time slot due to extreme heat.Credit:Chris Hopkins <p> The report finds that rising average temperatures are already having an impact at both professional and local levels , and that the issues will only be accentuated if trends continue . <p> One of the report 's key findings is that consideration should be given to moving the MCG Test to November or March " if no effective climate mitigation action is taken " . <p> Advertisement <p> " All the science warns climate conditions are going to worsen further -- unless strong action is taken to cut climate pollution from the burning of coal , oil and gas , " ACF campaigns director Paul Sinclair said . <p> " Cricketing authorities are yet to champion the national and international action needed to combat the root causes of climate change . <p> " Cricket Australia 's 2017-2022 strategy report and its 2018-19 annual report make no mention of climate change , environmental responsibility , the considerable air travel associated with the elite level of the game , or any attempts to improve the energy efficiency of cricketing premises . <p> " Cricket Australia 's major sponsor Alinta Energy and its parent company produce 11.3 million tonnes of climate pollution in Australia each year . " <p> CA said it was committed to ensuring cricket retained its place in Australian society for years to come . <p> " As a sport , we acknowledge that a stable and sustainable natural environment is necessary for players and fans to recreate , compete , officiate and spectate , " the governing body said in a statement provided to The Age and The Sydney Morning Herald . <p> Loading <p> " Cricket Australia actively supports the sports industry globally to deliver collective progress on climate change and sustainability . We acknowledge that a more holistic approach to sustainability is required to achieve greater progress to build cricket 's leadership in sustainability and lessen the impact of climate change on the natural environment . <p> " Developing practical frameworks , committing to targets and standing for meaningful and lasting change are ways we can create a legacy to significantly impact policy and action through the game from our national teams and community cricket . <p> " We acknowledge we have a role to play and our executive team are currently in the process of proactively developing a strategy for sustainability . The strategy is in the early stages of development and significant progress will be made to bring this to life in 2020 . <p> " Through our membership to the Sports Environment Alliance , we are seeing meaningful and significant change in the way sports in Australia are working to combat the impact of climate change through debate and example . Cricket leads the industry on a number of fronts through best practice and industry-leading initiatives in the building of new facilities around Australia and players who are subject to heat stress . "
