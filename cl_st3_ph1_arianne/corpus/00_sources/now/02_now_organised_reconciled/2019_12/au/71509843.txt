<h> ' We 're smoke refugees ' : Sydneysiders flee the city for a breath of fresh air <p> Sydney may be quieter than usual this summer . While the bushfires are devastating vast swaths of the state , many Sydneysiders are planning to leave the city for respite from grey-brown skies and polluted air . <p> Others , like Adon Sammut , 28 , are preparing to leave for good . Mr Sammut will put his home in Menai in south-western Sydney on the market in January and buy land in Tasmania . <p> Adon Sammut , 28 , from Menai has taken to wearing a P3 gas mask when outside on smoky days.Credit:Dominic Lorrimer <p> " Two words : climate change , " Mr Sammut said . While there are fires in the Illawarra/Shoalhaven region , the far south coast is mostly smoke-free for now . <p> The Hazard family from Gladesville is among those joining the temporary exodus . Bashi and Damien Hazard and their three children were originally planning a relaxing Christmas celebration at home and to stay in Sydney for New Year 's Eve . Instead they have made a last-minute decision to visit family in Malaysia and will stay at least three weeks depending on conditions in Sydney . <p> Ms Hazard 's asthma had flared up , the children were teary-eyed and wheezy , and last weekend her youngest son was hospitalised and put on a ventilator . <p> " Usually this time of year it 's quite nice in Sydney ... but we ca n't be outdoors so we decided to pull the plug , " Ms Hazard said . <p> Miriam Hart and Nick Ward from Manly pulled their daughter Lila , 7 , out of school before the end of term and headed to the south coast seeking fresh air . " We 're smoke refugees , " Mr Ward said . <p> Ms Hart said the couple decided to try to work remotely over summer because Lila and her sister Argenta , 3 , were developing croup inflammation of the larynx . The girls have previously required an ambulance service and adrenalin injections because of croup . <p> Loading <p> Not everyone can afford to leave , given peak holiday prices for transport and accommodation , or take time off work . Skye Clarkson and husband Michael Bell would love to take a break with their two-year-old son Wyatt but they can not afford to go far . <p> They have cancelled plans to camp in a tent at Del Rio Resort near Wisemans Ferry on the Hawkesbury because of the poor air quality . <p> Ms Clarkson is six months pregnant and already suffering because of the " atrocious " air quality caused by smoke from the huge Gospers Mountain fire 20 kilometres from their home in Narara near Gosford . <p> " There 's no way we can risk our health by going into a tent and having the wind pick up and bring all that smoke into the camp ground , " Ms Clarkson said . " I ca n't be breathing it , my toddler ca n't be breathing it and my partner ca n't be breathing it . " <p> The family emailed to cancel 20 days before their stay but were told there would be no refund .
