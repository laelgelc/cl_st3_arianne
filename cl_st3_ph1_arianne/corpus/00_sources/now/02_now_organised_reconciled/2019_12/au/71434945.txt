<h> Climate change forcing millions out of homes : report <p> Climate-fuelled disasters have forced about 20 million people a year to leave their homes in the past decade , according to a new report from Oxfam . <p> This equates to one every two seconds - making the climate the biggest driver of internal displacement for the period , with the world 's poorer countries at the highest risk , despite their smaller contributions to global carbon pollution compared to richer nations . <p> People are seven times more likely to be internally displaced by floods , cyclones and wildfires than volcanic eruptions and earthquakes , and three times more likely than by conflict , according to the report released today . <p> Climate-fuelled disasters have forced about 20 million people a year to leave their homes in the past decade , according to a new report from Oxfam . ( EPA/AAP ) <p> The issue is one of a range of topics set to be discussed at the United Nations Climate Change Conference COP 25 , which starts today in Madrid . <p> Oxfam is calling on the international community to do more to fund recovery programs for poorer countries affected by the climate emergency , which is set to intensify as extreme weather events are projected to increase in both severity and frequency . <p> Low and lower-middle income nations , such as India , are more than four times more likely to be affected by climate-fuelled displacement than high-income countries like Spain and the US , according to the report . <p> Geography also plays a role , with about 80 per cent of those displaced living in Asia . <p> Small island developing states ( SIDS ) , such as Cuba , Dominica and Tuvalu , are particularly badly affected , making up seven of the top 10 countries with the highest rates of displacement from extreme weather disasters between 2008 and 2018 . <p> People living in SIDs are 150 times more likely to be displaced by extreme weather disasters than those living in Europe , according to the report , which analysed 2008-18 data from the Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre . <p> Natural phenomena like cyclones , earthquakes and fires are all displacing people across the world . ( AAP ) <p> Increasing risk as climate crisis intensifies <p> Internal displacement has a social cost as well as a financial one , according to Tim Gore , Oxfam 's head of policy on climate and food justice . <p> " It 's always the poorest , the most vulnerable , and women in particular , that are worst affected , " Gore , who worked on the report , told CNN . <p> " This kind of displacement really tears at the social fabric of communities . " <p> There is more acute risk in those countries where extreme weather and conflict combine , such as Somalia , said Gore . <p> Sudden extreme weather events such as cyclones grab a lot of attention , but slow onset phenomena like rising sea levels also have an impact , he added . <p> For example , floods affecting agricultural land in low lying coastal areas can leave it unusable for farming , pushing inhabitants to leave the area for good . <p> Who should pay to combat the effects of climate change ? <p> Oxfam is calling on world leaders to reduce emissions as fast as possible . <p> Developing countries are also set to push for support from developed countries through a financial mechanism to deal with loss and damage . <p> First discussed at a climate summit in Warsaw in 2013 , such a mechanism will involve richer countries financially helping poorer countries to deal with the impact of climate change . <p> Oxfam is calling on the international community to do more to fund recovery programs for poorer countries affected by the climate emergency ( AAP ) <p> " Nobody has been prepared to talk about money and so that 's one of the critical issues that will be on the table in Madrid , " said Gore . <p> " Ultimately somebody is going to have to pay the price for these impacts and at the moment that price is being paid by the poorest communities in the world . " <p> And while current data shows lower risk in developed nations , projections suggest that is set to change . <p> " Rich countries are not immune either from the threat of displacement , " said Gore . <p> " Climate change is not going to discriminate . " <p> Bob Ward , policy director at the Grantham Research Institute on Climate Change and the Environment , told CNN that increasing numbers of internally displaced people can be attributed in part to a growing population living in high-risk areas . <p> Geography also plays a role , with about 80 per cent of those displaced living in Asia . ( AP ) <p> Displacement can also be a measure of success in some cases , he added , citing early warning systems that allow people to get out of danger before an extreme weather event hits , avoiding the major loss of life that has occurred in the past . <p> Ward also emphasised that climate-fuelled displacement is a security issue . <p> " Although it 's hard to show that climate change is in itself creating political instability and conflict , the way in which the national security community describes climate change is as a threat multiplier , " he said . <p> " When you get large populations displaced that 's when you get instability and conflict . "
