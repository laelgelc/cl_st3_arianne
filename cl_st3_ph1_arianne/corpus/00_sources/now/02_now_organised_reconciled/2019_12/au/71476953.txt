Wilson , Miami <p> Electricity prices <p> THE Australian Energy Regulator has just rejected Energy Queensland 's Tariff Structure Statements for Ergon and Energex and revised both distribution networks ' regulatory proposals down for the 2020-25 period . <p> In its Draft Determinations of the Energex and Ergon Energy Regulatory Proposals , the AER was not convinced with the amount of money they were applying to recover from consumers to operate their networks and proposed a reduced figure . <p> Energex and Ergon Energy had proposed raking in $13 billion from customers through their poles and wires networks over the next five years from 2020 . <p> However , AER has slashed this figure by $1.4 billion and criticised the government owned power distributors over their attempted cash grab . <p> AER found Energex and Ergon Energy wanted to milk their assets for extra money above current market rates and foist the cost of upgrading their networks to cater for more solar , batteries and electric vehicles on to consumers . <p> With the AER 's final decision not published until April 30 , 2020 and retail tariffs still to be finalised , sugar cane farming families will have a matter of months , June 2020 , to assess the new retail tariffs and work out what was best for their business and if any structural change or new equipment was needed , and more importantly in a period of very low world sugar prices , do they have any money to make changes . <p> Irrigation electricity tariffs for our cane farming families in Queensland have risen a minimum of 136 per cent over the past decade , and for some more than 200 per cent , while CPI has increased by just 24 per cent over the same period . <p> Has the AER draft determination highlighted the government owned electricity providers may be trying to gouge customers by up to $1.4 billion more than they should have ? <p> I call on the Queensland Government to order its distributors not to challenge the AER determination so Queenslanders could get extra savings .
