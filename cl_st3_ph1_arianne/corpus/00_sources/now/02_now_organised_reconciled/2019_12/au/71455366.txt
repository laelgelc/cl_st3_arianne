<h> More <h> Premium homes in Sydney to outperform in 2020 <p> Sydney 's premium market is poised to grow by 4 per cent in 2020 , while Melbourne is set to rise by 3 per cent -- trumping Vancouver , New York and Dubai -- amid growing confidence bolstered by lower interest rates and limited supply pipeline , the Knight Frank Prime Global Forecast 2020 shows . <p> The report , which monitors the performance of prime properties in global cities , is predicting Paris to lead the charge with a 7 per cent growth as strong tenant and second home buyer demand outpaces supply . <p> Luxury home prices in Sydney are poised to grow by 4 per cent in 2020 . <p> Top-end homes in Berlin and Miami are set to grow by 5 per cent while Geneva is forecast to rise by 4 per cent . <p> Hong Kong 's luxury market is set to stagnate , dragged down by the political upheaval that started in May . <p> Dubai 's premium home prices are forecast to drop by 2 per cent , while New York 's high-end properties are set to fall by 3 per cent due to the recent oversupply backlog . <p> Advertisement <p> Prime properties in Vancouver are expected to slump by 5 per cent over the next 12 months , reflecting an improving sentiment amid shrinking supply and buyers gradually adjusting to market regulations . Prices have been dropping at a rate of 15 per cent each year , according to the report . <p> Buyers were also deterred by tighter property market regulations in some cities and oversupply of luxury homes . <p> The Knight Frank Prime Global Cities Index for the third quarter of 2019 found that prices for prestige properties in 45 cities grew by just 1.1 per cent on average -- the slowest growth recorded in a decade . <p> " From the interminably tedious Brexit negotiations to the US-China trade tensions , Hong Kong protests and climate change , the level of uncertainty ramped up a gear in 2019 , " said Kate Everett-Allen , Knight Frank 's global research analyst . <p> Global economic slowdown was ranked as the most influential risk for high-net-worth buyers , followed by the escalating global trade war , local economic slowdown and changes to property market regulations . <p> " Many buyers are monitoring risk carefully -- many with a heavy dose of realism . However where sound demand and supply fundamentals outweigh short-term political or regulatory risk , many are identifying opportunities , " she said . <p> Despite the recent weak performance , global prime real estate prices have been growing strongly during the past 10 years . <p> Advertisement <p> Berlin , the top performing city , recorded a 145.7 per cent growth in prime home prices , followed by Vancouver at 85.3 per cent . <p> Sydney was third as the best performing city for luxury property with 69.8 per cent growth , followed by Melbourne at 64.1 per cent .
