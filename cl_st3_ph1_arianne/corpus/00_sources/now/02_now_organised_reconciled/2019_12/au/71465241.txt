<h> OPINION : A ' fair go ' for our region <h> OPINION : A ' fair go ' for our region <p> THERE is a lot going on in the media regarding discussion around climate change and the unprecedented and uncharted direction of the country . <p> I went to school at Alligator Creek and Sarina and love Mackay and the Whitsundays and the coal fields . My daddy used to drive a bulldozer morning to night clearing trees , a precursor to the coal mines , before taking up a position with the Big Australian . He would come home one day a week and that lasted for some years . <p> I have lived the majority of my life around the region . Most people in and around Mackay I have found to be very genuine and hard working . Approaching 50 , I have had many memorable occasions over the years of fishing around Hay Point , up and down our majestic coastline . It 's all my dad wanted to do with his time away from the coalfields . <p> I speak of these things because I am an advocate for change . I want our region to flourish and be a shinning light to the world , but we ca n't do exactly that unless we address the elephant in the room . <p> Climate change is beginning to bite hard , to the core of this great nation , and we must face every challenge , explore every opportunity of climate abatement strategy and leave no stone unturned if we are to avoid leaving the next generation without an inherited climate hangover . <p> I love fishing as much as my dad but am finding it increasingly difficult to organise myself to get a line wet , let alone to bag out . <p> I have chosen to raise my family here and some days are absolute diamonds and some rock bottom . Mackay and region have come a long way in the 40 years I have been here , and the memories are fond ; however , there is so much work still to do and I am astounded by the overall lack of federal support the region receives . <p> Admittedly , George does a fine job with the resources at his disposal and there are a lot of buts between what could be done and what has . <p> I want to encourage each and every person who reads this and has the ability to talk to their family about coal , climate change and the environment to do so , because all these things are important to our well-being and prosperity and our future ; and this is why I have chosen to address my concerns to the Resources Productivity Commission Inquiry , with George leading the way , at the helm driving influence for Dawson and Queensland . <p> It is without saying that coal still has a long future to run for our region , even with autonomous truck fleets ; however , the effects of climate change and its trends are challenging our model economy and have many far reaching consequences . And our ability to remain stationary and assume our wages will incline vertically with our standard of living is fallible , a myth . <p> If anything , autonomous trucks represent a future that we all knew was coming , only we 've had the time to adjust our attitudes . Can the same be said for climate change ? Are we able to adjust in time or will it be thrust down upon us ? <p> In my submission to the Productivity Commission Inquiry I have addressed my perspective as to how to address carbon abatement and how that relates to the condition of fishing around the region and how to create a merger between the coal and fishing industries through artificial reefs specifically designed with incorporating the development of technology using recycled mining tyres . <p> After writing to George I have received a total lack of interest , but if I do n't try that money will go elsewhere -- maybe the cows , the koalas , buying flying water bombers , just not to the Dawson community . <p> The permit and application fees to go to sea trials and begin the research are around $5 million -- well above my income capabilities , though is about the same as what the glossy new Whitsunday turtle and bat fish and stingrays and sea urchins cost the taxpayers . <p> I believe we can not change the direction of the road down which climate change is taking us ; however , we can choose the road we travel . <p> The further the journey though , the less opportunity for our region to capitalise on emerging technologies , with the real risk of having the narrative defined for us as opposed to making it our pathway into the carbon reduction future and our access to an equitable portion of the $4.55 billion Emissions Reduction Funds . <p> Already we see vast swathes of royalties going to Canberra and Brisbane and I firmly believe in the Commission 's ability to deliver a win for Dawson in regard to investment and productivity , with George at the driving wheel . <p> I do understand through the LNP 's aversion to the climate change debate centered around voter denial and I consider it impertinent if we do n't have someone in Canberra who represents all views of the electorate to advocate for our position , as opposed to a derivative of intervention , we end up being completely overrun with bureaucracy . <p> Furthermore , if we fail to do our fair share of the heavy lifting without making a significant contribution to carbon reduction targets a primary focus , we then risk exposure to insurance companies branding households as vulnerable communities , further driving up insurance premiums . <p> This aberration of circumstance attached to climate change and its perceived destructive natural forces is now stretching as far as Sweden , which has dumped Queensland Bonds for our lack of agility to respond to the emerging climate crisis . It is just another red flag that has potential to harm the hip pocket of mums and dads doing their very best . <p> And now NAB are in on the conspiracy with other banks to follow no doubt . <p> Our coal is the very best in the world from an export perspective , but that does n't mean we are exempt from external forces . It may very well mean our impunity on climate abatement strategies , or our inability to rein in our per capita carbon footprint , may consequently translate to higher prices on just about everything ( but milk it seems ) from the likes of everyday consumables to insurance , to rates and electricity even postage stamp , with the only thing not rising being wages . <p> With what now seems to be a permanent stay on the proposed HELE ' Clean Coal ' power station we must begin to -- not by choice but as an act of punitive due diligence -- investigate and invest further in alternative methods and models of the economic drivers for Dawson and the surrounding area , or we will miss the boat on $4.55 billion of the Federal Government 's flagship Emissions Reduction Funds . <p> If I had a firm grip on the Dawson purse strings I would like to see funding for a wind turbine at the tip of Hay Point . <p> The public is presently locked out by the Ports Corporation , or who ever owns the land now , but this would definitely reduce dependency on state power supply if it came to fruition , clearing the way for reduced emissions . <p> Above all though , I remain unrepentant that we must begin to research and provide investment opportunity that paves the way for Mackay 's first artificial reef . <p> We have seen other places , especially Western Australia , South Australia and now the Whitsundays with its shiny new Underwater Sculpture Trail , exceed expectation when these investments are implemented , especially from a recreational fishing perspective ; and now the State Government wants fish attraction devices placed everywhere south of the GBRMP . <p> We are being held to ransom by the Marine Park Authority , for no other reason other than that 's their operational model and they 're sticking to it . <p> Well that 's not good enough anymore . Maybe in a pre-climate crisis world , but now we all must contribute to the heavy lifting to avert this worldwide catastrophe and the Marine Park Authority and GBR Foundation must either invest more resources into the ocean adjacent Mackay or relinquish some of its power . <p> This ' artificial reef strategy ' is called evidence-based science coupled to situational awareness and maybe if Mackay began the process of building an artificial reef all those years ago , we would still see the majestic Roylen Cruises plying our waters . <p> All things being equal , and if we threw the textbook out today and began afresh tomorrow , the capital expenditure afforded to green zones around the inshore reefs of the Mackay/Whitsundays post Cyclone Debbie has failed to materialise and equates to nothing more than ( bad ) pseudoscience being thrust down our throats with the edifice of hope and prayers to support its founding principles and we are all totally locked into this belief from above . <p> We must look to the future and build its possibilities , learn from the past and edify each person 's chosen path for the good of the whole country , otherwise the memories of those that fought hardest for our rights and privileges that we all share in today will be lost forever , for they laid down their lives so that we as individuals may have opportunity to determine the outcome of our lives . <p> If I fail to achieve what I set out to do , please let it solely rest at my feet and not at the feet of some bureaucrat in an airconditioned office 1200km away . <p> If you read this today and fail to grasp what I 'm trying to achieve then keep doing what you do by all means , but if by any chance you , the reader , have any curiosity please go to the Resources Productivity Commission Inquiry web site on the link below , its there for all Australia to read : https : **39;1607;TOOLONG ... All I 'm asking for is a fair go for Mackay and Dawson -- I do n't think that 's to much but I know the Boomers resent the idea . Maybe now is a good time to start a conversation .
