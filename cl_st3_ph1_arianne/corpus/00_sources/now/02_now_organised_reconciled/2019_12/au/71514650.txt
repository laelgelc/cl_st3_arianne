<h> More <h> Halcyon days of bank profits are over : RBA <p> The profitability of Australian banks has fallen by as much as one-third on a key measure since 2006 , but technological change , tighter regulations , and climate change mean greater pressures lie ahead , the Reserve Bank said . <p> RBA head of financial stability Jonathan Kearns said banks ' response to mounting challenges in the present would decide their future . Alex Ellinghausen <p> The fall in banks ' return on equity to around 13 per cent in 2018 was more pronounced than the 20 per cent decline in their return on assets . <p> RBA head of financial stability Jonathan Kearns said the post-Global Financial Crisis strengthening of a " prudential regime that was already tighter than global minimum standards " had broadly reduced risk-taking . <p> The upside was the banks ' Tier 1 capital ratios , a measure of resilience that divides core equity capital by risk-weighted assets , was up 50 per cent over the same period and , according to best available research , " sufficient to withstand the majority of past international banking crises " . <p> But the danger was that responsible lending standards , which do not apply to small businesses , nonetheless tilted the banks ' " internal procedures " against taking necessary risks . <p> Advertisement <p> " If there was no risk in making a business loan , there would be no pay-off either to the entrepreneur or the bank , " Mr Kearns told the Australasian Finance and Banking Conference , noting that the growth in small business loans remained " anaemic " despite successive interest rate cuts . <p> Mr Kearns noted that the increase in banks ' holding of high-quality liquid assets came in part from pulling back from international banking activity in the UK and Asia " where returns had been disappointing " . <h> Stiff competition <p> The central bank also identified growing pressure on the banks from three sources . <p> First , technology could challenge the banks ' hold on consumer data and payments systems . <p> But the rise of online retailers , social media giants , and payment platforms would also challenge the banks ' incumbent advantages on data and handling payments . <p> this was anticipated by regulators , but that " higher costs for banks can push more borrowing to non-bank financial institutions or into capital markets , " potentially affecting financial stability . <p> Third , banks , like most businesses and even central banks , faced growing physical , transitional , and reputational risks associated with climate change .
