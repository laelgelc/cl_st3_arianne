<h> Opinion <h> For 300 days , Labor needs to remember what it learnt from Goss <p> Labor diehards will take time over the next week to celebrate the 30th anniversary of the party 's greatest triumph , the election of the Goss government and the start of its three-decade-long ascendancy . <p> It 's an anniversary worth celebrating . It aligns with the reform of the state 's democracy , the liberalisation of laws seen as oppressive to modern social attitudes , the investment in modernising health and education and a generally easier approach to life after the corruption of the Bjelke-Petersen government . <p> So here 's three cheers to Wayne Goss and his dream team that came out of the wilderness to shed some light on what was popularly known as the " Moonlight State " . <p> And Labor will cheer the political supremacy that has kept its rein on government for almost all those 30 years , with the breaks in between having little lasting impact on the life of the state . <p> Advertisement <p> But everything comes to an end . And it 's now up for grabs whether this year 's celebrations will extend to a 31st anniversary after an election that ( for the first time ) has its date fixed in October next year . <p> The winner of that election has ( for the first time ) a four-year term or close enough to 1500 days to impose its political agenda on a state that has drifted from being the boomtown of Australia to the laggard in economic growth . <p> Labor has just 300 days left before it goes into caretaker mode before that election - 300 days to use its authority in government to make the case for the next 1500 days . <p> It 's worth contemplating the personal discipline Wayne Goss brought to government - and his willingness to impose it on a party room that could easily have become delirious with its first whiff of power since the 1950s . <p> Goss came to office with a substantial agenda of his own - but picked up and ran with a reform agenda written by Tony Fitzgerald after his corruption-busting inquiry . <p> He also imposed new disciplines on public sector decision-making and was dogmatic about state spending and , particularly , borrowing . <p> Loading <p> And that 's the discipline Labor needs to find if it is to make the most of the next 300 days . <p> Sloppy breaches of standards by ministers do n't help . They just suck oxygen out of whatever the government would prefer to advance as its agenda . <p> But Labor also needs to capture the public imagination with an agenda that deals with the real issues of everyday life in Queensland in the 2020s . <p> It 's active on climate change , but does a city like Warwick really have to be almost dry before the government floats the idea - and that 's all it 's done - of linking it to the rest of the state 's water grid ? <p> Loading <p> It talks accountability but does it really take the political embarrassment of an ill-advised property purchase to legislate for politicians to be treated on the same grounds as public servants ? <p> It 's committed to jobs creation . Which jobs and where ? And what are the obstacles to private sector job creation ? <p> And it professes discipline , but where is the authority Wayne Goss held as leader , or any acknowledgment of the role of the Premier by the factions jockeying below ? <p> This week 's death of two children after concerns about them were raised with Child Safety officers are symptomatic of what 's going wrong with modern government . <p> We have inquiry after inquiry into child safety , good people get engaged in fixing the system yet frontline staff are continually burnt and community alerts go unnoticed . And children die . <p> Queensland Labor 's three decades of triumph started with Wayne Goss , but at least one of those decades belongs to Peter Beattie , who would never have left alone the opportunity to find out how this happened and how to stop it happening again . <p> With 300 guaranteed days left to govern , Labor can find plenty to fix . <p> The first thing is to get rid of the distractions and then get on with the job .
