<h> Opinion <h> ' How good is this carbon tax ! ' Psst , PM 's speech falls off the back of a truck <p> A leaked copy of a speech to Parliament to be delivered by Prime Minister Scott Morrison on the occasion of the December 2019 mid-year budget update ... * <p> Thank you , Mr Speaker , and thank you to my many colleagues gathered in this chamber to hear me speak today . But it is my fellow Australians -- wherever you are watching this , from an inner-city living room or from our great outback -- to whom I now address my remarks . <p> We know that we are a great and vibrant nation . Despite our laid-back image , we know that we are a deeply passionate people . This passion often leads us to disagree , and when we do , we hold our debates out in the open -- the way a free and passionate democratic nation should . <p> On no other issue have we been so politically divided in recent times as on climate change . Fifteen years ago , after a particularly long and hot summer , one of our greatest prime ministers convened a special taskforce to investigate the proper design of a scheme to put a price on carbon emissions . <p> Advertisement <p> Unfortunately , John Howard did not remain prime minister long enough to see such a scheme implemented . It fell to the Rudd and Gillard governments to take up the mantle of designing a policy to price the damage done to our environment and economy caused by the increasing concentration of greenhouse gases in our atmosphere . <p> One of their attempts -- the Carbon Pollution Reduction Scheme -- was defeated , let us not forget , by the so-called Greens party which voted against it despite the support of Malcolm Turnbull and others . Ultimately , however , a Coalition government led by Tony Abbott axed Labor 's scheme . There can be no disputing this record of events . <p> Illustration : Dionne GainCredit : <p> But , my fellow Australians , history is no straitjacket . As the leader of Australia 's great Liberal Party , my message to you today is this : when we know better , we do better . <p> The terrifying and deadly bushfires have marked a turning point in this nation 's history . Unprecedented in their timing and ferocity , these fires have forced us all to look within and accept the reality of a changing climate . Our prayers and thoughts continue to lie with the many Australians who have lost their homes , their livelihoods and their loved ones . <p> But the time has come to turn thoughts into action . Today , I am proud to announce my government will do just that . Today , we complete the work begun by John Howard all those years ago . Today , I announce a simple " pollution tax " , to be applied per tonne of carbon emissions , modelled along the lines of the Canadian government 's tax introduced this year . <p> Julia Gillard used weasel words to deny her scheme amounted to a carbon tax . I intend to be upfront with you . What I announce today is a new tax . But as taxes go , it 's not a bad one . <p> That was then , this is now ... the then treasurer , Scott Morrison , with a lump of coal during question time in Parliament in February 2017 . Credit:Andrew Meares <p> It was the British economist Arthur C. Pigou who first proposed the idea of applying taxes to goods or activities that society has collectively decided it would like to discourage . The beauty of such " Pigouvian taxes " is that they not only change behaviour , they also raise revenue that can be used to cut other taxes . <p> We in the Liberal Party will always be the party of lower taxes , of giving greatest reward for individual effort . And so I am also proud today to announce the biggest package of personal income tax cuts and household payments ever delivered by an Australian government . <p> For every dollar raised through the pollution tax , I pledge that Australian households will receive a full dollar via a " pollution tax rebate " . I personally guarantee that no Australian household will be worse off under our pollution tax and rebate scheme . <p> Families need certainty . So today I announce our pollution rebates will be paid upfront and in advance , starting before Christmas . Thanks to our good budget management , we can afford to send a cheque to every Australian household to spend or save as they see fit . My colleague and Treasurer , Josh Frydenberg , will release the details later today . <p> Australian businesses have been calling out for certainty and today I give it to them . Australian households , too , have been calling out for help with skyrocketing power bills . Today I give it to them . <p> My fellow Australians , we will keep your lights on and , in the longer term , we will make power cheaper again by complementing our scheme with increased incentives for alternative energy investment . <p> Now , I know many -- even within my party -- will doubt these actions . And to them I say : doubt is good . Doubt is the questioning voice that drives better policy . But it lies with us , as leaders and as policymakers , to take action based on our best assessment of the evidence . And Australians know the evidence is in . <p> Loading <p> There are others who would say our action can do little in a global context of rising emissions . To them I say : that may be true , but it nevertheless falls to us to map out a sustainable path forward for our economic development . And to my opponents , who would engage in recriminations and excessive fearmongering to our youth , to them I say : take heart . <p> Where there is a will , there is a way . Democracy is a messy process . But in the end , with no small dose of humility and a large dose of humanity , democracy finds its way .
