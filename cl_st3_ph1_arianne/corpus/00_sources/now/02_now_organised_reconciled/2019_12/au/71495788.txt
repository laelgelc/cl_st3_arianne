Credit:Twitter / @stampymichael <p> Because the paper was so well preserved , they also believe the bottle probably washed onto shore within a year of being thrown and lay buried for more than a century in damp sand . <p> On the paper were two significant details : the date June 12 , 1886 , and the name of the ship . <p> More research , and with help from authorities in the Netherlands and Germany , revealed that the bottle was part of a long-term German Naval Observatory program studying global ocean currents . <p> An entry in the Paula 's meteorological journal written by the captain detailed the bottle being tossed overboard on the same date listed on the paper . The handwriting also matched his , down the extra curl in his Cs . <p> The museum 's report lauds the discovery 's scientific significance . <p> " Ocean current and drift patterns are still not completely understood , " it reads . <p> " The need to understand long-term climate change patterns has also seen historic data , such as that recorded in Paula 's meteorological journal and other 19th century ships ' logbooks , added as datasets into global climate models . " <p> The report links the bottle to German scientist George von Neumayer who implemented a drift-bottle experiment from 1864 to 1933 that involved thousands of bottles being thrown overboard with pre-printed message slips inside . <p> Ship captains were expected to write in details on one side of the paper - the date it was jettisoned , the co-ordinates , the name of the ship , its home port and travel route . <p> Those who found the bottles were asked to fill out the back and return the notes to either to the German Naval Observatory in Hamburg or the nearest German consulate . <p> Only 662 message slips were returned and none of the bottles . <p> The Wedge Island find takes that total to 663 and is the only known example of the type of bottle used . <p> The last bottle and note to be found was in 1934 in Denmark . <p> The previous world record for the oldest message in a bottle was 108 years , four months and 18 days between jettison and discovery . <p> Ms Illman has lent the bottle to the museum to display for the next two years .
