<h> Opinion <h> Stop the climate politics and let NSW become the Saudi Arabia of green energy <h> By Matt Kean <p> December 11 , 2019 -- 5.03pm <p> The bushfires we are facing are unprecedented . The fires are likely to impact more hectares than at any point in our history , the smoke blanketing Sydney is the worst in living memory and the conditions on the ground have rarely been so bad so early in the fire season . <p> Right now , the state government 's first priority is supporting our firefighters and those communities affected by this emergency . <p> Our firefighters are showing the best traits Australians are known for . Many are volunteers working away from their families and putting themselves at risk to keep others safe . A lot of people have also lost their homes from these fires and they need our support now more than ever . <p> But we also need to look at the causes of these bushfires so we can avoid this bushfire season repeating itself in the future . That includes addressing climate change . <p> Advertisement <p> These fires are not an isolated event . We are going through the worst drought in the state 's history . Last year was the hottest year on record and this year is on track to be the second hottest . <p> These extreme weather events are exactly what scientists have warned us about for decades : longer drier periods and more heatwaves resulting in worse bushfires . Climate change is no longer just a forecast . <p> Fight the fires now , but also know what is causing them ... the Orangeville blaze last week . Credit:Nick Moir <p> The global economy needs to reduce its emissions and we need to make sure we are prepared at home for the risks posed by climate change . We can not continue to let ideology and politics get in the way of taking real and meaningful action on climate change . <p> The NSW Liberal-National government has been a leader on this issue . We were one of the first jurisdictions to set a net zero carbon-emissions target -- before Britain , Germany or France ; we are reducing our emissions faster than the national average ; and we are developing Australia 's first co-ordinated renewable energy zone . <p> Of course , getting to net zero will require us to do more and I will have more to say on this over the coming months . But taking action to protect our environment does not need to come at the expense of our economy ; in fact , it presents an economic opportunity it would be negligent to miss . <p> Loading <p> Renewables are now the lowest cost form of new electricity generation . We are seeing families around the country installing solar panels on their rooftops not just because it 's good for the environment but because it 's good for their household budget . <p> More than 60 counties have committed to net zero by 2050 . The global transition to net zero is going to require trillions of dollars of investment in the energy system alone . We need to capture a slice of that opportunity here in NSW . <p> We are well positioned to do that and more . We can become the Saudi Arabia of solar and put ourselves at the centre of a new global order on energy . Our wind and solar resources give us the opportunity to lead the world in producing the low-emissions products and fuels , such as hydrogen , that other countries are going to need . <p> I do not want to see the events of this summer become the new normal and I do not want to see us miss the economic opportunity of the century . To avoid that , we need to take responsible and decisive action on climate change and we need to do it now .
