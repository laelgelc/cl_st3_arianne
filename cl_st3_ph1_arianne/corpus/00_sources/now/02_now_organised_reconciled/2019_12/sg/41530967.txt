<p> The war on dengue has received a boost with the opening of a new facility in Ang Mo Kio that can breed millions of mosquitoes a week to prevent urban female Aedes aegypti mosquitoes from producing offspring . <p> The launch yesterday comes at the tail end of a year in which Singapore has experienced its third-biggest dengue outbreak . As of Nov 21 , more than 14,470 people here had been infected with the disease , with 20 lives lost . <p> The new facility will allow the National Environment Agency ( NEA ) to scale up Project Wolbachia by producing up to five million mosquitoes a week . <p> The project involves the release of male Wolbachia-Aedes aegypti mosquitoes to suppress the urban Aedes aegypti mosquito population , which is the primary vector of dengue , chikungunya and Zika here . <p> When the released male Wolbachia-Aedes aegypti mosquitoes mate with urban female Aedes aegypti mosquitoes that do not carry Wolbachia , the resulting eggs do not hatch . <p> Speaking at the launch of the new facility , Senior Minister of State for the Environment and Water Resources Amy Khor said : " If we are serious about confronting climate change and other challenges , dengue control can not simply be just ' business as usual ' . We can not just do more of the same . " <h> Related Stories <p> Dr Khor , who is also Senior Minister of State for Health , said the dengue situation is likely to worsen over the next few decades as a result of global warming . <p> This is because higher temperatures result in mosquitoes breeding faster , and cause the dengue virus to replicate at a quicker rate . <p> The new $5 million facility in Techplace II is expected to provide scalability in the production and release of Wolbachia-Aedes aegypti mosquitoes needed for Project Wolbachia . <p> NEA chief executive Tan Meng Dui said : " This facility and the technologies it houses will play a key role in Singapore 's efforts to deploy Wolbachia technology as part of our integrated vector control programme to tackle these diseases ( dengue , chikungunya and Zika ) . "
