<h> Minister tells UN conference that ' climate change is existential ' for the Republic <p> Singapore has indicated that it will do more to cut down its greenhouse gas emissions . <p> The Republic will update its climate pledge to guide its policies over the longer term , said Minister for the Environment and Water Resources Masagos Zulkifli . <p> Already , Singapore has committed to ramping up its use of solar energy and greening its land transport sector , with the aim of having nine in 10 peak period journeys by 2040 made via walking , cycling or rides on public and shared transport . <p> It also wants to make electric vehicles more viable here . <p> Mr Masagos reiterated this commitment some five years after the Republic made its first climate pledge - which outlined its plans to limit its planet-warming greenhouse gas emissions by 2030 - under the historic Paris agreement . <p> Speaking at the annual United Nations climate conference in Madrid , or COP25 , Mr Masagos said Singapore is especially vulnerable to rising sea levels .
