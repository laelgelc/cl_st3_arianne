<p> The heatwave is another alarm bell about global warming in Australia , where this year 's early and intense start to regular summer bushfires has heaped pressure on the government to do more to tackle climate change . <p> Hundreds of bushfires have been raging across Australia for months , including a " mega-blaze " burning north of Sydney . <p> Smoke from the fires has engulfed Sydney , raising air pollution to hazardous levels in an event leading doctors have labelled a " public health emergency " . <h> Related Stories <p> At least three million hectares of land has been torched across Australia , with six people killed and about 700 homes destroyed . <p> Scientists have said the blazes have come earlier and with more intensity than usual because of global warming and a prolonged drought that has left the land tinder dry and towns running out of water . <p> The fires have sparked climate protests targeting the conservative government , which has resisted pressure to address the root causes of global warming in order to protect the country 's lucrative coal export industry . <p> Record spot temperatures were recorded this week in Western Australia , where firefighters have also been battling blazes raging across thousands of hectares of land . <p> Parts of New South Wales are forecast to reach the mid-40s deg C today . <p> On Saturday , as conditions worsen , west Sydney is due to tip over 46 degC . <p> " Over the next few days we are going to see firefighters , the emergency services and all those communities close to fires ... challenged with a new threat , " New South Wales fire commissioner Shane Fitzsimmons said yesterday . - AFP
