<p> Singapore -- On Sunday , December 22 , diplomat and professor Tommy Koh wrote a column in The Straits Times ( ST ) wherein he listed the five tests Singaporeans should pass before becoming first-world people . This references a remark Mr Koh made in October , wherein he called Singapore " a First World country with a Third World people . " <p> While some netizens heartily agree with Mr Koh and have written in what they consider as characteristics of first world people , others reject his premise and draw attention to how those in so-called first world countries behave . <p> But first , a summary of the five tests Mr Koh wrote about : The first test concerns the need for Singaporeans to stop littering , since " First World people such as the Japanese , South Koreans , and Taiwanese do not litter , " he writes . On the contrary , they do n't only not litter , but go out of their way to clean up the litter that they see and call out those who throw their rubbish anywhere . <p> He adds that while Singapore has come a long way because of the campaigns from PAP to clean up since the 1960s , of late , people have seemed to go back to their old ways of littering . Mr Koh wrote , " When I look around Singapore today , I see trash everywhere . " <p> - Advertisement - <p> The second test concerns having public restrooms that match the cleanliness of those of Japan , South Korea and Taiwan . <p> Again , he acknowledges that there has been a big improvement in the condition of our toilets , but mentions that " the remaining 30 per cent , found in markets , hawker centres , coffee shops and even some restaurants , are still very Third World . " <p> The third test has to do with civic-mindedness and good manners -- even habits as basic as saying " Please " and " Thank you " . <p> He adds that there are still Singaporeans who " rush in without waiting for those inside the lift or train to make their exit first , " or those who do n't stand on the left side of an escalator to let others pass , as well as youths who take the priority seats in public transport , writing , " This lack of civic-mindedness is deplorable . " <p> Mr Koh also gives the examples of not returning trays in hawker centres and cleaning their tables , and talking loudly in restaurants , theaters or concerts . " In First World countries , people do not commit such inconsiderate behaviour . " <p> He also added , " A First World people should be active volunteers and generous philanthropists . " <p> The fourth test has to do with cultural literacy , since , " a First World people is a cultured people , " who are " gracious and kind , " " treat those who serve them with courtesy and appreciation " and appreciate culture and the arts , read books , visit museums and show interest in Singapore 's history and heritage . <p> The final test is the country 's attitude towards nature and the environment , including awareness of the dangers of climate change . He wonders why Singaporeans love to set their air conditioners at 18 degrees , when this consumes so much energy . " I wage a daily struggle with hotels , restaurants and clubs to persuade them to reset their thermostats to 23 deg C , " he wrote . <p> He ended his column by writing , At the risk of making myself the most unpopular man in Singapore , I stand by my comment that Singaporeans are not a First World people . <p> I hope that we will pass the five tests I have posed . Only then can we say that Singaporeans are a First World people . " <p> Many netizens agreed with Mr Koh 's points and even added some examples of " third world " behavior they see in daily life . <p> Others believe that the many foreigners and newcomers to the country should be taught how to behave well . <p> Other commenters did not jump on Mr Koh 's bandwagon . <p> Some netizens say that the situation is not actually better in places that are considered to be first world . <p> Others questioned if anyone can actually be considered to be truly first-world.
