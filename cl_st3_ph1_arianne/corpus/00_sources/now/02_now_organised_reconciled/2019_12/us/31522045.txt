<p> The following is a guest opinion from Kalamazoo County Board of Commissioners Vice Chairperson Tracy Hall . <p> Three weeks ago , the Kalamazoo County Board of Commissioners received a Freedom of Information Act ( FOIA ) request from MLive regarding the termination of the former corporation counsel for Kalamazoo County , Beth White . As required by FOIA , I turned over all of the requested records , including email , text messages and a voicemail I received from Kalamazoo County Board of Commissioners Chairperson Julie Rogers . In the voicemail , Rogers encouraged me to delete messages relevant to the FOIA request . After listening to the message , I knew that what Rogers had asked of me was wrong , and may even break the law . I knew the right thing to do would be to talk to the County 's lawyer about it . So I did . And he confirmed my gut feeling . <p> I turned over the voice message from Rogers . <p> As an elected official , I listen to people every day sharing their hopes and dreams for the future , but more recently , sharing their fears about our government and the moral clarity of our elected officials . Grappling with difficult life and death issues like gun violence , homelessness , severe income inequality and climate change , it is easy to believe that our political leaders are stuck in the past , making deals that help only a privileged few in secret meetings behind closed doors . In such an atmosphere , truth itself has become partisan , and while none of us has all the answers to restoring trust in government , greater transparency from our elected officials is the place to start . <p> According to the Pew Research Center 's Public Trust in Government poll , the percent of Americans claiming to trust government to do the right thing always or most of the time has fallen steadily from 73% in 1958 ( the first year Pew ran the poll ) to 17% in 2019 . Not surprisingly , during the Obama presidency , more Democrats trusted government than Republicans , but during the Trump presidency more Republicans now claim to trust government to do the right thing . As the press struggles against the spin-heavy public relations machines churning out half-truths , innuendo and outright lies , we receive more of our information from biased sources like political parties , corporations , special interest groups or propagandist cable news shows than ever before . The resulting lack of transparency is taking its toll on our democracy and nation . <p> Secrecy in government results in catastrophic breaches of public trust in ways that severely damage our national interest . In 1971 , Daniel Ellsberg 's release of the Pentagon Papers forced American politicians to confront the grim realities in Vietnam , hidden for so long from public view . Just this month , the Washington Post reported on thousands of government documents demonstrating the failure of three presidential administrations , Republican and Democratic , to honestly report the lack of progress in Afghanistan and the staggering loss of blood and treasure in the nearly 20-year-long military conflict . <p> This goes without mentioning the current impeachment proceedings against the President of the United States for abuse of power and obstruction of justice , charges stemming from a whistleblower complaint . <p> To some in elected office , the freedom of information can seem inconvenient . But the truth is that we can not do our jobs effectively without public watchdogs like journalists and concerned citizens holding us accountable . In today 's climate , we simply can not govern without transparency . <p> This is why I embrace my responsibilities under FOIA . At the end of the day , we need to hold ourselves to a higher standard that engages our communities about the day-to-day operations of government . This may require painful revelations to the public . However , we have no choice but to shine a light of transparency , particularly on the most troubling public information . Our democratic experiment is simply too important to die behind closed doors . <p> -- Tracy Hall is a Kalamazoo County commissioner , representing District 3 in the city of Kalamazoo . Hall is seeking the Democratic nomination in 2020 to the 60th District state House seat . County Chairperson Julie Rogers and Commissioner Stephanie Moore are also seeking the Democratic nomination .
