<p> Climate change is melting giant ice sheets covering Greenland and Antartica faster than previously expected , scientist have uncovered . As the ice melts , the rate of sea level rise will increase in South Louisiana , according to a new NASA tool that links melting of specific ice sheets to 293 affected coastal cities . <p> Though Greenland and Antartica may seem too far away to have such a direct impact on the Big Easy , the distance between the locations actually increases the impact , said Eric Larour , the project lead and co-developer of the Ice Sheet System Model . <p> Ice sheets have enough mass that they exert their own gravitational force , attracting nearby waters . As the ice sheets melt , water nearby recedes . " This means , that close to the ice melting , sea level decreases , but far from it , it increases , " Larour wrote in an email . <p> Furthering this effect is bedrock rebound below ice sheet melting . Bedrock rebound works like a foam mattress that expands back up when you wake up and get out of bed in the morning , Larour said . As ice melts , reducing its weight , the bedrock below rebounds pushing away nearby waters and dropping the sea level near the source of the melting . <p> Combined , these factors are why the melting of Greenland and Antartica will have a greater impact on sea level rise around New Orleans than melting in Alaska . While the melting of northwestern glaciers in Alaska will contribute to sea level rise in New Orleans , melting of glaciers in southeastern Alaska will actually decrease sea level near New Orleans because of the gravity effect . <p> In addition to these factors , as climate change warms the oceans it causes water to expand . This accounts for about 50 percent of sea level rise in coastal cities around the globe , while melting of all the ice in the world contributes to about 46 percent of sea level rise in New Orleans . <p> Sea level rise in South Louisiana is compounded by sinking land , also known as subsidence . The highest sea level rise estimate in the state 's 2017 coastal master plan predicts sea levels will increase by 2.72 feet over a 50-year period . <p> Partial melting of the Greenland ice sheet would cause a 3 foot rise in sea level , according to the NASA website . But determining the probability and time frame of the melt has proven difficult , Larour said . That 's because the models can not keep up with the observed melting . Previous models have proven overly conservative . " And , that 's not a good thing , " he said .
