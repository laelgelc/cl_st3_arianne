Berkley Bedell , U.S. Sen. Tom Harkin and U.S. Rep. Neal Smith , Democrats from Iowa . ( Photo : The Harkin Institute ) <p> Officially , Berkley Bedell retired from the U.S. House of Representatives in 1987 . But unofficially , he never really retired at all . <p> Although he made a fortune in fishing tackle and made the " Berkley " brand synonymous with fishing around the globe , he never lost his interest in important issues facing our nation and never stopped trying to make a difference . He was always trying to get more people to focus on dealing with the income gap or climate change , the federal budget problems or water quality , or the divisiveness in our nation 's politics . <p> I got to know Berk -- that 's what he always called himself -- when I was the Register 's opinion editor . He would regularly submit guest columns for me to consider for publication on our opinion pages . Without exception , his topics were in the news , and Berk wanted to stimulate the thinking of Register readers . <p> It 's not surprising that two months before his death , Berk was still at the keyboard of his computer , where he wrote a timely , and deeply personal , op-ed column for USA Today on the problems that sky-high salaries for corporate executives are creating in our nation . His views were significant , because Berk was an entrepreneur long before everyone started using that word willy-nilly . The fishing tackle business that he started in his bedroom when he was still in high school brought him nationwide attention in 1964 when he was named U.S. Small Businessman of the Year . <p> Berkley Bedell ( Photo : Special to the Register ) <p> After I joined him in retirement our friendship grew , but our shared interest was n't fishing . I never had his patience waiting for fish to bite . Instead , I traveled to Spirit Lake and to his winter home in Florida to talk with him about issues and ideas and solutions he was contemplating to address a wide range of issues facing our country . He was gracious and humble and always signed his letters and emails either " Peace , Berk " or " Love , Berk . " <p> Berk was n't someone who just wanted to spout off about what he thought ought to be done about this issue or that . When I began writing a column in retirement for several Iowa newspapers , Berk asked to be added to the distribution list . He was interested in my observations and ideas and would often respond with lengthy emails in which he added his thoughts and commented on mine . <p> Buy Photo <p> Randy Evans ( Photo : BILL NEIBERGALL/THE REGISTER , The Register ) <p> The fishing tackle business made Berk a very wealthy man . But during a reception he threw in Spirit Lake for his friends several years ago , I was struck by the wide assortment of people who were there -- the businessman from Minnesota who entered the U.S. House in January 1975 at the same time Berk did ; the men and women who worked for his Spirit Lake company ; the fishing buddies from around the Midwest ; and the community leaders and church friends from the Iowa Great Lakes . <p> In these times when too many of us view people as enemies if their political views differ from ours , we ought to remind ourselves what Berkley Bedell stood for .
