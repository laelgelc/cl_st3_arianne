<h> Bridges urges ' glorified social worker ' police to crack down on Extinction Rebellion <p> Simon Bridges has described police as " glorified social workers " and is urging them to be tougher on Extinction Rebellion protesters who vandalised his electorate office . <p> Bridges , National Party leader , told Magic Talk while he thinks it 's important people have the right to protest , the vandalism against his office in Tauranga was " unlawful " . <p> " I 've got workers who will be turning up there and will spend - I presume - a couple of hours or more cleaning this icky stuff off and that 's not right . " <p> Members of the environmental movement poured molasses , a thick dark syrup , over the steps to the Opposition leader 's office over the weekend to represent " dirty oil " . <p> In a Facebook post explaining the group 's intention , Extinction Rebellion 's Doug Doig said : " This is a climate and ecological emergency - we need people like National Party leader Simon Bridges to tell the truth about the state of things . " <p> Bridges is now urging the police to come down harder on the climate activists . <p> He said police have " become glorified social workers " and said their " job seems to be to have a hui and not any sort of doey when it comes to these sorts of things " . <p> Newshub has contacted the police for a response . <p> Bridges criticised Extinction Rebellion protesters in October when they blocked the entrance of the Ministry of Business , Innovation and Employment ( MBIE ) - the ministry for oil , gas and minerals - on Wellington 's Stout St. <p> " I think climate change is real and think we 've got to act , " Bridges said at the time . " But this emergency rebellion stuff is nonsense , it scares a lot of young people , and it does n't achieve anything . " <p> He told the protesters it would make more sense for them to march on Parliament and to blame the " woke " Government doing " nothing " on climate action . <p> Doig said in the Facebook post that Extinction Rebellion listened to the Opposition leader 's advice , " so this time we brought it right to his doorstep " . <p> As the Extinction Rebellion 's focus moved to Taranaki on Monday to protest oil giant OMV 's permit to search for offshore oil and gas , Bridges described the group as " woefully , woefully misguided " . <p> He told Magic Talk unlawful behaviour needs to be challenged , pointing to the likes of about 30 protesters who boarded the OMV " henchboat " Skandi Atlantic in Timaru and occupied it for three days . <p> The Skandi Atlantic was preparing to leave the Port of Timaru and travel north to meet the OMV oil rig off the Taranaki Coast , but was delayed by the occupation . <p> OMV is set to begin drilling three exploratory oil wells there , and another in the Great South Basin off the Otago coast . <p> Bridges said the protesters are breaking the law and suggested the police are letting them get away with it . He said it 's " no laughing matter " and that if the law is broken " the police should investigate " . <p> The former Energy Minister pointed to a 2013 law change he oversaw that protected the rights of oil companies making it illegal to intentionally damage or interfere with mining structures and vessels within a certain area . <p> Former Labour MP Maryan Street described it at the time as the " Strip New Zealanders of Their Right to Protest Bill " . <p> Bridges said it 's still the law and " in so many cases the police - maybe from Government instruction - are n't following the law " . <p> The Government banned new offshore oil and gas exploration permits in April 2018 , but existing permits would be honoured , which is why OMV is still able to explore for oil .
