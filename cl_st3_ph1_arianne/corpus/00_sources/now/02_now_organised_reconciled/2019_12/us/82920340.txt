If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <h> Decade rewind : Wild weather events keep Kitsap County on its toes <p> The past decade started with intense rain and flooding that left a whole neighborhood stranded , wound down with a tornado in Port Orchard in 2018 and ended with a week of back-to-back snowstorms in February that buried Kitsap County in accumulations over 2 feet in some areas . <p> Along the way , Kitsap residents were entertained by a near-total eclipse , dramatic lightning and the Skunk Bay Missile Crisis . <p> Here 's a look back at some of the decade 's major weather events . <h> 2010 - 2012 : Flooding , ice and wind <p> On Dec. 12 , 2010 , more than 6 inches of rain in a 24-hour period caused flooding and mudslides , closing roads and resulting in a washout on Hunter Road . The deluge eroded soil around a culvert under the road that could n't keep up with the flow . About 200 households were stranded on the far side of a 30-foot chasm . There were washouts on Wicks Lake ( with 50 families stranded ) , and on Lake Helena and Lake Flora roads . <p> Nov. 22 , 2010 , started with a pleasant blanket of snow , but by the evening commute , an arctic blast of cold air turned roads and highways into skating rinks and caused widespread power outages . Countless cars careened into ditches . Major highways became virtual parking lots . First responders scurried from one incident to another . By 5 p.m. , the Washington State Patrol had responded to more than 100 collisions along Kitsap roadways . <p> Buy Photo <p> 12/16/12 FILE Colby Kenfield owner of Al 's Olalla Public Store has sandbags on a bulkhead to keep the high tide away from his store on Monday morning . LARRY STEAGALL / KITSAP SUN ( Photo : Larry Steagall ) <p> On Dec. 17 , 2012 , a near-record high " King Tide " augmented by heavy rain the night before caused flooding of lowland buildings . Water breached the bulkhead in Olalla , flooding an outbuilding at Al 's of Olalla grocery and submerged the back deck of Barbie 's Seabeck Cafe . Bay Street in Port Orchard was closed by flooding , many of its buildings awash despite buttressing with sandbags . NOAA measured the Seattle-area high tide at 14.47 feet , just below the record of 14.48 feet . Similar flooding had occurred on Bay Street about a month earlier . <h> 2015 - 2017 : Wildfires , heat and sun <p> A late summer heatwave in 2015 made football players -- and other high school athletes -- sweat the start of the season . Some schools shifted practices into the evening as daily temperatures hit the high 80s , and some even practiced indoors . Hot , dry weather contributed to more than 150 active wildfires burning in British Columbia . Experts have said forest management practices over the past 100 years have contributed to an increase in the intensity of wildfires. <h> 2018 : Conspiracies , smoke , tornado <p> In June 2018 , Hansville weather tracker Greg Johnson created an Internet sensation when he published a photo from his continuous weather cam station of what looked initially to Johnson like a missile launch from Whidbey Island . The image , linked to Johnson 's Skunk Bay Weather website , became fodder for a rash of conspiracy theories . Johnson later concluded it was a helicopter from Airlift Northwest with its searchlight pointed straight down . <p> Wildfire smoke was back in 2018 , creating a visible haze for days on end during the summer . Air quality in Kitsap reached levels " unhealthy " for the general population . Smoke from wildfires in Canada and the Cascades , along with stagnant air , was the cause . <p> The Maple Fire on the Olympic Peninsula , which began as a small fire on Aug. 4 , 2018 , spread to 3,000 acres and lasted into September . Two men accused of illegally harvesting big maple trees were found to have started the fire by pouring gasoline on a bees ' nest and setting it on fire . <p> The hazy summer of 2018 was followed by a windy , wet winter , during which power outages were common . On Dec. 18 , a tornado touched down shortly before 2 p.m. in Port Orchard , one of two ever recorded in Kitsap County . Trees snapped like toothpicks behind Walmart remain as testimony to the twister 's force with winds peaking at an estimated 130 mph . A year later , some families were still displaced from their homes . <p> Buy Photo <p> 2018 FILE ART Hannah Mueller carries sopping wet Christmas presents to the driveway of her parent 's home as she helps them salvage what they can from their now roofless house on Tiburon Court in Port Orchard , Washington on Wednesday , December 19 , 2018 . It started Feb. 3 , with just a few inches . Six days later , frigid air from the north met moist air rolling in off the ocean . Heavy snow set in , burying the county with nearly a foot of powder in places . <p> The big snow was followed by two smaller bursts over the next three days . By Feb. 12 , when the snow petered out , the total accumulation in Bremerton was 20.7 inches , beating the record of 20 inches in 1949 . Some areas reported more than two feet accumulation . <p> Buy Photo <p> Garrett Stephan ( left ) pulls daughter Sonja , 3 , in the sled while son Marcus , 6 , runs alongside as the family enjoys the snow at Raab Park in Poulsbo , Washington on Sunday , February 3 , 2019 . Predictions of another bad fire season did n't materialize , however , with rain coming and going throughout the summer . <p> A dramatic lightning storm Sept. 7 lit up the region with more than 1,000 strikes , by one meteorologist 's estimate . It was followed less than a week later by another of less intensity . <p> Buy Photo <p> A rare lightning storm lights up the sky above the Bremerton Harborside Marina on Saturday , September 7 . ( Photo : Larry Steagall , Special to the Kitsap Sun ) <p> An unseasonably dry spell in late fall and early winter was broken Dec. 20 , with multiple bursts of rain over 24 hours that totaled the equivalent of a 10-year storm , according to one county official . The rain caused flooding throughout the county and erosion at one South Kitsap construction site . <h> Temperatures trending upward <p> Although it may feel like we 've gotten dumped on more over the past decade than usual , average precipitation trends show little change from mid-20th century , according to National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration climate data on the Puget Sound lowlands cited by Cliff Mass , professor of atmospheric sciences at the University of Washington . <p> Temperature is another story . Average temperatures in the Puget Sound lowlands -- encompassing much of Kitsap County -- have increased since the 1950s , ' 60s and ' 70s , data show . Global warming has occurred and will continue , said Mass , who has reported extensively on climate change . <p> While some areas of the country are seeing impacts of climate change in the form of sea-level rise and extreme heat , the Pacific Northwest , cooled by onshore flow from the Pacific Ocean , wo n't see significant impacts until later in the 21st century . Along with rising temperatures , precipitation will increase by 10% to 15% . The heaviest rain will increase by 15% to 40% , Mass predicts , and the snowpack will decline . <p> " You 're not going to ski in Snoqualmie Pass after 2050 , " he said . <p> Mass said there 's been much " hype " about climate change , on the one hand , by those who dismiss that humans have contributed to the process , on the other , by those who make overly pessimistic predictions about its potential impact on humankind . <p> He predicts people will adapt and respond . Options include moving away from flood-prone coastal areas , better managing forests to reduce the fuel load , developing viable alternative energy sources and technology to remove carbon dioxide from the atmosphere . <p> " Some global warming is guaranteed , " Mass said . " The most important thing is there 'll be changes , but it 's not going to be catastrophic or existential . It 's going to be slow enough around here we can take steps and protect ourselves from the impact . "
