<h> Fresh Talk : Have we become numb to the struggles of others ? <p> By Rowan O'Connell <p> Special to Hartford Courant <p> Dec 18 , 2019 6:00 AM <p> " Armed with a constant awareness of the problems of others outside of our community , it seems like an impossible task to help everyone , " the author writes . " Many of us simply become numb to the world beyond our immediate surroundings as a method of self-defense . " ( Eunika Sopotnicka/Getty Images/iStockphoto ) <p> My parents and nearly every other American alive at the time can remember in eerie detail where they were when a plane struck the first tower , catalyzing the events of 9/11 . <p> But can they recall where they were during the Las Vegas , Sandy Hook or Stoneman Douglas shootings ? How many people can call to mind what they were doing when a catastrophic earthquake struck Haiti in 2010 ? <p> Advertisement <p> As members of a society that has become characterized by violence and sensationalized media coverage , many of us are not easily shocked . We 've moved so far away from the days when the news had to be carried from town to town by horseback or pen and paper , or when entire oceans separated us from the news of disaster . Today , it takes mere seconds for the news of tragedy to illuminate our screens . There is no longer a filter to shield us from catastrophes and scandals big and small . <p> The massive influx of news may put the suffering of others at the top of our news feeds , but it is a far greater task to make it top of mind . Armed with a constant awareness of the problems of others outside of our community , it seems like an impossible task to help everyone . Many of us simply become numb to the world beyond our immediate surroundings as a method of self-defense . <p> In mid-century America , the news media was at the center of American life , with every morning beginning with the daily newspaper and the sun setting with the nightly news . Today , many of us avoid the news altogether , hoping to spare ourselves the gory details of the global disasters unfolding . However , this has only hindered quantitative change and legislation from being passed to improve these problems . We prefer to look past the hard truths that weapons , drugs , social prejudice and climate change are bringing about the moral and physical decay of the world as we know it . It may be a hard pill to swallow , but without knowing how these issues are affecting others all over the world , there will be no motivation for change . <p> While isolationism may seem necessary to remain positive about the state of society , it raises the question : are we losing our sense of empathy ? <p> Reviving empathy seems to be an insurmountable challenge . We ca n't restore empathetic behavior through concrete means or legislation . We have to alter behavior in hopes of spurring a more conscious approach to modern problems . An organization called Narrative4 , the brain-child of a collective of leaders in social justice , seeks to generate empathy through storytelling . Armed with research from the Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence , N4 encourages story exchange . This curated method of story-telling involves trading stories one-on-one followed by a group reflection . By creating awareness for the struggles and triumphs of others , while giving participants the ability to share their own experiences and participate in self-reflection , N4 hopes to create a society that takes a more empathetic approach to a myriad of societal issues . <p> The need for empathy in a world divided by prejudice was also the driving factor behind the creation of The Museum of Lost and Found , which launched in London in October . The exhibit features the possessions of 16 people who have been victims , survivors , or those left behind by battles with mental illness . The objects featured in the exhibit are intended to show the gravity of mental illness . United for Global Mental Health , the organization responsible for the initiative , hopes the exhibit will put faces to the lives being lost . <p> In a society that has become numb to the struggles of others , organizations are hoping to clear the fog and reclaim compassion and spur change . <p> Rowan O'Connell is a junior at the University of Connecticut . She is from Durham .
