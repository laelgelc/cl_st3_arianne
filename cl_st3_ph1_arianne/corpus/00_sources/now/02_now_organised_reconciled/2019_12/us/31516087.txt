" My car smelled -- even after hand-washing it and going through a car wash -- like rotting fish , " said Delsa . <p> Steve Pavlovich , an entomologist for Mosquito Control Inc. of Metairie , said midges may be swarming in higher numbers due to increased freshwater in the area . The tiny bugs are just one indicator of a broader disruption that can be traced to the opening of the Bonnet Carre spillway . <p> Water from the Mississippi River has thundered through the spillway for a record-breaking number of days this year . Not only is it the longest the spillway has ever been open in one year -- 96 days as of July 1 -- but it 's also the first time in its 88-year history that it 's been opened twice , and the only time it 's been opened in back-to-back years . <p> The spillway is meant to protect New Orleans and surrounding areas from potentially devastating floods . It diverts water from the river into Lake Pontchartrain , sluicing millions of gallons of freshwater and runoff into the lake and coastal areas . <p> While the spillway has served its purpose of protecting New Orleans , experts say the influx of freshwater and all that it carries with it has tilted the environmental scales . <p> Capt . George Ricks saw the first dead dolphin on April 10 in Lake of Two Trees in St. Bernard Parish . He saw the second one the same day , just two miles away . <p> " I knew something was wrong because I 've been fishing these waters for 50 years , " said Ricks . " I 've seen two in 50 years of fishing that looked like they were boat strikes . " By July , he had recorded at least 37 other dead dolphins in the area . <p> " A beautiful creature like that -- it 's disturbing , " said Ricks . " We 're seeing a lot of live ones with the lesions on them . From what they tell me , it 's a slow , painful death . Bacteria get in these lesions and it gets infected . " <p> More freshwater can contribute to an already-tenuous situation for the area 's bottlenose dolphins , still in recovery after the 2010 Deepwater Horizon disaster unleashed 168 million gallons of oil just 41 miles from Louisiana 's coast , said Erin Fougeres , the stranding program administrator for the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Agency , or NOAA . <p> The agency has declared an official " unusual mortality event " in the northern Gulf of Mexico , including Louisiana , Alabama , Mississippi and the Florida Panhandle . They recorded 286 stranded dolphins -- three times the usual number -- between Feb. 1 and June 27 . Of the 286 recorded deaths , 91 were along Louisiana 's coast . <p> A historic season of high water on the Mississippi River may finally be drawing toward a close . <p> The Gulf 's dolphins are especially vulnerable because of their previous exposure to the oil spill , said Lori Schwacke , chief scientist at the National Marine Mammal Foundation . They have more chronic health issues like lung disease , fertility problems and a lower threshold for stress response . <p> " The influx of freshwater is definitely contributing to the issues , " said Schwacke . Though researchers ca n't pin down exactly why three times as many dolphins are dying , 26 percent of those documented had lesions indicating they had been exposed to unhealthy amounts of freshwater . <p> The deaths could be related to a virus , the freshwater , the oil spill or all of the above , but issues that dolphins face can also be a red flag warning of potential impacts to humans . <p> As temperatures rise and New Orleanians head to Gulf Coast beaches , they could be greeted by an unsettling sight : the bodies of dolphins , dead ... <p> " Dolphins are the apex predator and they live in coastal areas , as we do , " said Fougeres . " We do think of them as a sentinel species . " <p> Fougeres sees the deaths as a harbinger of a bigger problem . <p> " It 's more of an indicator of the effects of climate change , " she said . " This is an unprecedented year . " <p> Experts who follow events like the spillway openings , the spreading algae bloom , the uptick in dolphin deaths and the swarm of pellet-like midges keep using the same word : " unprecedented . " <p> In Lake Pontchartrain , an algae bloom birthed by the influx of nutrient-rich river water is expanding , spreading to beaches where families across the Southeast normally travel to spend July 4 . More than a dozen beaches along the Mississippi and Louisiana coast are closed . It never came down significantly , said Lopez , triggering the spillway opening on Feb. 27 . <p> The spillway stayed open for 44 days , closing on April 11 after letting in a volume of water equivalent to about 140,300 Olympic swimming pools each day . <p> Less than a month later , the spillway was opened again to keep the water below the threshold of 1.25 million cubic feet per second . It remains open , bringing the total number of days open in 2019 to 96 so far . <p> But it was n't just freshwater entering the ecosystem . The water carried with it nutrients that rob the lake of oxygen , creating unlivable conditions for some species . <p> Padmanava Dash , an assistant professor of geosciences at Mississippi State University , has been sending students to sample water quality since last March . When they conducted sampling mid-June , they only found oysters at two of five sites . Of those , the oysters they did find were all dead . <p> " Oyster mortality is at the highest level it 's ever been , " said Dash . The Mississippi Department of Marine Resources found mortality rates of between 87% and 100% in a recent survey . <p> Dash is certain the influx of freshwater from the Bonnet Carre Spillway is killing the oysters . <p> The algae bloom in Lake Pontchartrain has taken a toll on recreational pursuits in St. Tammany Parish , with the parish 's public fishing pier a ... <p> The freshwater also introduces nitrogen and phosphorous , which encourages algae blooms . Those are n't good for human or animal health while they 're active , but they also impact oysters long-term . " After algae dies , it sinks to the bottom and becomes food for microbes , " said Dash . " That also lowers the oxygen level . " <p> Those conditions led St. Bernard Parish President Guy McInnis to declare a state of emergency after the first opening of the spillway in 2019 . Flooding an oyster bed is just like flooding any other farmer 's land , said McInnis . <p> " People do n't see these crops dying . If you flood in Nebraska you see it ; it 's easy to understand . But we get flooded , too -- it 's just in open waters , " said McInnis . <p> For oyster farmer Brad Robin , May through October is typically planting season , when he would start making new oyster beds from mounds of crushed concrete in a pile 65 feet high that spans 300 feet on his rock yard in St. Bernard Parish . But this season , the rock yard is a ghost town . Thousands of acres of oyster beds have died off , said Robin , making oystermen like him question whether the industry can survive without receive federal aid . <p> Robin pointed to a boat named Lady Robin . " That boat is a $600,000 investment . That one is a $200,000 investment , " he said , pointing to a smaller boat . ' " <p> Robin , 65 , said the experts are asking for the opinions of oyster farmers like him . <p> " They ask us , ' What do you think ? ' " said Robin , whose family has been fishing in St. Bernard for decades . " In 100 years , no one has ever seen this . We really do n't know . We do know if slime and algae is there , oysters wo n't grow . " <p> Oysters take three years to grow in the best of conditions . " I expect this to knock out one-third of the oyster farmers in the impacted areas , " said Robin . " Unless we have a miracle , the devastation is not going to go away next year . It 's going to get worse . " <p> Everyone agrees that the spillway has to be open . " We ca n't flood our communities , " said McInnis . <p> But they note the Morganza spillway , over 100 miles northwest of Bonnet Carre , has n't been opened this year . It 's only been opened twice in its history ; it flushes freshwater into the Atchafalaya Basin . <p> " The question we have from our fisherman is , ' Why ca n't we split the difference ? ' " said McInnis . " The damage to our estuary on the east side wo n't be as bad if we share the damage , " he said . <p> The Corps is conducting modeling to evaluate the Old , Mississippi , Atchafalaya and Red Rivers and consider alternatives to flood risk management . The effort will take another three to five years , a representative said . <p> Everyone also agrees that the heavy use of the spillway is a symptom of a bigger problem -- what Lopez calls " climate weirdness .
