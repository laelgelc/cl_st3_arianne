<p> Companies increasingly talk about being climate positive , but they disagree on what that means and how to measure it -- a problem business leaders took a first step toward resolving this week . <p> Mounting concerns about rising temperatures and other environmental impacts from human consumption are propelling consumer-facing brands to make a variety of green pledges . But the proliferation of terms tied to this effort is stirring debate and dissent . <p> Environmentalists and some companies worry that ambiguity is hurting environmental progress , confusing consumers and opening the door to greenwashing -- where companies appear more sustainable than they are . <p> " Our objective is to avoid a huge divergence in what it means to be climate positive , " said Mark Griffiths , who works on climate issues at the World Wide Fund for Nature . WWF facilitated an event Tuesday in Madrid at the United Nations ' conference on climate change , known as COP25 , for companies to discuss the parameters for a global definition . <p> It is n't clear how this would be implemented , but executives say an independent certification body could help set standards and verify they are being followed . <p> IKEA has pledged to be climate positive by 2030 and said last month that it would spend ? 200 million ( $221 million ) toward this effort . It will shift to renewable energy , be more energy efficient , halve emissions tied to travel and invest in reforestation to remove carbon from the atmosphere and store it . <p> The furniture giant is leading the drive to establish a definition and plans to outline its approach at the U.N . conference . <p> IKEA thinks companies should be climate positive on an absolute basis , regardless of growth , rather than a relative basis . Many technically recyclable materials go to landfills because of high processing costs or poor infrastructure . <p> Agreeing on the definition took about a year , said Sander Defruyt , who works on plastics issues at the foundation . <p> " We very quickly conceptually aligned on the need to move away from stuff being theoretically recyclable to actually recyclable , " Mr. Defruyt said . IKEA is n't counting on offsets and does n't think others should . <p> Climate campaigners also think the practice is largely undesirable .
