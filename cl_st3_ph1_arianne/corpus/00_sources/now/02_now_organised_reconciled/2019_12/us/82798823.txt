<h> Germany signs off on flagship climate plan <p> by Coralie Febvre , Kit Holden <p> Merkel 's government is under increasing pressure to tackle climate change <p> The German parliament on Friday formally approved a sweeping package of climate policy reforms , in a relief to Chancellor Angela Merkel 's government as it faces growing pressure to take environmental action . <p> The so-called climate package , which includes plans to make train travel cheaper and increase taxes on air travel , will take effect on January 1 after months of wrangling . <p> Previously blocked by a dispute over costing , the bill was passed by the upper house after MPs reached a compromise on a higher carbon price of 25 euros ( $27 ) per tonne earlier this week . <p> " We have achieved a national consensus on climate that will give us fresh momentum to reach our climate goals , " said Economy Minister and close Merkel ally Peter Altmaier . <p> The package is intended to help Europe 's largest economy reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 55 percent by 2030 compared to 1990 levels . <p> That promises to be a huge challenge in a country that despite its green reputation abroad remains heavily dependent on polluting coal power and has a fondness for gas-guzzling cars . <p> The climate package has already attracted criticism from environmentalists and business lobbies , but the government was keen to show it was taking global warming seriously after months of massive " Fridays for Future " protests . <p> The bill 's approval also comes hot on the heels of European Commission chief Ursula Von Der Leyen 's ambitious new " Green Deal " unveiled earlier this month , aimed at making the bloc carbon neutral by 2050 . <p> CO2 price <p> Germany 's climate package is estimated to cost the government around 54 billion euros by 2023 . <p> The slew of measures will see long-distance rail tickets become roughly 10 percent cheaper , while flights will face surcharges of 13 to 60 euros depending on the distance . <p> Following the compromise struck between the government and lawmakers , Germany will charge a starting price of 25 euros per tonne of carbon dioxide emissions in transport and construction from 2021 . <p> The government had initially proposed a rate of just 10 euros per tonne -- outraging environmental campaigners , the opposition Greens and even members of Merkel 's own coalition partner the Social Democrats . <p> Coal still accounts for a third of Germany 's energy mix <p> The rate is set to increase to 55 euros per tonne by 2025 , before eventually being incorporated into an EU-wide carbon trading system . <p> To offset the higher costs for consumers and companies , the climate package includes subsidies for electric cars and tax incentives for greener heating , electricity and housing . <p> Merkel on Monday welcomed the carbon price compromise as a " positive contrast " to the lack of progress achieved at last week 's inconclusive COP25 global climate conference in Madrid . <p> " The different parties ... showed a willingness to tend towards a solution without letting things go on forever , " she said . <p> ' Too late ' <p> But academics from " Scientists for Future " said the CO2 price was " too weak " to convince households and companies to change their behaviour . <p> " It 's too little , too late , " the campaigners said in a withering verdict of the climate package . <p> Winfried Kretschmann , a leading figure in the Green party and state premier of Baden-Wuerttemberg , was cautiously optimistic . <p> " It 's a step in the right direction " but " many issues remain unresolved " , he said . <p> One of the most hotly-contested topics is Germany 's planned coal exit by 2038 , a controversial decision that has pitted environmentalists against those wanting to protect jobs in the sector . <p> The dirty fossil fuel still accounts for around a third of Germany 's energy mix , in part because of Merkel 's 2011 decision to phase out nuclear power . <p> Holger Loesch , deputy director of the Federation of German Industry ( BDI ) said Germany faced higher gas and electricity prices as a result of the climate package that would hurt businesses competing against foreign rivals . <p> " It 's uncertain how German companies will stay competitive in the future , " he warned . <p> This document is subject to
