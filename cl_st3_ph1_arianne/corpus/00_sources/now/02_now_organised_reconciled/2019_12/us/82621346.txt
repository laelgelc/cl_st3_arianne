If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <h> York County 's new commissioners must be leaders rather than managers <p> Jim McClure Published 10:42 a.m . ET Dec. 11 , 2019 <p> CLOSE <p> I was reading through idea-packed Rusk Reports 1.0 through 3.0 this fall , prepping to write about three York Daily Record/History Center forums on affordable housing and health care , jobs and the economy . <p> I thought about how to unpack all the ideas for economic and social revitalization put forth by urban planner David Rusk in those reports that covered York County in the past 50 years . <p> What is one thing that emerges ? I thought . One idea that is indispensable to spark progress in the county ? <p> I was reminded that many of Rusk 's possible solutions to long-standing problems in our 900-square-mile region west of the Susquehanna River are countywide in scope . <p> Of course . That was the big takeaway : We must get better at thinking as a county , or even regionally , rather than as 72 individual townships , boroughs and the city . <p> Loading ... <p> This chart , prepared by urban planner David Rusk , shows an overall rise in the county poverty rate in the past 50 years , from 5.6 percent to 7.3 percent . This rate is influenced by the rise in the city 's overall poverty rate from 10.2 percent to 32.7 percent . Rusk 's big point in his three reports is that the city ca n't solve poverty by itself . If the county does n't own the problem , the overall county rate will just continue to rise . <p> Here 's just one reason : <p> The city 's poverty level has risen , and so has the county 's . In the past 50 years , the county 's poverty rate has risen from 5.6 percent to 7.3 percent . We did n't adequately address the city 's poverty challenge , and predictably that has impacted the inner-ring suburbs and the rest of the county . <p> Rusk puts it this way in his 2002 report : " But I concluded that ' suburban sprawl , concentrated poverty , and fiscal disparities are eroding York County 's quality of life . They are n't just York City 's problems . They are county-wide problems . They must be addressed through county-wide strategies . ' " <p> With a new board of county commissioners coming in , it 's time to recommit to seeking solutions with a countywide scope . <p> After three forums and discussion about the county 's challenges , here 's my thinking about four things the new board -- Julie Wheeler , Ron Smith and Doug Hoke -- should know : <p> Today , Highpoint in eastern York County is a beautiful county park . To get there , a protracted legal battle cost the two county commissioners who persevered in a political act of courage through the controversy -- Lori Mitrick and Doug Kilgore -- their offices . ( Photo : File ) <h> Serving with political courage <p> In the past 15 years , certain members of two boards of county commissioners would qualify as recipients of York County profiles in courage . <p> You can disagree with their decisions , but these four commissioners took those actions knowing that they would be unpopular with the public . <p> Here 's my take on these two controversial issues : <p> What was at stake with Highpoint , and the associated Lauxmont property , was residential development of perhaps the two most spectacular pieces of hilltop properties in the county . Thousands of people today benefit from Highpoint and other county park sites gained from the commissioners ' politically costly intervention . <p> The York County almshouse or poorhouse , dating back to 1805 , came down before many other magnificent York County structures were demolished in the 1960s and 1970s . The Asylum Project website says the buildings were demolished before 1950 . Alexander D. Goode Elementary School today stands on its footprint . The York County Hospital and Home ( Pleasant Acres ) , which today serves as a conventional nursing home , is a successor to the almshouse . In a controversial move , the York County Commissioners , by a 2 to 1 vote , sold the home to a private company in 2018 . ( Photo : York County Heritage Trust photo . ) <p> With Pleasant Acres , the old York County Home , one should wonder why the county would still be in the nursing home business in the 21st century , a vestige of the county-run poorhouse that went up in 1805 . Other Pennsylvania counties outsourced this piece years ago . <p> The Highpoint controversy cost Mitrick and Kilgore their elected positions . And the wrath of some residents about Pleasant Acres poured upon Byrnes and Reilly . <p> So history shows that to be effective and foster change , the new board of commissioners must show courage and hold on to their elected positions lightly and lead as if they 'll serve for only the next four years . <p> In fact , to be effective , all our elected officials should always lead as if this is their last term . <h> Acting as countywide leaders <p> The new commissioners must decide if they 're going to lead change in the county or manage county operations . There 's an important difference . <p> Leadership covers management , but management does not necessarily equal leadership . <p> This example of the difference between a leader and a manager has made the rounds over the years : Two people are stuck in the bottom of a deep , dry well . One of the captives , the leader , figures how to get a ladder to climb out of there . The other captive , the manager , decides which spot on the floor to secure the ladder when it arrives and which is the most substantial wall to lean it against . <p> The commissioners need to lead the county to act as a county . They must persuade county residents to face challenges to the quality of life in York County as issues that we all must own , no matter the geography . <p> If for no other reason , it 's a matter of self-interest . If the eastern part of the county is flood ravaged , then we must help as a county in the recovery . Towns along the Codorus Creek could be next . <p> You might think you have a green oasis in some corner of York County , but the same factors challenging more populated areas will find you : Rusk cites these two inevitable laws : " The new beats out the old , " and " Today 's winners become tomorrow 's losers . " <p> For years , the three commissioners have divided up management responsibility for the various functional areas : Children and Youth Services , the 911 center , the county prison and all those other county departments . They spend a lot of time broadly overseeing the directors of these important areas . <p> It 's time for our new board of commissioners to find a way to lead in the larger picture -- in addition to the functional part of managing a county . Leadership would include the bringing together of what Rusk called the four economic regions -- central York , York-Hanover , York-Harrisburg and York-Baltimore . <p> Rusk does not advocate mergers of municipalities , but the creation of more formalized inter-municipality agreements to handle joint trash collection and other costly services . <p> The commissioners could foster these -- called " Communities of Common Interest " ( CCI ) -- and knock down impediments , allowing , say , struggling fire departments to consolidate . <p> The current board of commissioners gave us a bold and Rusk-like example of thinking countywide in proposing a small tax increase to preserve land in the county . In retrospect , they could have broadened their scope and covered preservation of historic buildings that could be applied to the county-owned Strickler farmhouse and other at-risk properties . <p> What the new county commissioners need is a consistent big-picture strategy , and they need to make that strategy a priority . They need to be talking about finding solutions to broad quality-of-life issues . <p> In short , the new commissioners need to refresh their knowledge of the countywide thinking behind Rusk 's proposals , even if they do n't buy into all of his specific recommendations . <p> The urgent issues -- the 911 center and Children and Youth Services -- can be consuming , but leaders can -- and must -- rise above such management challenges . <h> Thinking , speaking aspirationally <p> The commissioners have a high pulpit , a bully pulpit , to appeal to all residents of York County to act as a unit and to work toward maintaining and improving the quality of life in this old county , a place that will celebrate its 275th anniversary in 2024 . <p> The governor speaks aspirationally about big issues that face Pennsylvania , and the mayor gives the big picture of where the city should be . <p> Consider the scope and vision shown by Mayor Michael Helfrich , in a recent interview , in discussing updating the city 's comprehensive plan : He wrote that the first planning meeting would " focus on public safety , public health , economy/poverty , housing quality , neighborhood preservation , transportation , education and climate change . " <p> He focused on sweeping issues , not department functions such as lighting in a park or trash collection in an alley , as important as they are . Those are issues that can be handled if the people aspire for more , for a better quality of life . <h> Solving problems behind the problems <p> Our county commissioners have big jobs . <p> They need to deal with all kinds of state and federal mandates . <p> They must address the functional areas and face daunting challenges in Children and Youth and 911 . But as we 've said , those are vertical , management issues . <p> Now is the time for the commissioners to lead broadly , to think and scan horizontally across York County 's rolling hills , to look across the river to Lancaster and other parts of the region for partnerships and to speak accordingly . <p> Leaders seek to inspire people to solve the problem behind the problem <p> If they do so , poverty and other besetting issues that cause , say , the Children and Youth Services issues , would be less daunting . <p> Our county leaders should make their own run through the Rusk Reports and other such calls to action and discover what strikes them . <p> Putting it another way , they must search for -- and find -- the ladder that leads us out of the well and into the future . <h> Upcoming events <p> I will present on the topic " The Susquehanna Plays a Role in the Birth of our Nation " at 6 p.m .
