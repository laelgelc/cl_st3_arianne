If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <h> Climate change : If you worry about global warming , the next car you buy should be electric <p> If you 're concerned about climate change , the next car you buy really should be electric . <p> Why ? Because the average car , SUV or light truck in the United States is on the road for 11.8 years . So vehicles bought this coming year will be part of the shift away from fossil fuels that climate scientists say needs to be well underway within 10 years . <p> " By 2030 we need to be really well into this transition . Which means people need to be buying these cars now , " said Lewis Fulton , director of the Sustainable Transportation Energy Pathways program at the University of California , Davis . <p> " This thing is just super cheap to drive . And the only maintenance I 've had to do in two years is buy a $10 air filter , " said Dan Workman , of Zimmerman , Minnesota , about his Chevrolet Bolt . He commutes 45 minutes each way to his job in Minneapolis , where he 's an engineer . <p> People who make the decision for environmental reasons say they find the cost-effectiveness is an added bonus . <p> " After seeing the latest climate reports , we decided we could no longer wait to act , since the future of our three young children was at stake , " said Eric Laine of Chenango , New York , where he teaches at the State University of New York in Delhi . <p> After driving his Bolt 8,000 miles in three months , he realized what a significant savings he was making by not having to buy gas . <p> " I can confidently say that we will not be purchasing another gas-powered vehicle , " he said . <p> Eric Laine 's family went hiking while they charged their Chevrolet Bolt electric vehicle in Taughannock Falls State Park in Ithaca , New York . " We decided to stop at a free public Level 2 charger located in this New York State park . We did not need the charge , but we wanted to try out public charging while we went hiking . It was a great experience for the kids and made for a great opportunity to teach the kids how easy it is to charge up an electric car while we hiked and enjoyed a picnic lunch , " he said . ) <h> Fun , not politics , drives many EV owners <p> Talk to electric vehicle owners for more than five minutes and they 'll inevitably start using words like " fun " and " peppy . " Electric vehicles have more torque than gas-powered vehicles and the power goes straight to the wheels , so they accelerate fast . <p> In Jonesborough , Tennessee , Darlene Denis , 52 , said she used to love to drive when she was younger but eventually it just became a chore . Then she got a Bolt . <p> " Now I want to drive all the time , everywhere , " she said . <p> California has 10 times as many electric-vehicle owners as any other state , and the number of owners is rising nationwide , though sometimes not with entirely open arms . <p> Blake VanWinkle , 35 , works in oil and gas exploration . He bought his Tesla 3 simply because it 's a great car . Living in Wichita Falls , Texas , he says he sometimes runs up against prejudice against electric cars that he feels is unwarranted . <p> " Being a Republican , I do get the eye from some misinformed conservatives that think it 's linked to the Democratic Party , " he said . <h> Range anxiety slowly going away <p> Range anxiety -- the fear that you 'll run out of charge before you get to a charger -- is slowly going away as electric vehicles ' batteries get better . <p> Electric vehicle drivers say they learn to plan just as they did when they had gas cars , said Jeannie Faye , who lives in Palm Beach County , Florida . <p> In her Tesla 3 , " the 240-mile battery range is roughly equivalent to having a full tank of gas anyway , " she said . <p> An electric car requires a slightly different mind-set , said Timothy Kramer , who commutes 80 miles each way from his home in Poplar Grove , Illinois , to his job in Chicago . <p> " Every night while I sleep , the car fills up , " he said . " I do not miss the days of getting in my car , in a hurry to get somewhere , only to find out that I was too lazy the night before to fill it up and now it 's on ' E. ' " <p> Most homeowners can easily charge in a garage or carport , giving them a usual range of between 150 to 250 miles a day . Things are a little harder for renters , who make up about 35% of the U.S. population , according to the U.S. Census Bureau . <p> Kramer , who works at his family-owned real estate company , says charging stations are becoming significant considerations both when they 're developing new properties or upgrading old ones . <p> They have to " in order to stand-out against competing properties , " he said . <h> Some vehicle game-changers on the horizon <p> The top-selling electric vehicles in the United States are Teslas , Bolts and the Nissan LEAF . <p> Loren McDonald is an electric vehicle analyst who 's writing a book on the factors that will drive mass adoption . He sees several key things happening in the next 18 months that will begin to tip the scales to electric vehicles . <p> The first is Ford 's Mustang Mach-E , an all-electric sports coupe with an expected range of 300 miles before it needs charging . The other is the arrival of an electric Ford F150 , also expected in 2021 . <p> " This could drive a shift in the thinking of a lot of people . ' If Ford is making an electric Mustang and an electric pickup , maybe this is real , ' " he said . <h> Electric cars not yet mainstream <p> Nationwide , electric vehicles and hybrids still make up just a smidgen of what 's on the road -- just 2% , according to research firm IHS Markit . <p> Sales of electric vehicles have more than doubled from 2017 , but it 's a doubling of a small number . That year , they were 0.6% of all cars registered in the United States . In 2018 , they were 1.2% of all cars and as of last month they were 1.4% of all cars registered this year , said Tom Libby , an automotive analyst with the Southfield , Michigan , company . <p> By the company 's count , there were 722,388 electric vehicles in operation in the United States as of Oct. 1 . <p> The biggest hurdle is that they 're something new and different meant to replace something well-known and trusted . " People are really not familiar with the technology , with the fuel , with the charging time , the range , " Libby said . <p> For some , overcoming that was a matter of faith . Ellen Wade of Louisville , Kentucky , was moved to buy an electric car because her church advocates environmental conservation . <p> Driving her quiet and smooth car " has been like a dream come true , " she said . " And I 'm proud in the knowledge that I am reducing my use of fossil fuels . "
