The Republican-controlled Congress is moving to overturn an Obama administration rule intended to clamp down <p> WASHINGTON -- A leading natural gas trade association came out in support of carbon taxes Tuesday , as pressure increases on the oil and gas industry to better work with government on addressing climate change . <p> Representing companies including Exxon Mobil , Chevron , ConocoPhillips , Cabot and Southwestern Energy , Natural Gas Supply Association President Dena Wiggins said member companies were unanimous in their support for creating a carbon price and suggested more in the industry were likely to follow . <p> " It 's such a huge , important national conversation , every trade association has to be thinking about it , " she said . " We 're likely not going to be the last . " <p> The Natural Gas Supply Association said it is not yet lobbying for any one specific proposal , but broadly supports putting a price on carbon while also eliminating existing regulations on carbon and delivering revenues not to government but directly to American consumers . <p> The move comes as a number of state regulators are debating how to go about decarbonizing the power grid , including direct subsidies for nuclear plants , which have been closing at a fast pace in recent years amid increasing competition from gas and renewables . At the same time some cities are weighing bans on natural gas , in a bid to address climate change . <p> But Wiggins said that was not why her trade group chose to come out in support of a carbon tax . <p> " It might help our image but that 's not why we did it , " she said . " We think it 's the right thing to do and want to be part of the conversation . " <p> A number of individual oil companies , including Exxon , BP and Royal Dutch Shell , have already said they support a carbon tax . <p> Not included in the NGSA 's announcement were other greenhouse gas emissions such as methane , which oil companies routinely flare in lieu of building pipelines and other infrastructure to get natural gas to market . Methane also escapes from leaks in pipelines.
