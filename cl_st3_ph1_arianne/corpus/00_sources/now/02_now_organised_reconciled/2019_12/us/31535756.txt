The city has seen 474 killings so far this year . That 's obviously nothing to celebrate in a city many Illinoisans call home but the last time Chicago registered fewer than 500 homicides was in 2015 . <p> As significant and encouraging as the latest numbers are -- especially given a record 769 people were killed in 2016 -- Chicago has again eclipsed totals in New York and Los Angeles . The city 's 2019 homicide rate is about three times higher than Los Angeles ' and six times higher than New York 's . <p> Also sobering is the persistent " safety gap " in Chicago , where the most violent police district has a homicide rate 45 times higher than the safest district . The 11th District on the West Side ( the most violent ) has had 69 homicides so far this year . The 20th and 19th Districts on the Northside ( the safest ) have had zero and four homicides , respectively . <p> -- SENATE " CAMPAIGN " : Voters have no say in one of the most-watched campaigns of the season -- that 's the unofficial race to be named president of the state Senate . It 's a position that will be voted on behind closed doors by state senators in Springfield next month . Sens . Don Harmon , Kimberly Lightford and Elgie Sims are all in the running for the job that requires political savvy as well as an ability to raise money for fellow senators . Maybe to prove that second point , Harmon and Lightford have been in a fundraising frenzy ( So far , Sims has n't jumped into the money fray ) . <p> In December , Lightford 's personal campaign raised $124,200 from 41 donors , including $30,000 from Sen. Heather Steans , according to the State Board of Elections . The Senate Democratic Leadership Fund , which Lightford created to help other senators , raised $466,900 from 90 supporters , including $57,800 each from Steans , Sen. Andy Manar , and Lightford herself . <p> During the same period , Harmon 's campaign fund , which he has traditionally used to help fund other candidates , has raised $744,301 from 37 donations in December , including some $300,000 he transferred to himself from Oak Park Dems and his committeeperson funds . <h> ENDORSEMENTS <p> -- State Treasurer Mike Frerichs has endorsed Democrat Dani Brzozowski in the race for the 16th Congressional District seat now held by Republican Adam Kinzinger . The beloved political strategist died the day after Christmas last year of a brain hemorrhage . ( His birthday would 've been this coming Sunday . ) <p> Frank Capra 's George Bailey and Ebenezer Scrooge found meaning in life after encountering angels and ghosts . So how would life in Chicago be different had Sleet lived ? <p> " No question , I would n't have been in a runoff , " Ald. Roderick Sawyer told Playbook of the race for his Council seat . He saw Sleet as his most trusted adviser . Sleet 's political insight also helped Kim Foxx defeat incumbent Anita Alvarez for Cook County State 's Attorney in 2016 . He likely would have advised Foxx during the initial fallout of the Jussie Smollet case had he been alive . <p> Sleet loved the neighborhoods , but he was gaining a national footprint working for the Kivvit public affairs firm , where he became a spokesman for the Obama Foundation . " Brian was like a political outsider and insider at the same time , " Kivvit 's Eric Sedler said . " He had amazing relationships across the board . I think that 's something that made him so sought after as a political adviser . " <p> Just before his death , Sleet was gearing up to join Bill Daley 's mayoral campaign , but you have to wonder whether he would have been advising Lori Lightfoot after the run-off . They both believed in building up neighborhoods . <p> Lightfoot adviser Joanna Klonsky , who worked with Sleet on campaigns , tells Playbook : " There are many times I 've been called in for advice by the mayor and I think ' What would Brian say right now ?
