Many of the protesters ca n't drive yet , but , together with the help of community allies and parents who can drive them to protests , they have pressured local school administrators and politicians to do more to address our local carbon footprint . <p> Along the way , they enlisted the help of 16-year-old Thunberg , fresh off her scathing condemnation of world leaders at the United Nations . <p> The strikes have ranged in attendance , from one to thousands , but the common denominator has been City High student Massimo Paciotto-Biggers , 14 . <p> Buy Photo <p> Massimo Paciotto-Biggers , 13 , sits for a photo during a weekly walkout demanding solar panels on school buildings , Friday , April 5 , 2019 , outside the Iowa City Community School District offices along North Dodge Street in Iowa City , Iowa . Through the year , the teenager has worked with peers and community partners to apply a steady pressure on elected officials <p> Climate activism is also familiar in Paciotto-Biggers ' family . The teen 's dad , Jeff Biggers , is an author and climate activist , who around the time the protests started , penned a column calling for Mayor Jim Throgmorton to bolster the city 's climate goals or resign . <h> Moving the needle <p> The climate strikes have seen results , though protesters have not gotten everything they asked for . <p> The strikes started with a handful of students walking out of classes to downtown Iowa City in April of 2019 , but soon moved to the hill outside the ICCSD administration building . The group demanded solar panels at every campus and that environmental sustainability lessons are built into the curriculum . <p> At Iowa City school board meetings , they stressed state and international indicators of climate change : Flooding in Iowa , news of worsening heatwaves , a UN report of unprecedented extinctions and the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration 's reports of carbon dioxide levels reaching an 800,000-year high . <p> Paciotto-Biggers told board members he was frustrated administrators did not meet with students on the hill , despite media coverage and declarations of climate emergencies across the nation . <p> " You have three choices tonight : You can do nothing and dismiss us as if we do n't matter , you can do something symbolic that does n't really amount to much , " said a nervous Paciotto-Biggers at a May school board meeting , nine weeks into the weekly protests . " Or you can make a climate resolution that really means something , that is something that will last . The board agreed to create more curriculum around climate change and to increase recycling efforts , but it did not agree to the group 's target for reducing district greenhouse gas emissions to that of the rates that are forecast to limit global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius , according to the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change . <p> The IPCC estimates human-caused carbon dioxide emissions need to drop by 45% from 2010 levels by 2030 and drop to net zero by 2050 . This goal has become a standard for the Climate Strikers . <p> But where the strikers called for a 45% reduction of carbon dioxide emissions by 2030 , the school board resolved to only hire a consultant to calculate current carbon dioxide emissions and work to reduce emissions . <p> Around this time , the protesters started striking at the City of Iowa City offices . They met with city officials , traded a testy Twitter interaction with mayor Jim Throgmorton and on Aug. 6 , the council declared a climate emergency . <p> With the declaration , the city council amended the Climate Action and Adaptation Plan passed less than a year ago . Prior to the changes , the city 's plan called for the city to reduce carbon dioxide emission by 26% to 28% by 2025 and by 80% by 2050 . <p> Now the city aims to reduce emissions in line with the IPCC 's reduction levels . This December , the City of Iowa City released its plan to reach this new accelerated goal . <p> City officials tasked with meeting the new goals say doing so will require the city to garner community buy-in and to incentivize carbon emission reductions . <h> Interested in this topic ? Joe Bolkcom and a dozen former UI students signed onto the open letter , which also criticized UI 's impending public-private partnership to run utilities . <h> Greta Thunberg comes to Iowa City <p> The group 's biggest validation came from Greta Thunberg 's visit to Iowa City to strike with students . Paciotto-Biggers recalls the shock of seeing on Twitter that Thunberg would be joining their strike the Tuesday before the Friday strike . <p> " It was insane . It was crazy . We never thought she would actually come , " Paciotto-Biggers said . " I was really just blown away , it was like ' wow , we 're really being listened to by the rest of the world . ( Photo : Joseph Cress/Iowa City Press-Citizen ) <p> Thousands crowded into an intersection of downtown Iowa City , in the eyesight of the Old Capital on the UI campus to hear the 16-year-old Swede , who just a week earlier addressed world leaders at the United Nations . <p> Thunberg 's UN speech in late September , full of emotional condemnation , captured international attention . <p> " The university 's sustainability goals were written in 2008 -- some people in this crowd were n't even born then , " Paciotto-Biggers said from the stage downtown . " The university goals are obsolete , and the university must step up and declare a climate emergency . " <p> The silence that followed was disappointing , recalls Paciotto-Biggers . He says , along with classmates and fellow strikers Ian Martinez and Alex Howe , they did not hear from UI following the strikes . <p> " I was surprised because she 's the biggest climate activist in the world , " Howe said . " And if she was n't going to get them to do something -- it kind of let me down . " <p> In December , UI awarded international companies ENGIE and Meridiam a 50-year deal to run the University of Iowa utilities Tuesday , in a move that was celebrated by the governor and the Iowa Board of Regents . <p> In announcing the contract , university officials noted that they would be able cease burning coal by 2023 , two years ahead of the university 's goal . In explaining the partnership to regents , Harreld stressed a need for UI to reduce the use of natural gas and find more innovative ways of reducing carbon dioxide emissions beyond becoming coal-free . <p> A day ahead of regents ' vote on the deal , Harreld acknowledged a " climate crisis " in the Daily Iowan , and reiterated the need for UI to address the crisis . <p> Buy Photo <p> Iowa City and University of Iowa students participate in a " Global Climate Strike " protest , Friday , Sept. , 20 , 2019 , in Iowa City , Iowa . ( Photo : Joseph Cress/Iowa City Press-Citizen ) <p> Harreld and university officials did not mention the thousands who protested near campus in conversations with regents , but the Climate Strikers claim some credit for pressuring the university into speeding up their coal-free goal . <p> " With the university , they did n't say anything to us for three months , then just out of the blue , they declared the climate emergency , " Paciotto-Biggers said . <h> What 's next ? The Governor 's office <p> The months of consistent protesting have instilled in Paciotto-Biggers the value of persistence . <p> " I feel like we ca n't give up no matter what , " he said . " We have to keep showing up and striking , just showing up , and we have to be very persistent . That 's how we got the city council and the school board to go with us . We were just very persistent . " <p> Persistence , and some degree of antics and creative thinking . The Climate Strikers spend a fair amount of time thinking not just about the cause , but about an interesting way to demonstrate . They 've hosted NFL and Iowa Hawkeyes star-turned-solar panel entrepreneur Tim Dwight and Iowa poet laureate , Mary Swander , at strikes . In November , the group " issued a past due bill " to the University of Iowa Power Plant for failing to reach the same IPCC climate goals they convinced the City of Iowa City to strive for . They held up a fictitious invoice for a billion dollars , stamped with the words " final notice .
