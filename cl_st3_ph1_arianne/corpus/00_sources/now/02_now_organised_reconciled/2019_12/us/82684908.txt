<p> Climate change is still a hot topic as the summit sponsored by the United Nations dragged on past its final day Friday and into the weekend as delegates deadlocked over plans to fight climate change . <p> " We must show the outside world that we deliver , " pleaded Carolina Schmidt , Chile 's environment minister , who is leading the talks in Madrid . <p> Representatives are arguing over the structure of an international " carbon market " that would set prices on government-issued emissions permits -- as well as a system that would funnel funds from industrialized nations to poor countries to compensate them for climate damage . <p> But environmental activists said the agreement being hammered out does not go far enough -- and could undo promises made in the 2015 Paris climate accord , which President Trump has decided to abandon . <p> " This text is completely unacceptable , " said Jennifer Morgan of Greenpeace . " It would be a betrayal of people around the world suffering from the impacts . "
