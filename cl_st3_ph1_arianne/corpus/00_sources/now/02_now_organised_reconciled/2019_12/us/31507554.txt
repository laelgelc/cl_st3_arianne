<p> The Gulf of Mexico 's largest research ship will be named after a doctor and civil rights leader who helped desegregate Gulf Coast beaches during the 1960s . <p> Dr. Gilbert Rutledge Mason was a Biloxi , Mississippi , physician who led " wade-in " protests at the city 's beaches , nearly all of which were closed to black people . <p> " Commemorating Dr. Mason 's life and achievements will be a good reminder that this ( ship ) will not only open the waters to further discovery , but it will open opportunities to a wide range of researchers , " National Science Foundation ocean science director Terrence Quinn said in a statement . <p> The foundation announced in September it would pay for a $106 million ship to help scientists explore some of the critical issues affecting the Gulf , including climate change , hurricanes , fisheries ' health and oil pollution . <p> At 199 feet long , the ship will be the largest vessel devoted to scientific research in the Gulf region . <p> The name Research Vessel ( R/V ) Gilbert R. Mason was chosen out of more than 160 nominations from scientists and the public . A 10-person committee with members from USM and LUMCON selected the name . <p> LUMCON Executive Director Craig McClain said he hopes the ship will inspire " greater inclusivity and diversity in ocean science . " <p> Mason graduated Howard University 's medical program with honors , moved to Biloxi in 1955 and was a doctor there until 2002 . His wade-ins were nonviolent , but the response from Biloxi 's white community was n't . <p> During a 1960 protest , police watched while mobs attacked black beachgoers , including elderly people and children . The attacks continued throughout the city until the mayor imposed a curfew and ordered officers to halt the violence , according to news reports . <p> Mason and his wife , Natalie Hamlar Mason , filed one of the first school desegregation lawsuits in the Deep South . In 1964 , Biloxi 's public schools were the first in Mississippi to desegregate . <p> Gulf Island Fabrication in Houma will build the new ship , along with two others also funded by the foundation . The other , nearly identical ships will go to Oregon State University and the University of Rhode Island . <p> LUMCON scientists say there are n't enough research vessels to meet the demand . The U.S. research fleet was reduced from 27 vessels to 20 between 2005 and 2014 , and it is expected to shrink further as ships are retired . <p> Editor 's note : This story was changed Dec. 18 to correct when Mason graduated from medical school .
