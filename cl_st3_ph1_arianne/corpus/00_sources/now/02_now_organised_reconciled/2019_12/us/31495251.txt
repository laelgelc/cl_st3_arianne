This site is protected by reCAPTCHA and the Google Privacy Policy and Terms of Service apply . <h> On the Hill <p> NDAA 'S FINAL STRETCH : Top lawmakers have reached an agreement on the National Defense Authorization Act , House Armed Services Chairman Adam Smith and ranking Republican Mac Thornberry said at the Reagan forum this weekend . <p> If all goes to plan , lawmakers said , the conference report should be signed and filed today and the House should vote on a final bill Wednesday , followed " shortly thereafter " by a Senate vote . <p> A breakthrough came when negotiators reached a " tentative agreement ... to include a provision that requires 12 weeks paid parental leave for all federal employees , " a source familiar with the negotiations told our colleague Connor O'Brien . <p> The deal gives Democrats a major policy win that extends far beyond the Pentagon . It could also give many of them a reason to support the bill after some Dems panned negotiators for dropping efforts to include broad protections and cleanup measures for toxic PFAS chemicals . <p> Both Ivanka Trump and Democratic Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand hailed the move . " This means that the largest employer in the country now provides basic parental leave , a huge step , and we must work to ensure that paid leave for other family emergencies will be added in the future , " the New York Democrat said in a statement . <p> Space Force : The legislation is expected to create a military service to deal with space . Both House and Senate defense bills proposed a space service , but they differed on how to structure it and dueling proposals became a late sticking point in NDAA negotiations . <p> " There 's a lot of people who love it . There 's a lot of people who hate it , " Smith said in an interview with POLITICO at the Reagan forum . " It was a problem to navigate through all the different people who are involved in that to get to a decent outcome . " <p> Widow 's Tax and Feres Doctrine repeal ? Smith seemed to indicate that Democrats scored wins on ending the Widow 's Tax and rolling back the decades-old legal precedent that bars troops from suing for medical malpractice . <p> " We were able to get paid parental leave , take care of the widows , deal with the Feres Doctrine , " Smith said . " There 's a lot of stuff we did that I frankly thought would 've been difficult in this environment . " <p> The House version of the bill included provisions to end the Widow 's Tax and roll back the Feres Doctrine . But pressed for details , Smith would n't specify how exactly the issues shook out , saying of the bill , " We got a lot . We did n't get it all . " <p> It comes down to outside issues : While Smith said he supported attaching many provisions outside Armed Services ' jurisdiction , he 'll be more skeptical in future bills because of the way they weighed down negotiations . <p> " One of the biggest things that I learned in the process is ... I 'm going to be a lot more critical up front about anything being put on this bill that 's not within our jurisdiction , " he said . " I 'm not going to let it hang in the House . If you 're telling me that I somehow ca n't then force Jim Inhofe and Donald Trump to agree with you , you 're going to kill my entire bill . " <p> SYRIA PLAN : Defense Secretary Mark Esper and Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen.
