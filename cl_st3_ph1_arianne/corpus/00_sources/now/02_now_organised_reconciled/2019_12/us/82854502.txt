Jessica Onsurez <p> New Mexico state officials are looking for ways to curb methane emissions from oil and gas operations , intending to enact government regulations alongside industry-driven solutions . <p> The State 's Methane Advisory Panel , led by the New Mexico Environment Department ( NMED ) , and the Energy , Minerals and Natural Resources Division ( EMNRD ) , released a draft technical report which outlined methane-emitting sources in the oil and gas sector and provided potential methods to reduce such emissions . <p> The agencies are seeking public feedback on the report through Feb. 20 , 2020 . <p> The Panel was comprised of 27 members holding expertise in numerous parts of the oil and gas industry , and included input from Los Alamos National Laboratory , Colorado State University and the New Mexico Institute of Mining and Technology . <p> Membership on the Panel was made up of a combination of environmental groups such as the Sierra Club and Earthworks , along with major oil and gas companies like Devon Energy , the Chevron Corporation and XTO Energy . <p> Members met during the past four months and discussed a range of topics including controlling methane emissions , pneumatic controllers and pumps , compressors , engines , dehydration units and venting and flaring . <p> Other topics included well completions and stimulations , along with heaters and storage vessels , work-overs and liquids unloading . <p> The report recommended various strategies to reduce emissions , such as switching to low- or no-bleed pneumatic controllers and increasing leak detection and monitoring equipment . <p> The need for increased midstream infrastructure was also identified to reduce venting and flaring , although the report contended flaring , or the burning off of excess natural gas during extraction , was a necessary part of the process . <h> Interested in this topic ? " This is exactly the baseline information we need to effectively tackle methane emissions in our state . " <p> EMNRD Cabinet Secretary Sarah Cottrell Propst said the technical report was the first step in the state 's efforts to reduce carbon emissions and address global warming and climate change . <p> " The technical report produced by MAP members is a thorough document that goes a long way in informing our options on reducing methane waste , " Propst said . <p> " With the MAP meetings following our initial public engagement over the summer , EMNRD and NMED have a great technical foundation moving forward . We encourage the public 's input on this next step in our process . " <p> Staal Gjervik , president of XTO Energy -- a member of the panel -- said wide-ranging state regulations would be helpful in pushing the industry to adopt more methane-controlling policies alongside voluntary measures already taken by oil and gas companies themselves . <p> " Our experience shows that methane emissions can be mitigated in a cost-effective manner , " he said . <p> " While voluntary efforts by individual companies are important , full industry participation with the collaboration of the environmental community and the state are required to maximize the benefits to society and assist the state in the development of regulatory requirements associated with methane management . " <p> Stronger regulations for methane emissions from oil and gas were critical , said Hillary Hull of the Environmental Defense Fund , another member of the Panel , as oil and gas booms in New Mexico . <p> She commended the State for including a " broad " range of perspectives in its regulatory process . <p> " The state 's process in developing this analysis has been broad and inclusive , representing countless hours of research and input from a diverse cross-section of stakeholders including community groups , industry and environmental advocates , " Hull said . <p> " Next year will be the critical time for moving forward on enforceable , nationally-leading methane rules that protect our air , address the climate crisis and improve education funding for New Mexico families and future generations . " <p> Trade groups representing the oil and gas industries in New Mexico and Texas reported a decline in methane emissions in both states over the past seven years , as production tripled . <p> An analysis authored by Texans for Natural Gas , the Permian Basin Petroleum Association and the New Mexico Oil and Gas Association ( NMOGA ) also showed reductions in flaring per data from the World Bank . <p> Flaring and emissions intensity -- the amount of methane burned off or emitted per barrel of oil equivalent was reduced by 64 percent in the Permian Basin during the past seven years , read the report . <p> If it was considered a country on its own , the Permian Basin would rank 45th in the world in flaring intensity , read the report , behind other major oil-producing countries such as Venezuela , Iran and Russia . <h> Interested in this topic ?
