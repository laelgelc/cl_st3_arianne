<h> Hollub aims to make a killing in West Texas while cutting carbon dioxide emissions . <p> Vicki Hollub has a bold strategy to change the company she leads , Occidental Petroleum . And if it works , she just might change the world . <p> This year , she bought Anadarko Petroleum , roughly doubling the size of Occidental and becoming the largest producer in the Permian Basin . Many scoffed at the deal , including some of her key shareholders , concerned about buying assets at a time when oil prices are middling and show little sign of rising . ( Carl Icahn took his complaints to court . ) But Hollub did not back off . Instead , she found friends to help pull it off . With an investment from Warren Buffett 's Berkshire Hathaway and an agreement to sell assets to Total , Hollub put the pieces together to finish the deal . <p> In this undated file photo provided by Occidental Petroleum Corp. , Occidental President and CEO Vicki Hollub poses for a photo . ( AP ) <p> It 's a big merger and we hope Hollub 's confidence is affirmed , and not just because it would strengthen the Texas oil industry . It 's also because the key to Hollub 's business model is technology to inject carbon dioxide into oil wells to boost production , thereby sequestering the greenhouse gas underground . If her development of processes to capture the CO2 from industrial polluters and inject it into oil wells is effective , it could make reducing carbon emissions a profitable endeavor . <p> " If you look at all the models , there 's really no way to have the impact that we need to have on the environment , to reduce the volume of CO2 that we need to reduce , without having carbon capture . Renewables are n't enough to get us there , " Hollub said in April during the Columbia Global Energy Summit . <p> For her bravery and tenacity in leading the oil and gas industry and Texas in a new direction , Hollub is a finalist for 2019 Texan of the Year . <p> Hollub grew up in Alabama , studied mineral engineering at the University of Alabama , and soon joined Occidental Petroleum . In 2016 , she became the first female CEO of a major U.S. oil company . She quickly made an unusual decision . As oil prices turned south and other producers laid off employees to cut costs , Hollub instead retained her talent , affecting many employees at Occidental 's Houston headquarters . She set folks to work to find ways to reduce operating costs and boost production . <p> She also departs from her peers in her approach to climate change . She calls this the " social license to operate , " that is , the support an oil company needs within society to continue to work without obstruction . <p> " The social license to operate is the one box we check that really nobody else can check today . And that box is our ability to lead our industry into a low carbon environment . We believe that it 's our moral and social responsibility to take this lead . Because we 're the world 's largest handler of CO2 for enhanced oil recovery , " Hollub said in an investor presentation in May . <p> And if reducing carbon dioxide emissions can be profitable for one oil giant , surely carbon capture can be profitable for other producers . And if the oil industry develops this technology , other industries could make use of it in their own way , too . In that way , Vicki Hollub could make West Texas ground zero for both oil production and carbon dioxide reduction in the world . <p> Dallas Morning News Editorial .
