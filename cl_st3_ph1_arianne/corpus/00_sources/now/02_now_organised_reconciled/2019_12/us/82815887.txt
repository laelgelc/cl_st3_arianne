<p> U.S. District Judge Brian Morris expressed skepticism over government arguments that Trump has unilateral authority to approve the $8 billion pipeline . In a separate ruling , the judge said the Rosebud Sioux and Fort Belknap Indian tribes had valid claims that approval of the line violated their treaty rights . <p> But Morris denied a request from environmentalists to impose a court injunction blocking preliminary work on the pipeline , since no such work is planned until spring 2020 . <p> Morris had blocked work on the line in 2018 , prompting Trump to issue a new permit in March in an attempt to circumvent the courts . <p> The 1,200-mile pipeline would transport up to 830,000 barrels ( 35 million gallons ) of crude daily from western Canada to terminals on the Gulf Coast . <p> Opponents worry burning the tar sands oil that will be carried by the line will make climate change worse , and that it could break and spill into water bodies such as Montana 's Missouri River . <p> TC Energy of Canada first proposed the project more than a decade ago but has been unable to get past the numerous lawsuits against it . Trump has been a strong supporter and revived Keystone XL after it was rejected under President Barack Obama.
