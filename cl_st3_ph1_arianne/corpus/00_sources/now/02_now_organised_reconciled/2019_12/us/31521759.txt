The Democratic Unionist Party ( DUP ) , on whose votes the ruling Conservative Party has depended , said they would not support it . <p> Although a bill to implement the deal passed an early hurdle in parliament , lawmakers rejected the government 's fast-track timetable . EU27 countries have since approved another Brexit delay . <h> How does Boris Johnson 's divorce deal differ from Theresa May 's ? <p> Many elements of the legally-binding Withdrawal Agreement remain unchanged from the one Boris Johnson 's predecessor , Theresa May , agreed with Brussels in November 2018 following more than eighteen months of negotiations between London and Brussels . <p> The main differences are to be found on issues concerning the island of Ireland . <p> The deal that May agreed envisaged -- in the event talks on a future trade deal failed to avoid a hard border between the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland -- the introduction of a backstop or fall-back position . <p> In the absence of a free trade deal or alternative solution , that would have seen the UK remain in the EU 's customs union , and Northern Ireland would follow some of the bloc 's single market rules . <p> But Johnson 's wing of the Conservative Party opposed the backstop on the grounds it could keep the UK in the EU for years to come . <p> No north -- south border , but what about the Irish Sea ? <p> This latest deal sees Northern Ireland leave the EU 's customs union , but remain partially aligned to the single market . <p> However , it will remain in the UK 's customs territory and follow EU procedures for goods arriving into the country . <p> There will be no customs checks on the island of Ireland - they will be done in ports . <p> For goods crossing from Great Britain to Northern Ireland , that are deemed to be staying there , no EU tariff will apply . <p> No EU tariffs would be paid on personal goods carried by travellers across the Irish frontier and for a second category of exempted goods that can only be for immediate consumption rather than subsequent processing . <p> Principle of consent <p> The Northern Irish assembly will have to give consent after Brexit for the region 's continued alignment with the EU regulatory regime . <p> Four years after Brexit , the assembly will have to decide by simple majority of those taking part in the vote whether to apply it . If the vote is positive , the system is extended for another four years . <p> If another vote then is positive with cross-community support , the system is extended by another eight years until another vote . <p> If consent is not granted , there is a two-year cooling off period during which sides need to find a new solution to prevent the return of a hard border on the island of Ireland . <p> If the regional assembly does not sit or vote , the system continues as the default position . <p> Unlike the " backstop " solution in the original deal , rejected by the British parliament , this system would not be replaced by a new free-trade deal between Britain and the EU . <h> Money and rights <p> The revised deal retains the measures in the 2018 accord to settle the other two priority issues to untangle the UK 's 46-year EU membership : of the EU : the so-called " divorce bill " , and citizens ' rights -- those of EU nationals living in the UK , and Britons living on the continent . <p> It establishes a mechanism for calculating the financial settlement -- money the UK owes the EU to settle its obligations . No figure is mentioned but estimates have put it above ? 40 billion . It includes contributions to be paid during the planned transition period -- to run until the end of 2020 . <p> The EU 's Chief Brexit negotiator Michel Barnier said the transition period could be extended to last another one or two years if an agreement was reached . In such an event , more payments would be due . <p> On citizens ' rights , under the deal , EU nationals in the UK and Britons in the EU -- plus family members -- would retain residency and social security rights after Brexit . Freedom to move and live within the EU and UK would continue during the transition period . People would be allowed to stay when it ends and apply for permanent residence after five years . <p> However , the right for British citizens settled in an EU country to move freely after Brexit within the bloc -- as they currently can -- remains up in the air and subject to a possible future agreement . This concerns those who want to retain as many of the UK 's existing EU benefits as possible . <h> Legal disputes and other matters <p> Provisions for these issues are also unchanged from the original withdrawal deal . <p> Although a joint UK-EU committee and an arbitration panel would try to resolve disputes , the UK would remain under the jurisdiction of the European Court of Justice ( ECJ ) during the transition . Afterwards , its rulings would no longer have direct effect in the UK but it would retain influence . <p> One contentious issue -- that of fishing rights -- is left to be dealt with on another day . The agreement says the EU and UK should do their best to strike a separate deal on access to UK waters for EU fishing boats . <p> A protocol on Gibraltar -- the British territory on the southern tip of Spain -- seeks to ensure in particular that citizens ' rights are respected . Another , on Cyprus , aims to preserve the current situation -- keeping the British military base in the EU 's customs territory . <h> What 's in the Political Declaration on future ties ? <p> Boris Johnson also signed up to a " level playing field " with the EU over issues including state aid , competition , social and workers ' rights , the environment and climate change . <p> However , this engagement is included in the revised Political Declaration on future EU-UK relations -- which is legally non-binding -- rather than the withdrawal agreement which would have the force of an international treaty . The document provides a framework which could form the basis for a future trade agreement . <p> In the original declaration negotiated by the previous UK government , Theresa May envisaged the UK remaining closely aligned to the EU . Boris Johnson wants to diverge and pursue a much more independent trade policy . <p> The new document relates to a future EU-UK economic relationship where the UK opts for a Free Trade Agreement ( FTA ) . But in return for zero tariffs and quotas , the UK makes " robust commitments on a level playing field " to ensure " open and fair competition " . <p> The new political declaration is causing concern among some Brexit opponents -- especially given the Johnson government 's avowed intention to diverge from EU rules and forge an independent trading path . <p> Euronews is no longer accessible on Internet Explorer .
