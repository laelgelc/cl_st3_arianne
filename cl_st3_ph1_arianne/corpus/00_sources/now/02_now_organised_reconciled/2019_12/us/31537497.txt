<h> For her fifth album , jazz singer-songwriter Diane Nalini focuses on the future <p> Singer-songwriter Diane Nalini allowed a decade to pass between the release of her fourth and fifth albums -- with good reason . <p> First , there was the matter of her impressive career in the federal public service . In the last 10 years , Nalini , a Rhodes Scholar with a doctorate in applied nuclear physics , worked first on climate change policy at Environment Canada before moving most recently to Natural Resources Canada to turn her attention to nuclear energy policy . <p> It 's not as if Nalini had just set music-making aside . She 's been plenty active as the resident vocalist of the Ottawa Jazz Orchestra , and performed some of her songs in that setting . <p> Still , Nalini says : " I do n't think I expected to go so long between albums . I 've been doing a lot musically over the past few years , but never found the right time to make a new album until I finally took three months off to work on it full-time . " <p> The result of Nalini 's fall 2018 sabbatical is her album Future Perfect , a collection of 13 songs she and her band will launch Saturday during two concerts at the National Arts Centre 's Fourth Stage . <p> During the first month of her time away , Nalini took songs she had written in recent years and crafted arrangements for the band that would record them , paying particular attention to determining precise tempo markings for her material . <p> " Especially for passages where the lyrics are dense , it can really kill a tune if you count it in too fast . It wo n't breathe right , " Nalini says . " Conversely , if you count a tune in too slowly , it can become a dirge . So I do that work ahead of time so that there wo n't be false starts in the studio because of a count-in that was too fast or slow . " <p> A month into her hiatus , Nalini recorded in Montreal , where she grew up and lived before studying at Oxford University . <p> Joining her in the studio over three days were her long-time friends and collaborators Dany Roy on tenor saxophone , guitarist Mike Rud , bassist Alec Walkington and drummer Camil B ? lisle . <p> " They made everything sound even better than it could in my head ! " says Nalini . " Every one of them is so talented and yet very humble and grounded . And so musical . I love the way they listen , and when we all listen to each other and have musical conversations with space to breathe , that 's when the magic happens . " <p> All but Rud , who this year moved to B.C. , will be at the Fourth Stage . Ottawa guitarist Tim Bedner will step in . <p> After the session , Nalini spent time reviewing rough mixes and choosing the best takes . Her mixing sessions were mostly finished last December . <p> Her new album is called Future Perfect , although there is no song on the recording with that title . <p> " As I put together the list of songs that I wanted to record , I realized that every one of them dealt with the future in some way , " Nalini says . " Either they explored my hopes and dreams for the future , or concerns about the future of the planet , or a sense of nostalgia for a love that might have been in an alternate future . " <p> The new album reflects Nalini 's growth as a vocalist and songwriter . <p> " As a vocalist , I 've become more disciplined about regular practice , including working more on technique and physical endurance . This is crucial when singing with a big band , " she says . " I 've also been doing more teaching , helping singers who want to develop good jazz phrasing and who want to start improvising . This definitely builds more awareness of one 's own process for unpacking harmony . As a result , I 'm much more aware of my own instrument . " <p> The album also includes Nalini 's able scat-singing , which did not figure on her previous recordings . " On my new album , I improvised on several tracks and I had lots of fun engaging in musical conversations with the musicians , throwing melodic themes back and forth at each other , " she says . <p> As a songwriter , Nalini recently found a new tool to help her -- the ukulele . <p> " I 've fallen in love with the tenor and baritone ukulele , " she says . " After some misadventures with acoustic guitar some years ago , I was left feeling it was just too big for my hands . I still used it for composing , but it never seemed to fit . Enter the ukulele . I can noodle on it while sitting on the sofa and it 's small enough to travel with . I took it with me to an artist residency in Spain in 2017 to complete some of the songs for this album . It 's a brilliant songwriting tool . " <p> In true polymath fashion , Nalini writes and sings music in four languages -- English , French , Spanish and Portuguese . <p> " I love singing in different languages because they really bring out a completely different aspect of my personality , " she says . <p> " Each language has unique expressions that do n't have their exact equivalent in the other . The metaphors and jokes are completely different . It 's a different way of thinking . And therefore also a different way of singing . <p> " If I had to over-simplify , I 'd say Brazilian Portuguese has a soft , sensual and syncopated quality that is the perfect vehicle for bossa nova . <p> Spanish is passionate and dramatic . French is romantic and sarcastic . English is the language of Shakespeare and of jazz and blues . So taken together , that 's an emotional journey worth taking . " <p> Nalini says that while Future Perfect is " definitely a jazz album , " it covers a lot of ground with the subjects of its songs . <p> " There are love songs , songs about wonder , about the joys of little things , songs about needing solace , facing an uncertain future but staying strong . Songs about the joys of getting away from it all by unplugging , and questions about where our obsession with technology will lead the planet , " she says . <p> " I wanted to explore many topics with this album . So I hope people will enjoy going on this journey with me .
