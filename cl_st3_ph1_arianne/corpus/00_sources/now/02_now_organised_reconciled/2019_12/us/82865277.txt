If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <p> OPINION <h> Report from Iowa : How the Democrats ' Caucus campaign looks on the ground as 2020 closes <p> About this series : The Iowa Caucuses , which are the first in the country , play an outsize role in selecting the next president . University of Delaware professor David Redlawsk , an expert in the caucuses , is spending several months observing and studying them this year -- he is sharing his observations with Delaware Online readers . He also tweets regularly @DavidRedlawsk . <p> The end of 2019 is fast approaching and the pace of campaigning for president in Iowa is picking up . <p> While there have been brief breaks for Thanksgiving , and Christmas week will be quiet , there is a palpable sense that the end is near . The frontrunners -- Joe Biden , Pete Buttigieg , Bernie Sanders , and Elizabeth Warren -- are honing their final strategies for the last month of campaigning . <p> David Redlawsk ( Photo : Submitted by David Redlawsk ) <p> The candidates who might also have a viable path in Iowa -- Amy Klobuchar and Andrew Yang -- are trying to identify exactly what that path might be . <p> The rest of the remaining candidates -- Michael Bennet , Cory Booker , Julian Castro , John Delaney , Tulsi Gabbard , Tom Steyer , and Marianne Williamson -- are looking for lightning to strike , but thunderstorms during an Iowa winter are pretty rare . <h> Kamala Harris and Joe Biden redux <p> I have to start with an update . When I last reported on Iowa , Harris and Biden were both in the midst of campaign reboots . Harris had never lived up to her early-summer hype and polling bump . Biden had been drifting down in the polls , and the media was suggesting he 's just too out of touch to win over the voters he needs . As the clickbait headlines always put it , you 'll never believe what happened next . <p> Kamala Harris speaks at an event in Iowa . ( Photo : Courtesy of David Redlawsk ) <p> As December began , Harris gave up , shocking most observers . While the consensus was she was flailing , there was also some sense that if she could keep going and not be crushed in Iowa , and if Joe Biden faded badly , she might have a path with nonwhite voters in South Carolina . As I noted in November , she was building a focus there , especially on black women . <p> But it was n't to be . A devastating New York Times story about dysfunction in her campaign did n't help . So Harris was out , right after I had devoted a good portion of my November update to her efforts . The turnaround was cut short by Harris ' failure to develop a clear , compelling message , even one that connected with African American voters in South Carolina . who found her past as a prosecutor somewhat less than appealing . <p> On the other hand , the reports of Biden 's almost demise were , as he would say , malarkey ! <p> Leaning into the " out of touch " critique , Biden took an early December bus tour of more rural parts of Iowa , where his natural base of older , working class voters dominates . <p> During one of the days he rolled out the endorsement of former Secretary of State and 2004 Democratic nominee John Kerry , holding several events with Kerry . Kerry spoke at length about climate change , and about Biden 's experience in foreign policy . The latter was a key theme of the No Malarkey Bus Tour . <p> I saw two of the bus tour events , both of which drew solid crowds , many of whom are already committed to Biden . His very personal brand of politics was on display at both , as he took questions and stuck around afterward to talk to the many voters who wanted a moment with him . <p> Folks wait on rope line for Kerry &amp; Biden as they come around to shake hands . Meanwhile staff asking people to sign commit to caucus cards . **35;2418;TOOLONG <p> The media seems to find this kind of politics an anachronism , which they do n't quite know how to handle . And younger voters find it kind of odd . But Biden 's base , which seems pretty solid and is the age group most likely to show up to caucus , does seem to love it . <p> Biden 's pitch is pretty straightforward . He 's the one candidate who has been in the Oval Office , in the middle of critical decision making , and a full partner with President Obama . He believes there is still hope , but everything is at risk in 2020 . <p> The bus tour certainly got people talking , and Biden 's embrace of No Malarkey gave him a way to break through the noise . Even my University of Iowa students -- who laughed at " malarkey " -- agreed that the campaign had done a great job of turning the story on its head . <p> A recent Emerson College Poll put Biden back on top in Iowa , and while no one should put much stock in any single poll , it does suggest that Joe Biden can not be counted out yet . <h> In other news <p> Oh , Steve Bullock and Joe Sestak also dropped out . Sestak was not at all surprising , Bullock only slightly less so . <p> But Bullock must be particularly frustrated . You would think the popular Democratic governor of a deep red state ( Montana ) who pitched himself as a practical progressive who could win over Trump voters , would certainly be more interesting than a couple billionaires , an inexperienced entrepreneur running on giving everyone free money , and a host of other unknowns . But it was not to be , and Bullock is back in Montana doing whatever it is a Montana governor does . Guess we wo n't be giving Trump the boot . <p> I have n't been able to catch up with Amy Klobuchar in a long time . That same Emerson poll , however , showed her at 10% , in double digits for the first time . And right before Christmas , she too is doing an Iowa bus tour . I 'll try to give her some focus in January . <p> Pete Buttigieg continues as an Iowa top-tier candidate even as he 's had some challenges addressing attacks by Warren in particular . One thing I am watching for is the potential for Warren and Buttigieg to go negative on each other , with Sanders chiming in as well and Biden staying out of that fray . <p> If so , the attackers may suffer while those who hang back may benefit . That was one of the explanations for John Kerry 's surprise victory in 2004 , when Howard Dean and Dick Gephardt destroyed each other in the final month of the Iowa campaign . <p> Will history repeat ? Something else to keep an eye on . <h> Yang goes on his own bus tour <p> Andrew Yang kicked off his bus tour outside the Iowa State Capitol on a bitter cold afternoon . Hopefully I will get to Yang 's bus before he starts the event . Still , he made the debate stage for December , and is now getting significant media attention . <h> Castro doubles down on attacking Iowa . In Iowa . <p> As I described in November , Julian Castro has taken the unique approach of attacking the Iowa Caucus process , even while continuing to campaign there . He has gone all in on the idea that the system has to change . <p> On Dec. 10 , he did two events where I saw him give this message . One was at a Union Hall in Ottumwa , Iowa , to about 30 people . The attack did not seem to go over all that well , but Castro uses it to show he will speak truth to power . <p> Later that evening he was hosted at a Town Hall at Drake University in Des Moines focused entirely on his critique of the Iowa Caucuses . The setup was clear : about diversity and its lack in Iowa . <p> The audience of more than 100 listen politely and mostly pushed back , although some were with him . To me it was especially interesting to hear Castro liken the Iowa Caucuses to what he says is a GOP focus on voter suppression . <p> Castro says caucuses seem like something Republicans would have invented with all the access issues . <p> He 's right that later states have fewer candidates . He 's wrong that the Caucuses are in some way about voter suppression , or some kind of Republican plot . <p> And it is absolutely the case that Iowa gave Barack Obama the push that led him to the nomination . While Castro recognized that , he also discounted it , saying that 's simply not enough . <p> I do n't think he won over many converts that night . But maybe he should get credit for coming into the lion 's den to make his argument . <p> Meanwhile , the Iowa Democratic Party announced 99 " Satellite Caucus Sites " with many to be held outside of Iowa , and even overseas . This is their attempt to open up Caucus access , although it falls short of allowing people to vote who simply can not show up at a specific time in a specific place ( like absentee voting does ) . <p> Certainly this wo n't satisfy critics like Castro , but it does represent the most significant expansion of the process since the modern Caucus campaign began after 1972 . <h> Quick thoughts on Warren and Sanders <p> Warren and Sanders have both toned down their rhetoric . What do I mean ? At relatively smaller events each held the same day in Ottumwa , Warren never used the phrase " big structural change " while Sanders did not once mention a " political revolution . " <p> Both have felt pressure to moderate at least to some degree , Warren more than Sanders . Her embrace of Medicare for All , which is not popular , has caused her problems , and she has in some sense walked back the most radical aspects but proposes a transition plan that sounds a lot like what other candidates espouse . <p> Warren has also mostly rebooted her town halls . Instead of the 30-40 minutes speech followed by three questions from the audience , she has begun to give a very short speech followed by a dozen or more questions . I first saw this in November in Iowa City , where she did an excellent job of responding directly to the questioners . But in Ottumwa , it was n't as good , as she took almost every question as an opportunity to give the parts of her stump speech she did n't at the beginning . <p> Meanwhile at the Sanders rally , we started with an excellent singer/songwriter whose name I got wrong .
