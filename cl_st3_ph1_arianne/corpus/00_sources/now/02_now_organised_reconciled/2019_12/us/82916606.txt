Across the state , we conducted community conversations and collected surveys at festivals , fairs and farmers markets . <p> Four overarching themes emerged from youth perspectives : climate change , recreation , a need for inclusivity , and a willingness to pay for all three . <p> Climate change is the No. 1 concern for Montanans ages 18-29 . Young people are distraught the changing climate will have negative impacts on the places they live , work , and play . The importance of taking action on climate is not just a national issue . <p> Montana youth are also overwhelmingly passionate about this issue -- likely because they have the most to lose . Young adults who responded to the survey indicated a commitment to taking proactive steps now to stem the effects of climate change and expressed concern that disenfranchised populations are already feeling the impacts of a changing climate . <p> Young respondents also strongly identify with outdoor recreation . Recreation is the largest sector of the state 's economy and young people recognize its value to local businesses and to their connection to outside spaces . They are also concerned that many favorite outdoor spaces are being loved to death . It is evident in the responses that young people wrestle with the impacts of recreation . <p> They want public land access for all , but recognize that too much recreation can degrade the health of many favorite spaces . For solutions , they suggest increased education on leave-no-trace practices , building infrastructure in high-use areas to mitigate impacts , and increased access to public lands to spread the impact across more area . <p> We heard that Black , Indigenous , and people of color , as well as people belonging to the LGBTQ+ community and folks living with disabilities experience barriers to outdoor spaces . Thriving in the outdoors can be expensive , both in terms of needed gear and transportation . Conservation and public lands messaging often do n't prioritize or reflect the diversity of folks who use outdoor spaces . <p> Changing recreation culture , with its predominant attitude of rugged individualism and hyper performance , will require a fundamental paradigm shift - outdoor organizations , tour agencies , local gyms and other groups at every level of the recreation economy will need to adjust their programs to be inclusive and welcoming of different ways of experiencing outside spaces . <p> The survey asked respondents whether they would personally invest to help address these challenges . Young people are overwhelmingly supportive , and are open to creative taxing solutions , like backpack taxes . They recommend allocating these additional funds to education , research and climate change reduction/infrastructure projects . For young folks , this is an investment in the recreation economy and in a Montana way of life . <p> The project talked more than 1% of Montana 's population . Public land agencies , elected <p> officials and the recreation economy at large would be wise to leverage the results of this project . <p> There 's no doubt the conversations we started will last far into the future .
