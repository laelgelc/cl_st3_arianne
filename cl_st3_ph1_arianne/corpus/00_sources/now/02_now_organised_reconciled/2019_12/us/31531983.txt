<h> Front Porch : Word of the Year selections become ominous <p> Thu. , Dec. 26 , 2019 <p> The word " they " is displayed on a computer screen on Friday , Dec. 6 , 2019 , in New York . The language mavens at Merriam-Webster have declared the personal pronoun their word of the year based on a 313 percent increase in look-ups on the company 's search site , Merriam-Webster.com , this year when compared with 2018 . Merriam-Webster recently added a new definition to its online dictionary to reflect use of " they " as relating to a person whose gender identity is nonbinary . ( Jenny Kane / Associated Press ) <p> On this day after Christmas , many of us are still surrounded by out-of-town family , merriment is still abounding , after-Christmas sales are beckoning and thoughts are beginning their turn toward New Year 's Eve plans . <p> But for others of us , perhaps the nerdier in our midst , this is the time when our inner logophilia eagerly rises to the surface as we begin our annual end-of-the-year search for announcements of the various official Words of the Year ( which are sometimes phrases ) . Happily , for those of us who love words , the lists are now forthcoming . <p> Three things are clearly emerging in the 2019 Word of the Year selections that have been announced so far . First , the torch has been passed , if not wrenched , from the hands of my generation , the Baby Boomers , to everybody on the planet younger than us . Second , we are venturing into territory not explored previously , which is OK . And finally , things are getting much more ominous out there . <p> I remember when the annual word selections included such culturally sweet words as tweet , geek , selfie and binge watch . No more . Take for example , the Oxford Dictionaries ' Word of the Year 2019 : " climate emergency . " <p> Generally , words of the year are chosen based on their use in reflecting the mood and preoccupations of the year just ending , words having the potential for lasting cultural significance . The people at Oxford noted their analysis of language data showed that use of the term climate emergency was 100 times as common as it had been a year ago . As a singular word in 2018 , the word climate was n't even one of the top words used as a modifier of the word emergency , when the most prominent emergencies people wrote about were health , hospital and family . <p> Even the Guardian noted back in May that instead of using the phrase " climate change , " it chose to describe the broader impacts of climate change in terms of " climate emergency , " " crisis " or " breakdown . " Editor-in-chief Katharine Viner stated that while still wanting to ensure scientific precision , " the phrase ' climate change , ' for example , sounds rather passive and gentle when what scientists are talking about is a catastrophe for humanity . " <p> Oxford 's shortlist for possible word of the year for 2019 included climate action , climate crisis , climate denial , eco-anxiety , ecocide , flight shame ( reluctance to travel by air because of pollutants emitted by aircraft ) and net-zero , among others . Not a happy or snappy word in the group . <p> However , as perhaps the latest skirmish from the generational wars , New Zealand 's Public Address readers have selected " OK Boomer " as their 2019 Word of the Year . It may well be a get-even response by millennials , who have unkindly been termed " snowflakes " by the Boomers . Or more likely , it was a natural follow from its use in a retort in New Zealand 's Parliament this year by one of its younger members when she was interrupted by an older member as she was discussing a zero-carbon measure . <p> Merriam-Webster selected the word " they " as its choice for 2019 through their data-driven process . The word , a singular pronoun that has become popular as a self-identification by nonbinary people who identify as neither exclusively female nor male , was looked up 313 percent more in 2019 than it was last year , Merriam-Webster reported . <p> Emily Brewster , Merriam-Webster senior editor , said the increase in lookups " was so significant and sustained that it stood out from all the other top lookups when we went to analyze the data . " Other words and phrases popular with Merriam-Webster users this year were " quid pro quo " and " impeach . " <p> Dictionary.com selected the word " existential , " which is broadly a concern about existence . It was cited for its prominence in 2019 as a word and theme in discussions about climate change , gun violence and even the survival of democratic institutions . The Dictionary.com folks said it was selected because it captured " a sense of grappling with the survival -- literally and figuratively -- of our planet , our loved ones , our way of life . " <p> Yikes . <p> So as the holiday season winds down toward its last hurrah on New Year 's Eve , logophiles everywhere can only wish that when one of the last major organizations to weigh in on words of the year , the American Dialect Society , holds its annual conference Jan. 5 in New Orleans , its voters might come up with a significant and prevalent word for 2019 with a more positive ring to it . Would n't that be hopeful ? Although for 2018 , they settled on " tender-age shelter , " described as a euphemistic description of government-run detention centers for children at the U.S.-Mexico border . Frankly , the signs for 2019 are not cheerful . <p> Not that we Baby Boomers do n't care about significant and world-impacting issues , but if a survey were taken among our membership , I do think there might be some other Words of the Year choices to consider . I offer a few suggestions of my own on words and word concepts of significance for 2019 : trekking poles , grandbabies , cruises to ( anywhere ) , prescription costs ( and the corollary , managing pill containers ) , outdoor play ( with sticks , balls and imagination ) , whiney **28;9143;TOOLONG post-millennials , music with a melody , memory loss and all topics related to aches and pains . <p> It all gets very personal at some point . <p> At any rate , goodbye to 2019 and hello to 2020 . May the new year bring us all joy , peace , health and kindness . And good vocabulary .
