<h> Post <h> Cobalt Usage Issues in Scaled Battery Storage <p> Battery costs are on their way down . Bloomberg reports that battery prices have fallen 87% between to $156/kWh in 2019 . By 2023 , average prices will be close to $100/kWh , reflecting another 36% price reduction . Reduction drivers include increasing project size , electric vehicle scaling and new technologies . As batteries become cheaper , more industries are adopting electrification and battery storage . As one example , GM recently announced a $2 billion electric battery plant in Ohio , scheduled to come online in 2023 , producing 30 GWh of batteries annually . For reference , Tesla 's much-vaunted Gigafactory 1 is currently producing about around 23 GWh yearly ( as of April ) . <p> Looking ahead to the day when solar+battery and wind+battery systems account for a major portion of our electricity production , any battery storage solution must be scalable as well as economic . According to the 2018 Energy Transition Forecast , " The electrification of industry and society will , of course , increase demand for associated resources , such as aluminum and copper , as well as lithium and cobalt . Most base metals are in plentiful supply , and recent concerns over lithium reserves have faded with the discovery of more ore deposits . " ( 2018 ETO , Executive Summary ) The only natural resource that was expected to be in short supply was cobalt . <p> Cobalt serves as an important element in cathodes in all lithium-ion batteries currently in production . " Forecast cobalt demand outstrips all terrestrial reserves available by a factor of 4 . " Additionally , cobalt mining centers on the Democratic Republic of the Congo , who in 2017 mined 66% of all the world 's cobalt . Congo being the main supplier subjects cobalt mining to political instability , volatile export tariffs , and human rights violations including the use of forced child labor . <p> Projections show that cobalt use in batteries is going to jump from 50,000 tons in 2016 to more than 320,000 tons by the year 2030 . Markets have shown increased volatility recently , with the price of cobalt jumping from around $30,000 per ton at the end of 2016 to above $86,000 by 2018 , before returning nearly to their starting point currently . The return to baseline prices was caused by the announcement of supply-side additions , but can the cobalt market continue to bring new resources online indefinitely to keep up with ballooning demand ? <p> Well aware of the present and future issues with cobalt mining , battery companies have been working proactively to reduce the amount of cobalt used in their battery chemistries . This July , for example , a European project called ECO COM'BAT , developed new materials that allowed them to cut cobalt requirements by 20 percent , using a special electrode coating and a high-voltage electrolyte . Newer NMC 811 battery formulations - so-called because they require 8 parts nickel to 1 part manganese and 1 part cobalt - represent cobalt efficiency gains over older , 622 , 532 , and even 111 formulations . <p> No organization or group has been more proactive than Tesla and their battery supplier , Panasonic . Tesla 's electric vehicles use approximately 60% less cobalt than first-generation battery models . Tesla uses a battery chemistry called NCA ( nickel , cobalt , aluminum ) that is already very low in cobalt . According to their 2018 impact report , their current NCA formulation uses " substantially less cobalt than the industry-standard batteries based on a nickel-manganese-cobalt oxide chemistry . " Elon Musk , Tesla 's CEO , has tweeted they " use less than 3% cobalt ... and will use none in next generation batteries . " The details on how they plan to do this remain hard to come by : battery chemistries and manufacturing techniques are company trade secrets . <p> Despite the lofty promise , phasing cobalt out of battery packs entirely is expected to be a difficult task for Panasonic and Tesla . Cobalt 's presence in the battery cathodes increases energy density and reduces operating temperatures . Going to very-low or no cobalt formulations can be dangerous if the runaway temperature problem is not solved : the Galaxy Samsung battery fires of 2016 were caused specifically by a low-cobalt formulation . The current timing of Tesla 's next-generation battery rollout remains unclear . <p> Regardless of the exact timing or nature of the solution , however , the cobalt issue is one that affects current battery supply chains and will likely continue to do so until technological breakthroughs are achieved . Battery storage has long been regarded as the " last frontiers " of fully integrated renewable energy systems . Without them , duck curves will remain a problem and renewable energy will remain largely a curiosity . But with them , renewable energy becomes a dispatchable resource , and can begin to displace hydrocarbon power sources on a truly societal scale . <h> Thank Trevor for the Post ! <p> Energy Central contributors share their experience and insights for the benefit of other Members ( like you ) .
