<p> Bing Crosby famously sang " I 'm dreaming of a white Christmas , " but in Montreal until recently , we did n't have to dream about them . It was simply a given that we 'd usually have a white Christmas . But that 's no longer the case . <p> " I hate to spoil everyone 's white Christmases but it could be ( the end of them ) , " said Lawrence Mysak , emeritus professor of climate research at McGill University . <p> " I 'm going out to the cottage this weekend and I 'm looking forward to some skiing , but I do n't think I 'll be doing any . ... The planet is warming up and the northern latitudes are warming up more than the tropics and so it 's consistent with warmer winters . ... I 'd say we 're warming up here and we 're heading for warmer winters and green Christmases . " <p> Environment Canada meteorologist Simon Legault said that in the last 60 years , 76 per cent of the time we had white Christmases in Montreal . So three out of four Christmases over that period were snow-covered . But in the past 25 years , that statistic has dipped to 70 per cent . The amount of snow on the ground at Christmas has also decreased . <p> " So we have less snow on the ground at Christmas and we have less frequent white Christmases , " Legault said . " And if you look at the vast majority of cities in Canada , that 's what we see . Less white Christmases and less snow on ground during this period . There is a trend there . " <p> The Environment Canada definition of a white Christmas is when there is 2 cm of snow on the ground on Dec. 25 . <p> The study from apartment aggregator nestpick.com predicts that by 2050 , Montreal will only have one day in December with a snowfall of more than 0.25 centimetres . <p> And you do n't have to have a PhD in climate change to guess that the dip in the number of white Christmases here is because of global warming . In fact , that 's exactly how a prominent climate-change specialist sees it . <p> " Winters have been getting warmer and at some point that will , by definition , translate into less snowfall , " said Damon Matthews , a professor in the department of geography , planning and environment at Concordia University . <p> " I 'm pretty confident December snowfall is on the decline and will continue to do so . ... Looking forward to 2050 , it 's kind of a no-brainer that there will be less snowfall in the winter and around Christmas because the winters are going to contract and November/December and March/April on the other side are going to be the first places where we would lose snowfall . " <p> Winter is warming faster than any other season , notes Matthews , and winters have gotten warmer in Canada by about three degrees over the last 50 years , which is more than three times the global average warming rate . <p> As of Dec. 12 , Montreal had only received eight centimetres of snow this month and there is currently only 3 cm of snow on the ground , according to Environment Canada . Normally , Montreal receives an average of 49 centimetres of snow in December , but snowfall is expected to be much less than that this year . <p> Last year , snowfall was also way down in December , with only 15 cm falling on the city . December 2018 was in fact a very rainy month , with 84 millimetres of rain . The average rainfall for December in Montreal is 39 mm . So the total accumulation of precipitation for December last year was about average , but it was more rain than snow . <p> Environment Canada 's Legault said it 's possible we 'll have a white Christmas in Montreal this year . " What we have right now is going to melt during the weekend . ... ( But ) we still have two weeks to Christmas and it 's possible during those two weeks that we 'll accumulate ( some snow ) .
