It 's local news you can trust . <h> Welcomes Supreme Court Ruling - Letter <p> There was welcome news this week that the US Supreme Court has decided not to hear the Smyth land case that argued land-taking by Falmouth in the 2012 Falmouth Conservation Commission ruling that the 250 Alder Lane lot in Wild Harbour Estates , North Falmouth was unbuildable under current wetland regulations . <p> This is good news in that it negates the town having to pay a total of $1 million , but more important that it sets an important legal precedent for future suits that are likely to come as the impacts of climate change ( for example , sea level rise , more-intense storms ) become ever more imposing on coastal property and infrastructure . <p> One can have some sympathy that the Smyth family has been paying taxes for a buildable lot , however , the current owner inherited the lot . Even unbuildable the lot is assessed more than the original purchase price , and it is incumbent on Ms. Smyth and all land owners to stay abreast of the regulations and options for development that obviously evolve over time to provide public benefit and protection . <p> It 's not reasonable to expect that Falmouth , or any town , routinely inform individual owners of potential implications of changes in regulations . That falls solely on property owners to inquire of the town or hire professionals to investigate options . <p> Falmouth 's wetland regulations are important to protect critical natural resources and landforms that maintain the character and economy of the town and region , and deserve to be observed and fairly enforced .
