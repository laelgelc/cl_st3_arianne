If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <h> People in Wisconsin talked to us about the 2020 election . Here 's what we learned . <p> Civil conversations about politics in this day and age feel almost mythological . <p> But our readers did it . <p> USA TODAY NETWORK-Wisconsin spent this fall meeting with readers in coffee shops and breweries to talk about politics and the 2020 election -- one where all eyes will be on our state . People in Appleton , Green Bay and Wausau joined me ( Green Bay watchdog and politics reporter Haley BeMiller ) and my colleagues to share what issues matter most to them . <p> Our goal was simple , yet lofty : We wanted to know how we as local journalists can help you stay informed . <p> We 're not done listening , and we hope you have more to say . In the meantime , here are five takeaways from our listening sessions . <h> Health care is on everyone 's minds <p> People brought up this mammoth of an issue at nearly every event we hosted . They 're worried about health care costs , particularly if they have or know someone with preexisting conditions . They want to know what proposals like Medicare-for-all would mean for their wallets and how it would affect other insurance systems in Wisconsin if implemented . We hear you , and we 'll work to answer those questions in 2020 . <h> Facts matter <p> Social media has allowed disinformation to spread like wildfire , and people are never really sure if they can trust what politicians say . Knowing the truth is incredibly important to our readers -- and to us . We will continue to help you discern the truth through our unique fact-checking service in collaboration with the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel -- PolitiFact Wisconsin -- and ongoing coverage of election-related issues . <h> Do our votes really count ? <p> People raised concerns about the influx of money into politics , including the use of dark money that ca n't be traced . Our readers also spoke out against gerrymandering and want to see Wisconsin draw nonpartisan legislative maps . Both concerns boil down to the same problem : Many believe the political system is rigged and no longer represents their interests . <h> Climate change is a hot topic <p> Wisconsinites love the outdoors , so it 's no surprise that climate change came up frequently in our discussions . Rampant flooding in communities like Green Bay this year left people concerned about how leaders will address shifting weather patterns . Some readers question whether climate change is a real phenomenon ( scientists say yes ) and want more information about the extent of the problem . Others are in search of solutions and how they can play a role . Either way , it 'll be part of the conversation in 2020 . <h> Politics are a drag <p> We asked people how they feel about the current state of politics , and more than once , they responded with groans . Most are frustrated by extreme partisanship and the vitriol coming out of Madison and Washington , D.C. As a result , they want leaders who can compromise and work across the aisle -- not just pledge to do so on the campaign trail . <p> Thank you to everyone who joined us in Appleton , Green Bay and Wausau to make your voices heard . <p> And last , but certainly not least , thank you to the businesses and organizations that partnered with us to host these listening sessions : <p> Copper Rock Coffee , Appleton <p> Appleton Beer Factory , Appleton <p> The Attic , Green Bay <p> Badger State Brewing Company , Green Bay <p> Current Young Professionals , Green Bay <p> Whitewater Music Hall , Wausau <p> Only 11 more months until Election Day . <p> Contact Haley BeMiller at ( 920 ) 431-8212 or hbemiller@gannett.com .
