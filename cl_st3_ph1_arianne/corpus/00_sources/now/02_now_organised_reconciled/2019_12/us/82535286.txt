If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <p> ASHEVILLE - City Councilman Keith Young is proposing a local " Green New Deal , " including what he said would be the state 's largest " community " solar farm and a renewal of the 1970s " dollar-a-lot " program to help lower-income residents buy homes . <p> Young made the proposal , a mix of environmental and economic programs , in a November release to the Citizen Times and a Dec. 3 Facebook post . He said the deal was modeled on federal legislation proposed in February by lawmakers , including Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez , D-New York , to combat climate change and boost poorer Americans economic status . <p> " We need tangible efforts to strengthen our climate resilience , get more people riding the bus , and prepare families to move from a position of surviving to thriving with better jobs , better homes , and better lives , " the councilman said in a statement to the Citizen Times . <p> It 's unclear , though , what support the deal will get and what changes it would undergo if enacted . A majority of the council did n't respond or declined to comment when asked if they supported it . Young said he will likely seek the council 's backing at its annual retreat , usually held in February or March . Public feedback may lead to changes before then , he said . <p> " I hope it 's a living , breathing document . " <h> Paying for a Green New Deal <p> While some items have costs attached , the deal 's overall price is unknown . When asked if he would support it , Councilman Vijay Kapoor criticized the " lack of specificity of what it would cost . " <p> Young said after costs are calculated , the city could borrow money with different types of bonds . One type of borrowing , a general obligation bond , would rely directly on property taxpayers . <p> One proposal , making the city bus system fare free and increasing service , would be covered by $1 million in county government funds , according to a proposal by Buncombe County Board of Commissioners Brownie Newman , who is up for reelection in 2020 . It 's not clear if a majority of commissioners agree . <h> The details <p> The specific proposals : <p> Solar farm . Building the state 's biggest community solar farm , where people rent panels , and get back a credit bigger than the rental fee , Young said . <p> Workforce development . The jobs and economic mobility program , " Surviving to Thriving , " conducted with the Asheville Area Chamber of Commerce . That program would look for ways to address the county 's African-American employment gap , by helping residents find living wage jobs with potential for advancement , said Chamber Director of Industry Expansion and Workforce Development April Brown . The program is still being created with no set timeline , Brown said . <p> Affordable housing . Having the city take on the role of developer to create affordable housing . <p> Land purchasing . Restarting the " dollar-a-lot " program where income-qualified residents can buy land for $1 to build homes . The land can be used as collateral for construction loans , Young said . <p> As part of the city 's equity and inclusion initiative the deal would look to hire women- and minority-owned contractors , Young said . " Building resilient communities with robust entrepreneurship will be a next step in equity . " <h> Support , criticism <p> Julie Mayfield , besides Kapoor the only other council member to comment , said she supported the idea of a local deal , and specifically the fare-free proposal . Mayfield is the director of environmental nonprofit MountainTrue , <p> " Who can argue with a Green New Deal ? I use those exact words in my Senate campaign in talking about what we need at the state level , " said Mayfield , a Democrat running for N.C. Senate District 49 . <p> But she questioned whether Asheville would be allowed to build a community solar farm since it does n't run a utility . In a Fayetteville example cited by her and Young , that city owns the utility . <p> Young said state legislation , specifically , North Carolina General Statute 160A-312(a) allows the city to operate an electric power system . <p> Leading members of the council-appointed Sustainability Advisory Committee on the Energy and Environment , declined to comment , saying they had n't had time to review the proposal . Chair Keith McDade and Vice Chair Renee Mazurek said they " would welcome a presentation " at a SACEE meeting . <h> Candidates give input <p> Four candidates in the 2020 council race offered varying levels of approval . Young is up for reelection but has not said he will run . <p> Political organizer and assistant grocery store manager Kristen Goldsmith said she supported fare-free transit , and an urban forester and called the dollar-a-lot program promising . But Goldsmith said she wanted a more " structural " change for affordable housing by changing zoning to allow more density in areas now reserved for single-family homes . <p> " ' Upzoning ' is responsible development that would help make our communities more equitable and inclusive , " she said . " It is the opposite of gentrification , because it enables racial and socioeconomic diversity in neighborhoods . " <p> Construction manager Shane McCarthy said he also backed the bus and forester plans as well as the city acting as affordable housing developer . <p> " Private developers are simply not getting the job done when it comes to housing for our most vulnerable residents , " McCarthy said . <p> McCarthy said the dollar-a-lot program might be difficult since construction loans are " less accessible " to poor people because they have higher down payments and interest rates and quicker payback periods than mortgages . <p> Candidate Nicole Townsend , regional organizer for the social justice group Southerners on New Ground , has said also wants some type of Green New Deal . Townsend said Young 's version was not " too far off " from what she would support and that it was important to get input from researchers , local activists and people most vulnerable to climate change . <p> " I think we do need to do a race and class analysis to make sure whatever Green New Deal we put in place in Asheville will support the people who feel the impacts of climate change first .
