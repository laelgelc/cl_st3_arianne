The year 2019 will be the second or third highest year on record , a year that saw a heat wave send temperatures to 115 degrees in France and later move over the glaciers of Greenland . <p> The second half of the decade has been notably warmer than its first five years . <p> The cause -- human-caused global warming , with the worst yet to come . <p> " If we do not take urgent action now , then we are heading for a temperature increase of more than 3 degrees Celsius by the end of the century , with ever more harmful effects on human well being , " said Pettori Taalas , secretary-general of the WMO . <p> " On a day-to-day basis , the impacts of climate change play out through more extreme and abnormal weather . Heat waves and floods which used to be ' once in a century ' events are becoming more regular occurrences . <p> One of the main impacts of climate change is more erratic rainfall patterns . This poses a threat to crop yields and , combined with population increase , mean considerable security challenges for vulnerable countries in the future . " <p> By " urgent action , " Taalas added that the world needs to " rein in greenhouse gas emissions , notably from energy production , industry and transport ... and meet the targets set out in the Paris Agreement on climate change . " <p> But , reported the WMO , the Paris targets are not being met . President Trump is pulling the United States out of the 2015 global climate accord . The 45th president did comment Friday , lampooning energy-efficient light bulbs and saying it " gives you that orange look . " <p> --The world 's temperature has climbed by 1.1 degrees Celsius since the pre-industrial era of 1850-1900 . One degree Celsius equals approximately 1.8 degrees Fahrenheit . <p> --The world has endured heat waves in unexpected places , such as two that struck Europe in June and July of this year . The temperature climbed to a record 115 degrees Fahrenheit in France ( 107 degrees in Paris ) , 109 degrees in Germany , 107 degrees in Belgium , and even 102 degrees in Great Britain . <p> --Ocean heat is rising to record levels , the result of oceans absorbing the increased carbon dioxide in the atmosphere . Oceans are 26 more acidic than in the pre-industrial era . <p> --Fires are expanding , and not just in such places as Australia and California . The summer of 2019 saw millions of acres burn in the Siberian Arctic as well as in northern Canada . <p> --The ice melt continues , in both of the world 's polar regions and at a rate in Greenland not expected until a decade hence . The extent of daily Arctic sea ice in September 2019 was the second lowest on satellite record . <p> --High-intensity tropical storms struck with deadly force in 2019 , notably Hurricane Dorian in the Bahamas and Cyclone Idai off the Indian Ocean , which hit Mozambique in March . <p> " Climate related natural disasters are becoming more frequent , more deadly , more destructive , with growing human and financial costs , " United Nations Secretary General Antonio Guterres said earlier this week , opening the U.N . Climate Conference . <p> The converse is also true . The WMO 's report noted delays of seasonal rains in Southern Africa . The crisis saw Capetown in South Africa in danger of running out of water .
