First in a series looking toward Ottawa 's next decade . <p> A fresh decade means fresh ideas . Beginning today , Brigitte Pellerin features some of the local people who are innovating -- some in small ways , some in big ones , some in community-focused enterprises . Today : Aaron Thornell describes the Ottawa Renewable Energy Co-operative . <p> * <p> Not every quixotic battle has to involve tilting at windmills . Striving for visionary ideals can mean regular folks banding together to tackle a common enemy . In this case , fighting climate change by investing in small-scale , sustainable , community-owned renewable energy projects . <p> Cervantes ' most famous character would have liked the work done by the Ottawa Renewable Energy Co-operative . Founded in 2010 , OREC gives people in Eastern Ontario the opportunity to invest in projects that reduce greenhouse gas emissions , promote the democratization of energy production -- and grow their nest eggs . <p> The City of Ottawa declared a climate emergency in April 2019 , and the Ontario government plans to reduce our emissions to 30 per cent below 2005 levels by 2030 , a target in line with federal commitments . But how can we , regular folks with modest resources , do our part for the climate without feeling like Sancho Panza 's travelling companion ? <p> Few of OREC 's 775 members are wealthy . " Our average investment over the eight or nine years with OREC has been somewhere around $8,000 , " communications manager Aaron Thornell said , with the minimum investment set at $2,500 . Talk about democratizing access to the energy industry . <p> Thornell hears from a lot of people curious to known where their retirement money is going . " Especially in Canada , a lot of it is tied to the fossil fuel industry , and that 's something that does n't line up with their values , " he notes . Pooling their funds through a co-op allows small investors to participate instead in the transition towards an economy that 's green , local and sustainable . <p> OREC 's existing projects include solar installations at the Canadian Museum of Science and Technology , the Twin Elm Rugby Park in Nepean , KIN Vineyards in Carp , as well as a 500 kW system in Alfred-Plantagenet built in concert with the Keewaywin First Nation on land owned by the township . This partnership between a co-op , a municipality and a First Nation is the first of its kind in Canada . <p> This partnership between a co-op , a municipality and a First Nation is the first of its kind in Canada . <p> Maybe it 's my own idealistic side talking , but I do like individuals making efforts to help the environment , even when small efforts seem futile . I take my reusable containers to Bulk Barn to buy nuts and noddles . I carry my water bottle everywhere and refill it at the tap or fountain . I estimate I save maybe two or three plastic containers a week . It 's not much , I agree . But if we all make small efforts to be more sustainable , collectively we 'll see a big difference . <p> When lots of people invest modest sums of money to put a solar energy project in Alfred or on the roof of their local school , it does n't cut emissions enough to hit official targets . But it all adds up . And it helps create a culture that cares . <p> And , as Thornell points out , there 's the added benefit for investors of being able to see their money at work right there near their own backyards . Projects feature a real-time meter showing how much energy is produced , how much CO2 is being saved , and how much revenue is generated from selling that energy into the grid . " That 's a sense of pride that people feel and they can rest easy knowing those projects are where their investment dollars are placed . " <p> This shift towards buying local , be it food or clothing or energy , is something I expect will continue into the next decade , especially as millennials start getting serious about saving for their retirement . <h> Related <p> It 's not just solar panels , by the way . Last year the people behind OREC founded a sister co-op , Co-energy to invest in energy conservation projects such as retrofits . " Five to 10 years down the road , there will be a big focus on that , " Thornell said . <p> Transitioning away from fossil fuels sometimes feels like a battle we ca n't win . Certainly not on our own . But at the same time , it requires everyone to do their part . Investing in a co-op may not be your weapon of choice . That 's OK ; as long as we 're all doing something , we 'll get somewhere . <p> It may sound quixotic , but that 's the only way it 'll work . <p> Brigitte Pellerin is an Ottawa writer . <h> Ottawa Renewable Energy Co-operative , at a glance <p> * Founded in 2010 <p> * 775 members in Eastern Ontario <p> * Goal : 5,000 members by 2020 <p> Mission : <p> * To generate decentralized renewable energy in Eastern Ontario <p> * To create a democratic , self-reliant , environmentally , socially and financially sustainable co-operative business model for community power in Eastern Ontario <p> * To increase accessibility of investment options and awareness of renewable energy production and energy efficiency <h> This Week 's Flyers <h> Comments <p> Postmedia is committed to maintaining a lively but civil forum for discussion and encourage all readers to share their views on our articles .
