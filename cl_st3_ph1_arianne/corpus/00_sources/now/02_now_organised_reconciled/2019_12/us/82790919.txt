The Department of Natural Resources has produced the first Wisconsin ruffed grouse management plan ; it covers 2020-30 . ( Photo : Paul A. Smith ) <p> For the first time , Wisconsin has a formal management plan for its unofficial state game bird , the ruffed grouse . <p> The document , produced over the last year by the Department of Natural Resources and stakeholders , was approved Dec. 12 by the Natural Resources Board . It lays out a broad series of goals and objectives for the treasured , native bird . <p> In the 1940s Aldo Leopold wrote : " There are two kinds of hunting : ordinary hunting and ruffed grouse hunting . " <p> Affection for the upland bird remains high among a segment of the public , but concerns have risen as the state 's ruffed grouse population has declined over recent decades , mostly in relation to a drop in acreage of its required young-forest habitat . <p> Gary Zimmer of Rhinelander follows Scout , his Brittany spaniel , through an aspen stand near Rhinelander on a hunt for ruffed grouse and American woodcock . ( Photo : Paul A. Smith ) <p> But other forces , such as diseases and climate change could also be driving down grouse numbers , according to leading researchers . <p> So following an unexpected population decline in 2017-18 , the NRB asked the DNR to come up with a management plan . <p> The agency put together a team of stakeholders to produce the document , including representation of the Great Lakes Indian Fish and Wildlife Commission , Ruffed Grouse Society , U.S. Forest Service , Wisconsin Conservation Congress , Wisconsin County Forests Association and Wisconsin Wildlife Federation . <p> It 's long been known the primary factor driving ruffed grouse populations is the availability of young forest habitat that meets the needs for the various stages of the bird 's life cycle . <p> The document details a decades-long decline in young forest habitat , with the greatest loss occurring in southwestern and central portions of the state . The state has lost more than 1.3 million acres of forest that 's under 20 years old since 1983 , according to the DNR . <p> A male ruffed grouse in Crawford County . ( Photo : Paul A. Smith ) <p> The plan establishes three ruffed grouse priority regions ( northern forest , central forest and Driftless Area ) where the DNR will work with partners to focus on habitat projects . <p> " This is where we 'll get the most bang for the buck , " Witecha said . <p> The plan maintains two ruffed grouse management zones but shifts the boundary . The new alignment places about the northern two-thirds of the state in Zone A , where the best grouse habitat is found , and the southern one-third in Zone B. <p> The zones better reflect the current status of ruffed grouse in the state , Witecha said . <p> It also shortens the hunting season in Zone A , a move supported by 67% of the respondents in a recent hunter survey . Zone A will have a five bird daily bag limit and run from mid-September to early January . <p> Zone B will have a season from mid-October to early December with a three bird bag limit . <p> The plan also calls for continued ruffed grouse population and disease monitoring . <p> Concerns have arisen in recent years about the potential impact on ruffed grouse of mosquito-borne diseases such as West Nile Virus and Eastern Equine Encephalitis . <p> A 2018 study found 29% of grouse sampled in Wisconsin had been exposed to WNV . Eastern Equine Encephalitis has also been found in grouse in Wisconsin , Minnesota and Michigan . <p> Gary Zimmer , a former NRB member and Ruffed Grouse Society biologist who now works for the Wisconsin County Forests Association , said the plan was a good start . <p> To be successful , it will need support from the forest industry in Wisconsin . <p> " While we emphasize that habitat management for this species is crucial -- which it is , no doubt about it -- little of that can be accomplished without a strong , vibrant forest industry , " Zimmer said . <p> The board approved the plan on a unanimous vote . <p> In separate action at the meeting , the board also approved a final rule to shorten the 2019-20 ruffed grouse season in Zone A. The season had been slated to end Jan. 31 , but under the emergency rule it will close Jan. 5.
