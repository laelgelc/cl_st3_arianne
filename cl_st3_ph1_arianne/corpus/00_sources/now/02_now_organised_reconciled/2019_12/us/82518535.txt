<p> OTTAWA -- Cutting taxes for middle-income earners will be the first policy priority for re-elected Canadian Prime Minister Justin Trudeau , who laid out his plans Thursday as he embarked on his second mandate , this time as the head of a minority government . <p> Mr. Trudeau also vowed to be more aggressive on environmental policy , saying a majority of Canadians voted in October for parties that advocated such an approach . The Liberal government would pursue that course while offering unwavering support for the country 's resource-rich regions , he added . <p> The broad outlines of Mr. Trudeau 's agenda were contained in a Speech from the Throne , a ceremonial event that marks the beginning of each new parliamentary session in Canada . The speech is typically written by the prime minister 's closest advisers but delivered by Canada 's governor-general , the official representative for the country 's head of state , Britain 's Queen Elizabeth II . <p> The speech , which contained no policy specifics , also highlighted other promises the Liberals made during the recent national election campaign , such as a ban on military-style assault rifles and a national plan to cover the cost of prescription drugs . <p> Mr. Trudeau speaking in the House of Commons in Ottawa on Thursday . Photo : Adrian Wyld/The Canadian Press/Associated Press <p> As leader of a minority government , Mr. Trudeau will need the support of another party to get legislation passed and to stay in power . Canadian minority governments tend to have a lifespan of 18 months to two years , although some have lasted longer . That being said , Mr. Trudeau and the Liberals will likely have some leeway because of the weakened state of other political parties in Parliament . <p> The main opposition Conservative Party could find itself in the throes of a leadership fight within months . Other parties , such as the left-leaning New Democratic Party , lack the financial wherewithal to fight another election anytime soon . " This will be about as stable as a minority gets , " said Yaroslav Baran , a principal at Ottawa lobbying firm Earnscliffe Strategy Group who served as a senior aide in a former Conservative administration . <p> Mr. Trudeau 's policies and pertinent legislation will be crafted with an eye toward getting the support of at least one other party , political analysts say . Under the legislature 's current configuration , the Liberals will require 13 additional votes to get bills passed . Canada 's lower house has 338 members . <p> The pledge on tax relief could be delivered shortly , perhaps by January . During the campaign , Mr. Trudeau promised to deliver 5.6 billion Canadian dollars ( $4.2 billion ) in breaks to households by increasing the amount of annual income exempt from tax . <p> " As its first act , the government will cut taxes for all but the wealthiest Canadians , giving more money to middle-class families and those who need it most , " the government said . <p> Since his Oct. 21 election win , Mr. Trudeau has limited his public appearances and focused instead on meeting rival politicians and regional leaders to find common ground on policy matters . He was thrust back into the national and global spotlight this week , however , after a video caught him commiserating with other world leaders about President Trump at a reception on the sidelines of a NATO meeting in Watford , England . Mr. Trump later called Mr. Trudeau " two-faced " for talking about him and his impromptu news conferences in his absence , although also adding he considered the Canadian leader a " nice guy . " <p> Mr. Trudeau introduced his cabinet last month , naming Chrystia Freeland , widely considered to have delivered a strong performance as foreign minister in his last government , as deputy prime minister . He also put her in charge of quelling regional tensions over resource development that have erupted since Mr. Trudeau 's re-election . Officials in energy-rich provinces such as Alberta and Saskatchewan have expressed concern that the more aggressive environmental policy the Liberals promise could choke off their livelihood . <p> In the throne speech , the Liberal government said it would take strong action to fight climate change , while at the same time " offer unwavering support to the hardworking women and men in Canada 's natural resources sectors .
