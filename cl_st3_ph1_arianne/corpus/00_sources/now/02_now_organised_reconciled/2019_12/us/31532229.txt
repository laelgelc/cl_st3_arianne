<h> A generation of ' do-gooders ' : Meet millennial volunteers and the causes they give their time to in Connecticut <p> By Nancy Weil Gottfried <p> Hartford Courant <p> Dec 26 , 2019 6:00 AM <p> Jeff Murray , 25 , of Newington volunteers at the Noah Webster House in West Hartford . ( Kassi Jackson / Hartford Courant ) <p> When Justin Palatsky was relocating to Connecticut , he made it a point to find an apartment close to an animal shelter . As a pet lover , he wanted to continue to donate his time to animal causes , just as he had done for the past seven years . <p> Living in Farmington made it easy for the 29-year-old to volunteer at the Connecticut Humane Society . He became involved with animal enrichment programs and a member of the Fur Squad , a group that helps cats and other small animals with behavioral concerns , such as fear and isolation , and various health issues . <p> " Before I volunteered I donated money , but you do n't feel like you 're doing anything by sending dollars across the internet , " Palatsky said . " I love watching animals and how they progress and benefit from being socialized . " <p> Sixty four percent of millennials volunteer locally , the charitable giving statistics say , and the most popular charities across all age groups are environmental and animal nonprofit organizations . <p> So while Palatsky gives to cats , Kevin Frisch traces bats . <p> Frisch , 27 , has been a volunteer " bat man " for the past two years at the state Department of Energy and Environmental Protection . Unlike the superhero , Frisch does n't fight crime . Instead , he traces bats along a preset route from Tolland through Andover to Amston for research . He leaves his home in West Hartford driving at 20 mph for a three-hour trek , while recording bat calls from a computer set up in his car . <p> Kevin Frisch , 27 , of West Hartford , is a volunteer " bat man " with the state Department of Energy and Environmental Protection . <p> " I volunteer at DEEP because I want to give something back to nature , " Frisch said . " I think my favorite part is knowing I 'm helping keep tabs on a pretty vital member of the food web . Bats are an amazing source of pest control when it comes to mosquito-borne diseases , and this seemed like a cool way to help collect more information on them . " <p> Frisch said he thinks volunteering may help shine a light on the value of bats in our ecosystem . <p> " I think humans have a lot of misplaced fear regarding certain animals , bats chief among them , because they do n't appreciate the services they provide , " he said . " If you ca n't change people 's minds , then you can at least try and help the thing that is being effected by their misconceptions . " <p> As an ambassador for the Hartford Marathon Foundation , Lisa Wawrzynowski of West Hartford volunteers for between three and five races a season . Her responsibilities include course monitoring , finish line duties , helping with warmups and training runs , and organizing large events . <p> Lisa Wawrzynowski , 26 , of West Hartford , is an ambassador for the Hartford Marathon Foundation . Here she 's seen volunteering at the Mitten Run in West Hartford 's Blue Back Square , with her dog . <p> " At HMF it is easy to get involved and stay involved ; they make sure to put you to work and it aligns with my interest in fitness , " the 26-year-old said . " There are ways to volunteer and still get to run races in the same day , which is great . " <p> Wawrzynowski said volunteering also provides a great social outlet . <p> " I have made a lot of friends and look forward to volunteering and seeing them at the events , " she said . " Overall it 's a great atmosphere and a rewarding experience . " <p> Jeff Murray of Newington is delving into his passion for history by volunteering at the Noah Webster House in West Hartford . <p> Advertisement <p> " I graduated from UConn with a degree in molecular and cell biology , " says Murray , who is now an invoicing analyst at Pratt &amp; Whitney . The 25-year-old has been conducting research on West Hartford for the past three and a half years . <p> Jeff Murray , 25 , of Newington volunteers at the Noah Webster House in West Hartford . ( Kassi Jackson / Hartford Courant ) <p> " I 'm a big fan of finding pieces to the puzzle , which allows me to form new questions and stories and put it all together , " he said . <p> With the demands that come along with a full-time job , Murray logs most of his volunteer hours at home , using his computer to search newspaper archives and other online sources . The biggest challenge , he says , is finding a gap in a busy schedule . <p> " I can find the time after work or on weekends , " he said . Yet despite the time issue , the most important thing to Murray is giving back : " It is rewarding to see so many diverse people unified for a common interest . " <p> People have reasons for volunteering that range from a desire to further a social cause , expand professional skills , connect to people with shared interests , or , like Murray , simply give back . Many volunteer opportunities originate at work , with companies supporting a wide range of causes . Like generations before them , millennials seem to find the greatest benefit in supporting causes that are personally meaningful . The Millennial Impact Report says 29% of millennial workers said they volunteered because " I was passionate or interested in the cause . " <p> Kevin Ehrlich , 31 , has been on the board of directors at Camp Courant since 2013 . He donates a few hours of his time every week throughout the year -- planning , recruiting , organizing , even performing manual labor -- to get Hartford 's free summer program up and running for children who might not otherwise have an opportunity to attend camp . <p> Kevin Ehrlich , 31 , of East Hartford , has been on the board of directors at Camp Courant since 2013 . <p> " I 've been very fortunate throughout my life -- with a roof over my head , clothes to wear , food on the table , and a good education , " the East Hartford resident said . " Many people do n't have those basic things , so I like to do what I can to make other people 's lives better . " <p> Ehrlich , who started volunteering at a young age , says it is important to set priorities . He considers himself fortunate that Pratt &amp; Whitney has a flexible work policy and is a huge proponent of giving back to the community . " Pay it forward , " he says . <p> According to Peter Levine , associate dean of research in public service at Tufts University , 30% of high schools in the U.S. today have service learning programs that did not exist in the 1980s , making the concept of giving familiar to a younger generation at an impressionable time in their lives . <p> " It is imperative to get the millennial generation involved across the entire nonprofit sector , " says McKinley Albert , executive director of Camp Courant . " We are seeing many experienced , dedicated , senior level leaders retiring , many of whom are going to be replaced by millennials . <p> " We love having volunteers of all ages and backgrounds , but our young campers really seem to gravitate toward our younger volunteers because they fully immerse themselves in the camp experience , whether it 's playing a tough game of basketball , throwing around a football or dancing with our campers on a rainy day . I think Camp Courant is a special place for all of our volunteers . " <p> Advertisement <p> Approximately 16% of volunteers at the Connecticut Humane Society are between the ages of 25 and 44 , according to Executive Director Gordon Willard . <p> " Much of our millennial crowd comes to us through their companies and are more likely to help out in our foster program , as well as our ambassador program , " he said . " With 11,000 to 12,000 animals being touched annually , we welcome these passionate volunteers who are willing to help , mostly on weekends , after working full-time jobs all week . " <p> It can be a difficult balance for millennials to find time to volunteer , while juggling active lives and long hours , yet these volunteers are committed at the holiday season and all yearlong . For millennials , giving time is practically more valuable than giving dollars . <p> " If I had more time there would be more time to volunteer , " Wawrzynowski said . With a full-time job at Aetna ( now CVS ) and a puppy at home , giving is her passion , she says . <p> " I volunteer because I love giving back and helping out , " Wawrzynowski said . " I 've always worked hard to get where I am in life and appreciated all those who helped me along my journey or were course monitors at my races . I want to be that person for somebody . " <p> Palatsky says : " The hope is that each new generation gives back more than the last . We 've seen the fire in the hearts of a new generation in the wake of school shootings and government inaction on climate change and environmental disasters , so that keeps the hope . " <p> Ehrlich thinks his peers are more visible and have a greater ability to spread the word about cause work than previous generations : <p> " I do n't know if millennials volunteer any more than previous generations , but we 're definitely more vocal about it . In this age of digital media , people post about every aspect of their lives . Naturally , that includes volunteering as well . " <p> This story was written for The Thread , The Courant 's news source for Connecticut millennials , created by millennials.
