<h> A divided country hardens its stances <p> A divided country grew more divided in 2019 , as political warfare escalated , first over the release of a report on Russian interference in the 2016 election and then over impeachment hearings into whether President Trump pressured Ukraine to investigate his political rivals . <p> Rancor spread beyond Washington to the nation 's southern border , where the immigration debate intensified as migrants fleeing Central America were caught up in the president 's crackdown on illegal immigration and demands for a border wall . The political struggle over the wall led to the longest government shutdown in U.S. history . <p> Unified by their loathing of Trump , Democrats were fractured themselves as more than two dozen battled for their party 's 2020 presidential nomination . The issue will be settled by rapidly approaching Democratic primaries . <p> Division spread beyond the Atlantic and Pacific oceans , as the president 's trade war with China dented the economies of both countries , as well as the already slumping economies of Germany , France and Japan . China faced more turmoil as its bid for tighter control over Hong Kong sparked furious protests in the economically vital city . <p> Trump 's decision to withdraw U.S. forces from Syria over the objection of top military advisers drew bipartisan opposition in Congress to a move that threw the region into chaos and unraveled U.S. policy in the Mideast . The withdrawal overshadowed a daring U.S. commando raid that killed Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi , leader of the vicious Islamic State . <p> Mass shootings continued to plague the country and the world . Wildfires remained a constant threat in California , where power companies were forced to shut off electricity in an often futile attempt to prevent the fires , while debate raged over who is to blame . <p> A fire heavily damaged the iconic Notre Dame Cathedral in Paris , shocking the world , while the crash of two Boeing 737 Max jetliners called into question U.S. aviation safety practices . <p> Here are the top 10 national and international news stories of 2019 : <h> Constitutional clash <p> As the year began , the words " Mueller " and " Russia " dominated the headlines . As it ended , " impeachment " and " quid pro quo " took their place as controversy further engulfed the Trump administration . <p> In March , special counsel Robert Mueller submitted his report on Russian interference in the 2016 U.S. presidential election and allegations of coordination between Trump 's campaign and the Russians . Trump claimed exoneration as the report did not find sufficient evidence that his campaign " coordinated or conspired " with the Russian government as it interfered in the election . <p> Democrats howled as the report also called Russian interference -- mainly through hacking and fake social media stories -- illegal , " sweeping and systematic " yet welcomed by Trump 's campaign . It identified links between Trump officials and individuals with ties to the Russian government about which persons involved in the campaign lied or obstructed investigators . <p> Demands by leftist Democrats for Trump 's impeachment soon faded -- until reports of a July 25 call between Trump and Ukraine 's new president added more fuel to the impeachment fires . <p> Weeks of hearings by congressional Intelligence and Judicial committees culminated in partisan House votes on Dec. 18 to impeach Trump on two articles charging abuse of power and obstruction of Congress . Democrats who control the House accused Trump of pressuring Ukraine for political gain in a " quid pro quo " for the release of vitally needed military aid and blocking the House 's attempt to investigate . The move makes Trump only the fourth president in U.S. history to be brought to the brink of removal from office . <p> Political passions will flare early next year as Trump stands trial in the Senate . The impeachment effort will face an uphill battle in the Republican-led Senate , where a two-thirds vote would be needed to convict Trump and oust him from office . Analysts consider that an unlikely scenario in an election year . <h> Immigration <p> A U.S. Border Patrol agent stands near a section of privately built border wall under construction on Dec. 11 near Mission , Texas . ( John Moore/Getty Images ) <p> As he as done since taking office , Trump pressed efforts to curb legal and illegal immigration through executive orders and construction of 450 miles of barriers at the southwestern border . He also sought to cut down on the number of people who claim asylum in the U.S. Images of detained migrant children locked in cells at border stations galvanized opposition to Trump 's policies . <p> In September , the Supreme Court backed Trump , saying that the administration could block most Central American migrants from seeking asylum in the U.S. if they passed through another country and were not denied asylum there . That followed the court 's July ruling that the Trump administration could use $2.5 billion in Pentagon funds -- meant to combat drug trafficking -- to build a barrier along the border . <p> But in November , the Supreme Court said it would review a controversial Trump policy , blocked by lower courts , to end the Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals program started by President Obama in 2012 . DACA aims to halt the deportation of young immigrants brought to the U.S. illegally . <p> Trump 's immigration policies also ran into legal roadblocks thrown up by Democratic attorneys general nationwide . In October , judges in four states barred his administration from trying to withhold green cards from people using public benefits and rejected his plan to use other funds to build the border wall . <p> The immigration war led to the longest government shutdown in U.S. history , from Dec. 22 , 2018 , to Jan. 25 , 2019 . The 35-day shutdown stemmed from an impasse over Trump 's demand in an appropriations bill for $5.7 billion to build the border wall . It ended when Trump agreed to a stopgap bill that did n't include funds for the wall . He later declared a national emergency to bypass Congress and fund the wall . <h> Dueling Democrats <p> As of mid-December , Democrats had reduced their field of major presidential contenders to 15 -- but not the intensity of the combat that raged nearly all year . <p> Former New York City Mayor Michael Bloomberg jumped into the race on Nov. 24 . Once seen as a strong contender , former Rep. Beto O'Rourke dropped out in early November , while another leading candidate , Sen. Kamala Harris , quit Dec. 3 . <p> Bloomberg bills himself as a pragmatic Democrat who can beat Trump . But his late entry roiled the primary field as moderate contenders like former Vice President Joe Biden and South Bend , Ind. , Mayor Pete Buttigieg dueled with progressives like Sens . Elizabeth Warren and Bernie Sanders on the campaign trail and in televised debates . Sen. Amy Klobuchar moved to the center but remained in the back of the pack with Sen. Cory Booker and others . <p> Not wanting to be overshadowed by the House impeachment hearings , the Democratic contenders focused mainly on health care . Progressives embraced a single-payer , Medicare-for-All health care program , while moderates argued for improving the current system based on the Affordable Care Act and private insurance . Early polling in the bellwether states of Iowa and New Hampshire favored moderate contenders . <h> Trade battles <p> Investors and manufacturers had been hoping for a resolution to the U.S.-China trade war by early 2019 , but as the year drew to a close , they had to settle for a " phase one deal " in which China agreed to increase some imports in exchange for the U.S. delaying an increase in tariffs on Chinese goods . Markets rebounded at the end of the year , even though the deal did n't address the major grievances the U.S. and other countries have with China : theft of technology and unfair trade practices . <p> Analysts feared that any short-term deal would be political window dressing with little economic impact , and that in the long term , the U.S.-China trade war may be unresolvable , further damaging U.S. manufacturers . <p> As Trump was pushing for a China trade deal , the House on Dec. 19 passed the U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement , signed a year ago by Trump and the leaders of Canada and Mexico to replace the Clinton-era North American Free Trade Agreement . The USMCA deal modernizes trade rules between the three countries and includes tougher labor and environmental standards sought by Democrats . It was expected to boost manufacturing in all three countries . The Senate was expected to take up the agreement in January . <h> Hong Kong protests <p> A proposal in June by Hong Kong 's government to allow extraditions to mainland China -- seen as a bid by Beijing to limit the city 's freedoms and autonomy -- has triggered continued protests and created another flashpoint between the U.S. and China . <p> Hong Kong 's government has withdrawn the proposal , but protesters still demand an investigation of harsh police tactics and pro-democracy reforms . Some have called for an end to the Chinese Communist Party , outraging Beijing . After a bipartisan push in Congress , Trump on Nov. 27 signed a bill designed to support the pro-democracy protesters . <p> China views the protests as a U.S. attempt to foment " revolution " in Hong Kong . The conflict threatens to increase not only economic but military tensions between the countries if China decides to challenge the U.S. Navy in the South China Sea . <h> Disarray in Mideast <p> Despite the killing of brutal ISIS leader Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi in a daring raid by U.S. special forces in northern Syria , doubts grew over U.S. policy and alliances in the Mideast . <p> On Oct. 27 , Trump announced that Baghdadi had blown himself up during the raid . Any political gain for the president , however , evaporated amid questions about Trump 's Oct. 6 order to withdraw all U.S. forces from Syria , where they had been supporting the fight against Islamic militants . <p> Trump ordered the pullout against the advice of top military advisers , one of whom he called " the world 's most overrated general . " Their prediction of ensuing chaos in the region was fulfilled when Turkey quickly launched a ferocious attack on Kurds , U.S. allies in the region . Russia boosted its forces in Syria , bolstering its ally -- dictator Bashar Assad -- and occupied U.S. bases there . <p> In November , Trump partially reversed his pullout order , saying the U.S. would keep 500 troops in the country and was committed to defeating ISIS . But allies in the region , like Israel , worried that Trump might again change Mideast policy without any consultation . <h> More mass shootings <p> A father hugs his daughter after being reunited after a shooting at Saugus High School that left two students dead and three wounded on Nov. 14 , 2019 . ( Mario Tama/Getty Images ) <p> Mass shootings have occurred across the country with increasing frequency in recent years , and 2019 was no exception . <p> On Nov. 14 , a student in Santa Clarita , Calif. , fatally shot two high school classmates before taking his own life . It was the fifth mass shooting at a school or school event this year . Overall , 404 mass shootings were cataloged by the Gun Violence Archive in 2019 , as of Monday . <p> After last summer 's back-to-back shootings in El Paso , Texas , and Dayton , Ohio , left 32 people dead , Trump vowed to take action but backed down under pressure from the National Rifle Association . Instead , the administration backed legislation to tackle gun violence by helping law enforcement partnerships to better use existing gun laws . <p> Firearms businesses faced growing pressure to curb gun violence . In October , Dick 's Sporting Goods destroyed more than $5 million worth of semi-automatic rifles and said it was considering ending gun sales . In September , Walmart said it would stop selling ammunition that could be used in assault rifles and Colt , the gunmaker , said it would effectively suspend production of sporting rifles for the civilian market . <p> In a potentially significant case , the Supreme Court in November allowed relatives of shooting victims at the Sandy Hook Elementary School to sue the Remington Arms Co. , the maker of the rifle used in that 2012 massacre . <p> American-style mass shootings spread to the rest of world . On March 15 , a racist gunman killed 51 people at two mosques in Christchurch , New Zealand , prompting a swift ban on assault rifles there . <h> California wildfires <p> Political conflict flared as wildfires , blamed on climate change , again raged across California this fall . The fires , which have killed 85 people and caused billions of dollars in damage over the past three years , this year have consumed more than 281,000 acres and forced thousands of Californians to flee their homes . <p> Although less destructive and deadly than the past two years , the fires still had a big impact . Planned blackouts , meant to prevent wildfires , also kept up to 3 million Californians without power in November and risked the health of many needing electricity for vital medical devices . <p> As he did after wildfires in 2018 , Trump blasted the state 's readiness to confront fires . Conservatives blamed environmentalists for the buildup of small trees and brush that fueled the blazes , while liberals pointed to fossil fuels , corporate ineptitude -- particularly by the bankrupt Pacific Gas &amp; Electric Co. -- and suburban sprawl into areas vulnerable to wildfires. <h> 737 Max crashes <p> Boeing 's top-selling 737 Max jetliner remains grounded after crashes in October 2018 and last March killed 346 people in the Java Sea and Ethiopia . The crashes were blamed on malfunctioning anti-stall software and poor pilot training , prompting questions about the Federal Aviation Administration 's process for certifying aircraft and about whether Boeing rushed production to meet orders for 5,000 of the jets . <p> The grounding has disrupted the aviation industry worldwide and plunged Boeing into the biggest crisis it has ever faced . The largest manufacturing exporter in the U.S. , Boeing has lost 15% of its stock value , and its CEO was ousted on Monday . Even after months of work by Boeing and federal aviation regulators , the 737 Max will remain grounded at least until late January and probably longer . <p> Last week , Boeing announced it will shut down Max production in January , resulting in sweeping job losses at Boeing and its suppliers . <h> Notre Dame fire <p> Smoke and flames rise during a fire at the landmark Notre-Dame Cathedral in central Paris on April 15 , 2019 . ( FRANCOIS GUILLOT/AFP via Getty Images ) <p> Flames engulfed Notre Dame cathedral in Paris on April 15 when a fire broke out beneath the historic structure 's roof . By the time it was put out , the cathedral 's spire and most of its roof had been destroyed and its upper walls heavily damaged . However , the three iconic 13th-century rose windows suffered little or no damage . Still , authorities said , the cathedral came close to collapsing . <p> Months after the fire spewed 460 tons of lead from Notre Dame 's ancient roof and spire across Paris , alarming amounts of the toxic metal -- up to 20 times safe levels on the left bank of the Seine -- were found despite decontamination efforts . French authorities were accused of covering up or neglecting the problem , and city fire officials were criticized for poor response times and communications in fighting the blaze . <p> Because of the damage , no Christmas services were held at the cathedral this year for the first time in nearly two centuries .
