<h> Letters to the Editor <h> More than a century of Independent Journalism <h> Letters to the Editor <h> Campaign spending excessive <p> Published December 09. 2019 12:01AM <p> According to the campaign finance watchdog , opensecrets.org , $6.5 billion was spent during the 2016 presidential and congressional campaigns . With that money , every teacher in the United States could get a $2,000 raise . <p> Ironically , the amount of money spent is in sharp contrast with how much we like our candidates . Congressional candidates spent $4 billion in 2016 and yet less than a quarter of the population thinks they are doing their jobs , according to Gallup . <p> But the greater danger is corruption and control of elected officials by the rich . Is anyone naive enough to think that when a large corporation donates several million dollars to a candidate that there will not be anything requested in return ? When big coal and oil give unrestricted quantities of money to candidates , it is clear how that candidate will vote concerning anything to do with climate change issues . <p> What should be done ? The disastrous Citizens United decision needs to be overturned . All donations need to be public . <p> Campaign spending also needs to be limited . Maybe if our politicians spent less money for their campaigns , we would like them more . <p> If nothing is changed , it is almost certain that well over $6.5 billion will be spent in 2020 . <p> Robert J.
