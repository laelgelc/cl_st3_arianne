( Staff photo by Nicolaus Czarnecki/MediaNews Group/Boston Herald ) <p> Nearly 30 climate protesters were arrested for refusing to leave the State House at closing time Friday , police said , after news reporters and photographers were ordered out of the building . <p> Some protesters involved in a climate change demonstration told State Police that they " intended to be arrested . " The group said that they would " refuse to comply " with orders from law enforcement to leave the building when the State House closed at 5 p.m. , according to police , after hours of chanting throughout the halls . <p> Just before police handcuffed the 27 protesters who refused to leave , a Herald photographer and other members of the media were ordered out the building . Gov . Charlie Baker 's office declined to comment on the removal of the press , referring a reporter to State Police . <p> " When Troopers began arresting the demonstrators who refused to comply with repeated orders to leave the closed building , we determined that the entire floor had to be secured to ensure everyone 's safety , " State Police spokesman Dave Procopio said . " Twenty-seven individuals were arrested and others were escorted out . That process was completed in a peaceful manner , and once all arrestees were in custody and the situation secured , those restrictions were removed . " <p> Shortly after 5 p.m. troopers began ordering demonstrators to leave the building . The order was repeated several times , police said , and the 27 demonstrators who refused to leave were ultimately arrested and charged with trespassing in a state building , which is a misdemeanor offense . <p> " The dispersal of the majority of the demonstrators and the refusal by the 27 to comply was peaceful , " Procopio said . <p> Police arrested 15 women and 12 men , all of whom were over the age of 18 . They were transported to a nearby State Police barracks to be booked . All of them were expected to be released on the standard $40 bail clerk fee , with no bail set , police said . Those arrested are expected to be instructed to appear in Boston Municipal Court some time next week . <p> Mary Markos covers Massachusetts politics and state government .
