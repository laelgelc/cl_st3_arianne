In 2018 , state firefighters responded to a record 1,850 wildfires that burned 440,000 acres , Franz said . The 2019 fire season featured 130,000 acres burned , she said . <p> About 40% of wildfires the past two years were in western Washington , Franz said . <p> Costs to fight wildfires averaged $153 million per year the past five years , Franz said , and more than 2.2 million homes in the state are exposed to heightened wildfire risk . <p> The new money would pay for 42 new fulltime firefighters , 15 fire engine leaders and trucks , a helicopter , and various efforts to prevent wildfires in the first place , Franz said . <p> The Nature Conservancy supports the bill . <p> " It 's time to recognize the reality of fire in our forests and make the long-term investment our forests and communities need , " said Mike Stevens , state director of the environmental group . <p> Wayne Senter , director of Washington Fire Chiefs , said the group supports the bill because " it is local fire chiefs and the citizens we serve who depend on and benefit from " reducing wildfires . <p> State Sen. Kevin Van De Wege , D-Sequim , said climate change is causing more frequent and dangerous wildfires . <p> " Merely responding to fires , as we have traditionally done , costs far more in dollars , property and even human life than if we actively remove ground fuels " and take other steps to make forests more fire resistant , he said . <p> " That kind of strategy requires a stable revenue source , " he said . <p> This bill is different from a more complicated 2019 bill that failed to pass , in that the new bill contains a flat surcharge on insurance policies , Franz said . <p> It 's too early to predict how the 2020 fire season will look , Franz said .
