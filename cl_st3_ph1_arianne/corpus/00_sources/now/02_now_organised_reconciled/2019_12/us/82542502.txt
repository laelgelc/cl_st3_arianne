Each minute , the equivalent of a garbage truck full of plastic winds up in the ocean -- more than 8 million tons every year . <p> Every piece of plastic manufactured in the last 50 years is still around . It does n't go away . It just breaks down into smaller and smaller pieces called microplastics and these eventually wind up in our waterways and food chain . Fish ingest plastic and then we eat those fish and feed it to our children . Plastic byproducts have been detected in breast milk . <p> CLOSE <p> While plastic pollution of the oceans and waterways have been graphically captured in videos and photos on the Internet , it 's easy to forget that plastics contribute to climate change because they are derived from heat-trapping fossil fuels . They are a byproduct of fracking , a process that companies use to drill and horizontally blast water and a mix of mostly toxic chemicals into bedrock to release gas and oil . <p> This legislation has been bouncing around the Legislature for years . Taking up the slack has been a host of towns and cities statewide that have banned single-use plastic bags . Some have gone further and enacted bans also on Styrofoam food containers , plastic utensils and straws . <p> Those communities , as well as a wide range of groups including environmentalists , civic and faith-based organizations , environmental commissions and a Girl Scout Troop from Teaneck combine urgency with common sense . <p> With that push , the persistence of supportive legislators and Murphy 's veto of a much-weaker version of the bill last year , New Jersey is now poised to become a national leader .
