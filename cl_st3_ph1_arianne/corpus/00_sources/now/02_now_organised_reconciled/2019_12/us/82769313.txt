<p> BRUSSELS -- The European Union 's new leadership team has set a priority of deepening economic ties with China while protecting its own markets , a top official said , casting 2020 as a test of whether strains in Europe 's relations with Beijing can be reversed . <p> In an interview with The Wall Street Journal on Thursday , European Council President Charles Michel said he would lobby the bloc 's national leaders early in the new year on a more unified approach to Beijing , noting that past European efforts had lacked focus . He said while Europe would continue to discuss its approach to China with Washington , it would n't allow its ties , which were friendly but have turned warier in recent years , to be dictated by the U.S. <p> The EU and China have two summits planned next year , including a rare gathering involving all the bloc 's leaders . Mr. Michel , formerly Belgium 's prime minister , said he wants to make swift progress at the gatherings on trade and investment talks . He said the two sides should agree on a common approach to tackle climate change . Europe should also use the opportunity to push for better access for its companies in China , he added . <p> " We have to stop with the naivet ? , " he said . " If we open our markets , we have to get a guarantee that the partner ... will also open " its markets . <p> EU markets are generally open , meaning firms from around the world can invest without barriers , participate in public tenders and enter into joint ventures , among other things . <p> The EU and China launched talks on an investment agreement in 2013 but have made little headway amid disagreements on market access . Completing a deal next year , which could help address European concerns about forced technology transfers and intellectual-property theft , has looked highly ambitious . <p> Mr. Michel , whose job is to represent the views of EU national leaders , including at international summits , and to drive their agenda at EU summits , said he hoped streamlining the EU 's priorities would yield faster results . <p> " It is not my intention to have a new summit with China with 50 goals and 50 different interests , " Mr. Michel said . Next year " will be a moment of truth in the relationship between the European Union and China . " <p> The EU this spring approved legislation bolstering screening of foreign investment . It is also threatening to restrict access to its public tenders with China in mind . <p> EU officials have struck a wary note about China in recent months , labeling the Asian giant a systemic rival in a strategic paper this spring . There is nervousness about China 's investment in major infrastructure in some parts of Europe and concern about Beijing playing on divisions in the bloc over ties with China . <p> European officials have also said they feel disappointed by Chinese pledges to be a partner tackling climate change . While Europe is stepping up its ambitions to slash greenhouse-gas emissions , China is building more coal plants , its carbon emissions are increasing and it did n't back the EU 's push at a recent United Nations climate summit , which the U.S. did n't attend , to create a robust international carbon market . <p> The bloc has also faced strong pressure from the Trump administration to reject Chinese investment in Europe 's 5G telecommunications network , to take a more robust approach to Beijing 's military assertiveness in the South China Sea and to challenge China 's trade practices . <p> Mr. Michel , who took office on Dec. 1 as part of a new leadership team alongside the new president of the European Commission , former German defense minister Ursula von der Leyen , said European leaders , increasingly concerned about the spiraling economic and geopolitical tensions between Washington and Beijing , would n't allow the Trump administration to dictate its relationship with China . <p> " We are not a junior partner of the U.S. It means we have a strong dialogue with the United States , " Mr. Michel said . " But at the same time , we have our agenda and we have our interests .
