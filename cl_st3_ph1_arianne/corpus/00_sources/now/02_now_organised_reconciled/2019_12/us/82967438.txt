<p> LONDON ( AP ) -- The parents of teenage climate activist Greta Thunberg made changes in their lives to " save " their daughter , not the world . <p> The teenager 's father , Svante Thunberg , told the BBC on Monday that his 16-year-old daughter experienced depression for three or four years before going on school environment strikes . <p> He described how the young climate activist " fell ill " and stopped eating and talking to others . <p> To help her recover , he and his wife -- Swedish opera singer Malena Ernman -- made significant changes in their lives to become more environmentally friendly . <p> Svante Thunberg went vegan and Ernman stopped flying , which " changed her whole career , " he said . <p> " To be honest , she did n't do it save the climate . She did it to save her child because she saw how much it meant to her , and then , when she did that , she saw how much she grew from that , how much energy she got from it , " Thunberg said . <p> The father added that initially he did not support his daughter 's activism . <p> " We thought it was a bad idea , just the idea of your own daughter sort of putting herself at the very front line of such a huge question like climate change , " he said . " You would n't want that as a parent . " <p> His comments came during a special edition of the BBC Radio 4 Today program guest-edited by the young climate campaigner . <p> The BBC was heavily criticized for flying their presenter to Sweden to interview the teenager and her father , saying they " did not have time for other means of transport . " <p> The program also included interviews with outgoing Bank of England chief Mark Carney , who said the financial sector is responding too slowly to the climate crisis , and environment filmmaker David Attenborough .
