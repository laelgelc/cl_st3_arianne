If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <p> Only six California counties reported more possible cases of valley fever than Ventura County through the first 10 months of the year , according to new state data . <p> Cases of the difficult-to-diagnose infection surged locally two years ago in a rise that sparks debate about possible causes ranging from massive wildfires to global warming . <p> Preliminary disease counts from the California Department of Public Health suggest the fungal infection that one patient described as " hell , " has continued in the county at an elevated rate . <h> Breaking down the numbers <p> This tissue specimen shows the presence of coccidioidomycosis , also known as valley fever . ( Photo : CONTRIBUTED PHOTO FROM THE CDC ) <p> Through Oct. 31 , 260 Ventura County residents were identified with confirmed or suspected cases of an infection caused by breathing icoccidioides fungus spores lifted from the soil into the air by wind , construction , farming or other activities . <p> Many people escape infection with few if any symptoms . Others develop severe pneumonia and sometimes nodules in their lungs . In rare cases , valley fever can spread to the brain and spinal cord , bringing potentially fatal conditions like meningitis . <p> Ventura County 's 260 tally is on a rate that , depending on how many of the suspected cases are confirmed , could be the highest in at least two decades , rivaling the 249 cases in 2017 . <p> The numbers do n't surprise Dr. Glen Abergel , an Oxnard lung and infectious disease specialist . As of Tuesday , he had seen three patients with symptoms of valley fever in one week . <p> " They almost always have the same story , " he said , noting they 're diagnosed with pneumonia or bronchitis and given antibiotics that have little or no effect on the symptoms . <h> What are doctors saying ? <p> The cases come frequently enough that Abergel usually starts treatment with antifungal medication even before tests from specialized labs confirm the diagnosis . <p> Abergel believes the recent surge of patients may be linked to the massive Maria and Easy fires in Santa Paula and Simi Valley . He and other area lung specialists contend the Thomas Fire in 2017 and last year 's Woolsey Fire also spawned cases of valley fever . <p> " Absolutely , there is a correlation , " he said , contending fires and wind unleash spores into the air from coccidioides fungus that lives in the soil . " In two to three weeks after the fire , we see cases . " <p> Dr. Robert Levin , Ventura County public health officer , challenges the connection between the infection and fire . He noted the county once saw only dozens of valley fever a year before a surge of cases in 2017 that began several months before the December Thomas Fire . <p> The peak did n't last but subsided into a new baseline of more than 200 cases a year -- far higher than what the county once saw . <p> " Cocci is our canary in the ( coal ) mine , " he said , contending the infection is one of many things impacted by climate change . <p> Dr. Ramesh Nathan , an infectious disease specialist in Thousand Oaks and Simi Valley , said he 's seen an increase in severe complications of valley fever . In one case , a patient contracted the condition during pregnancy and developed meningitis . The baby was delivered and , after initial illness , appears to be doing well . <h> Some are at a higher risk <p> People at higher risk of severe complications include those with HIV or AIDS , transplant recipients , pregnant women , diabetics , African-Americans and Filipinos . Nathan said other questions remain . <p> " I do n't think we have a great understanding of some people do really poorly and some do n't , " he said . <p> California Department of Public Health officials said the number of cases in California have risen steadily over the past five years . They said the rise has brought action , including a coming awareness campaign and a new law that requires construction employers in many counties , including Ventura , conduct training to make sure workers understand the risks of valley fever . <p> State officials suggest people in areas where the disease is common stay inside during windy conditions , keep car windows closed and use recirculated air conditioning when driving . <p> Lynn Silveira , 69 , of Oxnard , takes the precautions seriously . During the recent fires in Santa Paula and Simi Valley , she wore a surgeon 's mask outdoors . <p> " The fires scare me , " she said . " The winds scare me . " <p> Her fear comes from experience . She was diagnosed with pneumonia in 2017 and given medication that did n't alleviate her symptoms before her husband suggested maybe the culprit was valley fever . <p> It was . <p> It took a year for her to recover . She lost 50 pounds . Constant fatigue meant all she wanted to do was nap . The fungal medication made her hair fall out . <p> " I went through what I considered hell , " she said , adding that a year later she feels much better . " Fantastic . I had a very good doctor . I got on the right medication . " <h> Valley fever symptoms <p> Officials from the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention say people should contact a doctor if symptoms last for more than a week .
