<h> City environment committee endorses ' bold ' climate change master plan <p> With the environment committee 's endorsement of draft targets for reducing greenhouse gas emissions produced by the City of Ottawa , council 's job might now be to convince people that the municipality is serious about climate change . <p> The committee on Tuesday voted unanimously in favour of a draft climate change master plan . <p> The proposed emissions targets for the city government are based on a 2012 baseline count . The targets are a 30-per-cent reduction by 2025 , 50-per-cent reduction by 2030 and 100-per-cent reduction by 2040 . <p> The proposed citywide emission reduction targets , also based on a 2012 baseline , are 43 per cent by 2025 , 68 per cent by 2030 , 96 per cent by 2040 and 100 per cent by 2050 . <p> The draft plan calls for annual reporting on the community and city government emissions and the progress toward the targets . <p> Buildings and vehicles are the largest greenhouse gas emitters in the city . <p> For the city government , the transit fleet accounts for half of its own emissions , driving for a call for OC Transpo to transition buses off diesel fuel . In fact , an " energy evolution " strategy , which is a companion document to the larger climate master plan , calls for a transit fleet with zero emissions . <p> The city has made some progress . Citywide emissions as of 2018 have dropped by 14 per cent since 2012 , while city government emissions have dropped by 36 per cent in that time . <p> Some of the 13 public delegates who addressed the committee , like Ella Mar , ratcheted up the urgency for council to set targets on reducing emissions . Mar told councillors that approving a climate change plan might be the most important action they take in their lives . <p> Other delegates questioned if the city government was up for the challenge . <p> Joan Haysom , an expert with the innovative energy group at J.L. Richards and Associates , said the city should consider climate protections when approving building permits and encourage tougher building standards . <p> David McNicoll said he has " weary skepticism " about the city 's plan , noting that the city has been talking about measures since the early ' 90s . <p> Ecology Ottawa 's Robb Barnes said the organization likes the city 's proposed emission targets and its intention for regular reporting , but Ecology Ottawa is concerned about a lack of progress on community emissions and the city 's slow pace of advancing energy evolution projects . Barnes , while flagging the cost of building new roads , urged the city to avoid expanding the urban boundary in the next official plan . <p> One delegate , Rob Dixon , criticized the plan for its potential financial impact to municipal taxpayers , prompting committee chair Scott Moffatt to explain that the city 's goal is to reduce city spending through energy-saving , environmentally friendly measures . <p> Despite hearing some skepticism , Coun. Shawn Menard said he sees a reason to be optimistic because he sees city staff who care about the plan . Coun. Catherine McKenney called the plan " bold . " <p> Support for the plan is likely a lock at a council meeting on Jan. 29 . Council declared a climate emergency last April , raising the importance of the next climate change master plan . <p> Meeting the new targets could require financial investments by city hall .
