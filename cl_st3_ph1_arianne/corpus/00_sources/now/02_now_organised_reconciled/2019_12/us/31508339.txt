<h> Emboldened Democrats say it 's a myth progressives ca n't take Texas <p> FILE -- Nurses hold up signs supporting Medicare for All proposals at a rally on Capitol Hill in Washington , Sept. 13 , 2017 . The question of whether to support a single-payer health care model has been one of the thorniest dilemmas for several Democrats considering a 2020 presidential campaign . ( Tom Brenner/The New York Times ) <p> Photo : TOM BRENNER , STF / NYT <p> WASHINGTON -- Texas has n't even turned blue and already a handful of Democratic candidates in the state are pushing further to the left heading into 2020 , advocating universal healthcare , aggressive action on climate change and decriminalizing border crossings . <p> Cristina Tzintz ? n Ramirez , the latest in the crowded field vying to take on Sen. John Cornyn in 2020 , checks all the boxes . She 's an Austin-based advocate for the rights of workers and immigrants who supports Medicare for All , the Green New Deal and wants to defund Immigration and Customs Enforcement . <p> Cornyn 's campaign on Monday called Tzintz ? n Ramirez a " hardcore progressive " -- a label she is n't afraid to own , even if her stands on issues put her to the left of most of the half-dozen other Democrats competing for the Senate nomination . <p> " Our ideas are actually not that radical , " Tzintz ? n Ramirez said . " Opponents will paint themas too ambitious . That 's to scare voters away from what 's possible and what they deserve . " <p> As the Democratic party has been supercharged by Beto O'Rourke 's near defeat of Sen. Ted Cruz last year , a raft of recent GOP retirements in Congress and a series of polls showing little name recognition and lackluster approval for Cornyn , a new brand of Texas Democrat is emerging -- one more closely aligned with Bernie Sanders or Elizabeth Warren than Joe Biden , or even O'Rourke . <p> The progressive candidates argue that a key to turning the state will be reaching voters who have not made a habit of voting in the past , especially young and minority voters who they believe can be energized by an unflinching progressive message . But their approach is creating heartburn among some establishment Democrats who favor moderates , especially because O'Rourke 's campaign proved the suburbs are the path to a 2020 victory . <p> " Texas is not a socialist state , it 's not a progressive state , " said Brendan Steinhauser , a Republican strategist who managed Cornyn 's 2014 campaign . " It 's a pretty center-right state . The good old Democrats who were pro-life and pro-gun are probably a better fit . " <p> Tzintz ? n Ramirez is one of a handful of progressives competing in the primaries . The Senate race she 's joining includes Sema Hernandez , a Democratic socialist from Houston who drew a quarter of the vote against O'Rourke in the 2018 U.S. Senate primary . <p> Jessica Cisneros , a 26-year-old immigration attorney in Laredo , meanwhile , is aiming to take down U.S. Rep. Henry Cuellar , a veteran Democratic congressman and well-known centrist . Cuellar has supported Democratic initiatives such as the Affordable Care Act , but also has n't shied away from things that would give other Democrats pause , such as accepting money from the National Rifle Association . <p> Cisneros , who was 11 when Cuellar was first elected , is backed by Justice Democrats , the group of ultra-progressives that helped U.S. Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez unseat a longtime Democratic congressman in New York . Cuellar 's district is among their top targets in 2020 . <p> Cisneros , who launched her campaign in June , said she has seen little resistance to progressive ideas in the district . Not a single person has told her they could n't get on board with Medicare for All , she said . But many of them do n't know about it because it has n't been part of the political discussion . <p> " There 's this myth that South Texas is so conservative and a progressive here would n't get much traction . We 're disproving that narrative , " she said . " Our district has n't had these conversations at all because we have had a conservative Democrat . " <p> Other Democrats hoping to snag nominations for Texas congressional races who have backed progressive planks such as Medicare for All or the Green New Deal include Sri Kulkarni , who came within 5 points of unseating U.S. Rep. Pete Olson in 2018 , Mike Siegel , a North Austin attorney running for U.S. Rep. Mike McCaul 's seat , and accountant Jan McDowell , who nearly beat Dallas-area U.S. Rep. Kenny Marchant in 2018 . <p> Still , their emergence is causing unease among Democrats . <p> " I just do n't want us to mess this thing up , " Gilberto Hinojosa , the chairman of the state party , told the New York Times recently , noting that " a lot of people in this state who vote for Democrats now did n't used to vote for Democrats . " <p> " The candidates are going to have to decide if they 're going to take positions that make certain people who have access to their health care uncomfortable , " he told the Chronicle , referring to Medicare for All . Hinojosa advocates for bolstering the Affordable Care Act instead . <p> Yet Hinojosa has welcomed each new Democrat to the primaries . In a statement Monday , he said " The Texas Democratic Party is proud Democrats , like Cristina Tzintz ? n Ramirez , are running . Texas Democrats know that with hard work , strong values , and bold action , victory is in our grasp . " <p> Hinojosa said even the recent converts to the party will line up behind whichever candidate wins the nomination against Cornyn . <p> " While you may have some people not wanting to be in a position where their health care is interrupted , they 're certainly not going to support a candidate like John Cornyn who wants to take it all away from you , " he said , referring to Cornyn 's vote to repeal Obamacare . <p> Meanwhile Cornyn , with more than $9 million on hand , has pounced , branding each of his opponents as an Elizabeth Warren-style progressives -- no matter how far left they actually are . <p> " While the Democrat primary is quickly turning into a contest of who can run to the left the fastest , we 're building our field operation and adding grassroots supporters , " Cornyn 's campaign manager John Jackson said in a statement last month . " Whoever limps out of the runoff will face a grassroots army motivated to elect John Cornyn and stop Chuck Schumer and Elizabeth Warren 's agenda . " <p> That statement was aimed at Royce West , a longtime state senator who is running in part on his track record of working with the Republicans who dominate the state Legislature . <p> " I ca n't control the narrative , " West said . " If people look at my record , they see that I 'm kind of a common sense person ... I would not have been able to serve in the Legislature and get things done had I been a firebrand liberal . " <p> " And I 've gotten things done , " he said . " I 've been able to compromise . " <p> Tzintz ? n Ramirez -- on the other end of the field , and with no political experience -- is banking on Texans wanting a change . <p> " I do n't think we 're asking for too much , " she said . " I think we simply have politicians who have n't cared too much about the American people . Texans are tired of that and they want to elect someone who will fight for them and stand up for them and understand the economic hardships they face everyday . " <p> Benjamin Wermund is the Houston Chronicle 's Washington correspondent . He previously covered federal education policy and national education issues at POLITICO , and before that covered higher education at the Chronicle and K-12 education at the Austin American-Statesman . He 's a Texas native and a diehard Spurs fan .
