( Ruth Fremson/The New York Times ) Photo : Ruth Fremson , NYT <p> The nearly all-white lineup of candidates at the first Democratic presidential debate in California on Thursday wo n't reflect the state 's racial and ethnic diversity . But viewers can still hope for questions about issues that resonate with residents of the world 's fifth-largest economy . <p> Jessica Bartholow , a policy advocate with the Western Center on Law and Poverty , wants to know what the candidates would do about the 130,000 homeless people in California . This week , the U.S. Supreme Court left in place a landmark ruling preventing cities from rousting people from street camps unless they offer them shelter or housing . <p> " As president , " Bartholow asked , " where would you stand on the ability of cities to criminalize homelessness ? " <p> * Jacqueline Martinez Garcel , CEO of the Latino Community Foundation , is particularly concerned that her group 's numbers show only 42% of Latinos in California own homes , compared with 64% of whites . Her organization wants to know how candidates will address the rising cost of rental housing and expand homeownership opportunities for Latinos . <p> " Yes , a majority of Latinos are renters , " Garcel told The Chronicle 's " It 's All Political " podcast . " But the real problem here is that we do n't have a pathway for people who rent right now to actually own their homes . " <p> * Bartholow also wants to hear more about the nearly 20% of Californians who live in poverty . Specifically , she wants to hear how they will have enough to eat . This month , the Trump administration tightened work requirements for the federal food stamp program , a move that Bartholow said " will cut hundreds of thousands of Californians " from the benefits . <p> Bartholow asked , " What would you do as president to end hunger and create a food economy where farmers , food producers and grocers can thrive ? " <p> * Stopping climate change is a top priority for many Californians , according to a Berkeley IGS Poll . Mary Creasman , CEO of the California League of Conservation Voters Education Fund , wants the candidates to explain how they would help the state as it " continues to face the threats of climate change -- deadly wildfires and droughts . " <p> " As president , " Creasman asked , " how will you help California and other states hold corporations accountable to meet the necessary climate action requirements , and how will you ensure that these pro-environment policies reach all communities , especially the most vulnerable ? " <p> California saw the number of its uninsured people drop after it became a national leader in implementing the federal Affordable Care Act . But that trend stopped as the Trump administration tried to undermine the health law , Jimenez said , particularly after the president signed tax legislation in 2017 that eliminated the financial penalty for going without health insurance . Close to 3 million Californians remain uninsured . <p> " What health care policies would you prioritize for states like California , " Jimenez asked , " that made significant strides in getting people health coverage and care , only to see that progress stalled and even eroded by federal actions ? " <p> * Other states are considering following California 's lead in requiring that many companies turn their gig workers into employees with benefits . Ken Jacobs , chair of the UC Berkeley Labor Center , hopes there is a question about the new law , AB5 . <p> Several of the candidates have endorsed AB5 ; Steyer has n't taken a position . But those endorsements came before resistance to the new law started rising from professions that are not exempted from its provisions . This week , the American Society of Journalists and Authors and the National Press Photographers Association filed suit in federal court saying freelance journalists would be harmed by the law . Last month , the California Trucking Association did the same on behalf of independent truckers . <p> Jacobs ' question : " Should gig workers be treated as employees and have protections like minimum wage , paid rest breaks , unemployment insurance and workers ' compensation if they are injured on the job ? " <p> * We 've heard a few mentions in the debates of one of California 's top crops -- cannabis . All the Democrats on stage Thursday support legalizing cannabis and removing its federal designation as a drug on par with heroin , except for Biden , who backs decriminalization instead . <p> Here 's a more leading-edge California question , from Tom Angell , publisher of Marijuana Moment , a news outlet that tracks cannabis and drug policy developments : " Do you support legalizing the possession , cultivation and retail sale of psilocybin mushrooms in California ? " <p> Otherwise known as , yes , magic mushrooms . This is n't as out there as you might think . Supporters are trying to place such a legalization measure before California voters next year . <p> Yang is game . <p> " We should explore making psilocybin mushrooms legal for medical and therapeutic use particularly for veterans , " Yang tweeted Sunday . <p> A veteran in Davenport told me that psilocybin mushrooms were the only treatment he found effective for his depression after returning home . We should explore making psilocybin mushrooms legal for medical and therapeutic use particularly for veterans . <p> The other candidates have n't weighed in yet , leaving the topic wide open for discussion .
