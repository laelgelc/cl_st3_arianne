( Photo : Courtesy of Rutgers Environmental Stewards ) <h> Rutgers Environmental Stewards of Union County recruiting volunteers <p> Union County residents can make a New Year 's resolution that will have a lasting impact by joining the Rutgers Environmental Stewards of Union County . The all-volunteer Environmental Stewards program helps members of the public learn how to take specific steps leading to improvements in the environmental health of their communities . The program is designed to help non-scientists learn more about the science behind the environmental issues impacting their communities . Environmental Stewards hold weekly classroom training sessions on 4 to 7 p.m . Thursdays beginning Jan. 30 through early June at Rutgers Cooperative Extension offices in the Union County complex at 300 North Ave . east in Westfield . The training sessions include classes on habitat conservation , pollinator preservation , climate change and its impacts on the Union County region , protection of the Rahway River watershed and more . Field trips are part of the interactive experience . Participants also work on environmental internship projects of their choice . Previous stewards have applied their knowledge to combat the destructive Ash borer beetle and to create environmental programs throughout the county . To register , visit **26;1464;TOOLONG or contact Michele Bakacs , County Agent and Associate Professor for the Rutgers Cooperative Extension of Union County at bakacs@njaes.rutgers.edu or 732-398-5274 . The organization will focus on educating the public about the benefits of creating a process that would allow the state to consider a governance change so Horizon Blue Cross Blue Shield of NJ , the state 's only nonprofit health insurer , can modernize its corporate structure and become a not-for-profit mutual , organizers said in a statement . According to them , this simple change will improve Horizon 's ability to invest in technologies and innovations that promote quality , affordable health care for their customers . Moreover , it would protect the public interests of millions of New Jerseyans who have relied on Horizon for almost 90 years , they said .
