<p> Vatican City -- Pope Francis and the secretary-general of the United Nations said that , like millions of people around the world , during the Christmas season their thoughts turn to yearnings for peace and for the well-being of those in need . <p> After a 40-minute private meeting in the papal library Dec. 20 , Francis and Antonio Guterres , the U.N . chief , recorded an eight-minute video , which was released a few hours later . <p> Francis began by saying that in the days before Christmas , " our gazes are turned toward heaven to entrust to God the people and situations dearest to our hearts . " <p> Giving thanks " for all the good there is in the world , " the pope especially singled out the people who dedicate their lives to helping others and refuse to give up working for a more humane and just world . <p> " We can not , we must not look away when faced with injustices , inequalities , the scandal of hunger in the world , poverty , children who die because they do not have the necessary water , food or health care , " Francis said . <p> " We can not turn away when there is any kind of abuse of children , " he said . " We must fight this plague together . " <p> " We can not close our eyes to our many brothers and sisters who , because of conflict and violence , misery or climate change , leave their homelands and often encounter a sad destiny , " he said . <p> " We can not remain indifferent when human dignity is trampled and exploited or to attacks on human life , both of the unborn and of any person in need of care , " the pope continued . <p> And , he said , " we can not , we must not look the other way when believers of different faiths are persecuted in several parts of the world . " <p> When religion is used " to incite hatred , violence , oppression , extremism and blind fanaticism " or to force people to stand on the sidelines of their community life or even to migrate , he said , it " cries out to God for vengeance . " <p> " But the arms race and nuclear rearmament also cry out to God for vengeance , " he said . " And it is immoral not only to use , but also to possess nuclear weapons , which have such a destructive power that even just the chance of an accident represents a grave danger for humanity . " <p> Francis called for attention to the wars that continue to take the lives of innocent people and pleaded for a greater commitment to dialogue and to the role of the United Nations . <p> " Let us recognize each other as members of one humanity and take care of our earth which , generation after generation , is entrusted to us by God for safeguarding , " the pope said . " The commitment to reducing polluting emissions and for an integral ecology is urgent and necessary . Let 's do something before it is too late ! " <p> Guterres , for his part , thanked Francis for being " a messenger for hope and humanity -- for reducing human suffering and promoting human dignity . " <p> The " core values " of the U.N . charter align with many of the Catholic Church 's efforts , he said , particularly efforts " to reaffirm the dignity and worth of the human person . To promote love of people and care for our planet . To uphold our common humanity and protect our common home . " <p> " Our world needs that more than ever , " Guterres said . <p> The U.N . leader also echoed Francis ' focus on the Christmas season as " a time of peace and goodwill , " and he denounced attacks on Christians , Muslims and Jews just because of their faith . <p> He praised the document on interreligious dialogue and human fraternity that Francis and Sheikh Ahmad el-Tayeb , grand imam of al-Azhar , signed in Abu Dhabi in February . <p> " The United Nations has also launched a ' Plan of Action to Safeguard Religious Sites ' and a strategy to combat hate speech , " Guterres said . " In these turbulent and trying times , we must stand together for peace and harmony . "
