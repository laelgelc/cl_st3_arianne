<p> Climate negotiators failed to strengthen targets to cut emissions or to create a global carbon-trading system , two main goals of the 2015 Paris accord , as the impending U.S. exit from the pact exacerbated challenges to cut record-high planet-warming gases . <p> Delegates from nearly 200 nations sparred for two weeks in Madrid at the annual United Nations climate summit without setting new emissions targets before next year 's U.N . convention in Glasgow or creating a framework to reward and encourage efforts to cut emissions . <p> " Regretfully , after all the hard work that you have all done we could n't get to an agreement , " said Chile 's Environment Minister Carolina Schmidt , who presided over the summit that ended Sunday . " The new generations expect more from us . " <p> Financing to help poorer countries transition into less-polluting technology also proved contentious . The issue deepened the divide between less-developed countries that account for a fraction of greenhouse-gas releases and economic powers responsible for the vast majority of it . <p> The past five years have been the hottest since temperatures have been measured . In September , a scientific panel under U.N . auspices said oceans are warming and ice sheets disappearing and that human communities and wildlife are at risk . <p> Major powers that account for more than 75% of human-generated global emissions -- including China , the U.S. , the European Union , India and Russia -- risk missing their emissions-reduction goals . Some countries are also taking steps that further undermine the climate pact . <p> China , the top emitter globally , is adding more coal-fired plants than the rest of the world combined , while also building them internationally . The U.S. , the world 's second-largest emitter , is rolling back emissions-reducing rules on energy and transport enacted by the Obama administration . U.S. carbon emissions rose 3.4% in 2018 , after three years of declines , according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration . <p> " The world 's largest emitters are not pulling their weight , and without them our goal is unreachable , " U.N . Secretary General Ant ? nio Guterres said before the climate summit convened . As talks concluded , he added : " The international community lost an important opportunity . " <p> In an effort to counter the backtracking among major powers , the EU committed on Friday to reaching net-zero emissions by 2050 . EU-member Poland , however , declined to implement the plan until at least June and demanded financial guarantees to transition its economy , which relies 80% on coal-fired power . <p> The EU , the world 's third-largest emitter , pushed at the U.N . summit for rules on international carbon markets . In the end , European negotiators refused demands from countries including Brazil , India and Australia that they thought would weaken efforts to reduce emissions . <p> The Brazil-led efforts , European negotiators and climate activists say , would lead to double-counting of emission cuts and usher in a trove of carbon credits granted under the Kyoto Protocol , the Paris accord 's predecessor . <p> Those would contribute to watering down the climate pact and amount to cosmetic emissions cuts without arresting rising temperatures , these people said . <p> " We are disappointed , " the EU delegation said in a Sunday statement . " We can not support global rules which would undermine environmental integrity and take us back instead of taking us forward . " <p> Despite the setback to a global carbon market , an existing framework suffices for national and regional initiatives , say climate experts . The EU already has the world 's biggest emissions trading system , covering energy-heavy industrial plants . This month , the EU and Switzerland linked their carbon markets , signaling a way forward for other countries . <p> But the EU is unable to drive the global agenda alone . After Mr. Trump 's June 2017 decision to leave the climate pact , China partnered with the EU to uphold and promote the Paris deal . The World Meteorological Organization said this month that average temperatures world-wide in 2019 were 1.1 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels . <p> With current commitments , global warming is on track to rise to 3 degrees Celsius by 2050 -- the date many scientists say is the deadline to reach carbon neutrality or risk irreversible changes to climate patterns and ecosystems world-wide . <p> Amid public pressure , countries fought over funds to help countries mitigate climate change . Developing countries need more than $4.4 trillion from 2020 through 2030 to implement measures under the Paris accord . <p> " The developed countries have a responsibility for historical emissions and for the impacts of climate change , " the group of like-minded developing countries , representing half the world 's population including China and India , said Sunday . <p> Wealthy countries promised to make available $100 billion annually starting in 2020 to help less poorer nations meet climate goals . The latest data shows that richer countries averaged about $54.3 billion from 2013 through 2016 , and hit $71.2 billion in 2017 , according to the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development . <p> The shortfalls are stoking concerns that developed countries may not deliver on their funding pledge and are fueling backlash against Paris accord commitments . Some African countries said they will resist the climate agenda altogether , citing their reliance on fossil fuel resources for future economic growth . <p> " Anybody out of the continent saying we should not develop oil and gas fields , that is criminal .
