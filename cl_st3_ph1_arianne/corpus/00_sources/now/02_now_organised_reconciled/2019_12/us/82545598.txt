A neuroscientist at Harvard explains the interface between the eye and the brain , looking at how this process converts light into information . <p> Life Changing : How Humans Are Altering Life on Earth by Helen Pilcher ( Apr. 7 , $28 , ISBN 978-1-4729-5671-2 ) examines various ways in which humans have changed other species at the genetic level , from the breeding of domestic animals , to the developments of methods for directly intervening in DNA , as well as the new evolutionary pressures accompanying climate change . <p> Unfit for Purpose by Adam Hart ( July 14 , $28 , ISBN 978-1-4729-7099-2 ) . The biologist and broadcaster looks at the mismatch between humans ' evolved traits and their created world . Harris ( June 2 , $35 , ISBN 978-0-231-18450-2 ) investigates the evolutionary history of big cats , emphasizing the often detrimental impact of humans . Hallett provides illustrations showing what paleontologists believe now-extinct kinds of big cats looked like . <p> Counterpoint <p> Desert Notebooks : A Road Map for the End of Time by Ben Ehrenreich ( July 7 , $26 , ISBN 978-1-64009-353-9 ) considers the issue of climate change in a narrative that combines the author 's personal experiences with considerations of geologies and ecologies of desert spaces , in particular Joshua Tree and the area outside of Las Vegas . <p> Trees in Trouble : Wildfires , Infestations , and Climate Change Hit the West by Daniel Mathews ( Apr. 7 , $26 , ISBN 978-1-64009-135-1 ) examines the damaging effects environmental deterioration has had on the U.S. 's western pine forests , once-resilient and stable places now in the midst of large-scale and rapid change . <p> DK <p> How the Brain Works : The Facts Visually Explained by the editors of DK ( Mar. 10 , $22 , ISBN 978-1-4654-8979-1 ) aims to make the workings of the human mind accessible to a lay audience . Physicist R ? yne explains the role played by different elements within the human body , and visits various sites around the world where these elements can be found . <p> Farrar , Straus and Giroux <p> The Better Half : On the Genetic Superiority of Women by Sharon Moalem ( Apr. 7 , $27 , ISBN 978-1-250-17478-9 ) looks at the edge enjoyed by females over males at multiple stages of life , in terms of resiliency , intellect , stamina , and immunity , and uses this data to argue against a male-centric approach to medicine . <p> 0-374-15733-3 ) surveys the historical artifacts that modern-day human activity will leave behind for the distant future , such as carbon in the atmosphere and nuclear waste buried within the earth . <p> Flatiron <p> Strange Bedfellows : Adventures into the Science , History , and Surprising Secrets of STDs by Ina Park ( July 28 , $27.99 , ISBN 978-1-250-20662-6 ) . The author of Ravens in Winter and A Naturalist at Large scrutinizes the lives of wild birds through his interactions with a pair of tree swallows who nest at his Maine cabin . <p> ( Mar. 17 , $19.99 , ISBN 978-1-62354-541-3 ) . The blogger for National Geographic 's Gory Details takes a humorous look at the odd , morbid , and even stomach-churning aspects of biology , anatomy , space exploration , nature , and other scientific fields . <p> North Atlantic <p> Insect Cuisine : Changing the Future of Food , Farming , and Nutrition by Robert Nathan Allen and Justin Butner ( Apr. 7 , $18.95 trade paper , ISBN 978-1-62317-403-3 ) .
