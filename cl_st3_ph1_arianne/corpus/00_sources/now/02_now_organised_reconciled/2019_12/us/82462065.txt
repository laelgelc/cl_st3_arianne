<p> The dueling 2020 presidential campaign arguments are taking shape right now in Washington , in full view . <p> On the Democratic side , the impeachment hearings that move this week to the House Judiciary Committee are n't important because they are going to result in the removal of President Trump from office ; they almost certainly wo n't do that . <p> What the impeachment process will do , however , is frame the Democrats ' central arguments for ejecting Mr. Trump from office in 2020 . The case is that he is an abuser of presidential power for his own gain , a destroyer of institutions and norms , and a violator of traditional constitutional checks and balances . <p> In short , impeachment will help make Democrats ' overarching case that Mr. Trump is a dangerous man . That argument is being made dramatically , in televised hearings . <p> Less obvious , perhaps , is the case that is being framed by President Trump and his team . <p> For many of the president 's supporters , a detailed argument based on his policies or results is n't really necessary . They support Mr. Trump as much for his attitude as his accomplishments . The very Trump attribute that most infuriates his opponents -- that he is a destroyer of norms and institutions -- happens to be what a lot of core Trump supporters like . <p> But the more substantive Trump argument can be seen and heard by watching Mr. Trump these days , and it is built on three pillars : <p> -- The economy . When all else is stripped away , this will be the central case Mr. Trump will make for himself . Since he took office , the economy has grown at a rate surpassing 2% every quarter , and growth has topped 3% in two quarters . The average quarterly growth rate has been 2.5% . <h> Newsletter Sign-up <p> Democrats will argue , correctly , that this merely continues a long stretch of economic growth that began under President Obama , and at almost the same rate . In the six years running from the start of 2010 , when the recovery from the deep recession of 2007-2008 really began , until the end of 2016 , quarterly economic growth averaged 2.2% . So Democrats maintain that this is as much an Obama as a Trump expansion -- and that the Trump part was artificially boosted by the stimulus of an annual federal budget deficit approaching $1 trillion . <p> Still , any sitting president gets a disproportionate amount of credit or blame for the economy , and the Trump economy has been a good one overall . Plus , the Dow Jones Industrial Average is up a whopping 41% since he took office , which bolsters the president 's argument . <p> -- Changed global economic rules . Beyond impeachment , the two most important substantive agenda items moving forward in Washington right now are two trade deals : the U.S.-Mexico-Canada , or USMCA , trade agreement that is awaiting congressional approval , and a so-called Phase One trade agreement with China that appears near at hand . <p> Those are the two major manifestations of Mr. Trump 's populist contention that the new global economy has been stacked against the U.S. , and that the way to change that is to force a rewrite of the most important rules , the ones governing trade . Taking on China , in particular , is an idea with bipartisan support . <p> There are important caveats : The new USMCA trade agreement is less revolutionary than Mr. Trump proclaims it to be ; it represents more an evolution of the old North American accord . The China trade deal is n't yet in hand , and falls well short of the all-encompassing makeover of the U.S.-China trade relationship that Mr. Trump has promised . <h> Related Video <p> As part of a series of Wall Street Journal interviews with Democratic 2020 candidates , Tom Steyer sat down with WSJ 's Greg Ip to discuss the economic challenges of climate change and how he would treat big banks if he were president . He has taken other populist moves recently that he will present as a sign of a different kind of Republican economic sensibility , particularly his administration 's rule forcing hospitals and insurance companies to disclose their secretly negotiated rates . <p> By contrast , his immigration policies are far more divisive ; those will be pitched more to core followers . <p> -- The Democrats themselves . A good barometer of Mr. Trump 's thinking is always his Twitter feed , and in the last week he has tweeted out 13 direct attacks on the Democratic Party . Most slam Democrats over impeachment , of course , but mixed in are jibes at the " Do Nothing Democrats " so obsessed with impeachment they are neglecting national business . <p> That critique fits in nicely with the other Trump line of attack on Democrats , which is that they have become a radicalized party of the left . The fusion was made neatly in an early November tweet : " The Do Nothing Dems have gone Radical Left Crazy . The sic ca n't get anything done ! " <p> In short , Democrats will be running as much against Mr. Trump as for themselves , while Mr. Trump will be doing the same thing in reverse .
