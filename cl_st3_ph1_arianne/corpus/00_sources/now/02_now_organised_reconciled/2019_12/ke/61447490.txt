<h> Summary <p> None of 15 categories of government content analysed is in machine-readable formats , stalling automation push . <p> advertisement <p> advertisement <p> The lack of access to free , up-to-date and machine-readable government data stands in the way of Kenya tapping into opportunities brought by artificial intelligence , a Nation Newsplex analysis reveals . <p> The country tops Africa and ranks 52 globally out of 194 countries on the government 's readiness to adopt artificial intelligence ( AI ) in public services but only manages positions seven in the continent and 78 out of 94 globally in the availability of government data , according to two key reports . <p> The Government Artificial Intelligence Readiness Index 2019 report , by the International Development Research Centre ( IDRC ) and Oxford Insights , factors in 11 inputs grouped into four main categories -- governance , infrastructure and data , skills and education as well as government and public services . <p> " An AI-ready government will display both strong political will and capacity to push for innovation , " says the report . <p> No African country features in the top 50 AI readiness index , whereas only 12 are in the top 100 . <p> " Not only will they not reap the potential benefits of AI , but there is also the danger that unequal implementation widens global inequalities , " the report says . <p> The Global Open Data Index 2016/17 by the Open Knowledge Network Foundation , on the other hand , gauges the availability of government data and hints at how much data might be available to model and train AI algorithms to carry out various tasks . <h> Also Read <p> Kenya scores a measly 15 percent , meaning that there is little useful government data easily available for AI experts or learners in Kenya to use to develop credible solutions . <p> This despite access to information , which includes data , being a citizen 's constitutional right . In comparison , the leading country , Taiwan , scores 90 percent . <p> According to the Access to Information Act of 2016 , every citizen has the right to access to information held by the State , except in special circumstances such as when it undermines national security or impedes due process of law . <p> While the government has made efforts to ease access , this information is largely not in as many formats as possible for widespread consumption , in this case , machine-readable formats such as CSV , XLS , XML and JSON . <p> computer systems <p> Artificial Intelligence , which involves training computer systems to do tasks normally done by human beings , has been embraced by developed nations as the next frontier of development . <p> The IDRC report cites concerns over low and middle-income countries located in the southern hemisphere lagging as their northern and more developed counterparts move to enjoy the gains made available by AI , such as improved quality of service and citizens ' experience of government . <p> In Kenya , it would come in handy in areas such as boosting education outcomes through personalised learning , disease control , citizen inquiries , weather forecasts and predicting food security issues . <p> take-off <p> Over the last decade , Kenya 's total value of the investment in AI is estimated at Sh13 billion , which is tiny compared with South Africa 's Sh165.8 billion and Nigeria Sh60.3 billion , according to a Microsoft 's Artificial Intelligence in the Middle East and Africa 2019 Outlook report . <p> This investment is in the form of support for start-ups and entrepreneurs , with the government investing little . <p> Many ICT experts believe that data , more than any other factor , is key to the development of AI in the country and there ca n't be take-off if that is not sorted out . <p> " Data is the engine of AI since AI creates algorithms for predictions mainly based on historical data . If that 's missing , the power and impact of AI will be suboptimal , or worse still , have a negative impact , " explains John Walubengo , an ICT lecturer at Multimedia University and a technology blogger , who was also part of the Blockchain and Artificial Intelligence Task Force , whose mandate was to provide the government with recommendations on how to harness these emerging technologies over the next five years . <p> One of the proposals made by the task force , which the government formed last year , is to increase data availability . <p> " Local public data tends to be in short supply . The availability and amount of such public data would also need to be improved to ensure the development of AI solutions that address local needs , " states the task force report published in July this year . <p> In 2011 , Kenya launched its first open data portal , providing the public government data in a machine-readable format . However , several Newsplex spot-checks on the portal showed that some of the datasets are raw and not up-to-date . <p> Government data <p> South Africa leads the continent in access to government data with 40 per cent , followed by Tunisia ( 22 percent ) , Tanzania ( 20 percent ) , Zambia ( 19 percent ) and Lesotho and Ghana with 16 percent each . <p> The openness of government data is measured under six factors in 15 categories . <p> The factors include whether it is openly licensed , downloadable at once , up-to-date , publicly available , free and in a machine-readable format . <p> The Kenyan government scored nil in 10 out of the 15 categories analysed . <p> However , it scored 45 percent each in government budgets , national statistics , procurement , national laws and draft legislation . <p> In the five areas , the information was available free of charge and publicly accessible , but none of it was in machine-readable formats that can be easily fed into algorithms . <p> Australia and the United Kingdom have the second-highest open data score ( 79 percent each ) , followed by France ( 70 percent ) , Finland , Canada and Norway ( 69 percent each ) . Myanmar scores the least ( one per cent ) . <p> Resistance to change <p> The World Economic Forum lists resistance to change as one of the five major barriers to AI adoption in government and public entities , owing , in part , to their long-established processes . <p> " In parts of the private sector , a strong culture for experimentation encourages employees to innovate , and positive performance is rewarded . <p> " In government , there can be less encouragement for employees to take risks , " it says . <p> This willingness to adopt AI in the private sector is visible from responses given by executives of various companies in the Middle East and Africa . <p> Three in four companies studied say they wish to use AI for predictive analysis such as forecasting customer behaviour and maintenance of their machinery , according to the Microsoft report . <p> public service <p> Artificial intelligence and open data aside , Kenya and African countries are generally ranked lowly in embracing information technologies in the public service . <p> The country ranks 122 out of 193 countries globally and 11th in Africa in the 2018 e-Government Development Index by the United Nations , which measures how a country is using information technologies to promote access and inclusion of its citizens . <p> Similarly , in the 2019 Change Readiness Index by KPMG , which measures a country 's ability to respond to changes like new technologies and climate change , the Kenyan government , at position 68 out of 140 nations ranks lower than the civil society ( 64 ) and enterprises ( 63 ) . <p> too expensive <p> To address the government 's poor showing , Mr Walubengo proposes the creation of progressive policies that would provide safe spaces for public sector experimentation while at the same time shielding the decision-maker from any form of punishment . <p> Second to the data challenge is the high demand and low supply of AI experts , as most people with such knowledge and skills are found in the private sector , at a cost too expensive for governments to hire . <p> " There are few AI groups centred in sharing AI knowledge practice , but they are focused on enterprise-type of AI interventions , largely in the banking , insurance , mobile lending and transport sectors , " says Mr Walubengo . <p> He adds that counties could also benefit a great deal from AI , especially in areas such as agriculture and health if policymakers in the devolved units would engage with AI experts .
