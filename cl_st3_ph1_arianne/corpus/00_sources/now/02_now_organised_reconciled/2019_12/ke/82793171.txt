<p> Monitoring climate change adaptation : the importance of linking global and national reporting <p> The Paris Agreement provides a foundation for the most robust climate change transparency system to date . IIED research has identified ways to improve this reporting by aligning global and national activity . <p> The Paris Agreement asks countries to monitor and report on their progress towards their adaptation targets . But this is not an easy task . Many adaptation targets -- such as better adaptive capacity or improved resilience -- are qualitative . So , unlike mitigation , adaptation can not easily be measured using any single metric . <p> Adaptation also has to be assessed on many different levels : global , national , local and sectoral . <p> At present -- despite considerable international attention for adaptation monitoring and evaluation ( M&amp;E ) -- there is limited evidence that M&amp;E systems are linking across these different levels . Yet doing so would provide many benefits . It would help to : <p> Streamline workflows and avoid duplication <p> Reduce the reporting burden <p> Minimise resource wastage <p> Improve downward accountability <p> Improve country-level decision making <p> Share learning between national and international frameworks , and <p> Win ' buy-in ' from the people responsible for making sure these systems work . <p> IIED is looking at ways to meet these challenges . Its latest paper on this subject compares the results measurement frameworks ( RMFs ) used by global climate funds with the M&amp;E systems used by developing countries , and argues that aligning these would benefit both sides . <p> The paper , ' Assessing adaptation results : aligning national M&amp;E systems and global results frameworks ' , looks at four major global climate funds that are investing in RMFs to monitor and assess adaptation activities . It also looks at the different approaches taken by some country M&amp;E systems . It then gives recommendations for aligning these global funds and national systems through two routes : aligning with well-established existing systems or supporting less established country M&amp;E systems .
