by just a few metres . <p> Norway 's King Harald will formally inaugurate the field in January , but production began back in early October and 350,000 barrels are already being pumped up each day . <p> That " probably " makes it the most productive field in western Europe , according to the Norwegian Petroleum Directorate . <p> When it hits its peak in late 2022 , the field -- which also includes companies Lundin of Sweden , Aker BP of Norway , and France 's Total -- is expected to produce almost double that , or 660,000 barrels per day . <p> The installation -- consisting of four platforms , soon to be five , connected by suspended walkways -- are so large that workers use big blue three-wheeled scooters to get around . <p> The site is run on electricity from land , supplied by a 160-kilometer ( 100-mile ) underwater cable . <p> And it 's clean energy , sourced from hydroelectric dams . <p> At the production stage , each barrel has a carbon footprint 25 times lower than the global average , says Rune Nedregaard , head of operations at Johan Sverdrup . <p> " That 's important in a climate perspective . Considering we do need oil , it 's important to produce that oil as efficiently as possible . " <h> Cash cow <p> But climate change knows no borders , and when the oil is burned , it is just as polluting as any other petrol , argue environmental activists . <p> " It 's the oil policies that lay the groundwork for climate policy , and it should be the other way around , " charges the head of the Norwegian branch of Friends of the Earth , Silje Ask Lundberg . <p> Oil and natural gas have enabled the Scandinavian country to amass the world 's biggest sovereign wealth fund , with more than 10 trillion kroner ( $1 trillion ) stashed away for future generations . <p> But a growing number of Norwegians want the country to speed up its transition to a green economy , opinion polls suggest , and more and more political movements have called for the oil sector to be curtailed or even phased out entirely . <p> Two NGOs have sued the state to get it to cancel drilling licences recently awarded in the Arctic . Having lost their case in district court , the NGOs now await a verdict in the appeals court . <p> " The government can no longer ignore the dangerous impact its exported oil is having on the climate , " stresses Frode Pleym , the head of Greenpeace Norway , one of the plaintiffs in the lawsuit . <p> " Oil is oil , no matter where it is burned . " <p> To limit global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius in line with the Paris Agreement , scientists say it will be necessary to be carbon neutral by 2050 . <p> Norway sees itself as a climate champion , heavily subsidising the purchase of electric cars and generously funding efforts to protect the rainforests . <p> But it is one of only a few countries in Europe that has higher greenhouse gas emissions now than in 1990 . <p> The Norwegian oil and gas industry has seen emissions soar by 73 percent during that period , and they now account for 27 percent of the country 's total emissions , official statistics show . <p> The government has insisted on the need to prepare the economy for the post-oil era , but at the same time it continues to award a record number of exploration licences . <p> Clearly , it 's tricky to cut off a cash cow that finances a fifth of the budget . <p> " The person who will switch off the lights on the Norwegian continental shelf is not born yet , " Prime Minister Erna Solberg said in late 2018 . <p> phy/po/wai <p> 1/3Norway is one of only a few countries in Europe that has higher greenhouse gas emissions now than in 1990 <p> 2/3 " The person who will switch off the lights on the Norwegian continental shelf is not born yet , " Prime Minister Erna Solberg said in late 2018 <p> 3/3The Johan Sverdrup oil field in the North Sea could become the most productive field in western Europe , the Norwegian Petroleum Directorate says
