<p> In the concluding part of this report , OLADIMEJI RAMONhighlights more areas of the Nigerian court system that will benefit from the use of Information and Communication Technology gadgets <p> A pen and plenty sheets of paper are the closest companions of a typical Nigerian judge in the courtroom . Sitting at the head of a courtroom crowded with lawyers and mountains of case files , the judge patiently listens to each lawyer and writes down their submissions word for word . <p> Many times , in the middle of a case , the judge will pause proceedings to write a ruling on interlocutory arguments . While the judge is busy writing this ruling , lawyers are busy bantering among themselves . The bantering continues until the judge finishes writing the ruling and begins to read it . <p> A Senior Advocate of Nigeria , Dr Babatunde Ajibade , says in this ICT age , this method of writing lawyers ' submissions in longhand is an unnecessary burden on judges . Not only does it waste time , it also wears out the judges , Ajibade notes . <p> He describes it as " a striking example of the causes of delay in the majority of our courts . " <p> " It is a tedious and laborious process that contributes in no small measure to the delays we see , " the SAN says . <p> A Nigerian lawyer , who practises in the United Kingdom , Mr Femi Aina , says the use of a transcription machine will solve this problem . <p> " Honestly , the Nigerian judicial system is anachronistic ; in the 21st century , things are still being done like in the olden days . And that is why judges fall sick often because they work flat out , " he says . <p> Apart from speed , the use of transcription machine makes for better accuracy in the record of court proceedings . <p> Despites its obvious benefits , however , majority of Nigerian courts lack a transcription machine and a stenographer to work with . Until a few months ago when a few other judges acquired it , there was but only one transcription machine at the Federal High Court in Lagos where 12 judges sit . It was a similar situation at the Lagos State High Court in Ikeja as well as in Abuja . <p> Aina notes that in the UK , each judge has a gadget that converts speech to text , even saving judges the trouble of writing their judgments in longhand . <p> He adds that the world has moved away from the practice where lawyers sit in court for hours listening to a judge reading judgments . <p> He says , " Take for instance , what happened during this year 's presidential election petition tribunal ; lawyers sat in court for almost eight/nine hours while judgment was being delivered ( laughs ) . Why ca n't you have a summary of the judgment being read out for 45 minutes and thereafter , the full judgment is uploaded online ? That is how it is done in the UK . <p> " When there was the case about prorogation of Brexit , they read the summary and sent the full judgment online and the moment the judges stood up , the judgment had already been uploaded . " <p> Courts burdened with papers in an electronic age <p> On October 29 , 2019 , the legal team of telecommunication firm , MTN Nigeria Communication Limited , transported copies of a 9,100-page document to the Federal High Court in Lagos in a N3bn lawsuit against the Attorney General of the Federation . For that day , the 9,100-page document was just one out of many papers filed in the court 's registry . <p> The world is in the electronic age , but operations of the Nigerian courts are still largely paper-based . The huge volume of papers being filed is a burden on the court as the papers compete for space . For instance , like any of the other courtrooms at the Federal High Court in Lagos , Court 10 where Justice Nicholas Oweibo sits is tiny and suffocating with piles of files and papers . The stacks of case files are spilling out of metal cabinets and have overtaken the witness stand as well as the dock , where accused persons are meant to stand . <p> Aina believes that Nigerian courts must move away from the era of paper-based operations . <p> " I was really taken aback by the volume of papers in courts here ; it gives you a serious concern and when there is a seminar on climate change tomorrow , Nigerian judges will be the first to arrive there . If seriously we care about the environment , it is high time the judiciary embraced technology , " he says . <p> Aina notes that in the UK , " everything is online . " <p> " If you want to file a motion , there is a standard form and if you go the court 's website in the UK , all court forms have numbers ; if it 's about land , there is a claim form . You will just fill the form online , upload your particulars of claim , you send and pay and get an acknowledgement . It 's as simple as ABC . " <p> A drop in the ocean <p> Though the Nigerian courts have conceived the dream of doing away with paper filing to embrace electronic filing of documents , the dream has not fully materialised . <p> But Lagos-based lawyer , Mr Yemi Omodele , says this e-filing has not reduced the volume of paper being filed in court . <p> He says , " E-filing in Lagos State High Court has not reduced the volume of paper being filed . It is the paper that you file that you take to the e-filing section . They merely scan it and upload onto the website of the court . If you file 1,000 pages , they scan the 1,000 pages . " <p> Omodele says though the high court e-filing has addressed the problem of missing court documents , it has rather slowed down justice administration . <p> " At times , it takes the officials over one hour to scan just a file . In the process of scanning the document , they may end up disorganising the document , putting page one at page 10 , etc . <p> " Because of e-filing , when you file cases in Badagry , Epe , Ikorodu , it may take up to a month to get a permanent suit number , without which the bailiff can not serve the court processes on respondents and the judge can not hear the case , " Omodele says . <p> However , another Lagos-based lawyer , Mr Olukoya Ogungbeje , says the Court of Appeal and the Supreme Court have perfected their e-filing processes and advises that the high court take a cue from them . <p> Ogungbeje says , " E-filing has commenced at the Court of Appeal and the Supreme Court . We now have legal e-mails , which we use now to file processes at the Court of Appeal and the Supreme Court . Every correspondence , including hearing notice , is sent through that email . It was one of the innovations introduced by the former CJN , Justice Walter Onnoghen , and it has been working . <p> " Though we still file papers because it is a new innovation , which is not even up to a year old yet ; I believe that after this test-run stage , it will get to a point where we will no longer file papers . <p> " We are getting there . The Supreme Court has set the pace ; the Court of Appeal has followed suit . It is for the high courts to catch up . " <p> Phasing out era when bailiffs serve court papers <p> Another major cause of delay in Nigerian courts is the time it takes to serve court processes on parties to a lawsuit . This job of serving court papers is done by the court bailiffs . They travel distances to physically serve the court papers on the parties . When they return , they put a Proof of Service in the case file . Without this Proof of Service , the judge can not entertain the case . Bailiffs often run into difficulty where the person they are to serve the court paper on is evasive . This development is a common cause of delay in the hearing of cases in court . <p> But apart from being time-consuming , the use of bailiff to serve processes is also fraught with corruption and inefficiency . Lawyers say except they are bribed , the court bailiffs will abandon the court processes " till Thine kingdom come . " A Lawyer , Mr Tope Alabi , recalled how his court papers were once abandoned for eight months until he bribed a court bailiff with N2,000 . <p> " There was a time that I filed a suit in my name and I paid the official service fee and was expecting the bailiff to do what he was statutorily employed to do , which is serving court processes . This bailiff did not serve my process for good eight months . He would tell me that he went to look for the respondent , but he had not been seeing the man . I kept saying that this man resumes to work before 8am , how come you do n't see him ? Then , the other bailiffs said , ' Lawyer , our friend has spoken ; he does n't see the man , you are the only one seeing him . ' <p> " So , at that point , I gave the bailiff N2,000 . I had not reached PWD ( about five minutes drive from the high court ) when that bailiff phoned me that he had served the process on the man . Then I asked , ' How come you saw him now ? ' He then told me that after I gave him that money , the scale that covered his eyes just fell off and he was able to see clearly , " Alabi narrates to this reporter . <p> Aina says with online filing of processes in the UK , a lawyer needs not meet with bailiffs to get court processes served . <p> He says , " The moment you pay , that payment goes to the court ; and the moment you issue your processes everything is in the hands of the court . Whether the court has a bailiff to serve the process is not your business . Your process is in the court . <p> " But when you have these two layers of going to the issuing office , they issue ; and you go to the bailiff , so that they can serve , it boils down to exploitation and it is a restriction on access to justice . The system needs to be effective to be respected . " <p> Ogungbeje said with the advent of legal email at the Court of Appeal and the Supreme Court , the era when bailiffs wield so much power may be coming to an end . <p> " At the Supreme Court , the bailiff work has drastically reduced . Now hearing notices are served through the legal email , so they do n't need any bailiff to go and be looking for anybody to serve them with the court processes , " Ogungbeje says . <p> He notes that though the Lagos State High Court and the Federal High Court have also started looking in that direction by providing for legal email in their 2019 Civil Procedure Rules , the legal email is not yet functional . <p> While introducing the Lagos State Civil Procedure Rules 2019 last year , the Chief Judge of Lagos State , Justice Kazeem Alogba , said the time wasted on the service of court papers had a negative effect on the appraisal of the judges . <p> " Six months in the life of a case , we are still talking about inability to serve and time keeps running such that fundamental human rights cases last two years , sometimes more , in court . And when you file your returns , and the supervising authorities see a fundamental human right case that has spent three years before a judge , they 'll ask , ' What was he doing ? ' As if it was the fault of the judge , " Alogba said . <p> He said to address the problem , " we have now latched onto emerging trends in technology . All means of electronic communication , once verifiable , will be allowed in Proof of Service of processes . This is to ensure that difficulty in service is no longer a reason for delays in our courts . " <p> Nigerian court 's poor case management <p> In Nigerian courts , it is common for a judge to have 20 cases listed for hearing in a day . But between the court hours of 9am and 4pm , the judge may not attend to more than half of the cases . He later sends the unattended lawyers away to return another day . <p> At other times , lawyers travel long distances , including from one state to another , only to get to court and be told that the judge is not sitting for the day . <p> Aina wonders why in an age when mobile phones are cheap and common , the court can not send text messages to lawyers to save them the time and money wasted in this process . <p> He wonders if the Nigerian court system is designed to impoverish lawyers . He says a modern day lawyer does not have the luxury of sitting in court all day , when he can be in his office attending to clients and making money . <p> He says , " When the judge is not going to sit , maybe he is going for a conference or the judge is sick , the clerk has a diary ; why ca n't the clerk pick the phone and inform lawyers not to come to court ? <p> " In Nigeria , we should be able to give lawyers specific time to come to court ; there is no point in all lawyers turning up in court in the morning and sitting down all day , looking stressed . <p> " There is no point listing 20 cases for hearing before one judge when at the end of the day , the judge will eventually have to adjourn 15 of them . <p> " It is unfair on the lawyers because that time , they can sit in their offices and be making money . It is unfair on the client , who will pay an appearance fee to the lawyer , notwithstanding that the case could not go on that day . " <p> Mr Ope Olugasa , the Managing Director of LawPavilion -- an IT firm with vision to revolutionalise the Nigerian judiciary with technology -- says the need for the introduction of technological tools to manage cases in Nigerian courts is obvious . <p> He adds that this must be complemented with the training of both the judges and their support staff . <p> " Our judicial officers need to be properly trained in the practical use of available technology tools that truly add value , showing them the possibilities , " he says . <p> Lack of finance , a stumbling block <p> Olugasa , an engineer , says his experience working with the judiciary is that the system is open to the use of technology " but the constraint of funds is quite limiting . " <p> " The number one general constraint is funds . Most courts complain of lack of funds to buy the much-needed tools required to oil the wheel of justice . As long as they still rely on the Executive for allocation of funds , this issue might still linger for some time , " Olugasa says . <p> Also , Ajibade identifies inadequate resources as the major stumbling block in the quest by the Nigerian courts to embrace technology . <p> He also calls for a change of mindset among the justice sector stakeholders . <p> The SAN says , " To eliminate delay , we will require a significant investment in the hardware and in training of the personnel that should deliver this service . It will also require a change in the mindset of judicial officers . " <p> The Chairman of the Nigerian Bar Association 's Section on Business Law , Mr Seni Adio ( SAN ) , says the fact that the Supreme Court has since 2012 introduced the Nigerian Judiciary Information Technology Policy is evidence that the system is not averse to technology . <p> " The policy acknowledges the significance of technology and , for example , videoconferencing to aid the obtaining of information from experts , witnesses located outside the jurisdiction of the courts and , even parties , who may not be able to be physically in the courtroom due to exigent circumstances . " <p> Adio says the fact that the Evidence Act 2011 was silent on videoconferencing is no excuse not to allow it . <p> To make up for the lacuna in the Evidence Act , Justice Alogba said the Lagos State High Court Civil Procedure Rules 2019 , which became effective January this year , has now made provisions for the use of videoconferencing to take evidence of witnesses who are out of the court 's jurisdiction . <p> Alluding to the problem of finance , the Chief Judge , " As of now , we do n't have a generalised infrastructure for videoconferencing but in the way we have done it , we can start like that and build on it and when we have infrastructure from government , it will be a general thing in all the courts and probably in our magistrates ' courts too . " <p> We 'll digitalise courts , says Lagos <p> The Attorney General of Lagos State and Commissioner for Justice , Mr Moyosore Onigbanjo ( SAN ) , says the state is ready to invest in court gadgets that will phase out paper operations by 2020 . <p> He says , " We 're going to support the judges by using the latest technology to assist them in their work . <p> " Our goal is that before this administration leaves office , the courts should be completely paperless as we are deploying technology which is available in other parts of the world . <p> " We do n't want to see our judges writing down in longhand again . We want to ensure that when people go to court , relying on audiovisual will not be a difficult thing . <p> " This administration wants to ensure that Lagos has the best digitally-enhanced courts in Africa . " <p> Aina says it is in the best interest of the legal profession that the courts embrace technology without further delays . <p> " If the Nigerian judiciary continues this way , they fail to embrace technology to make the court user-friendly and reduce the stress on the judges , what will happen in the long run is that legal practice in Nigeria will become yesterday 's business , " the lawyer says . <p> Copyright PUNCH.All rights reserved .
