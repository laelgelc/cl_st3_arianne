<h> Denial or inaction more criminal ? <p> I read Dr Jarrod Gilbert 's " Why climate denial should be a criminal offence " piece in The New Zealand Herald on July 26 . <p> He said to argue against such overwhelming scientific consensus is to perpetrate a dangerous fraud . <p> I agree . However , let 's forget the deniers for now and ask why the rest of us refuse to act on the advice of 97 percent of climate scientists -- along with environmentalists , religious leaders and others . <p> * In January at the Tolaga Bay Climate Change Hui , a Ministry for the Environment spokesman excused our country 's weak commitment with words to the effect that New Zealand produces only a small percentage of global GHG emissions and wo n't make a substantial difference to global warming . <p> Prior to the last election , our National-led Government refused to commit to a legally-binding target for the Kyoto Protocol , and produced ridiculously low emissions targets for Paris . <p> The Earth continues warming at an alarming rate , and fossil fuel-burning continues to increase . <p> In April a surge in demand for air travel was reported . Last year aircraft produced 12 percent of all transport emissions , accounting for 781 million tonnes of CO2 . <p> Cars dominate transport emissions , and this worsens as congestion and travelling times within our cities steadily increase . <p> Many believe electric and driverless cars are long-term answers to maintaining our present , highly mobile lifestyles . The reality I 'm afraid is more simple -- we will be forced to adopt more locally-based lifestyles as our eco-damaging fossil fuel age fades . <p> Solar , wind , tidal , biofuels and even nuclear power will only deliver a fraction of the energy we use now . <p> In January 2013 , Victoria University climate scientist Dr Jim Renwick said countries like New Zealand needed to slash emissions by at least half this decade and bring them down to zero by the end of the century to limit global warming to 2C . <p> With less than three-and-a-half years of this decade left , when are we going to start ? <p> Dr Renwick also expressed concern about what kind of world we leave our grandchildren . <p> Our commitment here in Tairawhiti is incredibly weak as well . Fossil fuel madness predominates and there is no sign of easing in our food miles impact . <p> Livestock and milk travel hundreds of kilometres for processing , where they end up in plastic containers , plastic wrappings and plastic bags everlasting . <p> With our rail link mothballed , our district 's transport options have been reduced to high carbon-emitting heavy transport and air travel .
