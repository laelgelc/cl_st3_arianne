<h> Ian Foster - good enough for NZ Rugby <p> Jim Kayes says Ian Foster was a good steady first-five for Waikato who could make a good steady All Black head coach . <p> It 's evolution , not revolution ; and it 's hardly surprising . <p> This is New Zealand Rugby we are talking about , the same organisation that quietly appointed a member of its board to be it 's next chief executive . <p> Little wonder Mark Robinson was on the panel that decided Steve Hansen 's assistant should replace him as head coach of the All Blacks . <p> Ian Foster has a two-year gig and is the third successive head coach who has n't played for the famous team . <p> He was always the favourite , despite the semifinal loss to England at the World Cup - because all the talk of other coaches dropping out and that it was an embarrassingly short list of contenders , ignored the inherent conservativeness of NZR . <p> Scott Roberton may have been the people 's choice but the 45-year-old surfer from Tauranga who breakdances when his teams win titles ( and they have won plenty ) was a square peg for NZR 's round hole . <p> He was the radical 's choice and it could be argued that having had largely the same people involved in the All Blacks for 15 years , something radical was required . <p> The NZR is as far from radical as it comes . <p> Instead the panellists have predictably gone for safety , for a known quantity , for a continuation of a coaching dynasty that began under Graham Henry in 2004 and continued under Hansen in 2012 and that has been , it must be said , pretty successful . <p> They 've won two World Cups and lost two , beaten and drawn with the British and Irish Lions and dominated the game whether playing in the Rugby Championship or on their November tours . <p> It speaks volumes for their sustained success that defeats still attract huge attention because they are so few and usually so far between . <p> Things went massively awry in 2007 when the All Blacks were knocked out of the World Cup in the quarterfinals but when Henry convinced NZR that he , Hansen and Wayne Smith were still the best options , Foster was always in the box seat , despite his group 's loss to England in Yokohama . <p> Foster , 54 , has remained silent on his head coaching aspirations until today when he acknowledged it was a huge privilege to be named as head coach . <p> But Hansen made it clear he favoured continuity in the role and argued that those calling for a refresh would still get that under Foster . <p> That 's because others in the coaching and management team are leaving too and Foster will bring in at least three new assistants , one of whom is expected to be Hurricanes head coach John Plumtree . <p> Hansen also argues Foster is his own man , he 's not a Hansen clone and he will take the All Blacks in a different direction . <p> That remains to be seen . <p> Foster is certainly not an overwhelming favourite with the public and it will be interesting to see how he copes with the pressure if he does n't enjoy early success . <p> He has witnessed , from close range , the white-hot scrutiny that goes with the job and as , Wayne Smith found out , some people are better suited to being a few steps outside the intense spotlight that goes with being head coach of the All Blacks . <p> Foster is a good bloke and an interesting fella to deal with . He has a great sense of humour and can be as cheeky and funny as Hansen . <p> He is intelligent and thinks carefully before he speaks . <p> Interviewing him for television is unsettling , as there will almost always be a ( in TV terms ) lengthy pause before he answers questions as he works through what he wants to say . <p> Dealing with the media , which is a huge part of the job , he is more likely to bark where Hansen bit , but can be just as fun and engaging when things are going well . <p> His detractors point to his limited success with the Chiefs , when he took them to the final just once in more than 100 games in charge , as evidence he should never have got the job . <p> Those same critics probably said the same thing when he was named assistant coach . <p> Hansen and All Blacks great Grant Fox , who became a selector when Foster came on as an assistant coach in 2012 , say Foster is a massively improved coach since those days with the Chiefs . <p> Cory Jane once told me Foster is the best coach he played under and there are plenty of others who praise his abilities . <p> What 's not yet known is whether Foster has the ruthless edge required to succeed . <p> Head coaches need to know when to cuddle and when to cuff , when a player needs a pat on the back or a kick up the bum , when a quiet word is needed or a public dressing down . <p> They have to be parental and also be pricks , operating in that weird world where players are mates that may need to be dropped . <p> Foster is Waikato 's most capped player with 148 games and he has his photo on the wall in the tunnel at Waikato Stadium . But he was never an All Black . <p> He was n't as flash as Frano Botica or as polished and prolific as Fox - the two men who initially kept him out of the national side . <p> He was n't as quick and audaciously talented as Carlos Spencer and Andrew Mehrtens who played ahead of him later in his career . <p> He was a steady , quality first-five . <p> There is absolutely no disgrace in that and Foster may yet prove to be a very good head coach of the All Blacks . <p> He is evolution , when revolution was on offer . <p> We value fearless , independent journalism .
