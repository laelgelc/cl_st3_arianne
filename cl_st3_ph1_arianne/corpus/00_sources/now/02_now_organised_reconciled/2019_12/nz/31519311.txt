<h> Singles going steady <p> Jorden Lahood makes coffee at Big Fig , where staff use the coffee machine as a billboard to spread the SUCfree message . Photo : Gina Dempster <p> You may still be able to get a single shot in Wanaka in 2022 , but not in a single-use cup , writes Gina Dempster . <p> Coffee , it 's the legal fix that gets us through the hustle and bustle of our day . But a Wanaka hospitality initiative is inviting coffee drinkers to take a few minutes to smell the roses so Wanaka can become the first town in New Zealand to be free of single-use coffee cups . <p> Wanaka Hospo-Goes-Eco is a group of Wanaka cafes who got together after the 2018 One Summit , Wanaka 's climate change summit . Their goal : to make Wanaka 's cafe culture more sustainable . Their first target : single-use coffee cups . Supported by Plastic Free Wanaka , they set the ambitious goal of making Wanaka single-use cup free ( SUCfree ) by 2022 . <p> At present , about 1,024,000 single-use cups go to landfill from Wanaka each year . <p> In October 2019 , the group officially launched Wanaka SUCfree at the 2019 One Summit . <p> Reusable cups get crochet stylings . Photo : Jana Reulecke <p> The " sit , bring or borrow " campaign has been incredibly successful . <p> Only a handful of cafes have gone completely SUCfree around the country , as any unhappy customers are likely to take their business elsewhere . <p> Cardrona skifield is one business that successfully adopted a SUCfree policy for the past ski season . At the launch of Wanaka SUCfree , its success was put down to providing a range of alternatives for customers and support and training for staff at the front line of the change . <p> The support of cafe staff for Wanaka SUCfree is driving the campaign faster than was originally planned . <p> Some of the Wanaka cafes involved are on the verge of eradicating takeaway cups for this summer . <p> Big Fig front of house manager Jorden Lahood said the staff were totally behind the change . <p> " We have a lot of young staff , and they all care about the planet . We 're all psyched for anything like this . " <p> The staff have had several meetings on how best to support their customers through the change , and how to handle any difficult conversations . <p> " We 're figuring out how to have that conversation in a positive way , " she says . <p> Offering customers a range of ways to enjoy their coffee without a takeaway cup helps . <p> " We 're trying to make it as simple as possible for people . " <p> The Wanaka SUCfree campaign encourages people to avoid single-use cups by taking one of three actions : sit , bring or borrow . <p> Plastic Free Wanaka marketing co-ordinator Jana Reulecke says taking five minutes to enjoy a coffee and the beautiful surroundings can add to the value people get from the transaction . <p> " It 's the best thing you can actually do , in a place like this . People might ask themselves , ' Why am I in a rush ? ' . Sit down , take a minute and enjoy your coffee . " <p> If sitting is a step too far , then standing might be more comfortable for customers in a rush . Big Fig is planning to build a standing bar at the front of the cafe to encourage customers to drink before they go . <p> " People can still feel like they 're on the go , " Jorden says . " People might have a quick chat . We have the big open doors which look out over the lake . We want to build that culture . " <p> But giving up single-use cups is not just about taking time to enjoy your coffee . Many customers , especially tourists , used to pay more at Big Fig for a single-use cup then drank their coffee in the cafe . <p> Jorden says some planned to take it away , then changed their minds . But others prefered the feeling of a cardboard cup in their mouth . <p> " It 's habitual . " <p> But there are all sorts of reusable alternatives for people to embrace , she says . If you do n't like drinking out of cafe crockery , you can bring a glass , ceramic or stainless steel cup . People can change habits quickly once they accept the need to change . <p> " We did it with plastic bags , and it happened so quickly , but nobody 's feeling the pain , " says Jorden . " Coming together as a community is going to be the most important and impactful thing to make SUCfree Wanaka normal . If we all get on board together , we could be an entire town where single-use cups just do n't exist anymore . " <p> Grafitti'ing the coffee machine is one of the ways the Big Fig staff spread the message . " Did you know takeaway cups can not be recycled ! " it reads . <p> Ben Quinn , the staff member in charge of environmental sustainability , researches the facts , once staff have discussed which messages it would be useful to focus on . <p> " People are confused about what happens to single-use coffee cups , " says Jorden . <p> " They can see they 're made of cardboard , and they think they can be recycled . " <p> In fact , single-use cups ca n't be recycled . To hold coffee and other hot liquids , single-use cups are lined with plastic and all are destined for landfill . <p> Yet many single-use cups are put into recycling bins , contributing to high contamination levels in public bin recycling . <p> The game-breaker for Wanaka cafes is offering a " borrow " option . Twelve cafes have got together to support a returnable cup system . For a $3 deposit , customers get an Again Again stainless steel cup to take away . The cup can be returned ( clean or dirty ) to any of the participating cafes , and the $3 deposit redeemed . <p> Wanaka broke all Again Again 's records in the first three weeks of the scheme , when 2225 cups were borrowed . Some will be with tourists who have left town but the great thing about Again Again is that it 's a national scheme , so a cup borrowed in Wanaka can be returned at participating cafes in Te Anau , Glenorchy , Christchurch , Wellington or Auckland . <p> Jana , of Plastic Free Wanaka , is working closely with cafes and promoting the scheme on social media . Her Wanaka SUCfree 2022 work is funded for six months by a grant from Queenstown Lakes District Council 's Waste Minimisation Community Fund . <p> She believes the cup-borrowing scheme fits in well with a shift by younger generations to own less stuff . <p> " There 's a shift to a borrowing society , where we do n't need to own so much stuff -- how cool is that ? Cars and bikes , that generation is happy to rent or borrow them when they need them . " <p> Again Again cups come with a single-use cardboard sleeve to make them easier to handle . The sleeves can be recycled , but waste-free advocates have questioned whether a single-use element makes sense in a reusable cup scheme . <p> Jana says because the cups are made from a single layer of metal , the sleeves play a functional role for coffee without milk , as otherwise the cups would be too hot to hold . <p> Creative people are already finding reusable alternatives to the single-use sleeves , including adorable crocheted animal sleeves . And that 's what SUCfree Wanaka is all about , creative solutions that are more appealing than the status quo . <p> " It 's not about shaming people , " says Jana . " We want it to be a positive experience . " <p> Jorden agrees and believes going SUCfree will add to Wanaka 's appeal . She says Wanaka can be a leader by offering experiences instead of stuff . <p> " We can say to visitors , this is the way we operate . Look at our beautiful town , have a beautiful time . Enjoy it . "
