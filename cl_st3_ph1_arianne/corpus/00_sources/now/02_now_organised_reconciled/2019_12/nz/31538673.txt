<h> Why human beings need to resist the machines <p> Author Paul Mason says the world order is being ripped to shreds by an alliance of ethnic nationalists , women-haters and authoritarian leaders who are harnessing the power of machines and algorithms to do it . <p> Our humanity is being stripped away , Mason says . He believes in the last 30 years we have created " selves " that are defenceless in the modern world . <p> " We created a kind of self that was good at choosing between latte and cappuccino in a coffee shop , but very bad at making moral decisions , " Mason says . <p> Having spent his career at the front line of reporting politics and economics , Mason says something more fundamental is amiss . <p> " I 've got this hunch it 's no longer just about politics and economics , it 's about deciding who you want to be deciding what a human being is , and what the value of a human being is . <p> " So many of the adversaries of democracy , the rule of law and universal human rights , want to indeed stratify us into the worthy the ones that can be saved , the ones that should have privilege , and the ones that ca n't . " <p> The rise of Fascist ideas has some parallels with the 1930s , he says with one important difference . <p> " We 've got a very high level of human development now . We have a highly educated society , the elites who perpetrated fascism and Nazism in Europe had total control , and of course Stalinism in Russia , had total control over the means of communication . They do n't now , we have networks , we can we can share our own ideas , we can orginate ideas , and it 's very difficult to suppress ideas . " <p> To have this level of incipient Fascism with this level of human development , is a new problem , Mason says . <p> People 's attachment to democracy is " frighteningly fragile , " he says <p> " In my country , 52 percent of people just polled say they want a strong leader who breaks all the rules . I mean , this is a country that 's got its problems , but this desire for authority is something that I think is certainly new in my lifetime . " <p> The subtitle of his book is ; ' A Radical Defence of the Human Being ' . <p> " I 'm trying to say that if you are to defend yourself against anti-humanists , anti-universalists - this is the other thing right wing authoritarian thought that says blacks are sub-human , that people of colour are subhuman , this is what is swirling around as on the internet - if you want to defend yourself against that , you have to have a theory of human beings . " <p> Something secular , utilitarian society lacks , he says . <p> " Religion has a theory of human beings . It says they 're all sacred , and they 're all the same . They 're all equally valid . But for many of us in secular society , who are not religious , we 're disarmed against this problem , unless we ourselves have a theory of human nature , which is rooted in , I argue , our ability as human beings to free ourselves . " <p> Mason says this is not a popular opinion in academia . <p> " You 'll hear again and again , human beings are just a bag of bones , DNA and their experiences , their entire behaviour is predetermined , they have no free will , no agency , no freedom . <p> " This is this is almost the orthodoxy of our time , shared on the left as well as the right . " <p> Mason says the book is an attempt to " argue in favour of human agency and our ability to rediscover it . " <p> Fatalism is the enemy , Mason says . <p> " Judeo Christianity and Islam all taught us that we do have choices to make , but the problem is in a secular society , on what basis do you defend that ? And I want to advocate that . " <p> The " performative lifestyle " adopted under 30 years of neoliberalism has morally weakened us , Mason says . <p> " The market is a great taskmaster , because it never asks me what do I believe ? And you can tick as many boxes for progressive hiring , quotas or women on boards but there 's no tick box that says , here 's what I believe . " <p> When things turned for the worse post-2008 this revealed itself to be a charade , he says . <p> " It turns out that all the people ticking the boxes for progressive policies harboured a bunch of neo-colonialist , white racist , misogynist ideas which incidentally are always perpetrated and peddled by popular culture . " <p> A new moral thinking is needed , says Mason . <p> " Moral thinking is about having a theory of human beings . Markets do n't have a theory of human beings , other than they 're acquisitive and can choose between latte and cappuccino . <p> " Machines do n't and the more we hand over to machines - all Facebook is is a machine that controls your behaviour - we have to fight to force these companies , first of all to obey regulation , but second to have ideas about morals and ethics . " <p> The existential threats posed by climate change require internationalism , not separatism , he says . <p> " You ca n't turn the corner unless you have a global system , inadequate and flawed though it is , a multilateral system of mutual obligations , whether it be the WTO or the Paris Climate accord itself which is essential for dealing with this global problem . <p> Photo : Juergen-Bauer <p> " But what is every single right wing authoritarian force trying to do ? They 're trying to tear apart the global system , whether it 's Trump with Paris , Trump , walking away from the WTO , whether it 's Brexit with Britain , walking away from Europe . <p> " We ca n't deal with any problem unless we try and reform and improve the global system . "
