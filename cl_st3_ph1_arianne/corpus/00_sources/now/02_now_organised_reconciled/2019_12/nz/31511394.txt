<h> Lower producing methane sheep garners worldwide attention <p> News of a world-first tool that gives New Zealand sheep farmers the ability to breed animals that emit less methane has been positively received locally and internationally . <p> Since the methane breeding value was launched in early December , good numbers of breeders have been looking for more information and enquiring about having their flocks measured , says Pastoral Greenhouse Gas Research Consortium ( PGGRC ) general manager Mark Aspin . <p> " We have had international interest from Australia , UK and Spain on the story , " he told Rural News . <p> It follows a 10-year multi-million dollar collaboration between the Pastoral Greenhouse Gas Research Consortium ( PGGRC ) , New Zealand Agricultural Greenhouse Gas Research Centre ( NZAGRC ) and AgResearch , supported by the Ministry of Business , Innovation and Employment and Ministry for Primary Industries . <p> Ram breeders wanting to pursue the methane breeding value will need to measure a portion of their flock using portable accumulation chambers . These chambers are on board an AgResearch-operated trailer which travels to individual farms . <p> Sheep spend 50 minutes in the chambers , where their gas emissions are measured . <p> This happens twice , at a 14-day interval . The resulting information is then used alongside other genetic information to calculate the methane breeding value . <p> Aspin says they launched the tool now because they have a sound scientific basis to introduce the trait to breeders . <p> He describes it as a major breakthrough because it 's a method of directly reducing methane produced by sheep grazing . <p> " The impacts are small -- only 1-2 % reduction a generation , but genetics is cumulative so it will build on itself in time . " <p> Overall though it wo n't make a huge difference to climate change as " that relies on the radical reduction in the use of fossil fuels by humans , " Aspin said . <p> He says the new breeding value takes advantage of the fact that individual sheep vary in their levels of methane emission and these differences are passed on to the next generation . <p> " This is a global first for any species of livestock . Launching the methane breeding value gives New Zealand 's sheep sector a practical tool to help lower our agricultural greenhouse gases . This is significant . Up until now , the only option available to farmers wanting to lower their greenhouse gas emissions has been to constantly improve their overall farming efficiency . <p> " This takes us a step further -- towards actually lowering sheep methane emissions , in keeping with the sector 's commitment to work towards reducing its greenhouse emissions . " <p> Aspin says it is important to note that the biggest influence on methane emissions is the amount of feed an animal eats . <p> Interested farmers will have access to rams within two years -- the time it will take to breed and grow rams on a commercial scale . <p> Aspin says it takes two years from selection of sires to seeing progeny on the ground , which is a normal generation interval .
