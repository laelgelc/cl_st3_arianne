<h> Peters ' admires courage ' of Hong Kong protesters <p> The Government has finally issued a response on the Hong Kong protests , with Foreign Minister Winston Peters saying he admired the courage of the protestors and that the " one country , two systems " rule must be upheld . <p> New Zealand has yet to issue a formal response to the unrest in Hong Kong , which was sparked by an extradition law that appeared to violate the territory 's autonomy by allowing Hong Kongers to be extradited to mainland China to face trial . <p> Since Hong Kong was handed back to China from the United Kingdom in 1997 , it has been governed by " one country , two systems " . This means that while Hong Kong is technically a part of China , it has been allowed to preserve its own freedoms like freedom of the press , freedom of association , and an independent judiciary . <p> That agreement was meant to expire 50 years after handover in 2047 , when Hong Kong will be fully absorbed into China . <p> Winston Peters was present at the handover ceremony in 1997 , when Prince Charles , then-Prime Minister Tony Blair , and the last Governor of Hong Kong , Chris Patten , formally handed the territory back to China . <p> Patten 's speech touched on the political independence of Hong Kong , and has often been harked back to during the protest . <p> " Hong Kong people are to run Hong Kong , " Patten said . <p> " That is the promise . And that is the unshakeable destiny , " he said . <p> Peters said he remembered the speech . <p> " That 's what this protest is about and you have to admire their courage . <p> " I recall what was promised to them in 1991 the first Legislative Council election and that 's all they expect and I 'm sure that 's all the world expects , " he said . <p> Asked why New Zealand had yet to make a formal statement on the protests in Hong Kong - unlike Australia , the United Kingdom , and the Untied States - Peters said his remarks constituted a formal statement . <p> We value fearless , independent journalism .
