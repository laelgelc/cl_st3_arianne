<p> by Ian Apperley A bumbling year for the pre-election Wellington City Council . Transport , housing , Shelly Bay , the airport expansionism , and climate change were all hot topics , and little progress was made . Instead , the city was off on flights of fancy with the election effectively stopping any movement forward for months . <p> However the Eastern suburbs are pumping , and with so much activity out here because of the film industry and the airport , the traffic is far worse than previous years . <p> Many of us who used to work in the city have now opted to work at home , a growing trend . It 's cheaper , less stressful , and more productive . Tradies are being driven nuts by the now normal 45 -- 60 minute commute at either end of the day , with tailbacks heading deep into Miramar and Seatoun at peak . <p> There 's no relief in sight for that . With a broken bus system , the number of workers who have gone out and bought a $1,000 car to get where they need to go has skyrocketed . Mechanics out east have never been busier , with more cars on the road each week . You can expect a week 's wait for a Warrant of Fitness these days and another week if anything needs to be fixed . <p> Housing prices and rent are rapidly changing the demographics of the suburbs . People can not afford to buy here generally and rent increases are pushing lower-income families and individuals out of the area . If you want an idea of the madness of the increases , then look no further than my property to highlight it . <p> A modest , 100 square meter , ex-state house on the edge of Strathmore that we bought a few years back , my property has increased in value by over $100k this year . It had a $20,000 increase in the last month . Prices are n't slowing , they are super-heating , and it ca n't be sustainable . Of course , that 's all trapped capital that ca n't be used . <p> If we were buying today , it would be up the coast or in the Wairarapa . It would be impossible , even with a decent wage , to buy anything other than a single bedroom unit in Wellington . <p> The cost of living has increased significantly as well , I suspect right across the city . Some of that is due to wider national factors -- however anyone who pays a rates bill will be hurting , with some properties attracting multi-thousand dollar increases this year . Wellington is becoming one of the most expensive places to live and work in . <p> All that price pressure is changing the demographics of the area , with lower-income families pushed out the door and those with money moving in . While central government is creating more housing where it can , it 's a minuscule amount in terms of the demand here . <p> The city council has no control over this or chooses not to control it . Untangling the district plan development rules to allow more housing is not going to help when those houses are unaffordable to most people . Unfortunately , as money moves in , it seeks to retain the capital it has invested in housing , so we can expect that any plans to introduce infill housing , affordable housing , or similar measures will meet stiff opposition . <p> Shelly Bay continues to slowly fall into the harbour with no easy resolution in sight . Everyone wants something to happen with the site , but no one wants a block of expensive apartments dominating a pretty little bay . Worse , the tangle around development , who bought what , which councillors voted or did n't , and the the ongoing struggle of Mau Whenua have created a bird 's nest that wo n't be unravelled for a decade . <p> Hunter S Thompson once said ; " Never create anything , it will be misinterpreted , it will chain you and follow you the rest of your life . " <p> Never has this been truer than the City Council 's ill-thought-out declaration of a Climate Emergency without an actual plan to do anything . The declaration haunts every press release , every story , every move that the council makes . It has made the council seem hypocritical and will be used as a beating stick at every opportunity by the entire political spectrum . <p> And it is hypocritical . You ca n't declare an emergency when you own a third of the airport , when you are encouraging more and more tourists to visit , subsidising Singapore Airlines , allowing development in areas that will end up underwater , spending over a hundred million on a 1950 's convention centre , not sorting out traffic , and a host of other activities that are the antithesis of the pledge you made . <p> That Thompson quote was number one of seven that he wrote as advice to everyone . The Council has also adopted Thompson 's fifth rule , which is : " If ever asked to look at yourself , do n't . " <p> Transparency bottomed out this year . Leaking has become the norm and infighting at times boiled over into the media . Consultation is now considered to be a joke and when given the opportunity to examine their own processes , Shelly Bay as an example , the Council has chosen to vote it down . <p> Except for some good operators and the hope that some new faces bring , the Council has been a failure for the city this year , in my opinion . And it is going to suffer the fires of a general election next year . Social media trolls , bullies , and those on the extreme left and right are already active , and I 'd encourage people to tune out as early as possible . <p> It is time for a revolution , though we all know that wo n't happen . <p> My prediction is that National will form a government after the election . I have no political allegiances of my own ; over the years I have worked with and been consulted by most major parties other than NZ First , who I am quite sure think that technology is witchcraft . But barring a minor miracle I think we 'll see a swap at the end of next year . <p> That could drag the house prices down , as the government is culled back . It will also affect Wellington 's economy in general , as costs are cut . Mind you , if they win , construction will be back on the agenda with more motorways and tunnels mooted . <p> Next year for Karene and myself is one of change . Both of us grew up on and around farms ; some of my earliest work was as a farmhand . We 've decided to fly the coop and find a block of land somewhere in eastern Wairarapa . Not an easy task as it turns out . Half of Wellington is looking at the same thing as the city becomes a less attractive and more expensive place to live . We could sit on a lot of locked-up capital or we can release it and try and make the world a better place at least on a minor scale . <p> Growing sustainable food , living sustainably in general , not being stuck in traffic , and putting some good back into the country is appealing . <p> So , here 's to you and yours . I genuinely hope you have a decent break over the holidays and thank you for your support this year . I am constantly surprised by the consistently positive , if sometimes challenging , comments and feedback . You are all helping me to become a better writer , and I have to single out Wellington Scoop 's Lindsay Shelton , who has continued to patiently edit my work , buy me lunch once in a while and mentor me , while inspiring me to keep creating content . <p> See you next year . <p> Scoop readers - Help to support quality , independent news &amp; journalism that is freely available to the public .
