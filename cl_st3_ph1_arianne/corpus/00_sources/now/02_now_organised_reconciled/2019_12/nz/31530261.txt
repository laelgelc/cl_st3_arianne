<h> Episode 10 : Why your iPhone is a deflationary engine <p> The world changed in 2007 when Apples ' Steve Jobs held up the first iPhone . Inflation dived , interest rates collapsed and share prices took off . <p> Newsroom Pro 's managing editor Bernard Hickey says billions of people are carrying a deflationary engine in their pocket . <p> " They 've effectively driven down prices in all sorts of areas , " he says . <p> Take taxis , which are being replaced with Uber or Zoomy connect to drivers and share rides in a way that reduces cost . In turn that has has driven down the cost of transport , and competes with public transport . <p> We send messages -- free - with WhatsApp . Airbnb has introduced a whole new supply of rooms into the hotel market and depressed prices there . There 's no need to buy a camera any more -- you can use the one on your phone . <p> Hickey says billions of users of smart phones have effectively doubled again the size of the global workforce . <p> " You can use your phone to access services you 've never had available in the country before , " he says . <p> " And not only have prices dropped , in some cases they 've disappeared . They seem free at least -- of course we 're paying for them with privacy . " <p> Hickey says we are using our phones in a really aggressive way , in a reach far more than just talking to people on them . The use of GPS , cameras , and the ability to use data networks has infected the global economy in a way that continues to surprise officials and economists . <p> " They 're continuing to forecast inflation higher than we actually get , " he says . <p> " Part of the reason is this structural change in how the global economy is worked . <p> " You do have tactical inflation which happens when maybe the price of oil goes up ... or when swine fever forces pork prices up . When things have recovered the prices will go back to normal . " <p> That 's what happened when of billions of iPhones were rolled out at fast pace all around the world . It has changed the structure of industries , changed the nature of work , and changed the price of goods and services . <p> Economists call it a supply shock , when a whole bunch of extra supply of the thing you want has landed on the market . <p> When inflation falls below a certain level the Reserve Bank reacts by cutting the interest rates to try to stimulate the economy . <p> Hickey says that was one of the reasons the Reserve Bank Governor this week cut the official cash rate -- the rate of interest it charges on loans to commercial banks - to a record low of 1.5 percent . <p> " It 's like oiling the wheels of the economy , " he says . <p> The Detail was made possible by the RNZ/NZ On Air Innovation Fund . <p> We value fearless , independent journalism .
