<h> Combating the climate change deniers <p> If you find yourself in an argument with climate change-denying relatives this Christmas , here 's the ammunition you need . <p> If you had n't noticed , the holiday season has come around again . <p> It 's a time for festive cheer , good kai , and family . <p> But it 's also the time when relatives with questionable stances on certain matters come out of the woodwork . <p> And with 2019 looking to be one of the hottest years on record , do n't be surprised if the small matter of climate change comes up . <p> So , to help you deal with any difficult discussions , The Detail spoke to University of Auckland 's Nick Matzke . He 's a senior lecturer in biological studies - with a lot of experience in pseudoscience and denialism . <p> " I actually worked in the States for a non-profit called the National Centre for Science Education . That was the only non-profit in the US that was devoted to teaching strong science in the public schools of the US , especially evolution and climate change science . " <p> Given his experience , what does Matzke believe drives denialism ? <p> " The main thing is inconvenient facts . Facts that , if true and if you accept them , require some sort of action from you or change in your complacence . <p> " One way to react is to do something about it ... and so another route to go if facts prove inconvenient to you is to try and deny them . " <p> Listen to the podcast to hear Matzke dispel some of the more popular climate myths -- the ' natural cycle ' , ' lack of consensus ' , ' unreliable records ' among them . <p> But if you 're short on time , Matzke has another way to get the message across to climate change deniers who pop up at Christmas . <p> " My recommendation is to buy those folks a book for Christmas . Buy a book in climate change science and give it to them . <p> " Often if you get them reading , six months or a year later they 'll convert to the other side .
