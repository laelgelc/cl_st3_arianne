<h> M. bovis milestone in sight <p> The Mycoplasma bovis eradication programme is closing in on another milestone as 2019 draws to an end . <p> On the latest figures , 124,050 cattle have been culled and the 125,000 milestone seems certain to be surpassed very soon . <p> When the eradication programme was formally adopted in May 2018 , 26,000 had already been earmarked for slaughter and it was estimated that another 126,000 would need to be culled . <p> The total number of farms confirmed as infected passed 200 in October and now stands at 211 -- 49 in the South Island and 162 in the North . 54 were dairy farms , 113 beef and 44 other . Of those , 22 are still active and 189 cleared . <p> In positive signs , however , the number of farms under active surveillance -- which dropped sharply when testing protocols were changed to streamline the process in September -- has continued to trend downwards since then and now stands at 269 . The number of farms under a Notice of Direction ( NoD ) is also down from an October high and now stands at 260 . <p> The Minister for Rural Communities and Agriculture , Damien O'Connor , recently announced extra funding for the Rural Support Trust . <p> " I know that rural families are worried about some of the challenges facing them , including the ongoing uncertainty created by the Mycoplasma bovis outbreak . <p> " Those concerns sit alongside ongoing worries about bank debt and how best to meet the challenges of improving our waterways and meet New Zealand 's climate change commitments . <p> " To that end , I 've spoken to the Rural Support Trust and , alongside our Mycoplasma bovis programme partners , Beef + Lamb New Zealand and DairyNZ , we 've set aside an extra $250,000 to help with their work talking to farmers on the ground . <p> " The trust will develop a plan for using the money , in addition to the other farmer support mechanisms provided by the M.bovis programme , and the DairyNZ and B+LNZ compensation assistance teams ( DBCAT ) . <p> " The trust does a fantastic job and is well placed to deliver additional help . They 're experienced and practical people who can coach farmers through difficult times . ' ' <p> O'Connor said the new funding is on top of previous increases by the Government . <p> " The Government has already boosted funding for the Rural Support Trust from $386,500 a year to $626,000 for their daily work . Outside of that , the trust is budgeted to receive more than $1m in the next year for M.bovis related work . " <p> Meanwhile , the rural support trusts have published a list of services offered to help farmers affected by Mycoplasma bovis , saying they are " more than just an ear to listen " . <p> " As well as someone to talk to about your concerns , we can also help you navigate through the process , as we have training in and experience with the M. bovis programme and know how it works . <p> " Depending on the individual farming operation , it can be a lengthy process , involving a number of M. bovis programme teams , and RST offers support throughout the process and the ability to help shift things along if they get stuck . <p> " We are rural people helping rural people . We are farming people who understand the challenges of rural life . "
