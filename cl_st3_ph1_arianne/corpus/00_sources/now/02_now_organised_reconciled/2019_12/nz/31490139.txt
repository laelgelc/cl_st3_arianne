<p> Alisha Tyson is a writer , and librarian based in Wellington . She has an MA in Creative Writing from the IIML , and her stories and book reviews are featured in many places , some of which include The Sapling , Turbine , and LEFT ( published by ' We Are Babies ) . She can be found in libraries reading to children about bears who feel strongly about hats . <h> Saturday short story : Moving house , by Alisha Tyson <p> " The neighbour takes my hand and walks me over the concrete to my parent 's back door " : a look inside Hell by Alisha Tyson . <p> I have a mattress on the floor next to my bed . I keep it for times like this when I wake at night , hips hurting with growing pains . I worked out that if I just keep moving I do n't feel my bones growing . <p> I use my floor mattress to practise flying . I jump off my bed , knowing if I land on my knees in mid-air then I can take off and fly -- through my half open window , above square gardens , pointed roofs , toward the power-lines so thread-thin and high as if drawn by God with a Sharpie . I 'd sit up there with dark birds that come out only at night , and we 'd watch the cats fight until morning . <p> But I never land in mid-air . I hit the mattress hard every time . This is why my knees are always bruised . <p> I turn on my nightlight , the light catches the plastic lunchbox I kicked beneath my bed , then reported lost to Mum . <p> The walls move in and out like a breathing person and I wish I had n't learned the world is always rolling over -- that I could be on the upside-down part of the planet and not know it . <p> I open my door . My brother Scott 's door is shut and his light is off . He is not old enough for his bones to wake him like mine do . Mum says boy bones grow slower than girls until all at once they are large , and it is like all the careful growing you did as a girl has gone to waste . <p> I walk down the corridor . My toes curl from fear when I see the painting , hung up high , with gold words that say , " Be still and know that I am God . " I pass the bathroom that smells of chickenpox shampoo and enter the laundry where our dog sleeps when it 's raining and too cold to put him in the garage . His name is Barney , like the dinosaur , and he is a Chow Chow . He looks like a dog-sized Grizzly bear and has a purple tongue . He hides his face in his paws when I rub his stomach . <p> Mum and Dad are sitting on the couch near the fireplace in the lounge . <p> " What are you doing up ? " <p> " Go back to bed , sweetie . " <p> " I want to go tenting . Nine is old enough , okay ? " I tell them . <p> Dad goes to pick me up , but I dodge him . Dad loses hair from his head every time he moves , though we know not to mention it . To make up for it he has a brown beard so thick anything could be beneath it -- anything at all . <p> Dad looks at me carefully , and I hold my breath . Finally he nods . " Okay , you can go tenting -- tomorrow . " <p> * <p> They fell for it . I am going tenting and from today onwards I will not live in my rectangle room anymore . <p> Mum says to pack only the most important things , so I pack my spikey dinosaur and my remote-control car into a plastic bag I find near the front door . The bag is torn but it still works . <p> Mum finds me looking at my animal ornaments . She leans against the door and says , " You ca n't take those . They 're fragile . You ca n't play with them like you do with Spiderman . " <p> " He 's the Mysteriously Dark Spiderman -- you do n't even know ! " I shut the door on her . I was n't thinking of packing the ornaments anyway , I was keeping an eye on them . And they are n't fragile . They are thick with stones in their bellies and throats . I do n't love them ; I live with them . <p> I have dreams that I am small , and my ornaments are large and they do n't love me either . They want to hold me , and I hate being held . They want to squeeze me until I fall asleep and wake up bigger than them again , still choking . <p> After dinner , Scott and I go straight to our new tent home in the backyard . This is where we play our made-up game " Heaven And Hell " . We walk over the concrete near the garage , which we call Hell , into the grass , which is Heaven . <p> The tent is a nice shape with a triangle roof and its walls shift like wings . It 's okay that this new room needs to be pegged down ; it makes sense for wing walls to try to fly away . <p> The air is still and heavy as though the weather will turn . <p> " Air mattresses are gross , " I say , squashing the lungs out of mine . Scott has his face turned away and he shivers . <p> " I miss Mum and Dad . " <p> " Yes , they are very far away . " A whole Heaven and Hell away . A whole afterlife from where we are . <p> Scott sits up and holds his sleeping bag in bunches . I lie back and watch the roof . Rain starts falling and it 's like sleeping under an umbrella . In the baggy bits of the roof , water pools . <p> " Do you like your new room ? " I ask , " Not the tent , I mean . " <p> " It 's okay . At Christmas I watched next door . I saw Santa . " <p> " You did n't ! " <p> " I did ! He was on the roof . I think that makes my room quite good . " <p> I hold my words in and change them , " Do you like the shape of your room ? " <p> Scott stares at me , " What ? " <p> " You know . Is the shape a good one ? " <p> " It 's square . What 's the best shape for a room ? " <p> " Probably a circle shape would be good . " <p> " I guess . But not a triangle . " <p> " No , because the corner would be pointy and you could n't get small things out of it . " <p> " Do you miss our last house ? " Scott asks . He means in Australia . <p> " No , there were huntsmen everywhere . " Spiders the size of babies . " And cockroaches in the toilet . " <p> " Yes , this one is lots better . I think I like New Zealand more than Australia . " <p> " I do n't . No one likes the way I say dance or six . " <p> " But the magpies are n't as mean . " <p> " That 's true . " <p> I look at the walls . At least you can see the skeleton of a tent . It 's made of string and bendy tubes . A tent does n't hide things . <p> " Even though there were spiders and it was always too hot and the kookaburra in the garden was loud in the morning I was happier there , " I say definitely . <p> " I think you 're remembering wrong , " Scott says . <p> I think about this . " Maybe I 'm just too old for a house now . Maybe I 've outgrown houses . " <p> " That 's silly . You 're not bigger than a house ! " Scott says this like it is the final point to be made . <p> We are quiet for a long while now . There is the sound of the TV too loud inside and Mum laughing . <p> " I think I 'll go inside , " Scott whispers . " Is that okay ? " <p> I sit up , " I 've never been alone in a house before . " <p> " But this is n't a house , it 's a tent . " His eyes look wet . He needs Mum and does n't want to disappoint me . <p> " I 'll be fine , " I lie back down . " I 'm too old to be in a house , anyway ... " <p> Scott peels his sleeping bag off like thick rolls of skin . He stands in a crater in his air mattress and stares at the floor , making up his mind . Then he picks up his pillow and disappears into the wet air , zipping up the wall as he goes . <p> * <p> Soon I will outgrow this tent and I will live somewhere else and then I will never move . Never no matter what . Not even if my garden becomes the ocean . Not even if wild animals move onto my porch . I will never move . My house will always be the same and I will know how far the walls are from my bed . I will wake in the morning without wondering where I am or how I got there . And I will live in the same place forever . <p> * <p> I try to fall asleep with my arms crossed to keep warm . <p> The rain has stopped . I give up sleeping and unzip the wall . Water is seeping on to my floor . Lucky for me the uneven ground means it wo n't reach my mattress . <p> Outside in the veggie garden I spot a hedgehog . It shrinks down when it notices me . I breathe out slow . Then it stands on frighteningly long spindle legs and sprints across the concrete toward the metal gate . <p> " Hey ! Wait ! " I run after it , the grass sloshing under my bare feet . The hedgehog darts across the concrete and smacks into the gate . It is scrambling , stuck between the diamond spaces of the linked wires . It misunderstood its own shape . <p> I stop at the edge of the concrete . In our Heaven and Hell game when you stand on concrete you become a demon . You can not go back to Heaven unless you are Barney , because he is part dog , part bear . Once on the concrete you can only retreat into the house where the walls move . Those are the rules . <p> In the night the rules must be much more serious . <p> The hedgehog makes a terrible hissing sound . I need a way to reach it where I wo n't touch the ground . <p> * <p> I return to the edge of the grass with my sleeping bag , flopping it onto the concrete . It 's just like a flying carpet , only difference is it lies still and does nothing . I inch to the very edge of the bag and try to reach the hedgehog , but even then I am a whole Hell city away . <p> I pace up and down the sleeping bag . It squelches and feels glossy , so I take it between my toes and squeeze it . I snap my fingers , back up onto the lawn and get ready to take a running jump . <p> When I jump off the sleeping bag the concrete rises fast to meet me . <p> My legs shoot with pain . Like the time I rode my bike too fast and crash-landed over the handlebars , removing sheets of skin off my knees , and a whole thumbnail as I skidded to a stop on the road . <p> I open my eyes and I am floating several inches high above the concrete . The pain must be the price for flying . <p> I see Hellscapes far beneath me . Every window in the buildings below holds a room on fire , bodies squirming in smoke . Red demons , with thick skin and bat wings , flap above a blood lake . They ca n't fly high like me , though . <p> The hedgehog thrashes . <p> I move slowly forward , so slowly anyone watching me would think I was not moving at all . <p> Inch by inch I defy gravity and float forward , dragging my knees through space . So long as I concentrate I wo n't end up in Hell . They teach you that in church . <p> * <p> Light shines through cracks in the neighbour 's wooden fence . A bunch of bones are tossed over into our yard . <p> " You 're not allowed to do that , " I say without thinking . <p> The neighbour rests his chin on the top of the fence . For a moment I wonder if his neck is extra-long , because he 's up high and is only two years older than me . But I realise he 's likely just stepped up and jammed his feet between the fence 's slatted wood . <p> " It 's for your dog , " he says . <p> " I know . But dogs can only have frozen bones -- cooked ones get stuck in their throat . " <p> He twists his mouth . " They 're big bones for a big bear-dog like yours . " His words are sharp . He has n't said anything about me floating . <p> The rain starts again , soaking my flannel pyjamas . The neighbour nods at the gate , " You going to get that hedgehog free or should I jump over and help ? " <p> " The hedgehog will die if you do n't do something . " He snorts , " Let me guess -- the floor is lava . " <p> " I do n't play that game anymore . " <p> My focus wobbles . The hedgehog is trying to curl up on itself with the gate at its centre like a new bone . Blood runs off its paws , and I am less and less sure I 'll stay afloat . I can feel Hell beneath me , but now it feels more like being too still on sharp stones , than a white-hot fire . I feel my stupidness form a ball in my throat that ca n't be swallowed . <p> I shove a hand out to our neighbour , my fist tight . " If I give you something , will you take care of it ? " <p> If I do n't look at him then maybe he is n't there . Maybe I made him up like a house that has lungs that breathe , or concrete that is a shell for the afterlife . <p> " Throw it to me ! I 'll catch it . " <p> I feel how painfully small I am . But his hand is out and open , so I toss him something . I have n't decided what it is yet -- maybe it is something as large as my heart with just as many knobs and fleshy bits , or maybe it is something as small as a coin that I can slip behind my ear to hide . <p> He catches it , holds the same hand up to his mouth , and eats it up . <p> I feel the world fall apart just like a sandcastle when waves lick the beach . Stones pinch my skin and suddenly I 'm on my knees . The concrete catches me and the gate rushes forward . I hear the world 's thin feet breaking off and turning into asteroids . <p> I stand up , hurting and bleeding . I look at the boy who made everything different . <p> " Well , hurry up ! " he says . <p> * <p> I undo the hedgehog from the gate 's wire . I get so many cuts on my fingers from its quills and the loose metal . The rain washes my red knees . After I put the hedgehog down he tries to duck through the same place in the gate like a baby chipping away at a puzzle piece until it fits the wrong spot . <p> " No ! " I tell it . I go to pull it back , but it hisses again and tries to nip my hand . Startled , I trip , grabbing the gate to keep from falling . The neighbour laughs . <p> I open the gate quickly and the animal , still stuck , is dragged along . Finally it finds a way to free itself and runs on its oddly long legs , up our driveway to the park , and away forever . <p> The neighbour and I catch each other 's eye . He climbs the fence , and just like I do n't want him to , he takes my hand and walks me over the concrete to my parent 's back door . <p> * <p> Mum uses cotton wool and Dettol to clean my wounds . The neighbour is talking to Dad in the kitchen as Mum puts me to bed . <p> I lie in my rectangle room and watch the walls , but they wo n't move . The walls wo n't dare move . I stare at the walls until my eyes are too dry to close . <p> The neighbour has everything that made my life interesting burning up in his stomach . I know because I asked Mum once what happens in a stomach and she said it is full of acid . The acid takes everything apart -- that 's why it hurts when you throw up , and it 's why you 're not allowed to open your stomach and see in . <p> I know how many steps it is from my bed to the ornaments . Three large ones and one half one . I decide to test it . As I get up I slip a hand beneath my bed , something I would n't normally dare do in the dark . I find the lunchbox I pretended I 'd lost . When I open it I smell stale Dunkaroos . <p> I walk to the other side of the room , and the other side of the room does n't retreat from me . The wall is very still and the stone bodies are still too . I fit every last ornament in the box . I close the lid and slide my lunchbox back into the hidden recess beneath my bed . <p> I wait to feel afraid , or better , or finished . I wait in the dust at the foot of my bed . <p> Next week 's short story is by Emma Neale . <p> We value fearless , independent journalism .
