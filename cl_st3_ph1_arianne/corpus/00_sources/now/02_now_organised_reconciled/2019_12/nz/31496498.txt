Photo : Getty Images <h> RSE scheme ' transformed ' the NZ fruit growing industry <p> Millions of dollars worth of New Zealand fruit and grapes were at risk of rotting on the branch due to a shortage of local pickers . So a visionary group of Central Otago growers took a chance on guest workers from the Pacific , who also took a chance on them . <p> In the early 2000s the orchards and vineyards of central Otago were heavy with fruit . Peaches , cherries and grapes were ready to be plucked , boxed and shipped all over the world . But there was a problem . There were n't enough people to pick them . <p> Hiring backpackers and students on holiday was the usual practice , but it was risky , James Dicey , the man behind Mt Difficulty wines says . <p> " I remember a Swedish backpacker who turned up . I did the paperwork , gave him his tools , trained him . He spent two and a half days with me , got enough money to get on the juice and do a bungy jump , then disappeared . " <p> For Basil Goodman , another grower from Cromwell , the staff shortage was becoming demoralising . <p> " For two of three years , we did get to a stage , where we did not harvest all the fruit we grew . You put your whole year 's work into a box of beautiful looking peaches or nectarines and apricots and then they just do n't quite get to the box . " <p> Last month , Basil retired after a lifetime in the industry , just a few weeks before his 80th birthday . One of his proudest achievements is starting what is now called the Recognised Seasonal Employer scheme , or , the RSE . <p> The government now leads the project and it 's become the biggest formal overseas worker scheme in the country . It has two purposes : to fill the labour gap during the busy harvest seasons and to boost pacific economies . <p> Last year , about 12,000 workers took part , mostly from the Pacific . <p> And after years of industry begging government , the cap 's set to jump to 16,000 over the next two years . <p> The government is yet to collect thorough data yet on how much money is sent back to the islands . But a 2014 study put the total earnings of just Vanuatu RSE workers at about 130 million , that year . <p> Meanwhile , the fruit and wine industries ' exports are now worth $5.5 billion , more than double what it was in 2005 . <p> But about 25 years ago , finding reliable workers was becoming a gamble . There is a window of only one week before a piece of stone fruit becomes too ripe for export , Basil says . <p> " So if you did n't do it , you did n't do it . Getting people became our prime focus . " <p> He left the fruit-growing business out of frustration and turned his focus towards finding out what was going on for the fruit and wine industries . <p> From a 1993 survey , he discovered the local industry needed 5000 workers for the coming season and there would be a shortfall . <p> " That was the nucleus of needing to do something together to make something happen . " <p> Seasonal Solutions was set up with a mandate to urge the government to expand its working holiday visa . Within a few years travellers from all over the world were turning up at the farm gate . <p> " It solved the problem for a little while , but never quite . " <p> Because then , backpackers figured out there was bigger and better money to be made jumping from orchard to orchard . <p> Losing staff overnight makes life as an employer tough after effort goes into training , paperwork and health and safety , James says . <p> But then fortuitously , in 2005 , the then-mayor of central Otago Malcolm Macpherson met Dr Manjula Luthria from the World Bank . <p> She had just written a book about Pacific labour mobility and Malcolm introduced the idea to Basil . <p> " She offered us $150,000 to fund a team of guys from the Pacific to go and work our fruit for a season . " <p> Basil was sold . <p> Seasonal Solutions got sign off from New Zealand and Vanuatu to go ahead with the trial and in 2006 , 45 men from a bunch of different villages were on a plane to the South Island . <p> Pete Bumseng - the son of a chief , a father to three - was one of those 45 men . <p> " The first time I came , it was a very surprising experience . It was freezing in the morning , there was snow everywhere . " <p> Despite the cold , it worked for Pete and he 's now in his 13th season . That makes him , plus two others , the longest serving members of the scheme . <p> But initially , there were nerves . In the past , their ancestors were also recruited to work overseas . And some never made it home . <p> " In the community , it was a bit scary thinking is it going to work the same ? Are we going to be treated as slaves , as our forefathers were treated ? " <p> Vanuatu is one of the poorest Pacific Islands . <p> While the project was good for the growers , it was also created to help Ni-Vans - the colloquial name for people from Vanuatu . <p> And despite the fears , after sending money home in that first season , it became clear the scheme was working for everyone . <p> For Pete , his money contributed to building new houses , improving kitchens , toilets and health facilities in Vanuatu . <p> But those improvements took a personal toll . Learning to deal with a long-distance relationship was tricky and missing his three children was equally tough . <p> " My younger son is now 14 . I still remember , he was one year old when I got on that plane but when I went back home , he did n't know I was his dad , he was afraid of me . " <p> For his boss James , there were also a few teething problems with the workers , especially when it came to cooking , setting up bank accounts and paying tax . <p> And there was some racism from the community , he says . <p> Pete remembers being stared at in that first season . <p> " You could see the reactions ' where are these guys from ? What are they doing here ? They might be taking jobs from the Kiwis ' . " <p> But that was n't the only reaction . Nearly all Ni-Vans are Christian , and the Cromwell churches jumped at the chance to boost their congregations . <p> And Pete and his crew found a way to break the ice , through music events . <p> " When we displayed who we are , that 's when we started building a connection with the community . " <p> The pilot was deemed a success . <p> Growers queued up to take part here and back in Vanuatu , men put up their hands to join . <p> The year after , the government picked up the scheme and created a special visa category so that 5000 workers could sign up . <p> There have been some problems as the scheme has grown and changed . <p> Some workers have been exploited , others not given adequate housing . Some have n't been paid properly or given good safety gear . <p> And there is fear that jobs are n't going to kiwis . <p> Those issues remain and advocates are fighting to make sure things are done by the book . <p> But for Pete Bumseng and his community , the programmes have made a big difference back home , especially when it comes to education , health and building cyclone-proof housing . James Dicey likes to sum up RSE with one word : <p> " Transformative . It 's transformed the Ni-Van 's lives , it 's transformed our access to labour here , our ability to invest in kiwis to provide more permanent jobs , our ability to invest in additional hectares and machinery to support that . " <p> As for Pete and his wife , they made it work . And now the whole family comes for Christmas . <p> " If you ask me the question what makes me keep coming back : it 's just another home .
