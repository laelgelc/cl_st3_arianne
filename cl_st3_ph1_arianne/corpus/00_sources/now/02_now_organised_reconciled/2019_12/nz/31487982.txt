<h> Hoping ' the big one ' holds off . . . <p> Gisborne City and the Poverty Bay Flats have an insurance policy called the Waipaoa Flood Control Scheme that is no longer comprehensive . <p> The council describes the scheme as one of its most valuable assets and has embarked on a project to increase its level of flood protection to a 100-year return period , accounting for anticipated climate change effects out to 2090 . <p> This involves raising and widening the 64km of stopbanks along the Waipaoa River , with the eastern side first as it protects the largest area of the Flats and the city . Earthworks began south of Ormond earlier this year , restarted in late September and a report to councillors last week said the 4km section between Ford and Ferry roads was on track to be completed before Christmas -- with work then shifting to a 2km section between the rivermouth and the Matawhero SH2 bridge . <p> Now , the big problem with this project is that it has escalated in scope and cost . Updated river modelling and technical peer review confirmed the original plan to raise the stopbanks by 1m was insufficient , and they are now being raised up to twice as much . <p> A secondary problem is that this was not factored into budgets for the 2018-2028 Long-Term Plan ( LTP ) where it is treated as a $16.4m project , while it is now estimated to cost $30m-$35m . ( As well as the design height changing , the original budget failed to account for inflation and staff project management costs . ) The consequence is a slower start to implementing this project than would otherwise be the case , until budgets are revised as the 2021-2031 LTP is put together . <p> The report for councillors says $300,000 of funding for 2020/21 has been brought forward to undertake detailed design , inform budgets/estimates for the next LTP , and to prepare for next year 's construction contract . It also says the project will still meet the overall budget for the two-year period 2019/20-2020/21 -- meaning there wo n't be a great deal of actual construction work in 2020/21 . <p> There is a possible saviour in the form of the Provincial Growth Fund , although an application by the council for $6m to cover stopbank upgrades around the Waipaoa rivermouth to Matawhero Bridge area , including a rollout of cycle trails there , was submitted over a year ago . A council spokeswoman said yesterday that there had been " no formal decision on this yet " . <p> If the decision is " no " , should n't the council find more money from elsewhere to make more progress on this vital project in 2020/21 ?
