<h> Greens reflect on year of climate change action <p> The Green Party has emerged relatively unscathed from 2019 , unlike their partners in Government - Labour was rocked by a sexual assault scandal only somewhat tidied up by Christmas , and allegations of electoral fraud will hang over NZ First into the New Year . It 's polling is stable and it retains its clear brand . <p> Co-leaders James Shaw and Marama Davidson are able to count big wins and few failures , confident they know their voters and know what they 've delivered - even if the Government has n't , as they 're happy to concede . <p> Despite a promise to focus relentlessly on economic inequality , the Greens ' wins mostly sit on the greener end of the spectrum - specifically climate change . <p> ROBERT KITCHIN/STUFF <p> Shaw said that if he had n't kept National onboard with the Zero Carbon Act he would have looked " suspect " . <p> The Zero Carbon Act is high on the list of wins . The bill sets up a framework for climate action for the next decade at least , with an independent Climate Change Commission recommending " emissions budgets " for Governments to follow . Crucially , it has the support of National - a task that took Shaw months and led to some scaling back of ambition . <p> But this is not to say there has n't been losses . Veteran MP Gareth Hughes announced his retirement from politics in November , and with it criticised the Government from failing to be " transformational " . <p> Both Shaw and Davidson say they share that critique , and have said it themselves in as many words . <p> " It 's been transformative in pockets , " Shaw says . <p> He set a firm line in the sand earlier this year saying that if the Government failed to enact a capital gains tax it would n't deserve to be re-elected . Yet the Government failed to enact a capital gains tax , and Shaw still wants it to be re-elected . <p> Shaw recounted the moment he got the call from Ardern saying she was canning the tax for this term and any other where she is Labour leader . <p> " Look , this has happened , " Shaw remembered Ardern saying over the phone . <p> His response : " I actually have quite a bit of empathy for her because she had through four election campaigns on that . And , as she has said , she still believes it was the right thing to do . " <p> The lack of wins in other areas like poverty might have something to do with the people who actually vote for the Greens - urban elites , not working class people , who generally vote for Labour or National . <p> " If you are a 25- to 35-year-old female who is tertiary educated , lives in a downtown urban area , works in the public service , you have a 73 per cent chance of voting for us , " Shaw says . <p> " We know that we 've got much , much broader appeal ... We 're pretty confident in our core vote , but we know that somewhere between - and this has gone now through three election cycles - anywhere between 25 and 30 per cent of the population rate us . " <p> Other wins are probably less obvious , they say . Both leaders tout the strength of their eight-strong MP cohort , and say many are doing good work that goes unseen . <p> " Every single one of them are blood , sweat , and tears for their portfolios , " Davidson says . <p> Davidson cites Jan Logie 's work on domestic and sexual violence law for victims . Chloe Swarbrick , who has pushed the Government further with drug reform , could also be on the list . <p> Shaw says Golriz Ghahraman 's policy work and deal with Justice Minister Andrew Little to get the Green 's sought amendment to the terror suppression control order was a strong win . Little needed the votes to get the bill through . <p> National MPs , already trying to cut a tougher deal with Little , were extremely mad . They credited Ghahraman for their outrage , Davidson says . <p> Are they enjoying the wins , when they come ? With that comes a sly grin and nod of the head from Shaw . <p> " It would be very impolitic to say yes ... The game stuff drives me nuts , so I guess that detracts from the enjoyment , " Shaw says . <p> Davidson flips it around and says the satisfaction she finds in is essentially keeping the tories out of power . <p> " The satisfaction is when we 're able to protect and ward off from their oppressive approach . That 's , like , absolutely important to us . " <p> CO-LEADERS ' HOLIDAY PICKS <p> Favourite Christmas song : Fairytale of New York by the Pogues ( Shaw ) ; Jingle Bell Rock ( Davidson ) .
