<h> Going green <p> Moving to green vehicles is a small part of Gisborne District Council 's work to reduce its carbon footprint " but every bit helps " , says Mayor Rehette Stoltz . <p> The council 's first low-emission vehicle -- a Toyota Prius Prime that can run on electricity or petrol -- was acquired about four months ago . <p> With a new car due to be bought for Mayor Stoltz , she was keen for it to be environmentally friendly . <p> The quiet Mitsubishi Outlander hybrid she has been driving around for the past few weeks is working out well . <p> It has a battery range of about 55 kilometres , which is about the distance she consistently travels when on council business in Gisborne . <p> The ability to switch to petrol power helped allay " battery anxiety " . <p> She plugs in the SUV at home overnight so it is fully charged -- at a cost of about $3 -- for the next day . <p> Mayor Stoltz says the big focus for the council in terms of climate change is in planning for its effects on infrastructure such as roads and underground utilities . <p> Gisborne District Council asset manager Simon Jeune says the council has about 70 vehicles in its fleet . <p> About two-thirds of these are utility vehicles that need to be four-wheel drives and able to travel long distances . <p> There is currently no hybrid or electric vehicle to viably replace these , Mr Jeune says . <p> The rest of the fleet is made up of passenger cars , and about half of those do not need to travel off-road so can potentially be replaced with green vehicles . <p> Switching out petrol or diesel cars will occur as each one becomes due for replacement . <p> The council 's passenger vehicles have a lifespan of 60,000 to 90,000km , or three to four years . <p> The decision will come down to the potential electric or hybrid vehicle 's performance -- being able to do what the council need it to do -- and affordability in comparison to an equivalent petrol or diesel vehicle . <p> The council recently bought two 2016 Nissan Leafs for about $22,000 each . <p> Along with the two hybrid vehicles , these fully electric cars will have access to three chargers being installed in the carpark at the council building on Fitzherbert Street . <p> Mr Jeune says electric vehicles have been a consideration in plans for the redeveloped council building , which opened in 2018 with a tube for wiring laid under the carpark . <p> Another charger will be installed at the council 's service centre in Te Puia Springs . <p> The four chargers were partly funded by a $20,000 grant from the Government 's low emission vehicles contestable fund in August , with the council contributing $20,000 to make up the total cost . <p> In the same round of grants , Eastland Group was given $97,000 to expand its fast charger network in Tairawhiti , including in Ruatoria and Tokomaru Bay . <p> Mr Jeune says as well as moving to green vehicles , which will reduce fuel bills , an emissions profile for the council is being worked on , allowing it to monitor its output and track emissions reduction over time , Mr Jeune says . <p> Council staff also have access to an e-bike and five e-scooters that are plugged in at various departments in the council building , as well as five ordinary bikes . <p> These have replaced car trips for council staff heading into town for meetings or inspections . <p> Staff wanting to use e-scooters , which are limited to 300W or 25kmh , must first go through an induction . <p> Electric vehicle technology is advancing rapidly so it is likely more viable options to replace long-distance , off-road vehicles will become available in time , Mr Jeune says . <p> TAKE YOUR PICK : Rehette Stoltz with the new mayoral car -- a Mitsubishi Outlander hybrid -- and Gisborne District Council asset manager Simon Jeune with one of the council 's e-scooters and an e-bike , which are part of a drive to get the council moving in an environmentally friendly way .
