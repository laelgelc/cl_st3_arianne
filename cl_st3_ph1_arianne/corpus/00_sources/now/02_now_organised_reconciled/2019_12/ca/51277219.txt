<p> After presenting his case for what he 's been billing as " a fair deal for Alberta " -- and specifically , the actions that , in his view , need to be taken by Ottawa to make it happen -- in a standing-room-only " barnburner " lunch speech yesterday , former Harper cabinet minister turned premier Jason Kenney is set to continue what the Star 's Tonda McCharles describes as a " full court press " on the minority Liberal government during an afternoon tete-a-tete with Prime Minister Justin Trudeau himself . <p> As the Star notes , Kenney descended on the capital on Monday " with a team of eight cabinet ministers , numbers and charts in hand , to warn that separatist sentiment in the Prairies is real and tied to federal policies that are crushing the energy sector , " and offer his five-point proposal to assuage key regional concerns , including a major rewrite of the Liberals ' signature environmental assessment regime , a boost in financial support for cash-strapped provinces and , of course , getting the proposed Trans Canada pipeline extension up and running . <p> And while he acknowledged that he " does n't expect to be walking out of the prime minister 's office with written agreements on these things tomorrow , " he is hoping for " indication they are prepared to move . " <p> Kenney is n't the only premier on the prime ministerial itinerary today : He 's also booked in for a sitdown with Nova Scotia 's Stephen McNeil , which is currently scheduled to take place at 3:30 PM , one hour before he 's set to meet with Kenney . <p> Also on Trudeau 's to-do list : A morning huddle with his cabinet and an appearance at the Liberal caucus " holiday party " this evening . <p> He 'll likely also be staying in close contact with Deputy Prime Minister Chrystia Freeland , who jetted to Mexico City on Monday afternoon for what could be the final round of negotiations on the latest revisions to the new North American free trade deal , which , as the Canadian Press notes , " has been stalled for months " at the ratification stage due to a cross-aisle standoff between US Democrats and the White House , who have reportedly struck a tentative deal to get it through congress . <p> If successful , that would trigger a similar push for Canada to ratify the redrafted deal , although given the rapidly approaching holiday hiatus , it likely would n't make it to the House floor until January , when it would be all but guaranteed safe passage through the minority Chamber courtesy of the Conservatives , who have made it clear that they would n't seek to delay implementation of the new continental accord even if they do n't think the Liberals were able to get the best possible deal for Canada . <p> Elsewhere on the international diplomatic front , Foreign Affairs Minister Francois Philippe Champagne is in Berlin for the Alliance for Multilateralism conference on Advocating Human Rights in the 21st Century , where , according to his office , he 'll hold a one-on-one chat with his German counterpart and participate in a panel discussion on " human rights and climate change .
