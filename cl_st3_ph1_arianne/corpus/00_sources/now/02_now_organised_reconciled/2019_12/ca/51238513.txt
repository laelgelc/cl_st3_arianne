<h> Refrigerated skate trail at park rejected by city council <p> A $3.1 million artificial skate trail envisaged for Couchiching Beach Park melted into thin air after a majority of city councillors voted against the plan at a budget committee meeting Tuesday at city hall . <p> Coun. Ted Emond made an impassioned plea to green-light what he termed a " visionary " plan as part of a spirited debate about the project . <p> " I think it 's an important investment , " said Emond . " It 's an opportunity and we have relatively few of these in the budget where we can invest in the future of Orillia and , at the same time , build on the heritage and past in Orillia . " <p> The Ward 1 councillor said heritage is about more than buildings and read a passage from a just-released book , Christmas in Mariposa , by former Packet &amp; Times reporter Jamie Lamb . <p> That passage harkened back to " joyful " times skating on the lake - something that is less and less possible due to the impact of climate change . <p> Emond conceded the timing was n't ideal due to this year 's budget pressures , but suggested timing was good in terms of the planned redevelopment of the waterfront . <p> Coun. Mason Ainsworth said he had concerns about the ongoing operational costs associated with the skate trail , which staff estimate to be about $150,000 per year . <p> He said a skate trail could " be something great " but the price tag is " way too much . " <p> He also said when he talks to residents , they constantly bring up roads , sidewalks and snow removal - not a skating trail . <p> That was a sentiment echoed by Coun. David Campbell . He said during his year as a councillor , he has heard " more opposition about this skate trail than any other thing . " He referenced the ongoing operational costs as a key factor . <p> " My job here as a councillor is to represent ( the citizens ) and I have overwhelmingly heard they do not support this , so I wo n't . " <p> Mayor Steve Clarke supported the idea and said he recognized it 's a vision . <p> " A refrigerated skate trail sounds a little crazy and futuristic , but it could be used in plus 5 weather , " said Clarke , citing the difficulty of maintaining outdoor rinks amid the wild swings of weather in recent years . <p> He also likened the skate trail to the new park in West Orillia , which has a similar price tag . He noted it , too , has annual operational costs . <p> Emond noted the city had put the project forward recently as one of two projects vying for provincial/federal grant money . <p> He warned council that if they turned down the concept , the upper levels of government would not likely agree to funding . <p> However , in the end , a majority of council did not support the initiative . <p> The idea behind the skate trail was to " provide a year-round destination on the waterfront . " <p> A report to councillors noted it would be " both a popular winter destination and a programmable concrete trail in the summertime . " <p> Staff said increased activity at the waterfront in the winter would have " numerous positive " results . During summer months , the trail was to support community festivals and special events " by providing space for tents with excellent access to the Port of Orillia " , the boardwalk and could support pop-up performances with amphitheatre-style seating . <p> The project would have included an addition to the Orillia Waterfront Centre to accommodate a skate change room and a second small service building to house the ice resurfacer and the ice-making equipment . <p> Council was still wrapping up its budget deliberations as of Tuesday at 5 p.m . <p> We welcome your feedback and encourage you to share your thoughts on this story .
