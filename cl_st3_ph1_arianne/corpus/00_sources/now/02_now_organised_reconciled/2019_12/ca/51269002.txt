<h> Long wait seen for electric planes despite historic first flight <p> The world 's first fully electric commercial aircraft takes flight . The Harbour Air ePlane is magnified by the magniX magni500 , a 750-horsepower electric propulsion system . <h> An all-electric commercial plane flies for the first time , spurring hopes of replacing fuel-powered jets with green alternatives <p> By Emeline Wuilbercq <p> LONDON -- The pioneers behind the world 's first fully electric , commercial flight say they have made history , but climate-change activists see electric air travel like a dream yet to come true . <p> The Harbour Air seaplane -- which seats six -- took off close to Vancouver in Canada on Tuesday and flew for less than 15 minutes . Its pilot , Greg McDougall , declared the mission a success , saying in a statement : " We made history . " <p> It was the first time an all-electric commercial plane had taken to the skies , unleashing hopes that fuel-guzzling jets -- a key cause of global warming -- could be replaced by a green alternative . <p> Climate-change activists welcomed the maiden flight but played down hopes that electric planes carrying mass tourists , commuters or business travelers would take off any time soon . <p> " We 're still a long way from having a commercial service , " said Lucy Gilliam , aviation and shipping campaigner at Transport &amp; Environment , a European umbrella group of NGOs campaigning for cleaner transportation . <p> " This is a milestone , but we need to be realistic that electrification will play a role in short-haul flights , but not in the long-haul , " she told the Thomson Reuters Foundation by phone from Brussels . <p> The record test flight -- albeit short -- came as eco-friendly travelers try to reduce their carbon footprint and use " flight shaming " to persuade others to follow suit . <p> Teenaged climate activist Greta Thunberg -- named on Wednesday as Time magazine 's person of the year -- took the lead in August when she used a racing yacht to get to the United Nations Climate Action Summit in New York . <h> REVOLUTION <p> But long-distance electric flying -- which cuts maintenance costs and has zero emissions -- is still decades away , they said . <p> " This is paving the way for private jets to be electrified , " said Leo Murray of Britain 's Possible charity , which campaigns for climate action . <p> But he said long-distance journeys in large airplanes " make up the bulk of the climate damage from air travel " . <p> Others agreed there was lots more work to be done . <p> " If we are considering electrification of aircraft as a potential solution to aviation 's impact on climate change , then this is only the start , " Nikhil Sachdeva , from consulting firm Roland Berger , told the Thomson Reuters Foundation . <p> The lithium battery used in the Canadian flight could only power a seaplane for about 160 km , almost the distance by plane from Brussels to Luxembourg . <p> Harbour Air plans to start electrifying its fleet of more than 40 seaplanes by 2022 , once certification and regulation processes are completed . <p> " We do n't have perfect science and perfect data on exactly how ( the electric aircraft ) will perform . So the regulators need time to gather that data and to learn from the technologies before they can say this is safe " , said Sachdeva . <p> NetNewsledger.com or NNL offers news , information , opinions and positive ideas for Thunder Bay , Ontario , Northwestern Ontario and the world .
