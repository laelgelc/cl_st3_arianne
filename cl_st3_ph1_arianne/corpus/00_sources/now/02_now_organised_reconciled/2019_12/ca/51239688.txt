<h> Throne speech to offer Canadians a preview of how Trudeau will govern <p> Thursday 's throne speech will offer hints to how the Liberal government will act in the new parliament . <p> The address , to be read by Governor General Julie Payette in front a packed Senate chamber , will be a closely watched affair by stakeholders of government who are hungry to find out what the key priorities are -- something taking on added significance in a minority parliament . <p> But Liberal strategist Shane Mackenzie said he believes the two most important themes of the speech will be national unity and addressing climate change . <p> " I think that you 're going to hear perhaps a fact check about Trans Mountain pipeline actually getting done -- the fact that shovels are in the ground and work is ongoing , " he said . <p> " And in true Trudeau Liberal fashion , they 're going to also signal that they 're going to continue on climate action with increasing targets , mandating them into legislation and putting forward just transition acts that they promised . " <p> But Mackenzie , a senior consultant at Ensight , said the Trudeau government will try to use the throne speech to signal to Canadians that the federal government can be above political battles and be a meaningful partner in getting things done , especially in the wake of regionalism and internal divisions affecting the Conservatives . <p> " The Liberals have to show that their mind is still on Canadians and their priorities , not political squabbling , " he said . <p> " This is an opportunity to set a tone and also remind people about these policy issues that they actually plan to tackle in the next two to three budgets , or four , if they can make it to a full term . " <p> He said proposals made during the election , such as gun control , pharmacare , tax cuts on personal income and for clean tech will likely be highlighted during the speech . <p> Nelson Wiseman , a political science professor at the University of Toronto , said the throne speech acts as a " signalling device " that will include broad Liberal messages on the " middle class " and fighting climate change , with references to more specific initiatives led by various departments . <p> " The Governor General will speak in governor generalities , " he said . " What will count is when you actually start getting bills that have specifics . " <p> Wiseman said it 'll be more interesting to see what the first pieces of legislation would look like . He noted in the past , governments have tabled legislation with or immediately after a throne speech . <p> Within the first few lines of the 2015 throne speech , delivering a tax cut for the middle class was mentioned as an " immediate priority . " Less than a week later , Finance Minister Bill Morneau tabled Bill C-2 in the House of Commons . <p> But for the most part , promises mentioned in throne speeches do n't offer specifics on timelines or details in policy . <p> The Liberals have said their first order of business once parliament returns is to pass a personal income tax cut promised during the election . <p> The government will also have to table a bill revising the law on physician-assisted death . In September , a Quebec court ruled that the requirement that only those near death can qualify for medical-assisted dying was unconstitutional . The court gave Ottawa until early April 2020 to amend the law . <p> Mackenzie said he predicts those two things will be mentioned on Thursday . He said a throne speech will ultimately provide to stakeholders what the gist of the Trudeau government will be . <p> " It signals a kickoff and the sort of energy that the Trudeau government is going to be bringing back , " he said . <p> Wiseman said it will be interesting to see what is mentioned about pharmacare , because the issue was not referenced by the Liberals as much on the campaign trail as he believed it would . <p> In contrast , Jagmeet Singh 's NDP frequently mentioned their pharmacare plan during the campaign and have now staked their support of the governing Liberals on whether they will implement a universal single-payer program , among other actions . <p> Last month , he also warned that NDP MPs are prepared to vote against the speech if it does n't " respect the values that we have as a party . " <p> The first " do-or-die " vote wo n't actually be on the throne speech , but opposition members will have a chance to bring down the government on or before Dec. 10 , when the first confidence vote will be on allowing government spending to continue .
