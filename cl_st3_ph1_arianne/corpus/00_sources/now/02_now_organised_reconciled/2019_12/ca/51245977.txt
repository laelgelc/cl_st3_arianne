<h> National focus needed on suicide crisis among Indigenous youth : AFN <p> National Chief of the Assembly of First Nations Perry Bellegarde marches in the closing ceremony of the AFN 's Annual General Assembly in Fredericton , N.B. , Thursday , July 25 , 2019 . Hundreds of First Nations chiefs from across Canada are gathering in Ottawa today for a special assembly . THE CANADIAN PRESS/Stephen MacGillivray <p> OTTAWA -- The federal government will support the creation of a national suicide-prevention strategy for young people , with a suicide crisis gripping a First Nation in northern Saskatchewan , Indigenous Services Minister Marc Miller told a gathering of First Nations chiefs in Ottawa Tuesday . <p> Miller was short on details on what this strategy would look like , saying only that the government would work with First Nations communities on solutions they propose . <p> On Tuesday , a special gathering of chiefs of the Assembly of First Nations from across Canada began with a call for the federal government to step in . <p> National Chief Perry Bellegarde said action must be taken to stop Indigenous children and teens from feeling so hopeless they harm themselves . <p> " We know that too many of our young ones are taking their lives . And so we say to those young people that you 're special , you are gifted and you 're loved . Never forget that , " Bellegarde said . <p> " We do call on this government to finally work with ( our ) leadership and families and people to finally implement a youth-suicide-prevention strategy . That is needed -- one that supports all of our young people . " <p> The Makwa Sahgaiehcan First Nation at Loon Lake , Sask. , has raised concerns in recent weeks about " cluster suicides " in the community , about 360 kilometres northwest of Saskatoon . <p> Band leaders say eight people , mostly youths , have tried taking their lives since the First Nation declared a state of crisis in mid-November . <p> That came after three deaths by suicide , including one of a 10-year-old girl , over three weeks . <p> Speaking on behalf of the AFN 's youth council , Rosalie LaBillois broke into tears as she talked about her concern for young Indigenous people not only in Saskatchewan , but across Canada . <p> " We want to commit to making changes for our young people . Because , as leaders , I do n't believe we 're just here to look pretty . I want to get stuff done . I want to tell you the hard , cold truth that 's happening in our communities , " she said , as a number of chiefs surrounded her in support . <p> Miller said he has heard a proposals from different First Nations and Inuit organizations to help curb this distressing trend . He says his department will work with them to support a distinctions-based approach to developing a strategy -- one that recognizes that different communities need different measures . <p> " The federal government will be there to support you , " Miller said . <p> Bellegarde also called for more aggressive action on climate change as a way to offer more hope to young people . <p> Despite almost daily scientific reports being released painting a dire picture for the future of the planet if greenhouse-gas emissions continue to increase , Bellegarde said he believes there is a reason to hope for a positive outcome if governments are willing to work with First Nations on real climate action . <p> " The time is now for bold vision and decisive action , " Bellegarde said . <p> " Their future is in all of our hands and they ( youth ) are telling us we all need to do better . " <p> The meeting marked the first time Miller has publicly addressed the AFN since being tapped as the new minister of Indigenous services . He began with a long passage in Mohawk , which drew applause from the chiefs . <p> He acknowledged the federal government has " decades and decades of wrongs to right , " but also noted that the new minority government will be working with Indigenous peoples " in a different , more difficult context . " <p> Miller made a special note in his speech to talk about the recent ruling by the Canadian Human Rights Tribunal ordering Ottawa to pay $40,000 to each First Nations child who was inappropriately taken away from his or her parents after 2006 , as well as to their parents and grandparents . <p> Ottawa is appealing the ruling , arguing in Federal Court last week the ruling erred in law , while Liberal politicians say they want to negotiate a better , more comprehensive settlement for victims . <p> Miller said Tuesday government does not question that children harmed by past child-welfare policies deserve compensation . <p> " We 're committed to working constructively , quickly and effectively with the parties to reach a comprehensive settlement that will benefit First Nations children and families . To do this , we 're going to sit down at the table with partners and work it out . " <p> Several other Liberal cabinet ministers are to address the assembly on Wednesday , including Crown-Indigenous Relations Minister Carolyn Bennett , Justice Minister David Lametti and Canadian Heritage Minister Steven Guilbeault.
