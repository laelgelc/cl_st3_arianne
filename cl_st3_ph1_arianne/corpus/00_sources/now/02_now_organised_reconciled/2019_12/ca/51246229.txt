<p> According to the Canadian Press , the speech -- which , by tradition , is penned by the prime minister 's staff but delivered by Governor General Julie Payette -- " will aim to set a collaborative tone " in an effort to secure the support of at least one of the two smaller opposition parties that could control the balance of power in the newly reconfigured House of Commons . <p> Under the current cross-aisle math , the Liberals will need either the Bloc Quebecois or the New Democrats to vote with the government on matters of confidence , which includes the roadmap laid out in the speech from the throne . <p> As per the informed-but-unnamed sources cited by CP , that will mean " highlighting Liberal commitments to stronger action to combat climate change , tax breaks for the middle-class , pharmacare and stricter gun control , all of which featured in the platforms of the New Democrats , Bloc Quebecois and Greens . " <p> ( With just three MPs , the Greens ca n't unilaterally keep the government from falling , but it would give the Liberals a credibility boost if they were able to garner an on-the-record endorsement from the Greens for their climate change plan . ) <p> Before the rite and ritual surrounding the speech itself can formally get underway , however , MPs will gather in the Commons Chamber at 9 AM to perform their first collective duty of the new parliamentary session : Electing a speaker . <p> The latest official bulletin confirmed that , as reported by iPolitics last month , five MPs are vying for the best seat in the House : incumbent speaker Geoff Regan and his Liberal caucus colleague Anthony Rota , as well as Conservative MPs Bruce Stanton and Joel Godin and New Democrat Carol Hughes . <p> Each candidate will be given a few minutes to address their colleagues before the voting -- which is done by secret preferential ballot -- begins , with the winner expected to be revealed before noon , at which point the House will adjourn until 2 PM , when MPs will be formally summoned to the Senate Chamber to hear the speech , which is currently expected to begin at 2:40 PM . <p> ( Trudeau , for his part , will take advantage of the brief pause in parliamentary proceedings to hold a quick one-on-one meeting with visiting Northwest Territories Premier Caroline Cochrane . ) <p> Although the first round of debate on the speech wo n't get started until tomorrow at the earliest , it 's a good bet that all four opposition leaders will hit the Foyer shortly after Payette begins to speak to offer their initial takes on the content -- and , more crucially , announce whether their respective parties are prepared to support the overall plan when it is put to a vote next week . <p> It 's worth noting that the government has also served notice that it will be putting forward a motion asking the House to sign off on its pledge to boost the baseline personal tax exemption from $13,000 to $15,000 . <p> Although not a formal ways-and-means request for spending authorization , that motion would also be treated as a confidence matter , and may end up coming before MPs before the standard motion stemming from the speech from the throne . <p> As it is a standard motion , and not a bill or a ways-and-means request , it is also amendable and debateable , which would allow the opposition parties to lay out their concerns or suggested changes , which could also be put to a vote , thus fulfilling the government 's obligation to test whether it has the confidence of the House .
