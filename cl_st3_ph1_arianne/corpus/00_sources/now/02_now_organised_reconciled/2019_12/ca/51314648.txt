<h> Social Sharing <h> Magnitude of situation facing huge numbers of world 's children in 2020 is ' heartbreaking ' <p> Children stand next to a metal barrier as newly arrived refugees and migrants wait to be registered at the Moria camp on the island of Lesbos , Greece , on Nov. 27 . ( Elias Marcou/Reuters ) <p> This column is an opinion by David Morley , president and CEO of UNICEF Canada . For more information about CBC 's Opinion section , please see the FAQ . <p> We are entering the final year of what has been one of the worst decades for children . War , disease and extreme weather events are robbing them of their right to simply be a kid -- to have a safe , healthy and carefree childhood . <p> In far too many communities around the world , sounds of youngsters laughing and getting into mischief are being replaced by silence . It does n't have to be that way . <p> This month , UNICEF launched Humanitarian Action for Children 2020 , a report on the world 's emergencies . It highlights some of the key challenges children are facing around the world : severe malnutrition , conflict , infectious disease , climate change , and access to education . <p> Children displaced by crises are also vulnerable to increased harm , including gender-based violence , recruitment as soldiers , exploitation , and trafficking . <p> A boy holds a rifle as he attends a rally of Houthi movement supporters to mark the Ashura in Sanaa , Yemen , on Sept. 10 . ( Khaled Abdullah/Reuters ) <p> The sheer magnitude of the situation is heartbreaking . <p> The report identifies 59 million children in 64 countries who are living in crisis zones and in need of urgent humanitarian assistance , including 2 million children with disabilities . These are the highest levels since we began keeping records . <p> The projections for the year ahead are just as dire . <p> The main causes are threefold -- conflict , climate change and major outbreaks of disease . <h> Conflict <p> The number of armed conflicts is at a three-decade high , with many civil wars engulfing entire countries . <p> Fighting has been going on for six years in both the Central African Republic and South Sudan . <p> Syria 's civil war is now in its ninth year . <p> And these conflicts have no end in sight . Some have lasted so long that many kids are growing up only knowing war . <p> Displaced Yemeni seven-month-old baby Mohammed Hussein , suffering from malnutrition , is brought by his mother to be checked at a hospital in Yemen 's conflict-ridden northwestern Hajjah governorate . ( Essa Ahmed/AFP/Getty Images ) <p> Top priorities in these war-torn regions include providing children with protection , safe water and sanitation , health care and nutrition . And the need is great . <p> In the coming year , for example , UNICEF expects it will need to treat 5.1 million children for acute malnutrition , provide 4.5 million children and caregivers with mental health and psychosocial support , and distribute emergency cash assistance to 1.7 million families to meet their most urgent needs . <h> Education <p> Immediate responses to crises in the year ahead must also be balanced with the longer-term needs of children , most importantly their education , which is also under attack . <p> During disasters , schools are often used as shelters or distribution centres , since they are usually among the strongest structures . <p> In conflict zones , they are increasingly becoming targets . <p> Afghan schoolchildren study at the destroyed Papen High School in Deh Bala district of Nangarhar province in July . ( Noorullah Shirzada/AFP via Getty Images ) <p> More than 3,000 schools have been closed in Burkina Faso , Mali and Niger in the past three years because of violence and insecurity . <p> Kids are incredibly adaptable , but in any emergency , getting them back into a classroom as quickly as possible is essential . Education to help them reach their potential is obviously important , but letting them be with their friends in a safe space also gives them a return to normalcy . <h> Disease <p> As if wars and conflict were n't enough , millions of children are also threatened by outbreaks of highly contagious and dangerous diseases . <p> Major outbreaks are now hitting the most vulnerable in conflict zones , greatly complicating the ability of aid agencies to respond . <p> A Ugandan health worker administers Ebola vaccine to a child in Kirembo village , near the border with the Democratic Republic of Congo in the Kasese district of Uganda , in June . ( James Akena/Reuters ) <p> The current Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo is the second-largest ever recorded and the first to occur in the middle of a conflict zone . There are 3,000 confirmed cases , including more than 900 children . <p> Measles has also become a threat to children despite safe and affordable vaccines . As with any public health campaign for children , educating the adult decision-makers is the key to eliminating this deadly threat . <h> Climate <p> Climate change is also affecting the most vulnerable . <p> More than 500 million children live in areas that have experienced extreme flooding , while 160 million live in regions that are subject to severe drought . <p> And the number of extreme weather events keeps rising . Mozambique was hit by back-to-back cyclones earlier this year , for example , and rebuilding will take years . <p> Children help clean the makeshift migrant camp after major rainfall hit the island of Chios on Dec. 11 , where thousands of refugees and migrants live in squalid conditions without water and electricity . According to government figures , hundreds more are arriving daily . ( Louisa Gouliamaki/AFP via Getty Images ) <p> Regions like the Sahel in Africa , where livelihoods rely on agriculture , grazing and fishing , are especially susceptible to the effects of climate change . <p> Solutions demand a long-term commitment . The positive news is that humanitarian organizations have made tremendous progress working with host governments in areas likely to experience extreme weather and flooding , helping to build more resilient communities and better prepare for disasters . <p> Still , the threats children are facing are overwhelming . The amount of funding for emergencies that UNICEF projects it will need in 2020 is $4.2 billion US , a staggering 3.5 times the amount required in 2010 , underscoring how frightening the world has become for young people . <p> But as someone who has worked in the international humanitarian sector for going on four decades , I refuse to be discouraged . We can not give up . <p> The international community , governments and civil society need to come together to protect and defend children 's rights . <p> The government of Canada is playing a global leadership role in prioritizing education for children in conflict zones and other protracted humanitarian crises . But as a nation we must do more to help children around the world . <p> People in this country clearly care about children 's welfare . I am confident Canadians will rise to the challenge and defend , for every child everywhere , the right to a safe and healthy childhood . <p> This column is part of CBC 's Opinion section .
