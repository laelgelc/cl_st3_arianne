<p> Governments have been making these promises since the early 1990s and they are never kept because the political pressures are far stronger from those who profit in the present -- the fossil fuel , automobile , shipping and aviation industries -- than from those who are merely frightened for their children 's ' future . <p> The industries are well organized , have lots of money to spend and focus tightly on stopping changes that threaten their business model . Private citizens are less organized , have far fewer resources and have many competing demands on their attention . <p> Inevitably , the industries succeed in sabotaging most attempts to cut emissions . <p> For a long time , the main strategy of the industries was denial . <p> At first , they denied outright that carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gas emissions were changing the climate . Never mind the science , just listen to this other guy in a white coat who says it is n't happening . <p> That worked for a while and the initial rapid response to the climate change threat lost speed through the late 1990s . <p> Flat denial became increasingly untenable in the early 21st century , however , and the emphasis of the deniers shifted to spreading doubt : the climate is always changing , lots of scientists do n't believe the warming is caused by human activities , the jury is still out . <p> Those lies worked for another 15 years , but gradually the real scientists realized they , too , had to organize . <p> There is now no government in the world ( except the United States ) that still goes along with the denialism . <p> Every major international body has accepted evidence that climate change is actually happening and that we are the cause . <p> So , it is time for another change of strategy by the fossil fuel industries and their allies . <p> If they can no longer hope to discredit the science or confuse the public about the evidence , maybe they can at least deflect and divert the pressure for effective action on climate change to targets that do not directly threaten sales of their products . <p> That 's where we are now and it was Dr. Michael Mann , distinguished professor of atmospheric science at Pennsylvania State University and director of the Earth System Science Center , who first spotted the new strategy of the fossil fuel industry 's shills . <p> " There is an attempt being made by them to deflect attention away from finding policy solutions for global warming towards promoting individual behaviour changes that affect people 's diets , travel choices and other personal behaviour , " he told The Observer newspaper in Britain recently . <p> " This is a deflection campaign and a lot of well-meaning people have been taken in by it . " <p> What gives the deflectors credibility is that they seem to be on the side of the angels . <p> They are not denying that climate change is real ; they just want you to use your bike more , eat less meat and recycle your waste . <p> What could be wrong with that ? <p> Nothing , of course . <p> You should be doing all of those things as they are a necessary part of the solution . <p> But they want you to do that instead of campaigning ( or at least voting ) for action that directly targets fossil-fuel use . If you feel you are already doing your bit in the climate emergency by changing your personal behaviour , then the pressure is off them . <p> They also encourage " doomism , " the notion that it 's too late in the game to do anything useful about climate change . <p> " This leads people down a path of despair and hopelessness and , finally , inaction , which actually leads us to the same place as outright climate-change denialism , " Mann said . <p> It really is quite late in the game . <p> We would have to cut global emissions by seven per cent a year ( instead of increasing them by 1.5 per cent annually ) to avoid breaching the never-exceed limit of 2 C higher average global temperature . That 's far beyond what we have ever done before , so there is considerable justification for pessimism . <p> However , pessimism is a luxury we can not afford . We have to keep working away at the task because every cut we make in emissions , however inadequate , gives us a little more time to deal with the rest of the problem . <p> The deflect , divert and distract campaign is often hard to distinguish from genuine attempts to change people 's lifestyles in positive ways -- and , frankly , there 's no point in trying . <p> Just do what they 're advocating ( bikes , meat , recycling , etc. ) and remember to also do the hard political and legal work of eliminating fossil fuel use .
