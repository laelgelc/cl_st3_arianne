<h> Letters to the Editor : Dec. 7 <h> Bike lanes snarl traffic <p> King Street used to be one of the better moving streets in the city . With the addition of bike lanes , they lost traffic lanes . Now , King street backs up from Wellington , to as far as Ridout and beyond . They have created a new bottleneck for the city . <p> King Street is full of buses . There used to be places where they could pull over . That is gone . Every block on King street between Ridout and Wellington has a bus stop and a traffic light . If you are driving in the right lane , you potentially are making two stops in each block , once for the bus , and then at the street light . <p> With so many bus stops , people are driving in the left lane . But now , the left lane backs up . Any drivers who have to turn left at a street or into a parking garage create a long queue of cars . <p> Worse , drivers who need to make a right turn drive in the left lane , then change lanes in small and dangerous spaces , to get around the buses . <p> Further , some bus stops are immediately after the intersection . When the light finally turns green , the bus accelerates only to immediately stop just after the intersection . No other cars can proceed through the intersection because the bus in front of them stopped . Or they try to proceed and get stuck in the middle of the intersection , blocking traffic , and frantically trying to switch lanes to go around the bus . <p> The city invested our tax money to create new traffic bottlenecks and they ruined King Street . <p> Andrew Mastronardi , London <h> Suspend foreign aid <p> I am so impressed that we are starting to talk about homelessness and addictions with a tone of compassion and care , as well as having multiple groups working together to discuss planning and implementing solutions . <p> We all must help , including our three levels of government . Instead of Canada sending so much of our hard earned tax dollars to other countries , I feel we need to keep our focus on our own people for awhile . I know that sounds selfish , but we need to get our own house in order . Canada should not have homelessness . <p> We need programs for people with mental health issues or hospitals where they can be cared for as human beings , the same as our elderly . We need to take care of them better . Keep our tax dollars in Canada . <p> Thank you for the articles . Keep them going . Let us read about any positive impacts and how the everyday person like me can make a difference . <p> Michelle Robere , London <h> Canada the best <p> In these days of dissatisfaction on all sides of the political , demographic and cultural spectrum I was fortunate to meet a special couple recently who shared their story with me . <p> This husband and wive are Chinese , but born in Cambodia . He suffered persecution in Cambodia and eventually paid an exorbitant sum to be ferried to Vietnam . From Vietnam he migrated to Thailand where he was interred for five years in a refugeee camp that essentially was a prison . He met his wive in this prison and they eventually were sponsored in 1989 as refugees by Canada . <p> Here the family has achieved a middle class economic status through hard work and probably some luck . Their daughter is a graduate of the Ivey Business School and doing well in $commerce . <p> They think Canada is the best country in the world . <p> It was inspiring to hear this couple speak of Canada as a great country . It made me think perhaps we have been spoiled and have had it too good or maybe we have lost the work ethic of our fore-fathers . <p> Andy McGuigan , London <h> Nuclear not answer <p> We see the provincial government has set up an advisory panel on climate change . <p> This gives the appearance that the government of Premier Doug Ford is serious about climate change . However , the province really only is looking at the after-effects rather than the prevention of a climate change emergency . <p> While it is important to be prepared for emergencies , unless we alter our lifestyles , and governments and corporations take responsibility for damage done , this could be a waste of money . <p> The science is in . We are , and will be seeing many more disasters on a scale we can not be prepared to deal with effectively . <p> Meanwhile , our province looks at building small-scale nuclear plants . <p> I wonder how our young people , who already are extremely concerned about their futures , feel about possibly being left to deal with more nuclear waste . <p> Margo Does , London <h> Killing the economy <p> It is becoming evident that the misplaced focus of the federal government now is concentrated on climate change issues at the expense of wealth producing commerce . <p> Increasingly we are hearing that investment and industrial activity in Canada is declining due to punitive climate change regulations . This is harming economy while the Canadian climate initiatives will have no effect on air quality or global warming . <p> The collapsing industrial sector is due to the distorted singular priority of tree huggers , bleeding hearts , and environmental do gooders . <p> John Peake , London <h> We all must act <p> Letters have been refuting the work of scientists and United Nations , quoting publications that provide statements against climate concerns that have funding from oil and tobacco , misinformation for a price . <p> Climate change is natural when occurring during thousands or millions of years , but not during 30 to 50 years . People say Canada is a small player . We rank ninth highest in greenhouse gas ( GHG ) emissions . By population , we are 39th . <p> London has declared a climate emergency but needs to act . Ontario should act now , remove conservatives now . Canada should not bow to the west . Alberta is the largest polluter due to oil and coal . Most provinces GHG emissions decreased but Alberta 's and Saskatchewan 's have increased . <p> Ron Casey , London <h> Help , do n't enable <p> Let me get this straight . Someone who wishes to commit a criminal act -- the ingestion of illegal drugs -- is provided with indoor space , a clean syringe and a professional advisor , all at taxpayers ' expense . How does this reduce the consumption of illegal drugs ? If anything , it encourages it . Where is the incentive to quit ? After " safely " injecting the drugs , these addicts then go out onto the street where police are expected to save their lives when they overdose . <p> Politicians should recognize that by allowing individuals to continue to inject illegal drugs , they are not assisting them in turning their life around , they are perpetuating and aggravating the problem . Taxpayers ' money would be better spent on rehabilitation services . <p> John Lisowski , London <h> Leave it to ' big boys ' <p> NASCAR stock car racing in the U.S. , known as the Monster energy series , starts in February and ends in November with racing almost every weekend .
