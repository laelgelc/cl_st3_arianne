<p> The evening grosbeak that persisted at Fanshawe Conservation Area through January is London 's bird of the year for 2019 for Free Press nature columnist Paul Nicholson . ( Paul Nicholson/Special to Postmedia News ) <p> After 12 months of bird watching , I enjoy reflecting back on the many aspects of the year that members of the Southwestern Ontario birding and nature community were able to savour . Here are my highlights . <p> London 's bird of the year : A female evening grosbeak first seen at Fanshawe Conservation Area by London birding expert Lucas Foerster last December hung around well into January , giving lots of local birders the opportunity to admire it . Because of poor boreal forest seed crops , there were more evening grosbeaks than usual in southern Ontario last winter . Other London birds that get honourable mention include the American white pelicans seen at the Komoka Ponds in July , sedge wrens found at the Kelly Stanton Environmental Significant Area , a summer tanager last month at the Thames Valley Golf Course , and this month 's slaty-backed gull . <p> Best bird podcast : I love the Songbirding podcast series that debuted this year . Recorded , produced and narrated by Hamilton birder Rob Porter , these free podcasts will transport you to the Bruce Peninsula where you will be led on wonderful virtual birding hikes during which you will be enveloped by birdsong . You can try one at songbirding.com . <p> Best YouTube bird series : The Birds of North America videos hosted by the charismatic Jason Ward and directed by Rob Meyer became popular for a good reason : they are tremendous . At less than 10 minutes , each of the more than 20 episodes is short and sweet . This is n't simply a bunch of species profiles . Ward talks us through some wonderful and surprising aspects of birdwatching life . <p> Most important bird study : The Decline of the North American Avifauna , published in the journal Science , documented what an international research team that included the Cornell Lab of Ornithology and Environment and Climate Change Canada called a staggering decline of bird populations . Since 1970 , there are 2.9 billion fewer birds representing a loss of 29 per cent . For the first time , the results showed widespread losses even among common birds . Ouch . <p> Best bird movie : The feature-length documentary that profiled the magnificent Philippine eagle , one of the world 's rarest eagle species , was called Bird of Prey . Produced by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology and directed by Eric Liner , this film looks at the conservation efforts in the face of population growth , habitat loss and greed . The messaging of the film is important for all of us . To stream the movie visit birdofpreymovie.com . <p> Best unsung Southwestern Ontario birding location : Springwater Conservation Area in east Elgin County might be a best-kept secret . An excellent eight-kilometre trail system winds through 240 hectares of Carolinian forest and around a pond and marsh . This spring , take the easy 40-minute drive from downtown London to enjoy Springwater 's range of woodpeckers and migrating warblers . <p> London 's green leader of 2019 : As executive director of the London Environmental Network , Skylar Franke has done a tremendous job leading on collaborative green initiatives that range from hands-on workshops to promotion of sustainability programs for businesses . For details visit londonenvironment.net . <p> Best new bird guide book : Greystone Books ' new Best Places to Bird in Ontario by brothers Mike and Ken Burrell is a great addition to my collection of field guides . Thirty provincial hot spots are profiled in detail and a useful birding strategy for each is shared . <h> Nature notes <p> Early results from London 's Dec. 14 Christmas Bird Count included a slaty-backed gull . The bird was found by the dump south of Highway 401 on Friday the 13th . It hung around for the next day 's count . This was a first-ever record for Middlesex County . A Wilson 's warbler was also found by Pete Read and Rob Read .
