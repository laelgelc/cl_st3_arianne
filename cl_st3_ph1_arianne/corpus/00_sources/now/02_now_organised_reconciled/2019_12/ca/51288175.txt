<p> In an effort to help reduce conflicts between humans and bears -- including an attempt to decrease the number of bears being killed -- B.C. conservation officers have been ramping up efforts to ensure residents remove bear attractants . <p> Each year , the B.C. Conservation Officer Service ( COS ) receives over 25,000 reports of conflicts between humans and wildlife , with many related to bears and attractants . <p> The B.C. Ministry of Environment and Climate Change Strategy stated in a news release today ( December 20 ) that this year , the COS conducted bear-attractant audits across the province starting in the summer and increasing in September and October . <p> Officers inspected residential , recreational , and commercial areas to check if attractants were secured using bear-proof bins , if excess fruit has been picked from trees , and electric fencing was used around livestock . <p> In particular , officers focused on areas with a history of bear conflicts and where garbage , pet food , birdseed , or compost had attracted bears . <p> After 704 inspections , the audits resulted in a total of 732 enforcement actions , including 76 charges , 301 warnings , and 355 dangerous wildlife protection orders , which direct property owners to remove an attractant or receive a $575 fine . <p> In B.C. 's South Coast region , which includes Metro Vancouver and the Fraser Valley , 79 inspections were conducted and 202 enforcement actions <p> A second phase of audits will be conducted in the spring when bears emerge from hibernation . <p> The B.C. environment ministry explained that access to non-natural food sources alter the natural behavior of bears and wildlife , which lose their fear of people and develop appetites for non-natural food . <p> " Relocated wildlife often fail to adapt to their new habitat , " the B.C. government news release states . " As a result , they make long-distance movements , starve , or return to their original area or another community in search of easy food . The ministry and COS are determined to significantly reduce the role of attractants through both education and , where necessary , strengthened enforcement measures . " <p> In an effort to reduce the numbers of wildlife that are destroyed , conservative officers are trying to educate humans about changing their behavior instead . <p> " The conservation officer service can not stress enough that the best way to keep people safe and bears from being destroyed is to secure attractants around your home , business or campsite , " COS chief conservation officer Doug Forsdick stated . " The conservation officer service hopes that through these attractant audits , the public will recognize that more needs to be done to ensure everyone does their part to help keep wildlife wild . " <p> Fines due to attractants can range from $230 to $575 . <p> For more information about managing wildlife attractants , visit the WildSafeBC website.
