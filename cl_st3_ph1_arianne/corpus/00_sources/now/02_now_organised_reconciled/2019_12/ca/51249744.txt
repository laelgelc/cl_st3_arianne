<h> MICHAEL FEKETE : Volunteering to build a better world can be an antidote to despair <p> Contributed <p> Published : Dec 05 at 6 a.m . <p> Sorry you must be at least 19 years of age to consume this content . <p> Volunteers Shelley Hounsell and Kate Newton arrange cutlery for the evening dinner at St. George 's United Church in Halifax on Jan. 12 , 2019 . St. George 's serves Saturday meals to those in dire straits . Hounsell has volunteered for several years ; Newton , who donated food , was serving for the first time . - Tim Krochak <p> MICHAEL FEKETE <p> These are challenging times to be an optimist . There is growing alarm in the face of climate change . We are witnessing daily attacks on liberal democracy . Public discourse increasingly amplifies messages of anger and division . <p> As these threats become overwhelming , we run the risk of despair taking hold of our collective consciousness . We are left asking : what can we do in response ? <p> A game-changing response is to engage in our communities and the world by volunteering our skills and experience to advance human rights . History -- including our own experience in Canada -- is filled with examples of ordinary people banding together to improve lives . By volunteering together , we can ensure that tangible action prevents despair from taking root . <p> Dec. 5 is International Volunteer Day . It is a day that is not as widely celebrated or acknowledged in Canada as it should be . <p> The United Nations states , " volunteerism benefits both society at large and the individual volunteer by strengthening trust , solidarity and reciprocity among citizens , and by purposefully creating opportunities for participation . " <p> Volunteering contributes to social cohesion , providing all of us with tangible opportunities to be part of the solution to problems that we face . <p> I know firsthand that the act of volunteering can be as meaningful to those giving as it is to those receiving . <p> A dear friend and colleague , the late Randy Pepper , was a leading litigator who found purpose and belonging through volunteering . Randy first volunteered with Crossroads International , a Canadian-based global human rights organization . His overseas experience in Lesotho changed his world view and gave him higher purpose . <p> It was the beginning of a lifelong engagement with international development . Randy 's passion for volunteering was infectious . He inspired countless people around him to take action -- myself included . <p> For more than a decade , I have volunteered with Give a Day , a campaign that encourages donations of a day 's pay to the Stephen Lewis Foundation in support of communities in sub-Saharan Africa affected by HIV/AIDS . The premise is that we can all give of ourselves for a day to bring about positive change . <p> When we combine our efforts and work together , we are more attuned to progress and hope . We witness change . Even if incremental in the moment , the sum of people 's contributions can be enormous . Consider the incalculable impact that volunteers , doctors , activist grandmothers and women 's rights champions around the globe have had on the fight against HIV/AIDS . <p> Volunteering brings us into community with people whose experiences differ from our own , deepening our understanding and fostering dialogue . <p> Supporting organizations like the Stephen Lewis Foundation and Crossroads International gives us a tangible way to act ; it gives us opportunities to support the growth and resilience of local communities , whether these communities are in our backyards or on the other side of the world .
