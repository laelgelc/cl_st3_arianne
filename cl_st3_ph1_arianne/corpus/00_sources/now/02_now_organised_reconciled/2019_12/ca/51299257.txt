<h> Hanes : Is Montreal losing the war on the car ? <h> Hanes : Is Montreal losing the war on the car ? <p> Car-sharing service Car2go is ending on Feb. 29 . The loss of this alternative to car ownership is bad news for the fight against climate change.Dario Ayala / Montreal Gazette files <p> The demise of Car2go , a popular vehicle-sharing service used by many Montrealers , says a lot about the state of transportation in this city at a time when this issue is most crucial . <p> The parent company of the diminutive smart cars scattered around Ville-Marie and the Plateau-Mont-Royal , announced it is pulling up stakes across North America on Feb. 29 . Citing fierce competition , it said it will concentrate its efforts on its European operations instead . <p> In Montreal , that will leave Communauto as the only fleet of short-term rental vehicles floating around the city for urban dwellers to use on that trip to Ikea or jaunt to the cabane ? sucre on a Sunday afternoon . At least we 'll have that . <p> Let 's hope Communauto can meet the demand left by car2go 's departure and thrive . <p> As Montreal seeks to diversify its transportation options with the likes of Bixi , e-bikes , e-scooters or Ubers , the quickie curbside car rental provides an indispensable service -- and one available all year round in this wintry city . It offers the convenience of a car to meet punctual needs without the hassle of permanent vehicle ownership -- like finding parking in the dense inner boroughs . <p> Though Car2go specifically had limitations ( its two-seaters were n't exactly family friendly or conducive to transporting large purchases ) , car sharing in general is an important bridge between occasional vehicle reliance and total dependence . As Montreal and all cities try to reduce congestion , improve health and slash emissions , getting people to drive less is essential . And subscription rentals act as a stepping stone to changing habits -- at least in the neighbourhoods where they are available . <p> Because let 's face it , as much as Montrealers hate traffic , eschew pipelines and say we care about climate change , we have an enduring love affair with the personal automobile . <p> The latest data from the Observatoire de Grand Montr ? al reveals that the number of vehicles in the 82 municipalities of the Communaut ? m ? tropolitaine de Montr ? al rose by 6.8 per cent between 2013 and 2018 . That 's higher than the 4.6-per-cent increase in the population . What 's more , a greater proportion of those 1.9 million vehicles on the road are SUVs . <p> Keep in mind these region-wide statistics may not account for disparities in the vehicle-ownership statistics between downtown Montreal and sprawling exurbs . But from travel patterns , it 's a safe bet that these SUVs and cars are ending up on Montreal 's teeming roads and highways . <p> Another study by McGill School of Urban Planning and consulting group Green Resilience Strategies provides an telling look at the distances being driven by residents in and around the metropolitan area . Whereas Montrealers drive on average 19 kilometres a day , off-island residents travel 34 kilometres . And drilling down deeper , residents of central Montreal neighbourhoods drive just 12 kilometres on a typical weekday while those in the furthest reaches of the region drive two or three times as far . <p> Perhaps the real shocker is not that suburbanites drive so much more , but that Montrealers , who have alternatives , still motor around so much . Even as Montreal eliminates parking spots and some boroughs raise the cost of vignettes for SUVs , promotes public transit , encourages more cycling and walking , and pilots trendy novelties like e-scooters or e-bikes , the car is still king . <p> The latest inventory of Quebec 's greenhouse gas emissions , those for 2017 , released in recent weeks , demonstrate the extent to which we are losing the so-called war on the car . <p> After dropping off , emissions have been on the rise since 2013 . And Quebec 's Number 1 source is transportation , accounting for 43 per cent of the total . This includes air and rail , of course , but road traffic makes up the lion 's share , 79 per cent of transportation output . Between 1990 and 2017 , Quebec 's tailpipe emissions have climbed 23 per cent . <p> These are sobering statistics . Not only is the trend headed in the wrong direction , this major source of greenhouse gases is not covered by cap and trade -- the centrepiece of Quebec 's reductions plan . <p> So the so-called war on the car is not some sanctimonious liberal urban ideology as many detractors would have us believe ; it 's the battle to win the climate emergency and the fight for a sustainable future . <p> But old habits drive hard , whether due to the necessity of moving to a far-flung bedroom community because of Montreal 's growing inaffordability or the personal choice inherent in driving a gas-guzzling SUV . <p> So as we wait for the new R ? seau express m ? tropolitain to be built , suffer through overcrowding on the m ? tro , wait for buses stuck in a maintenance backlog , or brave the bone-chilling cold of winter biking , the departure of Car2go has Montreal shifting into reverse .
