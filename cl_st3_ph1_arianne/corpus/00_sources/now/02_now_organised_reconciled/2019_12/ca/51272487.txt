Two leaders of the opposition , Conservative leader Andrew Scheer and Jeremy Corbyn , U.K. Labour Party leader , were unceremoniously left by the political wayside in different ways . <p> Scheer was taken down by his own party , weeks after an election defeat , while Corbyn was rejected by the British people when they anointed Boris Johnson as their prime minister for the next four years . Corbyn immediately resigned on election night . <p> An electoral defeat is one thing , but to be hounded out of a leadership position post-election is worse . Scheer is likely wishing he resigned on election night , saving himself , his family , friends , staff and colleagues from feelings of pain , betrayal and confusion . <p> Still , there was some humanity . Scheer 's tired eyes when he announced " the most difficult decision " of his life signaled a hurried and emotional decision . Prime Minister Justin Trudeau , undoubtedly reminded of his own experience as the son of a prime minister , clearly empathized with Scheer as he paid homage to Scheer 's family and to his record of public service , including the position as speaker of the House of Commons . <p> However , that impressive record failed to translate into substantive leadership skills . Scheer 's personal and political compass appeared broken while errors mounted . Unable to explain his job experience as an insurance broker , he also surprisingly revealed that he held American citizenship . At a time when voters were asking for respect , he aggressively attacked Trudeau at an official campaign debate . <p> After the election , he demanded that the House of Commons be returned , but when it did , he was absent from Question Period on the opening day . More inexplicably , he chose a Liberal floor-crosser as deputy leader . In addition to faulty judgment , he was completely out of tune on social issues , such as LGBTQ rights and remained indifferent to the climate change crisis . <p> The final nail in the coffin was information that his children 's fees at private schools were partially funded by the Conservative party . Similar to corporate positions involving a promotion or move , subsidizing a leader 's lifestyle , is part of the relationship between a leader and the party . Typically , these arrangements lack transparency , although they definitely require auditing . <p> The revelation has created an unexpected dilemma for Tory donors and frankly , for members of all parties . As a result , the Conservatives now find themselves at a crossroad of ethical and political decision making . But it will not be easy unless a draft for one candidate is on the horizon . <h> YOU MIGHT BE INTERESTED IN ... <p> A leadership contest , while exciting and energizing , is usually anything but civil . The lust for power often overwhelms partisan manipulators . War rooms will be set up , schemes will be endlessly discussed and " back channels " will be required to reach other candidates . <p> If unchecked , leaderships can become a microcosm of very nasty politics unleashing forces within a party -- forces that can be hard to control . Scheer sensed this danger in his speech , when he asked that his party remain united . <p> There are endless decisions ahead for the Conservatives . Will red Tories battle social conservatives for the soul of their party ? Having merged once , is it possible that two separate parties will emerge again ? Who actually holds the reins of power and influence in the Conservative Party today ? Is it Stephen Harper ? Or does Brian Mulroney still hold sway ? Was this an orchestrated political knife attack by one potential candidate ? If so , who was that candidate ? <p> Finally , what does it mean for the country and for Canada 's place in the world ? Scheer was prepared to return partisanship to what is increasingly an independent Senate . Will a new leader feel the same way ? How will the chosen candidate reconcile support for energy projects and the imperative to deal with climate change ? Under Harper and Scheer , the Conservatives were very wary of China . Will a new Leader continue that foreign policy vein ? Scheer also wanted to cut foreign aid by 25 per cent , igniting loud opposition . Does that remain a policy ? <p> Forcing a leader to resign is messy . Choosing a new one and achieving a policy mix is likely to be even messier .
