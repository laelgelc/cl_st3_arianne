<p> There are some 37 million Canadians , and it would be unwieldy to ask each of them what they thought all the time ; in the same way , we ca n't sample each square inch of soil for nutrient level . <p> So we use statistical reasoning to sample just enough to grasp the larger picture . On a hundred acres if you randomly sample 10 or more spots , you will have a more than decent sample average . In statistics , it is generally accepted that sampling 100 individuals of any group will achieve the task at hand ; but more commonly , in assessing millions of people , a sample group of 1000 is used . <p> That is to avoid that one chance in 20 of missing the group altogether . <p> I am not here to talk about soil . <p> Any given week just in agriculture , the results of one poll or survey or another are released . It is a familiar tactic in the forever war of assessing or influencing public opinion . Sample one thousand people with questions and interpretations massaged enough to come up with the desired answer , and you can send a bill to someone . <p> One example this week is some poll or another about whether Canadians feel the food system is heading in the right direction . So 1000 clumps of soil -- I mean , Canadians -- had their moment in the sun to express an opinion about something they typically do n't think about , or could care less about unless it is just before lunch . <p> It is all nonsense , because no survey is ever publicized that delivers an answer the sponsoring organization is n't in favour of . <p> I used to wonder who pollsters were asking , because I can not remember ever being formally asked anything in my entire life . Can you ? <p> The answer to that lies in the numbers . <p> If there are 37 million Canadians then we should assume about 25 per cent of them are not able to be sampled -- too young or too old or living off the grid or under a rock . So let 's just say , if all samples are of 1000 people , on average we should expect as individual Canadians to be asked our opinion once in every 27,750 polls or surveys . <p> That seems remote , and it is . If we can estimate that there is at least a poll or survey a day in Canada , it turns out that we can expect as Canadians to be asked our opinion on something only once every 76 years . <p> If there are two surveys a day , or three , we maybe can look forward to being asked our opinion two or three times in a normal lifetime . These are still all trivial numbers . <p> I will be so annoyed if my lifetime moment comes and it is something like , do you think toilet paper should hang on the inside or the outside ? <p> It does n't matter though , because when my moment does come , I will likely do what I suspect so many Canadians do : refuse to be sampled . <p> Maybe my moment will come in a climate change survey , as there seem to be quite a few of these going on now . <p> Pollster : " Do you believe the earth is warming , that it is caused by humans , and placing us all in peril ?
