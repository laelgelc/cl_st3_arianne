" <p> Wilkinson 's brutally honest answer highlighted the burning flame of misogyny -- a hatred and contempt of women -- that looms over Parliament . <p> Imagine if the Prime Minister had appointed a male politician as environment minister in 2015 . Would there have been dissension with the provinces about the federal imposition of a carbon tax ? Would there have been an uproar about Bill C-69 -- the legislation designed to impose strong environmental , social , health and economic regulatory oversight on designated projects ? <p> Yes , of course . The debate was always going to be fractious . But as Wilkinson instinctively recognized , it may not have reached the same vicious level that it did with McKenna , who has since become infrastructure minister . <p> Indeed , she was already receiving cyber-smears when former Conservative minister Gerry Ritz coined the derogatory phrase " Climate Barbie , " uncorking a misogynist genie . No longer could McKenna ignore the insulting derision . She demanded an apology from Ritz which was quickly given along with a call from Andrew Scheer . <p> So far , so good . <p> Yet , the issue did not disappear . The day after McKenna 's hard-won victory in Ottawa Centre , her campaign office sign was defaced with huge red letters across her mouth depicting the c-word . <p> Personal attacks are nothing new in politics and men , too , have to face them . <h> YOU MIGHT BE INTERESTED IN ... <p> When Conservative ads in 1993 made fun of Jean Chr ? tien 's facial features , the country erupted with fury at the Tories and support for the Liberals . <p> But the current attacks on female politicians , especially when integrated with contentious issues such as climate change , are in danger of becoming national security issues . <p> Jo Cox , a British Labour MP , was brutally murdered by a right-wing extremist because of her anti-Brexit stance . <p> And if a female is a member of a visible minority , misogyny will often be combined with racism , creating a double jeopardy . <p> Sadly , misogyny shows no signs of slowing down . It is fuelled by the anonymity of the internet and enablers like Donald Trump . And it gains traction from so-called Incels ( embittered , often violent men or boys who feel they have been rejected by women and call themselves " involuntary celibates " ) . <h> YOU MIGHT BE INTERESTED IN ... <p> The consequences of ignoring the hatred are serious . <p> In 1989 , we received a shocking taste of what was to come , when Marc L ? pine , sometimes known as Canada 's first Incel , separated women from men at Montreal 's ? cole Polytechnique , then killed 14 women . Ten other women were injured along with four men . <p> That attack , instead of discouraging women , spawned a wave of activism which must not falter now . <p> Sadly , though , it might be . <p> Only a third of the candidates running in the upcoming British election , for example , are female and many sitting MPs chose not to run . <p> Losing women from participation in public life would be a dreadful consequence of unchecked misogyny . <p> A politician 's life is tough at the best of times . If an extra layer of aggravation or security concerns are added , decision-making capacity can be impacted . Relations with colleagues , staff or family can become strained . Stress may affect physical health . <p> While academic research reveals the causes of misogyny , it is incumbent on the rest of us to raise its ugly profile and unite against it . <p> Get more opinion in your inbox <p> Get the latest from your favourite Star columnists with our Opinion email newsletter . <p> Misogyny must be stopped , not by one individual woman who may be the " target of the day , " but by all of us individually and by our institutions , including political parties , Parliamentary administration , Elections Canada and law enforcement agencies . <p> And yes , by social media . Freedom of speech does not include freedom to hate . <p> A new minority Parliament opens this week . Despite contentious issues , surely it is time for unqualified , all party support for the rights of women to safely enjoy their freedom to work . <p> Penney Collenette is an adjunct professor of law at the University of Ottawa and was a senior director of the Prime Minister 's Office for Jean Chr ? tien .
