<p> The union representing thousands of secondary school teachers says it offered to remain in the classroom if the province would commit to keeping class sizes and e-learning status quo . <p> But the offer was rejected , says Glen Hodgson , president of District 4 of the Ontario Secondary School Teachers ' Federation representing Near North high school teachers . <p> " We were willing to take the money off the table to avoid this job action , but they refused . " <p> Near North was one of nine school boards across the province to participate in a second one-day strike , Wednesday . <p> Teachers participated in a province-wide walkout on Dec. 4 . <p> " The minister continues to say there 's no money , yet the government 's top bureaucrats received a 14 per cent wage increase on top of the 20 per cent increase to their housing coffers , " Hodgson said . <p> " There 's no money for the workers , but there 's lots for ( Premier Doug ) Ford 's friends . The province spent millions taking down windmills , millions breaking clean energy contracts and they 've spent millions fighting climate change in the courts . The government 's priorities are the problem . " <p> Troy Simkins , president of Educational Support Staff Professionals District 4 , representing 280 Near North support staff , spent the day visiting his members on picket lines . <p> " The reason they 've closed all of the elementary schools is because there 's not enough staff to safely supervise all of the children , " he said outside Woodland Public School . <p> " Supervision is a huge job and teachers ca n't do our job while we 're on the picket line . They had to shut the schools for safety reasons . " <p> Simkins said support staff such as educational assistants and early childhood educators are an integral part of the education system who assist many students . <p> " It says a lot for our school board when they have to shut down their schools . " <p> In a statement , Nipissing MPP Vic Fedeli said the province 's aim is a fiscally sustainable education system . <p> " Union leadership has made clear they will strike again unless the government accepts their demand for a $1.5-billion increase in compensation , which includes pay and benefits . We value teachers and their commitment to our students . However , OSSTF teachers on average make over $92,000 per year and are the second highest paid in the nation . " <p> Near North teacher Ryan Marshall holds a picket sign Wednesday in front of Widdifield Secondary School.Jennifer Hamilton-McCharles , The Nugget <p> Fedeli claimed the province has been fair , reasonable and focused on keeping teachers in class . <p> " However , repeated escalation at the expense of our students ' education , to advance higher compensation , higher wages and even more generous benefits is unacceptable for parents and students in our province . " <p> Fedeli said the province has called on OSSTF to cease from escalation , stay at the table and focus on improving learning in classroom , not enhancing compensation for its members . <p> " Yesterday ( Tuesday ) , our government delivered another tentative agreement with education workers ( EWAO ) that keeps students in class . This is yet another example of our government demonstrating reasonableness , and a focus on our students at the bargaining table , " he said . <p> " As shown with EWAO , and the Canadian Union of Public Employees earlier , the government has remained a constructive force at the table working hard to ensure parents have the predictability they deserve . We will continue to negotiate in good faith with a sincere hope that the other education unions will match our reasonable offerings and make students the centre of our focus . " <p> The NDP riding association for Nipissing pledged its support to education workers . <p> " We completely support OSSTF in labour actions that put quality public education for future generations first . Education workers want class sizes that support real learning and the elimination of the mandatory e-learning that will put so many students at risk , especially students with special needs and those who do n't have access to quality broadband , " said association president Rob Boulet . <p> " While the ( Doug ) Ford government has had no issue giving raises to its ministers , they have attacked the charter right to fair collective bargaining . Education workers have proposed a way forward that will keep students in the classroom while protecting public education and their rights at the same time . As a riding association , we urge Minister Stephen Lecce to accept these proposals and negotiate . " <p> The teachers have been without a contract since their last one expired at the end of August , and their union has blamed the Progressive Conservative government for the lack of progress in their negotiations . <p> The two parties have n't held talks since last week , but Wednesday afternoon the province and OSSTF confirmed they would hold new talks early next week . <p> Education Minister Stephen Lecce said he believes a " voluntary agreement " can be achieved , but called on the union halt further job action . <p> " My message to OSSTF is to cancel the strike that is needlessly hurting students in the classrooms of this province , " he said . <p> Union president Harvey Bischof said his team will to return to the table at any time , but the government needs to get serious about the talks . <p> " What they should do is bring proposals to the table , " Bischof said . <p> The government has said the key issue at the bargaining table is compensation , with the province offering a one per cent annual wage increase and the union asking for around two per cent . <p> Bischof said the union has not ruled out taking further job actions -- including a full strike . <p> " Those things are certainly a possibility , " he said . " It 's one that I hope we never have to get to . "
