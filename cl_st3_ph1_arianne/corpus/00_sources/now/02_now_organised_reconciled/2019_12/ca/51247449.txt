<h> Flood report singles out North Bay wastewater facility <p> Temporary piping is laid across Memorial Drive in May to help pump treated effluent from the wastewater treatment plant after continued rainfall and rising water levels on Lake Nipissing threatened to back up the city 's storm sewers . A report on this year 's flood events has recommended the City of North Bay install bypass piping at the plant to limit the impacts of future flooding . Michael Lee/The Nugget <p> Ontario 's special flood advisor is recommending the City of North Bay take certain steps to protect its wastewater treatment plant from future flooding after rising water levels on Lake Nipissing prompted emergency pumps to be installed this past spring . <p> The Ontario government released a report last week , prepared by special flood advisor Doug McNeil , which included a recommendation for North Bay in particular , but also any other municipality in a similar situation , to install appropriate bypass piping at its wastewater treatment plant in order to improve its " resiliency " and limit the impact that flooding could have on public health and safety . <p> On May 9 , the city installed three emergency pumps across Memorial Drive at the wastewater treatment plant , at a cost of $159,500 plus HST , after rising water levels on Lake Nipissing threatened to flood the facility . <p> The report notes that in a worst-case scenario , a failure at the plant could have resulted in large volumes of untreated wastewater being released onto the shores of Lake Nipissing , weeks if not months of reduced wastewater treatment capabilities , sewer backups in low-lying areas , and possible evacuations . <p> Speaking to reporters after Tuesday night 's council meeting , Mayor Al McDonald said staff would look at the recommendation closely , adding that a major project such as this would require a cost analysis . <p> " I think they did determine that due to climate change we 're going to start seeing these things happen more frequently , that we have to look at that plan , " he said . <p> The report from the special advisor includes a section on the impact the spring floods had on North Bay and several surrounding communities . <p> Both the Town of Mattawa and Municipality of West Nipissing declared states of emergencies in May , while approximately 60,000 sandbags were deployed in the Jocko Point and Beaucage areas on Nipissing First Nation . <p> Local businesses , roads and infrastructure in the Municipality of Callander and Nipissing Township also were affected . <p> Along with the emergency pumps , North Bay put a lift station bypass plan in place to limit flows to the wastewater treatment plant , if needed . <p> But a report to council from city staff dated in August said the plant is the only sewage treatment facility for North Bay 's 52,000 residents , meaning a flood at the plant would have caused thousands of homes to back up with sewage . <p> Coun. Chris Mayne , who chairs the city 's infrastructure and operations committee , said the issue at the plant was caused by water levels on Lake Nipissing getting so high that the facility began to lose its ability to drain treated water into the lake . <p> He said staff met with the special advisor in September and shared their concerns about having to use the temporary pumps , which ran for approximately four to five weeks . <p> On whether additional piping will be installed , Mayne said it may only be used every 10 years and could cost more in the long run to maintain . <p> He suggested it may be better to lease the equipment from a company instead , but said the piping will be looked at . <p> " There 's nothing in this year 's budget , but it will be considered in the next few months and that may become part of our capital budget over the next few years . "
