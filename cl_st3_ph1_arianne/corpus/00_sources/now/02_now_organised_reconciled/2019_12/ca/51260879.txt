<h> The question for New Democrats in 2017 : Who are we now ? <p> EDMONTON -- If any political happening has been flying low under Alberta 's political radar recently , it 's the federal New Democrats ' leadership race . With the provincial merger of the Alberta PCs and Wildrose in the rearview mirror and the United Conservative Party 's own leadership vote coming up next month , even Alberta 's politics junkies have been largely mute on the federal race . <p> Pat Stogran managed to generate some interest here when he was considering entering the fray . Stogran was a former commander with Edmonton 's Princess Patricia Canadian Light Infantry ; a lot of veterans remember him and like him for the work he tried to do as the federal veteran 's ombudsman . He 's a democratic reformer who believes that Ottawa is broken . When he bailed ( because , according to him , party brass did not want him to win ) , interest in the contest out here largely evaporated . A few Alberta NDP MLAs were planning to support him ; with no candidate west of Churchill , Manitoba , I expect interest in the race between Portage Avenue and the foothills will remain anemic . <p> But we 're in the voting phase of the contest now , so on Sunday I tuned in to the final campaign debate . The crowd at the Hamilton Convention Centre heard a lot of references to Tommy Douglas and Jack Layton , the twin legends of CCF/NDP politics . What they did n't hear , or see , is any evidence that the legends live on in some way . <p> Do n't get me wrong : I know three of the four candidates -- Niki Ashton , Charlie Angus and Guy Caron , the current members of Parliament -- and know each of them to be a very capable parliamentarian . But none of them is an orator on Douglas 's level . None of them is as instantly likeable and relatable as Layton . <p> Angus probably comes closest on likability -- a former punk rock guitarist who likes to play up his blue collar background ( I learned on Sunday , for example , that his grandfather died in a mining accident ) . Out of the four , he 's probably the one you 'd choose to have a beer with . <p> Niki Ashton is smart and probably the most ideological of the four . She 's a proud feminist who takes no-prisoners public stands on income inequality and climate change . She is young and relates well with millennial voters . Young voters helped to propel Justin Trudeau into office ; Ashton thinks she can follow the same path with young progressives . She comes from a New Democrat family ; her father was an NDP MLA from Manitoba , a former cabinet minister and a recent candidate for the Manitoba NDP leadership . <p> Guy Caron believes he can win back much of the NDP 's lost support in Quebec . Layton was able to bring 59 Quebec MLAs to Ottawa in 2011 ; today , the NDP 's Quebec caucus has just 16 members . Without a provincial wing to provide infrastructure , the NDP is a tough sell in Quebec . I suspect the 2011 Orange Crush was an anomaly that wo n't be repeated . <p> The candidates themselves seem to be getting along but it 's not at all clear that their individual constituencies -- the voters they 're going after -- can comfortably co-exist . <p> Which brings us around to the purported frontrunner , Ontario MPP Jagmeet Singh , a practising Sikh who wears a turban and carries a kirpan . Even with the Parti Qu ? b ? cois out of power , Quebec remains a political minefield for visible minorities who wear religious symbols . One Quebec NDP MP , Pierre Nantel , said Quebecers are uncomfortable with politicians sporting " ostentatious religious symbols . " That message , of course , was meant for Singh and his supporters . <p> Singh will be interesting to watch . He spoke eloquently in Hamilton ( he gives a good speech , by the way ) about the challenges of being a member of a visible minority . He 's calling for a ban on racial profiling and claims he has been stopped by police for no reason other than the colour of his skin . He appeals to suburban voters , especially in the critical electoral region surrounding Toronto . <p> Looking over the field , however , it seems to me the big question facing New Democrats now is existential . Who are they ? Who do they want to be ? The candidates themselves seem to be getting along but it 's not at all clear that their individual constituencies -- the voters they 're going after -- can comfortably co-exist . <p> NDP MPs may have been distancing themselves from Nantel 's comments , but the core of his statement remains absolutely true : A great many erstwhile NDP supporters in Quebec would be uncomfortable with a leader in a turban . Similarly , Ashton 's appeal to millennials and her support among the party 's hard left might not play well with Angus 's lunchbucket supporters , and vice versa . <p> The NDP/CCF has always been a tent big enough to include both farmers and union members , and more recently environmentalists and feminists . But still the question remains : What do New Democrats want New Democrats to do ? Do they want to be a pragmatic , electorally competitive party that might actually contend for power one day , as they were under Jack Layton ? Or do they want to remain ideologically pure and be a party of conscience , embracing radical policies like the Leap Manifesto ? <p> My suspicion is that question will remain unresolved and , unless the NDP formally abandons ideological purity , it 's probably destined to be a third party indefinitely . <p> That 's not to say New Democrats are electorally irrelevant . Far from it . They are locked in a constant battle with the Liberal party for the center-left . Each candidate is capable of pulling voters back from Trudeau 's embrace -- particularly Caron in Quebec , Ashton with millennials and Singh with visible minorities and suburbanites . <p> So the Liberals will watch the race with a certain about of anxiety , while Conservatives watch with hope in their hearts . Without a doubt , Layton 's impressive seat total in 2011 , coupled with vote-splitting with the Liberals , is what gave Stephen Harper his majority . <p> The CPC wants to see a stronger showing from the NDP in 2019 . So whoever ends up leading the NDP , one thing is certain : That person will not figure prominently in Conservative attack ads . <p> The views , opinions and positions expressed by all iPolitics columnists and contributors are the author 's alone .
