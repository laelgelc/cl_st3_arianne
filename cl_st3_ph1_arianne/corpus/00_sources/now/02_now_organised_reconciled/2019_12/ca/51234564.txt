<h> What We 're Watching : Premiers ' meeting set for Monday ; Parliament to resume on Thursday <p> Let the doors be opened : 43rd parliament set to get rolling on December 5 <p> A little over six weeks after Canadian voters rebalanced the power dynamics in the Chamber by downgrading the governing Liberals from majority to minority status , the House of Commons will officially reopen for business on Thursday morning . The 338 new and returning MPs elected under the banner of the 43rd Parliament will descend on the Chamber that day to await word from the Senate that their attendance is requested for the Speech from the Throne . <p> Before that invitation can be issued , however , they 'll have to perform their first collective task of the new session : Choosing a speaker , which is done via secret ballot . <p> Under House rules , the ballot lists the names of all eligible MPs who have n't requested that their names be withdrawn from consideration -- which inevitably includes at at least a few MPs who have absolutely no interest in the job but failed to have it removed from the list before the cut-off . <p> But as previewed by iPolitics last month , there are also at least five MPs who have been actively campaigning for the job -- most notably Geoff Regan , who held the post during the previous parliament . Regan will be competing against a trio of MPs who served as deputy and associate speakers -- Conservative Bruce Stanton , New Democrat Carole Hughes and Liberal Anthony Rota -- as well as Conservative MP Joel Godin , who told iPolitics that he would be " the best choice ... for protecting the rights for all MPs . " <p> This is also the first time that the speaker has been selected via preferential ballot , courtesy of Conservative MP Scott Reid 's successful backbench bid to streamline the process , which is expected to cut down on the time required to elect a speaker by a considerable margin . <p> Under the previous system , multiple ballots were usually required , which , with a one-hour break between each vote , could end up eating up most of the day , which is why the Throne Speech itself was usually scheduled for the next sitting day . <p> This time around , however , MPs will likely be filing into the Red Chamber by early afternoon , although as of this weekend , the full opening day programme has n't been released . <p> As for what to expect from the speech itself , Team Trudeau has been surprisingly -- some might even say uncharacteristically -- successful at keeping the details under wraps so far . However , as the Star 's Chantal Hebert points out , they may find themselves forced to walk a very narrow line in balancing their oft-stated commitment to fighting climate change with the need to restore harmonious -- or at least not actively hostile -- relations between Ottawa and the west , particularly Alberta and Saskatchewan . <p> The New Democrats have also made it clear that they 're expecting to hear exactly how the Liberals intend to honour their campaign pledge to plunge ahead in forging a national pharmacare plan , which , according to Jagmeet Singh , will be a key factor in whether he and his party will ultimately support the Liberals during that all-important initial confidence test . <p> As per the latest precinct buzz , that mission-critical vote is now expected to happen before the House shuts down for the holidays but wo n't necessarily be on the Throne Speech itself -- it could be be held as part of the end-of-year supplementary estimates request , or come into play as part of a last-minute push by the government to get its proposed changes to the tax formula through the House and Senate before the break . <p> Finally , barring all-party agreement to shut down for the weekend after the speech , the first cross-aisle dust-up of the new House will take place on Friday morning , although it 's rare for party leaders to show up for the final question period of the week . <p> Before the main event gets underway in Ottawa later this week , provincial and territorial premiers are set to hold a one-day huddle in Toronto to compare notes and trade ideas on dealing with the newly reinstalled federal government in the wake of an election that , as Canadian Press puts it , " left the nation in a partisan patchwork . " <p> Although the get-together will be held on Ontario Premier Doug Ford 's home turf of Mississauga , it 's being chaired by Saskatchewan Premier Scott Moe , who , as per CP , " all but stormed out of his sit-down with Trudeau more than two weeks ago , insisting he 'd heard nothing that gave him any confidence the Liberals were serious about addressing the concerns of the West . " <p> Moe now seems to be making a deliberate attempt to dial back his frustration to focus on his efforts to forge a cross-country consensus on what he sees as three key issues : the federal carbon tax , the new federal environmental assessment rules and " fiscal stabilization , " which is how he now seems to be framing the latest debate over equalization . <p> For his part , Ford has advised reporters that his " top priority " for the closed-door confab is to launch a joint pitch to get Ottawa to boost health funding by increasing the transfer rate from three to 5.2 percent . <p> " Pallister announced this week that his government was taking out ads in Quebec , welcoming those impacted by Legault 's controversial Bill 21 -- which prohibits those in positions of authority such as judges or teachers from wearing religious symbols -- to his province , " she reported last week . <p> " Legault has shot back at Pallister , saying that the Manitoba leader should be focusing on his own province . " Spend your money on French services , not on trying to attract Quebecers , " Legault said on Thursday , additionally casting doubt on the idea that many Quebec residents would pack up and move to Manitoba . " <p> Despite those differences , it 's likely that the premiers will have little trouble coming up with and signing off on a curated list of demands to present to their federal counterpart ahead of the next first ministerial meet-up , which is expected to take place early in the new year . <p> Trudeau makes the rounds at NATO anniversary summit in London <p> While he 'll undoubtedly be keeping a close eye on the goings-on in Mississauga , Trudeau may have to hit pause on both that and his last-minute pre-session prep work to get an emergency briefing on the latest twists and turns on the international front before jetting off to London to make the rounds at a NATO leaders ' summit to celebrate the 70th anniversary of the alliance . <p> According to the official advisory , Trudeau is hoping to " reiterate Canada 's unwavering commitment to NATO and to trans-Atlantic security , as well as the importance of working together to modernize the Alliance . But , as the Globe and Mail points out , that message may be drowned out by a surge in open acrimony amongst its members , with French President Emmanuel Macron the latest to publicly criticize the alliance for failing to live up to its mandate . <p> Adding to those tensions is the continuing presence of U.S. President Donald Trump , which , as CBC News notes , has left NATO partners increasingly uncertain about the American 's ' " commitment to .... mutual defence . " <p> Trudeau may also be bracing to be targeted for a direct attack from Trump over his refusal to comply with the United States ' demand that Canada increase its military spending budget -- which , given his government 's minority status and the general antipathy that both the Bloc Quebecois and the New Democrats harbour towards the current U.S. administration , is something that he likely could n't do even if he wanted , which there 's no indication he does . <p> Also this week : <p> Environment Minister Jonathan Wilkinson makes a pre-Throne Speech trek to Toronto to unveil his government 's plan to support " a clean economy " -- as well as take action against " plastic pollution " -- at the World Circular Economy Forum .
