<p> The process of creating the 2020 municipal budget began at city hall on Monday , with council reviewing the submission for running Cornwall 's water and wastewater systems next year . <p> The proposal would see the operating and capital budgets for both those system increase by just under $700,000 , or 3.8 per cent . If approved as is , the average residential water bill is expected to go up between $25 and $35 per year . <p> City administrators are expecting operating budget expenditures to grow by $231,000 or 2.42 per cent ; most of which can be attributed to increases in salaries . The remainder has been caused by inflation in the prices of goods , services and rents . <p> When it comes to capital projects , administrators are proposing $8.83 million in infrastructure improvements . <p> There is $2 million in rehabilitation work on 3.9-kilometres of existing water mains being proposed , which would see pipes on sections of Thirteenth Street West , Fifth Street East , Roberston Avenue , and Susan Avenue get relined . Mains on Jane Street from Power Dam Drive to Surgenor Street would be replaced . <p> There are also $1 million in sewer pipe replacements being proposed on Firth Street , Pitt Street , McKenzie Street , and Louisa Street . <p> " We have a ( 64-kilometre ) backlog of water mains that need to be addressed , and we have been picking away at it slowly , by 2020 we will have achieved 30 kilometres of replacement , " said manager Bill de Witt . " We are trying to get to a point where all the pipes are scoring a level of service of 75 or more ( out of 100 ) . To get there we would need to spend $37.5 million . " <p> So far this year there have been 46 water main breaks , and de Witt revealed the city has had particular problems with mains on Vincent Massey Drive and a feeder pipe on Pitt Street . They believe this is being caused by patches of corrosive soil . To help address the issues on Vincent Massey , administrators want to put in critical looping which would allow water to remain flowing to the surrounding neighbourhoods when the main is stopped for repairs . <p> Part of this project is a brand new water main is being planned on Power Dam Drive from Riverdale Avenue to Tollgate Road . The total cost of this project is estimated to be $1.2 million , but $550,000 of it would be paid for from the new development charges paid by property development companies . The remaining $650,000 has been set aside in previous budget years . <p> Another proposal for next year is renovations to the water purification plant building . A review of the facility found it is missing things often required by law , such as employee change rooms for preventing chemical contamination , and accessibility infrastructure on the ground floor . <p> " We feel accessibility is important , it will give more people the opportunity to tour the facility and also to have a job there , " said Carl Goodwin , division manager of environmental services . <p> The plant also needs improved bathrooms , a training room , and more office space for the additional staff that has been hired since construction . <p> All this is expected to cost just less than $1 million , and the city already has its concept design completed so that work can begin next year . Some $770,000 has already been set aside for the project in past years , with the remaining $225,000 being proposed for 2020 . <p> Coun. Eric Bergeron wondered why the plant needed a training room when the municipality has available rooms in other buildings . Goodwin explained an operator has to be at the plant at all times , so to do training somewhere else would mean some employees would have to remain behind and miss out on the training , sessions of which can be quite extensive and expensive . <p> Upgrades to Cornwall 's Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition ( SCADA ) system are also being proposed at a cost of $400,000 . SCADA is the system that allows operators at the purification plant to monitor the treatment process and to collect the data required by the Ministry of the Environment . The computers and instruments that run this system are now seven years old and need to be replaced . <p> At the wastewater treatment plant , two of the sludge-collection clarifiers need to be replaced . Two others have already been replaced last year with low-energy models , and this project would do the same . There is also a need for repair to deteriorating brick on the outside of the facility , as well as HVAC replacement , methane power generation , and insulation improvements . All of these would cost about $900,000 . <p> Many of the improvements to both plants are in service of a goal to make the facilities achieve net-zero energy requirements within four years , which was a goal several councillors praised . <p> There is also a proposal to spend $700,000 on separating storm and sanitary sewers in several areas of the city such as Water Street , Alice Street , and Amelia Street . This will help cut down on overflows , which have caused 257,000 cubic metres of sewage to be dumped into the river this year . <p> " There are huge parts of the city that still have combined sewers . So when it rains it picks up both wastewater and rainwater , " explained de Witt . " During large rain or snowmelt events , the interceptor at Brookdale Avenue ca n't take it all , and it allows undiluted sewage to go into the river . " <p> Mayor Bernadette Clement wondered if the amount of overflows has been increasing with climate change , and de Witt responded that there certainly has been an increase . There were 25 overflows this year , whereas in past years 17 to 18 was normal .
