<h> P.E.I. premier offers advice to a Conservative movement in search of direction <p> Amanda Hamel with the P.E.I. Executive Council Office and Darin Foulkes of Foulkes Productions work around P.E.I. Premier Dennis King and The Guardian 's chief political reporter , Stu Neatby , at MacLauchlan Arena for the newspaper 's annual year-end interview with the premier . The full video will be available online at theguardian.pe.ca on Dec. 30. - Jim Day <p> CHARLOTTETOWN , P.E.I. -- <p> Dennis King has some advice for a Conservative movement that will be entering a period of soul-searching in 2020 . <p> The Progressive Conservative premier , who defied the expectations of many by leading his reinvigorated party to victory in last April 's provincial election , believes federal Conservatives need to use the upcoming party leadership race to rediscover their progressive side . <p> And King believes a renewed focus on environmental issues is the best place to start . <p> " I think the recipe for the federal party , from a Conservative stand-point , would be to move a little bit more progressive on some of the social issues . To actually embrace the environment , to realize this is the seminal issue of our time , " King told The Guardian this week . <p> " I think it 's very important that they stop dancing around that issue and sort of paying lip service to it . " <p> The federal Conservative party learned last week from leader Andrew Scheer that he would be stepping down . The party had seen a disappointing showing in October 's federal election despite a Liberal government tarnished by the SNC-Lavalin scandal . <p> The Conservatives had unveiled their policies on climate change well before October 's election , but the plans were not embraced by environmental groups . Scheer also declined to participate in October 's youth-led climate marches in cities across Canada . The marches drew the participation of well over half a million Canadians in total , including hundreds on P.E.I. <p> " It 's on the minds of Canadians every day , whether you live in Alberta , Prince Edward Island or the Yukon or anywhere in between , " King said . <p> King made his comments during The Guardian 's year-end interview this week . The full video of the interview will be posted online starting Dec. 30 . <p> King said he believes Canadian federal politics have become more polarized , partly due to the heated partisanship currently on display in the US political arena . <p> " I think most Canadians , though , are kind of in the middle , " King said . <p> King said he was not surprised to see Scheer resign , but added the federal Tories were the only party to see an increase in both seats and total votes after the election . <p> " Politics is a jealous mistress . It 's tough if you do n't win , " King said . <p> " People ask questions . And I think the questions just became so plentiful that Mr. Scheer had to say ' well , you know what ? It 's time for somebody else to try . ' " <p> In the interview , King spoke about a number of issues , including tensions related to health-care staffing , the province 's response to post-tropical storm Dorian and the proposed Northern Pulp effluent pipe in Nova Scotia . <p> King also said he was very pleased to see the work between his own party and the opposition Green and Liberal parties . <p> " I love the fact that we have a minority government . I think it 's the greatest gift I 've been given , " King said . <p> King said the more co-operative approach to legislation was positive and said he believed Islanders have been pleased with it . <p> " I do think that we 're in a transformative time in politics , geopolitically around the world , " King said . <p> " It 's exciting to be part of that and to hopefully be leading something a little bit different that I hope will catch on . "
