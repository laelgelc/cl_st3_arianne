<p> Prime Minister Justin Trudeau unveiled his new cabinet Wednesday making room for western voices at the table , but also putting one of his best performers at his side to deal with fraying relationships with premiers across the country . <p> Former foreign affairs minister Chrystia Freeland will become Trudeau 's first deputy prime minister and shift her focus to intergovernmental affairs , negotiating with premiers like Alberta 's Jason Kenney and Ontario 's Doug Ford on issues like carbon taxes and pharmacare . <p> Freeland will maintain her focus on U.S.-Canada relations , helping to ensure the renegotiated NAFTA agreement gets across the finish line . <p> Trudeau said Freeland 's handling of the important files at foreign affairs gave him the confidence to put her in this new role . <p> " Chrystia and I have worked very closely on some of the biggest files facing Canada , " said Trudeau . " Our ability to work well together on these issues that touch national unity , touch energy and environment , touch relations with all provinces and regions in this country is going to be an extremely important thing at a time when we see some very different perspectives across the country that need to be brought together . " <p> Although she was born in Peace River , Alberta , Freeland represents a downtown Toronto riding , but she 's probably the closest thing the government has to an Albertan in caucus after the Liberals were wiped out in the province . <p> After the Rideau Hall ceremony , Freeland said people in Alberta and Saskatchewan sent the Liberal party a message and going forward they will have to listen . <p> " What we need to do as a federal government , when it comes to the west and when it comes to all our provincial relationships , is really listen hard . " she said . <p> Trudeau also appointed Winnipeg MP Jim Carr to act as the special representative for the prairies . In the last parliament , Carr was natural resources minister and then trade minister , but he was recently diagnosed with cancer . <p> Trudeau said Carr 's first focus is his health , but he is absolutely prepared to take on the job . <p> " He was very clear to me and very clear to everyone that being able to continue to be a strong voice for this government in the west and a strong voice from the west to this government , is going to be important to him . " <p> What we need to do as a federal government , when it comes to the west ... is really listen hard <p> Kenney , who has been a thorn in Trudeau 's side , took the opportunity to extend an olive branch to the new ministers . <p> " The Government of Alberta hopes to find common ground with the federal government to create jobs and growth , in part through responsible resource development , " Kenney wrote on Twitter . The premier said he particularly looked forward to working with Carr , along with Freeland and Seamus O'Regan , the new natural resources minister . <p> Saskatchewan Premier Scott Moe also said he looked forward to working with Freeland , but said it 's actions that will matter most . <p> " It is not about who is sitting around the table , it is about the decisions they make . " <p> Trudeau moved Environment Minister Catherine McKenna to infrastructure and replaced her with B.C. MP Jonathan Wilkinson . <p> Wilkinson said Canadians endorsed stronger action on climate change during the election , but he said the government has to recognize the legitimate economic concerns of western provinces . <p> Natural resources and environment are two especially contentious portfolios in western Canada , where the federal carbon tax and the Liberals ' overhaul of Canada 's environmental assessment regime are very unpopular . <p> Winnipeg MP Jim Carr , seen here with Minister of Crown-Indigenous Relations Carolyn Bennett earlier this month , will be Prime Minister Justin Trudeau 's special representative for the prairies.Patrick Doyle/Reuters <p> Trudeau picked O'Regan to head up natural resources , a job which will include getting the Trans Mountain pipeline built and put into service . <p> An MP from oil-producing Newfoundland , O'Regan likely has some understanding of the issues facing the oil and gas industry in western Canada . Still , the former journalist and co-host of CTV 's Canada AM faced criticism for his performance as minister of veterans affairs , a position he held from 2017 until he was shuffled to Indigenous services in January . <p> In 2018 , he drew the ire of some veterans when he compared his transition from journalism to politics to the experience of military members leaving the forces . <p> " Not to say he was n't a great talk show host , but he has not exactly been sterling as minister , particularly in veterans affairs , " said Jack Mintz , president 's fellow at the University of Calgary 's School of Public Policy . <p> " I think it may be an issue of concern to the oil and gas industry and Alberta government that he wo n't be that strong of a minister . " <p> But Martha Hall Findlay , president and CEO of the Canada West Foundation and a former Liberal MP , said the ministers matter less than the actions Trudeau takes to address concerns about resource development . " If the Prime Minister 's Office does n't recognize that some significantly different approaches are needed , things could just arguably get worse , because people will feel that you 've just played musical chairs , " she said . <p> Some observers were surprised that Trudeau elected not to give the environment portfolio to prominent Quebec environmentalist Steven Guilbeault , a star rookie MP from Montreal who was named heritage minister instead . <p> Prime Minister Justin Trudeau in front of his newly named Liberal cabinet ministers at Rideau Hall in Ottawa.Adrian Wyld/The Canadian Press <p> But Findlay said Trudeau made the right decision , and that Guilbeault 's appointment would have been " disastrous " for relations with western Canada . <p> " Steven Guilbeault , for all of his competence ... he is an avowed keep-it-in-the-grounder , " she said . <p> Chris Barrington-Leigh , an assistant professor at the McGill School of Environment , agreed that it would have been a " risky " move to give Guilbeault the environment portfolio . <p> But he also said the Liberals have made their position on key environmental issues like carbon pricing clear , regardless of the minister . <p> " That 's a battle they won , " he said . " The thing is implemented , and people will find it does n't actually change their life drastically . " <p> New Brunswick MP Dominic Leblanc was met with a huge ovation as he walked up to accept a role as president of the Queen 's Privy Council . Leblanc has been undergoing treatment for leukemia and appeared at the ceremony completely bald and wearing a surgical mask . <p> As a counter-weight to the big changes , Trudeau also kept a core of experienced ministers in their portfolios . Most notably , Bill Morneau will keep his job as finance minister , Marc Garneau stays on at the transportation department and Carolyn Bennett will continue to be minister for Indigenous relations . Justice Minister David Lametti will stay in the job he got in the wake of the SNC-Lavalin Affair , when Jody Wilson-Raybould was shuffled out of the justice department and later resigned . <p> Environmentalist and Liberal MP Steven Guilbeault arrives to be sworn-in as heritage minister in Ottawa on Nov. 20 , 2019 . Adrian Wyld/The Canadian Press <p> Lametti read a new oath for the job that promises to uphold independence of the judiciary and prosecutors . <p> Conservative Leader Andrew Scheer said the cabinet was " bigger and more bloated " version of the previous Trudeau cabinet and did n't represent real change . <p> " Naming a new cabinet was an opportunity for Trudeau to change course and abandon his reckless approach of dividing the country with failed policies , " said Scheer . " Instead he has doubled down on the same faces and the same failures . " <p> Along with Scheer , NDP Leader Jagmeet Singh said he was more interesting in what the government will actually do . <p> " Changing the ministers or not , it does n't give me any more or less confidence in this government , " said Singh , who could be an important player in the minority Parliament that opens for business on Dec. 5 . <p> It will be the policies that get NDP votes , Singh said , not the people running the departments . <h> This Week 's Flyers <h> Comments <p> Postmedia is committed to maintaining a lively but civil forum for discussion and encourage all readers to share their views on our articles .
