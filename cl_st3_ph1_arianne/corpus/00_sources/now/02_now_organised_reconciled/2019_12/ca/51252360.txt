<h> Ford government climate plan not supported by ' sound evidence ' : auditor general <p> TORONTO -- The Ford government 's plan to address climate change is not supported by sound evidence , and the province is not likely to achieve its proposed emissions reduction targets as it stands , Ontario 's auditor general has found . <p> In her office 's annual report , which was released on Wednesday , Auditor General Bonnie Lysyk found that the provincial plan " double-counts " some of its proposed initiatives , which overestimates emission reduction impacts . <p> While the ministry had estimated that its pitched initiatives would result in reductions of 17.6 Mt by 2030 , Lysyk 's analysis showed the government 's proposed initiatives had the potential of achieving somewhere between 6.3 and 13 Mt in reductions . <p> In August of this year , Lysyk says her office asked that Ontario 's Ministry of the Environment reassess its 2030 projection for emissions that would occur if no additional initiatives were taken -- and the government ended up with a figure that was 2.7 Mt higher than the projection that it had originally based its plan on last year . <p> " They could do more with modelling their future decision making before they publish a target plan , " Lysyk told reporters of the government 's plan on Wednesday afternoon . " At this point in time , when we look at these numbers , we would be saying that more work needs to be done . " <p> She pointed out one particular section of the report , pertaining to the ministry 's assumptions about the number of electric vehicles in use across Ontario increasing over the coming years . The Ministry , at the time that the data for the report was compiled , was estimating that there would be 1.3 million electric vehicles on Ontario roads by 2030 -- or an increase of more than 3,000 per cent from the 41,000 such vehicles documented in 2019 . <p> However , in the summer of 2018 , the report notes that the Ford government cancelled incentives for buying electric vehicles and installing charging stations that were in place . " The ministry has not yet identified any planned initiatives that could increase the uptake of electric vehicles in order to achieve the estimated greenhouse gas reduction , " it says . <p> The government also may be harming its own efforts , the report states -- with decisions from other ministries and agencies such as the expansion of natural gas infrastructure , changes to the Building Code , and amendments to the Greater Golden Horseshoe 's growth plan deemed by Lysyk 's office as " likely to adversely impact Ontario 's emission-reduction goals . " <p> Right now , the average emissions per person annually in Ontario sits at 11 tonnes , according to Lysyk 's report . That 's the second lowest average in Canada , but a figure that clocks in higher than in many other developed countries worldwide . As for other facets of the Ford PCs ' environmental plan , including their projections around increased renewable and compressed natural gas use , Lysyk found the government had " no feasibility analyses . " <p> " For other initiatives , such as future innovation , the ministry was unable to provide any evidence to support the estimated reductions , " she wrote . <p> Environment Minister Jeff Yurek defended their plan on Wednesday afternoon , saying that as a Conservative government , he felt they were " held to a higher standard on climate change and environmental policies . " " We have a plan . The auditor general did n't say it was terrible . She said it needed to be tightened up , " Yurek told reporters at Queen 's Park . <p> " It was an ambitious plan we ran right out of the gate with , " he continued . Later in the media availability , Yurek added : " I will not say it 's flawed . It 's a comprehensive plan that takes in more than just one item . " <p> As for the electric vehicle file , Yurek confirmed to reporters that they were n't currently discussing bringing back rebate programs , but he believed there would be an uptick over the next decade nonetheless -- due to increasing options for such vehicles , and more charging stations . <p> The provincial PCs ' approach to climate change and environmental initiatives has been subject to scrutiny during their time in office . Ontario 's former environmental commissioner publicly blasted Ford 's government last fall , saying they had cancelled a plan that was making progress in emissions reduction to replace it with a " much too weak " version . <p> " Ontario has gutted most of its climate-change programs , " then-commissioner Dianne Saxe said in a statement last year . " Most of the cap-and-trade money was funding energy-efficiency programs in Ontario communities -- in schools , in public housing , transit and hospitals , for example -- that would have reduced ( emissions ) and saved millions of dollars in energy costs . " ( Scrapping cap-and-trade was among Premier Doug Ford 's campaign pledges in his most recent bid for office . ) <p> Yurek characterized the government 's environmental plan as evolving on Wednesday , saying it would continue to change over the coming years as they approach the 2030 benchmark . " It 's always been a draft plan . It was misrepresented on the website as the environment plan , " he said . <p> His comments drew scrutiny from opposing parties at Queen 's Park . " To suggest that they can reach any targets at all without having a plan ... is completely ridiculous . We have no confidence whatsoever that this government is serious about climate change , " NDP leader Andrea Horwath said . Green Party leader Mike Schreiner alleged that the province was relying on " magic math and magic markets , " and interim Liberal leader John Fraser claimed the government was " fudging " their environmental plan . <p> In recent weeks , the provincial government has come under fire from the opposition for several issues on the environment file , first for earmarking $231 million to cover the costs of scrapping more than 750 energy contracts -- including the cancellation of a wind farm project that the province pledged would come at zero cost to taxpayers . ( Associate energy minister Bill Walker told reporters that the move would retain $790 million . ) <p> The PCs also faced heat late last month , after energy minister Greg Rickford cited a website during question period that rejects " consensus science " on climate change , calling it one of his " favourite periodicals . "
