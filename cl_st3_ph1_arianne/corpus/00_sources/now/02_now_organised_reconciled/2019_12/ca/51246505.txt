<h> Global warming blamed for driving the cost of food up nearly $500 next year <p> Climate change can affect grocery prices through everything from a higher number of storms to bacterial outbreaks , both of which impact produce supplies and prices . <p> The average Canadian family will pay up to $487 more on food next year , according to an annual food price report by Dalhousie University and the University of Guelph . The report blames climate change as a major culprit of the price increase . <p> Food prices in Manitoba will most likely rise around the same amount , said Sylvain Charlebois , the director of Agri-Food Analytics Lab . <p> The report forecasts a two to four per cent increase in food prices in 2020 . It predicts the typical Canadian family will spend $12,667 on groceries and going out to eat . The report 's " average Canadian family " consists of a couple , both 39 years old , a 15-year-old boy and an 11-year-old girl . <p> " The Prairies are going to be hit , " Charlebois said . <p> However , inflation can be a good thing , he said . It 's necessary that the cost of food matches the current economy . <p> " The food industry is there to make money and provide a good product for people , " Charlebois said . " All these products will come at a cost . " <p> Charlebois said problems occur when food prices increase beyond three per cent . <p> " When it actually goes above three per cent , you are leaving a lot of people behind , " Charlebois said . <p> He said Manitobans need to help people who ca n't afford to buy meals . He also recommended people save money on food by eating at restaurants less and heading to grocery stores more . <p> Unexpected snowstorms , droughts and other weather events have impacted crops and food prices in the past , said Simon Somogyi , lead researcher from the University of Guelph . <p> But for 2020 , he and others behind the report highlight climate change as the main cause . <p> " We 're deliberately pointing out that , you know : climate change is causing the droughts , is causing the bad snowstorms that 's impacting prices , " he said . <p> The report says " the elephant in the room " is the impact of changing weather patterns on our food systems through droughts , forest fires , heavy precipitation , reduced freshwater access and rising sea levels . <p> The expected rise in food prices comes as Canadians face relatively steady inflation . The consumer price index rose 1.9 per cent year over year in October , according to Statistics Canada . <p> Next year , researchers expect price increases in all food categories they track , with the biggest jump -- four to six per cent -- in meat . <p> Pork prices will likely lead that ascent because of the African swine fever outbreak in China that is roiling pork markets . <p> Food sold at restaurants , and seafood and vegetables , will likely see a two to four per cent increase in 2020 . <p> Storms and other weather events wreak havoc on logistics , Somogyi said . Weather can slow down transportation and create a shortage that results in higher prices . <p> A hotter climate contributes to bacterial outbreaks such as E. coli because hotter temperatures and unpredictable heat waves increase bacterial growth , Somogyi said . In recent years , multiple E. coli outbreaks prompted grocers to pull romaine lettuce off their shelves and restaurants to stop serving the leafy green . That tends to drive up prices for alternatives , such as spinach . <p> Produce prices also trend up because Canada imports lots of fruits and vegetables -- the colder weather in the country limits what can be grown . Imported goods can fluctuate in price due to trade issues that create slowdowns at borders or weather events impacting deliveries . <p> Food trends can prompt big swings . This year , celery costs skyrocketed partly due to a fad diet based on the purported health benefits of celery juice . In May , one major Canadian grocer advertised celery stalks for $5.99 online . A kilogram cost roughly $3 in the years prior to April 2018 , when Statistics Canada stopped tracking the vegetable . <p> It 's difficult to put a number on how much of the anticipated 2020 food price increase comes from climate change , said Somogyi , but it is responsible for " the bulk " of last year 's vegetable sticker shock . <p> Mickey Karasinskli is a Winnipegger who shops at No Frills and Young 's Market to save money on groceries . It would reduce time to market , costs and risks of bacterial outbreaks , he said . <p> The federal government released a food policy paper in June 2019 -- the first in Canada . The policy looks at boosting community access to healthy food , making Canadian food a top choice domestically and internationally , supporting food security in Northern and Indigenous communities and reducing food waste . <p> Certain initiatives in the policy could support vertical or indoor farming , according to Agriculture Canada spokesperson James Watson in an email . <p> Vertical and greenhouse production show potential for increasing food security ( particularly in remote areas of Canada ) , decreasing transportation costs and increasing health for Canadians .
