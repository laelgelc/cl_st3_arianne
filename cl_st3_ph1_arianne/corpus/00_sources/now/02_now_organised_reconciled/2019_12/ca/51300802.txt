<p> Malpeque MP Wayne Easter said he was " a bit surprised " by Monday 's decision by federal Environment Minister Jonathan Wilkinson to not carry out a federal environmental assessment of the proposed Northern Pulp effluent pipe . <p> But Easter said the decision rested on a belief that the company would still need to abide by stringent federal pulp and paper effluent and pollution regulations . <p> Wayne Easter <p> " What the feds are saying ( is ) it can be just as effective as an environmental review , " Easter told The Guardian . <p> " The federal regulatory processes will have to be met in order to proceed . And those are very stringent . " <p> In all , regulations from five federal departments -- Fisheries and Oceans Canada , Environment and Climate Change Canada , Transport Canada , Public Services and Procurement Canada and Health Canada -- would have to be met for Northern Pulp 's proposed effluent pipe to operate . The Pictou County , N.S. company would need to abide by these regulations even if it passed an environmental assessment from the Nova Scotia government . <p> On Tuesday , Nova Scotia Environment Minister Gordon Wilson ordered the company provide more scientific data about the project 's impacts , as part of the environmental assessment . This could take up to two years . <p> Charlottetown MP Sean Casey , who worked closely with Wilkinson prior to October 's election as the parliamentary secretary for Fisheries , Oceans and the Canadian Coast Guard , admitted the decision may provoke frustration from local fishermen . <p> The Northern Pulp mill is seen in Abercrombie Point in 2014 , with the Town of Pictou in the background . - FILE <p> The P.E.I. Fishermen 's Association , as well as all three major provincial political parties , have pushed for the federal government to conduct an environmental assessment of the project instead of the province of Nova Scotia . <p> " ' No pipe in the Strait ' fits very nicely on a bumper sticker . But to explain the rationale behind not going ahead with the federal environmental assessment ca n't fit on a bumper sticker . " <p> Sean Casey <p> Casey said the more stringent federal environmental assessments are best used for larger industrial projects , such as oil and gas pipelines . <p> During the last federal election , all four incumbent Liberal candidates , including Casey and Easter , expressed support for Island fishermen and concern about the effects of the effluent pipe . In a debate hosted by The Guardian in October , Casey said he supported a federal assessment of the project . <p> Northern Pulp has proposed pumping millions of litres of treated effluent into the Northumberland Strait from its pulp and paper mill in Pictou . The company claimed in its focus report that the project would not harm marine species in the area and has said approval of the project would be needed to keep the mill from closing . <p> The mill is legally obligated to close its existing treatment facility , located within the Pictou Landing First Nation , by January 2020 . <p> Ian MacPherson , executive director of the P.E.I. Fisherman 's Association , said he has concerns about the Nova Scotia government 's financial interests in the Northern Pulp mill , and the impact these interests could have on a provincial environmental assessment . <p> An indemnity agreement between Nova Scotia and Northern Pulp 's former owner could put the province on the hook for millions in damages . <p> " There 's a lot of interconnectivity between the mill and the province , " MacPherson said . <p> P.E.I. Minister of Fisheries and Communities Jamie Fox said that , because P.E.I. fishermen would be impacted by the proposed effluent pipe , the assessment should be carried out by federal regulators . <p> " We have an issue in one province that could consequently have an impact on the same body of water which is fished by another province , " Fox said . <p> Fox added he was pleased with the decision by Nova Scotia to require more scientific information , but disappointed by the decision of the federal government .
