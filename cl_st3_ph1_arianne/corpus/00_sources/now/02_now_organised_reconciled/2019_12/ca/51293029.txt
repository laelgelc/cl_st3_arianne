" <p> " I 'm a reporter with the Canadian Energy Centre , " the phone message said . <p> " Our website is launching next week and we will be writing a story about the 3% Project , which has raised concerns among some parents who reached out to us following presentations at their children 's schools . It recently opened its office in downtown Calgary . <p> " Government cracking down on you -- I was n't quite sure what to make of that , " Lee said . <p> " A few individual parents angry with what I 'm saying is understandable . Although climate is a major focus of Lee 's talks , his goal is to get students thinking broadly about the world they will graduate into , he said . <p> " It 's not just climate change . It also claimed inaccuracies in the group 's materials , available on its website . <p> Those materials quote an International Monetary Fund working paper that found Canada subsidizes its fossil fuel industry by about $46 billion a year . <p> The centre pointed out that a working paper does not represent an official IMF position . It also questioned the credibility of the figure , quoting other studies that set the subsidy level closer to $3 billion . <p> In an email to The Canadian Press , a co-author of the paper said his work had been fully peer reviewed within the IMF . Its story said the study really showed most of that funding came from conservative foundations , not industry . <p> The centre did n't mention that the study found funding for those foundations -- which is untraceable -- increased at the same time industry stopped directly funding climate change denial . <p> " The correspondence is suggestive of an effort to conceal funding of ( denial ) , " the paper states . <p> Centre spokesman Grady Semmens , who said staff are not advised to call themselves reporters , defended the article . <p> " The information addressed in our story is based on our review of the group 's publicly available handbook , information on their website and the concerns highlighted by the parents we spoke to , " he said . <p> Lee said his plans wo n't change as a result of the war room 's attention , or dozens of hate messages and death threats he has received -- most of them from Alberta .
