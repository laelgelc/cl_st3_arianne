RACHEL EMMANUEL/IPOLITICS <p> The political year that was and the political year to come . The great , splashing irony of it all is that someone who is , with all due respect , unimpressive and clearly out-of-his-depth should have been so prominent before , during , and after the election . <p> Andrew Scheer received more media than one would have considered possible , and the trend has still not stopped . In many ways I feel rather sorry for the man . He won the leadership of his party by less than one per cent of the vote and on the 13th ballot . Very few people voted for him , but many voted against Maxime Bernier . <p> He was most people 's least offensive choice , was severely underqualified , and -- important this -- never had the backing of the party machinery . Good Lord , even the right-wing Roman Catholic anti-abortion crowd , his natural and genuine allies , eventually condemned him -- for them he had compromised too much on social issues , whereas for most people he came across as a fanatic . <p> Right-wing parties traditionally turn their weapons inwards . Once Scheer failed to deliver he was a dead man walking , but was too unworldly to realize what would happen . That he could n't beat Justin Trudeau , who had been swamped with allegations of unethical or even illegal behaviour over SNC-Lavalin , said so much about Scheer 's inability to feel the public pulse and react to the situation . <p> In 2020 the party has to evolve , resemble European rather than American conservatism , and leave issues such as abortion and sexuality far behind . If it has any sense , it will also elect a woman leader , and Rona Ambrose is emerging as the choice of voters as well as party establishment . If she does become leader , that 's a problem for Justin Trudeau , but if they opt for another man in another suit , especially from the current likely candidates , the Liberals will be celebrating . <p> In terms of media in 2019 , and this plays directly into electoral politics , there was an increasing disconnect between what Ottawa journalists found fascinating , and what the Canadian people paid attention to . We saw this in particular with SNC-Lavalin , which is n't to say that it was n't important , but that public fatigue set in fairly early , and that too many columns and articles said the same thing . Whether the recent legal decision changes any of this remains to be seen . <p> SNC-Lavalin should be an open wound , in that it has n't been properly resolved and leads us to ask how Ottawa actually works . But sympathy with and interest in Jody Wilson-Raybould diminished some time ago . The news cycle is ever briefer , and that trend shows no sign of declining . <p> Similarly with the black/brown face incident , which -- by the way -- would have trashed most political leaders . I was back in my hometown of London , England in November and every journalist I spoke to agreed that such a revelation would have ended a political career in Britain . The incidents rightly outraged journalists and commentators , but seemed to make little difference during the election , especially among voters who were the most likely to be offended . As we enter a new year , I ca n't see the subject even being discussed , and the oft-mentioned notion that it would dent Trudeau 's international reputation does n't seem to be true . <p> The NDP has to seriously consider its future direction and profile if it is to move forward . The social democratic conscience of the country should be presenting a constant moral reflection on Ottawa 's failings . It can afford to be provocative , in that it 's not in government or likely to be , but it continues to fail to break through the cloak of virtual anonymity , something that caused so many problems during its electoral campaign . A new leader might help , but the problems are deeper and more systemic than that . This is not an irresponsible or extreme party , has so much to offer , and deserves more of a voice . There are no signs of that becoming a reality in the year to come . <p> As if so often the case , economics , jobs , and money influence all else in the body politic . Nationalism , separatism , and despair evaporate if employment is high and the economy booming . Economic change is inevitable in 2020 , but nobody has any reliable idea what that will mean to most Canadians . The country has long been relatively sheltered from global trends , and there 's no reason to think that this will be dramatically different in the coming months . <p> Alberta ? The support for autonomy is loud and raw , but based largely on volume and fantasy . The province does n't speak for Manitoba and certainly not B.C. , and the west has never spoken with a single voice . <p> This is where Canadian politics becomes more interesting and important : do we embrace sanity by securing a future for coming generations by properly dealing with climate change , or do we play the governance of immediacy and continue to pretend the problem is far away ? That dilemma , naturally , applies far beyond Canada 's borders . The challenge that can not be escaped is that a serious climate change policy requires major adjustment and , however much we might deny it , the possibility of short-term job losses . That 's never going to be easy for a government to implement in a democracy that demands popularity if its policies are to continue . When in 2013 Justin Trudeau unwisely said that he admired China 's " basic dictatorship " I 'm pretty sure that 's what he was referring to . <p> Post-Brexit Britain will offer trade opportunities for Canada , the new NAFTA is encouraging in much of what it promises , the China dispute will surely be resolved ( both countries want it to be , and China is piling up too many enemies at the moment ) , and steady immigration and expansion have will improve the conditions of all , as they always have . <p> The foundational sin of this country -- the treatment of First Nations people and the darkness of colonialism -- shows few signs of being dealt with to the degree that morality and decency demand . There is an enormous amount of goodwill in the political class , and cynicism gets us nowhere , but because the problems are so deep , tampering with surface issues simply wo n't do . A new generation of native people is more aware and active than ever , and rightly demanding something greater than gestures and words . <p> All of this has to be reported if the public are to know and respond yet media itself is fighting a losing war against alternative news sources , a decline in advertising , and financial pressure . It would n't be a surprise if a major newspaper closed its doors in 2020 . <p> Still , we do n't have Donald Trump as our leader , we do n't have to negotiate our way out of the European Union and , fear not , Don Cherry wo n't be returning to the CBC .
