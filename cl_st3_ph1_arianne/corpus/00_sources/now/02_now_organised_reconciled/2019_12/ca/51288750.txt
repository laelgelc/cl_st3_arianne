<h> The Sprout : Congress schedules vote on USMCA <p> President Donald Trump and Canadian Prime Minister Justin Trudeau walk along the Colonnade outside the Oval Office , Monday , Feb. 13 , 2017 , at the White House in Washington , D.C. ( Official White House Photo by Shealah Craighead ) <p> Good day and welcome to the Sprout , where sadly on this Wednesday in the week before the winter holidays start we do not have time to properly commemorate National Roast Suckling Pig Day . <p> Here 's today 's agriculture news . <p> The Lead <p> A vote on the trade deal between Canada , the United States and Mexico that will replace NAFTA is scheduled for a vote in the Democrat-led House of Representatives on Thursday . <p> As Bloomberg reports , the vote 's timing comes just after Congress is expected to impeach U.S. President Donald Trump . <p> The bill to implement the U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement ( USMCA ) cleared the House 's tax and trade committee on Tuesday , which was the only stop in the process to passing the deal in which it could have been amended . <p> The bill needs only a simple majority to pass the Republican-led Senate , which likely wo n't vote on the agreement until after it concludes an impeachment trial of Trump . <p> Prime Minister Justin Trudeau weighed in on Canada 's timeline for passing the deal on Tuesday , indicating that MPs would n't be rushed back to the House of Commons to pass legislation to implement the deal before they 're scheduled to on Jan. 27 . Reuters has more details about Trudeau 's comments . <p> Around Town <p> The Canadian Food Inspection Agency is allowing for additional livestock sectors to receive the same " transition period " to new transportation regulations as shippers of beef and dairy animals are receiving . Real Agriculture has more details . <p> In Canada <p> Agriculture Minister Marie-Claude Bibeau emerged from a meeting with her provincial counterparts on Tuesday with a promise to make minor changes to the federal government 's AgriStability program . According to the Western Producer 's reporting , the feds will allow for private insurance to complement its own crop insurance program . The government is also launching a pilot project that will leverage tax return information to make the process of applying to AgriStability simpler . <p> Producers have called for the price margin limit under the insurance program to rise from 70 per cent to 85 per cent . Bibeau said yesterday the government was " not ready at this stage to go forward with such a significant increase . " It 's estimated such a change would cost taxpayers $300 million per year . <p> Internationally <p> The European Union 's Agriculture Commissioner , Janusz Wojciechowski , said they should go in the direction of reducing soy imports from third countries by supporting the production of protein plants in Europe . Janusz Wojciechowski said there is space for this new direction in the new Common Agricultural Policy , reported Euractiv . <p> " We can not change it overnight , but we could systematically reduce the imports of soy , also by promoting homegrown feed productions , " he said . <p> Climate change has reduced Australian farms ' average annual profitability by 22 per cent , or around $18,600 per farm , in the past two decades , according to the agriculture department . A report from the Australian Bureau of Agricultural and Resource Economics and Sciences found that average temperatures increased by about 1 C since 1950 . <p> Report co-author David Galeano said adaptation to climate variability " is certainly helping " -- and without it farms would have experienced a 26 per cent reduction in profit , according to The Guardian . <p> Dutch farmers and construction workers say government pollution policies are putting them out of business . Those affected took the streets to protest Wednesday , using tractors to block a major highway with Germany , The Associated Press reported . <p> China is increasing its efforts to encourage small farmers to resume pig production amid growing pressure to boost pork supplies after a disease outbreak decimated its national herd . Small pig farmers have been hit hard by African swine fever , which kills almost all pigs it infects , and many farmers have been left with large debts after seeing their entire herds wiped out . While several large producers are expanding rapidly to replenish output , many millions of small farmers are wary of restocking their farms because of ongoing risk from the disease and a lack of capital , reported Reuters .
