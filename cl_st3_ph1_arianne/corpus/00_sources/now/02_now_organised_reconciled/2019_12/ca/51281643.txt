<p> Near record-high precipitation in fall has resulted in above-normal to well-above-normal soil moisture conditions for most of the province 's watersheds heading into freeze up , according to Manitoba Infrastructure 's Hydrologic Forecast Centre . <p> Additionally , the Centre says base flows on most rivers are above normal to well-above normal for this time of the year . The Red River is expected to remain at above-normal flows and levels prior to the spring 2020 run-off . <p> The 2019 fall conditions report also indicates there is a risk of spring flooding within these river basins , however that risk is strongly dependent on future weather , including the amount of winter and spring precipitation , and melt conditions . <p> According to the forecast centre , the latest long-term forecast from Environment and Climate Change Canada indicates normal precipitation from December to February for most of southern Manitoba , while the U.S. National Weather Service 's outlook indicates above-normal precipitation within the U.S. portion of the Red River Basin . <p> The province says if above-normal precipitation occurs , it will lead to a higher risk of spring flooding . <p> When the cities of Winkler , Altona , Morden , Carman , and the rest of Southern Manitoba want local news , weather , and sports online and on-demand , they turn to Pembina Valley Online .
