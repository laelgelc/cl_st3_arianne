<h> Baranyai : Dialling up an apt description of this dwindling , divisive decade <p> We do n't yet have a popular way to refer to the decade now drawing to a close . Neither " teens , " " tens " nor " tweens " has gained much traction . We 'll need something to stick for a catchphrase . As with the Roaring Twenties and Dirty Thirties , there is an irrepressible compulsion to capture the spirit of the times . <p> How might future observers distill this decade to its essence ? It 's an interesting thought experiment : searching for a unifying theme in a period rife with division . <p> One defining global development is the ascendancy of the smartphone . Some 3.3 billion people worldwide now have one , according to Statista . More than five billion -- fully two-thirds of the planet -- are equipped with at least one mobile device . <p> The sheer ubiquity of the technology has radically altered how we engage with the world . It has delivered new modes of connectivity , while eroding social cohesion . <p> It shapes how we interact with each other , both setting an expectation of being permanently reachable ( some of us remember it was n't always so ) , and shifting more of our social lives online . <p> It shapes how we interact with news , with virtually instant access to current events . A generation of citizen-journalists can record history as it unfolds , upending authoritarian traditions of information suppression . At the same time , the proliferation of click-seeking commentary has introduced a flood of misinformation and disinformation online , which certain leaders have weaponized to undermine the checks and balances of a free press . <p> The smartphone has shaped important social movements , including #BlackLivesMatter . The video camera is both a sword and a shield , empowering individuals to bear witness to -- and deter -- excessive force . <p> It has also fundamentally changed the nature of protest . Initially , the smartphone was heralded for letting organizers connect with a broader audience and adapt plans on the fly . But it also allowed us to slump into armchair activism , where personal commitment too easily dwindles to so many disgruntled tweets . <p> It continues to reshape norms of communication . The written word is rapidly ceding ground to emojis and acronyms ( imho ) . But the shift goes well beyond how we write . The rise of social media has been matched by a corresponding decline in civility , if not an outright breakdown . Hyper-partisan commenters feed on a steady diet of outrage inside their own echo chambers , built to increase engagement and ad revenue . <p> The twin pressures of maintaining a highly curated screen presence , and making unrealistic comparisons , have taken a particularly high toll on youth mental health . <p> It also comes at a high cost to privacy . Just this decade , Facebook users were unwitting subjects of mood-manipulation experiments ; personal data was harvested without our consent and used for targeted political advertising ; and foreign bots and trolls were allowed to pursue an unfettered agenda of cultural destabilization and election interference . <p> " The death throes of democracy " is not a particularly cheering catchphrase for a decade , and yet it may prove an apt summation of where these forces are leading . <p> The smartphone also shapes how we engage with the world in a literal way : each one requires dozens of minerals and rare earth metals . Unsustainable depletion of finite resources is hardly unique to this decade ; however , the dawning acceptance that climate change goals ca n't be merely aspirational runs sharply at odds with our constant clamouring for the latest release . <p> It 's ironic that while smartphones help engaged citizens organize climate strikes , we may well run out of the materials needed to build these devices shaping our modern era .
