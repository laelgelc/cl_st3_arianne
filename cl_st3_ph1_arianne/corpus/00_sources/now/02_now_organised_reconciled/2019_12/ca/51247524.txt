Here is what 's on the radar of our editors for the morning of Dec. 5 . <p> What we are watching in Canada ... <p> The 43rd session of Parliament -- and a new era of minority government -- opens today with a speech from the throne that will emphasize the issues on which Justin Trudeau 's Liberals believe they can find common ground with opposition parties . <p> The throne speech is penned by the Prime Minister 's Office but is to be read by Gov . Gen. Julie Payette in the Senate chamber . <p> Government sources , who spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to speak publicly about the speech , say it will aim to set a collaborative tone , as befits a government that will need the support of one or more opposition parties to pass legislation and survive confidence votes . <p> The speech is to give only a rough sketch of the priorities that will drive the government in the days to come . <p> Some of the details will be filled in when Trudeau issues marching orders to each of his 36 cabinet ministers in mandate letters , expected as soon as Friday . <p> The speech is expected to highlight Liberal commitments to stronger action to combat climate change , tax breaks for the middle-class , pharmacare and stricter gun control , all of which featured in the platforms of the New Democrats , Bloc Quebecois and Greens . <p> -- <p> Also this ... <p> The Supreme Court of Canada is to release a decision today on whether it will hear an appeal from an Alberta man convicted of killing two seniors who disappeared on a camping trip . <p> Travis Vader was sentenced to life in prison for the deaths of Lyle and Marie McCann . <p> The couple , in their 70s , vanished after leaving their home in St. Albert , a bedroom community north of Edmonton , in July 2010 . <p> Their burned-out motorhome and a vehicle they had been towing were discovered days later west of the city , but their bodies have never been found . <p> A trial judge determined Vader was a desperate drug addict who came across the McCanns and killed them during a robbery . <p> Justice Denny Thomas convicted Vader in 2016 of second-degree murder , but later substituted the verdict with manslaughter , because he had mistakenly used an outdated section of the Criminal Code . <p> Lawyers for Vader asked the Alberta Court of Appeal for a new trial or for his manslaughter charges to be stayed because of the judge 's mistake . <p> --
