<h> UNICEF leads on water access <p> A displaced Adara woman pours water on a pot before cooking for a group of Adara displaced people at an IDPs refugee camp in Kajuru , Kaduna State , Nigeria , on April 14 , 2019. - Hundreds of Adara people lives in IDPs in Kaduna region after being displaced due to the conflict . ( Photo by Luis TATO / AFP ) LUIS TATO/AFP/Getty Images <p> The following is part two in a three-part series focusing on issues surrounding water around the globe . <p> " Over the past several decades , the frequency of local water crises has been increasing , affecting health , food security , environmental sustainability and economic development , " according to Carleen McGuinty of UNICEF Canada . <p> " Water is becoming an increasingly scarce and expensive resource -- especially for the poor , the marginalized and the vulnerable , " added McGuinty , who serves as director of international programs and policies at UNICEF Canada . <p> Last month , UNICEF , with the assistance of the World Health Organization ( WHO ) , issued an eye opening report on water related issues . The report -- Progress on household drinking water , sanitation and hygiene I 2000-2017 -- assesses the progress made in reducing inequalities in access to water , hygiene , and sanitation services through the lens of the Sustainable Development Goals ( SDGs ) . <p> " The 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development commits UN member states to take bold and transformative steps to ' shift the world onto a sustainable and resilient path ' , ' realize the human rights of all ' , ' end poverty in all its forms ' , and ensure ' no one will be left behind ' , " notes the report . <p> The SDGs are made up 17 broad goals and 169 specific global targets . For example , SDG 6 commits the world to ensuring " availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all . " <p> According to the report , approximately 2.2 billion people do not have access to safely managed drinking water services . Moreover , the report notes that 4.2 billion people do n't have safely managed sanitation services , and three billion go without basic handwashing facilities . <p> A human right From the perspective of the Sustainable Development Goals , why is it important for families to have access to clean water ? <p> " Access to clean drinking water is a human right and recognized in the Convention on the Rights of the Child , " replied McGuinty . " Children need safe and supportive environments to grow and thrive and access to clean water is an essential part of this . " <p> The UNICEF official pointed out that diseases related to unclean water and poor sanitation are a leading cause of death for children under the age of five . An estimated 814 child perish daily from diarrhoea caused by the lack of safe , sanitation , and basic hygiene . " Securing access to safe water and sanitation systems plays a key role in reducing illness and death , especially among children , " McGuinty stated in an email . <p> " Clean water and sanitation are at the core of sustainable development and underpin poverty reduction , economic growth and environmental sustainability , " continued McGuinty . <p> Sanitation and health According to the report , " over half of the global population or 4.2 billion people lack safe sanitation . " And this has serious implications for water safety and child health . <p> Open defecation perpetuates a vicious cycle of disease and poverty , " explained McGuinty . " Countries where open defection is widespread have the highest number of deaths of children under five . " <p> UNICEF has put forward a strategy to address the lack of proper sanitation . The Game Plan to End Open Defecation " focuses on 26 high-burden countries that with more than 5 million people or more where more than half of the population practises open defecation , " explained McGuinty . <p> " These 26 countries account for more than 90 per cent of the global open defecation burden , 821 million people in 2015 , " she continued . " Progress is critical to meet the SDG sanitation target of ' adequate and equitable sanitation and hygiene for all , and eliminating open defecation ' by 2030 . " <p> " Access to water is also fundamental to ensuring children -- especially girls -- can access education , " she said . " Without proper sanitation facilities in schools , girls stay away because they are menstruating and lack access to safe latrines . " <p> The report points out that there are significant inequalities in service levels between urban and rural populations when it comes to access to water . Closing this gap would improve the quality of life of the poor . <p> " Water from reliable and more accessible sources allows people to spend less time and effort physically collecting it , freeing them to be productive in other ways , " McGuinty explained . " This can also result in greater personal safety by reducing the need to make long or risky journeys to collect water . Better water sources also mean reduced health costs as people are less likely to fall ill and are better able to remain economically productive . " <p> Climate change and water scarcity Climate change is reportedly causing extreme weather , including droughts . The World Bank predicts that by 2050 , water scarcity will be a global problem . <p> If water scarcity continues to worsen in the coming years and decades , will the health and development of children suffer ? " Water and climate change are inextricably linked , as the effects of climate change are first felt through water -- droughts , floods and storms , " McGuinty answered . " These disasters can destroy water supplies and sewage systems , leaving behind contaminated water and putting the lives of millions of children at risk . " <p> Regions vulnerable to drought and floods tend to have poor access to water and/or proper sanitation . " To tackle climate change we need to build more resilient communities and increase equitable access to sustainable water sources and improved sanitation , " said McGuinty . " That means even in times of crisis , every child is given a chance to survive and grow . " <p> As water scarcity increases , " reusing wastewater -- to recover water , nutrients or energy -- is becoming an important strategy , " said McGuinty . For example , wastewater is being used to irrigate farmers ' field . <p> " In developing countries , reused water represents seven per cent of irrigated land , " added the UNICEF staffer . " While this practice poses health risks if done inappropriately , safe management of wastewater can yield multiple benefits , including increased food production . Management of all water resources will need to be improved to ensure provision and quality . " <p> UNICEF takes the lead Regardless of what the future holds for the planet 's climate , UNICEF will continue to take the lead on helping kids access safely managed water resources . <p> " As the world 's leading agency for children , UNICEF plays a key role in providing children with Water , Hygiene and Sanitation ( WASH ) services , " said McGuinty . UNICEF currently runs WASH programs in more than 100 countries . Last year , the UN agency provided 18.6 million kids and their families with access to clean drinking water . <p> " UNICEF WASH programs include supplying the ' hardware ' of water trucks , bore holes , hand pumps and latrines , " she continued . " It also includes the ' software ' of working with families , supporting policy development , building the capacity of institutions , creating sustainable and innovative systems , and raising awareness about good hygiene . " <p> McGuinty revealed that " UNICEF 's WASH program is already using innovative solutions , such as solar power water pumps and rainwater harvesting , to reduce the impact of climate change on children and help protect their future . "
