<h> Dreaming of a green Christmas : 5 steps to be more environmentally friendly <p> If you 're concerned about the rampant consumerism surrounding Christmas , in the face of our need to reduce our collective carbon footprint , Joanna Bull has two simple words to keep in mind : " Be thoughtful . " <p> Not thoughtful in the sense that you need to get everyone in your life a gift , but in considering each choice you make in holiday giving , decorating and indulging , and the larger implications those choices carry . <p> As the Ecology Action Centre 's volunteer and community engagement coordinator , Bull has several suggestions for celebrating a green Christmas that do n't diminish the spirit of the season while honouring the ecological principles of the three Rs : reduce , reuse , recycle . <p> Sometimes the best gifts do n't come in a box . ( How the Grinch Stole Christmas / Warner Bros . Television Distribution ) <h> Experiences over stuff <p> The most important is the first : reducing the number of products ( and their packaging ) that we accumulate , and ultimately dispose of . In the spirit of waste reduction and fighting climate change , gifts that provide experiences and make memories are preferable to supplying more gadgets , appliances , electronics and batteries that will eventually wind up in a landfill . <p> " What do we really need ? Do we need a new version of something when the old one is good enough , or is there another thing we can do instead ? " asks Bull . <p> " It 's not about feeling guilty , it 's about giving some thought to the choices we 're making . Reducing the amount of stuff we 're buying means we 're reducing the amount of stuff we 're throwing out . " <h> Buy local and regift <p> When it comes to gifts , something like a certificate for dinner at a local restaurant or a night out at the theatre , a concert or the movies carries a deeper personal touch than a new toaster or novelty slippers . Other options that leave less of a carbon footprint include locally made products , handmade items or foods , and even that old standby , regifted presents . <p> " Obviously , there 's some etiquette involved there , but regifting can be a good thing to do , so we do n't have to consume so much , " she says . " There are so many things that already exist , and they still have value . We do n't necessarily have to keep making more all the time . <p> Bull is a big fan of making Secret Santa a tradition among families or groups of friends , so you get one meaningful present for a friend or relative instead of multiple items out of a sense of obligation . And those presents can be wrapped in paper saved from previous years or reusable gift bags that can transport holiday surprises for years to come . <h> Real vs. fake <p> Holiday gatherings are often centred around that most traditional of symbols , the Christmas tree , and the EAC has been supplying hundreds of them to Nova Scotians with its popular annual tree sale which began in 1992 . This year , their orders have all been filled with organic , pesticide-free trees grown on a farm near LaHave , but there are still lots of options for getting a greener , locally grown fir or spruce without having to go to to much trouble . <p> " Most folks do n't use pesticides anymore , but most still use herbicides and fertilizers so it 's good to ask those questions , " says Bull . " In general , if you can get a spray-free tree , that 's great , and supporting a local farmer growing real trees is the more ecologically-friendly choice than getting an artificial tree . " <p> Fake plastic trees might feel like a tree-saving option , but Bull points out that they 're made of petrochemicals , shipped over long distances , and will eventually wind up as part of a landfill no matter how long you think they 'll last . " And a lot of the plastic Christmas stuff is produced in factories that have pretty poor worker conditions , which is also not great , " adds Bull . <p> " In contrast , if you get a nice little balsam fir from down in Lunenburg County , it 's probably 12 to 15 years old before it gets cut , and in all that time it 's been busy sequestering carbon , sheltering animals and birds and things that live around it , and it 's produced locally which supports the local economy . <p> " At the end of its life , it can be composted . Here in Halifax the city will pick it up , it will be mulched and goes back into the earth , so it 's a lower-impact option . " <p> Even greener is the idea of having a live potted Norfolk pine that will live for years and can be an annual Christmas decoration with the right amount of care , just like any other houseplant . <p> On a similar note , Bull says the same goes for Christmas wreaths , which can be obtained from local craftspeople at the Brewery Market and elsewhere , with boughs taken from harvested or live trees using natural ornamentation instead of plastic bells and bows . <p> Giving to help others never goes out of style . ( It 's a Wonderful Life / Paramount Pictures ) <h> Best gift of all <p> But the greenest idea of all is giving a gift that keeps on giving , an idea the Ecology Action Centre can fully endorse . Making a donation to a charity in someone 's name , not only reduces the negative impact that we have , it makes a positive change in the lives of others and the community as a whole . <p> A charitable donation might not be the ideal gift for a child hoping to find a new Nintendo Switch or a Fortnite Nerf Blaster under the tree , but for those in your life who are older and have prioritized what 's important in life , it can be a win-win all around . <p> " Once you 're a grownup , you 're wondering what to get your aunt or anyone else who does n't need anything , " says Bull , whose own organization frequently gets requests for gift memberships for others as well as regular charitable donations . <p> " If there 's a cause that you know they care about , then they can have a nice , warm fuzzy feeling about it . Different charities have different setups for that , but it 's a great way to support good work in the community in honour of somebody or as a gift . It 's thoughtful and you do n't need to go out and buy more stuff at all . " <p> 5 Steps Towards a Greener Christmas <p> Reduce : Think about what a gift means , and whether it will be truly cherished <p> Buy local : Lower carbon footprint , better for the community <p> Give experiences : A night out at local theatre or concerts creates more memories than waste <p> Real &gt; fake : Genuine Christmas trees serve a purpose before and after they 're harvested , and become compost , not landfill <p> Charity : Supporting a loved one 's favourite cause is the purest expression of the reason for the season
