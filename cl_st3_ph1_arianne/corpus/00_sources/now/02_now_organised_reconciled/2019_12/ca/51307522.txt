<p> QUEBEC -- As provincial legislatures across Canada wound up business before Christmas , Premier Jason Kenney , accompanied by a large delegation of his ministers , descended on Ottawa , seeking federal support for Alberta 's petroleum-based economy . <p> For his part , looking to the future , Quebec Premier Fran ? ois Legault went to Hollywood , chequebook in hand . <p> " I want to make you a proposal you ca n't refuse , " the premier would say , meeting officials from Netflix , Disney and other players in the growing media streaming industry . <p> Other provinces and states have expressed interest in the carbon market , created by then-California governor Arnold Schwarzenegger and Quebec premier Jean Charest . <p> Now the only members are California and Quebec , although New Zealand has expressed interest in joining . <p> While in California , Legault told La Presse canadienne news agency he intends to serve only two mandates , sticking to a commitment made when he formed his Coalition Avenir Qu ? bec party in 2011 . <p> Back in Montreal , Legault received a visit from Justin Trudeau , as part of the prime minister 's post-election contacts with Canada 's premiers . <p> " There are lots of things we agree on , " Trudeau said after the meeting , where Legault pressed for federal funding for proposed tramway projects and brought up his government 's controversial Bill 21 , which includes a ban on wearing religious signs . <p> Completing his government 's first full year in office , Legault remains one of Canada 's most popular premiers , with a 60 per cent satisfaction rating in a recent L ? ger Marketing poll and acceptance by francophone voters for Bill 21 . <p> The L ? ger poll gave his CAQ a 38-per-cent share of voter intentions , higher than the 37.4 per cent that brought him to power Oct. 1 , 2018 . <p> Pundits , who pronounced the honeymoon with Legault over in early December , were confounded by the CAQ 's easy win in the Jean-Talon byelection , taking a Quebec City riding that has only ever elected Liberals and depriving the Quebec Liberals of their only seat east of their Greater Montreal stronghold . <p> Ironically , it was belt-tightening by the Liberals under defeated premier Philippe Couillard , leading to vote dissatisfaction and a Liberal loss , that handed Legault a budgetary surplus and sound public finances . <p> Legault 's government has pursued its grab-bag of populist measures , such as its law on secularism , another to abolish school boards , limits on immigration , kindergarten starting at age four and raising the legal age to consume cannabis in Quebec to 21 , from age 18 set in the federal law legalizing recreational cannabis across Canada . <p> In his election campaign , Legault made the point that Quebecers wanted to turn the page on decades of the debate in Quebec over federalism versus independence . <p> Defining himself as a Quebec nationalist , not a federalist , Legault wants greater autonomy for his province within Canada and promises there will be no referendum on leaving Canada as long as he is premier . <p> If there is a common thread to Legault 's conservative , pragmatic -- yet interventionist -- policies it is standing up for Quebec , its French identity , and aiming to wean Quebecers off Ottawa 's Equalization Payments , with more well-paying jobs attuned to the future . <p> " Des Qu ? b ? cois Plus Riches , " read the podium slogan at Legault 's end of session news conference , recalling his election promise to make Quebecers more wealthy . <p> " Fasten your seatbelts , " the former Air Transat founding investor advised , promising more to come in the New Year to reporters who noted the busy legislative schedule of his CAQ government in its first year of office . <p> The highlight of his first year was Bill 21 , formally designating Quebec as a secular jurisdiction , but also banning religious signs worn by " authority figures , " including judges , prosecutors , peace officers , court officials , government lawyers and teachers . <p> Few , if any , " authority figures " in the province display religious signs , with the exception of teachers , and specifically Muslim women teachers , who wear the hijab religious head covering . <p> Court challenges of Bill 21 are underway and in the run-up to a December meeting of the premiers the Ontario legislature adopted a motion calling for repeal of Bill 21 . <p> As well , the Manitoba government bought ads in Quebec newspapers , inviting opponents of Bill 21 to move to their province . <p> Legault said during the federal election campaign that other Canadians should not oppose Bill 21 , that it is moderate , in the sense that the ban only applies to hours worked , and that Quebecers , unlike other Canadians , oppose the concept of multiculturalism . <p> After the Ontario and Manitoba interventions , the Quebec National Assembly , in a unanimous roll-call vote , approved a motion affirming the legitimacy of laws adopted by the assembly . <p> The Quebec Liberals and Qu ? bec solidaire oppose Bill 21 , but joined the CAQ and its Parti Qu ? b ? cois allies in voting to affirm the assembly 's powers . <p> Qu ? bec solidaire MNA Vincent Marissal advised politicians in the rest of Canada to " mind their own business . " <p> And that view seems to be shared by a majority of Quebecers . <p> Pascal B ? rub ? , interim leader of the PQ , was close to Legault when he was a PQ minister and believes the premier one day will realize the rest of Canada does not accept Quebec 's values , reverting to his sovereignist past . <p> " Sooner or later this coalition will crack , " B ? rub ? said in his end-of-session summary . <p> Legault was a PQ minister from 1998 until the party 's 2003 defeat by Charest 's Liberals . <p> He was a keen sovereignist in those days , at one point touring the province in a campaign bus to drum up support for Quebec independence . <p> In 2009 , after two election defeats , Legault , by far the most dynamic member of the PQ opposition at the time , quit . <p> He re-emerged in 2011 , proposing ideas to change Quebec , chiefly dropping the goal of Quebec independence , setting the groundwork for creation of the CAQ . <p> The CAQ merged with Action d ? mocratique du Qu ? bec , a populist party created by dissident Liberals that came close to winning power under leader Mario Dumont in the 2007 election . <p> Going into to the 2018 election , with referendum fatigue and polls showing shrinking support for Quebec sovereignty ; his message that it was time to put to rest the goal of independence struck a chord with voters . <p> And not only voters were swayed . <p> Many of the CAQ 's political staffers were previously PQ and Liberal staffers . <p> Jean Charest 's international affairs advisor Mario Lavoie , now plays the same role for Legault . <p> Val ? rie No ? l-L ? tourneau was press secretary to Pascal B ? rub ? when he was a PQ minister . No ? l-L ? tourneau campaigned for the CAQ and was Legault 's press secretary before taking up a position with Quebec 's New York delegation . <p> Legault is a voracious reader , with bedside titles on issues such as climate change and artificial intelligence , but also titles by former prime minister Stephen Harper and Serge Savard , a star player with Montreal Canadiens in their glory years of the 1970s . <p> He told reporters he agrees with Harper that nationalism is a rising force in politics and he invited to his office Savard , whose father was an organizer for the Union Nationale , the right-of-centre party that ruled Quebec for almost a quarter century . <p> " The Union Nationale is now called the CAQ , " Legault told Savard . <p> The Union Nationale was a coalition of dissident Liberals and Conservatives led from the 1930s until his death in 1959 by Maurice Duplessis . <p> It was Duplessis who placed a crucifix in the Quebec legislature over the Speaker 's chair and his government that adopted Quebec 's blue and white flag . <p> Duplessis would boast he led the only Catholic government in North America . <p> He was also a social conservative , campaigning against Jehovah 's Witnesses and Communists , using his police to break up strikes and financing his party through a system of kickbacks from companies awarded government contracts . <p> If Legault sees any common elements between his CAQ and the Union Nationale , it is in the Quebec nationalism both parties share and their common goal of provincial autonomy . <p> Party financing reforms make kickbacks by contractors difficult now and Legault sent an anti-corruption message in naming as his justice minister , Sonia Lebel , a prosecutor for the Charbonneau Commission into corruption in Quebec 's construction industry . <p> When Legault was recruited as a PQ minister , after his business career as a co-founder of Air Transat made him independently wealthy , he said he was to the left , but " the efficient left . " <p> He is open to gender parity and artificial intelligence and sees innovation as the way to make Quebecers richer . But he is personally conservative . <p> As the premier of a province that has declared itself officially secular , he raised eyebrows in Quebec when he greeted California Gov . Newsom , saying , " You 're Catholic . I am too , " going on to say all French Canadians are Catholics . <p> Legault 's challenge for the New Year is dealing with climate change , which he views through the lens of sustainable development . <p> Under pressure from the opposition and citizens ' initiatives , he promised last May to unveil his plans to deal with the climate crisis in 2020 . <p> Legault is on the record opposing the Energy East pipeline , intended to bring Western Canada crude across the country , through Quebec , and on to the Irving oil refinery in New Brunswick . <p> He has spoken favourably though of GNL , a planned project to bring natural gas from Western Canada to Quebec 's Saguenay region . The gas would be liquefied in Quebec and shipped overseas in LNG tankers , raising concerns about large tanker ships passing through waters that are the habitat of whales . <p> Confrontations with environmentalists can be expected . <p> There will be tough negotiations with Quebec 's 550,000 public sector employees , including teachers , hospital workers and civil servants . <p> But the Trudeau government in Ottawa could prove to be an ally for Legault , with shipbuilding contracts for the Chantier Davie shipyard and federal financing for Quebec 's proposed green transit projects .
