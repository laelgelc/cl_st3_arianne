<h> The Drilldown : Oil prices drop as trade tensions between U.S. and China loom <p> On Friday , OPEC+ -- including oil producing countries such as Russia and Saudi Arabia -- decided that they would reduce their joint crude production by 1.7 million barrels per day . This decision has resulted in a continuous drop in oil prices this week , and more are likely to come with the deadline for the implementation of new U.S. tariffs on Chinese imports around the corner . <p> " Now with OPEC+ meetings out of the way , the market will likely focus back to trade talk developments , particularly with the December 15 deadline fast approaching , said Warren Patterson , an analyst for ING , reported Reuters . <p> According to the U.S. Agriculture Secretary Sonny Perdue , the Trump-led administration does not want to act on the next round of tariffs , but Trump is hoping there is " movement " from China . <p> In Canada <p> The Senegal-based Massawa gold project , run by Barrick Gold Corp. , is expected to be sold to Toronto-based West African gold producer Teranga Gold Corporation for US$430 million . <p> Teranga will be paying Barrick US$300 million in cash and another US$80 million in stock , with another US$50 million to be paid over the next three years depending on the gold levels that are collected . Once this deal is complete , Teranga 's second biggest shareholder will be Barrick . <p> Meanwhile , according to the First Nations LNG Alliance , the next 150 years of domestic fuel consumption and global exports could be supported with the natural gas reserves found in northern B.C , most of which exist on unceded Indigenous territory . The B.C legislature has adopted the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples ( UNDRIP ) , which Fort Nelson First Nation Chief Sharleen Gale explained will help push the need for business partnerships with First Nations , reported The Vancouver Sun . <p> The Haisla First Nation has signed on to a 25-year export licence and has decided to develop a floating LNG plant near the Kitimat . The Haisla nation has also fostered partnerships with Metlakatlaa , Nisga'a First Nations and Lax Kw'alaams who all have made a target of mitigating climate change with LNG . <p> Findings from the Canadian Council for Aboriginal Business expect that , by 2024 , the Indigenous economy will increase from $30 billion to $100 billion , and that in Canada , Indigenous businesses are growing nine times faster than non-Indigenous businesses . <p> Sixteen First Nations are members of the Pacific Trail Pipeline , and in the past four years , twenty-four First Nations have signed agreements for natural gas expansion . <p> Internationally <p> In other news , the East Mediterranean Gas Forum , which includes Egypt , Israel , Cyprus , Jordan , Greece , Italy and the Palestinian Authority , is seeing pushback from Turkey when it comes to exploiting the natural gas reserves in the eastern Mediterranean . <p> Turkey , who is not involved in the gas forum and has a growing tension with Cyprus , has reservations about the resource extraction . Turkey vocalized that it is not in favour of Cyprus being a part of the resources extraction process unless revenue is shared with Turkish Cypriots . The most efficient route for a new pipeline project would go through waters in the region of Turkey , and without Turkey 's participation , a new energy project would be difficult to properly develop , according to Bloomberg.
