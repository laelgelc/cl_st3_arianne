<h> Market News <h> Explainer : What is at stake in talks on next EU long-term budget ? <p> BRUSSELS ( Reuters ) - The leaders of 27 European Union countries will try to narrow their differences over the bloc 's next long-term budget at a summit on Thursday , but the discussions are likely to drag well into 2020 . <p> There has been a lot of haggling over the joint budget for 2021-27 because Britain 's departure from the EU means there will be less money in the pot . New priorities like fighting climate change and managing migration will also use up funds that have in the past gone to supporting farmers and poorer regions . <p> Below are the main issues at stake in talks on the so-called Multi-annual Financial framework ( MFF ) : <h> SIZE MATTERS <p> The size of the budget is the biggest battle , even though the gap between the " frugal " camp and those pressing for heavier spending is only between 6 billion and 15 billion euros a year , not much for a bloc of about half a billion people . <p> The EU 's executive proposed the budget be 1.11% of the gross national income ( GNI ) of the 27 member states , virtually the same as the 2014-2020 budget if Britain is excluded from the calculations . <p> The European Parliament wants the budget at 1.3% while the governments of Germany , Austria , the Netherlands , Sweden and Denmark , which pay more into the budget than they get from it , want it to be no larger than 1% . <p> Finland , which currently holds the rotating EU presidency , proposed the budget be 1.07% , or 1.087 trillion euros over the seven years . <h> WHERE DOES THE MONEY COME FROM ? <p> The EU budget gets its money from : <p> ** import duties on goods entering the bloc from outside <p> ** a fraction of national income from VAT <p> ** national contributions based on the size of the economy <h> WHAT IS THE MONEY SPENT ON ? <p> The two biggest items in the EU budget , which together account for some 70-80 percent of spending are : <p> ** the Common Agriculture Policy that supports farmers across the bloc ( Finnish proposal : 347 bln euros over the seven years ) <p> ** research , innovation and digital policies , investments , strengthening the single market and competitiveness of companies <p> ** dealing with migration and guarding borders <p> ** Security and defence <p> ** the EU 's external policy , like development aid for EU neighbours , migration issues outside the EU and help for countries preparing to join the bloc <p> ** EU administration <p> Reporting by Jan Strupczewski , Editing by William Maclean <p> Disclaimer : The views expressed in this article are those of the author and may not reflect those of Kitco Metals Inc .
