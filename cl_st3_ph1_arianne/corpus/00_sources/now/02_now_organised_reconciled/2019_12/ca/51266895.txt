<h> In throne speech , Liberals recommit to pledges on climate , economy and reconciliation <p> The Trudeau government recommitted to campaign pledges to lower taxes , bring in a pharmacare system and achieve net-zero emissions by 2050 , in a largely unsurprising throne speech on Thursday to kick-off the 43rd Parliament . <p> In the speech , delivered by Governor General Julie Payette in the Senate , the governing Liberals hit familiar notes about the urgency of fighting climate change , supporting the country 's " middle class " and pursuing reconciliation with Indigenous peoples . <p> On climate , the Liberals committed in the speech , as the party had in the fall campaign , to make energy efficient homes more affordable , incentivize the purchase of zero-emission vehicles , support the development of clean technology businesses and make " clean , affordable power available in every Canadian community . " <p> Specifically , the speech echoed campaign pledges to protect 25 per cent of Canada 's land and 25 per cent of the country 's ocean mass , to further reduce plastic pollution and to plant two billion trees . <p> It also made mention of the Liberal promises to cut the cost of cell and wireless services by 25 per cent , boost pensions , lower the interest rate on student loans , help support Canadians upgrade their skill and to increase the federal minimum wage . <p> Again , the speech reiterated that as the first legislative act of the new Parliament , the Liberals will look to raise the basic personal tax deduction by nearly $2,000 to $15,000 . <p> And in a nod to the regional divisions that played out at the polls in the Oct. 21 election , the government in the speech said current regional economic concerns are " both justified and important " and pledged " unwavering support to the hardworking women and men in Canada 's natural resources sectors , many of whom have faced tough times recently . " <p> On Indigenous issues , the government in the speech said it would take action to " co-develop and introduce legislation to implement the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples in the first year of the new mandate . " <p> As well , it calls for continuing the work of eliminating all long-term drinking water advisories on reserve by 2021 , " and co-developing new legislation to ensure that Indigenous people have access to " high-quality , culturally relevant health care and mental health services . " <p> The Liberals also promised to continue work on implementing the Truth and Reconciliation Commission 's Calls to Action , and the National Inquiry into Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Girls ' Calls for Justice , working with Indigenous communities to " close the infrastructure gap by 2030 " and taking new steps to " ensure the government is living up to the spirit and intent of treaties . " <p> " The path to reconciliation is long . But in its actions and interactions , the government will continue to walk it with First Nations , Inuit , and M ? t is peoples , " Gov . Gen. Payette said in the speech . <p> The Liberals in the speech again reiterated that they would " take steps to introduce and implement national pharmacare so that Canadians have the drug coverage they need , " improve mental health supports for veterans and develop a national action plan to address gender-based violence in Canada . <p> There is also a call to ban military-style assault rifles and take steps to introduce a buy-back program ; give municipalities the power to ban handguns ; and provide funding to " help cities fight gang-related violence . " <p> Also , the government in the speech said it would " work with provinces , territories , health professionals and experts in industry and academia to make sure that all Canadians can access a primary care family doctor , " partner with the provinces , territories and health professionals to develop mental health standards in the workplace and ensure Canadians have access to mental health care ; and " make it easier for people to get the help they need when it comes to opioids and substance abuse . " <p> In one of the few surprises of the speech , the Liberals said they are open to new ideas like " universal dental care , " and then " encourage Parliament to look into this . " The Liberals also cited fighting money laundering and making parental benefits tax-free , a policy pitched by the Conservatives during the campaign , when saying " there are good ideas across parties . " <p> " This government is ready to learn from you and work with you in the years ahead , " Payette said in the speech . <p> In a statement released on Thursday , Prime Minister Justin Trudeau said his government has a mandate from voters to " fight climate change , strengthen the middle class , walk the road of reconciliation , keep people safe and healthy , and position our country for success in an uncertain world . " <p> " Canadians have chosen to keep moving forward , and this is our plan to do just that , " he said . <p> Conservative leader Andrew Scheer said the party will be proposing an amendment to the throne speech during House debate on Friday . <p> " We are very disappointed in this throne speech , " he said . <p> Scheer said Canadians need a signal that Trudeau learned the lesson sent from the election , which reduced the Liberals to a minority government . As well , he said the speech was an " insult " because it did n't address the anger in Alberta or Saskatchewan amid growing separatism sentiments . He added that the approach sends a very negative signal to many parts of the country . <p> " Trudeau has pitted region against region and that is not the way to keep this Confederation together , " he said . <p> Scheer the Liberals could have indicated support for the West by repealing Bill C 69 , legislation which overhauls the environmental process , or by committing to a national energy corridor . <p> The speech also mentioned that the Liberals would continue to move forward in implementing the new North American free trade agreement and provide compensation to farmers in supply management sectors who are losing market share as part of recent trade deals . In fact , in the throne speech , the Liberals note that many farmers in the " dairy sector will receive their first cheques this month . " <p> The government also promised in the speech to " review the rules currently in place " in the digital sector to " ensure fairness for all , " as well as " remove additional barriers to domestic and international trade for businesses and farmers , continue with ambitious investments in infrastructure , and reduce red tape so that it is easier to create and run a start-up or small business . " <p> At the end of the throne speech , the government said it knows it needs to work with other parties in a minority setting , calling the election mandate a starting point , " not the final world . " <p> " Focus on your shared purpose -- making life better for the people you serve , " Payette said to parliamentarians . <p> In his comments , Bloc Qu ? b ? cois leader Yves-Fran ? ois Blanchet said he could n't necessarily vote against the throne speech because the word " oil " was absent from it . Payette said the government would work to get resources to market , a likely reference to oil , without explicitly naming oil in the speech . <p> The Bloc have had long standing issues with the oil industry in the West . Blanchet took issue with the prospect of a fossil fuel pipeline crossing Quebec and the Trans Mountain Pipeline Expansion project . <p> Meanwhile , NDP leader Jagmeet Singh said the current speech is not sufficient because it lacks specific proposals.The speech indicated the government 's commitment to implementing a national pharmacare program , but Singh said the government did n't commitment to single-payer program -- something the NDP have long advocated for . <p> " After seeing that throne speech , we need to talk , because it 's not good enough , " Singh said . <p> Singh also took issue with the government 's pledge to continue their line of action on affordable housing . He said this means the Liberals will continue to " spend 19 per cent less as a portion of GDP " than the Conservative government before them .
