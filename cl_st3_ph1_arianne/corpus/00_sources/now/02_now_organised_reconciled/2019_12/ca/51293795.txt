<h> Feds accept New Brunswick 's consumer carbon tax plan <p> The Trudeau government on Wednesday announced that New Brunswick 's proposed fuel charge meets federal stringency requirements . <p> As such , Ottawa plans to revoke the federal backstop consumer carbon tax in New Brunswick once that provincial charge goes into effect in April . The provincial charge slaps a $30 per tonne price on carbon emissions . <p> In a statement , Environment and Climate Change Canada noted that like the other participating provinces , New Brunswick 's pricing system will be subject to an annual review . The federal benchmark price rises to $40 per tonne in 2021 and $50 per tonne in 2022 . <p> The federal pricing system for heavy emitters , which operates like a cap-and-trade system , will remain in effect in New Brunswick . However , the federal government says it 's currently reviewing New Brunswick 's pollution pricing plan for heavy industry . <p> " We 're seeing growing momentum for pricing pollution in Canada because it 's a cost-effective way to cut pollution and deliver clean growth , " Environment Minister Johnathan Wilkinson said in a statement . <p> " We congratulate New Brunswick 's government for moving forward with an effective price on the carbon pollution from fuels throughout its economy . " <p> Earlier in the year , the Trudeau government imposed a federal backstop carbon pricing system , which includes both a fuel charge and heavy emitters scheme , in New Brunswick , Ontario , Manitoba and Saskatchewan . It also announced plans to impose the system in Alberta after the Jason Kenney-led United Conservative Party assumed power in the spring and vowed to scrap that province 's own carbon tax . <p> The move came after the right-leaning governments in those provinces refused to implement their own pricing system , and launched legal challenges disputing the constitutionally of a federally-mandated carbon price . Saskatchewan 's legal challenge of the price will go before the Supreme Court early next year . <p> Earlier this week , the Trudeau government announced that it had accepted Alberta 's heavy emitters pricing system , and as such , would n't implement a federal one in that province . However , it will still move forward with the federal fuel charge .
