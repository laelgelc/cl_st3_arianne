<p> A new study says " climate shocks " are reducing fish populations in the North Atlantic region , leading to fewer jobs and lower wages in New England 's fishing sector . <p> Fishing communities along the northeastern U.S. seaboard have long struggled with warming waters , dwindling fish stocks and rising unemployment . <p> The research published Monday in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences is the first to directly link climate change with declining fishing jobs . <p> It found that climate fluctuations caused a 16 per cent drop in fisheries employment in New England from 1996 to 2017 . <p> The findings suggest Atlantic Canada 's fisheries could also potentially experience increasing variability in fish stocks , revenue and employment due to climate change in the coming years . <h> Possible rise before the fall <p> Kimberly Oremus , assistant professor , University of Delaware . <p> Kimberly Oremus , the study 's author and an assistant professor at the University of Delaware , said further research is needed to measure what potential impact climate change will have on Atlantic Canada 's fishing jobs . <p> She said it 's possible the region 's fish stocks -- and fish harvesting jobs -- could see some gains initially , but that with continued warming the longer-term impact could shift again . <p> " Even if you get some better boom years , you might get some really bad bust years as well , " said Oremus , adding that extreme weather related to climate change could lead to more volatility in fishing sector employment . <p> Those extremes will make it harder for fish harvesters to make a reliable living from year-to-year , she said . <p> " If they have really good years and really bad years , they would have to be very savvy on how to save money to carry them over those bad years , " Oremus said . <p> She said fisheries management will have to be more dynamic to respond to fluctuations in fish populations . <h> Time to start cross-border cooperation : prof <p> In addition , Oremus said given fish populations may become more unpredictable , Canadian and U.S. fisheries managers should work together to ensure sustainable harvesting . <p> " Since stocks are moving back and forth across the border ... Canada and the U.S. will really need to co-manage those stocks well , " she said . <p> The new research adds to the mounting number of scientific publications warning about the impacts of climate change on the ocean . <p> A UN-backed panel of experts released a study earlier this fall that said marine life will suffer if carbon emissions are not reduced dramatically . <p> The IPCC Special Report on the Ocean and Cryosphere in a Changing Climate found that an excess of atmospheric carbon is rapidly warming the oceans , putting marine species , ocean ecosystems and coastal communities at risk .
