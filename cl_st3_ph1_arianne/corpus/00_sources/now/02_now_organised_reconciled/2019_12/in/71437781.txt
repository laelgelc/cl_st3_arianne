AP <p> Trump , who arrived in London late Monday for two days of meetings , called the trip " one of the most important journeys that we make as president " before departing Washington and noted Democrats had long known about the meeting . <p> The president lashed out at Democrats again soon after arriving in the UK . He said on Twitter that he had read the Republican report designed to counter Democrats ' impeachment case on his flight . <p> The report , which was obtained by The Associated Press , called Trump 's hesitation to provide military aid to Ukraine " entirely prudent . " <p> " Prior to landing I read the Republicans Report on the Impeachment Hoax . Great job ! Radical Left has NO CASE . Read the Transcripts , " Trump wrote on Twitter . " Should n't even be allowed . Can we go to Supreme Court to stop ? " <p> Just landed in the United Kingdom , heading to London for NATO meetings tomorrow . Prior to landing I read the Republicans Report on the Impeachment Hoax . Great job ! Radical Left has NO CASE . Read the Transcripts . Should n't even be allowed . Can we go to Supreme Court to stop ? <p> It was not immediately clear under what legal grounds Trump was calling for the high court 's involvement . <p> Trump 's trip comes amid ongoing quarrels over defense spending by NATO allies and widespread anxiety over the president 's commitment to the alliance . <p> The president said his trip would be focused on " fighting for the American people . " But in the more than two months that the impeachment inquiry has been underway , he has constantly drifted back to what he frames as the Democrats ' unfair effort to overturn the results of his 2016 election . <p> The House Judiciary Committee is scheduled to hold a hearing Wednesday on the constitutional grounds for impeachment before Trump wraps up at the NATO meeting . <p> Trump , Secretary of State Mike Pompeo , White House counsel Pat Cipollone and presidential counselor Kellyanne Conway all complained about the timing , with Pompeo saying the hearings would " distract America 's president from his important mission overseas . " <p> Trump insists he 's solely focused on scoring domestic and foreign policy wins , including revamping NATO so that allies spend more on defense . But he 's often appeared consumed by the day-to-day battle against impeachment . <p> In recent days he 's repeatedly lashed out about the " impeachment hoax " and the " scam " inquiry , even delving into impeachment at a ceremony to celebrate NCAA athletes and at last week 's annual Turkey pardon . <p> White House aides say the summit offers Trump an opportunity to counter the impeachment narrative in Washington and demonstrate to voters that he 's keeping a business-as-usual approach while Democrats concentrate on the probe . <p> But soon after Air Force One departed , Trump took to Twitter to slam " Do Nothing Democrats " for scheduling the hearing during the NATO meeting as " Not nice ! " " <p> House Speaker Nancy Pelosi , in Madrid for a UN conference on climate change , declined to comment about the impeachment inquiry , saying , " When we travel abroad , we do n't talk about the president in a negative way . We save that for home . " <p> Trump is only the fourth US president in history to face an impeachment inquiry . The gravity of impeachment is likely to play into the calculus of how other global leaders engage the president going forward , in the view of some analysts . <p> " In one sense impeachment is weakening his hand diplomatically , " said Ted Galen Carpenter , a senior fellow for defense and foreign policy studies at the Cato Institute in Washington . " For a normal president , it would be seen as a substantial problem . For Donald Trump , he 's going to try to blow right through it and act is if that 's not a relevant factor . " <p> The NATO leaders meeting is a complicated backdrop for Trump to make his first extended overseas visit -- he made a quick Thanksgiving visit to US troops in Afghanistan -- since Democrats launched the impeachment inquiry . <p> Trump has repeatedly criticised fellow NATO members and complained that too few nations are on track to meet the alliance goal of spending at least 2 percent of GDP on defense by 2024 . French President Emmanuel Macron recently lamented that a lack of US leadership was causing the " brain death " of the alliance . <p> Trump 's former national security adviser John Bolton has said the president could move to leave the alliance if he wins reelection . <p> Democratic presidential candidate Joe Biden , who has warned that a second Trump term could mean NATO 's effective dissolution , jokingly said Monday that he 'd " say a prayer " as the president heads to London . <p> " There 's time for redemption , " Biden told reporters as he campaigned in Emmetsburg , Iowa . " But so far he 's treated NATO like it 's a protection racket . " <p> In the leadup to the summit , White House aides sought to soften Trump 's past criticism of member nations as deadbeats . <p> White House officials noted that before Trump took office only four NATO members had reached the 2 percent benchmark that was set in 2014 . Now there are nine countries that have reached the threshold , according to the White House . Eighteen of the 29 members of the alliance are projected to meet the 2 percent threshold by 2024 . <p> Trump is n't the first US president to attend a NATO summit with impeachment looming . <p> In June 1974 , Nixon faced criticism when he headed to Brussels as NATO commemorated its 25th anniversary . He met with fellow alliance leaders and tried to convey a message that he remained fully in power even as the Watergate investigation gained steam . By summer 's end , Nixon resigned . <p> In contrast , Trump heads to NATO confident that there are n't the votes needed in the Republican-controlled Senate to convict him should the House vote to impeach him . Even so , he still might not be able to resist throwing attention back to the impeachment inquiry in Washington . <p> " While Nixon remained determined to rise above the swirl of impeachment and pretend he was n't distracted , Trump ca n't help himself , " said Derek Chollet , executive vice president for security and defense policy at the German Marshall Fund of the United States . " Moreover , for Trump , being in charge hardly means projecting steady confidence -- it means proving that he can keep everyone off-balance and nervous about what 's coming next . "
