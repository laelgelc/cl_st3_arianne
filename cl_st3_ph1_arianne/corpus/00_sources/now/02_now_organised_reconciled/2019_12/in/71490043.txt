<h> Specfix : It 's been a good year for SF/F -- II <p> To pick up from where we left off last week , where we looked at three science fiction &amp; fantasy anthologies viz. Strange Worlds ! Strange Times ! ( Ed . Vinayak Varma ) , The Gollancz Book of South Asian Science Fiction ( Ed . Tarun K. Saint ) and Magical Women ( Ed . Sukanya Venkatraghavan ) , here 's a quick look at three notable books SF books published first in India that made 2019 quite a special one for science fiction readers here . <p> Move over werewolves , because this year there was a new beast in town , courtesy the author of Immortal and the Aryavarta Chronicles -- Krishna Udayasankar . A science fantasy thriller , Beast introduced us to a new species that lives among us : the Homo Pantheris Leo or werelions . Or simply , the Saimhas . A hominid species that has co-existed with Homo Sapiens , these therianthropes have integrated into human society . But an internal conflict among the saimhas threatens their secret and that sets the tone for Beast , fast-paced novel that begins with the gruesome murder of three gangsters in Mumbai . Assigned to the case , ACP Aditi Kashyap finds herself falling in with the saimha enforcer Prithvi Narasimha , as they try to solve the murders while trying to stay alive from the murderous attacks of an unknown enemy . Over the course of Beast , we come to know more about the past of the secretive saimhas and of the reclusive Prithvi as he tries to grapple with his own past and his identity , by way of an action-packed thriller . <p> This was also the year that the first book in Numbercaste author , Yudhanjaya Wijeratne 's Commonwealth Empire trilogy hit the shelves . Published first in India , The Inhuman Race is a thought-provoking alternate history novel that explores of artificial intelligence , sentience and what constitutes ' being human ' . Set in an alternate 2033 in a world where the British Empire never fell and Ceylon is still ruled by royal houses , The Inhuman Race follows the adventures of the Silent Girl , a mute , mysterious protagonist who holds within her some secrets that threatens to upend status quo , which is chaotic enough to start with . Well-plotted and suspenseful , the story unravels as we see the Silent Girl discover more about the tough world she inhabits , trying to overcome the tougher living conditions in an adventure that will see her discover the secrets of not just the world around her , but about herself . <p> Also seeing the light of the day for the first time was the long-awaited collection of SB Divya 's short fiction , and pleasantly enough , it was first published in India . Contingency Plans for the Apocalypse and Other Possible Situations collects 14 pieces of SB Divya 's short fiction , 13 short stories and one novella including her Nebula Award-nominated Runtime , that have appeared in science fiction magazines and sites such as Tor , Lightspeed Magazine and Uncanny , etc . From a pollution-ravaged future Bangalore , human-machine hybrids and time travel to near immortals , amorphous beings and magic beetles , the stories in this collection span a wide spectrum of sub-genres and subjects , but despite their broad ambit what all these stories have in common is that they all lies at the intersection of technology and humanity against the backdrop of possible futures and climate change . <p> They are as much about how technology shapes and influences human societies as much as how people re-shape technology . Themes of love , <p> belonging , aspirations and sacrifice pervade this collection that belongs on the shelves of anyone interested a good rad , science fiction or otherwise . Well , that brings us to the end of the 2019 recap , and as we go forward into a new year , I am hoping that 2020 will be as good -- if not better -- for SF/F in India . Live long and prosper !
