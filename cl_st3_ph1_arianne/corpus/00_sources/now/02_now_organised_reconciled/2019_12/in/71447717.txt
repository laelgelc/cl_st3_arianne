<h> This new photography show by Selvaprakash Lakshmanan is a call to do our bit for the planet before it 's too late <p> The dire situation of climate change is seldom understood by all . Fortunately for the coastlines of India , photographer Selvaprakash Lakshmanan never tires of reiterating this warning . From the time he had gone in 2009 to document the issue of Tamil fishermen who had strayed into Sri Lanka 's waters , Lakshmanan has probed enough to realise that the lives of these fishermen and the coastline were affected by erosion and pollution . <p> He has placed 22 photos in his solo show -- Life in Troubled Waters -- as pictorial evidence of the state of the coastlines in West Bengal , Tamil Nadu , Kerala and Karnataka . <p> For someone who is from this inland city , Lakshmanan is well-versed with the issues that the coastlines face . After all , he has photographed over 1,000 kilometres of them . From the " alarming " situation of the foaming Marina Beach in Chennai to the salt mines that raise the salinity of the soil , destroying mangrove forests , there is a lot more . <p> Look at India from Google Earth , Lakshmanan urges . There is a black line running around the coastline . " This is the sea wall built by the government , in an attempt to stop the massive erosion of our seafront . But these walls are not really working as they may protect one area but then cause changes in wave patterns which erode other areas . " Case in point is a place called Iramandurai , near Kanyakumari , where they have lost the first row of houses closest to the sea . The same goes for Puducherry where there is massive erosion . As he says , " Erosion is not a problem for the future . These places show it is happening now . " <p> He goes on to talk of Karunagapalli in Kerala where , due to sand mining , chemicals are washed into the sea . " The ground water is also affected , and even the water has turned red in colour . " Sadly , coastal pollution has multiple victims . Ninety per cent of sea birds have plastic in their stomachs . Fish and the livelihood of fishermen get affected . The polluted coasts mean they stray further into the seas and when neighbouring countries are close , such as India and Sri Lanka , the matters become political and sticky when fishermen have encounters with professional navies . <p> " I hope people realise the immediacy of the problem and the difficulties these communities face . I hope these photographs spur them to either pressurise our government or make small changes such as using less plastic or segregating waste . " Because he believes industries are not the problem as much as they are " the symptoms of our need to consume .
