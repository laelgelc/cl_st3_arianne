<h> The grape expectations <p> Wine expert Nicholas Pesudovs talks about the effects of climate change on winemaking and reveals tools of his trade <p> While we often talk about the effects of global warming in the context of climate change and natural calamities , one can overlook the impact it has on agriculture and farming . <p> Australian winemaker Nicholas Pesudovs , who was in Bengaluru recently to launch a line of artisan wines , touched upon the topic while conducting several wine tasting events . " Over the years , I have noticed how the vintage period of harvest is actually contracting . Typically , vintage extends from January to end of March . But now , the fruits are ripening a lot faster so they tend to all come in over a period of three weeks leading to bedlam at the winery , " Pesudovs says . So far , this has n't impacted flavour negatively as they mainly grow Mediterranean varietals suited to warmer climes . <p> However , there is much ongoing research to deal with the phenomenon . " Australia is pretty forward-thinking that way . There are several wine research institutes that are experimenting with new varietals of wine that are better adapted for warmer climates , " the winemaker adds . But it 's colder regions -- large parts of Europe -- that grow wine varietals better suited to cooler temperatures that will be impacted the most in the coming years , Pesudovs predicts . <p> As the longest serving winemaker in Australia 's South Burnett region , Pesudovs takes a lot of pride in his job . " Honestly , winemaking is not a job . It 's an all-consuming passion , " says the 50-year-old , who has a major in wine science from Charles Sturt University . Currently , working as chief winemaker with Clovely Estate , a family vineyard with small-scale production , Pesudovs spends his time keeping a close eye on the many barrels of wine maturing at the winery . But it 's during the harvest period that the winery truly comes alive . " It 's not as romantic as one imagines it to be . It 's a truly hectic time and requires endurance . As soon as the fruits come into the winery in the morning , it has to be processed pressing and extracting the juice followed by the all-important process of fermentation which might last for a month for white wines and seven to 10 days for reds , " Pesudovs explains . Aging comes next and varies greatly depending on wine varietals and styles . " Most red wines will mature in a barrel for as long as the winemaker decides . It 's a constant tasting process . Every month , I will go through every single barrel and tank . " Oak ( from the barrels ) also plays an important part in adding layers to the wine . <p> But the really tricky part of the job -- also Pesudovs ' favourite phase -- is putting the wine together . " If you try to make a wine that everyone will love , it 's frustrating . I have to make the wine for myself and hope it translates to the consumer as well . " One of the tools that Pesudovs relies on when he has a wine ready is to take a bottle of the blend home and drink it as an ordinary person would . " So , I have dinner , watch TV or play music and drink some wine , " he explains . The idea is to disassociate from the work space . " My office looks more like a lab and it 's a very clinical environment . So this helps me think differently . After three or four glasses , when I am not thinking consciously about the wine , sometimes , I taste something on the palate that may not sit well and I realise it needs more work . " <p> Entering the Indian market for the first time , Pesudovs did come with some preconceived notions . " I thought that obvious sweetness in the wine would work better but none of the four wines that we are launching chosen after they were tried by Indian F&amp;B and wine experts are sweet . In fact , the Nebbiola an iconic Italian varietal and Petit Verdot are quite dry and people loved it . " As palates continue to evolve , Pesudovs hopes that it will open the door to more boutique brands in future .
