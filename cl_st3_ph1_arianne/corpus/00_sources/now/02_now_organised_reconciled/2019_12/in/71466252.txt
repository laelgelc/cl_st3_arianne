<p> WHO : Indian government must take urgent steps to tackle source of air pollution <p> New Delhi : The World Health Organisation ( WHO ) has slammed Indian Environment Minister Prakash Javadekar for his utterances in Parliament that air pollution has nothing to do with people 's life spans . <p> The international public health body said , " We wish that air pollution does not kill people , but sadly it does . " The top UN health body 's reaction comes after Javadekar said in Parliament that there is no study in India which links air pollution to the reduced lifespan . <p> Speaking from COP 25 meet in Madrid , Neira further said that there is an urgent need to tackle air pollution in India as some cities have crossed safe limits of pollution and that is affecting the health of the people . <p> The Union minister on Friday ( November 6 ) strongly contended in the Parliament that no study links air pollution to people 's health and that a fear psychosis is being created by suggesting so . <p> The minister also questioned the veracity of the existing data and claimed that these studies were not based on first-generation data . <p> Countering the Indian minister , the WHO director also said that studies are already available to suggest a direct link between people 's health and air pollution . <p> She also asked the Indian government to take urgent steps to tackle the source of air pollution that is causing health complications to the people . <p> The report also quoted WHO 's climate change coordinator , Diarmid Campbell-Lendrum , about the impact of air pollution on public health . <p> Campbell-Lendrum said , " We have collected and analysed thousands of studies showing complications of air pollution on health . These studies were collected from every part of the globe . We are yet to come across any study that shows air pollution does not affect human health including in India .
