<h> The real test of Uddhav <p> With the Sena boss as chief minister , will the new government listen more to different shades of opinion ? <p> The Maha Vikas Aghadi that has formed the new Maharashtra government is , in many ways , a clue to the future . It is not that coalitions have not been formed before . Some have succeeded . A few may have failed . But rarely have we seen three parties with disparate histories and ideologies , often in conflict with each other in the past , ready to sink their differences and get together to offer a cohesive alternative on the basis of a common minimum programme that allows each one of them to retain their identity and yet work together as a government offering an alternative to what was an abrasive , arrogant regime deaf to public opinion . <p> The Aghadi seals , in some ways , the fate of ideology in coming times . Like the rest of the world , we are now about to see a new politics where , hopefully , the grand narratives of the past -- caste , religion and ideology , and their warring histories -- will be put to rest for a while and we are likely to see an easy alliance between the forces of change -- technology , data , media , environmental concerns , stuff that drives the future of the human race today . If nothing else , that would align us with the rest of the world and hopefully offer a way out of the politics of conflict and confrontation that is burning up this nation . <p> It is also likely to bring about a much needed change in the way Maharashtra has been governed over the past five years and we will possibly , once again , have a government in power that is ready to listen to all voices of protest , be it indigent farmers or out-of-work youth , tired of being forced into suicide , small local businesses that once formed 80 per cent of our real economy and are now languishing on death row , ever since demonetisation and GST destroyed their livelihood , or worried citizens concerned about climate change and environmental degradation . <p> The first few steps taken by Uddhav Thackeray demonstrate that , if nothing else , he is ready to listen to dissenting voices . He may sound tentative , but clearly he is charting out a course of action that will co-opt the concerns of all those who bore the brunt of the irascible deafness of the Fadnavis regime . The farmers have been promised immediate relief . He has said that not a single more tree will be cut in Aarey to accommodate the metro car shed . Aarey , he assured me , could go back to being the forest in the heart of Mumbai that it always was -- the city 's most precious ecological asset . Work on the metro will continue , of course , but the car shed may have to find another home . <p> He has also assured us that the bullet train to Ahmedabad will be looked at again , its viability studied , the uprooting of people and villages on its route will be examined and , if the state finds that this so-called technological showpiece of the union government is not worth its cost -- not just in money terms but in terms of human displacement -- it could demand it be scrapped and the money spent on renewing Mumbai 's lifeline , the city 's suburban railway network , which desperately needs upgradation . People need to go to work first before they need to travel out . ( In any case , it would be interesting to research how many Mumbaikars actually travel to Ahmedabad in a year -- apart from the angadias ferrying cash and gold -- and how many of those can afford the ticket cost on a swanky bullet train . ) <p> FIRs are also being withdrawn against those anguished young men and women who protested against the killing of thousands of trees in Aarey in the dead of night . Promises made to farmers that were not honoured ( and were never meant to be ) are now likely to be done . The mistakes of the past few years will possibly be repaired . But , what is more important , we are now likely to have a government -- and a chief minister -- ready to listen to people . That is what will make a difference . It does not matter how many parties are working together and whether they have aligned ideas on Hindutva or secularism . It does not matter how long the government will survive . It does not matter if the deputy chief minister in Fadnavis ' 80-hour government illegally sworn in becomes the deputy chief minister here . What matters is intent . What this government wants to do . And whether it will listen to its citizens . <p> This is where Rahul Bajaj 's warning acquires serious significance . If citizens are fearless to protest , we know that we have a good government in place , a government that knows how to treat its own people , a government that is ready to listen to its critics too . That is precisely what Maharashtra missed for five years . Thackeray can bring that back again and show us that the future of democracy is safe . And so is the future of Maharashtra . <p> Always remember this . A strong government is not important . A strong citizenry is . <p> Disclaimer : The views expressed here are the author 's own .
