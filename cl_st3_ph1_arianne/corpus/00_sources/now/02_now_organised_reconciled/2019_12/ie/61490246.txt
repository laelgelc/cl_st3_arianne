<h> Easy steps to combat global warming this Christmas <p> From the record breaking heatwaves in Australia , to the global climate strike in September , to the impact of 2019 person of the year , Greta Thunberg , we 've been encouraged to be far more environmentally conscious this year . <p> But when it comes to Christmas time , our climate change concerns tend to go out the window . <p> Our environmental and global warming concerns seem to go out the window when it comes to the Christmas season , one of the most wasteful times of year . Pic : Getty Images <p> Global warming is not slowing down any time soon , as the Met Office warns that 2020 is expected to be one of the hottest years on record . <p> To combat that , here are some festive tips to easily reduce your carbon footprint during the Christmas season . <p> Similarly with Christmas cards , plain cards can be recycled , but anything with glitter can not . <p> Bows and ribbons are also harmful to the environment , and can wreak havoc in the recycling plant by getting caught in machinery . To reduce waste this Christmas , try to reuse them as much as possible before throwing them in the general waste . <h> Christmas Jumpers <p> Festive Christmas jumpers have quickly become an essential part of the holiday season . <p> According to environmental charity Hubbub , one in three people under 35 buy a new Christmas jumper every year , while only 29% of shoppers know that most Christmas jumpers contain plastic . <p> Christmas jumpers are one of the ' worst examples of fast fashion ' as they usually contain plastic and other non-recyclable materials . Pic : Getty Images <p> Because most Christmas jumpers contain plastic , and other damaging materials , they are ' one of the worst examples of fast fashion ' . <p> ' We 'd urge people to swap , buy second-hand or re-wear -- and remember a jumper is for life , not just for Christmas ' said Hubbub project coordinator . <h> Packaging <p> As of this year , Ireland receives 32 tonnes of packaging from online retailers every day . <p> In 2019 , Ireland is generating 11,740 tonnes of packaging waste from online shopping per year . <p> Although you may have your Christmas presents purchased already , make sure to dispose of the packaging properly to reduce their environmental impact . <p> Packaging generated from online sales could surpass 100,000 tonnes in Ireland by 2032 , which all contribute to waste and global warming at Christmas time . Pic : Shutterstock <h> Decorations <p> Is it better for the environment to have a real or fake Christmas tree ? <p> Artificial trees are made entirely of plastic and often shipped from China . <p> Though they can be reused , a household would need to reuse their plastic tree at least 12 times to match the carbon footprint it creates . <p> Nordmann Firs -- which are the most popular Christmas tree -- absorb carbon from the air while they 're growing , which is hugely important in the fight against emissions and global warming . Pic : Getty Images <p> Natural Christmas trees , on the other hand , are biodegradable and can be used for compost , generating very little CO2 . <p> Plus , local authorities across the country have facilities for recycling natural Christmas trees for free every year . <p> Tinsel , when you 're throwing it out , can go straight in the general waste as is n't recyclable . <p> Glass baubles are the same , and plastic baubles ca n't be recycled in Ireland , so it 's best to get as much use out of your decorations as possible . <h> Food <p> Recent research shows that we throw away a mind-boggling two million turkeys , and 17 million Brussels sprouts every year . Pic : Getty Images <p> To cut down on food waste this Christmas , get as much use as possible out of your leftovers , and donate any unopened food to charity . <p> Every year , St. Vincent de Paul launch a food appeal in the lead up to Christmas . <p> Aldi also launched their Food for Good campaign this Christmas for the second year in a row to combat food waste and donate surplus food , so there 's no excuse for any food going to waste this Christmas .
