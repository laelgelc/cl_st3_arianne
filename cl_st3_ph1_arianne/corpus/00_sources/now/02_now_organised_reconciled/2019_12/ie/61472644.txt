<h> Looking for ideas on how to set your table for Christmas dinner ? <p> By Kate Ryan of Flavour.ie <p> CHRISTMAS is full of nostalgic moments ! <p> One of my favourite memories as a child was on Christmas Eve , arriving home after spending a mini-Christmas with my gran while my mum and dad whizzed around the city gathering the last of the presents . <p> The last few jobs would be to decorate the tree , lay out the festive treats on the big , old dresser in the dining room and , of course , set the table . <p> These things were invariably done after bath time , robed in a new set of Christmas pyjamas . A festive movie would be on , my mum would be banging pots and pans together in the kitchen singing Christmas songs and also issuing orders from a never-ending to-do list to my father . <p> A beautiful wreath made by Suzanna Mellin of Banana Melon Kitchen . <p> But what I loved the most was decorating the tree . The decorations , having descended from their lofty abode , would be subjected to an inspection . Each little glass ornament was carefully unwrapped from its tissue paper blanket , scrutinised for breakages or re-threading if the cotton had snapped , and then the oh-so important decision about where to place them upon the tree branches . <p> These decorations had been in the family for generations , passed down and treasured every Christmas for decades . My mum still decorates her tree with the same foil and glass decorations today , and I delight whenever I see them . <p> I recognise that those heady days of childhood Christmases gave way to an excess of indulgences . There were no heirloom-worthy decorations for the tree . Rather , each year new decorations were purchased against a backdrop of that year 's theme or colour pallet . Decorations became disposable , and lost their emotional value . <p> 2019 has been the year when talk of climate change , zero waste and plastic free have broken through into the mainstream . My own social media feed is telling me not to buy mass-produced crackers containing little pieces of plastic nonsense that immediately head to the bin -- not to mention the nightmare scenario of how exactly to recycle a spent cracker ! <p> Table decorations by Suzanna Mellin of Banana Melon Kitchen <p> So , this year , I want to bring that sense of nostalgia , together with a mindful approach , to decorating my home for the festive season , but I still want it to look beautiful and sophisticated , as well as having that hand-made feel . <p> Suzanna Mellin , of Banana Melon Kitchen , based in Bishopstown , has such a gorgeously curated Instagram feed , I thought , who better to ask about creating a stylish setting for my Christmas table this year , all the while with one eye on sustainability . She is definitely not short of ideas and was only too happy to share them with us . <p> But before we delve in , let 's find out a bit more about her and her business . <p> When did you set up your business ? <p> I established Banana Melon Kitchen about two years ago after moving from Dublin . <p> I had no definitive plan , just an Instagram account and a big appetite ! Whilst doing other food related jobs and design work , the business grew slowly ( very slowly ! ) but organically on the side . <p> Instagram , without doubt , has been a catalyst for our relative success and despite a modest following , the creative projects and collaborations we have done just would n't have happened without it . <p> What is your background ? How did you get into food styling , food photography and cookery ? <p> I have a very diverse background ! I have a Masters Degree in Interactive Design . Whilst in Dublin and working in Web Summit , I changed to a more plant-based diet . I was n't excited with the food options so decided to make my own ! I think I always wanted to work for myself , and despite the non-linear career path , everything I have done feeds into what I am doing now . <p> Some decoration ideas from Suzanna Mellin of Banana Melon Kitchen . <p> Describe a typical Banana Melon Kitchen pop-up ? <p> I like to position Banana Melon Kitchen where art and food meet , so expect to find this ethos in each event we do . We focus on plant-based food with an emphasis on colour and creativity ; the introduction of alchemy and beautiful presentation makes it an interactive experience you wo n't forget ! <p> The end goal is for people to not even realise they are eating vegan food . <p> Suzanna Mellin of Banana Melon Kitchen with some of her edible sugar designs . <p> What was the highlight of 2019 for you ? <p> The highlight was definitely DesignPOP . I partnered with Shane O Driscoll to create a pavilion that was an edible museum of sugar art inspired by the stained glass artist Harry Clarke . As a result of that project , I have produced stained-glass window inspired sugar-craft lollipops for Crawford Art Gallery and Brown Thomas . <p> What is in the pipeline for Banana Melon Kitchen in 2020 ? <p> Establishing a proper Banana Melon Kitchen ! Having a permanent HQ where we can operate will be game-changing for us . <p> We have some exciting collaborations to come and also plan on getting into the product side of things : sauces and plant-based cheeses . I have wanted to do food products for the longest time as it 's the perfect food/design hybrid , and of course any excuse to design some beautiful packaging ! <p> Looking to Christmas 2019 ... <p> When I spoke to Suzanna about sustainable Christmas table settings , her eyes lit up . <p> " I 've just got a new dining room table , so this is perfect timing ! Plus I 've been experimenting with a few design ideas around the idea of sustainability and reusability too . " <p> Here are some of her top tips and ideas ... <p> " The key to my table is layering ; I build it with an abundance of textures and colours to create an opulent look . Using plates and boards , usually circular , I create a platform to layer things up on like candles , flowers in the middle of the table , having things at different heights creates contrast and draws your eyes to them . <p> " You will always find foliage and flowers at the centre of my table , and I like to use spare foliage from the centrepiece and place it on the napkin and then tie it up with some velvet ribbon or brown string . <p> " I have an extensive and varied ceramic collection . Beautiful plates are a must and I have lots of mini plates and bowls I use for sauces . <p> I have a bit of a weakness for gold cutlery , and this looks particularly well for Christmas time . " <p> Charity shops and swap shops are a great place to pick up second hand and pre-loved crockery , cutlery and glassware . The trend for shabby chic and vintage style has n't really gone away either , and mismatched items work for this aesthetic . <p> " Every table benefits from the warmth and atmosphere of candles , so find an abundance of these , especially beeswax ones . " <p> If scent is an important factor to you , create this using good quality oils in an oil burner instead . Scented candles should never be on a table anyway , as it can play havoc with your sense of taste ! <p> " I will use more neutral toned tablecloths and runners , if at all , to really let the food , plates and flowers stand out -- so no prints round here ! " <p> If you have limited storage for such things as table linens , invest in a beautiful table that has instant appeal . <p> Suzanna recommends consciously thinking about creating a beautiful table setting with sustainability in mind to create a table that overflows with festive exuberance , luxury and a very personal touch . <p> Suzanna Mellin of Banana Melon Kitchen . <p> Here are her top six tips for creating a beautifully festive and sustainable Christmas table setting . <p> Use linen , not paper napkins <p> " They can be washed , reused and reinvented in years to come i.e. dying , or adding beads and embellishment . <p> " They also feel far more luxurious than paper napkins . " <p> Using natural materials for your center piece or decoration <p> " Dried flowers , which can even be partially spray painted with gold or silver , add decadence and can be mixed in with Christmas foliage , such as holly , eucalyptus , spruce or mistletoe . " <p> Become a conscious consumer . Purchase mindfully <p> " Taking a few seconds to ask yourself " ' ill I use this in years to come ? ' and ' do I love it ? ' will eliminate mindlessly buying more unnecessary plastic . " <p> Create your own festive wreath <p> " I love using Eucalyptus in wreaths . To make one of your own , take a wooden embroidery hoop , some fresh eucalyptus , dried flowers and fairy lights ( preferably led powered instead of batteries ) . <p> Use some clear wire or cat gut to secure everything and , voila , you have a beautiful and sustainable Christmas wreath ! <p> Any spare eucalyptus you have can be hung around your shower head to create a luxurious aromatherapy , spa experience . " <p> Create your own ornaments <p> " I love putting a tree branch up above the table or anywhere else and hanging ornaments from it for a Scandinavian and elegant feel . Attaching the ornaments with ribbon looks even more beautiful . " <p> Shop locally <p> " Try shopping locally and buying decorations from Christmas markets , that way you are supporting local business and saving on the excessive packaging that comes from online goods . "
