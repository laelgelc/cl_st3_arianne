It is a condominium governed by the Antarctic Treaty System and has become a symbol of peace and scientific research . <h> Why is the scientific community interested in Antarctica ? <p> Thanks to the treaty , Antarctica has remained largely preserved from human activities such as mining or weapon testing . At the same time , it serves as a barometer of climate change . <p> Researchers from diverse fields , ranging from Astronomy to Environment , conduct research that would be impossible to do elsewhere . Environmental scientists take particular interest in this polar continent and can study various phenomena such as ozone depletion and sea-level rise . <p> Moreover , the Antarctic Treaty specifically states that scientific investigation and scientific cooperation should be free to be performed . This allows the peaceful coexistence of research stations from all over the globe . <p> It is based on 2017 's Maud Rise polynya that appeared in the Lazarev Sea sector , east of the Weddell Sea . <p> Using satellite images and reanalysis data at the high spatiotemporal resolution , researchers found that severe cyclones lead to a strong divergence in the sea ice field , opening up the polynya . <p> According to the study , such cyclones were unusual at this point in the season . The cause of these cyclones is believed to be the transport of heat flux and moisture toward Antarctica . <p> Advertisement <p> The warm and moist air from the west side of the southern Atlantic Ocean significantly increased the potential for a cyclone in the area . <p> This is not unlike coastal polynyas that happen because of strong winds , as opposed to thermodynamic factors . <p> The consensus now is that the sea-mountain pushes the warm , dense , salty water upwards to the ocean 's surface . In the presence of a big storm , it gets mixed with the cool , fresher water floating atop the Weddell sea , leading to this phenomenon . <p> An article in Nature Magazine also explored a similar theory , attributing the opening of the polynyas to Southern Hemisphere climate anomalies . The article suggests that " ocean preconditioning and meteorological perturbations are responsible for the appearance of polynyas . " <p> It also focuses on the origins of these conditions in climate change . <p> Advertisement <p> According to the author of this article , Ethan Campbell , these polynyas are not only caused by climate change but could further worsen it . This is because the melting of polar ice releases a significant amount of carbon dioxide that was trapped in the ice into the atmosphere . <p> Not only is the temperature of the water affected , but the frequency and intensity of cyclones also get disturbed by climate change . As a result , the chances of such giant polynyas opening up rise significantly . <p> This is a cause for concern as it disrupts the global ocean circulation . <h> Research sources <p> This research draws upon the satellite images but is also shaped from data collected by sensors strapped to seals and float robots . These robots were originally deployed by the Southern Ocean Carbon and Climate Observations and Modeling Project to study the parts of the Antarctic that can not be accessed by humans . <p> Coincidentally , they became trapped in this spot , gathering important information . This information is particularly important as the images simply reveal the holes but do not give any insight into its effect on the water column . <p> The recorded data showed how deep ocean mixing happens in polynyas . Deep ocean mixing is the mixing of warm upper-ocean water and freezing , slow-moving lower-ocean water . <h> Implications of the study on polynyas <p> It is now widely accepted that cyclones can trigger polynyas to open up again . With rising temperatures , polar cyclone activity is likely to amplify .
