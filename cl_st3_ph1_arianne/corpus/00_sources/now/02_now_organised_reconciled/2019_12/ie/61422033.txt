<h> Climate crisis , bee mortality and Chinese " adulterated " honey curtailed harvest as well as the demand for European honey , beekeeper associations say . To save colonies , " the bees have killed all the males to get rid of extra mouths to feed " in Italy . <p> A bee gathers pollen from a flower on September 11 , 2019 in the countryside near Cuneo , northwestern Italy . Unusual weather driven by climate change has wrought havoc with bees , the crucial pollinators of our food crops that are now struggling to survive in northern Italy . ( AFP ) <p> This year has been a black one for many European beekeepers , particularly in France and Italy , where unpredictable weather has produced what is being termed the worst honey harvest ever . <p> ' Worst on record ' <p> Italy 's main agricultural union Coldiretti said 2019 has been a " black year " , with " a harvest almost halved " from the 23,300 tonnes of honey collected in 2018 . <p> In France , it 's expected to be " the worst on record " , according to the National Union of French Beekeeping , with " fewer than 9,000 tonnes " -- almost a quarter of the crop harvested in the 1990s . <p> Romania was the European honey " champion " in 2018 , with some 30,000 tonnes . But this year the country 's production will drop below its recent yearly average of 25,000 tonnes , the ROMAPIS association said . <p> And in Spain , the leading country in terms of the number of hives , the harvest has been poor since 2015 , with a drop of 5.2 percent in 2017 and a 2018 season which was " not up to expectations " , according to the country 's agriculture ministry . <p> From left : Lidia Agnello , president of the Honey Piedmont Producers Association , and bee technician Samuele Colotta process honeycombs at an apiary on September 11 , 2019 near Cuneo , northwestern Italy . ( AFP ) <p> Climate crisis to blame <p> January to early September saw over 1,000 extreme weather events in Italy -- -- up over 50 percent on 2018 -- -- including hail , storms and heatwaves , Coldiretti said . <p> In France , spring was curtailed by a sudden cold snap , followed by a heatwave at the end of June . In some areas of southern France , the heat melted the wax in the hives , trapping the bees , UNAF said . <p> Frost , drought and heavy rains resulted in fewer flowers or flowers without nectar , according to Italian , French and Romanian beekeepers , many of whom were forced to feed their bees to avoid them starving to death . <p> What next ? <p> The fall in production in Italy is expected to lead to a drop in income of 73 million euros this year , on top of feed expenses . <p> The worst-off beekeepers will struggle to cover their costs , and the blow will be especially harsh for young farmers in debt . <p> All are pinning their hopes on better weather conditions next year . <p> But UNAF 's president , Gilles Lanio , fears " a backlash " . <p> To save the colonies , " the bees have killed all the males to get rid of extra mouths to feed " . The lack of males for mating may lead to a " lack of fertilised queens " next spring , meaning fewer new colonies and bees . <p> Bee mortality has also shot up in recent years due to an " epidemic " of the Varroa parasitic mite , the uncontrolled spread of the Asian hornet in Europe and the " intense use of pesticides in agriculture " , according to the French Cyclops report . <p> Bee technician Samuele Colotta examines honeycombs at an apiary on September 11 , 2019 in the countryside near Cuneo , Northwestern Italy . ( AFP ) <p> Chinese honey <p> Beekeepers are sounding the alarm over massive imports of Chinese honey , which they say is " adulterated " , for example , by being cut with syrup . <p> There is currently no European legislation requiring producers to specify the origin of honey . <p> Labels can state it is a " blend of honey originating and non-originating in the European Community " , even if the product contains 99 percent Chinese honey and only 1 percent of , say French honey . <p> In Spain , beekeepers have held several protests against low-cost Chinese honey and authorities are planning to impose new labelling requirements which would list the percentages of honey included per country of origin . <p> In France , a decree is expected to come into force on January 1 , 2020 , which would list all countries that have supplied more than 20 percent of the honey in a jar , in order of importance . <p> Northern Europe <p> Thanks to a particularly hot summer in 2018 , there has been renewed interest in beekeeping in Norway and Sweden , although it remains a marginal activity . <p> According to the Norwegian Association of Beekeepers , which now has 4,000 members compared to 2,500 a few years ago , production is around 1,300 tonnes , which far from satisfies the local market . <p> In Denmark , where production was up in 2018 , local producers are finding it difficult to shift their produce . <p> The country 's association of professional beekeepers says stiff competition from cheaper foreign honey has left its members with an estimated 800 tonnes or more of unsold stock .
