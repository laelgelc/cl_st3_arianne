<h> Emission intensity of Irish beef vs . Brazilian beef outlined by MEP <p> The European Commission has been called on to insert a legally binding " climate-conditionality " clause in all future EU trade agreements to ensure EU ambition is " matched globally " . <p> This caution was aired by MEP for Ireland South and Fine Gael 's leader in the European Parliament , Sean Kelly , ahead of a debate in the European Parliament in Strasbourg set to take place this afternoon , Wednesday , December 18 . <p> The debate will focus on compatibility between the proposed EU-Mercosur Free Trade Agreement and the commission 's recent proposal for a European Green Deal . <p> " President von der Leyen 's proposal that respect of the Paris Agreement will become an essential element for all future comprehensive trade agreements is extremely welcome . <p> " However , without a clear and legally binding clause , this would not be a substantial improvement on the requirement for parties to ratify and implement the Paris Agreement , which is already in place . <p> " In order to maintain the EU 's global climate leadership , I believe that all new trade agreements should include a legally binding climate conditionality clause to ensure that our trade policy does not put EU producers under undue pressure as they try to comply with EU environmental regulations . <h> Beef emissions <p> Kelly went on to highlight the high emission intensity of beef production in the Mercosur countries ( Brazil , Argentina , Paraguay and Uruguay ) , which can reach levels that are more than double the emission intensity of Irish beef . <p> " Our trade policy can not be counterproductive to the fight against climate change . <p> " It does not make sense to incentivise an increase in beef production in South America through the EU-Mercosur agreement when the production system is at its current efficiency levels . <p> According to the FAO , the emission-intensity of Irish beef in 2017 was 17.5kg/CO2/kg of cattle meat . When compared to the Mercosur countries , the values range from 31kg/CO2/kg in Uruguay , to an enormous 47kg/CO2/kg in Paraguay . <p> " The value for Brazilian beef in 2017 was almost 35kg/CO2 , a figure that will surely rise in the years ahead when we account for the recent increase in Amazon fires to clear land for increased production . <p> " Negotiating the inclusion of stringent and legally binding climate commitments in our trade agreements would ensure fairness for our EU producers and avoid putting them at a competitive disadvantage with their global counterparts , " concluded Kelly , who is a member of the European Parliament 's Committee on International Trade .
