<h> " It is time to put ocean deoxygenation among our top priorities . " <p> The world 's oceans are losing oxygen at an alarming rate , a new study has shown . <p> So-called ' Dead Zones ' have existed in the ocean for years , but the prevalence of climate change and increased human activity has led to the sharp rise of deoxygenation across the globe . <p> A new report issued by the International Union for Conservation of Nature ( IUCN ) on Saturday showed that the ocean 's overall oxygen level has declined by two percent since the 1950s , while the volume of water entirely depleted of oxygen has quadrupled since the 1960s . <p> It is believed that approximately half of oxygen loss in the upper sections of the ocean are due to temperature increases . <p> Dropping ocean oxygen levels can have detrimental effects on marine life , forcing them to swim closer to the water 's surface where they risk falling victim to overfishing . <p> This will lead to biodiversity loss , as well as habitat destruction , with larger species like tuna , marlin , and sharks expected to be affected . <p> " With this report , the scale of damage climate change is wreaking upon the ocean comes into stark focus , " said Dr. Grethel Aguilar , IUCN acting director general . " As the warming ocean loses oxygen , the delicate balance of marine life is thrown into disarray . " <p> Isabella L ? vin , Minister for Environment and Energy and Deputy Prime Minister of Sweden , added that although the existence of ocean Dead Zones has been known for years , " ocean warming is now expected to further amplify deoxygenation across great swathes of the ocean . " <p> " With this report it is time to put ocean deoxygenation among our top priorities in order to restore ocean health . " <p> This sharp loss of oxygen could lead to more jellyfish and slime in the ocean , negatively affecting industries that rely on fishing to survive . <p> In turn , deoxygenation can lead to increased greenhouse gas emissions , including carbon dioxide and methane which are produced by the ocean and released into the atmosphere . <p> The report states that deoxygenation can be slowed by tackling the global climate issue . <h> Peter Boylan joins us on Girls With Goals this week to talk about his new book
