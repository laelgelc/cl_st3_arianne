<p> Climate change-related extreme weather hit every populated continent in 2019 , harming and displacing millions of people and costing billions , Christian Aid has said . <p> A report from the charity identifies 15 of the most destructive droughts , floods , fires , typhoons and cyclones of the past year , which each caused damage of more than a $1bn . <p> All of the disasters identified in the Counting the Cost report are linked with human-caused climate change , Christian Aid said . <p> In some cases , studies have shown that climate change made them more likely or stronger , such as Cyclone Idai in Africa and floods in India and the US . <p> In others , the event was the result of shifts in weather patterns , such as higher temperatures and reduced rainfall making wildfires more likely , or warmer water temperatures that " supercharged " tropical storms . <p> Of the 15 events identified , seven cost more than $10bn each , the charity said , and warned that the figures were likely to be an underestimate as in some cases they only include insured losses . <p> With each day now we are seemingly reminded of the cost of climate inaction in the form of ever-threatening climate change-spiked weather extremes <p> The most financially costly disasters identified by the report were wildfires in California , which caused $25bn in damage , followed by Typhoon Hagibis in Japan , which cost $15bn . <p> Floods in the American Midwest in March cost $12.5bn and China was hit by flooding between June and August that cost $12bn . <p> The events with the greatest loss of life were floods in northern India which killed 1,900 and Cyclone Idai , which killed 1,300 , Christian Aid said . <p> Cyclone Fani in India and Bangladesh in May displaced 3.4 million people , the report said . <p> The UK did not escape the weather extremes , with Storm Eberhard hitting the country along with Belgium and the Netherlands in early March , before moving east to affect Germany , Poland , the Czech Republic and Ukraine . <p> The storm caused damage across Europe costing one billion to $1.7bn . <p> Analysis suggests severe wind storms will be increasingly likely to hit Europe
