<h> Shopping for bookworms ? Here 's our Christmas gift guide for books <p> Christmas is coming , and as the the manic December days get busier and the shopping lists get longer , there 's no more efficient way to get your gifts bought than by raiding a bookshop . <p> From newly-published reads to timeless classics , you ca n't go wrong with a book no matter who you 're shopping for . <p> There 's also no messing when it comes to wrapping a book -- those uniform rectangles are a merciful blessing for those of us who struggle with the co-ordination needed to wield wrapping paper , scissors and sellotape simultaneously . <p> To help you navigate the bookshop floor , Extra.ie has compiled a handy guide to gifting books this Christmas . Pic : candwagency <p> Night Boat To Tangier : Kevin Barry -- Limerick-born author Kevin Barry has the kind of ridiculous talent that stands as a source of envy to all writers . Switching between darkly comic one-liners and deeply moving prose , Barry catches the quirks and cadence of Irish speech and culture with an incredible ear . His latest brilliant novel is about two ageing Irish gangsters stuck in a port in Spain , but his other novels , Beetlebone and City of Bohane , and his short-story collections would also make fantastic gifts . <p> The Lesser Bohemians : Eimear McBride -- She 's been compared to Joyce , but do n't let that put you off . McBride 's two novels , A Girl Is A Half-Formed Thing and The Lesser Bohemians are nothing short of magic -- dealing with troubled young female characters and weighty issues like abuse , McBride 's work is not for the faint-hearted , but her unique and heartwrenching prose , especially in The Lesser Bohemians , is seriously rewarding . <p> Nobber : Oisin Fagan -- A madcap , hilarious debut novel , Nobber follows four chancers as they travel around 14th century Ireland looking to benefit from cheap property prices as a result of the Black Death . It 's as wild as it sounds , but the sheer boldness of it carries the book , and would be an excellent gift to laugh your way through in a single sitting over Christmas week . <p> Olive Kitteridge : Elizabeth Strout -- Olive Kitteridge would make a great present paired with the recent publication of Strout 's follow-up stories about her cranky but beloved protagonist , Olive Again . With the formidable former teacher acting as a vital centre , these books relate the stories of the inhabitants of a small coastal town in Maine , New England , and serve as a bittersweet insight into universal concerns about marriage , ageing , family and death . <p> Girl , Woman , Other : Bernardine Evaristo -- Controversially , Evaristo 's novel Girl , Woman , Other won the coveted Booker Prize in conjunction with Margaret Atwood this year , with many arguing that the Londoner deserved solo recognition . Her daring book follows the lives of 12 characters , principally black women , over the course of the 20th century , providing a luminous voice to those those whose experiences have traditionally been marginalised in literature . <p> Lanny : Max Porter -- From the author of the gorgeous , zany Grief is the Thing with Feathers , comes Lanny , a fundamentally odd but deeply affecting story about a little boy who goes missing in modern-day England . Following Porter 's stated intention to create an ' English symphony ' , the book uses different voices to capture the spirit of a community in a way that can be darkly funny and achingly resonant . <p> For anyone with an interest in U.S. politics of recent years , Irish-American Samantha Power 's autobiography is hard-hitting and gripping , delving into the inner workings of the Obama Administration and exploring the outlook of a woman often called the moral voice of her generatio . Tamargo/Sipa USA <p> General Non-Fiction <p> The Education of an Idealist : Samantha Power -- For anyone with an interest in U.S. politics of recent years , Irish-American Samantha Power 's autobiography is a must . Hard-hitting and gripping , Power 's memoir delves into the inner workings of the Obama administration and explores the outlook of a woman often called the moral voice of her generation . <p> Seashaken Houses : A Lighthouse History From Eddystone to Fastnet : Tom Nancollas -- A little offbeat , this one , but trust me , Nancollas 's short book of essayistic tributes to the rock lighthouses of Britain and Ireland is fascinating , endearing and poetic in its beauty . This history of lighthouses is a really thoughtful , personal gift for anyone with a love of the sea . <p> I Heard You Paint Houses : Charles Brandt -- Documenting the true story behind Martin Scorcese 's latest thriller The Irishman , Brandt delves into the sensational life of Mafia hitman Frank Sheeran and his involvement in one of the most infamous murders in American history , the killing of Jimmy Hoffa . <p> Richie Sadlier 's memoir records his struggle to cope with a career-ending injury sustained at the age of 24 , and acts as a searing testimony to the former footballer 's resilience , as well as an occasionally uncomfortable commentary on Irish society . Pic : RTE <p> Sport <p> The Lost Soul of Eamonn Magee : An Authorised Biography : Paul D. Gibson -- Gibson 's authorised biography tells the incredible and harrowing story of world champion Belfast boxer Eamonn Magee , taking in his remarkable career , his violent experiences during the Troubles and his struggles with addiction , gambling and his mental health . <p> Recovering : Richie Sadlier -- Taking a slightly darker turn , Sadlier 's memoir records his struggle to cope with a career-ending injury sustained at the age of 24 , and acts as a searing testimony to the former footballer 's resilience , as well as an occasionally uncomfortable commentary on Irish society . <p> Camouflage : Eoin Larkin with Pat Nolan -- Known primarily for the role he has played on the senior Kilkenny hurling panel , Larkin 's autobiography also documents his military experiences on tour with NATO and the UN in Kosovo and Syria , as well as the battle with depression he hid for so long . <p> The third installment of the Aisling series has not been a disappointment for ardent fans , giving a guaranteed laugh on every page as the much-put-upon Aisling navigates her new relationship and business . Pic : Gill Books <p> Comedy <p> Once , Twice , Three Times an Aisling : Sarah McLysaght and Emer Breen -- The third installment of the Aisling series has not been a disappointment for ardent fans , giving a guaranteed laugh on every page as the much-put-upon Aisling navigates her new relationship and business . Full of the usual charm and hilarity , the latest Aisling offering will strike a chord with anyone who grew up in Ireland and also slips in some subtle life lessons . <p> Heartburn : Nora Ephron -- A timeworn classic , this short , largely autobiographical record of the breakdown of a marriage from When Harry Met Sally and Sleepless in Seattle screenwriter Nora Ephron is wry and wicked , using humour to make sense of heartbreak with some memorable one-liners and sardonic observations . <p> The Best of Myles : Brian O'Nolan -- Variously known as Flann O'Brien and Myles na gCopaleen , Brian O'Nolan 's writing is famed for its bizarre , whimsical , sometimes acerbic tone , and this compilation of his wittiest and zaniest columns would make a brilliant gift for anyone with fond memories of his tenure in The Irish Times , or indeed for those seeking to familiarise themselves with an Irish satirical legend . <p> A perfect gift for any fantasy fan , the timing of the publication of The Secret Commonwealth to coincide with the BBC 's new adaptation of His Dark Materials means that this Christmas is the ideal time to get acquainted with Pullman 's weird and wonderful universe . Pic : Alex Bailey/Marco Grob <p> Fantasy <p> The Secret Commonwealth : Philip Pullman -- The second installment in the follow-up series to Pullman 's best-selling trilogy His Dark Materials , The Secret Commonwealth follows Lyra in her dangerous adventures as a student , and documents the fractured relationship between Lyra and her daemon , Pan . A perfect gift for any fantasy fan , the publication of The Secret Commonwealth coincides with the BBC 's new adaptation of His Dark Materials , meaning that this Christmas is the ideal time to get acquainted with Pullman 's weird and wonderful universe . <p> Circe : Madeleine Miller -- A feminist retelling of Greek mythology from the perspective of the much-maligned sorceress Circe ( who famously turned a band of invading sailors into pigs ) , Madeleine Miller 's latest novel breathes new life into ancient stories , and re-imagines the familiar world of Greek mythology in a lively way , showing that we may have more in common with long-gone cultures than we think . <p> The Name of the Wind : Patrick Rothfuss -- For those Game of Thrones fans still mourning the loss of the epic series and looking for their next hit , Rothfuss 's as yet unfinished trilogy ticks all the boxes . In a violent and unpredictable world of magic ( known as sympathy ) and secrecy , a young man must make his own way as he tries to learn what happened to his family , and sets about seeking revenge . <p> For any fans of Dublin Murders , or indeed anyone still scratching their heads over what actually happened in the series , a gift of Tana French 's original book would make a perfect , if grisly , way of passing those in-between late December days . Pic : BBC/RTE/Dublin Murders <p> Crime and Thrillers <p> Into the Woods : Tana French -- For any fans of Dublin Murders , or indeed anyone still scratching their heads over what actually happened in the series , a gift of Tana French 's original book would make a perfect , if grisly , way of passing those in-between late December days , and would serve as an introduction into French 's wider ( creepy ) ouevre . <p> The Hunting Party : Lucy Foley -- You ca n't really beat a classic whodunnit , especially one using the typical Cluedo formula of a mystery death at a gathering , in which every guest becomes a suspect . Foley 's modern version , set around Christmas time as a group of 30 somethings reunite in Oxford , is both festive and gripping . <p> Idaho : Emily Ruskovich -- This recommendation may or may not be the sly attempt of a fiction fan who does n't love traditional thrillers to slip in another recommendation , but Idaho does feature the fearsome , bleak landscapes of rural America , as well as its fair share of violence and mystery so it counts O.K. ? It just also happens to be a beautifully written , almost poetic account of extremity , and the devastating , insidious impact of hostile environments on a culture driven by individualism , so , you 're welcome . <p> If All the World and Love Were Young : Stephen Sexton -- The new poetry collection by Northern Irish poet Stephen Sexton comes highly recommended by many , including Snow Patrol frontman Gary Lightbody . Using the virtual landscape of Super Mario games to navigate childhood grief and loss , Sexton 's delicate and careful poetry investigates massive themes like death and memory as well as championing the need for escape routes from reality . <p> Diary of a Somebody : Brian Bilston -- Brian Bilston 's often hilarious bite-sized poems are tailor-made for the generation raised on Instagram poets like Rupi Kaur , and would make a lovely gift . A collection of short , unassuming poems about everything from Brexit to duvet days , Bilston 's work acts as a heartwarming encapsulation of modern life . <p> 100 Poems : Seamus Heaney -- You can not , I repeat can not , go wrong with Seamus Heaney . This new selection of the Derryman 's work , curated by his family members , works as an ideal portable distillation of favourites for the stalwart Heaney acolyte , and as a comprehensive introduction for the newly initiated . <p> While the recipes in this book do n't guarantee an Instagram-friendly L.A. lifestyle like Donal Skehan 's , the Howth native does promise easy , healthy and quick dishes for those whose lives are as jam-packed with work and family as his own . Pic : RTE <p> Food <p> Veg : Easy and Delicious Meals for Everyone : Jamie Oliver -- Enjoy the selection boxes for breakfast and the ham and turkey sandwiches for now , but with January looming , many may be thinking about experimenting with at least part-time vegetarianism and veganism in the new year . Jamie Oliver provides a characteristically no-fuss , accessible compilation of veggie-based recipes apparently designed with his own father in mind to win round those die-hard carnivores to a greener way of eating . <p> Super Food in Minutes : Donal Skehan -- While the recipes in this book do n't guarantee an Instagram-friendly L.A. lifestyle like Skehan 's , the Howth native does promise tasty , easy and healthy dishes for those whose lives are as jam-packed with work and family commitments as his own . <p> Butlers Chocolate Cookbook : Butlers Chocolate -- Before Veganuary begins and the gym membership looms its threatening head , go the Pancake Tuesday route and use up all that leftover chocolate in one fell swoop to recreate some of the decadent and delicious recipes offered by Ireland 's best-loved chocolatiers . <p> With Greta Gerwig 's much-vaunted film adaptation of Little Women due for release in Irish cinemas on December 27 , now is the time to familiarise young readers with this beloved classic . <p> Children and Young Adults <p> Little Women : Louisa May Alcott -- With Greta Gerwig 's much-vaunted film adaptation due for release in Irish cinemas on December 27 , now is the time to familiarise young readers with this beloved classic . Despite the title , this charming novel will enchant boys and girls alike with its tales of the March sisters and their gentleman sidekick Laurie . There are also some really gorgeous editions of Little Women available , making the 150-year-old novel a gift to cherish for years to come . <p> Take Five : Niall Breslin -- Blizzards frontman Bressie returns with another lovely picture book for younger readers , instilling early ways of coping with issues like anxiety and providing tips on mindfulness and gratitude through the story of Freddie and his wise Nana . <p> Dubray Books Gifts for Booklovers -- For something a little different and really special for the young bookworm in your life , head to Dubray Books , which offers a charming personal shopping service for children aged between seven and 12 . For ? 50 , children can consult with a specialist in children 's books , choosing and curating a new library based on their own personal tastes . As well as allowing the personal shopper to choose books to the value of ? 45 , this lovely service also includes a badge and a pen . <p> No One Is Too Small To Make A Difference , a compilation of 11 speeches by climate activist Greta Thunberg , gives an inspirational and galvanising slant to the doom and gloom rhetoric of climate change . Pic : IONEL BONAVENTURE/AFP/Getty Images <p> Stocking Fillers <p> A Christmas Carol : Charles Dickens -- You ca n't compile a Christmas book gift guide without giving a nod to the creator of Christmas as we know it , Charles Dickens , and the legendary Ebenezer Scrooge . There 's a reason this one has endured , and the slim novella makes an ideal stocking filler to be re-read in one sitting every year . <p> No One Is Too Small To Make A Difference : Greta Thunberg -- A lot of the rhetoric surrounding climate change is deeply depressing and not really ideal for the festive season . However , this compilation of 11 speeches by the famous teenage climate activist Greta Thunberg is inspiring stuff , and could certainly be described as galvanising if not uplifting . <p> ' T was the Nightshift Before Christmas : Adam Kay -- A pocket-sized follow-up to his bestselling memoir This Is Going To Hurt , Kay returns to the secret lives of NHS junior doctors , this time compiling a collection of hilarious , tragic and just plain disgusting anecdotes with a festive theme .
