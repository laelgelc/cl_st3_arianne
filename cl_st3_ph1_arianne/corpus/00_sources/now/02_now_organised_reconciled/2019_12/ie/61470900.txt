<h> Olga Aughey <h> Weatherman hits out " amateurish " remarks by Met 's head of forecasting <p> Olga Aughey <p> Midland Weather Channel 's Cathal Nolan has responded to claims made by Met ? ireann that independent forecasters are " amateurish " . <p> It follows widespread criticism of the national forecaster for not giving enough notice to the likes of Galway County Council to prepare for Storm Elsa , that saw gusts of up to 126km/h batter the west coast , leaving motorists stranded in Strandhill , and more than 50 cars under water . <p> An exchange on national radio by the Mayor of Galway , Mike Cubbard , and head of forecasting at Met ? ireann , Evelyn Cusack , led to Ms Cusack calling organisations such as the Midland Weather Channel " amatuer " , stressing that the issuing of weather warnings is solely the responsibility of Met ? ireann . <p> " This is the second time Ms Cusack has criticised my issuing of weather advisories , with the previous event being in relation to Storm Ali during which I received a direct phone call and written message one day before the storm , " Cathal told the Westmeath Examiner . <p> " I find it to be both insulting and unbecoming of Ms Cusack describing the forecasting of the Midland Weather Channel ( MWC ) as ' amateurish ' , especially considering that on both occasions it was the MWC weather advisories that accurately predicted the consequences of both storms days in advance of predictions and warnings by Met Eireann who underestimated , and poorly informed the public of the impacts of both storms , thereby ensuring that many people in Galway , Mayo and the west of Ireland were caught off guard , " he said . <p> Cathal who has a BA in Physical Geography , an MA in Political Science , BSc in Climatic and Atmospheric Science , and the holder of a PhD Scholarship at UCC in the field of Climate Change Science , had in fact issued a red weather " advisory " to for both the south and west coasts , including counties Galway , Mayo and Cork , 16 hours ahead of the storm . <p> This fact was raised by the Galway Mayor , who questioned why Met ? ireann , with all its resources at its fingertips , failed up upgrade the weather warning for Galway from Yellow to Orange , less than an hour before the storm reached its full veracity , that saw waves whipped over the quays and barriers , flooding the Salthill promenade and its properties . The city 's emergency plan had to be put into action after southerly winds forecast at 40 to 45 knots hit 73 knots . <p> " Midland Weather Channel has zero funding and we predicted that this would be an aggressive strom days in advance . <p> " I do believe it is up to the national forecaster to issue weather warnings , but it is very frustrating for a qualified weather forecaster such as myself , who has a moral obligation to warn the public in advance of a dangerous and potentially life-threatening storm , and not to see the national forecaster act on the information it has at its fingertips given all its resources . <p> " During Storm Elsa , Fastnet Lighthouse which always gets the worst of the winds , recorded gusts of 133km/h , while Mace Head in Galway recorded 126 km/h . However , the sustained windspeed in Galway was over 80km/h , which automatically calls for a red warning , " he adds . <p> Cathal says the events of the last few days only reinforces the need for an independent public weather forecaster . <p> " The events of Storm Elsa and other such cases leads me to ultimately believe that freeing up the sector , in this case national weather forecasting , can only be an excellent for the standards of meteorological information in the country . The people must decide themselves , in a free market society , which service they wish to avail of . One professional organisation which costs the taxpayer significant sums of money annually , with questionable results as backed up by what occurred on December 18 . Or a non-profit ' amateur ' service which does n't cost the State a penny and has a clear advantage when it comes to the accuracy and timing of weather advisories . <p> " Both organisations can exist in tandem I believe , as competition can only benefit the standards of meteorological forecasting in this country . Again the success or failure of the MWC , soon to be Ireland 's Weather Channel , will ultimately be decided by the general public . " <p> *Cathal is currently giving climate change talks in both libraries and schools and can be contacted either through the Midland Weather Page on Facebook or via email at cathalgnolan@gmail.com.
