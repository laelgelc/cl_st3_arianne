The Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development stated last month that activity has been hobbled by weaker trade and investment in the past two years as US President Donald Tru ... <p> Political clouds over the United States , coupled with wider climate and digital transformations , suggest a tricky 2020 for the world economy , although experts say a lurch back to crisis is improbable . <p> The Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development stated last month that activity has been hobbled by weaker trade and investment in the past two years as US President Donald Trump pursued a trade war with China . <p> The OECD expects global growth to dip in the coming year to 2.9 percent - the lowest level since the world recession of 2009 . <p> Trump appears to have struck a truce with China for now , under a " phase one " pact announced this month , but pre-existing tariffs remain in place , and it will take time to demobilize their effects . <p> More broadly , the OECD contrasted the proactive steps taken by central banks with the policy foot-dragging by governments in the face of climate change and the march of technology . <p> Industrialists and investors are having to correct their climate strategies even as Trump sits firm in his policy of denial . Oil giant Saudi Aramco recently trimmed back the volume of its gigantic share offering . <p> The International Monetary Fund was a little more optimistic in its latest World Economic Outlook , forecasting 2020 growth of 3.4 percent but warning nevertheless of a " synchronized slowdown and uncertain recovery . " <p> At a time of populism and protests around the world , politics will remain an economic wild card next year . <p> Trump heads into a November presidential election under an impeachment cloud , and Britain 's Brexit divorce from the European Union could be sealed next month following Prime Minister Boris Johnson 's election triumph . <p> The rise of technological giants sitting on mountains of data is meanwhile challenging the distribution of wealth between governments and big business , which has the potential to reshape the world of work as artificial intelligence exploits that data . <p> The online arena has emerged as another front for Trump 's trade wars , after he threatened tariffs on France over its digital tax imposed on the likes of Amazon , Facebook and Google . Europe threatens a collective response . <p> Mistrust is felt well beyond the United States . <p> " We 're not worried about how to overcome a cyclical crisis - we know what to do , " said Ingo Kuebler , staff representative at Mahle , a German automotive supplier that has already opted to downsize as car buyers turn away from diesel engines . <p> " The big issue is transformation , digitalization , electric mobility , " he said , as he fears an influx of cheap Chinese car batteries that could see " the loss of many jobs . " <p> Since the financial crisis a decade ago , central bank policies have led to negative interest rates spreading in some countries , squeezing bank profitability and inflating private debt . <p> With growth faltering , the debate about wealth distribution will likely become still more acute . Anger at inequality runs like a thread through protest movements from rich Hong Kong to developing Chile . <p> In 2018 , according to Oxfam , 26 US dollar billionaires had as much money as the poorest half of the world . <p> " Even when people seem to enjoy basic material comfort , they may still experience the same level of misery and unhappiness as the poorest , " French academic Esther Duflo said in October after she won the Nobel Prize in economics . <p> American investor Steve Eisman of The Big Short fame thinks another global crisis is unlikely . But the best that can be hoped for is a slow strangulation of growth . It will be " your normal garden variety of recession , where the economy slows and goes negative and people lose money , " said the man who predicted the sub-prime crisis . " That 'll be painful enough . "
