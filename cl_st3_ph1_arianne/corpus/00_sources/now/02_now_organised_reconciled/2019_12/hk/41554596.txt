" Many believed that western civilization began with the Greeks and Romans , but he proposed that early civilization originated from the Middle East and shaped the Greco-Roman world . " <p> The institute has since developed in three main areas : archeological fieldwork , research in language and textual sources , and building up a museum . " We are ambitious to cover the whole ancient Middle East , but research into Egypt and Mesopotamia best represents us , " said Woods . Research into other areas , like Turkey and Jordan , are also a notable part of their work . <p> In celebration of its centennial , the institute has been decorated with significant works and achievements , such as fieldwork from the ancient Sumerian city of Nippur , in modern-day southeast Iraq . <p> " It is the institute 's longest standing excavation site that started in 1948 and ended in the 1990s , " said Woods . " It covered massive sequences and helped understand major historical developments . " <p> Another highlight is the Chicago House in Egypt , which has been up and running for 95 years . " There are famous Egyptian temples covered with writing and artworks that we spent 95 years recording and conserving , " said Woods . Their work is essential as the marks are now deteriorating quicker due to climate change . <p> The Chicago Assyrian dictionary , which took 90 years to put together , was one of the most significant milestones for the institute . " The people who worked on the beginning of the dictionary were not able to see the end of this dictionary , " said Woods . <p> The 21-volume text , completed in 2011 , is not just any dictionary . It recovered ancient languages and provided examples , allowing researchers to decipher texts that shed new light on the history of early humans . <p> By way of presenting their findings to the public , the institute 's museum contains the results of textual and archeological studies , displaying 5,000 of the 350,000 artifacts they have excavated . <p> Every artifact in the museum has a unique story . " Most museums purchase their collections , but at the OI museum , a large part of our collection was excavated by us over the last century , " said Woods . " We know the context well and can tell stories with field notes . " <p> The institute 's first expedition was an 11-month journey to the Middle East in 1919 , led by Breasted . A century later , technology has significantly changed the way excavation work is done . <p> " There has been a huge change in recording and groundwork since science evolved in the mid-20th century , " said the institute 's deputy director Jean Evans . <p> In the past , studying a site involved a physical presence on the ground , she said . But today , a satellite or a drone image means the work can be done remotely . " Technology and software is changing the work . " <p> A hundred years onwards , the institute continues to strive in their projects . " We have 12 field projects , and we are still working on the dictionaries . The researchers are also working on their personal research , " said Woods . The institute is also working with the US government on conservation work in Afghanistan . <p> The institute 's museum reopened its doors in September after a five-year renovation that involved rearranging and introducing 500 new artifacts for display - just in time for its centennial . <p> " Visitors are curious about where the artifacts are from , so we are telling them through the museum , " said Evans . <p> The new museum also features collaborations with contemporary artists . " One of the artists , Mohamad Hafez , is interested in the conflict going on at Damascus , so he built a sculpture on it , " she said . <p> Woods encourages the public to learn more about human history and culture through a visit to the museum or attending their trips and courses . " Education has always been a big part of our work . I always feel we can only survive if the public knows about our work , " he said .
