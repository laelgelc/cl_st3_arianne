( He 'll be replaced by Ioannis Vrailas , who was the permanent representative of the EU to the Organization for Security and Cooperation in Europe . ) Papastavrou , a widely respected diplomat , was in Coreper I at the peak of the first phase of the Greek crisis in 2010-2012 , before being promoted to Coreper II in October 2015 as the migration crisis was erupting . " Greece had to cope with extremely difficult situations , " as Papastavrou told Playbook . <p> No boring dinners : For people outside Brussels , it is difficult to grasp the importance ( or the meaning ) of the two Corepers , aka the committees of permanent representatives . Coreper I , composed of each EU country 's deputy ambassadors , deals with more technical issues in health , education , transport and so on ; Coreper II , made up of ambassadors , deals with the more political ones . They " are two different worlds that make one world , " Papastavrou said . Some diplomats ' partners complain that Coreper I dinners are more boring , but Papastavrou denies that . " No one should ever think that any of the issues dealt with in Coreper I are of lesser importance ; and let us not forget , Coreper I is the tangible Europe , " he said . <p> Do n't shoot the messenger : Despite Greece 's " difficult situations , " Papastavrou insisted he did n't feel isolated on the EU level . " Coreper II is always a place to share , " he said . When it came to the migration crisis , " My colleagues made me feel much better , my colleagues understood what I had to say and I 'm sure they conveyed the right message to their capitals . " And the message was that " nobody could cope with such a huge crisis . " The EU had structures to deal with asylum seekers , but they were put in place when there were " some tens , maybe some hundreds of migrants ; " in 2015 , " we started talking about thousands , " Papastavrou said . " The message was that they should not shoot the messenger . After all , it is a major European problem that concerns us all and must be treated as such . " <p> All 's well that ends well : During the Greek financial crisis of 2010-2012 , " I could sense that all could be lost , " Papastavrou said , but now , " I 'm happy with the outcome . " He added : " Of course this entailed quite a lot of heavy measures weighing on the shoulders of the Greek society ... but I 'm also proud of the fact that the Greek people in spite of the hardship , in spite of considerable loss of individual economic wealth -- and we lost 25 percent of GDP -- endeavored in decency and worked very hard to reach better days . " <p> What 's in a ' no ? ' At the October Council , France said " non " to Western Balkans enlargement and the outcome was no deal . At the last European Council summit , Poland said " nie " to the EU climate policy package -- but that did n't stop Council President Charles Michel and other leaders declaring there was a deal . What 's the difference between these two moments ? " It 's a very good question to which I do n't think anybody can give you an answer , but the idea is that unanimity in matters of foreign policy exists and , to be honest , it is an existential issue for the Europe of tomorrow , " Papastavrou said . " Unanimity is essentially a safety valve of vital importance . " <p> Migration harder than the MFF : Among the most difficult challenges in the next months , Papastavrou listed China , the multiannual financial framework ( aka the EU 's long-term budget or MFF ) and migration . Papastavrou , who has worked in Paris , Beijing , Munich and Jerusalem , reckons Charles Michel " will do all it takes to get a positive result " on the MFF , but migration will prove to be more complicated . " The MFF is something that will be more or less successfully over in a few months time , hopefully , " Papastavrou said , while " on migration , everybody will do their best ... but it 's not about numbers , it 's about people , it 's about the European society we want , it 's more intricate and complicated . Let 's take a closer look , and at the texts that can help us understand them . <p> THE ERA OF POLITICAL PHYSICISTS:Dominic Cummings , Boris Johnson 's top adviser and Brexit strategist , once wrote that to run an electoral campaign , one should hire physicists rather than communications experts . The amount of data now available makes possible Auguste Comte 's idea of having a " social physic , " where social behaviors become predictable and physicists work with huge amounts of data , argues Giuliano da Empoli , a former adviser of Matteo Renzi . In his book " Les Ing ? nieurs du Chaos , " or " Chaos Engineers , " da Empoli looked into the consequences of this new way of running campaigns , stressing that the use of Facebook marketing tools to personalize messages means parties are no longer pushed to run their campaigns from the political center , and radicals are no longer isolated , and are instead empowered to reach out to more easily found , like-minded voters . <p> WOMAN POWER : We enter 2020 with Ursula von der Leyen , Christine Lagarde and Kristalina Georgieva in top jobs . But will this mean a fairer and more inclusive society ? A possible answer comes from an experiment described by Oxford economist Paul Collier in his book " The Future of Capitalism , " where " social maternalism " was applied to the issue of single mothers whose children are taken away by the state . The " social paternalism " approach is to simply wait until the child is born and then remove it from its mother after she has shown she 's not equipped to care for it . " Social maternalism , " on the other hand , takes a different approach , working on boosting the self-esteem of the single mothers to make them fit parents , instead of focusing mainly on the punishment side . Data mentioned by Collier shows the experiment worked . But he also shows how public administration is often simply not ready for such a paradigm shift . <p> STREET POWER : The last time I wrote Playbook , I looked at how protestors have taken to the streets from Tehran to Hong Kong , from Santiago to Paris , from Barcelona to The Hague . But then , as first the financial crisis and then the migration one ( as well Crimea and Syria ) showed some of the weaknesses of the Western model , this urge to imitate stopped . <p> Macron 's poetry : Can Macron be the new champion of European liberalism ? " He has two very positive sides and one major vulnerability , " Krastev replied . " He sends clearly the message that the worst that can happen to Europe is to defend the status quo that does n't exist anymore . " And Krastev said he has sympathy for the way Macron is framing Europe 's sovereignty issues , " trying to see how Europe can be a global player . " <p> But here comes the problem : Macron " has the right intuition ... but basically most of his visionary talk is not necessary followed with a much more concrete vision . " This creates another problem : As the French president " speaks poetry , " the Germans " do n't want to commit on anything that does n't have footnotes . " Thirdly , Macron " tries to speak to Europeans the way he speaks to the French -- trying to pretend that all of Europe shares the French idea of a European sovereignty . That move is " allowing the EU to reinvent its soft power , " Krastev said .
