<p> The Met Office is predicting that 2020 is going to be one of the hottest years on record . <p> Next year is forecast to be around 1.11C above pre-industrial levels , extending the series of the Earth 's warmest years since records began in 1850 . <p> The Met Office annual global temperature forecast began in 2015 when global temperatures exceeded 1C above pre-industrial levels for the first time , while 2016 remains the hottest year on record at around 1.16C above the baseline . <p> In the absence of a strong El Nino weather phenomenon in the Pacific , which pushes up global temperatures , the hot temperatures in 2020 will be due to rising levels of greenhouse gases , experts at the Met Office say . <p> Dr Doug Smith , Met Office research fellow , said : " The forecast for 2020 would place next year among the six warmest years on record , which would all have occurred since 2015 . <p> " All of these years have been around 1.0C warmer than the pre-industrial period . " <p> 2020 is expected to be a hot one <p> The forecast is based on the key drivers of climate change , including greenhouse gases , but does not take into account unpredictable events such as a large volcanic eruption , which could create temporary cooling . <p> The Met Office 's forecast last year for 2019 is in line with what has been observed this year , at around 1.1C above the pre-industrial level . <p> Dr Nick Dunstone , an expert in climate variability at the Met Office , said : " Although the Earth has warmed by about 1C on average since pre-industrial times , this is n't spread evenly over the surface of the globe as much of the warming is occurring in the Arctic and over land masses .
