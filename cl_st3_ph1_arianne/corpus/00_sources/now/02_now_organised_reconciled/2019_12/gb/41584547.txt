<h> Who is Andrew Bailey ? <p> Bailey spent the vast majority of his career at the Bank of England , which he joined in 1985 , but is currently the chief executive of the Financial Conduct Authority ( FCA ) , the City watchdog . <p> He was chief cashier at the Bank of England during the financial crisis when , he told the Financial Times : " The RBS treasurer , John Cummins , came in and I thought he was going to have a heart attack ... and he looked at me and said : ' I need ? 25bn today , can you do it ? ' I said : ' Yes , I can do that . ' " <p> Bailey has also been deputy governor and was head of the Bank 's prudential regulation division , before leaving to join the FCA as its chief executive in 2016 . <p> He is highly thought of by colleagues and civil servants , says the BBC 's business editor Simon Jack , and will be paid ? 495,000 a year in the role . <h> What has the reaction been ? <p> Chancellor Sajid Javid said Bailey was " the stand-out candidate in a competitive field " . <p> " He is the right person to lead the Bank as we forge a new future outside the EU and level-up opportunity across the country , " he added . <p> Bailey said it was a " tremendous honour " to take on the role . " The Bank has a very important job and , as governor , I will continue the work that Mark Carney has done to ensure that it has the public interest at the heart of everything it does , " he said . <p> But shadow chancellor John McDonnell criticised the appointment , saying : " As an establishment figure with what some consider is a less than inspiring record at the FCA , Andrew Bailey will need to demonstrate early that he appreciates the need to address the deep structural problems of our economy and , like Mark Carney , understands the climate change threat . " <p> The FCA has been criticised in recent months over its regulatory scrutiny of the fund managed by the disgraced Neil Woodford , says the BBC . The fund was suspended in June and eventually closed , meaning investors are likely to lose considerable sums of money . <p> And an FCA report into the Royal Bank of Scotland 's treatment of small businesses was called a " whitewash " by MPs after it recommended taking no further action against the bank , despite RBS allegedly engineering defaults on loans so that it could acquire business 's property , says The Independent . <h> What challenges will he face as governor ? <p> Bailey is taking over at a difficult time for the Bank of England . <p> This week market sensitive information from the Bank was leaked to fund managers . The Bank said that a supplier had " misused " an audio feed , which allowed traders advance notice of information that could help them earn large amounts of money . <p> The matter has been referred to the FCA , which Bailey currently runs , with the watchdog saying it is " looking at the issue " . <p> The Bank is also dealing with the ongoing issue of Brexit , with city forecasters predicting that Bailey and the Bank 's policymakers will have to leave interest rates unchanged , reports The Telegraph . <p> Bailey will also have to deal with calls for a regulatory shake-up aimed at stopping investors withdrawing their money from funds instantly , after high-profile funds got into trouble this year because of high demand for withdrawal . <p> The new governor will also need to address criticism of a lack of diversity at the Bank . Only one of its nine-member rate-setting committee is a woman -- Silvana Tenreyro -- and Carney 's four deputy governors are all white , middle-aged men . There has never been a female governor of the Bank of England in its 325-year history . <p> And Bailey will need to take steps to handle the continued reliance on the London Interbank Offered Rate ( Libor ) , the interest rate used between banks and the benchmark for lending rates on financial contracts , that is being phased out by the end of 2021.
