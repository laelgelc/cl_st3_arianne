<p> Steve Jordan , CBC 's leader , says in the letter that this recommendation " ignored the wide ranging evidence and focused upon the anecdotal " and adds that bus journeys and cycling in Cheltenham have been on the increase since the Boots Corner trial began . <p> In a strongly worded letter to GCC he said that reversing the closure would be a ' backward step ' especially given the county council 's commitment to tackling climate change . <p> Tomorrow the county council 's cabinet will consider a recommendation from its traffic regulation committee to abandon the trial Boots Corner closure . If agreed , it would mean that the road would re-open to through traffic . <p> ( Image : Gloucestershire Live ) <h> The letter in full <p> Ahead of the meeting Mr Jordan wrote to GCC leader Mark Hawthorne as follows : <p> " Dear Mark <p> Cheltenham Transport Plan phase 4 <p> Clearly CBC were disappointed at the recommendation to abandon the trial closure of Boots Corner to through traffic , made by the Traffic Regulation Committee on Monday 16 December , especially in the light of the evidence <p> Significant modal shift with Stagecoach claiming an additional 270,000 additional bus passengers p.a and better punctuality as a result of the trial ; potentially the most significant modal shift success ever achieved within the County <p> Cycling through Boots Corner itself up by 185% since the trial began <p> Falling traffic flows in the town centre ( 20% reduction compared to 2008 ) <p> No adverse air quality impact , in fact , a slow decline in NO2 over several years <p> Positive town centre performance from a footfall and investment perspective as cited by commercial contributors at the TR committee <p> 7% increase in town visitors and 6% increase in overnight stays in the last 12 months i.e. within the trial period <p> 2019 CBC residents survey independently undertaken that identified ' Promoting walking , cycling and public transport ' within residents top three priorities <p> All from independent evidence sources and essentially demonstrating that the Cheltenham Transport Plan had delivered the outputs anticipated , and critically is making a positive contribution to climate change . <p> Boots Corner in Cheltenham when it was closed to traffic <p> Disappointingly , the Traffic Regulation committee did not spend time scrutinising the wide ranging data collected from a variety of sources , including GCC highways traffic monitoring , and were more focussed upon the individual concerns of individual speakers including a petition against the trial , to which TR committee members erroneously appeared to attach considerable weight . This petition had been considered fully by CBC within its own petition rules at a full Council meeting in January 2019 ; a petition that related to the initial scheme , not the scheme under consideration , which had been amended by GCC highways colleagues . <p> The recommendation has seemingly ignored the wide ranging evidence and focussed upon the anecdotal . For example , various objectors quoted declining footfall and closing businesses but this is not borne out by either the evidence presented eg a retail vacancy rate of 8% compared to a national rate of 12% or of major investors who spoke in support of the scheme . Sadly , there was no real challenge of the anecdotal against the empirical . <p> CBC fully recognise the concerns raised by residents but much was done by the scheme amendments delivered by GCC in June 2019 , and the highways report and presentation noted several other steps yet to be taken including the A4019 traffic light synchronisation , Rodney Road traffic calming and improved signage , plus potential to explore other concerns raised by the public including rat-running in St Luke 's and a potential 20mph zone in the centre . The highways team presentation noted that the number of representations made on scheme version 2 totalled 159 , compared to an initial count of 775 . <p> The scheme is simply a stepping zone to greater modal shift and climate change delivery . <p> Logically , any final decision on the Cheltenham Transport Plan needs to be informed by the paper later on the cabinet agenda for 20/12/19 , critically -- Leading the response to the climate change emergency -- Gloucestershire climate change strategy 2019/20 to 2024/25 , and the wider support for climate change confirmed by CBC and the other districts . <p> That document makes some very welcome assertions , critically to " put climate change at the heart of decision making " and reflects the fact that 84% of residents and 88% of businesses in the GCC on-line survey expressed concern over climate change and that there is a demonstrable need to show leadership . <p> Add to this the specific elements contained within the Local Transport Plan section such as " encourage a significant shift in travel behaviour " ... in the 6 districts plus " pursuing carbon busting options " for all forms of transport , would suggest that any decision on the Cheltenham Transport Plan phase 4 needs to be framed within this context and should not be considered until after the full debate concerning climate change . <p> The current agenda order creates the possibility of making two contrary decisions ; potentially rejecting and reversing the significant and potentially largest modal shift gains ever achieved in Cheltenham ( as recommended by the Traffic Regulation committee ) and then perversely supporting a policy that encourages modal shift to cycling and public transport . <p> The decision on the Boots corner trial is probably the first key decision post climate change motion and if the decision is to abandon the trial , this fundamentally undermines the objectives of the climate change policy , given that it could increase bus travel times , and ultimately encourage modal shift from bus and cycle back to private motor vehicles . <p> Notwithstanding the agenda order and risk of contrary decisions , there are some other key facts that need highlighting . <p> Prior to making a determination , the Traffic Regulation Committee made several caveats to their decision , which they felt needed attention irrespective of their final recommendation . These included the A4019 traffic light synchronisation scheme , signage and steps to reduce pedestrian/vehicle interface . <p> Our understanding is that funding has already been identified ( in the 2020/21 budget ) for the A4019 upgrade , and if cabinet should endorse the TR recommendation ; hopefully signage changes will be limited . However , several elements of the trial scheme do provide benefits for slowing traffic at Boots Corner and we would ask that if the decision is made to endorse the TR committee decision that respect is given to their suggestions . This could allow for the retention of the heavily used cycle racks and traffic calming at Boots Corner such that traffic speeds are reduced when traffic is allowed to re-enter the space , and also reinforces the message about modal shift and climate change . Removing the current temporary traffic calming measures would be in conflict with comments made at and by the traffic regulation committee . <p> These restrictions would need to be monitored to ensure that they do not impede bus performance and punctuality , as this would clearly be a retrograde step , and contrary to the GCC climate change commitment , to encourage a significant shift in travel behaviour . <p> Clearly there is much to do , recognising that it is generally accepted that 30% of carbon emissions are vehicle based , so losing any demonstrable sustainable advantage already secured through the Boots Corner trial will be a backward step in terms of climate change and risks alienating support from Stagecoach , as our primary bus partner . <h> Council backs plan <p> Councillor Andrew McKinlay , Cheltenham Borough Council 's cabinet member for development and safety said : ' " The trial scheme , having lasted for 18 months , has provided significant evidence to support the continued closure of Boots Corner that we see as being integral to our plan to put people before traffic and sustain the vitality of our High Street . <p> " We have seen a marked increase in pedestrian and cyclist movements , no adverse air quality impact , a significant increase in bus travel and a decline in overall traffic . It has also been noted that our high street has continued to attract investment with the arrival of some big brands and has less empty retail units than lots of other town centres , showing that it continues to punch above its weight . <p> " I feel strongly that the trial closure had led to the most significant modal shift success ever achieved within the county with a significant reduction in carbon emissions . " <p> Councillor McKinlay went on to say : " We do not want to put these gains in jeopardy and potentially reverse this modal shift and so undermine the county council 's own stated ambition to collectively reduce Gloucestershire 's carbon emissions by 80% by 2030 . <p> " In addition , the borough council commissioned an independent resident satisfaction survey and this revealed that walking , cycling and public transport ranked as a top 3 priority for residents , so the trial closure clearly chimes with local sentiment . ' ' <p> " I am therefore asking the county council 's cabinet to reconsider the evidence that we have submitted before making their decision . "
