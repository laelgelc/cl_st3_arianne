<h> BP is n't the only monster dividend stock in the FTSE 100 I 'd buy before Christmas <p> By now you 've probably finished your Christmas shopping . But the stock market is still open and I think there are still some bargains on offer , despite the bounce that followed the general election . <p> I 've been hunting through the FTSE 100 and have identified two dividend stocks that I think offer excellent value at the moment . <p> The first of these is oil and gas group BP ( LSE : BP ) . This business is in decent health but is firmly out of favour with investors at the moment . I think there are two reasons for this . <h> Fear vs reality ? <p> The first problem is that oil and gas prices have been fairly weak this year . BP reported an underlying profit of $5,169m for the first half of 2019 , 4.4% lower than the $5,408m reported for the same period last year . <p> Unless you 've been living on the moon , you 'll also be aware of the growing environmental pressure on fossil fuel firms . Big players such as BP are starting to make noises about cutting emissions and getting into alternative energy . But it 's not yet clear how these companies will adapt their businesses to address climate change concerns . <p> The markets hate uncertainty and are pricing this into BP stock . But the reality is that demand for oil and gas is expected to remain strong for many years to come . In some emerging markets , consumption is still growing . <p> The BP share price has now fallen below 500p , which I generally view as a buying signal . At this level the stock trades on about 12 times 2020 forecast earnings , with a dividend yield of 6.5% . I believe BP will adapt and survive , and rate the shares as a Buy . <h> Insider buying for this 11% yield <p> Tobacco group Imperial Brands ( LSE : IMB ) is another FTSE 100 company that 's seriously unloved . Understandably , many investors have ethical objections to smoking stocks . Recent developments in the US market have also put pressure on vape sales , adding to the challenges facing the group . <p> Imperial shares have fallen by more than 20% this year and now offer a dividend yield of 11% . However , Imperial 's Group Innovation &amp; Science Director , David Newns , spent ? 1.4m buying IMB stock in November . It looks like Mr Newns still has confidence in this business , even if the market is unsure . <h> A cash cow <p> One reason for his buying may be that this business continues to generate a lot of surplus cash . <p> My sums show that Imperial generated free cash flow of ? 2.3bn last year . That values the stock at just 7.5 times free cash flow , which is unusually cheap for a FTSE 100 stock . <p> It also means that the dividend -- which costs about ? 1.9bn each year -- is covered by free cash flow . This suggests to me that there 's no urgent need for a cut . <h> What happens next ? <p> Imperial does have problems . Chief executive Alison Cooper has given her notice and will leave when a replacement is appointed . <p> I suspect Ms Cooper 's successor will cut the dividend in order to free up cash for debt repayment and product development . I 'm okay with that . After all , even a 30% dividend cut would still give the stock a yield of nearly 8% . <p> Imperial stock currently trades on just 6.7 times 2020 forecast earnings . Although things could get worse , a lot of bad news is already in the price . I 've been buying IMB shares this year . <p> Roland Head owns shares of Imperial Brands .
