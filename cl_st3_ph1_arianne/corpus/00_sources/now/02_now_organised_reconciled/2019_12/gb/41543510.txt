A bigger role for councils in planning school places and opening schools . <p> Liberal Democrats <p> Promised an extra ? 10.5bn for schools in England by 2024-25 compared to 2019-20 . Starting salaries for teachers will rise to ? 30,000 . Ofsted will be replaced with a new HM Inspector of Schools , and league tables reformed to include information about pupils ' and teachers ' wellbeing . A ' English Climate Emergency Education Act ' would be introduced to give pupils more teaching on climate change . <p> Brexit Party <p> The Brexit Party 's " contract with the people " does not include much detail on its education policies . But on schools , the party says it will " further expand parental choice " , with the document claiming that " academies and free schools have improved results " . However , the evidence for this is mixed , with performance varying between different types of academies and free schools . <p> UKIP <p> UKIP 's manifesto promises to increase school funding by ? 4 billion per year . The party would " encourage the establishment of new grammar schools " , and scrap the 50 per cent cap on faith-based selection in faith academies and free schools . It claims children are being " indoctrinated by ' teacher activists ' who promote gender confusion , climate alarmism and a hatred of our own culture and heritage " . <p> SNP <p> Education is devolved to Scotland , with SNP leader Nicola Sturgeon describing closing the gap between rich and poor pupils as her " number one priority " in government . Minister have committed ? 750m over the current Parliament to this issue but progress has been slow . International research published this week showed while pupil performance in reading is improving , it has declined in maths and science to a record low . <p> DUP <p> The DUP says pupils have benefited from the confidence and supply arrangement with the Conservatives to the tune of ? 16.5m , including ? 700,000 for literacy and numeracy projects . It says real investment is benefiting real lives . Proposals include 30 hours childcare provision for children aged three to four , for 38 weeks and an action plan on adverse childhood experiences . <p> Plaid Cymru <p> Plaid Cymru has said it will provide an extra ? 300m for schools and colleges in Wales . The party has said it will develop new Welsh medium schools where there is local demand . It has promised to improve teacher retention by " cutting bureaucracy " and introducing mandatory continuous professional development . A ' National Cooperative Agency for Supply Teachers ' would extend training entitlements to supply teachers . <p> Summary : After the austerity years , more money is coming to schools whoever wins power . ( Photo : Getty ) <h> Universities <p> Conservatives <p> The Conservative manifesto is tight lipped on the future of university funding . It only says the party will consider " carefully " the Augar Review , which recommended cutting tuition fees from ? 9,250 to ? 7,500 per year . It promises to " look at " interest rates on loan repayments . The party says it will tackle degree grade inflation and " strengthen academic freedom and free speech " . <p> Labour <p> Promised to abolish tuition fees and bring back maintenance grants for students . The two policies are costed in its manifesto at ? 7.2bn a year . The party has said it will introduce post-qualification admissions to higher education in England , with students applying after they have their A levels , rather than the current practice of applying on the basis of predicted grades . <p> Liberal Democrats <p> Given the political damage sustained by the Lib Dems in the past over tuition fees , the party has avoided fleshing out a major policy on university funding . It repeats the wording used in previous manifestos pledging a " review of higher education finance " . However the party has promised to reinstate maintenance grants . Mental health provision in universities will be improved by legislating for a ' Student Mental Health Charter ' . <p> Green Party <p> Wants to scrap undergraduate tuition fees . It has also promised to write-off existing debt for former students who have studied under the ? 9,000 tuition fee regime . The party 's manifesto does not state how much this would cost , but acknowledges it would " add an additional sum onto the national debt " . The cost would probably run into the tens of billions . <p> Brexit Party <p> It has pledged to scrap all interest on student loans . The party has also said it will " abolish the target to push 50 per cent of young people into higher education " . The symbolic target was first set by Tony Blair in 1999 , and was finally reached for the first time in 2017-18 . <p> UKIP <p> Like the Brexit Party , UKIP have said they will " drop the artificial target " of 50 per cent of people going to university . The party says it will waive the repayment of tuition fees in subjects " vital to our national life " , listing science , technology , engineering , mathematics and medicine in this category . The waiver would be dependent on graduates working in their fields in the UK during the repayment period . <p> SNP <p> The SNP has committed to maintaining free higher education for all Scottish students who choose to study in their home nation , with " no return to tuition fees " while it remains in power . Ministers spend ? 1bn on universities every year , but the nation 's public spending watchdog warned in September that more money was needed , highlighting a 12 per cent real terms funding cut over seven years . <p> DUP <p> Supports developing a long-term plan between government , industry and universities to increase research and development in the UK to three percent of GDP , and for the UK to be a partner of choice for international research collaborations . <p> Plaid Cymru <p> Promises to " address the long term brain drain of young people out of Wales " . To achieve this , it plans to provide a subsidy to students resident in Wales who study in Wales . The manifesto says those taking subjects " vital to the Welsh economy " will pay no tuition fees in Wales , mentioning healthcare , science , engineering and technology . The same applies to students from " particularly challenging backgrounds " . <p> Summary : Voters have a variety of choices , with most parties offering changes to university funding or student grants to some degree . Several parties are pledging to abolish tuition fees altogether , although this comes with a significant price tag . <p> Jo Swinson CBE , leader of the Liberal Democrats , speaks to a group of female students during a free event promoting careers in asset-management at the University of Strathclyde ( Photo : Getty ) <h> Further education and skills <p> Conservatives <p> The Tories are promising to create a new National Skills Fund worth ? 3bn over the next Parliament . Details about how it will work are vague , but the manifesto says it will provide " matching funding " for individuals and small firms to access training . The party has pledged almost ? 2bn to upgrade the college estate , and wants to launch 20 Institutes of Technology to deliver science and engineering training . <p> Labour <p> Labour has pledged to give every adult a free entitlement to six years of study for qualifications up to level six ( undergraduate degrees or equivalent vocational courses ) . The commitment would be part of a new ' National Education Service ' , to give people access to education from " cradle to grave " . The party would also bring back the Education Maintenance Allowance - grants for 16 to 19-year-olds who stay in education . <p> Liberal Democrats <p> Aims to introduce a new ' Skills Wallet ' for every adult in England . The system would involve the Government giving individuals ? 10,000 throughout their life , which could then be spent on approved education and training courses . The party has also said it will introduce a ' young people 's premium ' which would give sixth forms and FE colleges extra funding for every student from a poorer background . <p> Green Party <p> Will " revive " the FE sector by increasing the funding rate for 16 and 17-year olds , and introducing a " capital expansion fund " for sixth form providers . The party promises to increase funding for adult education across England and Wales , and would integrate this programme with its ' Green New Deal ' - a project to decarbonise the economy . It plans to spend ? 2bn re-skilling people . <p> Brexit Party <p> Wants to " scrap " the apprenticeship levy , which it describes as " cumbersome " and blames for a collapse in apprenticeship numbers . Under the levy , larger employers have to pay a tax to fund the development of apprenticeships . The party says it will " improve tax incentives for employers to take on genuine apprentices " . <p> UKIP <p> Technical and vocational providers would be included in a UKIP push for a range of different types of schools in each area . The party has said it supports " real trade apprenticeships and apprentice degree courses " . However , it would review the apprenticeship levy , which it says " has in some cases been over-complicated and counter-productive " . <p> SNP <p> Since 2007 the SNP-run Scottish Government has invested more than ? 7bn in colleges , with over ? 600m coming in the current financial year , and says more full-time students are completing their courses . However , in May the public spending watchdog warned that the financial challenges facing colleges were intensifying , with most forecasting deficits in the next five years . <p> DUP <p> It supports a UK wide enterprise strategy which harnesses the skills and experience of those in advanced manufacturing , engineering and technology . <p> Plaid Cymru <p> Says it will increase funding for degree apprentices in Wales . The party says it will increase the pay of college lecturers to ensure parity with school teachers in terms of base hourly pay . Public transport would be made free for all FE and sixth form students and apprentices in Wales below the age of 21 in full time education and training . <p> Summary : Further education has long been a neglected part of the education system , but in this election there are encouraging signs that the parties are taking it more seriously . Reskilling adults is likely to become more important .
