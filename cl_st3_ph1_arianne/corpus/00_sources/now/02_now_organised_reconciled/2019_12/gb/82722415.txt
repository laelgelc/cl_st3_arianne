<h> Tesco , WWF launch ' green ' food metric <h> Tesco has teamed up with The World Wide Fund for Nature to devise a measure they say will gauge the environmental impact " of some of the UK 's most popular foods " . <p> Own-label suppliers of foods including salad tomatoes , Bourbon biscuits and ready-salted crisps will see their products tracked against seven environmental criteria under the " sustainable basket metric " . <p> Tesco and WWF say their goal is " halving the impact of the average UK shopping basket " . They have set 2030 as " a potential ambition " for that target to be reached . <p> The benchmarks , which include deforestation , food waste and packaging waste , have been chosen due to what Tesco and WWF said is " their scope and global impact ; their irreversibility and urgency ; and their direct impact on issues such as climate change " . <p> The criteria have been weighted , with percentage scores totalling 100% . Climate change has been rated highest , at 25% , with deforestation following at 20% . <p> Each criteria will have sub-metrics . For instance , " sustainable agriculture " includes considerations such as the percentage of farmers " taking robust action to improve soil health " . <p> For each metric , Tesco and WWF have established both a 2018 baseline and a " long-term " target . <p> Outgoing Tesco CEO Dave Lewis said : " Throughout our partnership , we 'll be carrying out industry-leading work to make food production more sustainable , including sourcing commodities like soy and palm oil from verified zero-deforestation areas , and improving soil health and water usage on farms in the UK . " <p> The retailer plans to use the metric across more of its products , with the criteria applicable to " a wide range of products " not included in the basket . The criteria related to lettuce , such as sustainable agriculture and food waste " will also relate to other similar vegetables such as spinach , chard and other salad leaves " , Tesco said . <p> Tanya Syeele , the CEO of WWF in the UK , said the new system would allow Tesco " to fully understand the end-to-end sustainability impact of some of the most popular foods " . <p> She added : " We want other retailers to take a similar approach and come together to ensure a more sustainable approach to food production .
