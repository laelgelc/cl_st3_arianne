<h> See how the candidates answered your questions -- a round up of the Isle of Wight County Press and the Isle of Wight Radio Hustings <p> ISLE of Wight prospective parliamentary candidates faced critical questions and clashed at last night 's sold-out Hustings from the Isle of Wight County Press and the Isle of Wight Radio at Cowes Enterprise College . <p> Starting off with a hush and not a bang , Red Rebels from the Isle of Wight Extinction Rebellion silently protested as the 400-strong audience walked through the doors , holding banners protesting climate change . <p> Questions ranging from the NHS and Brexit , to education and childcare challenged the candidates as they tried to bring the audience on their sides . <p> In a round-up of the answers , you can see how the candidates fared against their critical competitors . <p> Conservative candidate , Bob Seely , former MP for the Isle of Wight , told the audience he was proud of what he has achieved for the Island -- including the additional capital for the NHS and investment in Island Line . <p> Mr Seely promised extra funding for protecting the homeless , schools and childcare and the Conservatives will get Brexit done with Islanders benefitting greatly . <p> He said : " Mental health services on the Island have never been good enough but the Isle of Wight NHS Trust is working on its inadequate rating . <p> " One of the most difficult things for the government to figure out how and where to fund the NHS . The funding is increasing -- with ? 139 billion this year -- it is about how to manage that . " <p> However , Mr Seely was concerned about how candidates were going to pay for all the extra funding they had proposed . <p> " There is a balance for paying for stuff and getting the money in , " he said . " We need to work out where the money comes from -- from personal taxation and business taxation . " <p> In a Green Party target seat , Vix Lowthion , is the second best chance for a Green MP in the UK who has promised real climate action and a second referendum for Brexit , following the ' shambles ' of the last three and a half years . <p> As a teacher , Ms Lowthion has seen how bad the cuts have been to the Island 's schools saying " we have withdrawn investment from our youth and we should be listening to them now . " <p> She also said mental health should be treated exactly the same as mental health , allocating an extra ? 6 billion funding . <p> " Brexit is not helping the NHS . We must support our NHS and support our staff . <p> " We are more likely to be treated by a migrant than be behind one in the queue . <p> " Do we trust Boris Johnson with the future of our NHS ? " <p> Independent candidate Karl Love , was keen to put the Island first -- making the Island work for its residents -- but the NHS needs to be saved . <p> " We can not keep saying this is about money -- it is about training a workforce to have quicker interventions . " <p> When it comes to Brexit , Mr Love promised to uphold the views of 62 per cent of the Island and leave the EU . He said : " If we like it or not the people of this Island voted to leave and I have to uphold it . " <p> Receiving one of the biggest cheers of the night , Labour candidate Richard Quigley appealed to the audience to vote for real change . <p> Mr Quigley argued with Mr Seely , saying there was no new funding going into the NHS and waits patients had faced were unacceptable . <p> He queried the ? 48 million promised to the hospital . <p> He explained he would pay for his promises by : " We do a little bit of taxation , a little bit of borrowing and then we invest for a return . " <p> But the Island will not benefit from Brexit , the only way left is a second referendum , he said : " I am sorry I can not give you a single benefit but I can give you reasons to stay . " <p> Independent Brexiteer candidate Daryll Pitcher was n't intending to stand on the Isle of Wight but due to the Brexit Party candidate being withdrawn felt he needed to represent the Island as the Conservatives will not get Brexit done . <p> He said : " It is very important we listen to the will of the people . <p> " Economically , in the long term , we will be free -- if you do n't live in a democratic country , you live in a prison . " <p> However , Mr Pitcher said apart from Brexit , he was making very few concrete promises he can not keep . <p> But one he would definitely promise was free travel for patients and carers to the mainland and training more health professionals on the Island . <p> He said : " What we are actually doing is stripping other countries of their highly trained people . " <p> Carl Feeney , a fixed link campaigner , said he is very disappointed with the MP the Island has had for the last few years and promised a ? 10 minimum wage with subsidised childcare for one to three year olds and further investment in the youth . <p> As the self-professed ' fixed link guy ' Mr Feeney said if we connected up , like the Isle of Skye , the NHS on the Island would benefit from extra funding with more specialists and consultants . <p> However one point all the candidates agreed on was the fact that no oil drilling should be done on the Isle of Wight -- with trying to protect water supplies , wildlife and tourism high on their agendas . <p> This website and associated newspapers adhere to the Independent Press Standards Organisation 's Editors ' Code of Practice .
