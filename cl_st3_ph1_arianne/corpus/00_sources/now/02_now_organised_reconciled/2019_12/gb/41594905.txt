<p> Since February , hundreds of school pupils have been taking to the streets of Bristol nearly every month to protest against climate change . <p> Started by teenage activist Greta Thunberg last year in Sweden , the school strike for climate movement is now international and has inspired pupils in cities across the world . <p> During the past year , hundreds of students in Bristol have gathered in huge numbers , blocked roads in the city centre and rallied against expansion at Bristol Airport , all to protest for a less polluted planet . <p> However , with many of the group 's demands so far unmet , the movement is now deciding how best to promote its message in 2020 . <p> The organisers of the enormous marches are teenagers themselves , juggling activism with schoolwork and sometimes part-time jobs . <p> A youth strike gets under way in College Green back in February <p> One of them is 17-year-old activist Heulwen Flower , who decided to get involved with the movement after seeing a lack of action addressing climate change from both local and national government . <p> " My generation has grown up talking about climate change so I just think it 's strange how nobody has taken proper action , " she said . <p> " It 's just scary . We 're only children and we have little power . We try to do things with the council and at a higher level but the marches are one of the main ways to get attention . " <p> With thousands of other students feeling the same way in Bristol , the movement has grown steadily in numbers as climate change and the need for immediate action becomes more of a pressing issue . <p> In particular , the UK School Climate Network has a number of demands , which include the government declaring a climate emergency and a reform of the national curriculum to address the ecological crisis . <p> Heulwen Flower , 17 , one of the organisers of the Youth Strike movement in Bristol ( Image : Heulwen Flower )
