<p> The WMO warns greenhouse gas levels have reached another record high ( PA ) <p> Levels of atmospheric greenhouse gases have reached another record high , " with no sign of a slowdown , let alone a decline " , the World Meteorological Organisation ( WMO ) has warned . <p> Despite pledges made under the Paris Agreement to combat climate change , concentrations of carbon dioxide , methane and nitrous oxide all surged by higher amounts in 2018 than average for the past decade . <p> Levels of CO2 -- the main gas driving global warming -- reached new highs of 407.8 parts per million ( ppm ) in 2018 , up from 405.5ppm the previous year , a report by the UN body shows . <h> Download the new Indpendent Premium app <p> Sharing the full story , not just the headlines <p> This concentration is <p> Human activity is increasing greenhouse gas emissions , which are driving up these gases ' concentration in the atmosphere , where they have a warming effect on the Earth 's climate . <p> Since 1990 , there has been a 43 per cent increase in this warming effect by long-lived greenhouse gases , the report said . <p> According to the report , CO2 contributes towards 66 per cent of this warming effect , with methane at 17 per cent , and nitrous oxide at six per cent . <p> In addition , global greenhouse gas emissions rose again in 2018 at the fastest rate in seven years , according to a preliminary report by the Global Carbon Project . <p> The resultant heating of the planet is driving impacts such as melting glaciers and sea level rise , more extreme droughts , heatwaves and storms , also posing a threat to food security , water supplies and wildlife . <p> Large swathes of North Africa and the Middle East would likely become uninhabitable due to scorching heat and drought , with sea level rises affecting many of the world 's most populous cities . <p> The WMO warned that despite agreements made under the Paris Agreement , global emissions are not estimated to peak by 2030 , let alone by the 2020 date that scientists have said is necessary to curb dangerous climate change . <p> " The findings ... point us in a clear direction -- in this critical period , the world must deliver concrete , stepped-up action on emissions , " said the UN Environment Programme 's executive director Inger Andersen . <p> " We face a stark choice : set in motion the radical transformations we need now , or face the consequences of a planet radically altered by climate change .
