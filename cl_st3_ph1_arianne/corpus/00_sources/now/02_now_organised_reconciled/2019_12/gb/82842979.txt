<p> Carbon-intensive firms are likely to lose 43% of their value thanks to policies designed to combat climate change , a report says . <p> Meanwhile the most progressive companies will see an uplift of 33% in their value . <p> The forecast was commissioned by the UN-backed Principles for Responsible Investment ( PRI ) . <p> Representatives of fossil fuel companies told the BBC they were already adapting their businesses to take climate change into account . <p> But the PRI study suggests major winners and losers will emerge between , and within , big sectors . <p> Car-makers with the swiftest transition to electric vehicles ( EVs ) , for instance , are projected to increase in value by 108% , according to the study by Vivid Economics . <p> Manufacturers slow to move to EVs will see their value fall , as governments realise that petrol and diesel models must be phased out faster for climate targets to be met . <p> Warning on coal <p> Meanwhile , the study predicts that the world 's largest listed coal companies could fall in value by 44% . And the 10 biggest firms in oil and gas could lose 31% of current value . <p> Electric utilities with the strongest strategy for renewables could see values increase by 104% , while laggards could see them fall by two-thirds . <p> Miners producing minerals critical for the transition may see a 54% upside , while those with the smallest share of " green minerals " will witness valuations almost halving . <p> Image copyrightGetty ImagesImage caption Car firms should switch to making electric vehicles quickly , said the study <p> Agricultural firms with high exposure to " sustainable " biofuels and non-beef protein sources could gain at least 10% of current value . <p> Those exposed to under-pressure sectors such as cattle may lose between 15% and 43% - depending on their links with deforestation . <p> The figures are inevitably speculative , and rely on an assumption that politicians will be forced to respond strongly to the growing climate crisis -- which , given current political progress , remains debatable . <p> Firms already under pressure <p> But they do echo the warnings issued by the Bank of England governor Mark Carney , who said firms ignoring the climate challenge would go bankrupt . <p> Already some insurance firms are refusing to offer cover to new coal-fired power stations because the risk of policy change is so great . <p> The giant AXA , for instance , says it will stop insuring any new coal construction projects , and totally phase out existing insurance and investments in coal in the EU , by 2030 . <p> Image copyrightGetty Images <p> Fiona Reynolds , CEO of the PRI , said : " This analysis underscores the extent to which markets are under-pricing climate transition risk . <p> " One in five of the world 's most valuable companies are impacted by at least 10% in either direction . <p> " While the market-level effects of an abrupt policy response to climate change may appear manageable , this masks a much more complex and significant story , with some huge winners and losers emerging between sectors and within them . <p> " We are calling on investors to get real on climate policy risk , and this robust modelling exercise and analysis will enable them to do that . " <p> Industries " already changing " <p> Mike Tholen , from industry body Oil and Gas UK said : " The ( oil and gas ) majors targeted in this report are actively reducing their carbon footprints , pursuing technologies including Carbon Capture and Storage and diversifying their businesses into a broader mix of renewable energy . <p> " Oil and gas remain an important part of the energy mix for decades to come , and will be used in an increasingly low carbon manner to meet global energy needs . " <p> This confident response will alarm scientists who were warning at the UN climate conference last week that emissions from oil and gas were growing strongly , as coal growth slows . <p> And a spokesperson for the Society of Motor Manufacturers and Traders said : " Vehicle manufacturers and suppliers are investing vast sums in ultra-low and zero emission vehicles to help meet the same environmental goals . <p> " Now we need the right conditions to encourage investment , innovation and a competitive market . <p> " This must include giga-scale battery production and electrified supply chains , massive skills and infrastructure investment and long-term incentives to help companies and consumers make the shift sustainably. "
