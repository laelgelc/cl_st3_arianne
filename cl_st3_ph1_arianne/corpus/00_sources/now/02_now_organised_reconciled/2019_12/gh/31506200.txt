<h> Prime News Ghana <h> Election results 2019 : Five big things on Boris Johnson 's to-do list <p> Date : 14 December 2019 <p> By Mutala Yakubu <p> Category : World <p> With a substantial majority in Parliament , Boris Johnson can push ahead with his plans , confident that MPs will back them . So , what 's in the prime minister 's in-tray as he returns to work in Downing Street ? <h> 1 . Get Brexit done ... and trade agreed <p> The PM has a series of promises to keep . <p> Firstly , to leave the EU on 31 January . <p> He now has the majority in Parliament to push the necessary legislation through , so there 's no reason to think that wo n't happen on schedule . <p> But Mr Johnson then has to negotiate an " ambitious " trade agreement with the EU extremely quickly . <p> The PM has to get it ratified before the end of the post-Brexit transition period on 31 December 2020 . This is the time between the UK leaving the EU with a deal and finally quitting it altogether under the terms of a new relationship . <p> He has guaranteed that the transition period will not be extended - creating a potential crunch point , in just over a year 's time . <p> This will be far more difficult to achieve , especially if Mr Johnson wants to diverge from the EU rules and regulations that the UK currently follows . <p> Finally , Mr Johnson has promised that after the transition period there will be no checks on goods moving within the UK between Great Britain and Northern Ireland . The problem is that 's exactly what his own withdrawal agreement creates . <p> Anyone hoping to hear no more about Brexit after the end of January is likely to be disappointed . <h> 2 . Meet growing demands on the NHS <p> In their manifesto , the Conservatives - like all the main parties - promised extra funding for the NHS . Among other things , this would mean tens of thousands of extra nurses and about 50 million more GP surgery appointments a year . <p> But many health leaders felt these pledges did not go nearly far enough to address a fundamental problem - demand for patient care is rising relentlessly , but NHS resources and staff levels just have n't kept up . <p> Closing that gap will need time and money , but to actually bring down waiting lists and modernise services will require still more . <p> Training enough new staff and getting them into clinical roles could take several years . So there is a lot for the PM to face up to . <p> Big decisions on funding and workforce planning will have to be made quickly to ensure adequate care is available in future years . <p> Then there is the immediate challenge of the next few months and a potentially very difficult winter for the NHS , with increased pressures because of flu and other winter ailments . <h> 3 . Tackle the social care crisis <p> Finding a solution to the crisis facing the care system that supports older and disabled people should be near the top of Boris Johnson 's to-do list . <p> He 's promised cross-party talks within 100 days , but it is worth noting that it is more than 1,000 days since the Conservatives first promised to fix this overstretched , underfunded , yet vital system . <p> This is after more than 20 years during which successive governments have failed to reform adult social care . <h> Number of people receiving long-term support from councils <p> England , 65 and over <p> Source : NHS Digital , between 2014 and 2015 there were changes to the methodology , meaning figures are not directly comparable . <p> The " clear plan " the prime minister promised in July has not materialised , but his guiding principle is that no-one will have to sell their home to pay for care . <p> There will be an extra ? 1bn a year for the next five years to prop up the current system . However , it will be shared with equally stretched children 's social care . <p> Experts warn it wo n't be enough to tackle the pressures families face right now . <h> 4 . Work to hit climate change targets <p> In December 2020 , the UK hosts a massively important global climate gathering in Glasgow - the UN COP26 meeting . <p> Campaign group Friends of the Earth says the UK will be " humiliated " if it hosts the summit without setting out how it will meet its own carbon targets in advance . <p> The UK 's advisory committee on climate change , made up of politicians , climate scientists and others , warns that tens of millions of homes must be insulated soon . <p> Other experts say large-scale road and airport building are not compatible with cutting emissions of carbon dioxide , which scientists say is the major cause of global heating . <p> They say this is also the case even if cars are fully electric . And they recommend that people should be helped to walk and cycle . <p> The Conservative manifesto had comparatively little to say on these issues . <p> The USA has said it wo n't have climate change even mentioned in any trade deal . Meanwhile the EU is putting a border tax on countries that do n't cut greenhouse gases . It will be very hard to please both . <p> Last , but not least , is the state of nature . In October 2019 , the Conservative government published a radical environment bill , setting firm targets for conservation - against the wish of the Treasury . <p> The bill failed to complete its passage through Parliament before the election was called . Will it survive and be reintroduced into Parliament in the new term ? <h> 5 . Transform the immigration system <p> There are three big questions hanging over the easy-to-repeat slogan of an " Australian-style points-based system " . This ranks migrant workers according to their skills and abilities . <p> First , can it really be introduced by the deadline the Conservatives have set of January 2021 ? <p> The scale of the policy , legal aspects and staffing challenges ahead to make this happen are enormous - and government is a graveyard of failed IT projects . <p> Secondly , can anyone be sure the final scheme will look like the one being promised ? <p> Nations the UK hopes to strike trade deals with may demand preferential access for their citizens . Specific sectors of the economy - such as social care - may be allowed to bring in as many workers as they can find to ease chronic staffing crises . <p> Finally , what will be its impact on net migration ? <p> Boris Johnson says it will fall - but it 's hard to see how he can deliver that . This is especially so as he 's also promising no cap on skilled workers , or in other sectors , like hotels and construction . <p> In other words - we simply do n't know what the government will deliver , what the system 's impact will be - and whether leaving the EU and implementing it will make a jot of difference to the overall numbers .
