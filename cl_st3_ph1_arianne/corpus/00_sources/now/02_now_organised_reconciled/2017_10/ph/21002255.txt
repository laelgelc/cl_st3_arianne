<h> Guagua 's fight against climate change <h> Guagua 's fight against climate change <p> RESIDENTS of Guagua in Pampanga refuse to bend to the forces of nature as government officials and non-government organizations have been waging a war against the negative effects of climate change on this town with seemingly good results . <p> What would you do if you live in a town that sinks at the rate of 1.7 to 8.3 centimeters yearly in an area that is virtually a catch basin for flood water draining in from the western border towns of the province ? <p> The town of Guagua is , literally and figuratively , stuck in a quagmire as it faces this dilemma each year . And with climate change causing ocean levels to rise by two millimeters yearly , the people of the town faces the difficulty of draining flood waters into Pampanga Bay which drains into Manila Bay . <p> Report of local subsidence recorded from North Harbor , Manila to Obando , Paombong and Hagonoy in Bulacan also include Guagua . It is among Pampanga 's densely populated areas , with a population of 111,199 since 2010 . The town 's population is expected to at least double by 2021 . <p> Guagua is also home to the biggest public market in Pampanga and a couple of schools and business establishments that contribute to water consumption . <p> It shares the fate of neighboring Sasmuan villages of five Batang I and II , Mabuanbuan , Sebitanan and Malusac , which are sinking . <p> But even without the subsidence , Guagua has been historically a flood-prone town . It is only a meter above sea level and is traversed by several creeks and tributaries during rainy days , which collect and convey floodwater to the Guagua River and into the Manila Bay . <p> Aggravated by the eruption of Mt . Floods in 2008 alone affected 113,485 persons from 22,697 families and flooding that typically lasted for three months was estimated to have affected P90 million worth of business . In 2007 , the destruction of crops was valued at P3.90 million , considered the worst in recent times . <p> Guagua Mayor Dante Torres had initiated the project of rehabilitating the mangrove vegetation of the town near its water channels . This is in the hope of curtailing back flow water from water channels that drain toward the sea and also to provide home for fish to thrive . <p> Historical data show that Guagua 's water channels that drain toward the Manila Bay were once home to lush mangrove forests . <p> " We started with a few kilometers and we hope to return every year to continue planting more saplings , " Torres said . <p> Torres added that mangrove forests will enable the development of small ecosystems for fish life .
