<p> Small farmers in India are struggling to maintain even a minimum standard of living and urgently need policy support . This was the crux of the evidence collected from 17 villages in nine states of India , published as a book , which was titled How do Small Farmers Fare ? Evidence from Village Studies , edited by Madhura Swaminathan and Sandipan Baksi . <p> It was released at the Indian Society of Agricultural Economics ( ISAE ) conference at Umiam by Yoginder Alagh , conference president , in the presence of Abhijit Sen , president , ISAE , and other conference participants . <p> Terming the book a timely one in the context of the agrarian distress that has dominated public and political discourse , Alagh said , " The work of the Foundation was in line with the tradition of village studies pioneered by the Indian Statistical Institute . " <p> Introducing the ongoing Project on Agrarian Relations in India ( PARI ) undertaken by the Foundation for Agrarian Studies ( FAS ) , Sen commended the comprehensive questionnaire for the village surveys and invited members of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ book . He suggested they look at available data under PARI , for further research and analysis . <p> Giving an overview of the book , Swaminathan said , " The essays in the book examine the socio-economic characteristics of small farmers in relation to other strata of the rural population . " <p> " There is evidence of low and inadequate incomes among small farmers , and also of inequalities within villages and across agro-ecological regions . It points to a crisis of small farming , as small farmer households are unable to generate incomes to maintain a minimum standard of living , " she added . <p> In India , small farmers make up nearly half the total area under cultivation . While they account for a substantial rural population , they operate in a capitalist market economy where a small section of the population controls the bulk of the means of production . <p> Published by Tulika Books in association with FAS , the book is the fifth in the Agrarian Studies Series edited by V K Ramachandran . It makes a case for urgent public policy @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as to ensure them a minimum standard of living . <p> The 12 chapters in this book cover a range of livelihood issues , including household incomes , labour use , crop productivity , costs and prices , fertiliser use , access to credit , education and amenities . <p> While the introductory chapter deals with the classical literature on small farmers , there is also a chapter on the impact of climate change on small farmers .
