<h> Columnists <h> Public health challenge <p> It 's high time the Government should focus on preventing the spread of vector-borne diseases <p> While we keep talking about our top fatal diseases , most of them lifestyle and pollution-related , and India 's escalating health vulnerability on the global scale , what we completely ignore is the epidemic that vector-borne diseases have become . Their resultant death toll has become a matter of textbook entry and since the victims are mostly from the lowest socio-economic strata , they are bunched under the disposable life index . The World Health Organistaion projections about India being the next malaria capital of the world by 2030 or the fact that the Aedes Aegyapti mosquito is creating havoc , manifesting itself through dengue , chikungunya , Japanese encephalitis and more stubborn variants almost three-fold in less than a decade , are of no concern . <p> Neither the loss of a sizeable part of our productive workforce , nor the desperation of a woman -- who let her dengue-effected child die simply because she could not afford a prolonged treatment -- are snapping us @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the 2,215 cases of dengue recorded , 1,177 affected people were residents of Delhi , the rest being patients from other states . The same report listed the total number of malaria and chikungunya till September 16 to 869 and 472 respectively . What has n't been quantified is that vector-borne diseases have arrived earlier this year and are predicted to become an annual crisis given the rapid climate change and increased temperatures . <p> Clearly , the Government needs to drop its knee-jerk reactive strategies of drumming up an information campaign only after the rains , readying hospitals at the 11th hour and taxing a healthcare system that is already stressed . The time to act is now , not waiting for people to fall sick but ensuring they do not in the first place . The axis of public health policy should change from a treatment-based protocol to an aggressively preventive one . The onus should shift from individual hygiene , clearing water pools or fumigation . <p> What 's needed is a multi-dimensional approach : An effective vector control programme through mosquito sterilisation or creating a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Much needed are a parasite surveillance programme , research into rapidly mutating strains that evade diagnostic screening , establishing a pattern of behaviour and occurrence and reporting as much cases as possible through a network of field workers . It is also important to assess the health status of vulnerable or exposed communities and address the gaps in care delivery systems . Healthcare budget nationally is yet to move out of the single digit zone of the GDP and public health allocation in states is even measlier . Increased public health spending would certainly reap bigger benefits and bring down healthcare costs . If Sri Lanka could become a zero-malaria country , we could tame our outbreaks too rather than being ambushed by them .
