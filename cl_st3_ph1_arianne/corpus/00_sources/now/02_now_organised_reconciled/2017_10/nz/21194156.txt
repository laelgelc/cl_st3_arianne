Or should we learn past lessons and say never again , not at any cost ? And if we do decide to go for it , how and where should it be done ? <p> These are questions Regenerate Christchurch is asking the people of Christchurch right now in its latest request for feedback on the future of the lands . <p> Evan Smith <p> An experimental home being built on the edge of the Dallington green zone by Resilient Projects @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ opportunity to trial options for resilient housing . <p> The Government bought the land from the previous owners because the land could n't be efficiently and economically remediated on a lot by lot basis , but there was always an option to explore land remediation on a larger scale and rebuild on it . That time has come . <p> But do we need more land for housing in Christchurch ? And do we need this land or is it better used to give the river room to flood and accommodate sea level rise ? To give it over to nature and recreation ? <p> After all , since the earthquakes we now have sufficient re-zoned greenfield sites to meet the demand for new housing for at least a generation . <p> It could be argued however that there is a need for quality affordable housing and a global demand for experimental housing that explores and pilots new ways of living with natural hazards -- both seismic and those associated with climate change . <p> There is a wide diversity of views on this -- for many of us who @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ lands can bring back strong emotions : horror , anger , despair . For many the experience of the earthquakes and the red zoning that followed is still very raw and filled with deep angst , hurt and bitterness . <p> But then a number that were red zoned would jump at the chance to live back in the river corridor again . <p> For those in the green zone alongside the red zone there is also a mix of feelings . They did n't have the option to get out , but had to endure the hell of EQC or insurance , repair or rebuild processes ( some are still caught in this ) . <p> Some now living in a remnant community want their whole community back again ; others are enjoying the green-blue amenity evolving over the road or the back fence hoping that one day soon their property will be highly sought after and marketable . <p> One thing is clear from the information currently released by Regenerate Christchurch -- not all the red zone land can be built on . About half lies within the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the river that is particularly vulnerable to lateral spread . <p> But a number of the possible combinations of land uses for the remaining land allow for residential development in some areas . Do we have the appetite for this ? <p> If we do decide to go down this path where and how should we rebuild ? <p> We need to consider the treatment of the interface between residential communities and any green space amenity . The red zone boundary went down the middle of streets and back fence lines . Straight lines feature significantly . These are n't the most appropriate or desirable urban edges in such a context . <p> There is an opportunity to revisit the edge and look at rezoning that enables higher density housing on the fringe that increases the number of residents with direct access to amenity and improves the market value of the land . <p> There are options to unstraighten some of the edges -- to soften or feather them with pockets that dovetail along the edge like a jigsaw . <p> If new housing is introduced , we need @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a ballot for first dibs on any new housing -- surely they are owed that much at least ? <p> And if new housing is considered in the red zone let 's not flog off chunks to developers to do bland new developments . Let 's be innovative . Let 's not sell any of the land but hold it in perpetuity in public trust and lease back any land for housing . <p> Managed retreat from land is an extremely emotive and difficult process as we have witnessed already with red zoning and are experiencing in flood prone areas right now . Once we have gone through that with an area , why put that land back into private ownership and risk having to go through it all again ? <p> So if we do make red zone land available for residential redevelopment then it should be on a leasehold basis only -- there are precedents for this in Christchurch , for example the church leasehold lands in Bishopdale and Halswell , it can be made to work . <p> Housing can be a lot more affordable if we @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ should also be under condition that all dwellings are relocatable and sustainable ; that land is not insured but the building is and there is relocation insurance ( that covers the cost of relocation and reconnection of the home if necessary in the future ) ; that the annual fee for lease includes the rates and any additional infrastructure costs required to service the area because of the nature of the land ; that the lease can be forfeited on a period of notice relating to irremediable hazard risk ; and that the home owner agrees to participate in global research in innovative resilient housing and urban design ( " laboracity " ) . <p> If we are going to take the opportunity to rebuild housing , let 's be smart about it for once . <p> Evan Smith is the programme manager for Eastern Vision , a group of community leaders who want to help communities create a big-picture plan for the whole of Christchurch 's east that everyone supports and that will still work in 100 years time .
