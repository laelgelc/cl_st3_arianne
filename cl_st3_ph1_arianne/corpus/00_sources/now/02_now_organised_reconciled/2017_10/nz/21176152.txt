<h> Former St Kevin 's boy off to Cambridge <p> Overseas bound . . . Former St Kevin 's College pupil Oliver Hailes , a Wellington-based barrister and solicitor , has been awarded a Woolf Fisher Scholarship to study at the University of Cambridge in the United Kingdom . PHOTO : SUPPLIED <p> A former Oamaru man will study at one of the world 's most prestigious universities after being awarded a top-level scholarship . <p> Oliver Hailes , a former St Kevin 's College pupil , was recently a recipient of the Woolf Fisher Scholarship to study at the University of Cambridge in England . <p> The scholarship , which covers study and living costs at the university , is estimated to have a value of $300,000 for each scholar and is considered one of the most generous scholarships available to New Zealand students . <p> For Mr Hailes ( 25 ) , who holds a bachelor of arts in politics and bachelor of law degree with first-class honours from the University of Otago , said the news he would get to study at Cambridge was overwhelming . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ out on the afternoon following the interview , so it was a stressful couple of hours , " he told the Oamaru Mail <p> " It took a while to sink in . I felt excitement and disbelief , I suppose , because it is really an enviable opportunity . " <p> The barrister and solicitor , presently a judge 's clerk in the Court of Appeal , will begin with corporate law firm Chapman Tripp next year before pursuing his studies . <p> In his first year at Cambridge , he will study towards a master of law focusing on international investment law , international environmental law , the economics of law and regulation and the law of the World Trade Organisation . <p> For the next three years , Mr Hailes will undertake a doctoral research programme to complete a PhD . <p> His interest lies in developing domestic and global legal structures that will allow governing institutions to tackle major economic , social and environmental issues facing the world . <p> He wants to break down institutional barriers that address social injustice and climate change . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ so it 's a little bit scary to think that 's where I 'll be , " he said of Cambridge . <p> " I 'm very excited about it , and I 'm in conversation with various alumni about selecting colleges and confirming the papers I 'll be taking in the first year , which is the masters year . " <p> He was thankful for the messages of congratulations he 'd received from the school and people in Oamaru.
