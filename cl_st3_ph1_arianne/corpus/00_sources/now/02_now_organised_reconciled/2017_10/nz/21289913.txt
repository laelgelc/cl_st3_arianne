<h> ' Worst case scenario ' picture painted for Central Otago in face of climate change over next century <p> JO MCKENZIE-MCLEAN <p> Last updated 14:45 , October 25 2017 <p> Heliview Flights <p> The snow-capped Pisa Range looking west , with The Snow Farm and Southern Hemisphere Proving Ground in the centre . By the end of the century Central Otago is predicted to have a considerable reduction in mountain snowpacks and resultant water storage and , in the worst case , there would be very little mountain snow cover remaining . <p> Central Otago is predicted to get hotter , have less snowfall and experience more significant rainfall events , according to a new scientific climate change report . <p> The Central Otago District Council engaged Alexandra-based atmospheric research company , Bodeker Scientific , to investigate the likely effects of climate change in the district over the next century for its strategic planning . <p> Council 's environmental engineering manager Peter Greenwood presented the report to council at its meeting on Wednesday and highlighted several major predicted changes caused by climate change over the next century . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ The district is going to get warmer by several degrees until the end of the next century . Total rainfall is not projected to change , however distribution and intensity is likely to change so there is a greater likelihood of significant events . The other major thing is there will be less snowfall on the mountains around Central Otago . " <p> Councillor Stephen Jeffery said the report highlighted the pressures " coming at " the region , particularly given problems with water storage . <p> Councillor Barrie Wills said if the predictions were " true " consideration should be given to restricting development on hillsides given the threat of landslides . <p> " We have our eye on it but we are more about informing than regulation . " <p> Deputy mayor Neil Gillespie stressed the report was " not an oracle " of climate change for Central Otago , but a planning tool for the council to develop its 30-year Infrastructure Strategy and 10-year Long Term Plan . <p> " It would be wrong to say this is going to happen . They have to take a stab at the implications . I think different industries will be differently equipped and qualified to assess what the implications are . Around 10-64 more summer days per annum ( daily maximum temperature above 25degC ) - about double the current number depending on location . <p> - An increase in the highest annual daily maximum temperatures of 3.7-5.8degC . <p> - Summers in Central Otago will get warmer with the highest maximum temperatures increasing between 4 and 5.8degC , depending on location , and the lowest minimum temperatures increasing between 1.5 and 2degC , depending on location . <p> -
