<h> Low-lying Waikato towns at risk from sea level rise - report <p> Damaging severe weather events are predicted to become more frequent and more intense . <p> Low-lying small towns in the Hauraki and Thames-Coromandel districts are at risk from rising sea levels , a government draft climate change report shows . <p> The " risk census " undertaken by NIWA for the Parliamentary Commissioner for the Environment showed homes and public assets would be at risk in low-lying areas . <p> This includes Ngatea , a small service town of 1300 with low-lying assets valued at $175 million . The entire community is less than 50cm above the mean high water springs . <p> The data shows there are many more communities at risk throughout the district . Thames , which has 300 buildings lower than 50cm above sea level , has $400m of at-risk assets , Whitianga has $170m , and Tairua has $50m . <p> The report shows there are more than $19 billion worth of buildings at-risk nationwide , in areas where 130,000 people live . <p> Thames Coromandel District Council is currently developing coastal @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ district this month . <p> Thames man Denis Tegg has been researching the issue for six months , and will make a presentation to the Thames Community Board on October 16 . <p> Tegg believes there 's not enough being done by councils to prepare for rising sea levels and councils would need financial help from central government . <p> " What I think 's lacking is a sense of urgency and leadership . Other councils and regional councils have combined to do the full hazard mapping , " he said . <p> " The science is saying the rate is accelerating .
