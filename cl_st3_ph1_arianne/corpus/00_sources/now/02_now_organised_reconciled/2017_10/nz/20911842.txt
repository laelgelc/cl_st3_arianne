<h> Businesses ready to move forward on climate change <p> PATTRICK SMELLIE <p> Last updated 05:00 , October 5 2017 <p> OMER MESSINGER/GETTY IMAGES <p> When it comes to climate change and party politics , the future is all about choices . <p> OPINION : One of the great ironies of the National and Green parties being unable to discuss forming a government together is the consensus emerging from corporate New Zealand about the importance of a cross-parliamentary consensus on long term climate change policy . <p> That consensus is apparent in many of the submissions published this week by the Productivity Commission in response to its investigation into New Zealand becoming a low carbon emission economy . <p> Take this , from Contact Energy : " New Zealand 's response to climate change must be depoliticised . Decisions around how New Zealand responds to climate change , and how we move to a lower carbon-emissions economy , can not be at the mercy of our three-yearly election cycle . " <p> Who ordered the Productivity Commission investigation ? A National Party-led government . The same government that issued more @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ previous government in the RMA 's 27-year history to get belated national guidance on major environmental challenges . <p> That 's the same government that achieved more progress to establish national standards for freshwater management than any of its predecessors . <p> Unfortunately , it is also the same National-led government that has dragged its feet on climate change action at home , that has governed through a spike in river-polluting agricultural intensification , and has been as keen on oil and gas exploration as it has been on an electricity market producing world-beating levels of renewable generation . <p> Such are the ambiguities arising from any society that seeks both to improve the economic lot of its citizens while being part of the first generation to acknowledge , let alone try and grapple , with climate change . <p> Former Green MP Nandor Tanczos blogged on this problem , observing that while a government involving National and the Greens would be " politically suicidal " today , " at some stage in the future we must be prepared to seriously consider the idea " . <p> First , however @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ need to position itself like NZ First , between the two main parties of government , rather than as an appendage without leverage , to the left of the Labour Party . <p> Meanwhile , National 's efforts on environmental issues are overwhelmed by its need to pander to the most conservative elements of its farming and corporate support bases . <p> However , there are some hopeful signals for a multi-party consensus to emerge on climate change action , irrespective of who forms the next government . <p> While the Greens and NZ First both reject the emissions trading scheme that National and Labour support , both minor parties are in violent agreement that climate change is a number one policy priority . That 's progress . <p> So , too , is the work of the Globe cross-parliamentary group , representing all political parties in th last Parliament .
