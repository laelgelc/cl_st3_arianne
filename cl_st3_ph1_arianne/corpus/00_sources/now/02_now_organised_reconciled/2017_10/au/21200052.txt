<h> Bringing back an old idea for smart cities -- playing on the street <p> Analysis for The Conversation by Troy Innocent , City of Melbourne Knowledge Fellow 2017-18 , Senior Lecturer in Games and Interactivity , Swinburne University of Technology <p> Smart cities promise efficiency and resilience in urban design to combat climate change , population growth , transport congestion and other wicked problems . The processes that run a city may be abstracted into algorithms that feed on big data , their design optimised for efficiency , commuting , work and other purposes . <p> This is a positive development for urban spaces , but does this approach overlook other human needs ? <p> Everyone knows what play is , but as adults we often forget how to play or trivialise its significance . Play is a particular way of being , a different way of seeing the world . <p> The idea of play as pointless fun is challenged by the designers of serious games who remind us that it is fundamental to learning . Play activates cities and engages people . It appropriates and takes over urban space , changing what it means to people . <p> Playful citizens can then see their town or city in a new way , feeling a new sense of connection , and sensing new ways it could be . <h> Not entirely a new idea <p> While children used to play routinely in cities before the age of digital distraction , adult play in cities is not entirely a new idea . The emergence of the modern city in the 1900s led to changes in how people engaged with and experienced public space in the form of promenading and other social play . <p> More recently , the interventions into public space of playable cities have revitalised earlier strategies for urban play . These are things that are harder to achieve with other design strategies in public spaces . <h> A new creative platform <p> Playable cities appropriate smart cities infrastructure to engage people with their city and with one another . It 's a form of " hacking " urban space . Through a shared sense of community and ownership , visitors , residents and workers in playable cities may then become part of the conversation about urban design . <p> In this way , smart cities and playable cities may inform each other and provide a new creative platform for artists and designers . Developing encounters in a playable city blends disciplines such as urban design , public art and game development into a hybrid practice situated in urban space . <p> Technologies like augmented reality sit alongside live theatre , as players move between looking at the screen of their mobile to one another , observing the spaces of the city , or drifting through information architecture . <h> Invitations to be citizens of play <p> This approach recognises the multilayered nature of urban space @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ remapped into a playful experience .
