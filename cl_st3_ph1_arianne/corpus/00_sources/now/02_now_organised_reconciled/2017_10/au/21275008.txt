<p> If seeing is believing , then Donald Trump should take a trip to Miami . <p> A few weeks back I took a boat ride around Miami Beach with Dan Kipnis , a retired boat captain and Miami local of five decades . The shoreline is littered with multi-million dollar homes , huge multiplexes in the works , dozens of cranes lurking in the distance . <p> You would have no idea , but this is one of the most vulnerable places on earth . <p> I was in the south Floridian city for SBS Dateline , to report on populations in American whose communities are , right now , under threat from rising sea levels . <p> When you think climate change you do n't immediately think Miami , right ? Climate change is often seen as a threat to small coastal villages in the Arctic . But Miami is not a small coastal fishing village , it 's one of the largest cities in America and to put it bluntly ; it 's sinking . Slowly but surely . <p> Walking around Miami , it was hard @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , the skyscrapers in the distance , the cars zipping about the roads , might no longer be there 100 years from now . For me though , the question is what will happen to all the people ? What will happen to the millions of people who call Miami home ? Where will they go ? <p> I was with Dan Kipnis for a reason ; he 's already got an answer to that question . His double story brick family home with its double garage and its in-ground pool , a home he helped build , is now on the market . Dan is selling his house and leaving Miami because of climate change . It sounds like a drastic move right ? To leave the life you know because of what may or may not happen in the future . But he 's adamant that he does n't want to hang around and see what becomes of his city -- or the price of his home -- in the next few decades . Lately , after natural disasters , there 's always discussion about how much climate change is to blame . Geology Professor Harold Wanless from the University of Miami says human-induced climate change does n't cause hurricanes , it exacerbates them . So it did n't come as a huge surprise to him that Irma was the most powerful Atlantic Ocean storm in recorded history . <p> It may be alarmist to blame Hurricane Irma squarely on climate change , but it 's perfectly within reason to anticipate an Irma 2.0 to hit sooner and harder than expected . It 's not out of the question to expect Category 6 storms to become a reality ( Irma was a Category 5 ) . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . They live with the realities of rising sea levels every day .
