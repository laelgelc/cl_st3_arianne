<h> Govt may back away from clean energy target <p> The Turnbull government looks to be backing away from plans for a Clean Energy Target . <p> Energy Minister Josh Frydenberg has addressed a national energy summit in Sydney , and indicated the energy target might not be needed after all . <p> He said the costs of wind and solar energy are falling faster than expected and this may mean an earlier end to renewable energy subsidies . <p> ' It is against this backdrop of a declining cost curve for renewables and storage , greater efficiencies that can be found in thermal generation and the need for sufficient dispatchable power in the system that we are considering the Finkel Review 's 50th recommendation to which we 'll respond before the end of the year , ' Mr Frydenberg said . <p> The energy minister says the government 's emphasis is on electricity reliability , arguing reducing emissions can not come at the expense of reliability and affordability . <p> He made the case that it 's possible to put downward pressure on prices while still meeting international emissions @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ either reliability or affordability would mean diminished public support for tackling climate change . <p> ' This is in nobody 's interest , ' he said . <p> Prime Minister Malcolm Turnbull told reporters that his government is putting together an energy policy based on engineering and economics . <p> ' What we are determined to do is to ensure energy is reliable , affordable , and that we meet our emission reduction commitments that we 've made through the Paris Agreement , ' Mr Turnbull said on Monday . <p> ' We 've had a lot of failures in energy policy in days gone by and you 've seen this with energy policy being driven by politics and ideology , in some cases as much idiocy as ideology .
