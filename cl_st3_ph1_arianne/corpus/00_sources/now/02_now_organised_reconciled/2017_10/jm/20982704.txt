<h> Caribbean ' vulnerable ' to climate change <p> The effects of climate change on the Caribbean was high on the agenda as the Region 's Agriculture Ministers met at the CARICOM Secretariat in Georgetown , Guyana on Friday . <p> Speaking at the opening of the 71st Special Meeting of the Council for Trade and Economic Development ( COTED ) , Chairman Soeresh Algoe , Agriculture Minister of Suriname , Noel Holder , Minister of Agriculture of Guyana , and Ambassador Irwin LaRocque , CARICOM Secretary-General , discussed the responses necessary to confront climate change . <p> The region was recently devastated by the impact of two category five hurricanes . <p> Hurricane Irma devastated Barbuda , the British Virgin Islands , the Turks and Caicos Islands , Anguilla , St. Maarten , and St. Barts ; and significantly damaged The Bahamas and Haiti , and affected St. Kitts and Nevis . <p> Hurricane Maria left massive destruction in Dominica , and left the agriculture sectors in Barbuda and Dominica decimated . <p> COTED Chairman Algoe noted that the ferocity and frequency of storms are as a result of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> He took the opportunity to express his deepest sympathy to the affected nations and called on the Region to unite and assist " with any means and where possible " . <p> " The climate is changing drastically and we , as CARICOM Member States , are most vulnerable . In the same sense , climate change has also detrimental and adverse effects on the agriculture sector of our respective economies " , he said . <p> Minister Algoe acknowledged the role of COTED in providing the opportunity for dialogue , particularly on the challenges affecting the agriculture sector . <p> " It has ... been recognised as a facility to place agriculture and rural life in a prominent position , with which we can better acknowledge the strategic importance of the sector to economic , social and environmental stability , " he said .
