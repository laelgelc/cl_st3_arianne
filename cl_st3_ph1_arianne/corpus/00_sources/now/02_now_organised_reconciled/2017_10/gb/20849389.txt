<h> Mining history of iconic cliff-scape at Perranporth to be studied for the first time <p> Experts are studying the little-known mining history of the vulnerable cliffs overlooking the beach at Perranporth to show if the search for tin and copper in the area began in medieval or even prehistoric times . <p> Thousands of visitors enjoy the town 's beach , but most do n't realise that many of the caves in the cliffs are man-made . The coast was used for mining rather than leisure in the past , with the solid rock being tunnelled through by miners . Evidence of mining along the coast has eroded . <p> This is the first time archaeologists , historians and geologists have worked together to survey the coast to analyse when and how it was mined . They hope to discover the number of mines and their age , as well as more information about the recent history of the mines <p> The landscape is under threat because of erosion caused by the increased severity of winter storms . University students from both the Exeter and Penryn campuses ran a public survey to gather data on people 's perceptions of the cliff-scape , their awareness of the mining history and how they use the beach .
