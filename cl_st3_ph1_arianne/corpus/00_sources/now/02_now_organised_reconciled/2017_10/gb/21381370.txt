<h> Language as an enabling factor in climate policy awareness <p> Monday 30th October 2017 1:15 <p> The use of local languages for climate change awareness is essentially fundamental in development work . Many non-governmental organisations , government and implementing partners alike always find their development programmes failing , simply because they would want to sound sophisticated and knowledgeable to the detriment of achieving results . <p> By Peter Makwanya <p> Our local communities can not cope up with technically-related discourse of climate science , yet they are expected to be important stakeholders in this case . <p> For climate change awareness and adaptation to succeed , the language of local people becomes critical , therefore , it needs to be carefully harnessed and utilised for effective climate change mainstreaming activities . <p> Due to the living fact that local communities are quite diverse , ethnically and religiously , there is need to reach out to them in the medium they understand better . African communities are not a homogenous group like their European counterparts , but they are linguistically and culturally diverse . <p> As such , a linguistically-gifted @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ people feel wanted , accommodated and accepted as well as feeling at home . In the development literature crafted for them to implement , there is need for it to be harmonised in terms of being sustainably user-friendly . <p> Cross-cultural linguistic competence is the missing link as we speak . Missing link in attempts to integrate locals into meaningful development work . This competence is critical for successful communication to take place between the development practitioners and often-despised locals . <p> Climate science discourse is not even a favourite with the educated laypersons , who can hardly interpret it , what more , the downtrodden and vulnerable local people who face the challenges of hunger , discrimination , neglect and illiteracy . <p> The local person fails to decipher the English-written technical discourse , as a result development work and adaptation initiatives suffer . <p> If language use consists of the expression and communication of thoughts , then surely the mindset of the local communities is already affected .
