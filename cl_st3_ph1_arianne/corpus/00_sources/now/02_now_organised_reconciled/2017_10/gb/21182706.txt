<p> THE world 's first floating wind farm has started to supply electricity to the grid after being officially opened by Scotland 's First Minister . <p> Nicola Sturgeon was flown over the Hywind development , where giant turbines around 15 miles ( 25km ) off the shore of Peterhead , Aberdeenshire , could provide sufficient power for more than 20,000 homes . <p> The project has been developed by Norwegian state energy company Statoil , with floating turbines installed on about 2.5 square miles ( four sq km ) of water in the North Sea , where the average wind speed is about 10 metres per second . <p> Loading article content <p> The Hywind project features five turbines each 253 metres tall , with 78 metres submerged under the sea and anchored to the sea bed by cables . Each turbine is more than three times the height of New York 's Statue of Liberty . <p> " Hywind will provide clean energy to over 20,000 and will help us meet our ambitious climate change targets , " Sturgeon said .
