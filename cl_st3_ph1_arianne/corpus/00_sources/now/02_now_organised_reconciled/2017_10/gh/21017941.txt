<p> Professor Hans Adu-Dapaah , a former Director of the Crops Research Institute of the Council for Scientific and Industrial Research ( CRI-CSIR ) , has called for open , candid and informed debate on genetically modified organisms ( GMOs ) . <p> This , he indicated , was vital because whichever way the nation opted to go -- its acceptance or rejection , there would be implications for its food security in the years to come . <p> He was delivering the " 20th Pearson-Osae Appreciation Lecture " organized in Kumasi by the 1977 year alumni of the Prempeh College . <p> " Accepting or rejecting genetically modified foods ( GMOs ) , implications for food security " , was the topic chosen for the event . <p> It climaxed a four-day homecoming summit by the group to celebrate its 40th anniversary , which provided the opportunity to reflect on the past and discuss the way forward . <p> Prof Adu-Dapaah said with Ghana 's population expected to hit close to 52 million within the next 50 years , there was going to be a need to triple food @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ would likely lead to the rise in food demand and therefore it was necessary for the adoption of improved technologies like genetic engineering in food production . <p> He said although GMOs might have a downside , it could be essential for food security , if safely harnessed . <p> Prof Adu-Dapaah , himself an old student , pointed to the decline in soil fertility , climate change - heat and drought , pests and diseases , post-harvest loses and low crop yield , threatening the nation 's food security and said the push towards the GMOs could be in the right direction . <p> The GMOs , he noted , had so far passed global safety assessments and not likely to harm human and animal health . <p> " GMOs have proven to be resistant to pest , diseases , and heavy metals . They are drought , herbicides , and salinity tolerant , increase yields , improve family incomes , has better taste and without allergies , and digestible in the human digestive system " , he added . <p> He urged the government to resource and strengthen @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ laws and policies so that standards set for GMO procedures were upheld . <p> There was also the need for strong public-private partnership and capacity building of the key stakeholders including the Ghana Standards Authority ( GSA ) , Customs Division of the Ghana Revenue Authority ( GRA ) , Food and Drugs Authority ( FDA ) and the Environmental Protection Agency ( EPA ) for safe development , transfer and handling of GMOs . <p> Mr. Kwabena F.
