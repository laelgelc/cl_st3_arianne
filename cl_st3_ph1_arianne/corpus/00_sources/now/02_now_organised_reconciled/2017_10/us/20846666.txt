<p> Scientists have long known that animals ' farts and poop contribute to global warming but findings of a new study have suggested that researchers may have underestimated the impact of methane emissions from livestock on climate change . <p> Methane , a principal component of farts , is a natural byproduct of digestion produced when microbes in the animal 's gut breakdown and ferment food . <p> Methane is a gas known to contribute to the greenhouse effect , which traps the heat within the Earth 's atmosphere and contribute to climate change . While carbon dioxide is often blamed for global warming , methane is actually 85 times more potent when it come to trapping heat . <p> Sources of methane are varied . Some come from natural sources such as marshes and other wetlands but a bulk of methane emission comes from human activities that include cattle operations . <p> " We update information for cattle and swine by region , based on reported recent changes in animal body mass , feed quality and quantity , milk productivity , and management of animals and manure , " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ use this updated information to calculate new livestock methane emissions factors for enteric fermentation in cattle , and for manure management in cattle and swine . Taking into account the changes in the way humans use and keep livestock , they found that previous estimates that served as basis for the 2006 Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change , were off by 11 percent . <p> USDA plant physiologist Julie Wolf and colleagues reevaluated the data that were used to calculate the IPCC 2006 methane emissions estimates . These estimates were based on relatively modest rates of increase in methane emission from 2000 to 2006 but things dramatically changed after this period as methane emission increased by 10-fold over the next decade . <p> " In many regions of the world , livestock numbers are changing , and breeding has resulted in larger animals with higher intakes of food , " Wolf said .
