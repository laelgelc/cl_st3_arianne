They like to hide under rocks , fallen trees or some other locations where they can feel safe and protected . Here in America we have more kinds of salamanders than all the rest of the world put together . We are indeed the salamander capital of the world . <p> They also vary in size from a little over an inch in length to huge ones up to four feet long . <p> Salamanders seem to have a certain habitat that they must have to live . This is moisture . Lack of this @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ one of the reasons they hide under damp rocks or fallen trees and usually come out at night or rainy days . <p> Indiana , as does the rest of the eastern United States , has more than its share of these interesting amphibians , which some people mistake for lizards . Hoosierland has , or should have , 25 species of salamanders in various sections across our state . <p> Some may have been overlooked and yet to be authenticated in Indiana , but they may be just waiting to be discovered . Such is the case with the green salamander , only listed as being found in our state a few years ago . Other examples are the spring salamander , northern red , midland mud , mole and spotted dusky salamanders . <p> Another , the three-toed amphiuma , due to climate change , may possibly extend its range north into southwestern Indiana . This is one of the water-loving amphibians we call the large salamanders . The others that do already live in Hoosierland are the eastern hellbender , lesser siren and mudpuppy .
