<p> A trader turns off his truck 's engine at yet another checkpoint and , again , the soldiers ask him for cash . His truck is filled with charcoal from Myanmar , destined for factories in China 's mountainous southwest province of Yunnan . <p> The charcoal will be fed into furnaces to produce silicon metal , a material so versatile it is used to manufacture everything from metal alloys to silicon chips and solar panels . In 1885 , the English , who had already colonized Burma 's coast , set their eyes on the precious teak of the hinterland . They sent boats @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and snatched control of the country within a couple of weeks . The king and his court were sent downstream and then into exile in India . <p> In the following decades , the Irrawaddy would prove its worth to the British Empire . Teak 's strength and water resistance provided railway sleepers and ships ' hulls , bringing considerable income to the British . The teak logs that were felled in remote forests could be exported at unprecedented speed by floating them down from forest camps , to sawmills and then abroad . After Burma 's independence in 1948 , teak remained one of the country 's main sources of income and the Irrawaddy one of the main routes for its trade . <p> Now , charcoal takes the reverse route , up the Irrawaddy , to join the stream of natural resources flowing from Myanmar to China , which in turn nourishes the global demand for consumer goods . <p> Charcoal bags stored in a lot in Bhamo , Myanmar .
