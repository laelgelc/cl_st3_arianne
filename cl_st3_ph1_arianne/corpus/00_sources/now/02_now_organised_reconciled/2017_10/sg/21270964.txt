<h> Forest fires contributed to record global tree cover loss <p> A firefighter observes the flames while trying to extinguish a fire in Cabanoes near Louzan as wildfires rage in Portugal in October 2017 <p> A sharp increase in forest fires stoked record losses in global forest cover equivalent to the area of New Zealand in 2016 , a Global Forest Watch report said Monday . <p> The alarming pace of destruction -- 51 percent higher than the prior year with a loss of 73.4 million acres ( 29.7 million hectares ) , according to data from the University of Maryland -- was partially due to climate change that has increased the risks and intensity of wildfires by triggering temperature rise and drought in some places , the monitor said . <p> The 2015-2016 weather phenomenon El Nino , one of the strongest on record , also played a role , having created particularly dry conditions in the tropics . <p> Many of those tropical areas are not naturally prone to catching fire -- but vulnerability increased due to poor management and was exacerbated by El Nino . <p> Deadly @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the loss . This year , deadly blazes have again devastated regions of Portugal as well as California . <p> Brazil 's Amazon region lost 9.1 million acres of tree cover -- more than three times that of 2015 . <p> And Portugal saw some four percent of its forests go up in smoke in 2016 , the highest proportion of any other country . <p> Nearly half of all forests burned in the European Union in 2016 were in Portugal , where fire-prone eucalyptus and pine plantations along with poor soil encouraged the deadly flames . <p> The country is set to break the record for destroyed forests in 2017 , with recent disasters killing dozens of people . <p> Early 2016 saw one of the largest fires ever recorded in Central Africa , destroying 37,000 acres of forest in the Republic of Congo .
