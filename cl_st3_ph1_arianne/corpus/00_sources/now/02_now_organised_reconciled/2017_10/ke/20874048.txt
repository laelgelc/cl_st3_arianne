<h> ENVIRONMENT : World 's botanical gardens safeguard endangered plants <h> Botanical gardens are a Noah 's Ark for endangered plants , collectively safeguarding four out of 10 species known to face extinction <p> Botanical gardens are a Noah 's Ark for endangered plants , collectively safeguarding four out of 10 species known to face extinction . But the otherwise reassuring number hides a serious imbalance , researchers reported in the science journal Nature Plants , last week . <p> Such gardens , which often double as research centres , house only a quarter of tropical flora , as opposed to 60 per cent of plants native to temperate climes . <p> Species diversity is far richer in the tropics , but more than 90 per cent of vegetal safe havens are in the northern hemisphere . <p> Plants are essential for life on earth . They play a crucial role in regulating the climate , maintaining soil fertility , and the purification of water and air . They also provide food , medicines , building materials and fuel to sustain human life . And yet more than a fifth @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ research has shown . The biggest threat is humanity 's expanding " footprint " , already encroaching on three-quarters of all land area -- 40 per cent of which has been given over to agriculture . <p> " The global network of botanical gardens is our best hope for saving some of the world 's most endangered plants , " said senior author Samuel Brockington , a researcher at the University of Cambridge in England . <p> " If we fail to conserve our plant diversity , humanity will struggle to solve the global challenges of food and fuel security , environmental degradation , and climate change , " he added . <p> To find out what percentage of plant species are housed in captivity , Brockington and colleagues compared databases cataloguing all known flora to those held by a third of the world 's 3,269 botanical gardens . More than a third of Earth 's 350,699 known plant species are to be found in at least one such institution , the scientists reported . They also revealed that earth 's most ancient living flora , so-called non-vascular plants such as mosses and some algae , have been overlooked and neglected , comprising less than five per cent of species under care . " Non-vascular species are the living representations of the first plants to colonise land , " Brockington said . " They are essential for understanding the evolution of plants . " <p> Scientists have so far assessed the threat status of about 26 per cent of the world 's known plant species .
