<p> Limiting the population of white-tail deer and moose can help increase the population of caribou , research out of the University of Alberta says . <p> " It 's a complicated issue . Across North America , you have habitat change , like logging and oil and gas activities , and climate change that leads to more moose and deer ... when you have more of those critters , you have more predators . The caribou become incidental take , like bycatch , " said Robert Serrouya , a postdoctoral fellow in biological sciences . <p> The researchers found that reducing these numbers needs to be a slow and gradual process . Removing them all at once could have the opposite effect and lead to long-lasting impacts on the environment . <p> The researchers drew upon some theoretical predator-prey models , but they also had two field case studies to test this hypothesis , which is rare for wildlife management research , he said . <p> In one of these cases , a particularly cold winter in 1997 killed around 80 per cent of the invasive deer in the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ them as a replacement . <p> However , in the other case in 2003 , when the moose population north of Revelstoke , B.C. , was reduced slowly using management techniques over 10 years , it stabilized the caribou population . The gradual reduction of moose caused the wolf population to decrease as well , rather than leaving a large population of hungry predators in the area to take down the ailing ungulates . <p> " That 's kind of a rare success story for caribou in Canada , " he said . <p> These population reduction strategies , in some cases , involve drugging and physically moving the competing herbivores using helicopters . <p> The caribou situation in Canada is dire . There are , Serrouya said , 65 herds of the animal in Western Canada , and less than 10 of them are stable or increasing .
