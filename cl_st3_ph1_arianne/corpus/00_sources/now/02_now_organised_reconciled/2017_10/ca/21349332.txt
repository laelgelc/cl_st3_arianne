<h> Topics <p> Regular readers of this column know about the trouble that invasive , non-native plant species cause . Phragmites or common weed is choking out native plant and bird species in wetlands . Buckthorn that gets a toehold in meadowlands has similar outcomes . Norway maple and European mountain ash are other non-native plants . <p> The truth is , an invasive species can be described as any non-native species that poses a threat to a local ecosystem . It should n't surprise us that there are some birds that are invasive species themselves . These are birds that out-compete native bird species for food and nesting areas . <p> Many of our invasive bird species are now so entrenched that many do n't even realize they are non-native . The house sparrow is one of these . This bird is from Eurasia but it has become the most widely distributed bird on the planet . <p> " So what ? " many would ask . Apart perhaps from seeing too many of them at a bird feeder , what is the problem ? Starlings will also outmuscle native cavity-nesting species for territory . As its name suggests , the European starling was brought to North America from Europe and it adapted with too much success through the 1800s . <p> Interestingly , the online Global Invasive Species Database that includes birds , other fauna , and plants identifies the starling -- or Sturnus vulgaris -- as among the 100 worst invasive alien species on the planet . They note that starlings " contribute to the decline of local native bird species through competition for resources and nesting spaces . " <p> Unlike the house sparrow and starling , the mute swan 's beauty conjures up romantic notions . It too is an invasive species however . " <p> Mute swans can be extremely aggressive with respect to food , territory , and protecting cygnets . Their impact on local ecosystems is significant and negative . <p> A mute swan entry on the Environment and Climate Change Canada website says of this non-native , invasive species " unless control measures are expanded , these trends will likely continue until the population occupies all available habitat . Increased numbers and distribution will increase the risks that this species poses to Canada 's native wildlife , wetland habitats and people . " <p> The pigeon is yet another invasive species that has adapted very well . It is interesting to note that many invasive bird species have adapted so successfully to new environments that many do n't even migrate . <p> Unfortunately we have n't seen the last of invasive bird species .
