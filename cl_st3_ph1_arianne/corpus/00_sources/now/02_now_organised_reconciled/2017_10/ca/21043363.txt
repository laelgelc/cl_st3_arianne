<p> A huge hole has opened up in Antarctica and , well , scientists are n't really sure what it 's doing there . <p> The discovery is said to be as large as the state of Maine , notes Motherboard , and bigger than the Netherlands . In September it was 60,000 square kilometers ( 23,000 square miles ) in size , but at its biggest , it was 80,000 square kilometers ( 31,000 square miles ) . <p> This opening is called a polynya , and this particular hole is the largest seen in the Weddell Sea since the 1970s , when it was five times larger . It was found by the Southern Ocean Carbon and Climate Observations and Modelling ( SOCCOM ) group at Princeton University in Washington . <p> The region is essentially ice-free and may be the result of natural climate changes . However , its formation is not really understood . Actually mounting expeditions here is tough , too , but scientists hope that robotic floats ( small submersible objects , like mini-submarines ) could tell us more . <p> Rather amazingly , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ are now being studied , and scientists hope to reveal the secrets of how it formed . Unlike 40 years ago , we can gather a lot more information this time around . <p> " This is hundreds of kilometers from the ice edge , " Kent Moore , an atmospheric physicist at the University of Toronto , told Motherboard . " If we did n't have a satellite , we would n't know it was there . " <p> He added that it looked like someone had " punched a hole " in the ice . <p> The big issue at the moment is what role climate change played in the appearance of the polynya . It was n't expected to appear again , but it has now surfaced twice in two years . It may be the result of changes in the Southern Ocean due to manmade climate change . <p> The polynya is expected to continue releasing heat and sinking water until the warm spring air brings it to a halt . Until then , it will continue to be studied in earnest to learn more about this enigmatic phenomena .
