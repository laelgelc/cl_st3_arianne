Until Nov. 5 at Buddies in Bad Times , 12 Alexander St. buddiesinbadtimes.com or 416-975-8555 <p> How far north have you been ? The question is posed to the audience about a third of the way through this innovative production , billed as " a concert and a conversation . " <p> And it 's you who 'll be doing some of the chatting : audience members are invited to talk to their neighbours about northern travels and then to share what they discover with the rest of the crowd and the show 's two performers , Evalyn Parry and Laakkuluk Williamson Bathory . <p> Article Continued Below <p> The lively exchanges on the night I attended had Bathory locating Yellowknife on one of the big screens behind the stage , on which designer Elysha @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ spectator revealing he 's visited islands in the Bering Strait that no one had ever heard of . " We want to talk to you after the show ! " exclaimed Parry . <p> As will already be clear , spectators who like performances to stay on the far side of the footlights , and to be plot-driven and fictional may not be fully in their comfort zone at this show . But the creators ' formal approach here has a clear rationale , driven by their chosen subject matter : relations between those who live in the south of this country ( eg. the vast majority of Canadians ) and Inuit peoples and cultures . Perceived boundaries necessarily must be breached here , personal stories revealed , ignorance and discomfort acknowledged . <p> The two women met on a sea expedition from Nunavut to Greenland . Every member of the creative team is female -- notably Mennonite/Cree cellist Cris Derksen , who plays throughout the show , sometimes with Parry on guitar . <p> The show weaves together Parry 's and Bathory 's personal experiences and stories with a much broader historical , cultural and geographical context . Powerfully , it does so by connecting the idea of the individual body -- their individual bodies -- to the physical territory of the North and its transformation since the arrival of Western colonizers centuries ago . <p> From the icelike border of Kaitlin Hickey 's simple platform set to the many beautiful video and still images of snow-covered landscapes , the changing environment and its disastrous effects on northern peoples is a consistent theme -- in which we are all implicated . The question of personal responsibility -- how to square her identity as a queer , urban artist with ever-growing awareness of historical wrongs -- is clearly an ongoing struggle for Parry , reflected in the brittle intensity with which she delivers information about the forced relocation of Arctic peoples that 's happened in our lifetimes . <p> Bathory 's presence is softer , more elusive and knowingly paradoxical ; her hands move in graceful wave patterns as she shows us the oval-bladed knives with which her people chop the " country food " ( fish and seal meat ) that is their dietary staple . Part of me wished they 'd let this unforgettable dance speak for itself rather than explain its symbolism afterward , but this seems part of the production 's agenda to educate and to steer clear of objectification and misunderstanding . <p> In its commitment to enacting and documenting queer lives and stories , the production becomes part of an ongoing Buddies series that also includes The Gay Heritage Project and Black Boys .
