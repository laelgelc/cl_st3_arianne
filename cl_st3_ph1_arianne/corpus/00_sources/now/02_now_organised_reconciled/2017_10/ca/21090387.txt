CHRISTIAN ROACH/CAPE BRETON POST <h> ' During that period the easy thing to do would be to roll over and submit , but they stood their ground ' <p> GLACE BAY , N.S. -- Cape Breton coal miners who helped shape the province 's unions and labour policies were honoured with a ceremony and the unveiling of a plaque at the Cape Breton Miners ' Museum on Friday afternoon . <p> After a ceremony at the museum that featured speeches from a member of the Historic Sites and Monuments Board of Canada , representatives from the Cape Breton Miners ' Museum and Rodger Cuzner , MP for Cape Breton -- Canso , a plaque was unveiled commemorating the importance of the Nova Scotia coal strikes of 1922 to 1925 as an event of national historic significance . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ controlled their ( Cape Breton miners ) work , food , fuel and homes . It controlled everything , so when the strikes started they were starving people , " said Mary Pat Mombourquette , the director of the museum . She added that during four successive years of strikes , Cape Breton coal miners in particular helped shape unions and labour laws . <p> " With this ( striking ) they made labour unions stronger right across our nation , we 've known this , but it 's really nice when someone comes from away and says ' yes you are significant ' -- it just means the world to us . " <p> Because of the strikes during the 1920s , Nova Scotian miners brought national attention to Cape Breton and forced the federal and provincial governments to consider more conciliatory labour policies , marking a turning point in Canadian labour relations .
