Politicians , almost by nature , are ill-disposed to being told to be cautious . They prefer announcing the delivery of all manner of projects , services , welfare benefits and tax cuts to voters . Nothing epitomised this quite like the statement of former Taoiseach , Bertie Ahern , that those who had concerns during the Bubble should end their own lives . <p> The failure to assess risks is one lesson that has been learnt , at least in part , from the crisis . There is now a yearly national risk-assessment study and annual budgets devote considerable attention to the matter . Last week 's Budget was no exception . It looked at a very wide range of risks . <p> Foundational from a budget perspective is what happens to the economy . The Department of Finance adjudges international downside risks to the Irish economy as having declined in recent months . It is hard to disagree . Northern Ireland 's economy could be hit hard by the US-Canada dispute involving Bombardier , but there are two sides to that story and the US decision to slap tariffs on the Canadian plane-maker is not a madcap decision thought up by the current incumbent in the White House . <p> What seems slightly odd about the Department 's 2018 GDP forecast is that it sees the balance of risks - to the upside and to the downside - as being symmetrical . Prospects of a Europe-driven change to the corporation tax system are often talked of in Ireland as threatening growth - by driving multinationals away - and the public finances - by reducing the billions of euro foreign companies now pay annually in corporation tax . <p> The reason the department does n't include this risk , your columnist was informed upon further enquiry , is because it is official policy to deploy the veto Ireland wields around the table in Brussels if any change is proposed that could cut receipts . That hard-line position is based on a strong position in law - vetoes contained in EU treaties are not just legal niceties . <p> The department is right to play down the risk to Ireland 's corporation tax regime . But it may not be so wise to dismiss it entirely given that some tough trade-offs may be coming down the line .
