<h> National inventory of climate risks a ' milestone ' for New Zealand <p> The Government is creating a national inventory of assets and taonga threatened over the next century by climate change . <p> The National Climate Change Risk Assessment ( NCCRA ) will outline how the expected effects of climate change -- such as warmer temperatures , increased heavy rainfall , and rising sea-levels -- would affect natural , physical , and financial assets across the country . <p> It would , for the first time , map out what New Zealand as a whole risks losing to the varied effects of climate change . <p> The framework underpinning the NCCRA was released on Thursday by Climate Change Minister James Shaw , and outlined how the assessment would be carried out . <p> New Zealand has lagged behind other countries in creating a national assessment of climate risks , and has largely left adaptation measures to local Government . The United Kingdom , for example , carried out its own national risk assessment in 2011 , and is required to repeat the exercise every five years . <p> New Zealand experts have long advocated for national guidance @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ public and private property potentially at risk of coastal hazards . <p> Research released last month estimated that for every 10cm of sea-level rise , buildings worth $2.5 billion become exposed to potential flooding . Local Government New Zealand ( LGNZ ) has conservatively estimated that $2.7b of council roading , water , and building infrastructure would be at risk of damage after 0.5m of sea-level rise . <p> The risk assessment would broaden the threats considered beyond coastal hazards , and would measure the risks based on their " nature , severity and urgency " , Shaw said . <p> " We are already experiencing the effects of a changing climate such as coastal inundation and increasingly frequent and severe droughts , floods , fires and storms , " he said . <p> " This framework is an acknowledgement that we must start adapting " . <p> WHAT WILL IT LOOK AT ? <p> BRADEN FASTIER/STUFF <p> Flooding and coastal erosion from a storm surge along McKee Memorial Reserve in Ruby Bay , Tasman , in 2017 . <p> The NCCRA would primarily look at two things : Risks @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ climate-related hazards most likely to " result in substantial risks to the nation 's wellbeing " , including warmer temperatures , sea-level rise , ocean chemistry changes , increased fire risk , and coastal erosion . <p> The effect those hazards could have on assets - a term vaguely defined , but broadly meaning the natural , social , financial , and cultural things New Zealanders value - would be identified as risks .
