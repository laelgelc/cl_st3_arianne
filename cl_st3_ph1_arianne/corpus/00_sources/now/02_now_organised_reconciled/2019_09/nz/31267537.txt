<h> Climate despair and eco anxiety <p> It 's described by many as the biggest challenge humanity has ever faced . So where do we even start with tackling the seemingly impossible problem of climate change and its flow-on impacts ? <p> A Victoria University of Wellington academic who is rising to the challenge is sociologist Jonathan Oosterman , who 's researching the kinds of radical adjustments New Zealand society needs to make to deal with the wider effects of climate change . <p> Oosterman , a Teaching Fellow in the School of Social and Cultural Studies , specialises in the social aspects of responding to the climate crisis . <p> He will be joining School Strike 4 Climate national co-ordinator Sophie Handford , RNZ National Mediawatch presenter Colin Peacock and fellow Victoria University of Wellington speakers for the panel discussion ' A climate of social action ' during the University 's Toitu te Ao -- Sustainability Week , 9 -- 13 September , five days of free public events offering fresh thinking for a more sustainable world . <p> Oosterman 's latest research is about the process of a ' just transition ' -- that is , the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ economy while ensuring fairness and social justice are prioritised . <p> " I 'm interested in the economic issues the climate crisis raises -- we already know there 's a moral case for a just transition to ensure everyone affected by the climate crisis is looked after , but it 's also important to look at the strategic case , " he says . <p> " If we do n't address strategic issues around inequality at the same time as we address the climate crisis , we are not going to adequately respond to the climate problem . It 's not one or the other -- they are interwoven . " <p> If people do n't believe they 're going to be protected in the future as we respond to the climate crisis , they 're not going to get on side with doing anything about it . <p> Oosterman says this approach will require additional work on eco-social policy , combining climate concerns with the normal pillars of social policy , such as welfare , housing and health . <p> " If people do n't believe they 're going @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the climate crisis , they 're not going to get on side with doing anything about it . " <p> Existing policies on just transition tend to be focused on looking after workers in the fossil fuel industry , which Oosterman says is too narrow . <p> " There 's an obvious need to offer a just transition for dairy and tourism workers too , because they are both industries where cuts will have to be made .
