<p> Report from RNZ Locals are furious that a consent granted six years ago to bottle water in Upper Hutt went unnoticed because the Regional Council refused to publicly notify it . <p> The consent , granted to Heretaunga Water Ltd in 2013 , allows up to 180 million litres of water to be drawn from the ground each year for the purposes of water bottling . <p> The site at Refreshment Place in Upper Hutt sits out of view from the main road . Only if you drive down a small cul-de-sac do you see the processing plant and tanks behind an electric fence . <p> Previously , the site hosted the Coca-Cola bottling plant . <p> The 2012 consent application states the Tauranga-based company 's intention to take water for the purposes of bottling . <p> A later email in 2016 from one of the company 's directors Jeffrey Colby to the council confirmed it planned to begin a " long and expensive development " selling water to the international market . <p> Upper Hutt city councillor Angela McLeod said locals should have had a say . " <p> At the time the iwi 's spokesperson , Teri Puketapu , who was notified because of an agreement between the iwi and council , asked for the application to go out for public consultation . This was because the company had asked to take more water per second than what was permitted in the council 's freshwater plan . The council decided this was not necessary and the approval went ahead , largely under the radar . <p> Ms McLeod said that was not acceptable . <p> " For a start I think that that 's a slight on the mana of the iwi , but also because public notification should be given for such a thing as taking water out of our groundwater zone .
