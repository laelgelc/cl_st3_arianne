<h> UN climate report predicts the end of Australia 's beach culture <p> Global warming is threatening Australia 's beaches , the tourism industry and seafood production more than ever before , a United Nations climate panel has found . <p> Experts have also predicted that Australians may have to adapt their living conditions by building walls to keep the sea from damaging their homes or by moving away from the coastline entirely . <p> The latest climate report released by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) says sea levels are rising at an accelerating rate as ice and snow shrink , and oceans are getting more acidic and losing oxygen . <p> Sea levels will rise by up to 90 centimetres by the end of the century , fish stocks will be decimated and tropical storms like cyclones will be stronger and wetter , if steps are not taken immediately to reduce global greenhouse gas emissions , the report warns . <p> It also predicts the potential for extreme flooding in coastal areas in coming years and the probability that some island nations will become uninhabitable . <p> Report co-author and Australian National University climate @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ could face the flooding at least once a year by 2050 , when previously they were once-in-a-century events . <p> The dire effects will be felt on both land and sea , harming people , plants , animals , food , societies , infrastructure and the global economy . <p> The oceans absorb more than 90 per cent of the excess heat from carbon pollution in the air , as well as much of the carbon dioxide itself . Earth 's snow and ice , called the cryosphere , are also being eroded . <p> " The world 's oceans and cryosphere have been taking the heat for climate change for decades , " Ko Barrett , vice-chair of the IPCC , said . <p> Meanwhile Arctic sea ice in September - the annual low point - is down almost 13 per cent per decade since 1979 .
