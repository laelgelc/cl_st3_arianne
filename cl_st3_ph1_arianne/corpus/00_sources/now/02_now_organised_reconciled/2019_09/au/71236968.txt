<h> Renewables could supply all Russian and Central Asian energy needs by 2030 <p> Renewable energy could supply Russia and Central Asian countries with all the electricity they need by 2030 -- and cut costs significantly at the same time . Paul Brown from the Climate News Network and Komila Nabiyeva report . <p> A NEW study says that Russia and the countries of Central Asia could become a highly energy-competitive region by getting all their electricity from renewable sources within the next 15 years . <p> This would make all the countries more competitive by cutting their costs , but would require the building of a super-grid to allow countries to share the benefits of a range of renewable energy sources . <p> The geographical area of the research - which did not include transport or heating - covers much of the northern hemisphere . <p> Many of the countries in the area rely on the production and use of fossil fuels and nuclear power . Wind amounts to about 60 per cent of the production , while solar , biomass and hydropower make up most of the rest . <p> The total installed capacity of renewable energy in the system in 2030 would be about 550 gigawatts . Slightly more than half of this would be wind energy , and one-fifth would be solar . The rest would be composed of hydro and biomass , supported with power-to-gas , pumped hydro storage , and batteries . <p> Currently , the total capacity is 388 gigawatts , of which wind and solar account for only 1.5 gigawatts . The current system also has neither power-to-gas capacity nor storage batteries . <p> One of the key insights of the research is that energy sectors ' integration lowers the cost of electricity by 20 per cent for Russia and Central Asia .
