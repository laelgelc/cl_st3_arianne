<p> In Canberra 's north , ropes are being coiled , carabiners tightened and harnesses checked , all in the name of encouraging parrots to make a little whoopee . The vulnerable Superb parrot is the target of earnest endeavours by Canberra 's ornithologists as climate change forces a change in its usual behaviour . Rising temperatures in the north of NSW and southern Queensland where the rare , green , long-tailed parrot usually spends its winter , are sending the birds south and further east for longer periods . That 's why ACT Parks and Conservation woodland ecologist Dr Laura Rayner is finding herself high up Canberra 's mature gum trees , looking into hollows and checking the stoutness of their limbs , more often than normal . It 's all part of the job for the straight-talking member of the so-called " difficult bird research group " which is not just studying Canberra 's Superb parrot population but finding and creating ideal places where these little-known birds can nest and raise their young . Helping from the ground , somewhat unexpectedly , are a bunch of blokes from the Kaleen-Giralang men 's shed .
