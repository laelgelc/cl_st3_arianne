<h> Pursuit home <h> Governments must support science , not self-interest <p> The proposal of an ' independent science quality assurance agency ' will not address the research replication crisis and will hinder attempts to improve science <p> By Dr Martin Bush , University of Melbourne ; Alex O. Holcombe , Professor , University of Sydney ; Dr Bonnie Claire Wintle , University of Melbourne ; Associate Professor Fiona Fidler , University of Melbourne ; Professor Simine Vazire , University of California <h> A Quality Assurance Agency wo n't improve science <p> Establishing an agency with a mission to adjudicate on hand-picked scientific results may make things worse . <p> At best , such an agency will be one more review panel . At worst , it will be a bureaucratic front for the political agenda of the day . <p> Either way it will make scientists more cautious and closed , and delay the flow of information to policy makers . <p> The record of the politicians and lobby groups involved demonstrates that they have little genuine interest in improving science . AgForce , for example , " deleted more than a decade 's worth of data " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Such acts undermine any claim to support transparency . <p> Availability of data is absolutely critical for good science , and we support new measures to increase data sharing , but the record of AgForce is inconsistent with this . <h> The idea of scientific uncertainty has been exploited by industry lobbyists before <p> This misuse of the replication crisis was seen three years ago in the UK to justify inaction on climate change . More recently it has popped up in the United States . Local politicians and lobby groups are copying from an overseas playbook in their misuse of the replication crisis . <p> Scientists can never make pronouncements with the certainty of a politician . If , as a society , we want to enjoy a healthy scientific community , we will need to accept the idea of scientific uncertainties . <p> However , the existence of small uncertainties does not justify rejection of the best available evidence . <h> In order to defend science , we need to improve it <p> It is tempting to respond to politically motivated attacks on science with claims about @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ intentions of the vast majority of scientists . <p> We can point to much better reasons to trust science : because scientists have identified the roots of the replication crisis and continue to put better systems in place to catch their own errors . <p> A simple story , that science is the best knowledge we have , is rhetorically powerful . <p> But this strategy downplays the need for all of us to improve . <p> Research is hard work , and we ca n't expect scientists to never make errors or to provide complete certainty . Picture : Pexels <p> As advocates of cultural change in science we have been told before that pointing out problems helps the anti-science movement . We say that being open about our work to improve science is essential for building public trust . <h> TAKING THE Next steps <p> There are sensible policies to support the open science initiatives that will reduce error production and increase error detection in scientific work . Different fields need different approaches , but here are two ideas : <p> Improve funding allocation procedures : Reward self-correcting @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ piece of funded research to be ground-breaking . Do n't promote flawed metrics . Enforce best practice data management and open data practices , whenever feasible . This can all be done without establishing an inefficient agency whose likely effect is to delay action . <p> Establish a national independent office of research integrity : To allow errors in the scientific literature , whether deliberate or accidental , to be corrected in a manner that is fair , efficient and systematic . Unlike the ' Quality Assurance ' proposal , this would improve the process for all researchers , not just act as a hand-brake on the results targeted by lobby groups . <h> Science is self-correcting when we reward correction <p> Science is something that humans do . It is self-correcting when , and only when , scientists correct it . <p> Research is hard work , and we ca n't expect scientists to never make errors or to provide complete certainty . <p> Availability of data is absolutely critical for good science .
