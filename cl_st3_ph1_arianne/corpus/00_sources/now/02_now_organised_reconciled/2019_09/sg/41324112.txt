<h> Choa Chu Kang Waterworks equips world 's largest ceramic membrane <p> The Choa Chu Kang Waterworks has completed upgrades to utilise ceramic membrane technology in preparation for climate change , the Minister for the Environment and Water Resources ( MEWR ) Masagos Zulkifli revealed . <p> This was done under the three-year second phase of upgrading works that cost $162m . It is now considered to have the world 's single largest ceramic membrane system , that can treat up to 40 million gallons of raw water per day . <p> According to Zulkifli , conditions caused by climate change like warming of waters and nutrient runoff from intense rains may fuel algae growth in the reservoirs . This can cause the quality of catchment water to deteriorate . <p> Meanwhile , the ceramic membrane can last about 20 years and only causes water loss of about 1% , whilst polymeric membranes have a lifespan of about 5 years and result in water loss of 5% . <p> The upgrade also implemented ozone and biological activated carbon ( ozone-BAC ) filtration in their system . <p> Additionally , the Public Utilities Board ( PUB ) signed an MOU @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and British utility South West Water . <p> The Choa Chu Kang Waterworks was built in 1975 with a capacity to treat 80 million gallons of water daily from Kranji , Pandan and Tengeh reservoirs .
