<h> Yale-NUS course cancellation <h> Watch out for foreigners with own agenda <p> Former MP Goh Choon Kang 's article ( Singapore does not need a ' colour revolution ' , Sept 21 ) rings a much needed alarm . The protracted protests in Hong Kong are too close for comfort and Singapore is not immune to such activities . <p> Regime-change operations are hardly new . Sometimes they are blatant and take the form of outright coups ; other times , they are more covert and involve infiltration and influence . <p> University students are attractive targets because freedom of speech is supposed to be key to the pursuit of knowledge . It is also not hard to find in this energetic age group people who believe that change is needed and they are the ones capable of executing it . <p> The young person also tends to perceive rebellion as an act of independence , even though it never quite is . <p> The 1960s present plenty of examples to illustrate how students have the potential to detonate political scuffles in the larger society . America , for example , saw how one strike can incite millions @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . After World War II , students acquired a heftier weight as a social group . <p> From protests against inaction on climate change to undesirable wars , once students find their cause , they can quickly disseminate their ideas via media platforms to form a critical mass in protest . <p> Of course , not all revolutions are bad , but we need to guard against foreign influencers who could very well be eyeing our university students as agents for the promotion of their own agenda . <p> Lily Ong <p> A version of this article appeared in the print edition of The Straits Times on September 24 , 2019 , with the headline ' Watch out for foreigners with own agenda ' .
