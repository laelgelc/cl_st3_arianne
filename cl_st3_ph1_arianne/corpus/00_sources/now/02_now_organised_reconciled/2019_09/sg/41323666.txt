<h> " $278m lost ' to weather ' " <p> Barbados is likely losing about $278 million annually from " weather events " , a statistic that should prompt authorities here and in the region to examine and act on the economics of climate adaptation . <p> That advice has come from two officials of Swiss Re , one of the world 's leading reinsurance companies , who said this " economic loss potential " may rise to $558 million per annum by 2030 . <p> " For instance , protecting the Folkestone Marine Park on the west coast of Barbados and ensuring reef and mangrove revivals can lower losses by US$20 million annually for an annual cost of only US$1 million . <p> The report , which utilized research conducted by the Caribbean Catastrophe Risk Insurance Facility , found that the estimated $278 million in " expected losses " was mainly due to strong winds , while inland and coastal flooding " account for roughly a quarter of the losses " . <p> Mueller and Bresch said their analysis was based on an economics of climate adaptation model that " provides the facts and tools required to develop @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ plans in order to increase resilience against climate hazards " . <p> This included " the assessment of the expected annual loss to the economy from existing climate patterns ; a projection of the extent to which future economic growth puts greater value at risk ; and finally an assessment of the incremental loss that could occur over a twenty-year period under a range of climate change scenarios based on the latest scientific knowledge " . <p> Based on the methodology used , the report said the total climate risk facing Barbados was " the sum of today 's risk , increase due to asset growth until 2030 and climate change " . <p> It also noted that " adaptation measures are not yet taken into account " . <p> " Overall , expected loss as a proportion of GDP could rise to between two per cent and nine per cent in the high climate change scenario by 2030 . In absolute terms , expected loss may triple between now and 2030 , with wind remaining the single largest contributor . " ( SC ) @
