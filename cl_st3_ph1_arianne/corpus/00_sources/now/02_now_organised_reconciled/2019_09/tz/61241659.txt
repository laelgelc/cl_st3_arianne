<h> Hadza wins global prize for developing solution on climate change <p> THE Hadza indigenous communities in northern Tanzania have won the 2019 Equator Prize awarded by the UNDP Equator Initiative after advancing nature-based solutions for climate change and promoting local sustainable development . <p> The Yaeda Valley in Mbulu district , Arusha Tanzania <p> The Hadza are an ancient hunter-gatherer ethnic group relying on natural environment to sustain their traditional lifestyle of gathering wild fruits and tubers . <p> Conservation of the natural forest is done by the indigenous Hadza communities of the Yaeda valley in partnership with Carbon Tanzania through the Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation ( REDD ) project implemented in three villages namely , Mongo Wa Mono , Domanga and YeadaChini . The project prevents 22,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide annually from being emitted and prevents 18,700 trees from being cut down per year . <p> Carbon Tanzania , a company devoted to producing carbon offsets through natural forest conservation is supported by The Nature Conservancy ( TNC ) .
