<p> Coming up on Saturday ( September 21 , 7.30pm ) at Ulverston 's Laurel and Hardy Museum , among the star turns will be this year 's Second Poem and a Pint competition winners who will offer chapter and verse on their carefully crafted pieces . <p> Many of the winners and commended poets were from Cumbria : Kerry Darbishire and Clare Proctor , winner of the best Cumbrian poet award , and also Barbara Hickson , whose own shared anthology has just been launched in Lancaster , are members of Brewery Poets , and the winner of the prize , Maggie Reed , has Cumbrian origins . <p> Guest poet will be Carrie Etter , who was also this year 's Poem and a Pint competition judge . <p> Carrie was born and brought up on the prairies in Normal , Illinois . Since 2004 she has been reader in creative writing at Bath Spa University . She has published four poetry chapbooks , one collection of prose , and her fourth full collection , The Weather in Normal , was published in last autumn by Seren Books . <p> In her 2016 chapbook , Scar , Carrie explored the effects of global warming on her home state of Illinois apparently " with typical verve and originality , " in a single long poem which became the backbone of the third section of her 2018 collection . Extreme weather endangered her father in a very real way : the doctor could not come . This part of the book gives the reader a vivid impression of the haunting beauty of the prairie , of its cornfields stretching to the horizons , while at the same time man 's technologies and man-made contrivances crumble against the supremacy of the weather , and man is " the world 's curse " . <p> The second section of the book explores the effect of the loss of the family home in the long poem Afterlife .
