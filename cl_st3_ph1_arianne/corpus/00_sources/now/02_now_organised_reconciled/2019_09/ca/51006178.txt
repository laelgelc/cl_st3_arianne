<p> Nature Guelph is pleased to welcome Dr. Danielle Way as our October guest speaker ! Please join us at 7:30 pm on Thursday , October 10 , at the University of Guelph Arboretum Centre , 200 Arboretum Road , Guelph . <p> Increases in temperature and CO2 concentrations directly affect plant physiology , and there is growing evidence that the responses of vegetation to warming and rising CO2 will be limited by other environmental factors , such as water and day length . Elevated growth temperatures can alter plant water transport , and water may be a stronger control of plant mortality in a warmer world . And while warming is predicted to increase the length of the growing season and stimulate carbon uptake in northern latitudes , day length constraints may limit the ability of forests to positively respond to rising temperatures . Taking a multifactorial approach to studying global change is increasingly necessary if we are to successfully predict and mitigate the effects of climate change on managed and natural systems , such as agricultural species and Canada 's forests . <p> About Our Speaker : <p> Dr. Danielle Way is an Associate Professor in the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Canada , and an adjunct Assistant Professor at the Nicholas School of the Environment at Duke University . Dr. Way and her lab focus on the impact of elevated growth temperatures and CO2 concentrations on plant carbon fluxes , to better represent CO2 exchange between vegetation and our atmosphere in large-scale models under future climates .
