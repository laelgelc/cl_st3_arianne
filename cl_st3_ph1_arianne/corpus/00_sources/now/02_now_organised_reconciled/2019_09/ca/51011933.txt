<p> Ottawa has unveiled the final regulations on its carbon pricing system for large emitting industries , leaving draft proposals from last December largely intact but loosening limits for sectors that include steel and fertilizers . <p> Environment and Climate Change Canada officials announced today that it is keeping its pollution pricing thresholds the same for most of the 38 heavy-emitting industrial sectors under its " output-based pricing " system ( OBPS ) -- the more complex , lesser-known part of the federal carbon pricing plan . <p> But pricing thresholds were rolled back for sectors such as steel and fertilizers -- industries that have a high proportion of emissions from processing raw materials in their overall footprint . <p> OBPS thresholds are based on a share of an industry 's average emissions intensity , providing credits to facilities that pollute at levels below the metric , which they can later use or sell to others that pollute more . <p> Standards finalized today will be set at 80 , 90 or 95 per cent of an industry 's average emissions intensity depending on the sector 's exposure to competitiveness issues . <p> Emitters above the threshold
