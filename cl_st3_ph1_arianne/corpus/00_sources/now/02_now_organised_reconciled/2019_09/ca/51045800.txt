<p> The motivation for eating a vegetarian diet varies between individuals , but when it comes to choosing forks over knives for environmental reasons , a significant new study by Johns Hopkins University offers food for thought . <p> Published in the journal Global Environmental Change , the research analyzed the carbon footprint of nine plant-centric diets -- including vegan , ovo-lacto vegetarian ( vegetarians who eat eggs and dairy ) , pescatarian and no red meat -- in 140 countries . Findings suggest that cutting back on animal products -- the so-called flexitarian diet -- may be better for the planet than excluding meat altogether . <p> In what the authors dubbed the " two-thirds vegan " diet , limiting consumption of animal products to just one meal a day put less of a strain on the environment than ovo-lacto vegetarianism in almost all ( 95 per cent ) countries . In 64 per cent of study nations , abstaining from dairy resulted in lower greenhouse gas emissions than ovo-lacto vegetarian diets . <p> Veganism proved to have the lightest impact on the environment in almost all ( 97 per cent ) of the nations ; researchers concluded @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the board would result in food-related greenhouse gases dropping by 70 per cent per person . Notably , researchers found that diets high in vegetables and other plants , but incorporating animals low on the food chain -- mollusks such as clams , oysters and scallops , and small fish like anchovies , herring and sardines -- had footprints as low as veganism . <p> Of the 74 foods involved in the study , beef , lamb and mutton , and goat ( ruminant meats ) were " by far " the most greenhouse gas-intensive foods : The impact of each serving was 40 times more acute than pulses ( beans , chickpeas , lentils ) , nuts , soy or seeds . In general , plant-based foods were the least intensive , and insects ( crickets , mealworms ) and forage fish ( herring , sardines ) among " the more climate-friendly animal products , much more so than dairy . " <p> " Our data indicate that it is actually dairy product consumption that explains much of the differences in greenhouse gas footprints across diets . " It would be satisfying to have a silver bullet to address carbon footprints and the impact of food production ; however , with problems as complex and global as nutrition , climate change , freshwater depletion and economic development , that 's not possible , " Bloem adds .
