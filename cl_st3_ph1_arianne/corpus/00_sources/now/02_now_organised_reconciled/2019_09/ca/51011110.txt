<h> The degrowth movement wants to intentionally shrink the economy to address climate change , and create lives with less stuff , less work , and better well-being . But is it a utopian fantasy ? <p> In 1972 , a team at MIT published The Limits to Growth , a report that predicted what would happen to human civilization as the economy and population continued to grow . What their computer simulation found was pretty straightforward : On a planet of finite resources , infinite exponential growth is n't possible . Eventually , non-renewable resources , like oil , would run out . <p> Historically , we have considered growth a positive thing , synonymous with job security and prosperity . This skepticism has led to the degrowth movement , which says the growth of the economy is inextricably tied to an increase in carbon emissions . It calls for a dramatic reduction in energy and material use , which would inevitably shrink GDP . <p> The Green New Deal , popularized by Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez , seeks to decrease carbon by growing the renewable energy industry . But the degrowth movement believes we need to take this further , by designing a social upheaval that disentangles the idea of progress and economic growth once and for all . This new accounting of economic success would instead focus on access to public services , a shorter work week , and an increase in leisure time .
