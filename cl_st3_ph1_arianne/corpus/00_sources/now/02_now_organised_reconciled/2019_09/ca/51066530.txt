The new report recommends five specific areas in which to invest . <p> Early warning systems <p> Early warning systems are technologies that can accurately forecast when a storm , heatwave , or other adverse weather event is incoming . According to the report , just 24 hours ' warning can reduce the resulting damage by 30 percent , and investing $800 million in such systems in developing countries would prevent $3 -- 16 billion per year in losses . <p> Climate-resilient infrastructure <p> The @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ communities -- which might mean improving housing , water , sanitation , drainage , and waste management -- will build resilience and strengthen their adaptive capacity . More climate-resilient infrastructure adds about 3 percent to upfront costs but provides $4 in benefits for every $1 of cost . <p> Improved dryland agriculture <p> Investing in drought-resistant crops and modernizing irrigation systems could help protect small-scale farms from rising temperatures . If nothing is done , the report says , global crop yields could shrink by 30 percent by mid-century . <p> Mangrove protection <p> Mangroves -- trees that grow in coastal swamps -- reduce the impact of storm surges that threaten coastal communities . According to the report , mangrove forests prevent more than $80 billion per year in losses from coastal flooding and protect 18 million people . They also contribute just over $40 billion annually to sustain local fisheries . ( Incidentally , mangrove forests are also an incredible natural carbon sink . ) <p> Making water resources more resilient <p> Investing in water infrastructure and natural watersheds could expand access to clean water .
