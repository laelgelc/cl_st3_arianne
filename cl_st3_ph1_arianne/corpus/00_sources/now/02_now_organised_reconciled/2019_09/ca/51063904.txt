<p> The first time I saw Ian Tyson was in 1989 , beside the Oldman River down south off Highway 22 . I was a greenhorn , not that long arrived from the UK , and I had never heard of him . <p> He and his former wife , Sylvia , were there to play an open-air concert protesting against the proposed damming of the Oldman . Alongside him were Gordon Lightfoot and Murray McLauchlan , with special guests David Suzuki , Andy Russell , and tribal elder Joe Crowshoe . <p> I had , though , already discovered that glorious ranching foothills country , having been taken there by a classical composer , Ken Nichols , who grew up on a Pincher Creek ranch . I fell in love with the country , and with its people . <p> Ranching Alberta still feels like a spiritual home , with its sense of community , its nearness to nature without despoiling it , and its multigenerational camaraderie . And now , here I was , in the Winspear Centre 30 years later , to see Tyson performing with another Albertan country star , Corb Lund , and the backing of the Edmonton Symphony Orchestra , on Thursday , September 19 , the day before worldwide protests about climate change action . <p> Tyson 's 86th birthday is on September 26 , and Lund turned 50 this year . Both belong as much -- perhaps more -- to the great singer-songwriter tradition as to Nashville country and western . The overlap is that both sing about the cowboy -- or rather the ranching -- tradition , and they know that tradition intimately . Tyson has his own ranch at Longview , and Lund is often at his family ranch near Cardston . <p> What they both have in common with the heritage of Woodie Guthrie , Bob Dylan , and Leonard Cohen are deeply poetic lyrics of a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ stereotypes . <p> Tyson 's lyrics are evocations of the prairie and foothills landscape , so often tinged with something deeper . " Venus comes in the autumn dust , and she rules the southern sky . She hears our songs , she sees our tears , and she sends us no reply " he sings in Summer 's Gone , here turned into a gorgeous slow rhapsody with the rich cellos of the ESO and the saxophone of conductor-soloist Raymond Baril . <p> Lund 's own influences may include such cowboy singers as Marty Robbins , but he is the Bruce Springsteen of the west . His repertoire is wide-ranging , including such songs , hilariously rendered here , as Priceless Antique Pistol Shoots Startled Owner , an almost sardonic look back to Jimmie Rodgers , the Yodeling Cowboy . <p> But just as Springsteen writes of the American industrial decline , of the fate of American working classes , so Lund writes of the darker side of the ranching life , of its decline , and the fates of its people . It was n't just that Tyson is n't going to be able to perform forever , and that his singing " I 'm bound for moving on " is doubly poignant . His voice is now as gravelly , though a lot more melodious , as Leonard Cohen 's in his last years . <p> It 's that the songs they chose to sing were almost all an extended farewell to a way of life that has run all through Alberta 's modern history , and defined so much of the province -- even more than the songs they played in their 2018 Jubilee concert .
