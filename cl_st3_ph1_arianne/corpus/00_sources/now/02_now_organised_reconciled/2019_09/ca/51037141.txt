Spring had come early that year , followed by an abnormally hot and dry spell that lasted into the summer . <p> On July 11 , 1911 , when the Porcupine gold rush was at its height , a gale from the southwest whipped some small bush fires into flames . <p> The blaze formed a horseshoe-shaped front more than 36 kilometres wide . It laid waste to about 200,000 hectares . <p> Many people were drowned as they fled into Porcupine Lake to escape the flames , while others suffocated to death underground in the mines . <p> At one point @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ exploded , propelling the lake into waves three-metres high . <p> The exact number of dead is not known as the region contained an unknown number of remote prospectors at the time of the fire . Official counts list 73 dead , though it is estimated the actual toll could have been as high as 200 . The next day the fire swept through Cochrane . <p> The historic plaque is at the Whitewater Conservation Area on Shamrock Avenue South in Porcupine . There also is a monolith at Deadman 's Point overlooking Porcupine Lake at the Whitney Cemetery , where there are many graves related to the fire . <p> The great Matheson Fire was a deadly forest fire that passed through the region surrounding the communities of Black River-Matheson and Iroquois Falls in the summer of 1916 . <p> As was common practice at the time , settlers cleared land using the slash-and-burn method . That summer , there was little rain and the forests and underbrush burned easily . <p> In the days leading up , several smaller fires that had been purposely set merged into a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 64-kilometres across . <p> The fire moved uncontrollably upon the towns of Porquis Junction , Iroquois Falls , Matheson and Ramore -- destroying them completely . In all , the fires burned an area of approximately 2,000 square kilometres ( 490,000 acres ) . <p> At least 223 people were killed according to the official estimate , and it remains the deadliest fire in Canadian history . This historic plaque is located on Highway 101 and Second Avenue in Matheson . <p> The Great Fire of 1922 was a wildfire burning through the Lesser Clay Belt . <p> The preceding summer had been unusually hot and dry . In the fall when burning permits were no longer required , farmers and settlers started to set small bush fires to clear the land . Dry conditions had persisted past the usual " burn " season and , on Oct. 4 , the wind increased dramatically , fanning the flames out of control and combining the brush fires into one large inferno . <p> Over two days , the fire consumed an area of 1,680 square kilometres , affecting 18 townships . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Thornloe and numerous smaller settlements . Englehart , New Liskeard and Haileybury were partly burnt . <p> In all , 43 people died .
