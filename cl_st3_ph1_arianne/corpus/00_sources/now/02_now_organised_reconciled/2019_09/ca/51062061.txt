<p> As the federal election draws near , political platforms will inevitably focus -- at least for a few days -- on environmental policies . <p> Previous electoral campaigns have been dominated by one or two prominent issues , such as the economy and jobs in 2008 . This year , as was outlined in a recent Research Co. survey , the preoccupations of Canadians are dissimilar from sea to sea to sea . <p> About one in six Canadians ( 16% ) told us that the environment is the most important issue facing Canada today -- a smaller proportion than those who selected the economy and jobs ( 19% ) and health care ( also 19% ) , but higher than housing , homelessness and poverty ( 13% ) and immigration ( 11% ) . <p> When voters were asked which issue would define their vote in this year 's federal election , the environment climbed slightly to 18% . The Canadian version of this proposal is calling for the country to move away from fossil fuels , cut carbon emissions in half by 2030 , protect jobs , promote green transportation and deal with economic inequality . <p> At a time when coverage of U.S. politics is omnipresent on our airwaves , Canadians may have been exposed to the American Green New Deal , which was tabled as a stimulus package by members of the Democratic party . As expected , it was mocked by President Donald Trump as a " killer " of millions of jobs . <p> When Research Co. asked Canadians about the Pact for a Green New Deal , only 30% said they were " very " or " moderately " familiar with it . Quebecers ( 34% ) and Albertans ( 32% ) were more likely to know something about the policy proposal -- and for very different reasons .
