<p> We should know how to deal with the AI revolution because we have been down this road before . It 's a bit different this time , of course , in the sense that the original industrial revolution from 1780 to 1850 created as many new jobs ( in manufacturing ) as it destroyed ( in cottage industries and skilled trades ) . <p> The AI revolution , by contrast , is not producing nearly enough replacement jobs , but it is making us much wealthier . The value of manufactured goods doubled in the United States in the past 30 years , even as the number of good industrial jobs fell by one-third ( eight-million jobs gone ) . <p> Maybe we could use that extra wealth to ease the transition to a job-scarce future . <p> The climate emergency is unlike any challenge we have faced before . Surmounting it would require an unprecedented level of global co-operation and massive changes in how people consume and behave , neither at which human beings have historically been good . <p> These two crises are already interacting .
