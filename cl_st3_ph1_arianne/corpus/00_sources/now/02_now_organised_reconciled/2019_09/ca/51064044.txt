<h> Climate change is more than just economics <p> " Health-care providers are on the front lines . Our patients ' health is impacted by extreme weather events . We know leveraging the potential of new , clean and emerging technologies will demonstrate a transformation of health care in line with sustainable development goals . " <p> In the lead-up to , and since the start of the election campaign , climate change is emerging as a key concern for Canadians . Climate change is more than just economics . In fact , it is emerging as a significant threat to public health , and we need to wake up quickly because our entire health system is at risk . <p> Let 's look at the facts : we know air pollution is a big culprit in cardiorespiratory illnesses . But did you know heat-related illnesses are a more common occurrence than ever before ? And that burning of fossil fuels is responsible for 7,100 early deaths and $53.5 billion in health-related costs each year in Canada ? What about the untold post-traumatic stress following frequent floodings and wildfires right across this country ? We can not escape it @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is a sense of urgency for action everywhere . The UN Climate Action Summit in New York this week promises collective political ambition and movement in the real economy to support the summit 's objectives of the Paris Agreement . But is Canada really on board ? <p> The 2018 Lancet Countdown Canadian indicators demonstrate Canada needs to step up in a significant way to mitigate the impacts of climate change on Canadians . Phasing out coal-powered electricity to drastically increase efforts in reducing greenhouse gas emissions and air pollution is just a start . Also , we need to build in new health and economic opportunities in retraining workers in the fossil fuel industry . <p> But there is a crucial component of this action that revolves around health . <p> The next government of Canada , through local governments and national institutions , needs to modernize and standardize surveillance and reporting of illnesses and deaths caused or exacerbated by extreme weather events . Strategies need to be developed to inform Canadians about the threat of heatwaves to public health . Plans to minimize the health impact of increasing @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ come as climate change progresses . <p> Chronic diseases cost Canada about $200 billion per year in health-related costs . By focusing on health prevention and investing in public transit , cycling and walking , we can easily save lives , reduce chronic diseases and health care costs while fighting climate change . <p> This year , the CMA conducted extensive consultation and polling with physicians and patients across the country . Almost unanimously , both health and climate change were ranked among the top priorities in their minds . <p> Health-care providers are on the front lines . Our patients ' health is impacted by extreme weather events . We know leveraging the potential of new , clean and emerging technologies will demonstrate a transformation of health care in line with sustainable development goals . <p> We are calling on all federal political parties to bind these recommendations into their platforms : <p> recognize and acknowledge the link between climate change and the health of Canadians ; <p> invest in adapting our health system and public health capacity to address the growing health impacts of climate change in Canada @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ challenge . We need our politicians to pull together and show leadership , deliver on a much needed strategy to combat climate change and its effects on the health of Canadians . Now is the time to put health back on the agenda . To learn more about how you can take action in this election , please visit our website . <p> Our health-care system is already over-burdened . Ongoing pressures will affect generations to come . Do n't waste this opportunity to improve your health and the health of your loved ones and future generations .
