<p> Political tensions over wildfires in the Amazon are being fuelled by a long-standing Brazilian fear that the West covets the region 's natural resources , says a writer based in the country . <p> " The right-wing in Brazil has always feared that the Amazon was this soft underbelly that was ripe for invasion , " said Alexander Zaitchik , a journalist who has travelled extensively through the Amazon , writing about the natural wonder and the Indigenous people who protect it . <p> " In the past it was just about territory or resources , and now they fear that an international agenda will be undermining Brazilian sovereignty under the banner of a climate emergency , or environmental concerns , " he told The Current 's guest host Matt Galloway . <p> Zaitchik said protecting the Amazon is an important factor in fighting climate change , but countries must also make efforts on their own home soil . <p> " Developed countries -- which have destroyed their own forests -- are now turning to Brazil and expecting to have a strong say in Brazilian forest management , which makes sense given the stakes , " he said @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ has to be admitted , and if these countries want to have leverage they 're going to have to admit that their own behaviour has come into play here . " <p> In a statement to The Current , the Brazilian consulate said that " the Brazilian government is still investigating the causes of the forest fires . Up to now , there is no certainty about it . Even the numbers that have been publicized by the media are not consistent among themselves .
