<h> In Costa Rica , indigenous farmers change along with the climate <p> YORKIN , Costa Rica , Sept 19 ( Thomson Reuters Foundation ) -- W ith erratic rainfall in Costa Rica playing havoc with her harvest , Maura Lupario reorganized her farm to ensure it could keep feeding her family -- including getting her husband to give up his harvesting job at a banana company 50km away . <p> Like millions of indigenous farmers around the world , she always relied on her traditional knowledge . But increasingly unpredictable weather , linked to climate change , has upended her usual sowing and harvesting times , leading to lower yields . <p> " Before , you already knew in which months you could grow beans , " she told the Thomson Reuters Foundation from her farm in Yorkin Indigenous Reserve on Costa Rica 's southern border with Panama , which is home to about 500 Bribri people . <p> " But there are changes now . If we sow on the dates we used to , the rain comes early and we can no longer collect on time .
