The EPA wants to roll back Obama-era regulation on methane emissions . <p> " No climate plan for Houston can be considered legitimate unless it utilizes an abundant , clean burning , and Texas made fuel like natural gas , " the group 's online petition reads . <p> It 's true that when used to fuel power plants , gas produces about half the carbon dioxide than coal , which is why using it as a bridge to transition more fully to renewable energy sources makes sense . Texas has enjoyed a boom in the production of natural gas thanks to hydraulic fracturing and horizontal-drilling technologies . <p> But natural gas has an Achilles ' heel that must be addressed if the industry wants to be considered a viable part of any climate change strategy : methane emissions . <p> It 's aweakness the Trump administration is about @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ announced it will roll back Obama-era regulations limiting emissions of methane -- the principal component of natural gas and a harmful greenhouse agent . Although it dissipates much faster than carbon dioxide , methane traps more heat in the atmosphere , directly impacting climate concerns . <p> Under the proposed rollback , oil and gas companies will no longer be required to inspect for methane leaks in existing wells , storage tanks and pipelines . <p> EPA Administrator Andrew Wheeler said in a statement that the change removes " unnecessary and duplicative " regulatory burdens , and that the sheer value of methane is " an incentive to minimize leaks and maximize its use . " <p> But relying on state regulations and hoping the invisible hand of the market keeps an invisible gas in check is not a productive strategy when it comes to methane . Neither Texas nor North Dakota , two of the largest oil and gas-producing states , have any regulation regarding methane emissions . And the overproduction of natural gas has made it cheaper for some companies to vent or flare methane than it is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ it to market . <p> The industry itself is divided over how to handle methane emissions . <p> Large oil companies such as Exxon Mobil Corp . and Royal Dutch Shell PLC support the stricter regulations . BP has even partnered with the Environmental Defense Fund to develop new technologies to reduce methane emissions from oil and gas production and transportation . The British energy giant also announced this week that it will use drones to detect methane emissions at its existing wells , including in the Permian Basin . <p> Support for the new EPA rules comes from smaller companies , which represent about 80 percent of oil wells in the country and which have said inspection requirements are too burdensome . So just how much will the industry save at the expense of our climate future ? About $17 million to $19 million a year , the EPA estimates . For an industry that makes billions in annual profits , that opposition borders on immoral . <p> Current regulations barely do enough as it is managing greenhouse gases .
