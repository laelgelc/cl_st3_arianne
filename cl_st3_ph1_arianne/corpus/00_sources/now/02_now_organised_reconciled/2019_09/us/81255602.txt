<p> For the first time in more than 30 years , birdwatchers saw the resilient red knot in Eagle Creek Park this month . The little bird typically migrates thousands of miles from the Arctic to as far south as South America and back every year . <p> This year , the red knot took a pit stop in Indianapolis . <p> The red knot 's path is just one example of how Indiana 's moniker as the " Crossroads of America " does n't just apply to trucks and travelers , but to sparrows and sandpipers as well . <p> Indiana 's environmental diversity makes it an easy attraction for migrating birds , said Will Schaust , manager of the Eagle Creek Ornithology Center . Between prairies , forests and shorelines , Indiana offers a glut of habitats and food sources to its feathered tourists . <p> " And the nice thing , too , is that a lot of our agricultural fields play in very nicely , " Schaust said . In recent years , ornithologists have noticed some surprising visitors stopping by during migration season , which typically lasts from mid-August to late December . Birds that historically do n't come to Central Indiana have been stopping by , or even staying several months . <p> It might be changes in climate and it might be the addition of new habitats . Experts are n't sure what 's drawing in these new visitors . But they do know more birds -- or at least more varieties -- are flocking to Indiana in recent years . <h> Migration modifications <p> Typically , Schaust said , migration patterns are based on food and habitat availability .
