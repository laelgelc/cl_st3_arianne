<p> North Texans eager for sweater weather may have to wait just a bit longer . <p> For almost half a century , fall temperatures in Dallas-Fort Worth have been trending up , as summer-like temperatures linger later into the year , according to a new analysis . <p> The warming fall trend is true for much of the U.S. The analysis , produced by Climate Central , found that 95% of 244 U.S. cities have recorded a rise in average fall temperatures of at least 2 degrees from 1970 to 2018 . <p> During that 48-year span , Dallas-Fort Worth has seen a rise of 3.3 degrees -- greater than the nationwide average increase of 2.5 degrees . Three of the warmest falls on record in Dallas-Fort Worth -- 2015 , 2016 and 2017 -- have come in the past four years , according to the National Weather Service . <p> The warmest of those was 2016 , when the average temperature was 73 degrees , with an average high of 82.9 , according to the weather service . <p> The U.S. city with the greatest rise in fall temperatures is Reno , Nev. , with an increase @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the top spot with an increase of 5.4 degrees , the analysis found . <p> Climate Central , an independent organization of scientists and journalists who report on climate change , found these trends by looking at data from 244 weather stations across the country to find the relationship between average fall temperatures and the passage of time . <p> Different seasons are warming faster across the country , the study found .
