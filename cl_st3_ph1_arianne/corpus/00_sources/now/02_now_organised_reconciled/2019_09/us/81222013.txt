<h> Letter : Climate change wreaks havoc on farmers , nutrition <h> Sunday <p> The Sept 14 Dispatch article " A lost year for corn " stated Ohio 's corn crop will be down 34% compared with 2018 . This is due to rain-caused delays in planting , followed by dry conditions now . <p> Ninety-seven percent of climate scientists concur that climate change is causing more frequent and more severe extreme weather events , including drought , exceptionally heavy rains and tropical storms . The effects are not limited to corn , but will affect the quantity and nutritional quality of food worldwide . The nutrients in rice are projected to decrease with increasing atmospheric temperatures . <p> Farmers consume a lot of energy , in direct fuel usage and fertilizer . They also are on the front lines of dealing with climate change , as it affects their crops and livelihoods . <p> We need to address climate change , and we can start by reducing emissions of greenhouse , earth-warming gases . <p> H.R.763 , which is before the U.
