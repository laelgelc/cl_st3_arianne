<h> Peatlands trap carbon dioxide , even during droughts <p> Although peatlands make up only 3 percent of the Earth 's surface , they store one third of the soil carbon trapped in soils globally . Preserving peatlands is therefore of paramount importance for mitigating climate change , provided that these vulnerable environments are not themselves threatened by global warming . <p> To better determine this risk , two French scientists , including Vincent Jassey , a CNRS researcher at the Laboratoire Ecologie Fontionnelle et Environnement ( CNRS/Universit ? Toulouse III -- Paul Sabatier/INP Toulouse ) , studied carbon uptake by the two main species of moss that make up the Le Forbonnet peatland in Frasne ( Jura ) . They discovered that when temperatures were high and also during droughts , the two Sphagnum species behaved in opposite ways : Sphagnum medium resists drought , whereas the photosynthesis of Sphagnum fallax is negatively impacted ; conversely , in very hot but humid weather , photosynthesis , and thus carbon uptake , in Sphagnum fallax increases , whereas there is a negligible effect on photosynthesis in Sphagnum medium .
