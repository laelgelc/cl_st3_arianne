<h> Russia gives definitive approval to Paris climate accord <p> 3 Min Read <p> MOSCOW ( Reuters ) - Russia said it would implement the 2015 Paris Agreement to fight climate change after Prime Minister Dmitry Medvedev on Monday approved a government resolution signifying Moscow 's final acceptance of the deal . <p> The same resolution said Russia would not technically ratify the accord however due to a legal nuance . Medvedev said Moscow would adapt the accord to existing legal norms . It was unclear what , if any , the legal implications of failing to technically ratify the pact were . <p> Russia is the world 's fourth largest emitter of greenhouse gases and the biggest emitter not to have ratified the landmark global climate deal . <p> In Monday 's government resolution , Russia said it did not need to ratify the agreement because Moscow had already undertaken to honour its commitments when it signed the deal in April 2016 . <p> The government resolution was signed by Prime Minister Dmitry Medvedev and dated Sept. 21 . <p> Deputy Prime Minister Alexei Gordeyev said it was important for Russia to be part of the Paris accord @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ deciding any new measures to regulate greenhouse gas emissions . <p> " We get the chance to fully participate in shaping the modern global climate agenda , " said Gordeyev . " And any regulatory measures that are drawn up will have to take into consideration our national interests as much as possible . " <p> He said that legislation aimed at curbing greenhouse gas emissions had already been drawn up by the economy ministry and that he expected it to be adopted this year . <p> Medvedev said Russia had a lot of work to do if it was to transform its economy into a low-carbon one . <p> " It 's important and useful but demands quite significant investment , " Medvedev said of the Paris accord . " So this problem is quite closely linked with our economic development . " <p> Russia has previously expressed disappointment over a decision by U.S. President Donald Trump to pull Washington from the Paris pact , but said it still regards the accord as important despite the U.S. absence .
