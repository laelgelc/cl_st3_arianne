<h> Rochester leading the way in progressive climate policies related to housing development <p> Jolie Milstein , president and CEO of the New York State Association for Affordable Housing ( Photo : provided ) <p> New Yorkers across the state -- including here in Rochester -- know all too well how hard it is to find an affordable place to call home , and unfortunately , that process might soon become even more difficult as the result of another , equally serious problem : climate change . <p> Extreme weather takes a toll on all housing stock , but new research shows it disproportionately impacts affordable buildings . That means the affordable housing industry must prioritize sustainable development practices as it constructs new units . <p> The good news is we are already seeing this take place across New York -- particularly in the Flower City . That is why my organization , the New York State Association for Affordable Housing selected Rochester to host our 16th Annual Upstate Housing Conference on September 25 . This event convenes leading industry minds to discuss how we can work with communities across the state to enact progressive climate policies in housing @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ this undertaking . <p> Programs like Sustainable Homes Rochester , a city initiative offering financial assistance to low-income families to encourage the use of energy efficient products are already underway here . A family of four making less than $55,000 is eligible for a 100 percent subsidy on these products . This forward-thinking solution will help reduce building emissions across the city . <p> Individual developments are also making a difference and setting an example for the future . Long Pond Senior Housing , located just outside Rochester , is an affordable senior complex with a LEED for Homes Gold certification . It reduces water and energy consumption by 20 to 30 percent over the general code while providing 54 units for low-income seniors . <p> As New York moves forward with ambitious climate goals designed to limit emissions and create a clean energy economy , it is important to remember that these are not aspirational dreams . Now it 's time to expand and replicate them both locally and across New York .
