<p> SYDNEY , Sept 12 ( Reuters ) - The trial of a man accused of shootings at two mosques in New Zealand that killed 51 people has been delayed by a month because the original court date coincided with the Muslim holy month of Ramadan , a judge said on Thursday . <p> A lone gunman armed with semi-automatic weapons attacked Muslims attending Friday prayers in Christchurch on New Zealand 's South Island on March 15 , in the country 's worst peace-time mass shooting . The attacker broadcast the shooting live on Facebook . <p> Australian man Brenton Tarrant , 29 , a suspected white supremacist , has pleaded not guilty to 92 charges against him including murder and terrorism . <p> A trial was scheduled to begin on May 4 , 2020 , but High Court judge Cameron Mander said prosecutors had notified the court that " difficulties have arisen with the trial date because it clashes with the Islamic holy month of Ramadan which occurs over the month of May next year " . <p> " A number of the witnesses to be called at trial are of the Islamic faith , " Mander added @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 's defence team had agreed to the delay and the trial would begin on June 2 , he said . <p> New Zealand 's Muslim community had criticised the justice system for the time taken to bring the accused man to trial , and for holding it during Ramadan . <p> Prosecutors have said they expected the trial will take about six weeks , although Mander has said defence lawyers believe it could take longer .
