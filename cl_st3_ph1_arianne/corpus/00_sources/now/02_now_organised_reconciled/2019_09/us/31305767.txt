<p> There 's been a lot of attention lately to the harmful algal blooms that have erupted in some of New Jersey 's favorite lakes , including Lake Hopatcong and Greenwood Lake . People are wondering why the state Department of Environmental Protection ( DEP ) is advising people to stay out of the water , right at the peak of summertime fun and heat . <p> Here 's why . <p> On the day the DEP called elected officials to alert them to the algal bloom advisory for Lake Hopatcong , one official told us about a grandmother in the area who had reported that her grandchildren had developed rashes after playing in the lake . Several other residents also alerted DEP , which is why DEP scientists quickly went to the lake to test and assess the situation . DEP was as dismayed as the local community when tests showed high cyanobacteria counts all over the lake . <p> The bacteria counts found throughout Lake Hopatcong exceeded the state 's guidelines for safe exposure , triggering a health advisory to avoid contact with the lake water . That guideline is set at 20,000 cyanobacteria cells per milliliter @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ this number up . It is not arbitrary or excessively strict , as some have suggested . This is the level recommended by the World Health Organization and adopted by New Jersey in 2017 . <p> When that guideline is exceeded , the science shows that we can expect three in 10 people who have contact with the water to develop skin rashes or other allergy-like responses , or to experience gastro-intestinal distress . Not surprisingly , a mother at one of the recent public meetings at Lake Hopatcong reported that her children had stomach pains after swimming there . <p> New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection scientist analyzing water samples in the lab <p> In addition , algal blooms with this level of bacteria could , at any time , begin to produce more harmful toxins known as microcystins . Microcystin exposure can lead to even more serious health problems , such as toxic effects on the liver , kidneys and nervous system . That 's why DEP issued the recent advisories to avoid contact with lake water . That 's why the local health departments follow Department of Health regulations on closing public bathing beaches to swimming until the bloom dissipates and the bacterial cell count goes down . <p> To be clear : DEP has not " closed " Lake Hopatcong or Greenwood Lake . <p> DEP has issued advisories against contact with the lake water while the algal blooms persist above health guidelines . Both lakes remain open for boating , although DEP advises against water sports such as skiing and paddle boarding that involve accidental ingestion or direct contact with the water . <p> Dedicated DEP scientists are monitoring these blooms very closely , through an aggressive daily monitoring program using state-of-the-art buoys that collect real time data in Lake Hopatcong . DEP 's water monitoring scientists also visit the lake by boat to obtain water samples for microscopic analysis at DEP 's @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ utilize high-tech aerial sensors to estimate the concentration and movement of a bloom . We are fortunate in New Jersey to have one of the most technically advanced and protective water monitoring programs in the nation . <p> DEP will lift the advisories , as soon as the testing indicates that bacteria cell counts are trending reliably below the health advisory level . Unfortunately , control of those conditions at this point is in the hands of Mother Nature , who has not been kind lately as we have experienced heavy rains and elevated heat levels . <p> In the long-term , we know the steps we must take to prevent future algal blooms . Algal blooms are fed by nutrients , most notably phosphorus , from septic tanks , surface runoff , and stormwater that flows into the lakes , either directly or through the lakes ' many feeder streams . Nutrients in lawn fertilizers and animal droppings readily flow into the lake when it rains . <p> The solutions are not necessarily difficult or expensive . Much can be accomplished through simple methods - residents in the watershed @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ streets and clean stormwater catch basins ahead of the summer season . Property owners can reduce or forego fertilizer application , and install green infrastructure .. <p> Algal blooms in Crescent Cove in Lake Hopatcong on July 23 , 2019 . <p> The increasing prevalence of harmful algal blooms is a global phenomenon . The increasing temperatures and heavy rains associated with climate change are known triggers for algal blooms . <p> To protect our children and grandchildren , and all those who enjoy New Jersey 's beautiful lakes , DEP is committed to working with our many partners as we face the challenges to our community health and economies posed by these harmful algal blooms . <p> Working together , I am confident that we can succeed . <p> Catherine R.
