<h> Empowering young people will strengthen democracy <p> Seventeen-year-olds are already able to drive , contribute to family income and pay taxes . They are a force to be reckoned with in political movements from March For Our Lives to climate change strikes . They have taken to the streets , social media and national organizations to make their voices heard . <p> They should also be able to vote . <p> Under current policy , many of those who will choose which parties will take office in 2020 will have no say in who their nominees are . <p> Assembly Constitution Amendment 4 ( ACA 4 ) , a bill in the California state Legislature , would allow 17-year-old citizens to vote in a primary election if they will be eligible to vote in the next general election . <p> Advertisement <p> The 17-year-olds it would enfranchise are trusted to cast their vote in November for the general election . Why not trust them to vote in March as well ? <p> These teenagers will not be radically different people when , mere months later , they will be asked to make their decisions in the general election @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ready . They are watching the debates . They are reading the news . They are exploring their options and looking to make informed decisions when it comes time for them to cast their first ballot . <p> If they do n't care enough now to educate themselves for the primaries , they wo n't undergo a magical transformation on their 18th birthday . <p> Advertisement <p> In any case , many Americans older than 18 vote with limited knowledge about the issues on the ballot . Being uninformed is not a reason for us to deny some people a vote , unless , it seems , they are a teenager . <p> However , the assumption that teens are uninformed and will make poor decisions is unfounded . <p> Young people are more likely than their older peers to be involved in activism and political causes . <p> On issues from climate change to district attendance policies , teens are more capable than adults realize of forming strong opinions and defending them intelligently . We have just as much skin in the game . <p> Many of us have jobs and pay taxes . We can be tried as adults in criminal cases . Along with having jobs and schoolwork , we may also act as caretakers for our families . <p> If we are mature enough to take on these adult responsibilities , it is reasonable to question why we are not considered mature enough to vote as well . <p> For this reason , there is a larger movement to lower the voting age at the local and federal level . <p> Advertisement <p> Issues like school board elections affect students more than any other demographic . Why should n't we have a say ? <p> Even circumstantially , lowering the voting age will help ease young people into their role as democratic citizens . <p> My friends would be relieved to know they can register and cast their first ballot while still at home . The younger someone is when they vote for the first time , the more likely they are to continue voting . Civic engagement is a habit like any other . <p> Bills like ACA 4 are allowing our country to test the waters when it comes to enfranchising teenagers before we consider wider strides . <p> California has the chance to join the growing movement toward youth enfranchisement and a stronger democracy . <p> More than that , we have a chance to give young people a say in what happens in their immediate future and empower them for a lifetime . <p> Taila , a senior at Serra High School , is editor of her school newspaper .
