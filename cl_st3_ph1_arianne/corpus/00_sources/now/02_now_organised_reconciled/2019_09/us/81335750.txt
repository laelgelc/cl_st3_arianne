<h> Mobile students join in global climate strike <p> Students from the Alabama School of Math and Science 's environmental club lined Mobile 's downtown streets with bright , hand-drawn signs that read " Stop denying our Earth is dying , " and " Winter is not coming . " <p> Teens gathered in large numbers as a part of the Global Climate Strike on Friday during rush hour . Thousands of young people across the globe , all part of Fridays for Future , pick up their signs and head to the streets to strike . <p> " I feel like as part of this generation we should all join together to try to stop what is happening to our planet , said May Jung , 17 . " The youth is getting more involved and as the youth of Alabama , I wanted to represent our state and community . " <p> The protests will continue throughout the country . They initially began when 16-year-old Greta Thunberg , a climate activist from Sweden , pushed her government to take action . Her most recent speech at the United Nations Climate Summit sparked massive attention . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ back in school on the other side of the ocean , " said Thunberg , who traveled for two weeks on a solar-powered sailboat to reach the United States this month . " Yet you all come to us young people for hope . How dare you ? " <p> Jung and her fellow classmates stood in front of Government Plaza in Downtown Mobile , cheering and shaking their posterboard signs when cars honked in support . The teens received support from those heading home from work , but national support is lacking , Jung said . <p> " It 's upsetting , " she said , to hear political leaders deny climate change . " They are the leaders of the people , our communities , our nation and this is an important issue . They should be the people taking actions , making policy and plans that will effectively protect our environment . "
