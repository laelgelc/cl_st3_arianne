<h> Scientists discover one of world 's oldest bird species at Waipara , New Zealand <p> The ancestor of some of the largest flying birds ever has been found in Waipara , North Canterbury . <p> Bony-toothed birds ( Pelagornithids ) , an ancient family of huge seafaring birds , were thought to have evolved in the Northern Hemisphere -- but that theory has been upended by the discovery of the family 's oldest , but smallest member in New Zealand . <p> At 62 million-years-old , the newly-discovered Protodontopteryx ruthae , is one of the oldest named bird species in the world . It lived in New Zealand soon after the dinosaurs died out . <p> While its descendants were some of the biggest flying birds ever , with wingspans of more than 5 meters , Protodontopteryx ruthae was only the size of an average gull . Like other members of its family , the seabird had bony , tooth-like projections on the edge of its beak . <p> The seabird fossil was identified by the same team that recently announced the discovery of a 1.6 meter-high giant penguin from the same site . <p> Amateur paleontologist Leigh @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Waipara Greensand fossil site .
