<h> The proposed bottle bill expansion puts a deposit on the wrong kinds of bottles . <p> By Mike Paine <p> Special to Hartford Courant <p> May 01 , 2019 6:00 AM <p> Workers gather crushed glass in bags with forklifts at the South Windsor Recycling facility . ( Peter Casolino / Special to the Courant ) <p> Legislation is being considered in Hartford to expand the list of beverage containers subject to the state 's container deposit law , but the bill would mean higher costs for cities and towns and a loss of jobs at Connecticut recycling facilities . <p> The bill puts a deposit on containers of most juices , teas and sports or energy drinks , and it increases the deposit to 10 cents from five , among other things . <p> Advertisement <p> This legislation is unnecessary . These containers are currently managed effectively and efficiently in Connecticut 's robust and well-established municipal and business recycling programs . The state 's own waste study concluded that a very large percentage of these non-deposit plastic and metal containers are currently being recycled in our systems and that only a very small percentage of them end @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and commercial recycling is one of Connecticut 's best-ever environmental programs . It recovers nearly one million tons of discarded materials annually . Recycling protects the environment , slows climate change and gets people involved every day -- thus raising overall environmental awareness . All three of these things are slam-dunk winners for the state and all who live and work in Connecticut . <p> There are thousands of people whose employment is financed by these containers . They make their living at the recycling facilities and at their sister waste hauling companies that we run all over the state . Expanding the bottle bill to these plastic and metal containers will mean loss of jobs for many of these employees , because people would take them to redemption centers instead of putting them in curbside bins . <p> The value of recyclable commodities has helped underwrite the cost of our industry 's delivery of municipal and business recycling services over the years . So , since the proposed beverage container deposit law expansion will take away many of the valuable containers found today in the recycling bins and barrels @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that we speak up now to stop this expansion . We are doing this to help keep important items in the recycling bin that have positive commodity value -- which are vitally needed today to keep recycling services available and affordable for all in Connecticut -- and to protect the jobs of our workers . <p> Connecticut has excellent recycling laws and regulations , excellent recycling collection and processing systems and well-established access to domestic and international markets for re-use of recyclables , including these beverage plastic and metal beverage containers . Our curbside and business programs have proven to provide recycling services without interruption for decades . Taking these containers out of your recycling bins and barrels makes little sense and has too many negative impacts . Connecticut should not expand the bottle bill for these containers . <h> Latest Op-Eds <p> To help recycling , the Connecticut container deposit law should be amended to include glass bottles -- wine and liquor bottles specifically . These glass containers , and others , break when they are managed in the recycling system -- that is when you put glass in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ with our trucks and when processed at the recycling plants we run with our elaborate equipment . <p> When glass containers break , all the different colors -- clear , brown , green -- become mixed . When mixed like this , getting glass recycled is very difficult and expensive . Also , broken glass in the recycling our system is a nuisance at least for homeowners and a downright nightmare for recycling center operators , due to workplace safety issues and wear and tear on equipment due to the abrasive nature of fine glass pieces . <p> So to help recycling in Connecticut , wine and liquor glass containers should be the target of the expansion , not plastic and metal containers not already on the state 's container redemption list . They are currently put in recycling bins and barrels , and they are being managed effectively and efficiently and not disposed . <p> Mike Paine is the president of Paine 's Inc .
