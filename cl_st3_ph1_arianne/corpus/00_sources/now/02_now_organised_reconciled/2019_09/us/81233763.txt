<h> Des Moines library program explores Northwest Passage in the era of climate change <p> David Thoreson will be at the Franklin Avenue Library on Tuesday at 6:30 p.m. to talk about his voyages in the Northwest Passage . ( Photo : DMPL/Special to the Register ) <p> Iowa native David Thoreson has taken a unique path to becoming one of America 's most prominent explorers . Thoreson grew up sailing on the windy waters of Lake Okoboji in northwest Iowa . He has since sailed more than 65,000 nautical miles across the globe . He 's crossed the Atlantic three times , traveled below the Antarctic Circle and above the Arctic Circle , and circumnavigated North and South America . <p> Thoreson will be at the Franklin Avenue Library on Tuesday at 6:30 p.m. to talk about his voyages in the Northwest Passage . He is the first American to successfully traverse the Northwest Passage in both directions and seen first-hand the impact climate change has had on the region . <p> In 1994 , Thoreson was part of a crew that had to retreat from a Northwest Passage voyage . " We got about halfway in , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ just walls of ice , " he said . 12 years later , they returned and completed the trip due to how much less ice there was . " Very small changes have very drastic results in the polar regions , " Thoreson said . " These small temperature ranges are melting a lot of ice . " <p> Thoreson , who is also a well-regarded photographer , will discuss his journeys and show stunning photos from his trips .
