<h> Camel Milk ATMs boost milk traders ' income in Wajir <p> In the parched and arid landscape where Hadado village is located in Wajir West Sub County lives 50-year-old Salada Yusuf , who derives her livelihood from trading in Camel milk . <p> Salada has been selling camel milk in Hadado for over 10 years . Like other women in her village , she relies on camel milk as a crucial source of income to support her nine children . <p> As climate change escalates worldwide , the frequency of droughts is making life harder for residents of Hadado like Salada . In 2011 , Wajir faced its worst drought in 60 years . This was exacerbated by a similar drought in 2016 , whose effects are still felt up to now . <p> During these severe droughts , livestock and crops die out and resources like water and camel milk become scarce . <p> " There is too much drought nowadays , " Salada says . We have no other option " , Salada exclaimed , when remembering the drought period . <p> As a milk trader , Salada relies on a difficult and labor-intensive process . First , young men from Hadado would ride motorcycles to where the camels graze on the outskirts of the village . It is always a race against the clock : These " boda-boda " riders have to fill several plastic containers with camel milk and hurry it back to Hadado before it spoiled . <p> Women like Salada then boiled the milk to preserve it . But the boiling process took several hours , destroyed precious nutrients and flavor , and the women who did it had to find their own firewood . <p> The milk was then transported on a public bus to Wajir town , nearly 50 miles away . This trip could take as long as three hours , exposing the milk to dangerously high temperatures .
