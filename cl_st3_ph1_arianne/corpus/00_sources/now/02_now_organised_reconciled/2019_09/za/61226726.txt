<h> News feed <h> Female dragonflies pretend to be dead to avoid unwanted attention from males <p> Research shows that female dragonflies say no to sex by faking their deaths . <p> September 13 , 2019 <p> When a female dragonfly 's eggs have been fertilized by mating with a male she gets ready to lay her eggs and tend to them . <p> While doing research on the effects of climate change on dragonflies in the Swiss Alps , a doctoral student in the Department of Evolutionary Biology and Environmental Studies at the University of Zurich made an astonishing discovery . <p> Rassim Khelifa was collecting larvae of the moorland hawker dragonfly one day when he saw a female dragonfly being chased by a male . Suddenly the female being pursued froze in midair and dropped to the ground landing on her back . The male , thinking she was dead , abandoned his pursuit and the female turned over and flew away , according to the research published in the online journal Ecology . <p> Apparently females of the species Aeshna juncea play dead to avoid copulating with other males once their eggs have been fertilized . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ environment , Khelifa observed 27 females crashing to the ground or into shrubs , feigning their deaths . Of those , 21 succeeded in tricking the males . In each of the fake death attempts the female had just left her eggs or was on her way to tend to them again . <p> All of the females that kept on flying when they were pursued by males were grabbed by the males in mid-flight . <p> When a female dragonfly 's eggs have been fertilized , any subsequent mating with aggressive males can harm her . <p> Khelifa reported that hawker male dragonflies , when copulating with a female , first extract the sperm of previous males . This can damage the reproductive tract of the female dragonfly . <p> The feigned death behaviour is the first observed in a dragonfly , but Khelifa suggests it may occur in other species of dragonfly .
