<h> Subscribe to industry newsletters <h> Food systems development needs a new trajectory - FAO director-general <p> Noting that food systems are huge economic , social and environmental drivers of transformational change , FAO director-general Qu Dongyu believes turning them into accelerators of progress on the SDGs is essential to achieving several core objectives of the 2030 Agenda . These include ending extreme poverty , addressing hunger an all forms of malnutrition , protecting and restoring the world 's biodiversity and natural resources , and combatting and building resilience to climate change . <p> To do this , he stressed , we need to put food systems development on a new trajectory that works with and for - and not against - the 2030 Agenda . <p> " We must consider all the aspects involved in food systems , the nutritional , the environmental , the cultural and the social , because ultimately they must benefit the health and welfare of people , " adding that transformational change must be underpinned by political will , investments and innovation , including technology . <p> The FAO director-general delivered his remarks at the high-level side event , " Countdown to 2030 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , during the 74th Session of the United Nations General Assembly . <h> Accelerators of the SDGs <p> Qu strongly underscored the need to turn food systems into accelerators of the SDGs . He warned about slowing progress against hunger and rapidly rising rates of overweight and obesity , the continuing degradation of the natural resource base upon which food production and vital ecosystem services depend , the increasing marginalisation of the small and family-run farms which represent most of the 570 million farms in the world , and increasing vulnerability to extreme weather events and impacts of climate change . <p> Today , one in every nine people in the world are hungry , even as the world produces more than enough food for everyone . Two in five adults are overweight , and obesity is on the rise in almost every country in the world . For many people , better food supply and nutrition is a question of livelihoods and incomes as well as the affordability of nutritious diets . <p> The projected growth in both demand and supply of food is uneven across countries and regions @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ - regions , which are expected to be the most affected by climate change . In this context , the FAO chief highlighted the need for the improved producer incentives and capacities in all regions that comes from access to markets , and the key role of international trade in filling the supply-demand gaps . It is critical that disruptions and uncertainty in food trade are avoided , Qu added . <p> The event was convened by FAO with the International Fund for Agriculture Development ( IFAD ) , the World Food Programme ( WFP ) , the World Economic Forum ( WEF ) , Ireland and the Kingdom of Norway . <h> Collective action needed <p> IFAD president Gilbert Houngbo said : " We need a collective strong action and urgent change to transform food systems and create opportunities for youth in rural areas . The food system can be a solution to transforming the agriculture value-chain to work for youth . We need to create a momentum to make sure that the food system is at the height of the agenda and includes everyone .
