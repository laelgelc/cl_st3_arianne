Photo : Darshan Devaiah B **26;2608;TOOLONG <p> Karnataka 's Kodagu district , the origin of the Cauvery river , is currently battling with floods after having experienced drought in May this year . Moreover , it received more than double of its average August rainfall in just nine days . <p> Between August 1 and August 9 , the district got 36.8 inches ( 920 millimetres ) of rainfall . The average rainfall for Kodagu in August is around 15 inches . <p> In August 2018 too , the district had received extremely heavy rainfall which caused disasters like landslides that killed 11 people . But even in that month , the rainfall was a mere 19 inches . <p> " Last August , the rain was incessant . The average rainfall in the month of July for the district is 18 inches ( 450 mm ) . <p> The underlying reason is that the catchment of the Cauvery has been unable to hold water . There are a variety of reasons for this development including degradation of the Cauvery 's catchment , Kodagu 's newly-established plantation economy , change in land-use patterns along the river , tourism , climate change and illegal sand mining . <p> Paddy farming has become unviable in the region as the cost of production for the crop has risen . Consequently , the numerous rice paddies which have dotted the region since centuries , are now diminishing . <p> For instance , in 2010-11 , 35,000 hectares ( @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and its tributaries -- Hemavati and Lakshmanteerth -- were under paddy cultivation . By 2018 , this had reduced to 27,000 ha . <p> These paddy fields had acted as natural feeders to the Cauvery . With paddy farming decreasing , the vacant land has either been diverted for non-agricultural use or for a plantation economy . <p> Karnataka 's plantation economy has been booming . The area under plantations of crops such as coffee , palm oil and areca nut has increased in the state . <p> However , the problem with these industry-driven plantations is that unlike traditional trees , they do n't have deep root and hence , can not hold soil or water . <p> " The coffee plant roots do not go deep enough to conserve or restore rain water , " said S Janakrajan of the Madras Institute of Development Studies . <p> " A whole lot of traditional trees like Nandi , Honne , Rosewood , Jackfruit and all varieties of bamboos have been removed for cultivation of coffee , " environmental activist Roy Bopanna said . <p> Another problem is the development @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ from Global Forest Watch , which collects annual data on global forests using satellite imagery , warns that the biodiversity of the Western Ghats , where the origin of the Cauvery lies , is under threat . <p> This report states that Kodagu lost around 3,000 ha of tree cover in the last 17 years . The impacts of decreasing tree cover are now visible as the Cauvery dried up in March-May . <p> For the passage of a transmission line from Kaiga in Uttara Kannada to Kerala via Kodagu and Mysuru districts , 50,000 trees were cut in Kodagu in 2015 . <p> Another 300,000 trees are to be cut for two upcoming projects including widening of a national highway and laying of a rail track . <p> The rise of urbanisation in the catchment area is another major challenge . Many of the private lands , which once carried trees , now have huge resorts , hotels or homestays . <p> All this has led to an influx of tourists , especially IT professionals from Bengaluru . The Madikeri taluka , where the Cauvery originates , has witnessed a sharp decline in rainfall -- by around 8 mm per year . <p> Consequently , there has been a rapid rise of bore wells for both agriculture and drinking water purposes .
