<p> New Delhi:Changing patterns of rainfall due to climate change could reduce the ability of soil to absorb water in many parts of the world , shows a new study . This can have serious implications on groundwater supplies , food production , storm-water runoff as well as biodiversity and ecosystems . <p> The observations were made in a study led by US based Rutgers University which was published in journal Science Advances . <p> " Since rainfall patterns and other environmental conditions are shifting globally as a result of climate change , our results suggest that how water interacts with soil could change appreciably in many parts of the world , and do so fairly rapidly , " said co-author Daniel Gimenez , a soil scientist at Rutgers University . <p> The research was conducted over a period of 25 years in Kansas and involved irrigation of prairie soil , associated with temperate grasslands with sprinklers . Scientists found that a 35% increase in rainfall led to a 21-33% reduction in water infiltration rates in soil and only a small increase in water retention . <p> The findings are supported by previous studies which show that water infiltration @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ increased rainfall , and climate change is expected to boost rainfall in many areas of the world . <p> " We propose that the direction , magnitude and rate of the changes should be measured and incorporated into predictions of ecosystem responses to climate change , " stated the researchers in the study . This can affect rain-fed agriculture in several parts of the world . <p> Whether rain water will penetrate the soil or run off determines how much water will be available for plants . <p> The study comes a year after a research published in peer journal Nature , which was also co-authored by Gimenez , showed that regional increases in precipitation due to climate change may lead to less water penetration , more runoff and erosion , and greater risk of flash flooding . <p> The biggest changes were linked to shifts in large pores in the soil .
