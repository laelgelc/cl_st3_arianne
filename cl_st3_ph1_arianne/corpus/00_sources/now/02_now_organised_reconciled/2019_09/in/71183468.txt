<h> Ten myths and fallacies about afforestation <h> Last Updated : Tuesday 17 September 2019 <p> Across the world , there is increasing awareness about the perils of an imminent climate change due to the unprecedented levels of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere , one factor being the rapid loss of green cover of the planet . <p> The easiest way to partly mitigate this juggernaut of weather variabilities is practically and intuitively to increase the tree cover on the land . From various studies and assessments , it is evident that while the plantation area in India is increasing since 1980 , its natural forest cover is going down . <p> Here are ten myths and fallacies about afforestation : <p> 1 . All that is green is useful : All trees are green and will increase the greenery on the planet surely and help absorb carbon dioxide . But extrapolating the fact that a plant out of its desired location is considered as a weed in agricultural lexicon , how can a non-native tree be considered as useful in the ecological context ? <p> Trees not only have a role in regulating the atmosphere but also the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ systems , toppling the existing symbiotic processes in the soil and leading to the loss of native fauna . <p> 2 . Greening where there is no green : Most parts of the Indian Subcontinent such as the Deccan Plateau are home to deciduous forests that shed leaves during the summer and are thus drought-resistant . <p> They adapt and help the ecosystems by not only consuming less water during the summer but also by shedding leaves , adding to the organic matter of the soil and retaining moisture during the monsoons . <p> Replacing these with evergreen trees will not only lead to depletion of ground water and rapid evapotranspiration but also render the land devoid of annual organic inputs from fallen leaves . <p> 3 . Afforestation increases forest cover but where will the land come from ? Most forests are cleared for mining , infrastructure and construction projects for burgeoning populations , and the question remains where will the replacement land for afforestation come from ? <p> 4 . Tree planting may not translate to recovery of green cover : Trees need several years to adapt and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ their survival if proper post-planting care is not scheduled . <p> Planting trees of one species often with a possibility of their use , makes them vulnerable to harvest at some point of time and will need a replanting . <p> 5 . Ten for one equation : The idea that ten trees are required to be planted when one is cut is an empirical fringe rule only to drive home the idea that replacing a mature tree is more difficult . <p> It does not mean that ten trees will replace the function of the lost one as in principle it will be lost . <p> 6 . Relocation of trees : It is always better to relocate a mature tree rather than cutting it as the time taken by the tree to mature can never be regained . However , this is seldom being done in India although we have newer technologies available ( though , a bit expensive ) . <p> The forest department should attempt to relocate trees close to their existence rather than cutting them and planting new trees . <p> 7 . Utility factor @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in terms of food , fibre , fodder or fuelwood . Some trees in the forest add to the health of the ecosystem . Most trees used in afforestation have some kind of human connection . Utilisation either for the locals or the corporates that take advantage of vacant land availability due to loss of forests is the norm . <p> In this process , native trees like figs , banyan , peepul and other wild trees endemic to a region are ignored , causing depletion in local biodiversity . Huge tracks of lands , mostly in the hinterlands are planted with commercial plantations to feed the ever-hungry paper and pulp industries . <p> These deplete the natural resources and groundwater at alarming rates and render the soil barren in a few years with little or no benefit to the indigenous communities and ecosystems . These areas usurped under commercial plantations as distinct from the natural forest are a very disturbing trend . <p> 8 . Foresting non-forested areas : Forests are a kind of ecosystem . Mistaking vacant lands as barren is a mistake . Barren lands , especially @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ biennials play a huge role in nurturing many kinds of insects and other organisms that help to sustain food chains . Replacing such grassy habitats/wetlands with trees may actually alter the ecosystems adversely . <p> 9 . Joint Forest Management ( JFM ) -- a panacea for all forest issues : Forests regenerated under the JFM would not be considered as plantations , but as regenerated natural forests , although of a low quality . <p> However , genuine community participation in planning , decision-making processes , implementation and ownership issues remain a big challenge which ultimately leads to the question , is the forest really regenerated ? <p> 10 . Natural forest and plantations mean the same : The assessment of forest cover is based on the interpretation of satellite data . Differentiating between natural forests and plantations is difficult because reflectance of the two is same . <p> Therefore , good data collection mechanisms for assessing actual forest covers are needed . A natural forest includes herbs , shrubs , creepers and trees as compared to only a few tree species during afforestation . Trees do not grow without inputs of soil , water and care . Most exotic tree species planted under the various plantation programmes consume a lot of ground water and compete with native species , which is detrimental to the local agro-ecology . <p> There is a need for more applied research and community-led forest conservation and management initiatives . Sound comparative valuations are needed for ecological services including their contribution to forest people 's livelihoods , promoting biodiversity , aesthetic , cultural and recreational values . <p> A forest can not be looked from merely the point of its utility to humans .
