<p> Bhimashankar natives are trying to grow the wild vegetables they consume in an attempt to conserve them <p> Vegetables that grow only in the wild and were traditionally the mainstay nutrients for tribal folks , have also been losing ground to climate change and other intrusions . While wild vegetable festivals have helped to revive interest these indigenous food , there is now an attempt by the tribal groups of Bhimashankar to domesticate these varieties by growing them in nurseries . <p> The effort is not just to work around the limited yield of the wild produce but also an attempt at letting some of the existing sites for those vegetables regenerate by freeing them from exploitation , say the people involved in the experiment . " These wild varieties are unique , given that they grow only at specific sections of the forest . Yet , we 've managed to identify about eight species that can be grown in nurseries and cultivated , " said Pradeep Chavan , aresident of Bhimashankar . <p> The efforts are now on to grow Dukhal , Lutichi bhaji , Kumbha , Bharangi , wild banana , Tondya , Chaya flowers , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Locals admit this is the first time such an attempt has been made . " These grow in specific seasons and in small numbers . If the seeds can be conserved for the next season , there can be an assured yield , " Chavan pointed out . <p> Aresident of Yelavali , Subhash Dolas , added , " The approach to harvesting these wild vegetables has also changed . We are now striving to harvest the vegetables cautiously so that the core roots or seeds are not damaged harming regrowth . " He further explained that there is also a discussion to cordon off certain patches of the forest so they are left untouched as an exercise in conservation . <p> The demand for these species wasbeen on the rise due to the wild vegetable festivals that were flagged off about five years ago , drawing in many urban consumers . I have witnessed how the locals scramble for their supply during the festivals when scores of outsiders come calling to partake in the fare . During monsoons , these vegetables become the primary source of tribal food in the absence of other resources , " said city-based environmentalist , Saili Palande Datar . <p> However , proponents of the festivals argue that they were needed at the time they were started . " The wild vegetable festivals were started to revive the interest of the local people in their native food . As the younger tribal lot started savouring urban cuisines , they lost the palate for their own food , " recounted Chavan . With the city dwellers taking to the millets and fresh vegetables , the tribal youth were awakened to the value of their traditional food .
