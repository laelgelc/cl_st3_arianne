<h> Artificial Tree To Help Curb Pollution <p> Trees are the best air purifiers in nature by far , and we already know that our best shot at beating climate change is by planting many more of them . The problem is they take too long to mature . But what if we could instead supplement them with artificial trees until then ? <p> Mexican startup BiomiTech believes it may have a way . In fact , they might have a solution that 's even better than nature 's . They 've developed an artificial device they claim is capable of scrubbing as much pollution from the air as 368 trees . <p> BioUrban , as the device is called , is 14 feet tall and is currently in its second working version . But though it has a metal skeleton that more closely resembles a lamppost , the real magic happens thanks to the microalgae the device houses in its " basket " . <p> " What this system does , through technology , is inhale air pollution and use biology to carry out the natural process ( of photosynthesis ) , just like a tree , " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ tree weighs about a tonne , can capture up to 99.7 per cent of PM2.5 and 10 particulate matter from the air , and is estimated to clean at least as much as one hectare of forest cover would . Effectively that 's a single device cleaning up the air for 2,890 people everyday . <p> The BioUrban is also equipped with solar panels to be energy self-sufficient , has built-in air sensors , and requires low maintenance . So not only are they more effective and quicker to set up than actual trees , they also as such require much less space for carbon purification farms . <p> So far , BiomiTech has installed three of these artificial trees . One is in the startup 's home city of Puebla in Mexico , the other is in Colombia , and the third in Panama . They are also in the process of setting up " trees " in other parts of the country . <p> They 're not cheap unfortunately , with each BioUrban 2.0 costing about $50,000 .
