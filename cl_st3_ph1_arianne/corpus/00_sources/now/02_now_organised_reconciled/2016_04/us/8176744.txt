<h> Federal agencies increase regulation of oil industry <p> The Wall Street Journal ran an op-ed piece on April 5 by Paul H. Tice with the headline " Kicking the Oil Business When It 's Down . " <p> Tice , senior managing director and head of the Energy Capital Group at USCA Asset Management LLC points out that crude oil prices have declined drastically and the industry is struggling . Thousands of workers have lost jobs , companies have cut budgets and tried to refinance debt , and bankruptcies are back in the news with more to follow . <p> " Despite the strategic importance of oil and gas production to the U.S. economy and even its national security , however , the Obama administration has continued to push its climate-change agenda , leading to new rules , regulations and restrictions on fossil fuels , " Tice wrote . " The net effect will be to raise companies ' operating costs and limit their financing options . " <p> Since 2015 , there have been an overwhelming number of new regulations proposed by bureaucratic agencies . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Clean Power Plan , ozone restrictions , a methane rule for new equipment that was quickly expanded to include all oil and gas operations , changes to the " waters of the U.S. " regulation , and it continues with its study of hydraulic fracturing . <p> The Bureau of Land Management has issued new drilling and hydraulic fracturing requirements on federal and Indian lands , which is being challenged in the courts . <p> The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service has hundreds of animals and plant species listing proposals that threaten development activities and a newly proposed definition of " critical habitat " that would greatly increase the agency 's authority to expand areas that receive designation . The agency justifies the change as being part of the administration 's climate change initiatives . <p> The Occupational Safety and Health Administration have modified several regulations that expand liability for violation of its rules throughout the oil and gas industry with criminal penalties in some cases . " Access to credit is the industry 's Achilles ' heel .
