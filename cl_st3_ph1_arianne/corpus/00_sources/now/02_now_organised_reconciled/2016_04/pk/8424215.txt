<p> A record-breaking heat wave claimed more than a thousand lives in Pakistan last year , frying much of the country , as well as causing extensive damage to a bulk of cotton crops and rice paddies . The catastrophe was exacerbated by prolonged power outages and an acute water supply crisis across four provinces . Even though armed forces as well as humanitarian organisations set up many relief camps to aid heat stroke victims in the affected areas , these initiatives could not make up for the glaring absence of any effective damage control policiesfrom governmental quarters . <p> A scrutiny into the quality of country 's defences against throes of extreme weather conditions would not have been necessary had the climatic conditions been any better this year . Pakistan'smeteorological department , for quite some time now , has been predicting an even more intense heat wave to scorch Pakistan this summer . Federal government should also pay heed to the impending disaster , and immediately step forward with its own set of regulatory reforms . <p> Amidst other equally important measures , the energy sector should be immediately directed to reduce the excessive load-shedding , already troubling residents in both urban and local areas .
