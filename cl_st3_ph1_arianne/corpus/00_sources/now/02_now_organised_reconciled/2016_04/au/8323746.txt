<h> Company tax cut wo n't boost national income <h> Company tax cut wo n't boost national income <p> A cut to the company tax rate will encourage investment , boost GDP -- a measure of Australia 's output -- and increase real wages . Citing these apparent benefits , the Business Council of Australia , KPMG and others advocate a cut as though it is in the national interest . <p> But a cut in company tax would reduce gross national income -- the part of GDP that accrues to Australians , and the best indicator of our material welfare . It would probably even reduce real after-tax wages . <p> The key to these outcomes is Australia 's dividend imputation system . As Paul Keating explained in the Sydney Morning Herald earlier this year , dividend imputation makes Australian shareholders indifferent to the rate of company tax . Whatever tax the companies pay , Australian shareholders get their share back in the form of franking credits . A cut to company tax is a tax cut for foreign shareholders , not Australians . <p> Clement Letters @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ foreign investment accrues largely to foreigners , limiting the benefit to Australia 's gross national income . At the same time , the cut in company taxes creates a budget problem . This must be addressed by a reduction in public services or increases in other taxes . <p> When increases in other taxes are factored in , wage earners may not be better off either . <p> In a balanced debate , the Australian public should not be misled that a policy that promotes GDP growth is necessarily a policy that raises national income . Treasury , KPMG and Centre of Policy Studies agree that a cut to company tax delivers growth in GDP and wages . These outcomes create the illusion of a benefit to Australians .
