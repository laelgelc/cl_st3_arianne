<h> WA coral not immune to bleaching event <p> Published : 8:30 am , Saturday , 16 April 2016 <p> Coral along Western Australia 's Kimberley coast is not immune to an unprecedented bleaching event affecting northern Australian waters , with a recent study discovering climate change may overwhelm the organisms ' unique defence system . <p> The James Cook University study found when corals were exposed to a slight rise in water temperature , they were able to use it as a ' practice run ' to prepare ahead of a significant increase . <p> Despite their defence mechanism , the research found corals are reaching a tipping point as temperatures continue to increase , from which they may not be able to recover . <p> The study looked at 27 years of satellite data for sea surface temperatures , previous coral bleaching events and how corals responded to different ocean warming conditions . <p> It found an increase of just 0.5C could be enough to overwhelm coral , leading to extensive bleaching around Australia . <p> Australia is also in the midst of the El Nino @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> University of Western Australia professor Malcolm McCulloch said the study was crucial in understanding coral 's tolerance to warming oceans . <p> ' In fact it now appears that what is happening in the Great Barrier Reef and also our reefs of North Western Australia , may even be worse than even these pessimistic findings , ' Prof McCulloch said . <p> The research predicted different sections of the Great Barrier Reef will lose their protective mechanisms at different rates . <p> ' Our models suggest reefs that lose their thermal protection in the future will degrade faster and stay in a degraded state for a longer period , ' Dr Juan Ortiz from Queensland University said . <p> ' While reefs that maintain their protection have a better chance of maintaining coral cover if carbon emissions are reduced in the near future . ' <p> The findings will help scientists better understand coral 's capacity to survive bleaching events .
