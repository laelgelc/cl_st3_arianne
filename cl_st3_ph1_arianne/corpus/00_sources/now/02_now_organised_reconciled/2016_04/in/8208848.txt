<h> Marathwada waiting for rain for an eternity <p> -- By Gayatri Ramanathan Apr 12 , 2016 12:18 am <p> This is the first of a series of write-ups by the FPJ City Editor , who took a drought trek in searing heat . <p> Aurangabad : As the train winds its way through the brown hay-strewn countryside , one can see farm hands bent over a basin washing their hands and faces , prelude to a much-needed lunch break in the baking hot sun . One after the other , all the men and women dip into the same basin , for that 's all the water there is -- a single basin for a group of 8-10 workers . <p> Welcome to Marathwada , a parched , rain-hungry land of which the natives have a saying that goes , " Those born in Marathwada are twice cursed , first by their fate , then by the land . " In the five years since 2012-13 , there has been one good monsoon in this land ( in 2013-14 ) , a part of which is irrigated @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , hilly terrain that the Maratha warriors of yore used to their advantage in harassing the mighty Mughal army . It is here that the last great Mughal Aurangazeb is buried , having spent a lifetime trying to quell the rebellion of Shivajirao Bhonsle and his clan . <p> But for all its historic glories , today the land is parched from five years of deficit rainfall : desperate are the farmers on verge of despondency , burdened by failing crops and mounting loans and a squabbling administration that has found an easy refuge in " climate change " for its political bickering in this former pocket borough of the Congress and the NCP . Says Praksah Dandegaonkar , former minister of state in the Vilasrao Deshmukh government and a big landholder in the Basmath <p> taluka of Hingoli district , " In all my 62 years , I have never seen a drought like this . There is no water in the ground , no water in wells or bore wells since December . This used to happen in March-April , not in December . We have repeatedly asked for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for drinking water . Over a 100 villages applied for tankers , only 22 have been sanctioned but we are yet to get any . Our women have to trudge up to 5km to get water for the household , " says the former NCP legislator who lost his seat in the last elections . His erstwhile supporters seem to be coming back as the present MLA , Jaiprakash Mundada of Shiv Sena , is seen as having turned his back on the people when they needed him the most . <p> " He could have asked the administration to release water from the 33-TMC Yeldari dam , but he did n't even give us a hearing , " says Mundaji Waghmare of Ganeshpur village , near Basmath . Waghmare owns about 50 acres of land and grows sugarcane and turmeric . <p> This year , the sugarcane crop , which usually yields up to 40 tonnes an acre , gave only 12 tonnes -- " not even our input cost , " points out Waghmare . Utaara , or cane juice , from the crop dropped from 110 kg/tonne to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the largest producer in the state , gave 50 kg/acre yield this year , as against a normal yield of 25 quintals/acre . <p> Reasons for this state of affairs range from fragmentation of land holdings ( average holding size has shrunk to 2-5 acres across the region ) to capital intensive farming practices which are no longer bringing in the expected rates of return due to that all-encompassing reason -- climate change . That the rainfall pattern is changing has been obvious for a while now -- the Indian Monsoon is no longer a gentle continuous shower of bounty , it has turned capricious , raining at will , as it were . <p> That the rural economy is unravelling through ignorance and neglect is obvious as one drives through these areas . Where once proud fruit orchards and stands of sugarcane and wheat stood , there is brown up-turned earth , waiting for the rains , which are at least two and a half months away . And there is no certainty that it will rain enough .
