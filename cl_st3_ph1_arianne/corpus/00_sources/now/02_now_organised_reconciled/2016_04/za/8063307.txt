The latest measurements form part of a multi-year trend of decreasing winter sea ice and experts now believe that the Arctic will experience completely ice-free summers within the next 20 to 25 years . <p> Disappearing sea ice in the northern polar regions adds to rising sea level and has significant consequences for climate patterns elsewhere . A recent study shows that it also contributes to increased surface ice melting in Greenland . According to the authors , " reduced summer sea ice favors stronger and more frequent occurrences of blocking-high pressure events over Greenland . Blocking highs enhance the transport of warm , moist air over Greenland , which increases downwelling infrared radiation , contributes to increased extreme heat events , and accounts for the majority of the observed warming trends " . The result : even more sea level rise . <p> On the other end of the planet , researchers are becoming increasingly aware of the fragility of Antarctica 's massive ice sheets .
