At least 90 per cent of the organisms that ever lived on the Earth are extinct . <p> Several times in the Earth 's history , significant numbers of species have been lost through extinction events , each taking place over a short time , geologically speaking . About 20 of these are known to have occurred in the last 550 million years . <p> Five so-called mass extinctions were particularly devastating , each eliminating more than 50 per cent of all species . Fossil evidence shows the most devastating was the Permian-Triassic extinction , colloquially known as the " Great Dying " , about 252 million years ago . This removed more than 95 per cent of marine species and greater than 70 per cent of land species . <p> Assigning @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Candidates include asteroid impact , volcanic eruptions , climate change and the catastrophic release of methane from deep-sea ices called methane clathrates . Many scientists think it is probably a combination of causes which end up dramatically changing the Earth 's climate . <p> Perhaps the best known mass extinction is the Cretaceous-Tertiary ( K-T ) one , 66 million years ago . There is very good evidence that an asteroid 14 kilometres across , crashed into the Earth generating an explosion equivalent to 10,000 times that of the total nuclear arsenal . <p> Recent research on mass extinctions was summarised by Howard Lee in Scientific American earlier this year . He reports on work carried out by a team from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology ( MIT ) , who think four out of the five mass extinctions were due to enormous volcanic outbursts called Large Igneous Provinces ( LIPs ) . <p> The researchers supported this theory with evidence that the Permian event was due to cataclysmic and widespread volcanic activity at the north of what was the supercontinent Pangaea . The huge quantities of carbon dioxide that entered the atmosphere as a result of the eruptions , gradually , over millennia , were responsible for powerful global warming . <p> The team also suggested the Deccan Traps LIP in India contributed to the K-T extinction , which was probably set off by the tectonic havoc wreaked by the asteroid impact . The MIT team offered no explanation for the earliest of the big five , the Ordovician-Silurian mass extinction , 443 million years ago ) . <p> There is a strong link between the extinction events and our evolutionary history . NASA advisers Robert Brownlee and Peter Ward suggest that mass extinctions remove resource-hogging organisms . These can be become dominant and entrenched , restricting biodiversity and competition , hence slowing down the rate of evolutionary development . <p> In effect , a mass extinction cleans the slate , creating new evolutionary niches which promote a wide range of species , increasing biodiversity , competition and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to carve out their niche in the new world . <p> We are an example of the aftermath of mass extinctions . Life for our ancient shrew-like mammalian ancestors was hard before the asteroid impact 66 million years ago . Dinosaurs ruled the Earth and mammals were on their menu . <p> As a result our ancestors were probably furtive creatures , living mainly underground , venturing above ground only at night . They were fur covered to keep their small bodies warm . After the asteroid impact and the dust had settled , the dinosaur threat was removed and mammals ruled the kingdom . Sixty-six million years later we are here , thanks to the K-T event . <p> Currently we are witnessing an ongoing mass extinction called the Holocene Extinction . The Holocene epoch began about 12,000 years ago when the Earth began to warm in the present interglacial time within the longer current ice age . <p> The cause of the mass extinction is the impact of humans on the environment . This would clear the landscape for another species to subsequently dominate .
