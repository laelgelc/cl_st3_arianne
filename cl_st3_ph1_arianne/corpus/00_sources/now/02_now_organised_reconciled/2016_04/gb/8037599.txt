<h> Churches , mosques and synagogues urged to unite to save the bees <p> Oxford environmental expert Jan McHarry has written a guide for churches , temples and mosques on how to be more bee-friendly and help save the threatened insects . She is pictured at St Mary and St John Church in the Cowley Road . <p> RELIGIOUS differences can be divisive but an Oxford ecologist wants to unite Muslims , Christians , Jews and Hindus with a common aim -- saving the bees . <p> Environmental expert Jan McHarry , from Botley , has written a guide for mosques , churches , synagogues and temples on how they can be more bee-friendly . <p> The 27-page guide which offers advice on what flowers bees like , how to plant them to attract pollinators and tips on recruiting volunteers to help . <p> Ms McHarry hopes her bee-friendly toolkit , Creating a Buzz , published by Oxford Friends of the Earth , will help create harmony - and honey - in the city . <p> The Friends of the Earth group said it focussed on faith groups @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and also because creating " bee-friendly spaces " can be a " creative expression of a faith 's values and ethical teachings " . <p> Ms McHarry said the group 's priority was to help save bees , which are threatened more than ever by habitat loss and climate change . <p> Speaking at the launch of the bee guide in March , she said : " We 've found that people are keen to plant flowers and shrubs to provide food and shelter for bees but often they do n't know where to find the information they need . <p> " We hope that the Creating a Buzz toolkit will increase awareness of what can be done . <p> " We look forward to seeing more bee-friendly green spaces developed across Oxfordshire . <p> " Bees are vital for our food production and biodiversity but bee-friendly spaces can appeal to people too , and do n't necessarily have to be large . <p> " Even a few containers of flowers or herbs can make a difference . " <p> The UK is home to more than 250 species of bee @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , but many of those are in rapid decline . <p> Wild honey bees are nearly extinct in many parts of the country and managed honey bee colonies fell by more than 50 per cent between 1985 and 2005 . <p> A quarter of bee species are on the national " red list " of threatened species . <p> Changes in agriculture are a major cause of bee decline , and other factors include pesticides , loss of habitat and climate change . <p> Oxford Friends of the Earth hopes that churches , mosques and temples across cities like Oxford can create a green network in urban areas which can sustain bee populations . <p> St Mary and St John Church on Cowley Road has already started working with the group . <p> Ruth Conway , who co-ordinates a project to maintain and improve the churchyard , said : " Involvement in Creating a Buzz has enabled us to do more wildflower planting and to host a walk led by a local expert who gave us fascinating insight into the bees found in the churchyard .
