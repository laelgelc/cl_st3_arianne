<p> Limiting the take of just one type of fish could protect coral reefs around the world from the most serious immediate impacts of climate change , researchers have found . <p> Studying Caribbean coral reefs , Peter Mumby and colleagues from the University of Queensland found that enforcing a rule limiting the fishing of a single type of herbivorous fish -- parrotfish -- would allow coral reefs there to continue to grow , despite bleaching and other impacts associated with climate change . <p> Coral reefs damaged by bleaching or storms can recover when new baby corals settle and grow on the dead old corals . But the new recruits must compete with seaweed . If the seaweed outcompetes the coral , the reef can be lost forever , transforming into a seaweed-dominated ecosystem , where most of the biodiversity is lost . <p> Banning the taking of parrotfish under 30cm , and limiting total catch to 10% of the mass of the total population , would allow the reefs to cope with climate change until at least 2030 , the researchers found . <p> The Caribbean @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ were not directly applicable to Australian reefs , Mumby said . <p> " It 's relatively simple -- it has one tenth the number of species of coral and fish than we have here in Australia , " he told Guardian Australia .
