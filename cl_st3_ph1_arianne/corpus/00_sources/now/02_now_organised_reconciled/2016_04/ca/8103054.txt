<p> What an appropriate headline for Paul McLennan 's most recent column . In it , Mr. McLennan again argues against spending money to reduce CO2 emissions . He doubts that our use of fossil fuels is a significant contributor to climate change . He identifies himself as a " climate denier . " <p> Making sense of " climate denier " claims is a worthy activity . I spent my professional life teaching students the skills needed to make sense of science . That includes finding and using reliable information to inform your stance on important and controversial issues . <p> For the record , I hereby confirm that I do not find " climate denier " claims to be sensible . But I do n't think this makes me a " climate change fear monger " ( Mr. McLennan 's words for people who disagree with his views ) . Could we " climate acceptors " be referred to as " my honorable opponents " in future ? <p> Now , to the sense-making . <p> Mr. McLennan proposes that we should always follow the money @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ very active industry which generates information ( or misinformation ) to support the " climate denier " perspective expounded in Mr. McLennan 's columns . <p> Who pays for the conferences , the " talking heads , " the publications and the media productions of the " climate denier " movement ? Could its major financiers possibly be linked to the fossil fuel industry ? Are they folks whose source of wealth would be adversely affected by measures to reduce CO2 emissions ? Are such people likely to encourage a shift to renewable energy sources ? <p> We do n't have to wonder about this question . It has been answered . For a readable recent report , check out the book " Dark Money " by Jane Mayer . She details the persistent efforts and mind-boggling expenditures of Charles and David Koch ( aka the Koch brothers ) to fuel public distrust and legislative paralysis of action on climate change . <p> Follow the money . Does it make sense that these two shadowy American multi-billionaires , custodians of an industrial empire built on coal , would resist any change @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ideas from the think tanks , conferences , and publications which they and their friends finance ? <p> It 's always good to be skeptical . But amongst working researchers there is overwhelming agreement . About 97 per cent of current reports about climate change in the scientific press endorse a human contribution to global warming . Not much doubt there , even if the details are n't all worked out . <p> Which leaves us with the " so what ? " question . If scientists are right , and human activities contribute to an increasing rate of climate change , so what ? <p> Should we do anything about it ? That 's a moral question , not a scientific one . Should we do what we can to moderate our industrialized , fossil-fueled way of living , even though it might not be sufficient ? <p> Do we want to do anything about it ? That 's a question of individual and political will . That 's something for each of us to ponder and decide .
