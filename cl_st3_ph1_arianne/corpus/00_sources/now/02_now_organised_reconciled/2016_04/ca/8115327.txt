<h> News Local <h> Huron/Georgian Bay at 1998 levels and rising <p> A sign warns of low water levels at the Owen Sound Eastside Boat Launch Wednesday but April levels are the highest since April 1998 , thanks mostly to early snowmelt and lots of rain . Waters have been rising for two years in the bay following below-average levels for some 15 years . ( Scott Dunn/The Sun Times , Owen Sound ) <p> Georgian Bay and Lake Huron are 32 centimetres about the longterm average for this time of year and 13 cm higher than this time last year , lake level expert Frank Seglenieks said Wednesday . <p> Levels are the highest for this time of year in those connected bodies of water since 1998 , the year which roughly marked the tail end of a period of high lake levels . A slow decline in levels followed over the next 15 years , when lake levels were trending below the average . <p> All the Great Lakes have risen over the past two years and are " definitely above average , " Seglenieks said @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> " So we 've kind of gone back to the levels . . . at the end of the high water periods back in the ' 90s , " Seglenieks said . <p> What stands out for Seglenieks , a water resources engineer with the boundary water issues unit of Environment and Climate Change Canada , is the level rose 14 centimetres in February and March in Huron and Georgian Bay , when on average it rises 4 cm in that period . <p> That 's mostly because March 's spring-like temperatures sped up the snowmelt and there was lots of rain too -- it was almost twice as wet as the longterm average in the basin , which includes Lake Michigan , Lake Huron and Georgian Bay , Seglenieks said . <p> That may mean less of a rise in the weeks ahead due to spring rain and melting but Seglenieks said even if there were average conditions , the lakes ' seasonal rise will add 25 cm more by the middle of summer . <p> And even if spring and summer are dry , local lake levels @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " <p> Seglenieks attributes the rising lake levels to weather pattern changes which brought more precipitation . And it 's not all attributable to the El Nino effect , which is often cited for extreme weather changes , he said . <p> That effect refers to the influence of warmer waters in the Pacific Ocean off South America on the jet stream and on weather patterns . <p> " Because it 's been going on for a couple of years now , the higher water levels have started before the El Nino started . And El Nino peaked last November and it affects more the temperature , " as reflected in the mild winter we just experienced , he said . <p> " So it 's a general pattern also for the last couple of years of higher precipitation . And that 's just something that has happened and maybe in 10 , 15 years they might be able to look back and relate it to something else . But right now , that 's just the way it is. "
