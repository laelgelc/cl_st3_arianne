<p> Moving from a meat-based to plant-based diet globally could lower the mortality rate and slash greenhouse-gas emissions . <p> A striking new study -- but one that is bound to prove controversial -- has provided a calculation of both the health benefits and the reductions in planetary greenhouse gases that might be achieved if the world shifted away from meat-based diets . <p> The results , while theoretical in nature , certainly make a strong case for treating the food system , and animal agriculture in particular , as a key part of the climate change issue . Namely , the researchers find shifting diets toward eating more plant-based foods on a global scale could reduce between six and 10 per cent of mortality -- saving millions of lives and billions of dollars -- even as it also cuts out 29 to 70 per cent of greenhouse gas emissions linked to food by the year 2050 . <p> " Dietary change could have large health and environmental benefits , " says Marco Springmann , the lead author of the new study in the Proceedings of the National @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ University ( all four researchers involved in the work were from Oxford ) . Ruminant animals , such as cattle , belch methane into the air as part of their process of digestion .
