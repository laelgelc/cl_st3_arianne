<h> Ireland sign climate deal on World Earth Day <p> Ireland is just one of over 160 countries pledging to reduce greenhouse gas emissions rapidly , in a signing ceremony that is taking place on 22 April at the United Nations headquarters in New York . <p> Ireland is due to sign the Paris Agreement , as set out by the 2015 Paris Climate Conference ( COP21 ) , in a large ceremony overseen by UN Secretary General , Ban Ki-moon . <p> More than 160 governments from around the world are expected to sign the agreement to limit global temperature rises to less than two degrees above the temperature in pre-industrial times . However , there are no penalties in place for those countries who miss their targets . <p> Brendan Heneghan , Interim CEO of the Irish Wind Energy Association ( IWEA ) welcomes the signing of the agreement , stressing the need for governments around the world to think about future generations . <p> " The signing this week of the Paris Climate Change Agreement by global leaders is pivotally important in that it will @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ as we look to reduce our greenhouse gas emissions and implement innovative processes to protect our environment for future generations . " <p> Newly released figures by the IWEA show that Carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) emissions produced as a result of electricity generation in Ireland has decreased by 29% over the past 15 years despite a 19% increase in electricity consumption over the same period . <p> The IWEA say that this CO2 reduction equates to approximately 1.3 million roundtrip flights between New York and Beijing . <p> The signing of the Paris Agreement on Friday also coincides with World Earth Day . The theme for 2016 is ' Trees for the Earth ' . <p> According to the organisers the planet is losing 15 billion trees every year . The aim of the network is to plant 7.8 billion trees by Earth Day 2020 . <p> Events have been organised throughout the country celebrating World Earth Day including ' Earth Fest ' , a festival which takes place from 22-24 April in Slane Co Meath .
