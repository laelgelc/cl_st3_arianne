<h> SRJS project campaign in 21 villages tames degradation in Lake Rukwa <p> VILLAGERS along Lake Rukwa in Sumbawanga District of Rukwa region have embarked on a new campaign aimed at saving Lake Rukwa from disappearing due to uncontrolled human activities . <p> Two members of Upper Lwiche Water Users Association hanging beehives on a tree for their beeking project to protect the river that feeds Lake Rukwa . <p> They are residents of 21 villages of Sumbawanga District , who have developed a number of strategies to protect Lake Rukwa and water sources that flow directly into the Lake , which was damaged by farmers conducting agricultural activities and cattle grazing . <p> Through a five- years project ' dubbed ' Shared Resources , Joint Solutions ( SRJS ) project funded by the International Union for the Conservation of Nature ( IUCN ) National Committee of the Netherlands and World Wildlife Fund ( WWF ) also of the Netherlands , the communities were given tree seedlings for planting to protect water sources and also obtained modern beehives to engage in beekeeping activities . <p> So far a total of 5900 Acacia melanoxylon tree species have been planted along Lwiche River Basin , the main water source and biggest river that flows directly into Lake Rukwa . <p> About 32 modern beehives were donated to three villages and they are now engaging in beekeeping activities . <p> Among the beehives , 14 modern beehives were donated to communities at Malagano village , 12 at Pito village and the remaining six at Katumba village in Sumbawanga district . <p> Speaking in an interview over the phone , Shared Resources , Joint Solutions ( SRJS ) Programme Coordinator , and Andrew Mariki says the main goal is to conserve the lake environment , protect water sources , prevent deforestation while earn money to improve livelihoods and control human activities like pastoralism , fishing and agricultural activities along the lake basin . <p> Villagers decided to embark on this campaign after discovering that a large part of the lake has been reduced to muddy water due to siltation and other environmental degradation taking place in the upland area including water sources that flow directly into Lake Rukwa . <p> Before the intervention , the lake was in danger to disappear due to uncontrolled human activities including massive cattle grazing . The water level in the Lake has decreased by four metres to 9 metres in different parts of the lake . <p> The lake depth significantly dropped from 9.5 metres to 3.4 metres in the last 10 years , according to the 2014 Bathymetric Survey , which was jointly funded by the United States Agency for International Development ( USAID and Global Water for Sustainability . <p> Mariki said with funding from IUCN National Committee of the Netherlands and WWF the Netherlands , the five year project has witnessed significant progress from local communities involved in saving Lake Rukwa , improve biodiversity of the lake and restore water sources damaged by farming activities . <p> So far 21 villages of Sumbawanga District have been developed a number of strategies , including forming two water user associations to protect Lake Rukwa and water sources that feed the lake . <p> " Upper Lwiche Water Users Association and Lake Shore Water Users Association are the main water associations formed by villagers with support from SRJS project , in collaboration with the Lake Rukwa Basin Water Board of the Ministry of Water and Sumbawanga District . " the coordinator said . <p> The core function of the associations is to manage and conserve water sources , collect water users fees from each water user in collaboration with government as well as coordinate various projects in- terms of environmental and water resources . <p> The associations will ensure the livestock populations does not destroy water sources feeding Lake Rukwa . <p> They will conserve the biodiversity of the lake and prevent human activities in- terms of pastoralism , fishing and agriculture along the water basin . <p> The villages have developed an Action Plan for 2020 that will guide them on how to protect Lake Rukwa and receive 1m/- from the SRJS project to implement their planned activities . <p> " As SRJS we are very glad for this initiative because right now the associations have formulated by-laws that guide them to protect Lake Rukwa , water sources that feed Lake Rukwa and arrest people who violate the laws , by forming a patrol team . " he elaborated . <p> " Right now villagers are carrying out regular patrols in water sources that feed Lake Rukwa and we have a number of people from 12 villages who are engaging in beekeeping project just to reduce pressure from the Lake and put bread on the table . " he elaborated <p> Through SRJS project , WUA 's leaders were trained on financial management and how to come up with new projects that in turn will help them to get more funds to implement their planned activities . <p> The training was offered free of charge so that they use their obtained funds from what they did and benefit from it . <p> He says all this has been made possible through a five year project ' Shared Resources , Joint Solutions ( SRJS ) project funded by IUCN National Committee of the Netherlands and WWF the Netherlands . <p> Currently the project is implemented by ActionAid Tanzania , Haki Ardhi and the Lawyer 's Environmental Action Team ( LEAT ) in collaboration with Nsimbo , Mpimbwe and Sumbawanga district councils in Rukwa and Katavi Regions . <p> It is being implemented in 10 villages that include Nankanga , Ilemba , Ilalanguru , Mirumba , Kalaela , Sitalike , Matandalani , Majimoto , Ilambila and Ng'ongo villages in Rukwa and Katavi regions . <p> " Through the project villagers were trained on conserve their natural resources that is the main source of their daily needs , they also learnt gender equality issues , land use planning , Land rights , women 's land rights and how they can access legal aid services to solve challenges facing them . " The activist underline . <p> The main goal was just to help villagers save Lake Rukwa that is in danger of disappearing , improve biodiversity , combat climate change , enhance food security and ensure equal water distribution as the majority of Rukwa residents and other people depend on lake Rukwa for fishing , agricultural activities and transportation services . <p> " We have decided to introduce this project in both regions after discovering that Lake Rukwa was in danger of disappearing due to on-going agricultural activities and cattle grazing operations . Residents are in danger of being affected by mercury that get into lake Rukwa through artisanal small scale gold mining area . That is very dangerous for the population and wider ecology of the area . " He stated . <p> In a bid to reduce water pollution in the lake Rukwa and deforestation in Rukwa region , ActionAid Tanzania , Haki Ardhi , Lawyer 's Environmental Action Team ( LEAT ) have trained villagers on natural resource management , land rights , gender equality issues and how to reduce deforestation in areas where they conduct farming activities . <p> Before this intervention , the lake was in danger of disappearing due to on-going agricultural activities , cattle grazing operations while residents are in danger of being affected by mercury that get into lake Rukwa through artisanal small scale gold mining area that is very dangerous for human and species found in the lake , he reiterated . <p> Also the environmental situation in Lake Rukwa was not good as water pollution was very high and the lake ecosystem was extensively damaged , he pointed out . <p> Deforestation at Nankanga and Ilemba villages was high as villagers cut forests to provide more room for planting crops or grazing livestock , along with gathering wood to build houses . <p> Before the various NGOs began to provide advocacy and training on those issues , they conduct researched in Katavi and Rukwa region in 2017 . <p> The research findings showed that both in Katavi and Rukwa region there are large stretched of natural resources including indigenous forests especially in Katavi National Park , Uwanda Game Reserve , Lukwati -- Piti Game Reserve and Lwafi game Reserve that need to be protected and conserved . <p> Population growth is the main cause of deforestation because people engaged in farming activities and wood production . <p> " We want farmers to embark on farming activities that are friendly to the environment that will reduce water pollution in Lake Rukwa including water sources , " he added .
