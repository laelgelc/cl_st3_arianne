<p> But as a generation of forward thinkers , we also often consider how we can best prepare for the future , ranging from climate change issues to financial preparedness . <p> I was born in 1997 , which means I fall right on the cusp between the Gen Z and millennial generations . Yet while I definitely identify with certain aspects of the millennial mindset , I more closely relate to the younger generation . <p> This year , instead of being claimed by my parents , I filed my own taxes for the first time . My personal income qualified me to receive a stimulus check as part of the government 's coronavirus stimulus package . <p> I am fortunate I still have a job and can work from home , but during such an uncertain time , I wanted to use the money to set myself up for the best financial future . <h> After receiving the check , the first thing I did was start a budget for how I would use the money <p> In researching the best ways to start a budget , I came across a budgeting technique called the 50/30/20 method . <p> According to this method , 50% of your monthly income should go toward things you need -- rent , groceries , transportation -- and 30% should go toward things you want , such as new clothes , nights out with friends , and other " luxuries . " <p> Experts recommend the remaining 20% of your income be put into a savings account . <h> I focused on my ' needs ' first : I paid off my credit-card balances and set aside some money for my next month of rent <p> As a young person , I 've always lived in the moment , and this applied to my finances . <p> Before the coronavirus pandemic , the only expense I specifically allotted money aside for in each paycheck was my monthly rent and utility bill . I did n't worry too much about where the rest of my money went , even though I had credit cards yet to be paid off from years ago . <p> The very first thing I did with the $600 I allotted for my needs was to pay off my small credit-card bills . The immediate relief I felt after doing so made me feel so much more in control of my finances . <p> While I understand that many people need the stimulus check to pay for immediate needs , such as groceries , gas , and mortgage payments , being able to pay off my credit cards was something I 've always needed to do but simply had n't been able to without seriously cutting into my monthly budget . <h> I opened my very first savings account <p> caption <p> I opened a Wells Fargo Way2Save savings account . <p> source <p> Erin McDowell/Business Insider <p> I needed to deposit only $240 of the $1,200 check into my savings account to fulfill the 50/30/20 method 's savings requirement . But I decided to put in a little more right off the bat , and set up twice-monthly automatic deposits of $50 into my savings . <p> My " wants " while quarantining have been significantly less than usual . I 'm not going out to restaurants , buying new clothes , or spending money that I normally would on weekends out with friends , since we 're all staying safely indoors . Rather than putting 30% of my check on " wants , " I added a bit more to my savings . <p> Having a couple hundred dollars in my savings account may not seem like much , but growing a savings account is something I 've never had the extra money to do . Being able to have a nest egg of emergency funds makes me feel a lot more financially secure as a recession becomes increasingly likely . <h> I still made sure to set aside a small amount of money for ' wants ' <p> Social distancing and stay-at-home restrictions have meant many restaurants , shopping centers , and other businesses have been forced to temporarily close . Yet this wo n't always be the case . <p> I made sure to set aside a small amount of money -- about 15% of the check -- for things I will undeniably want to purchase after the quarantine ends , things that people of every generation undeniably enjoy , like movie tickets , birthday gifts , dinners out , and trips to the beach . <p> Even though not much of my stimulus-check money was left for luxuries , there 's enough to guarantee a fun night out once things return to normal . <h> I feel a lot more prepared financially than I ever have <p> Receiving the stimulus check was a happy surprise . Without it , I 'm not sure I would 've had the motivation to take control of my savings and debt . But even though the stimulus-check money was a one-time thing , I want to continue the same practices and continue with the 50/30/20 method . <p> In these uncertain times , it 's a relief to know that I have made a step toward being debt-free and having a small amount of money for emergencies -- no matter what happens . <p> Do you have a personal experience with the coronavirus you 'd like to share ?
