<h> Zimbabweans go hungry as coronavirus compounds climate woes <h> Tuesday May 19 2020 <p> Rosemary Pamire cleans her home in Harare , Zimbabwe , on May 9 , 2020 , during a nationwide lockdown to help curb the coronavirus disease ( Covid-19 ) spread . PHOTO PHILIMON BULAWAYO REUTERS <h> In Summary <p> Before the coronavirus outbreak , 7.7 million Zimbabweans faced food shortages after a drought and cyclone in 2019 and patchy rains this year , linked to climate change and worsened by rampant inflation and a foreign exchange shortage . <p> Now it faces a triple threat of climate breakdown , monetary woes and a new economic crisis caused by the lockdown . <p> Rosemary Pamire struggled to feed her family well before Zimbabwe entered lockdown in March to combat the coronavirus pandemic . Now she can hardly put together a meal a day as the country faces a deepening food crisis . <p> Sitting on a bed in her two-room lodgings in Harare 's poor Mbare township , Pamire told Reuters she had exhausted the little food she had stocked up during the first 21 days of an extended seven-week lockdown . <p> " We just eat once a day now . I wish the government could give us food to feed my family , " Pamire said . <p> Before the coronavirus outbreak , 7.7 million Zimbabweans faced food shortages after a drought and cyclone in 2019 and patchy rains this year , linked to climate change and worsened by rampant inflation and a foreign exchange shortage . <p> Now it faces a triple threat of climate breakdown , monetary woes and a new economic crisis caused by the lockdown . <h> Related Stories <p> The government 's latest figures show that 8.5 million Zimbabweans are now food insecure , while international aid agencies say up to 45 million people face hunger in southern Africa due to climate-induced food shortages . <p> The government has promised a food grant of $2.4 billion Zimbabwe dollars ( $96 million ) targeting 1 million people for six months , without saying where it would get the money . <p> It is pleading with donors , who would normally be reluctant to help because of its debt arrears , and this month it received $7 million from the World Bank . <p> Pamire said she had registered with social welfare officials but she , like many others , has yet to receive anything . <p> That has left the burden to fend for the family with her 19-year-old daughter Anna , who sells ice lollies and bottled water at Mbare vegetable market at the risk of arrest by police because it is illegal . <p> " At times in the evening when we do n't have maize-meal , mum will just tell us to have the ice lollies and water and we will just go to sleep , " said Anna . <p> On a good day Anna sells a pack of ice lollies for 110 Zimbabwe dollars ( $4.40 ) . After buying new stock , only $1 is left for the family of seven to buy food , including the staple maize-meal and sugar and cooking oil . <p> Pamire , who lives with her four grown children and two grandchildren , used to buy clothes and shoes from Zambia to resell at home and earned $100 after a good trip . But the border is closed , her passport expired , and she does not have money to renew it . <p> The market where Pamire 's two adult sons carted goods around for a fee has been shut for six weeks , just like all informal markets from where millions of Zimbabweans were earning a living .
