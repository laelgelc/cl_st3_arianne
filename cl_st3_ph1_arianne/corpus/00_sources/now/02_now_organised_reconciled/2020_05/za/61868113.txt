the coronavirus pandemic . <p> That is the view of former Eastern Cape health MEC Dr Bevan Goqwana , who after years in the wilderness has returned to assist the provincial health department in its battle against Covid-19 . <p> Goqwana was controversially axed in April 2006 by then premier Nosimo Balindlela after he fired his superintendent-general , whom he accused of mismanagement . <p> Her decision to sack Goqwana drew stinging criticism from the ANC at the time . <p> Since his axing 14 years ago , Goqwana has been practising as a medical doctor in Mthatha . <p> The then Eastern Cape ANC chair , Makhenkesi Stofile , slammed Balindlela for not informing senior members of the provincial ANC structures of the move . <p> Since his axing 14 years ago , Goqwana has been practising as a medical doctor in Mthatha . <p> His return to Bhisho last week is to shore up systems the department is putting in place to manage the virus , particularly in the rural areas where stigmas around Covid-19 are hampering efforts to curb its spread . <p> Since the virus entered districts like OR Tambo , the pandemic has caused widespread panic among residents . <p> Stigmas similar to those around HIV/Aids when it first emerged in SA have skyrocketed , the most challenging of which is the notion that Covid-19 is an automatic death sentence . <p> Goqwana has been studying both the country and province 's pandemic response from the sidelines and , now that he is back in the fold , hopes to assist in removing these fears and providing advice that can result in concrete solutions . <p> One of the points he wants to get across is that health officials should understand that not every situation is the same , and each should be treated on individual merit . <p> With winter approaching , Goqwana believes efforts to convince people in the rural areas of the facts of the virus need to be redoubled . <p> " People have stigmatised the virus and as a result people are scared . That needs to change because white blood cells go down with stress , so you are more vulnerable . <p> " I was dealing with somebody who just slept next to somebody who is positive . She was so scared , she could n't sleep at night , thinking that she has got it Covid-19 from this person . Yet they were two metres apart . <p> " We need to reduce this anxiety . I think it 's coming from the fact that people do n't understand what this virus is and what it does . " <p> Funerals , in OR Tambo in particular , have been a major source of infection in the province , and Goqwana is aiming to get the department more involved in how these are managed . <p> " We need to get to the point that if there is going to be a funeral , some days before that funeral , a team from health will assist them families with sanitiser and tell them what to do when there is a problem . " <p> It was inevitable that winter would bring more infections , but wisdom should not be sacrificed for fear , he said , adding that people should still take the virus seriously . <p> Another line Goqwana is pursuing is encouraging doctors , both in the private and public sectors , to treat patients over the age of 55 at home . <p> " Admitting them to hospital is a problem because you are sending them to a lot of people . So you rather treat the elderly , especially those who have comorbidities , at home . <p> " We 've got a process which goes from a GP to testing , and then to a hospital and quarantine if they are positive . If the person is negative , you give them whatever antibiotics so they can go home . <p> " I am trying to make sure this is going to be done by the private sector and the public sector in unity so that we can remove the stigma . " <p> As someone who was " born and brought up " in the Eastern Cape , Goqwana believes he is in a good position to understand the dynamics of different situations , whether they occur in the metros or rural areas . <p> Not that the former MEC , who occasionally serves on health portfolio committees , has entirely forgotten his political past and the events of 14 years ago . <p> " I was an MEC and I served there for seven years and with all the difficulties . I did what I did with a passion . <p> " The way I was removed ... was so bad but I decided to keep quiet because it was all about doing it for the people . There have been seven MECs after me . <p> " I 've been looking at how things are happening . <p> " I do n't want to comment much , but because I was removed I thought ' there was probably something wrong that I did , so let me keep quiet ' . But you know , politics is politics . " <p> Goqwana said he would not forget , but he could forgive . <p> " It 's not about me , it 's about the country , it 's about the province . " <p> On Thursday , the World Health Organisation cautioned that the virus might linger for a long time . <p> " It is important to put this on the table -- this virus may become just another endemic virus in our communities , and this virus may never go away , " Mike Ryan , head of the WHO emergency response team , said . <p> This is an opinion Goqwana seems to concur with . He said as time passed , the virus could be so mild that it 's just another infection that one can develop and recover from . <p> " While one is not really sure how antibodies form , there are certain people who are going to develop antibodies to it . <p> " When they 've developed antibodies , it wo n't be as serious as it is now . The worry is the elderly and those who have comorbidities . <p> " Sometimes with the viruses , they come as a parasite , and being a parasite , it ca n't kill everybody because it has to survive itself . If it kills everybody it will die , so it ca n't kill everybody . " <p> He was also convinced that the coronavirus would not be the only one to affect the world . <p> " Two , three years down the line you might find another virus , the existing one mutating or a new virus , especially with the climate change that is happening . <p> " We need our scientists to be on their toes , or try to be ahead of some of these micro-organisms. "
