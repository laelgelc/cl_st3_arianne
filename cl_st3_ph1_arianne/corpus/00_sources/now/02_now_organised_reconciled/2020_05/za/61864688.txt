<h> Sasol opens bids for clean energy producers to supply its plants <p> 18:28 14/05/2020 Sibongile Khumalo <p> Sasol , one of the country 's biggest emitters of greenhouse gases , has invited bids from independent power producers for the supply of renewable energy to its local operations in a move to reduce emissions . <p> The petrochemicals company , which operates mega facilities in Sasolburg and Secunda , where it converts coal into fuel and chemicals , said it had its sights on wind and solar energy producers with a generation capacity of at least 20 MW . <p> " We intend procuring , in total , approximately 600 MW of renewable electricity capacity , with the aim of reducing our greenhouse gas emissions by approximately 1.6 million tons per annum , " said Chief Sustainability Officer , Hermann Wenhold . <p> He added that the process would enable Sasol to deliver on its promise to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by at least 10% by 2030 . He was referring a commitment by the company in a Climate Change Report released in 2019 , where Sasol said it would reduce its absolute greenhouse gas emissions from SA operations by at least 10% , coming off a 2017 baseline . <p> " The company has identified renewable energy as a key lever for reducing its GHG emissions and moving it towards producing products in a more sustainable manner , " said Wenhold . <p> The Climate Change Report acknowledged that the company 's coal-intensive Secunda operations , and its significant role in the company 's greenhouse emission footprint , shaped its climate change management response . <p> It added that improvements in the Secunda emissions profile would require the introduction of lower or low-carbon energy sources , such as natural gas or renewable energy . <p> A government-imposed Carbon Tax Act came into effect on 1 June 2019 . Its first phase runs from 2019 to 2022 . The bill , which is with aligned with South Africa 's commitment to the Paris Agreement , set the tax at R120 per ton of carbon dioxide . <p> In its inaugural Climate Change Report , Sasol put its liability at an estimated R800 million to R1 billion in 2020 , escalating at consumer price index plus 2 percentage points until 2022.
