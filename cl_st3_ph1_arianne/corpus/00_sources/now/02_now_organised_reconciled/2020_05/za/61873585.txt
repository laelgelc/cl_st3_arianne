<h> News feed <h> Mass evacuations for major cyclone in South Asia <p> Millions of people were being moved to safety as one of the fiercest cyclones in years barrelled towards India and Bangladesh on Tuesday , but with evacuation plans complicated by coronavirus precautions . <p> by Sailendra Sil with Abhaya Srivastava in New Delhi and Shafiqul Alam in Dhaka Kolkata ( AFP ) 19 May 2020 7:53 <p> " Amphan " is expected to pack winds gusting up to 185 kilometres ( 115 miles ) per hour when it hits eastern India and Bangladesh on Wednesday afternoon or evening , and with a storm surge of several metres , forecasters said . <p> Bangladesh authorities fear it will be the most powerful since cyclone Sidr devastated the country in 2007 , killing about 3,500 people -- mostly due to a deluge of sea water sweeping in . <p> Junior disaster management minister Enamur Rahman said Tuesday that they have already evacuated several thousand people from low-lying areas . <p> " We will evacuate up to 2.2 million and try to keep casualties at zero , " Rahman told AFP . <p> To ensure social distancing in view of the coronavirus pandemic , the number of cyclone shelters has been doubled by using schools , with masks compulsory , Rahman said . <p> " We are also keeping separate isolation rooms in the shelters for any infected patients , " he added . <p> Yamin Chowdhury , an official in Bangladesh 's Barisal province , said the coast guard was helping to bring back thousands of people from remote islands . <p> Fishing boats have been ordered to return to shore and all ports and fish landing stations shut down . <p> " Coastal dwellers in Bangladesh are facing an impossible choice , " Snigdha Chakraborty from aid group Catholic Relief Services ( CRS ) said in a statement . <p> " There is limited space in existing evacuation shelters and people who have been on COVID-19 lockdown might hesitate to leave their less-sturdy homes to go to a central -- - and possibly crowded -- - shelter . " <p> She warned of " grim days ahead " , with poor sanitation , limited access to safe water and health facilities . <p> The coronavirus lockdown had also cut off many people 's income and eaten up their cash reserves , she added . <p> -- Diggers and chainsaws -- <p> In India , West Bengal state official Manturam Pakhira said more than 200,000 people were being evacuated from coastal districts and the Sundarbans , a vast mangrove forest area . <p> " Authorities are also supplying masks and sanitisers and making arrangements so that they can maintain safe distance from each other , " he said . <p> Arjun Manna , who works for the World Wide Fund for Nature ( WWF ) in the Sundarbans , told AFP that loud speakers were urging locals " to wear a mask and maintain social distancing in the shelters " . <p> In Odisha state , relief commissioner Pradeep Kumar Jena told AFP that 20,000 people have been evacuated , with 600 disaster teams " pre-positioned " and contractors standing by with diggers and chainsaws . <p> " We will evacuate more people depending on the situation . No one will be allowed to stay in huts with thatched roofs in coastal areas , " Jena said . <p> He too said that in order to ensure social distancing , additional temporary shelters have been identified , with the state ready to house 1.1 million people if needed . <p> " We have made arrangements for soap , water tanks for hand washing and face masks . We are also educating people about the need to cover their mouths , " he said . <p> -- Modi prays -- <p> Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi tweeted that he was praying for everyone 's safety after chairing a virtual meeting on preparations Monday . <p> Bangladesh 's low-lying coast , home to 30 million people , and India 's east are regularly battered by cyclones that have claimed the lives of hundreds of thousands of people in recent decades . <p> In 1999 , the eastern Indian state of Odisha was hit by a super-cyclone that left nearly 10,000 dead . In 1991 , a typhoon , tornadoes and flooding killed 139,000 in Bangladesh . <p> While the storms ' frequency and intensity have increased -- blamed partly on climate change -- deaths have fallen thanks to faster evacuations and more shelters .
