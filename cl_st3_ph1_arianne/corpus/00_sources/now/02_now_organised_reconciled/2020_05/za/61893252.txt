<h> Pandemic drives need for Africa to embrace tech <p> Kathy Gibson reports -- Technological advances in Africa have been buoyed by the Covid-19 pandemic . <p> Lesiba Mothata , head : strategic clients at Alexander Forbes , points out that working from home has become standard as lockdowns close traditional workplaces . <p> " It has n't been induced by legislation or government , but it may prove to be a permanent thing , " he says . <p> " We have said that data will become the new oil : Covid-19 has exacerbated that . When we work from home our cars are parked outside but data is vital . " <p> Alexander Forbes partnered with Mercer to ask companies about their plans for 2020 . <p> At the beginning of the year , flexible work was their key top of mind for 2020 , with 82% saying they are planning it . <p> " So there is consensus in the world that we are going to be doing flexible work , " Mothata says . <p> Financial services and technology are leading the industry sectors ready to work from home . The life sciences industry is also well prepared , followed by consumer goods and insurance . <p> The industries least ready for remote working are automotive and health . <p> In South Africa , 58% of employees feel trusted to work flexibly . <p> Mercer asked companies how many workers were remote before Covid-19 , and found that about a quarter did so on a regular basis . <p> " Now the world has changed , Covid has come , and it has expedited a few things , " Mothata says . <p> With Covid-19 , new concerns come to the fore . <p> Companies are concerned with the reliability of remote technology ( 72% in Africa and 58% globally ) . In Africa , 47% of companies provide subsidies for employees working from home , compared to 15% globally . <p> In Africa , a majority of countries do n't have universal internet access at home -- indeed , only 17,8% of African households have internet access and just 10,7% have a computer at home . <p> A lot of effort has been made to up the ante on these statistics , Mothata points out . At least 79% of African inhabitants are now covered by 3G , and a few countries will have rolled out 5G by 2025 , he adds . <p> In the last few weeks , a consortium of telcos has come together to build a new undersea cable to serve Africa , Mothata adds . <p> " Suppliers of tech have woken up . They have planned this for some time , but Covid has expedited it . The world has changed , and they are putting more than $1-billion to make this cable happen . " <p> The pandemic and increasing use of technology could drive fundalmental industry changes , Mothata says . Countries relying on a single commodity need to nurture sectors that have increased in significance , like agriculture , services , wholesale and retail trade in Nigeria . <p> Climate change is an issue that Africa needs to be concerned about , Mothata adds , with the cost rising as we go forward . <p> Technology can be applied to deal with climate change and its impact on agriculture . Things like crop sensors , drones , management systems and precision monitoring are examples of how technology can help . <p> In short , Mothata says , the pandemic is likely to expedite the adoption of new technology as work from home is entrenched , as climate change becomes an issue , as sectoral shifts take place , and as Africa leapfrogs the release of spectrum and related use of next-generation connectivity .
