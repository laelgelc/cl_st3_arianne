<h> Coronavirus Eastern Cape <h> Elundini community 's Garden of Hope <p> By Estelle Ellis* 19 May 2020 <p> Some of the women in the community after receiving food parcels . ( Photo : Lieve Claessen ) <h> When lockdown left Elundini Backpackers near Hogsback in the Eastern Cape empty , its owner and a permaculture activist started a project to feed a whole community -- even though this meant they had to carry water from the river every day . <p> As lockdown stopped the tourists from coming , Elundini Backpackers ' owner Lieve Claessen knew one thing : She was not going to sit on the couch until it was lifted . <p> CLOSE <p> The Elundini community lives in a remote rural village about 20 minutes from Hogsback in the Eastern Cape . <p> SA 's borders were closed in March when President Cyril Ramaphosa declared the outbreak of coronavirus infections in the country to be a National Disaster , leaving communities like Elundini that rely heavily on tourists without an income . <p> As they prepared for lockdown , Claessen started with education campaigns , filling water bottles so that people could wash their hands , making sure the communal taps were safe and setting up a rescue plan in case of an outbreak . <p> Many people in the village were dependent on their grants to buy food , but they had also been receiving money from relatives who had been working in the city , and with lockdown suddenly found themselves without work . <p> First Claessen made sure that people had food with the help of the local chief and donations from Hogsback residents . And she started a garden -- despite there being no reliable source of water in the village . Day after day they carried water from the river to water the garden . <p> " We have a diesel-powered pump that provides water to the village . If the pump does n't work , there is no water . The man who ran the pump has gone on pension and he was never replaced by government , but he is carrying on with the work because there is nobody else , " Claessen said . <p> Claessen 's business , Elundini Backpackers , is a social enterprise . <p> " All activities like hikes and village tours are organised by the community . The proceeds go straight to them . Our approach is for community members to learn to earn to own . " <p> She created the NGO LEO Foundation -- an acronym for Learn , Earn , Own . " But it also symbolises a strong lion , " she said . <p> Display Adverts <p> " We focus on self-sufficiency . We have a community garden and a daycare . We supply fencing for people who want to start a garden in their backyards because there are a lot of animals around here , " she said . <p> For now , their only focus is to get the 30 families of Elundini village through the pandemic . <p> " I am not going to sit on the couch and wait for this to be over . Our community was very stressed before the food parcels arrived . It gave them a bit of a boost . But next month is coming . Daily life is often a struggle here , " she said . <p> " We do n't have any infections here . People sometimes go to Alice for a few things but otherwise they remain here in the village . The social cohesion here is strong . You will know if someone is ill . People are doing their best to practice social distancing . " <p> She added that even before the pandemic their plan was to establish a sustainable community garden . <p> Permaculture activist and member of the Eastern Cape Water Caucus , Ntsiki Mteta , has been volunteering with Claessen since February . <p> " We were talking to people about sustainable gardening and drought mitigation . The Eastern Cape is a drought-stricken area . We have had an ongoing drought since 2009 , " he said . <p> Display Adverts <p> " We only got electricity about five years ago . The community waited for 25 years . There are many broken pipes and taps . And we have not received any extra water tanks from the government . " <p> But when the pandemic struck , they started talking about food security . <p> " We have created a community garden where we grow food and we have also started a nursery with cuttings for people who want to grow food in their backyards . We have cabbage and broccoli , carrots , peas , potatoes and onions . We are also propagating trees and planting them around the community to help fight climate change , " he said . <p> He said before lockdown they had trained people between the ages of 50 and 70 " and one young woman " to grow their own food . <p> " Everybody is so appreciative of the skills and knowledge that they are gaining , " he said . " But now we ca n't train any more . " <p> But they are carrying on with work to keep the garden going . <p> " To keep the garden going we must fetch water from the river every day , " Claessen said . <p> Display Adverts <p> She said lockdown made it difficult for them to access seedlings or seeds . <p> " We are asking people to grow trays of seed for us and donate and even to donate trees to us . <p> " We exchange and share . Food should be free . People should be able to exchange what they have with their neighbours . " <p> She said at some shops people are now paying R25 per cabbage . <p> " The focus of the community market garden is to create sustainability for Elundini . We want to send fresh produce to the community at a reasonable price , " Mteta said . <p> He added that they are also very keen to train people so the garden can become a hub for knowledge and skills in the community for people to adapt and get through the crisis . <p> Claessen said their water situation was very worrying because the community depended on the borehole with the diesel pump . <p> Display Adverts <p> " We only got electricity about five years ago . And we have not received any extra water tanks from the government , " she said . <p> " Sometimes we are without water for up to two weeks . We are lucky to have water at the moment .
