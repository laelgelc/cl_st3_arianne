<p> The EU on Thursday defended agreeing to let Beijing censor an article by European ambassadors published in Chinese media to remove a reference to the coronavirus originating in China . <p> The opinion piece written jointly with the 27 EU national ambassadors appeared in the China Daily , but a sentence mentioning " the outbreak of the coronavirus in China " was deleted . <p> A spokeswoman for EU diplomatic chief Josep Borrell confirmed the change was made at the request of the Chinese foreign ministry but defended the decision to go ahead with publication regardless . <p> " We regret that this joint op-ed was not published in full by the China Daily , " Virginie Battu-Henriksson told reporters in Brussels . <p> She said EU officials raised " very serious concerns " about the request to censor the article . <p> " The EU delegation decided nevertheless to proceed with the publication of the op-ed with considerable reluctance as it considered it important to communicate on very key messages on EU policy priorities , " she added , citing climate change , human rights and the global response to the coronavirus . <p> Borrell said that foreign diplomatic missions in China have to " work under the conditions and within the limits set by the Chinese authorities " . <p> " It is no secret that China is now a state where there is censorship and control of the media , " Borrell told reporters . <p> The views and opinions expressed in this site are those of the authors and do not necessarily reflect the official policy , position , views and opinions of MCD Multimedia Corporation .
