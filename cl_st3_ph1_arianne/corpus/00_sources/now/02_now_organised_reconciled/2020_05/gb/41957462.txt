<h> Letters : Over-70s face the threat of exclusion for even longer <h> Letters : Over-70s face the threat of exclusion for even longer <p> I READ Rebecca McQuillan 's article , " Keeping over-70s at home is n't victimisation . It 's protection " , ( The Herald , May 1 ) with interest . However , I think that she is missing the point . <p> I am no 75-year-old superfit vegan marathon runner , but as a semi-retired medical man on the wrong side of 70 , I am only too aware that my age increases the risk of me catching Covid-19 , possibly coming to a miserable , lonely end , then being seen off at the crematorium by an even smaller number of people than might otherwise attend . <p> But increasing age is a risk factor for many other things , as well . <p> When the lockdown was announced , towards the end of March , we were initially given the impression that we had signed up for a three-month affair . That was a bit of a problem but we anticipated that a more normal life might be resumed after midsummer . <p> I appreciate that Covid-19 is an entirely novel illness , with new information emerging daily , but I am concerned to hear that , although the lockdown may be lifted for others , it may , for us oldies , remain in place for considerably longer - at its most extreme for 18 months . <p> Even that may be contingent upon the development of a successful vaccine or efficacious treatment , of which there is no guarantee . <p> While I may be able to carry out some of my medical work from home I , and others like me who may be reasonably fit and active , but are nearer the finishing-tape of life 's race , envisage being cut off from the wide variety of outside activities that we enjoy , the pleasure of the routine company of our families , friends and colleagues , and being excluded from landmark events . <p> Some quality of life -- and we might still catch and die from Covid-19 . <p> Actually , I 'm sure that it 's a fiendishly cunning plot of the Remainers : we Brexit-voting oldies will pine away from loneliness , and they will grab our money , and take the UK back into the EU . <p> Christopher W. Ide , <p> Waterfoot , <p> East Renfrewshire . <p> IT is time that the care-home coronavirus crisis was put into the correct context . <p> The huge proliferation of care homes , residential homes and sheltered housing is mainly a result of UK government decisions in the 1970s to take old age care out of the NHS . <p> Most of the sector now is owned by multi-millionaires ( many of them with huge cash deposits offshore ) and huge , prosperous companies . <p> The workers in these facilities , however , see little real benefit from the huge costs paid by the residents . <p> If they are inadequately provided with the resources to do their jobs properly or inadequately provided with protection , the immediate responsibility to provide such lies with the proprietors . <p> It is obvious to all that this sector will inevitably see the highest number of fatalities of this pandemic but we see a UK government , which is suspected of trying to conceal the number of deaths in this sector , and the Scottish Government , which is honestly reporting them , both being attacked for failings in this area over which they have less than adequate control . <p> This is not the time to play politics but surely when we have got out of this crisis it will be time to look seriously again at how we provide old-age care . <p> Dave McEwan Hill , <p> Argyll . <p> ON the topic of lockdown I agree with Isobel Hunter ( Herald letters , May 2 ) . We are indeed moving from nanny state to police state . <p> If we are " all in this together " , why are the rich and powerful able to move from city to country retreat ? Why did Boris Johnson recuperate at Chequers and not in Downing Street ? There are enough rooms , kitchens and bathrooms there for Boris to have stayed separate from his fiancee . <p> This lockdown will fall apart in a dangerous way , with riots , unless it applies to us all equally . <p> My sister 's neighbours are in their eighties .
