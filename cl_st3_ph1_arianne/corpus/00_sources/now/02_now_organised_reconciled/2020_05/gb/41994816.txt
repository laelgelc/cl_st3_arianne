<h> I would happily trade the cut and thrust of the chamber for a better , more respectful hybrid parliament <p> The architecture of politics can not stay in a time warp , it has to adapt to reflect modern life . The hybrid proceedings prove this can be done in a way that augments the traditions rather than detracts from them . <p> History has been made in parliament with the first ever remote division taking place this month . <p> Without a hefty shove from coronavirus I suspect the Palace may never have moved on from a physical head-counting system . <p> Many members , sticklers for tradition as they are , see nothing wrong with dragging people from their sick beds to be on the estate for a division . They do n't mind wasting hours of precious debating time hanging about in lobbies to vote , when a simple press of a button would do . <p> That 's just how things are done down here , no matter how baffling it seems to the outside world . <p> Necessity is the mother of invention and it took a global catastrophe to introduce a remote voting system , brought in with uncharacteristic pace for this place , in just four weeks . <p> Those software engineers , clerks and House staff did a marvellous job and deserve our thanks and gratitude for keeping democracy moving . <p> Despite appearances , Westminster is not just a museum for foosty Etonians . <p> The architecture of politics can not stay in a time warp , it has to adapt to reflect modern life . The hybrid proceedings prove this can be done in a way that augments the traditions rather than detracts from them . <p> Contrary to the sneers of some traditionalists , I have never been busier . It works well . <p> MPs speaking from screens in the chamber both blend and contrast with those on the famous green benches , linked but visually disparate . <p> MPs seem less removed from everyday life in their home environment - it sends a piece of the parliament out to communities across these isles . It also cuts the waste of travel costs and increases time for constituency-based work . <p> Contrary to the sneers of some traditionalists , I have never been busier . It works well . <p> Sadly , there are members who are keen to return to the old ways with the same rapidity as the new system appeared . Some have tried to undermine the security of the new voting system , claiming anyone could vote , despite needing a password and multi-factor authentication . <p> You would n't hand your online banking over to a stranger when it was logged in , so why would anyone look to do this with their Parliamentary log in after all . <p> The Speaker Sir Lindsay Hoyle must be praised for the manner and speed at which he instigated hybrid proceedings . It ensured safety for all those participating in the Parliamentary estate , members and staff alike , during this coronavirus crisis which is far from over . <p> While there will be times in future when being in Westminster may be the sensible option , it makes no sense now . <p> Social distancing remains critical and it means only 50 of 650 MPs can attend in the chamber at one time . <p> If there was a sudden move to significantly more members physically attending Parliament , I would argue that the opportunities to participate would be significantly reduced as compared to our current hybrid model . <p> Even with the change of advice from the PM , the message from him remains to work from home if you can , and MP 's can . <p> We all have a responsibility to ensure people who need to travel by public transport can do so safely , which in the tube means only 15% of normal capacity . <p> This will take time to sort and given the climate change emergency encouraging more use of cars to go to work is not the ' new normal ' we want to build . <p> I would expect MPs in Scotland to continue to follow the clear Scottish Government advice to work from home and avoid all non-essential journeys - flaunting the rules would be irresponsible and put lives at risk . I 'm more than 400 miles away and have one of the easier journeys - for those that have to fly it 's impossible to do so safely . <p> In the longer term , having flexibility to be in the constituency when required and still take part in proceedings has huge advantages . <p> We should not go backwards and drop this useful new technological tool . <p> It may make me more remote to London but I am far less remote to those I represent , and that 's more important . <p> It is true that the hybrid debates lose something from the lack of interventions , but they also gain from the lack of bear-pit baying that so often prevents good points being heard . <p> The political theatre may be less dramatic but I would happily trade the cut and thrust of the chamber for a better , more respectful democracy .
