<h> Scientists seek help of butterfly spotters <p> The holly blue and speckled wood , inset , are among the butterflies spreading northwards in the UK because of the effects of climate change <p> ALAMY <p> Will Humphries , Southwest Correspondent <p> Wednesday April 22 2020 , 12.01am , The Times <p> Citizen scientists are being called on to help track the effects of climate change on butterflies as researchers can not leave their homes to gather data during lockdown . <p> Butterfly Conservation has asked the public to record butterfly and moth sightings in their gardens and on exercise walks . <p> The wildlife charity has butterfly and moth population data going back more than 50 years but the study of their cycles will be severely affected this spring by coronavirus restrictions . <p> Richard Fox , of the charity , said : " Studying the changing flight times and locations of butterfly species is vital to understanding the impacts of climate change . " It is making butterflies " emerge earlier in spring and some are spreading to new parts of the UK " , he added .
