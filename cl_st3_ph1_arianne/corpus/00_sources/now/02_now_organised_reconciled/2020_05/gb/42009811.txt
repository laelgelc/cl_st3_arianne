<p> It is a hidden gem ; a wild place protected by law where hen harriers court on the wing , merlin and short-eared owl hunt and black grouse strut their stuff . <p> Langholm Moor , in the Southern Uplands , is home to some of Scotland 's most iconic wildlife and has a natural and cultural heritage of which locals are proud . <p> For 250 years , they have been riding out on horseback , marking its boundaries in the Common Riding . Said to be the oldest equestrian event in the world , it is a spectacular tradition that continues today . <p> Now the community has a chance to buy the land they love and turn it into the Tarras Valley nature reserve . <p> The ambitious scheme aims to protect the moor , enabling it to flourish for future generations . It could also help turn the tide of climate change along with the fortunes of an ailing textile town . <p> But the 10,500-acre moor , owned by Buccleuch Estates , does not come cheap . The community buyout will cost more than ? 6 million -- ? 3m of which is being sought from crowdfunding . As the race to close the deal gains pace , we find out what makes Langholm Moor so precious . <p> Buccleuch announced its intention last year to sell off 25,000 acres of its land . It gave the buyout body 14 days to demonstrate community support for the plan . Within 10 days , 833 people signed a petition in favour of the bid . <p> There were also letters of support from groups including Langholm Common Riding , Scottish Natural Heritage , Forestry and Land Scotland , Scotland 's Regeneration Forum and Community Land Scotland whose policy director Calum MacLeod praised Buccleuch Estates for its proactive engagement in the process . <p> He said : " This is a really important development in terms of demonstrating how community ownership can provide a real stimulus for a renewal and sustainability of local community and landscape spaces . Trusting the community to do that should be something we should be encouraging even more throughout Scotland . " <p> The buyout body says the community fears that if another party purchases the land , forests could be planted or wind farms erected on the section that borders a Site of Special Scientific Interest . <p> It is now discussing backing from the South of Scotland Enterprise Agency , and has applied for ? 3 million from the Scottish Land Fund . <p> So far it has received ? 100,000 from the John Muir Trust , taking its total to ? 137,000 . <p> Buccleuch Estates confirmed it has had discussions with the Langholm Initiative and had made progress but milestones outwith its control had to be passed before a deal could be completed . <h> The habitat <p> Dr Chris Miles , chairman of the Southern Upland Partnership , was previously manager for Scottish Natural Heritage , and he rates Langholm Moor , with its 250-plus species of plants , a natural treasure . <p> But he says it is the area 's diversity that sets it aside . <p> " Langholm Moor 's geology and location make it fairly complex and it 's this complexity that leads to diversity , " said Dr Miles . " Its hilltops are broad and rounded with peat bogs that are host to their own range of plants like cotton grass , cloudberry , bog ashpodel , and sundews . <p> " The sides of the hills are steep and rocky with crags . They support habitats for ferns , one of the less common is green spleenwort . <p> " The moor is cut into by some significant watercourses , like the Tarras Water . This broad valley has remnant native woodland of mostly of birch and willow , but also unusual things such as aspen . " <p> He explained how calcium carbonate in boulder clay enriches the springs and flushes that emerge on the hillsides creating green areas where unusual plants can be found . " There are also nice populations of grass-of-Parnassus , as well as marsh valerian , which is relatively uncommon . " <p> They and other plants such as northern marsh orchid and the common spotted orchid are indicators of a " good , wetter habitat " . <p> Dr Miles said : " The buyout is an exciting proposal as those behind it would have nature as the driving purpose for its management . We are in an era where we are worried about our loss of important wildlife sites . <p> " We have to do something about it as a society . " <h> The wildlife <p> According to Duncan Orr-Ewing , RSPB Scotland 's head of species &amp; land management , Langholm Moor has more rare hen harriers than the whole of England . <p> He said : " More than 1% of the UK population ( around 500 ) is on Langholm Moor . It has as many breeding pairs as the whole of England . <p> " There are about seven pairs at present and the figure has been as high as the mid-20s . <p> " The Moor has been designated a Site of Special Scientific Interest because of its moorland breeding bird population , so there are other birds like black grouse , golden plover , curlew and various wading birds . <p> " The SSSI is because of the assemblage of birds , the collection of types of all the species that occur . <p> " In much of the Uplands the heather moorland habitat has largely disappeared because of commercial afforestation and overgrazing . <p> " Heather is the main food plant for species like red grouse and the main nesting habitat for birds like the hen harrier . But it is also endangered because of human persecution . " <p> He added : " Sadly , those species that occur at Langholm are declining on a national scale . We want to see them maintained . <p> " Everything the buyout team are proposing sounds sympathetic and what we would do . We hope they get backing to buy the moor . " <h> The buyout <p> Kevin Cumming with wife Ellen and daughter Freya <p> Kevin Cumming , the man leading the buyout bid , works for the Langholm Initiative . He runs its Wild Eskdale project providing outdoor education and promoting eco-tourism . <p> Kevin , 34 , who lives on the edge of Langholm with his wife Ellen , 32 , and their seven-month-old daughter Freya said : " The experiences of Langholm Moor are unique . <p> " In a single day you can stand under a sky of spectacular courting hen harriers , witness the silent hunt of a short-eared owl or be mesmerised by the intimate lekking of black grouse . <p> " This is a special place . But during the eight months I 've been leading the working group to investigate the community , we have seen the devastation caused by the Australian wildfires and the global impact of Covid-19 . <p> " That is why this buyout is important ; it 's a project of vision and hope when we need it , and it is a project for our future . <p> " We need to concentrate on this for our children and the generations that come after . The Langholm Initiative is proposing we undertake climate action , wildlife conservation , ecological restoration and community regeneration all built around a central goal -- creating the Tarras Valley Nature Reserve . <p> " We will carry out peatland restoration , new native woodland creation , ancient woodland regeneration and protect and enhance the incredible flora and fauna that lives here . " <p> The project plans to block drains allowing raised bogs to return and new peat to be created , locking in carbon and increasing biodiversity . <p> New woodland would also help carbon capture . And the buyout would create sustainable , responsible eco-tourism , with commercial spin-offs . <p> He said : " By taking Langholm Moor under community ownership , we can demonstrate how individuals united in a common purpose can perform something truly amazing . "
