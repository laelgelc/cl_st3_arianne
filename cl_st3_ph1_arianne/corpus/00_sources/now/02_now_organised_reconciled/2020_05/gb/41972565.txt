<h> Love of the landscape shapes work of Friends of the Lake District chief executive <h> Love of the landscape shapes charity 's work <p> Andrew Thomas talks to Douglas Chalmers , chief executive of Friends of the Lake District , about his passion for the landscape and the challenges and opportunities facing the area <p> Like many of us , Douglas Chalmers looks back on his life and can now trace the threads and influences that led him to his current role , while accepting that at the time a specific path was not clear . <p> Born in Fraserburgh , a town on the north east corner of Scotland , Douglas , 61 , describes himself as ' a simple son of an Aberdeen farmer ' . <p> He originally wanted to be a vet but , in the late seventies , the James Herriot novels were hugely popular and there were 1,300 applications for 60 vacancies to study veterinary science at Glasgow University . <p> Instead Douglas studied agriculture and animal science at Aberdeen before launching into a 20-year career as an animal nutritionist in the agricultural supply industry . During this time he moved around the country , became a sheep and cattle expert , met and married his wife Alex , had three children and eventually moved to Cumbria . <p> Farming , said Douglas , was ' in his genes ' but he had always also wanted a salaried job . The family settled at a small working farm at Hoff and during the foot-and-mouth outbreak in 2001 Douglas was appointed Regional Director for the North West with the Country Land and Business Association ( CLA ) . Much of his initial work involved helping farmers during and after foot-and-mouth . <p> As the CLA 's Director of Policy and Public Affairs he lobbied the Government so upland farmers gained parity with lowland farmers when it came to area payments under the Common Agricultural Policy . " I am really proud of that , " said Douglas . <p> Five years ago he had the opportunity to become chief executive of Friends of the Lake District . " I had worked with FLD and was always impressed with how they did things ; what their motives were ; their knowledge and passion and I thought all their arguments were really sound . " <p> FLD , a charity based at Murley Moss at Kendal , has 24 staff and around 6,500 members . <p> Douglas said one of its main roles was to protect and enhance the landscape . " Not just the physical landscape but the cultural landscape as well -- the way things are done , the commoners , traditional farming methods and the role of local communities within the landscape . Landscape is not just a view . <p> " I get cross if people use the term ' preserve ' about FLD . If you preserve something that is living and working you end up killing it . This is a living , working landscape . People live and earn their living here and that is what shapes the landscape . " <p> FLD also aims to inspire , educate and excite people of all abilities and backgrounds about the landscape . " We bring people into the landscape and show them how we can affect it for good or ill and how it can affect us for good or ill , " said Douglas . <p> FLD comments on planning applications and helps to shape planning policies through partnerships and lobbying the government . <p> It runs work parties on its land , such as in the High Borrowdale Valley , where people can learn dry-stone walling or path clearing ; and its Leaders Landscape Training courses help guides to better understand the landscape and cascade that knowledge to others . <p> FLD 's successes since its foundation in 1934 have included convincing the Forestry Commission not to mass plant conifers in the central Lakes ; getting more electricity and power lines underground ; and extending the Lake District and Yorkshire Dales national parks . <p> Current challenges , said Douglas , included climate change . " Storm Desmond showed us that Cumbria is not as tough and rugged as everyone thinks . Every time there is an extreme weather event you see more damage to the landscape and commons . " <p> ' Inappropriate development ' is another threat . " We are not anti zip wires per see but when they suggested wires across open water at Thirlmere it was the wrong idea in the wrong place . " <p> Other concerns are increasing tourist traffic and how traditional upland farm managers will be supported post-Brexit to do environmental and flood management , carbon storage and look after water quality . <p> What are his favourite places in the Lakes ? " It depends on the mood you are in , the light and the time of day . The first lake I saw was Ullswater on a frosty morning -- I just thought ' whoa , what is that ' ? <p> " Another time I was listening to Prince Charles speak at Keswick and he quoted psalm 121 - ' I to the hills will lift mine eyes ' -- and I looked into the jaws of Borrowdale and the view just took me over and I stopped listening to the heir to the throne ! " <p> Douglas describes himself as ' optimistic and resilient ' , but the other quality which shines through when chatting to him is his love of the Lake District and its unique landscape .
