You can unsubscribe at any time . <p> Work to build huge flood defences to protect tens of thousands of homes and businesses in Hull city centre has got underway - despite the current coronavirus lockdown . <p> The construction of a new flood defence wall at the city 's historic Victoria Pier is part of a wider ? 42m scheme of tidal defence work to protect the Hull from rising sea levels . <p> Despite construction site bosses up and down the UK calling time on projects to try and stop the spread of the Covid-19 during the pandemic , the Environment Agency has pressed ahead with the " essential " work . <p> The agency say it is being " carried out in accordance with government guidelines for safe working " . <p> The work centres around a new steel sheet-pile wall being built 2.5m into the Humber to protect the existing edge of Victoria Pier with sea levels expected to rise in the coming years as a result of climate change . <p> An image of the proposed new flood defences at Victoria Pier in Hull ( Image : Environment Agency ) <p> A new concrete flood defence wall is also being built on top of the structure , which will include glazed panels to maintain a view out into the estuary . <p> Temporary flood defences have been in place in Nelson Street for several months while the work goes on . <p> Helen Tattersdale , project manager for the Environment Agency , said : " In line with government advice , we are continuing to deliver all of our flood and risk management projects where it is safe for our teams and delivery partners to do so . <p> " Protecting residents ' homes and businesses is a huge priority for the local authority and its partners , and we welcome the start of these works . <p> " Following the number of extreme weather incidents experienced over the last six months coupled with the threat of climate change and rising sea levels , the need for protection is important now , more than ever . " <p> All sections of the Humber Hull Frontages flood defence improvements scheme are expected to be completed in spring next year , despite the coronavirus lockdown.
