<h> Follow the author of this article <h> Follow the topics within this article <p> The coronavirus pandemic has led to the toughest trading conditions ever faced by the ski industry , with a quarter of companies fearing they may go out of business in the coming months -- leaving thousands of British jobs at risk . <p> A survey of over 80 industry bosses , ranging from tour operators to retailers , found that 80 per cent have seen incomes fall since the spread of Covid-19 forced the premature end to the ski season and threw the world 's travel industry into turmoil . Two thirds are now experiencing reduced bookings for next winter ( 2020/21 ) , despite booking incentives and discounts . <p> The global health crisis is the latest in a string of challenges for the ski industry in recent years , following increased concerns around climate change and the pending restrictions on the industry surrounding Brexit . However , experts believe coronavirus poses the biggest threat to the industry in living history . <p> " In over 30 years of selling skiing and activity holidays , we 've been through a number of recessions , and events like 9/11 , but the current trading conditions are the most difficult we 've ever faced , " said Gavin Foster from short-break specialists Ski Weekend . <p> The survey was carried out at the end of April and completed by 87 companies , including one of the UK 's leading holiday firms HotelPlan , which operates Inghams , Esprit Ski and Ski Total . <p> Iain Martin and Rob Stewart , the ski industry experts who carried out the survey , believe thousands of jobs are at risk and the challenges businesses face are unprecedented . " Nobody has a crystal ball but one thing is clear from this survey and that is as an industry we are facing unprecedented times with many businesses fearing for their future . The impact has been huge and thousands of jobs are seriously at risk , " said Stewart . <p> Despite help from the Government 's business support and job retention schemes , with two thirds of those surveyed finding the packages announced by Rishi Sunak in March useful , one in five businesses have been forced to make staff redundant to stay afloat . Around 60 per cent have furloughed their staff . <p> Participants were asked : " In what ways has your business been affected by the spread of COVID-19 ? " <p> This has provided security for some , but a quarter of businesses surveyed fear they will cease trading completely if further help is n't provided , travel restrictions lifted and consumer confidence reinstated . <p> Other major strains on ski travel businesses include the costs incurred during the repatriation of guests , when thousands of British skiers and snowboarders were left stranded in the mountains as resorts closed , and the time it is taking many to process refunds and credit to customers . <p> When it comes to the future of travel , the majority ( 80 per cent ) of respondents are concerned about the impact potential travel restrictions on UK passport holders might have on ski holidays . This is compounded by existing concerns around Brexit , which has already had a significant impact on the industry , in particular changes to staffing and rising logistical costs . <p> And while 71 per cent believe ski resorts will reopen , the majority agree they will do so with social distancing measures in place .
