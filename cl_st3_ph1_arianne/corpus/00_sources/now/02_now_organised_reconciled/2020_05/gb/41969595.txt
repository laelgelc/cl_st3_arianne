<h> Scooter Braun on the future of the music industry post-coronavirus <p> As the man who guided Ariana Grande through one of pop 's darkest days , Scooter Braun knows all about crisis . Here , he predicts how different the world will look -- for music , for business , for all of us -- in the wake of a catastrophe felt in every corner of the globe <p> Scooter Braun always has plans , although even he has been sent sideways by the calamitous global effects of the coronavirus crisis . He has never been one to panic , however , and his vision of how his industry bounces back makes fascinating reading . As one of the major forces in the entertainment industry , the entrepreneur , philanthropist , studio head , record executive and the man who looks after the fortunes of -- among others -- Justin Bieber and Ariana Grande , his radar is more finely attuned than most . Consequently , his predictions for the future of the industry deserve close scrutiny . <p> Braun has had his own trials and tribulations recently , but is still considered to be something of a local hero in Manchester , where he helped organise the One Love benefit concert three years ago , following the terrorist attack on 22 May that year , at the Ariana Grande concert at Manchester Arena , where 22 people died . Just two months ago , Hashem Abedi , the brother of the suicide bomber , Salman Abedi , was found guilty of the 22 murders , at the end of one of the biggest terror trials ever held on UK soil . <p> As the race for the White House heats up this year , Braun also has things to say about the continued dearth of trust in politics , his feelings about Donald Trump ( not all bad , but mostly ) and the role that philanthropy can play going forward . He offers , in short , a guide to life . Or at least the life we have in front of us right now ... <p> The coronavirus has forced all of us to look at the way we run our businesses , but how do you think its effect is going to transform the entertainment industry , short to medium term ? Like everyone else , I 've been overwhelmed by the coronavirus crisis . The way I look at it is if I 'm unsure and scared about what 's going on in the world , I ca n't imagine what other people are going through . I 'm in a privileged position , so I feel I need to do my part . I 'm working on a bunch of stuff to try to make sure people are OK . We 're entering a new world and while I think that things always bounce back long term , we have n't seen anything like this before , something so devastating to our worldwide economy , in probably 100 years . <p> In the entertainment industry there has been this content war between all these companies producing more and more -- who 's going to have the most content ? -- but the demand actually was n't there . No one was sitting around their house going , " I do n't have enough content . " So I think you 're going to see a consolidation of that and you 're going to see the spending go way , way down . I think you 're going to see a lot of companies that were over-leveraged , built on debt , really suffer . Eventually , touring will come back , when there 's trust out there , and there is mass testing and we can identify where this thing is . But I think there is going to be a lot of suffering first . And it 's not just about making sure we are healthy , but there is going to be some real economic damage from all of this and we 're going to have to see what governments can do together to help us all . People are going to start thinking about their money in a very different way . This is a very humbling experience for all of us . <p> The US government has to make some very hard decisions and , right now , because of the decisions they 've made already , we are behind this . The bailout needs to be considered and prolonged . People need to be able to survive right now . We need a stimulus package to allow businesses to survive . We need to help small businesses and hold large businesses accountable . I run a reasonably large organisation and people at the top need to do their part . Leaders need to be leaders and if they have to take a personal pay cut in order to pay people , or send a message , then they should do it . If there needs to be anyone in our company taking a pay cut , it will be me first and then everyone else will follow . I 'm in the fortunate position of being able to afford to do that , but not all of my employees can . It 's going to be a wild ride . <p> The problem is , we have had this herd mentality of large speculation and then large deflation , continually borrowing money from the future . We keep saying , " We 'll get there . We 'll get there , " but we 're not there yet . So while it will be humbling for some , it 's going to be devastating for others . And those are the people we should be helping first . <p> The only thing that 's going to work here is our humanity . <p> How has the coronavirus crisis affected your inclination to run for public office ? The first thing I did in this instance was look at my immediate family . Are they OK ? The funny thing is , the person who got sick was me , but it was just a common flu , I do not have corona , although I probably will eventually get it . Secondly , I looked to my business and I seem to have made the right decisions , because consumption of streaming is at an all-time high right now . Netflix , Amazon , they 're all doing really well right now . So it is strong , but it will change . We have diversified enough to be able to cope with that . And our artists are in a good enough position for us to be able to help them when they decide to start touring again . But there are thousands of small businesses who are not going to be OK , so when you look at a situation like this , you have to consider how it 's going to affect others . <p> ' I 've got the Manchester bee tattoo . That city is emotional . It 's a city full of heroes ' <p> Public office is something I 've thought about in the past , but only because I get very frustrated by a lack of leadership . The reason I have shied away from it is because I have young children and I have to be careful ... Recently I was attacked very publicly by someone I do n't know , someone who refused to have a conversation with me , and I wish that person nothing but the best and hope that one day a dialogue is had , because I think it all could have been avoided with proper dialogue , but what it did teach me was that if my children were teenagers , if they were a little bit older , this could have been very hard for them . And I do n't know if I 'm comfortable being in public office knowing the amount of ridicule and exposure you get and I do n't know if I want to put my children through that . So right now I 'm trying to do the best I can from the private sector . <p> How much have you been changed by becoming a father ? Management changed me first , but being a father changed me dramatically . Management also made me more prepared to be a father . Because when you 're a kid you 're taught if something is wrong , you strike it down . Management is a very humbling job because you start to realise that people have very different points of view and while their point of view might not be reasonable , it 's reasonable to them . And if you 're going to get it their career to a place of success , you need to compromise and you need to find a common ground . You ca n't just shoot it down and say , " That 's ridiculous . " And you have to take ownership . If you disagree , you have to take a part in how you got here . So management made me a better man . Being a father gives a sense of purpose I 've never had in my life before . When you play a role in making life , that is the first time you truly understand your own mortality . Because when you meet your kid , you 're going , " OK , I do n't know you . You do n't have a personality . You 're basically this blob . But I love you more than anything I 've ever met in my life . And I do n't know why . And you have n't been here for any of the years in which I put in all the hard work to get to be the man I am today ! That means one day I wo n't be here . And just like you did n't exist , one day I wo n't exist . " Then you realise how finite your life is . You create life and then suddenly realise death is real . It was also funny when I had my second and third kids . When I had my second kid , I thought there is no way I 'm going to be able to love him as much as his brother ... I was really scared about that . And then you realise that the love for your kids is the only thing that grows exponentially . Your heart just makes room . It 's an absolute game changer . <p> It 's taken me a while to know myself , but my grandmother used to say , " Being mature is just being OK with your eleven-year-old self . " It took me a while to realise that I did n't have anything to prove to anybody . And just being that kid was OK . So as I 'm now happy in my own skin , I feel like being a father is my most important job . Of course all this sounds very clich ? d , which is why a lot of my friends think I 'd be very good if I went into politics ! Because you see politicians desperately trying to convince people that they are a good person , but I actually believe in this stuff . <p> What 's the secret of longevity ? It 's a combination of thinking long term and putting in the work . We work hard . And , you know , if you 're negotiating with someone , you 're going to be negotiating with them six months down the line , so you ca n't burn the bridge . And if you 're going to take care of someone , take care of them . If something bad comes along , do n't look at it in the moment , look at it as what it will mean five years down the line . This is an opportunity for you to make the right decision . The other secret of longevity , which I say to my clients and artists and friends , is do n't think you 're bigger than the moment . Being a superstar , it 's hard to get there , you 've got to maintain it to hold on to it . We 're not made to be worshipped , we 're made to help people . We are meant to serve , so when you are being worshipped , it 's confusing . And you make mistakes . Success kills . Every Greek tragedy has tried to tell us that . <p> What are the rules you live by ? What do you tell your team ? One , that we are a team . Ultimately you have to make decisions , but it is a team . And people in teams play different roles . I grew up playing basketball and you ca n't have five point guards . Everyone needs to play their role well . And if we 're going to scale then we need to trust that you 're going to play your role . We 're going to make mistakes but we 're going to work through that . We work as a team so mistakes need to be team mistakes . I hate it when people try to solve something on their own and then by the time they bring it to me it 's a disaster . If you can see trouble coming , bring it to the team immediately so we can solve it together . There is no shame in that . It 's actually the right thing to do . The other thing I tell them is being smart is not enough . Being loyal is more important . I fully believe that standing by your word is more important than being the smartest person in the room . I ca n't work with someone I ca n't trust . Also , this thing about business and pleasure should n't mix , I do n't believe in that . Things like the coronavirus , that 's a test and that 's when you find out what 's real in a relationship . <p> Who are your own business heroes ? One guy I 'm very close to is Universal Music chairman Lucian Grainge and the other is David Geffen , who I speak to all the time now and who I read about and who inspired me when I was 19 . We are different people and we do things differently , but he sees things that other people do n't see . The wisdom he 's given me , about both his successes and his mistakes , are invaluable . I cherish them . Howard Marks is also brilliant , from an economic standpoint . But some of the most inspirational people I know have n't been business people but coaches and fathers . A lot of my mentoring with David has been about how I handle myself , not other people . His journey at a young age was very similar to mine . <p> What valuable lessons have your learned from your mistakes ? Ownership , not of possessions but of decisions . It 's easy when you 're young to get angry at the way people treat you , but that 's not helpful . What I 've realised is I ca n't affect what other people do or how they think . I can only affect my own actions . And the best thing I can do is take ownership of what role I can play in the relationship . If someone is angry at me then I must have contributed to their feelings in some way . Right or wrong , I was part of that chain of reaction . Having the ability to say sorry is also very important . I 'm also much more efficient if I can let that go . It 's startling how many of these things that you worry about turn out to be simple misunderstandings . It 's so easily fixed if you 're smart . Being in a marriage taught me a lot of this , because marriage is all about compromise . If she 's shutting down on me , then I did something to make her shut down . So we talk through it and we get through it . But I 've now done this with every other area of my life . <p> ' Justin and Ariana are family . It 's not like an artist/manager relationship ' <p> If you 're having success at the age of 25 or 27 , part of that success is your arrogance at thinking you deserve it . And you 're brash enough to think you 're smart enough to deserve it . And that 's great because it gives you the confidence to go and do some really great things , but it 's also just not true . I 'm 38 now and I know that if we talk again in ten years ' time then I 'll be a lot smarter than I am now . <p> What are your proudest moments , apart from children ? As obnoxious as it sounds , it 's not the Grammys or the No1s or the huge success , because when they happen again and again you realise they 're never going to fulfil you . But when you 're helping someone , that 's fulfilling . It has to come down to philanthropy . Being a part of the Manchester show was the proudest I 've ever been . I 've got the Manchester bee tattoo on my biceps . Manchester , that city is emotional . It 's a city full of heroes . That show happened because 60,000 people were brave enough to show up . People forget it was a day after the London attack . That city is so incredibly brave . The other day I was in Vegas giving a talk and when I came off stage one of the guys working there had also worked on the Ariana show in Manchester . He took his jacket off and showed me his bee tattoo and then I showed him mine . And then we hugged . That 's the kind of humanity I want to be part of . <p> How would you describe your journey and your relationship with Justin Bieber ? He and Ariana are like my kids . They 're family . It 's not like an artist/manager relationship . The whole camaraderie of our organisation and the artists we work with is like family . Justin and I have been together so long and Ariana and I have been through so much together it 's like we 're related . I want to be respectful to their parents , but it really is like that . I care about him and his wife like I care about my family . It 's a strange kind of dynamic and our relationship is unique because we 've been through hell . He said to me the other day , the fact that we 're still so close after everyone trying to tear us apart , and the drugs , the things that he 's now been open about , it 's a very unique journey . <p> What effect is 5G going to have on brands , artists and the industry in general ? It would have been interesting if it had arrived in a time when the content wars were still very prevalent , but it 's coming in during a time when the spend on content is not going to be what it was . 5G is going to give us more and more access , but as we 're going to have consolidation in the streaming industry it might have less of an effect than we initially thought . There 'll be a lot of innovation and the fact that it makes it faster to do everything we 're doing now -- sharing , communicating with each other -- is a boon , but it 's not going to play out the way we thought . 5G is only going to be delivering the best of the best , because everything else is going to fall by the wayside . Money 's drying up in this area . When you 're spending billions and billions of dollars on content just to compete with your competitor , not necessarily because the consumer is asking for it , that 's a very dangerous game . I think because Netflix has done such a good job guiding this boat , making themselves this destination , that I think they 're going to be fine . Everyone else ? I 'm not so sure . You 're going to see consolidation by necessity , because a lot of these companies have so much debt . A lot of these companies who do n't have a lot of cash make great shows , but so what ? It does n't matter how good it is ... <p> Where do you see the future of philanthropy ? If we 're going to be subjected to more global phenomena , such as the coronavirus , for instance , the levels of investment needed in things such as R&amp;D can only be achieved by looking at the private sector , surely , as much as we 'd like to think the state will solve all our problems ... Again , it comes back to humanity . Also , we should think about changing the way we frame these companies . They should n't be called nonprofit , they should be called for-purpose . It 's belittling and also does n't allow us to give people what they need . So if I 'm the CEO of a tobacco company , it 's perfectly reasonable for me to be paid $5 million a year . Yet if I 'm the CEO of a philanthropic organisation , it 's absurd for me to be paid more than $150,000 . And as people have families , they start to leave the sector out of necessity and our best and brightest disappear ... <p> We also have to make every company understand how important it is to have a philanthropic arm . We have one : we joke that it 's our job to make the money and it 's their job to spend it . Our research shows that companies that have a philanthropic aspect to what they do statistically do better , because people trust them more . And they feel better about it . Jeff Bezos stepping forward with $10 billion for climate change is a great signal , but we need to see all of these major corporations getting involved . They need to prioritise and market their philanthropic projects the same way they do the rest of their businesses . We have to make it cool to care . And it sucks that this only happens in the face of tragedy . We need to make it commonplace . <p> Will Donald Trump get a second term ? If you had asked me a few months ago I would have said that it was definitely looking that way . But I think everything has changed now . We are in extreme times right now and it could very much change our economy , our wellbeing and change how the election looks . We do n't know how people are going to react . The biggest problem we have is distrust of the political system . Joe Biden is an amazing guy who has had an amazing career , but he has also had a long career in politics . And the reason Trump is in office is because people did n't trust politics . I do n't even think everything Trump does is bad . But I think he does so many things wrong that even when he does one thing right he says something wrong . Like I said before , it does n't matter how smart you are , it 's all about trust . We need leadership we can build some trust around . <p> What 's the best joke you 've ever heard about the music industry ? There 's a line in the David Geffen documentary when he 's talking about management . He says the job of a manager is to move mountains , but when they get moved , the client looks at it and says , " It was supposed to be there . "
