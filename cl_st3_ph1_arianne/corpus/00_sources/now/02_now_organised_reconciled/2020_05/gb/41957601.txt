<p> Environmental activists and diplomats welcomed the U.N . ' s decision to put off climate talks until next year , saying the delay would allow world leaders to recalibrate their plans in light of the coronavirus pandemic and avoid the uncertainty surrounding the next presidential election in the U.S. <p> Organizers said Wednesday that the U.N . Framework Convention on Climate Change conference scheduled for November in Glasgow , Scotland , would be postponed until sometime next year in light of the global pandemic . Under the original schedule , the talks were set to open six days after Election Day in the U.S. ; now negotiators will have at least several months to prepare for how to respond to the outcome . <p> " It was never ideal that the conference was going to start a couple days after the U.S. election , " said Sue Biniaz , a former State Department climate official who left the government in 2017 . " It might actually be better for the Paris agreement to give countries a little more time . In 2015 when we said let 's do this 2020 update of enhancing national pledges , we were certainly not imagining a global recession at this point . " <p> President Donald Trump can officially remove the U.S. , the world 's largest historical emitter , from the Paris climate agreement in November . Democratic White House contenders Joe Biden and Bernie Sanders have said they would rejoin the pact of nearly 200 countries . <p> The diplomats who designed the Paris agreement envisioned the 2020 meeting as a crucial political deadline for countries to enhance their climate commitments to more aggressively corral planet-warming gases -- even though many nations are still far from reaching the initial pledges they made in 2015 . But it has become increasingly clear that few governments have the capacity or luxury of working on comprehensive plans to green their societies given the effort to manage the coronavirus pandemic . <p> Some nations worry financing for climate change will come to a " trickle " as countries necessarily shift resources to combat the virus . <p> " I think it 's a very good decision -- it would be crazy to organize this , " said Laurence Tubiana , who led the process on the Paris agreement , of the postponement . " Everybody is fixed on the solution of the health crisis and the recovery . " <p> Tubiana and others said pushing the talks to next year could lead to a stronger outcome , if they encourage governments to craft post-coronavirus stimulus plans with climate action in mind . Tubiana suggested spending packages could focus on resilient infrastructure and preparing a workforce for zero-carbon energy , transportation and manufacturing sectors . <p> " Soon , economies will restart . This is a chance for nations to recover better , to include the most vulnerable in those plans , and a chance to shape the 21st century economy in ways that are clean , green , healthy , just , safe and more resilient , " said Patricia Espinosa , executive secretary of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change . <p> Even the European Union , which has positioned itself as the world 's climate leader , has seen coronavirus overshadow its ambitious " Green Deal " plan that aims for a sweeping decarbonization of its economic and energy system . <p> EU Green Deal chief Frans Timmermans said Wednesday the decision " makes sense , " adding the Commission wo n't " slow down our work domestically or internationally to prepare for an ambitious COP26 , when it takes place . " <p> Over the past year , European climate diplomacy has focused on persuading major emitters -- especially China , India and other emerging powerhouses -- to raise their pledges this year , and follow Europe 's lead in adopting a climate neutrality goal by 2050 . For the U.K. , hosting the conference was meant to showcase the country 's international reach -- even after Brexit . <p> " We will continue working tirelessly with our partners to deliver the ambition needed to tackle the climate crisis and I look forward to agreeing a new date for the conference , " COP26 President-Designate and U.K Business Secretary Alok Sharma said in a statement . <p> Last month , former Bank of England Governor Mark Carney , who 's advising the U.K. government on climate finance , warned that the coronavirus pandemic was making it more difficult to prepare for November 's COP26 . " Within the private finance work there is international and financial diplomacy which will have to be conducted by other means , but we will do our utmost in order to accomplish that , " he said . <p> Some nations also worry financing for climate change will come to a " trickle " as countries necessarily shift resources to combat the virus , said Ambassador Janine Felson of Belize , who is the deputy chairwoman of a group of smaller island states . She said delaying this year 's meeting might therefore be beneficial in some ways . Belize already is diverting funds and personnel away from climate initiatives to address the coronavirus , and the Central American nation will also face other pressures like preparing for the Atlantic hurricane season . <p> " Countries really are in a pickle , " Felson told POLITICO . " How do we expect countries will be able to put forward something meaningful and robust in terms of climate ambition ? " <p> The calls on the U.N . to postpone the conference had been growing louder as scores of international bodies , such as the U.N . Convention on Biological Diversity , had postponed their programs , and others moved meetings online . <p> Germany 's Environment Minister Svenja Schulze said late last month that Germany would hold April 's Petersberg Climate Dialogue , one of the political summits leading up to the climate conferences , via videoconference . The U.K. team leading the upcoming talks has been engaged in virtual diplomacy even while dealing with travel restrictions wrought by the coronavirus , noted Alden Meyer , director of strategy and policy with the Union of Concerned Scientists . <p> But insiders say the U.K. does n't want a repeat of Madrid where large blocs of vulnerable parties felt excluded from the process . Virtual consultations can cover some part of the work , " but you 're not going to get those agreed to until you can get people together physically , " Meyer said . <p> Activists and long-standing climate summit participants also cautioned that shifting negotiations online risked sidelining vulnerable and developing countries which might not have access to the internet and face other challenges to connect . Campaigners also worried that online negotiations would limit the number of outside groups that could participate and undermine transparency in the process . <p> Still , many say Covid-19 offered an opportunity to rebuild with an eye toward climate change . They noted climate change is another looming global crisis that scientists have for years warned nations are not adequately addressing or prepared to handle . <p> " Country delegations should use this extra time to ensure the economic response to Covid-19 does n't entrench the climate crisis , but instead accelerates the transition to a zero carbon world , " said Mohamed Adow , a climate activist with Power Shift Africa . <p> One of the main unresolved issues from previous climate talks that required extra attention in the run up to Glasgow hits at the center of economic recovery and climate change : How to boost green finance , especially for poorer nations and those most immediately affected by rising seas , drought and other events linked to climate change . <p> Given its role in global finance , the U.K. team has planned to make banking and money flows a main pillar of the COP26 program . Some climate advocates suggested the extra time between the crisis and the conference would enable countries to consider stimulus plans that put people back to work while also achieving long-term emissions reductions and preparing for the effects of climate change . <p> " Maybe you have really intense focus on the climate finance and economic issues -- bring those two together in the recovery from this virus , " said Rachel Kyte , the former World Bank vice president and special envoy for climate change who is now dean of Tufts University 's Fletcher School . " If in the teeth of this crisis the public sector has understood that it has a role and it can print money , we need to carry that new re-realization into this recovery for climate .
