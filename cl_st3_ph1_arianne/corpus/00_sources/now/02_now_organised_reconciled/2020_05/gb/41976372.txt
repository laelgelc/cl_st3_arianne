<h> Why it 's time to allow golf to get back into the swing of things <p> THIS is an open letter to the Scottish Government , to argue the case for playing golf . <p> I can go to the supermarket where I social-distance with over 75 people in an enclosed environment . <p> I can drive several miles to a major hardware store . When I arrive there I social-distance with over 50 people as I stand in a queue in the open air . I can go to my local park where I social-distance with over 100 people in the open air . <p> But I can not play my local golf course -- is this fair ? I do not believe so . My drive to the golf course is no different from driving to the hardware store . When I arrive at the course I can social-distance with no more than four ( or two , if that is more acceptable ) people in the open air . <p> When I finish my round of golf I leave the golf course promptly . There is no social interaction : the closest you are likely to come to any other golfers is probably a minimum of 50 yards . <p> As any golfer will know , social distancing is a natural occurrence and golfers are sensible people who know the importance of staying at least two metres apart at all times . <p> Golfers are responsible people , and appropriate protocols could be adopted which would mean that playing golf is actually safer than going to your local supermarket , park or hardware store . <p> I consider the case for an immediate return to playing golf ( under strictly controlled conditions ) is irrefutable . Please see sense and permit golf to be declared open . <p> Rob Campbell , Captain , Balmore Golf Club , Glasgow . <p> I AM pretty sure that I speak for most locals when I say that I want this current coronavirus scare to be over soon , if only to allow us to resume our daily routine . <p> I think that the main problem is in politicians assuming that we 're not capable of making sensible , informed choices . <p> For example , I 'll sit in my car while my wife joins a massive queue to enter , say , a local Tesco shop .
