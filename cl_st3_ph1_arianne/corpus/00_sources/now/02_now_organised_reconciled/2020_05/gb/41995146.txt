But they still have plenty of money from previous years ' activities to be able to step in with support . <p> Besides imposing curfews , they have been providing various public services . These vary hugely around the world , but there are some common trends . <p> For one thing , these organisations have been providing free food and other essential goods to poor people who are running out of cash . <p> A few weeks into the Italian lockdown , for example , this was happening in Naples and Palermo . <p> In Naples , the local camorra crime gang has even been making home food deliveries to people along with illegal drugs . <p> This comes as no surprise in a region where the state was slow to provide help even to those who are entitled to such benefits , never mind the unentitled millions who earn a living in everything from agricultural labour to domestic ? work but are not tax-registered . <h> You may also want to watch : <p> Criminal groups in southern Italy have also been providing financial help to individuals , with gifts of sometimes 300 euros or 400 euros at a time ( ? 266 to ? 354 ) . <p> In Mexico , the criminal cartels have also been providing food to the poorest in the states of Guerrero , Michoac ? n , Tamaulipas and Guanajuato . <p> They seem to take marketing more seriously than their Italian counterparts . The Gulf cartel of north-east Mexico , for example , has been handing out boxes of food and hand sanitiser sealed with a sticker bearing its name and logo . <p> Meanwhile , such has been the shortage of health supplies in parts of Mexico that some hospitals have even been seeking the help of the cartels to procure the necessary equipment . <p> Organised crime groups have also been delivering essential goods in Colombia , South Africa and Japan . <p> In Japan , for instance , the yakuza distributed masks and toilet paper when they were scarce in supermarkets . <p> In Brazil , at a time when president Jair Bolsonaro has been underplaying the severity of the pandemic , gangs have reportedly been offering hand sanitiser to people in the favelas . Such groups can easily access these goods by exploiting their business networks and trafficking routes . <p> Criminal groups are also providing financial help to struggling local businesses . <p> One example is the ' ndrangheta , the strongest mafia group in Italy , which is offering loans at interest rates lower than the local banks . These loans are primarily aimed at the likes of small businesses in construction and hospitality , who ca n't access credit from banks but urgently need liquidity . The ' ndrangheta and similar groups have plenty of funds to make available . <p> Mexican criminal groups are also giving out loans to small businesses . No doubt the coronavirus response from president L ? pez Obrador is an added incentive : he has been refusing to borrow to provide a stimulus package , while continuing the country 's austerity drive -- despite protests from many Mexicans . <p> Yet support can work both ways . The huge amount of money that some governments are injecting into the economy will provide optimal opportunities for fraudulent business claims and the like . The sight of mafia bosses in Italy being released from jail to protect them from coronavirus is only going to help these groups to take advantage . <p> Without a doubt , the help that mafia-type organisations are offering to households and firms will come at a high cost for many countries . <p> The criminals are trying to gain political capital and extend control over their territory . When the crisis is over , they will ask for favours in return , such as money laundering or protection from the police . <p> And a higher unemployment rate will tempt more people to join their ranks to secure stable earnings . <p> For the time being , the governments of countries where these mafias operate must not only deal with the coronavirus but limit the advancement of these groups at the same time .
