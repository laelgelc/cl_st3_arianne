<h> Retreating glaciers caused Adelie penguin population explosion over last 14,000 years <p> Retreating glaciers over the last 14,000 years caused a population explosion among Adelie penguins -- a trend that could continue as Antarctic ice shrinks further thanks to global warming . Scientists say the current environmental conditions are far more favourable to the species than colder conditions seen at the end of the last Ice Age . <p> East Antarctica makes up around 30% of the global population of Adelie penguins . In total , there are estimated to be 1.14 million breeding pairs living there . Over the last 14,000 years , their numbers have increased 135-fold -- largely due to the increase in ice-free land along the Antarctic coastline on which they form breeding colonies . <p> As glaciers retreat further , it is suggested populations should continue to grow . However , this does not necessarily make them ' climate change winners ' , scientists have warned . <p> Retreating glaciers allowed the population expansionWendyPyper <p> The study by the University of Tasmania , Australia , looked at how Adelie penguin populations have changed over the last 22,000 years by studying mitochondrial DNA from multiple living colonies . They sequenced 56 individuals from six different colonies to find the past population dynamics , including abundance and response to changes in the environment . <p> The findings , published in the journal BMC Evolutionary Biology , showed that long term population growth was coincident with the deglaciation of East Antarctica and subsequent Adelie penguin nesting grounds . In comparison , success over decadal and yearly time periods was due to changes in sea ice conditions . <p> Changes to sea ice , the time of its retreat and the extent of glaciation impacts Adelie penguins because of their breeding and feeding habits . An increase in sea ice can lead to adults foraging for longer , reducing how often they are able to feed their chicks . Indeed , in 2013/2014 , unusually extensive sea ice compounded with warm air temperatures led to high rainfall . Because the downy plumage of the chicks not being waterproof , the chicks succumbed to the cold and there was a 100% mortality rate . <p> Climate change will not necessarily lead to further population growth.University of Tasmania <p> Lead author Jane Younger said : " Shrinking glaciers appear to have been a key driver of population change over millennia for Ad ? lie penguins in East Antarctica . Examining these birds ' responses to past climate change means we can begin to predict how well Ad ? lie penguins will respond in the future . Up to now , research has focused on short-term changes in terms of years or decades , but the climate change that is underway right now is likely to have effects over thousands of years . We need to consider millennial-scale trends alongside contemporary data to forecast species ' abundance and distribution changes under future climate change scenarios . <p> " Adequate food supplies must be available to sustain an expanding population of Ad ? lie penguins . Whether this will be the case in the future remains to be seen , as the impacts of climate change on Ad ? lie penguin prey species , such as Antarctic krill , are unclear at this time . "
