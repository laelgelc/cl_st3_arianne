<h> Commentary : Why this ' new normal ' is n't new or normal <p> When Luke Wojtaszek , a member of GRID Alternatives ' Equity , Inclusion and Diversity team shared an article about a Sudanese man born and living in Denmark during the coronavirus lockdown subtitled , " Billions of Brown and Black people have known restrictions , repression and deprivation all their lives , " it resonated with me here in these United States of America . Especially when I see ( mostly white ) people standing around and riding up and down the street in downtown and suburban San Diego , waving American flags and acting like being on lockdown is something new . This is the essence of privilege . <p> As reported by The San Diego Union Tribune , a Black woman ca n't walk her dog on an open San Diego beach without being accosted and arrested by police , but groups of white folk can violate the social distancing and mask mandate by the dozens and not even receive a citation . This is the essence of white privilege . <p> And many of you reading this are chafing or have already stopped reading because you are chastened by the exposure of your white privilege . Maybe you were out in the crowd without a mask , while Black and brown Americans , people you do n't know , of course , bear the brunt of the COVID-19 crisis . This is the essence of white fragility . <p> This same privilege allows many of you to have comfortable blinders , making invisible those of us -- Americans just like you -- who are trapped in a world of stated and unstated but vehemently imposed racial , gender , homophobic and religious limitations on our liberty just for being who we are . No , you did not completely miss the Jim Crow racism that hides just under the skin of 21st century America . Some of us Americans ca n't go where we want to go and do what we want to do because we know there will be consequences . You know the feeling -- at least now you do . <p> Advertisement <p> What is the message sent to Black American men and boys when two white men hunt and murder a Black young man because he was " running through our neighborhood " ? How would you like to be attacked , threatened and harassed because you are Asian and there is a pandemic that has the name of one Asian country affiliated with it ? Would you like to be denied a seat on an airplane or denied immigration to the land of opportunity ( though not for all ) because you " appear " to be a Muslim ? Or worse yet , because you are ? <p> America 's post-coronavirus environment can not look like the pre-virus environment . America is faced with a grave opportunity . Will we go back to the old " normal " of unearned white , male , property owning , cisgendered privilege , or will we move forward to a new future that more closely represents the values this nation gives lip service to in its Declaration of Indepedence ? Will we consider that when you do nothing to stand up for inequality , you are complicit in the inequities of our society and consent to the treatment of the " other " because you are safe ? <p> One example of how to seize on this new opportunity is the nonprofit where I work . We see the connection between justice for all and access to affordable , reliable and renewable energy . Our Equity , Inclusion , &amp; Diversity Statement says , " Under-resourced communities and communities of color disproportionately bear the burden of environmental injustice and climate change . " Let 's take advantage of this resetting of the world 's priorities to build a new future that truly works for everyone -- employment opportunities , business opportunities , communities previously impoverished by systematic discrimination now thriving , and real liberty and justice for all . But we will have to give up white supremacy and unexamined white privilege to do it . The virus has changed the world . Let 's keep making changes for the better for everyone , especially those who were left out of the pre-virus equation . What will you do ? <p> Honor ? is Outreach coordinator for GRID Alternatives San Diego and past President of the NAACP San Diego Branch .
