<p> The number of diagnosed COVID-19 cases in the Bay Area continued to climb on Monday , but the rate at which the lists grew seemed to slow down . <p> San Mateo County health officials added 48 additional cases to its list on Monday . The update included figures from two days ; after adding 20 cases in the Saturday update , the county did not release new figures on Sunday . <p> Elsewhere , the daily increases were modest to tiny . Santa Clara County added 13 people to its list , its lowest one-day increase since last Wednesday and the fourth time since April 22 that fewer than 20 additional cases have been added in a day . <p> Alameda County 's list grew by 27 ; San Francisco 's by 22 , and Contra Costa County 's by only two . <p> Santa Clara County also reported two more deaths . They have the most deaths ( 117 ) among the 10 Bay Area counties and trail only Los Angeles ( 1,229 ) , Riverside ( 161 ) and San Diego ( 139 ) among other counties in the state . <p> The novel coronavirus has killed 63 people in Alameda County , 51 in San Mateo County , 29 in San Francisco and 28 in Contra Costa County . None of those latter four counties reported additional deaths Monday . <p> Santa Clara County health officials have confirmed 2,244 cases of COVID-19 , fifth among counties in the state behind Los Angeles ( 25,662 ) , Riverside ( 4,180 ) , San Diego ( 3,927 ) and Orange ( 2,743 ) counties . The cases have been split equally among men and women , and 52% of the patients have been between 31 and 60 years old . <p> The hardest-hit city has been San Jose , where about two in every three people with the virus live . <p> Also in Santa Clara County , 131 people were hospitalized with COVID-19 symptoms , and 44 were in the intensive care unit . The county had 1,226 beds available in case of a surge of patients . <p> Alameda County 's 1,776 cases are the second-most in the Bay Area . Monday marked the first day health officials there added fewer than 30 cases to the list since April 20 . Cases of the virus have been spread fairly evenly between males ( 51% ) and females ( 48% ) . Officials have said that 1 percent of cases have affected people of other or indeterminant gender . <p> About one in every three patients in Alameda County has been of Hispanic or Latino descent , and half of the county 's cases come from Oakland ( 535 ) or Hayward ( 362 ) . In Berkeley , which keeps its own statistics separate from the county , 54 people have been diagnosed with coronavirus . <p> The 48 cases added in San Mateo County brought that county 's total to 1,281 since the pandemic began . More females than men have caught the virus in San Mateo County , with 687 ( 54% ) having been infected . Health officials also said that 51% of the victims ( 656 ) are between the ages of 30-59 . <p> San Mateo County reported that 61 people were hospitalized with the virus , and that 17 of them were in the intensive care unit . The county had 61% of its ICU beds available and 94% of its beds and 92% of its ventilators available for a surge in cases . <p> In San Francisco , 1,624 people have tested positive , and the 22 added to the list Monday was less than half the number of patients ( 59 ) added Sunday . <p> Health officials said half of San Francisco 's patients caught the virus within the community , and that the virus has hit males hard . Two of every three victims have been male , and 56% of all the patients have been between 31 and 60 years old . <p> San Francisco hospitals were treating 90 COVID-19 patients on Saturday , the day health officials last updated those totals , and 30 were in the ICU . The city had 37% of its ICU beds available and all 312 beds it may need in case of a surge of cases . <p> The two additional cases in Contra Costa County marked the third time since April 24 that county health officials have added fewer than 10 cases in a day . In all , 947 people have tested positive in the county , and 53% of them ( 506 ) have been female and 18% ( 174 ) between the ages of 51 and 60 . The Hispanic population has been hardest hit ; they have accounted for 29% of the cases despite comprising just 26% of the population . <p> Health officials for seven Bay Area jurisdictions announced rules for the new stay-at-home order last Wednesday .
