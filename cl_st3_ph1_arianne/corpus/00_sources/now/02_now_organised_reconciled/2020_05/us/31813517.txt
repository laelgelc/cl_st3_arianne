This puts people off , but this does n't have to be the case -- especially at a time when we need them more than ever . <p> Even before the pandemic , strong support for a change was being felt on both sides of the Atlantic . In the U.S. the Whistleblower Protection Reform Act of 2019 passed through Congress with strong support from both Republicans and Democrats last September . <p> While in the U.K. , a new law seeking an improvement to the 1998 Public Information Disclosure Act is gathering momentum , and enlisting political support from both sides of the U.K. 's red and blue political divide . <p> Baroness Kramer , co-chair of the APPG for Whistleblowing tells Forbes that legislation is in the works to create The Office of the Whistleblower , and the early stage proposals have broad support across the political spectrum . <p> The Office , the first of its kind in the world , would have the powers to meet whistleblowers and act as a point of contact for individuals who wish to make a disclosure . Within the office would be a panel of 20 or more legal representatives to " to advise and support whistleblowers , " while financial obligations would come from a specialist " fund " for individuals whose disclosure is deemed , by the Office , to have harmed their employment , reputation or career . <p> Baroness Kramer tells Forbes that despite the U.K. 's 1998 legislation , " we really have n't managed to clamp down on retaliation against whistleblowers . " Adding , " So often a whistleblower ends up in an employment tribunal , which was never the issue . The issue was never -- were they a good or bad employee ? " But rather , " Has the issue they 've been talking about actually been investigated ? " <p> Kramer is not the only voice to describe whistleblowers as " the canaries in the coal mine " and people who are celebrated by the public but exiled and castigated professionally . Kramer says , " The public is entirely with the whistleblowers . " Now the law needs to catch up . <p> Dr Li Wenliang is remembered . <p> AFP via Getty Images <h> Compensation For A Conscience <p> Lawmakers and courts are currently approaching the same problem from different joining paths . Georgina Halford-Hall , CEO of Whistleblowers U.K. , says that legislation is needed to clarify that whistleblowing should be viewed positively , like " compliance . " Rather than negatively , like a form of corporate treachery that eventually unravels in costly , exhausting and embarrassing legal battles . <p> One of the most important issues that joins whistleblowers around the world is the need to stop treating them like enemies or traitors . This is as true in Wuhan , China , as it is in the corporate client world of accountancy and auditing . <p> Signs of change are moving through the system already . Just a fortnight ago a former partner at London-based global accountancy giant EY walked away from the U.K. 's High Court with $10 million compensation after he blew the whistle on billions of dollars ' worth of cash transactions and Moroccan gold deals that had passed EY 's audit without proper " due diligence . " <p> Casting gold bars . <p> Kirill Kukhmar/TASS <p> Amjad Rihan , a former partner at EY , took his case to the High Court against EY after he was forced to resign following the identification of major alleged violations committed by EY 's audit client , Kaloti , one of Dubai 's largest gold refiners . <p> Rihan , a specialist in " climate change and sustainability services , " identified the wrongdoing during an " assurance " audit in 2013 , seeking , according to court documents , an " independent written view on the quality and propriety of the audit client 's business practices . " <p> Court documents state that Rihan became aware of " serious irregularities " , adding , " Kaloti was knowingly dealing in gold bullion smuggled out of Morocco coated in silver . . . thereby avoiding restrictions on the export of gold from Morocco . The painted ' silver ' was then declared to be gold on arrival at Dubai , " according to the documents . Rihan also identified $5.2 billion in gold and cash transactions in 2012 . <p> EY recognized the wrongdoing , and in a statement to Forbes , acknowledge the " irregularities " unearthed by Rihan . The London High Court documents reveal that these irregularities , " gave rise to a reasonable suspicion that Kaloti was involved in money laundering , as EY accept . " <p> After a tangle with the Dubai regulator , a government body called the Dubai Metals and Commodities Center ( DMCC ) who " put improper pressure on the claimant and EY . . . to do its reporting work in a way that would reduce to vanishing point the visibility of the Morocco gold and cash transactions issues . " Rihan resigned in 2014 , " fearing for his and his family 's safety . " <p> EY <p> LightRocket via Getty Images <h> Vindication , But Little To Celebrate <p> Like many whistleblowers , Rihan was vindicated after Kaloti 's practices became public knowledge in a later audit by another leading firm . <p> But the damage had been done . He had lost a substantial amount in wages , his career was over , and he struggled to find work . Six of what could have been the best years of his career had passed . <p> However , earlier this month London 's High Court ruled in favor of the whistleblower . Rihan was awarded the sum of $10,843,941 in U.S. dollars and ? 117,950 for having been , according to his lawyer , " forced out " of his job and career at EY . <p> In a statement , EY Global told Forbes that they are " Surprised and disappointed by the judge 's decision , " describing the sum paid to the whistleblower as " unprecedented . " Adding , " We will appeal . " <p> The Dubai Metals and Commodities Center ( DMCC ) has not yet responded to a request for comment . <p> Kaloti has not yet responded to a request for comment . <h> Need For A Law <p> Georgina Halford-Hall , CEO of Whistleblowers U.K. , personally assisted Amjad Rihan and his lawyers during his case against EY . She tells Forbes that whistleblowers like Rihan need to be seen as a " compliance tool " with " economic as well as social benefits . " <p> For Halford-Hall , Amjad Rihan 's case was entirely avoidable . And legislation is the best way to nudge firms toward embracing a different approach to those looking to speak out . <p> Halford-Hall says forcefully that once the whistle is blown there should be no " personal sacrifice " and describes the accusation often leveled at whistleblowers -- " You know what you are getting yourself into " -- as " total nonsense . " <p> For Halford-Hall , " every whistleblower is the sign of institutional failure . " In an ideal world , they would n't exist . But today , as the coronavirus pandemic enters its truth and reconciliation period , we need them more than ever . <p> Should they be paid , or incentivised ?
