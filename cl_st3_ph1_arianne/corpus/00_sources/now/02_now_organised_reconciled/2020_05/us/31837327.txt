That 's a delay of several weeks from the previous moving target , which was next week . <p> The House Armed Services Committee , meanwhile , previously postponed its scheduled April 30 markup and has yet to reschedule it . <p> Senate Armed Services Chairman Jim Inhofe ( R-Okla. ) has argued negotiations on a final bill should n't be left to the final weeks of the year like last year 's defense bill . But with delays in the House and Senate and a legislative calendar already shortened by the 2020 campaign , lawmakers will likely be under pressure to hammer out a final bill on time . <p> House Majority Leader Steny Hoyer has promised to give lawmakers 72 hours ' notice before they are required to return to Washington , pushing the vote until Friday at the earliest . <h> Coronavirus <p> NEW PROBE OF NAVY 'S CORONAVIRUS RESPONSE : The Pentagon inspector general 's office announced on Monday it will investigate the Navy 's handling of the spread of coronavirus aboard its ships . <p> " The objective of this evaluation is to determine whether the Navy has implemented policies and procedures to prevent and mitigate the spread of infectious diseases , such as coronavirus-disease-2019 ( COVID-19 ) , on ships and submarines , " the internal watchdog said . " In addition , we will determine whether mitigation measures that are effective in preventing the spread of COVID-19 were implemented across the fleet . " <p> The Navy , prompted by the recent outbreak aboard the USS Theodore Roosevelt aircraft carrier and its controversial aftermath , is undertaking a review of its own , including identifying how further spread can be mitigated . A House oversight committee , meanwhile , is seeking detailed information from the Pentagon about the Roosevelt case . <p> In the clear ? Gen. He has also tested negative but is self-quarantining this week as a precaution . <p> Is the military health system prepared ? Not very well , according to an analysis published Monday by the Project on Government Oversight , which looked at the medical readiness of the military through the lens of the pandemic , which has now affected at least 5,000 personnel . <p> " The public show of military doctors aiding in the coronavirus response belies the fact that the military health system lacks the ability to handle even the routine health needs of the services during normal conditions , " the watchdog group says . <p> ' Unfunded priorities ' : Significant shortages of doctors at military hospitals and clinics , for example , are blamed on what the group considers misplaced priorities . " Shortages are due in large part to placing a much higher priority on spending for pet weapons programs , " the analysis says . " Evidence of this can be seen in the service 's most recent so-called unfunded priorities list , " which are dedicated mostly to weapon systems that did n't make it into the budget submission . <p> " Unless quick action is taken , " it adds , " the services may not have enough doctors and nurses to respond to either a domestic crisis or battlefield needs when the nation goes to war . Learn more <h> Top Doc <p> FIRST LOOK -- ' UNNECESSARY COSTS ' : Another voice is joining recent calls for the Trump administration to take immediate steps to extend the New Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty with Russia , which is set to expire early next year . <p> A new paper to be published later today from Women 's Action for New Directions , titled Mutually Assured Dividends -- The Case for New START Extension , lays out a series of reasons for retaining the nuclear pact . They include its extensive verification measures to keep the largest atomic arsenals in check and the financial predictability it gives the United States on what investments will be needed over the long term to maintain a viable nuclear deterrent stockpile . <p> Plan B : The group , which promotes demilitarizing U.S. foreign policy , also offers a backup plan should the treaty expire without a replacement . Congress " should consider restricting funding for a nuclear modernization plan that procures more nuclear weapons or delivery systems beyond New START 's central limits -- even if the treaty itself loses effect and enforcement , " it says . <p> " Especially in these uncertain times , the last thing the U.S. needs to do is increase the risk to our national security or create unnecessary costs by suddenly growing -- rather than reducing -- the number of nuclear weapons we have , " Nancy Parrish , WAND 's executive director , told Morning D. <p> CHINESE SPACE PROGRAM SCRUTINIZED : The U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission is out with a new assessment on China 's growing military and civil space programs . <p> It recommends a host of measures to constrain Beijing 's efforts to steal American know-how and to contain the potential threat it poses to U.S. space systems . <p> " Congress should enact new or enhance existing laws to prohibit U.S. government departments and agencies , national labs , universities , companies , fund managers , and individual investors from supporting China 's space program and activities that are inherently military in nature , " states the report from the congressionally-mandated advisory body . <p> It also recommends that the Pentagon , which delivers an annual report to Congress on military developments by the Chinese People 's Liberation Army , also compile " an annual unclassified report on PLA space/counterspace developments and other major players in space security .
