<h> Steven Bowman : Marine Resources working on behalf of coastal communities <p> By Steven G. Bowman <p> For The Virginian-Pilot <p> May 01 , 2020 12:05 AM <p> Steven G. Bowman , commissioner , Virginia Marine Resources(HANDOUT) <p> The critical need to stay at home to protect the health of our loved ones and essential workers has made for big changes in our coastal communities . <p> Missing is the familiar pre-dawn drone of the crabber 's deadrise and the chatter of watermen on the wharves . Oyster shucking houses are idle , charter boats are tied up at the dock and towns that would normally be preparing for an influx of tourists flocking to beaches and harbors are instead urging people to stay away . Places that are usually a flurry of human activity are quiet . <p> Advertisement <p> Nature , however , is louder than ever . In this time of crisis , many have taken comfort in pausing to watch and listen as our Chesapeake and Atlantic coastlines come alive with the springtime returns of osprey , dolphins , shad and a multitude of other fish and wildlife . Anecdotal evidence suggests that families are getting outside , taking more time to fish , paddle and walk beaches , while practicing social distancing . Both common sense and sound science tell us that is a good thing . Connecting with the natural world is a reminder of how it connects all of us . <p> As Virginians , we are fortunate to have the benefits our tidal shores and waters provide . In the 50 years since the first Earth Day , we have made great strides toward more responsible marine resource management . Now more than ever , we are recognizing the connection between our natural resources and our quality of life . <p> As commissioner of Marine Resources , I am responsible for working with my associate commissioners , staff , scientists and stakeholders to protect and manage the commonwealth 's coastal and marine natural resources for the benefit of all Virginians , including future generations . That responsibility includes stewardship of our fish , shellfish , submerged lands , beaches , dunes and tidal marshes . <p> These resources are essential to the economy , security and identity of our coastal communities . Research shows that they are also critical to human health and well being : our coasts , bays and oceans calm us , inspire us , reinvigorate us , regulate our climate , mitigate hazards and provide compounds that can help us develop life-saving medicines . <p> The dedicated public servants at the Marine Resources Commission are doing all they can to help people take advantage of opportunities to get outdoors and to access fresh Virginia seafood . <p> Our Marine Police officers are on duty day and night to ensure the safety of boaters and assist with fisheries compliance and enforcement . The Habitat Management Division is continuing to process permits related to critical economic activity on our shores and waterways , while protecting natural infrastructure that makes us more resilient to climate change and sea level rise . And the Fisheries Management Division continues its work to restore oyster reefs and collect data to assess the health of our fisheries . It 's also working with federal partners and impacted fishing businesses to provide assistance and relief , and to facilitate access to markets . <p> While telecommuting , social distancing and limits on gatherings have made some of this work more challenging , I am proud that we are finding new ways to serve the public through online reporting , permit applications and processing , and decentralized meetings with robust public participation to conduct critical , time-sensitive business . Some of our staff even took home equipment and supplies to set up laboratories in their garages to avoid disruption in fisheries research . <p> The COVID-19 pandemic has presented new challenges for all of us , but together , we will make it through this difficult time and emerge stronger on the other side with a greater appreciation for the things we truly value . The Virginia Marine Resources Commission will work tirelessly to support those who depend upon our waters for their livelihoods , protect the resources that drive coastal economies , and ensure responsible enjoyment of the outdoors .
