<h> The Epic Poem You Need for Quarantine <p> The " Rime of the Ancient Mariner , " written in 1797-98 , is harrowing and prescient , and suits the captive audience of the 2020 pandemic.Art work by Gustave Dor ? / Alamy <p> The British writer Philip Hoare , who as @philipwhale chronicles his predawn swims in Southampton water year-round on Twitter -- and makes his followers in the States shiver and feel like laggards , even if they get up at 6 A.M. -- has done it again . In collaboration with Angela Cockayne , an artist on the faculty of Bath Spa University , and with the backing of the Arts Institute of the University of Plymouth , he has assembled a company of actors , singers , writers , scientists , naturalists , and one polar guide for an online reading of Samuel Taylor Coleridge 's " Rime of the Ancient Mariner . " Following the pattern of the same team 's Big Read of Melville 's " Moby-Dick , " in 2012 , the Ancient Mariner Big Read pairs each participant 's recording with an art work and releases the readings one at a time , building up the poem at the rate of about sixteen lines a day . It began on April 18th , with church bells and a surging crowd , stilled by the seductive voice of Jeremy Irons : " There was a ship , " he rumbles , conjuring the graybeard with skinny hand and glittering eye as he waylays the wedding guest . <p> As you may remember from the Norton Anthology , Volume II , which has been sitting on the shelf since Survey of English Literature , Romanticism to Modernism , sophomore year in college , Coleridge started out as a wanderer in the Lake District with his pals William and Dorothy Wordsworth and ended up a derelict poet and drug addict . Many of the visions in " Kubla Khan " and " The Rime of the Ancient Mariner " were fed by opium . The " Rime , " written in 1797-98 , is so harrowing and prescient that one might suspect the team behind the Big Read of choosing it specifically for the captive audience of the 2020 pandemic . But Hoare , speaking by phone last week , said that the reading was five years in the planning and three in the execution . After " Moby-Dick , " he and Cockayne considered for their next act Defoe 's " Robinson Crusoe , " but settled on the " Rime , " he said , " because it is totemic as a work of literature -- people have a familiarity with it which they do n't realize they have . " He added , " We had no idea we would launch just as the lockdown came into force , as everyone was suffering isolation . " <p> Hoare , whose books include " The Whale " and " The Sea Inside " ( about his swimming habit ) , as well as a biography of No ? l Coward , clearly enjoys wrangling talent . He described sitting at the feet of Jeremy Irons while recording at the Bath Priory hotel . Tilda Swinton recorded Reading No. 8 in the Scottish Highlands -- " Water , water , everywhere , / And all the boards did shrink ; / Water , water , everywhere , / Nor any drop to drink . " She helped put Hoare in touch with Willem Dafoe ( Reading No. 5 ) , who Hoare knew had read the " Ancient Mariner " in preparation for his role in the bleak black-and-white film " The Lighthouse , " of 2019 . The actor 's voice , crisp and distinctive , is perfect for the cold line " I shot the albatross . " A pronounced American accent jumps out at Reading No. 9 as Iggy Pop , recorded in Miami , gives an anguished , over-the-top rendition of " what evil looks had I / from old and young . " At No. 16 , Alan Cumming lends his Scottish burr and creepy vibe to " dry as dust . " <p> The soundscape behind the voices includes all kinds of effects : bells , bubbles , footsteps ( you 'd swear it was Ahab 's wooden leg ) , howling winds , roaring waves , cracking ice , bird calls , whale songs . A clock ticking sets the stage for the surprising , piercingly high voice of Hilary Mantel ( No. 6 ) , recorded in the echoey church of Ottery St. Mary , in Devon , where Coleridge 's father was the vicar . There , Hoare met Samuel Coleridge himself , the **36;280;TOOLONG of the poet , who lives next door in the family manor . Samuel John Taylor Coleridge was the first descendant in more than two hundred years to be named after his uncle the poet , whose drug habit put him in bad odor with the family for generations . The latter-day Coleridge also contributed to the Big Read , recording in Ottery St. Mary , the church where his ancestor was baptized . Incidentally , the soundman in Devon was a young fellow named Ed Shakspeare , a descendant of William who looks like him -- if you shaved thirty years off the best-known image of the playwright ( that portrait ) and gave him a tangle of black hair and a puckish smile . <p> Folded into each day 's reading is a commentary providing biographical notes or literary references ( the horror and the beauty of the poem influenced Mary Shelley , Herman Melville , and Edgar Allan Poe ) ; remarks by the marine biologist John Spicer on climate change and the Cambridge-based nature writer Robert Macfarlane on melting polar ice ; and a strange little note by Tim Dee , the United Kingdom 's pre ? minent writer on birds , revealing that Coleridge had never been to sea when he wrote the " Rime , " much less seen an albatross , with its archangel wings , but he had been impressed by the sight of " a single solitary wild duck " ( No. 7 ) . There are also reflections by the readers themselves . Marianne Faithfull first read the " Rime " as a fourteen-year-old girl at her convent school , and her account precedes her reading ( No. 15 ) -- " I used that as a sort of tree limb , " Hoare said . He recorded Faithfull -- beloved of flower children , onetime lover of Mick Jagger -- in London , and noted her " sea-salt soft-creature eyes " and the tattoo " of a bluebird or a swallow " on her left hand . Her voice , now low and raspy , captures the narrator as sole survivor : " Alone , alone , all , all alone , / Alone on a wide wide sea ! " <p> The poem in its entirety will go live on May 28th , " a mosaic , a phantasmagoria of voices , " Hoare said , in which " The Rime of the Ancient Mariner " will " suddenly assume the shape you 've been wanting all along . " The Big Read will have lasted forty days , the traditional length of a quarantine . Perhaps by then we 'll be allowed to go to weddings again .
