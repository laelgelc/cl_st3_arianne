When you are able to produce a thought of compassion and understanding , it is healing and nourishing for yourself and the world . Just as an acidic cloud produces acid rain , so will the energy of anger , fear , blaming or discrimination produce a toxic environment for ourselves and others ... If today you can produce a thought of compassion , of love , of forgiveness , then that positive thought has the power to transform the negative thought of yesterday and to guarantee a more beautiful tomorrow . " <p> -- " The Art of Living " Thich Nhat Hanh <p> Buy Photo <p> Peter Donohue ( Photo : Times photo ) <p> The balance and symmetry of this paragraph aids in the acceptance of its accuracy , its credibility and is enhanced by its simplicity . As I float through these days of isolation , this paragraph struck me as so true and yet I could not easily put my finger on why it was so true , so right . <p> Only when taking it off the page it was written on , and isolating it , was I able to grasp the undeniable quality of the statement . You ca n't dispute it , you ca n't refute it . And yet in these polarized times , it is so hard to employ it , to live it . <p> We are buffeted by the scientists telling us to shelter at home to mitigate the spread of COVID-19 and the government telling us its OK to start reopening the state . It is not a partisan dispute , for we have a Democratic governor opening up the state consistent with the Republican president 's plan despite not having met the threshold recommendations of the scientific community . <p> Clearly this dispute should not be a dispute , and if it continues , science should win . After all , what do political officials know about the science of a pandemic when compared to people who devote their lives to the study of disease . <p> As I try to digest the difference of opinion on sheltering at home and the need to slowly put people back to work , I have tried to diminish the negative emotional responses of fear , despair , doubt and anger to avoid the " toxic environment " they produce . It is really hard to do . <p> Home alone gives me the time to pause and to reflect before I react , which is a tremendous aid in stopping the anger , doubt or despair from taking hold and influencing action . Sometimes its as simple as not touching the send button on the computer or the phone . <p> I find I often just erase what I had typed after thinking about it and recognizing that it will not produce a positive result and does n't need to be said anyway . There is a lot to be angry about these days and there is a lot to fear . We are facing a vast unknown that may be the most pervasive uncertainty of our lifetime . <p> The uncertainty of the future of my health , survival and economic status could undoubtedly keep me up at night and diminish the quality of my life . If allowed to continue , those concerns could easily intensify , eroding my happiness and enjoyment of life . <p> If caught in the whirlwind of fear , it is extremely important to come back to the moment , breathe deeply and focus on the here and now . I have reasonably good health ; I have my life and my bills are current . Gratitude rises and overtakes the fear . <p> It remains an exercise in perspective . There is no doubt that a cleansing is going on at home and outside . The reduction in travel has had an immediate positive impact on air quality . It would almost suggest that climate change might be the result of human conduct . <p> I am in control of what I do and where I go . There is a 35% increased probability of catching the virus if I attend a gathering inside where social distancing is not observed and a group of more than 10 congregates . <p> I can follow the science . I just read that there may be an antibody treatment that allows the resumption of normal activity . I was surprised at my reaction to this news . I was ecstatic . <p> In that instant I realized I had been troubled by the relaxation of the stay-at-home order . I do n't have to leave the security of my home . I do n't have to go where more than 10 gather until it is safe . <p> This is the opinion of Peter Donohue , who has been involved in the arts in Central Minnesota for more than 35 years . His column is published the third Tuesday of the month .
