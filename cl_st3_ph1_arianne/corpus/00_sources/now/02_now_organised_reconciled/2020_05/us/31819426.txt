<h> Winners and finalists in 15 journalism categories were announced Monday afternoon . <p> Dallas Morning News Photographer Tom Fox reacts after being announced as a finalist for The Pulitzer Prize for Breaking News Photography on Monday , May 4 , 2020 in Dallas . ( Ryan Michalesko / Staff Photographer ) <p> Winners and finalists in 15 journalism categories were announced Monday afternoon . The prizes recognize excellence in newspaper , magazine and online reporting , photography , commentary and criticism . <p> " Tom 's chilling main photo gives us a look at the mass shooter as we 've never seen him , " said Mike Wilson , editor of The News . " To tell the story , Tom drew on journalistic instincts honed over decades . We 're grateful for Tom 's work , for this recognition , and most of all for his safety . " <p> When a gunman clad in a mask and combat gear opened fire with an assault weapon outside the Earle Cabell Federal Building on June 17 , Fox was standing near the doors for a routine assignment . He heard gunshots , then saw a man coming around the corner of the building . <p> " So I pulled up my long lens and saw someone who I realized was the shooter , " he said . " And I think , ' Oh my God . ' I squeezed off a few frames as he picked something up -- a clip , I think -- and then I turned and ran . " <p> Fox later followed law-enforcement officers into a nearby parking lot , where the gunman lay after he was shot . <p> The Pulitzer board noted that Fox 's work was " photographed at great personal risk . " <p> Fox shared in The News staff 's 2006 breaking news photography Pulitzer for images of the aftermath of Hurricane Katrina in New Orleans . His wife , Laurie , and daughter , Avery , were with him at The News ' downtown newsroom to watch Monday 's announcement . <p> The staff of Reuters won the breaking news photography Pulitzer for their images of Hong Kong as citizens protested the influence of the Chinese government . Dieu Nalio Chery and Rebecca Blackwell of The Associated Press also were named finalists for their photographs of human-rights abuses and other horrors during unrest in Haiti . <p> The News has won nine Pulitzers , the most recent in 2010 for its editorial writing on social and economic divisions between northern and southern Dallas . <p> Following are the 2020 Pulitzer Prize winners in journalism : <p> Public Service <p> The Anchorage Daily News and ProPublica won the Pulitzer Prize in public service for illuminating the sparse policing of many Alaskan villages . <p> Breaking News Reporting <p> The staff of The Courier-Journal of Louisville , Ky. , took the breaking news reporting award for quickly unpacking racial disparities and other issues in a spate of governor 's pardons . <p> Investigative Reporting <p> Brian M. Rosenthal of The New York Times won the investigative reporting prize for an expose of predatory lending in the New York City taxi industry . <p> Explanatory Reporting <p> The staff of The Washington Post was awarded the prize in explanatory reporting for a series about the effects of climate change on the planet . <p> Local Reporting <p> The staff of The Baltimore Sun won in local reporting for coverage of the undisclosed financial connection between the city 's mayor and a hospital system she oversaw . <p> National Reporting <p> Dominic Gates , Steve Miletich , Mike Baker and Lewis Kamb of The Seattle Times were named winners for stories that revealed flaws in Boeing 's 737 MAX . Also named winners were T. Christian Miller , Megan Rose and Robert Faturechi of ProPublica for their investigation into a series of deadly naval accidents . <p> International Reporting <p> The staff of The New York Times was awarded the international reporting prize for stories about Vladimir Putin 's regime . <p> Feature Writing <p> Ben Taub of The New Yorker won for his reporting on a man who was kidnapped , tortured and held at Guant ? namo Bay for more than a decade . <p> Commentary <p> The Pulitzer Prize board awarded its prize for commentary to Nikole Hannah-Jones of The New York Times for an essay she wrote for the 1619 Project , which marked the 400th anniversary of the first enslaved Africans being brought to what became the United States of America . <p> Criticism <p> Christopher Knight of the Los Angeles Times won for his critique of a plan to overhaul the L.A. County Museum of Art . <p> Editorial Writing <p> Jeffery Gerritt of the Palestine ( Texas ) Herald Press was named a winner for his editorials on the deaths of inmates at a county jail before they had faced trial . <p> Editorial Cartooning <p> Barry Blitt of The New Yorker was awarded the editorial cartooning prize for his work skewering the Trump White House . <p> Breaking News Photography <p> The staff of Reuters won the breaking news photography Pulitzer for their images of Hong Kong as citizens protested the influence of the Chinese government . <p> Feature Photography <p> Channi Anand , Mukhtar Khan and Dar Yasin of The Associated Press won a Pulitzer in feature photography for images made during India 's clampdown on Kashmir , where a sweeping curfew and shutdowns of phone and internet service added to the challenges of showing the world what was happening in the region of 7 million people . <p> Audio Reporting <p> A first-ever award for audio reporting went to the staff of This American Life , Molly O'Toole of the Los Angeles Times and Emily Green of Vice News for " The Out Crowd , " an examination of the Trump administration 's " remain in Mexico " immigration policy . <p> Special Citation <p> The board of the Pulitzer Prizes also issued a special citation Monday to Ida B. Wells , the trail-blazing black investigative journalist . The board said the citation comes with a bequest of at least $50,000 in support of Wells ' mission , with recipients to be announced . <p> In honoring her during the prize announcements , the board noted " her outstanding and courageous reporting on the horrific and vicious violence against African-Americans during the era of lynching .
