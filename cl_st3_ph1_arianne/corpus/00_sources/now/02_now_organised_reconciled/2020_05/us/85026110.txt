With members mostly stuck at home for the past few months because of the coronavirus pandemic , Congress 's list of legislative to-do items has piled up -- especially in the House , which has operated on a more limited schedule than the Senate . <p> The House is facing deadlines on spending measures , a defense policy bill , a reauthorization of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act , a massive federal highway bill , flood insurance , water infrastructure , and the long-delayed reauthorization of the Violence Against Women Act . " We have a full agenda that people have been working on for a long time , so it 's a continuation of that , but also an intensification , " Speaker Nancy Pelosi told reporters last week . <p> Not to mention , there could be more coronavirus packages , while Democrats also have their eyes on November . They are eager to start passing bills that showcase their priorities and agenda . But the election year could also make it tougher to get any serious legislating done . " Nobody , I think , amongst the people with whom I 've been working with , believes that we ca n't get things done , " said Rep. Dean Phillips ( D-Minn . ) . " The question is , do we have the fortitude and the intention and the power in collaboration to do so . " <p> To help make up for lost time , the House will be in session for two days this week , which was originally scheduled to be a recess break . And lawmakers who are unable to return to D.C. will be armed with a new tool to work remotely : this will be the first week where lawmakers can vote by " proxy . " And a number of members have already signaled they plan to use the new system , which allows them to cast votes through a colleague . It was seen as a risky move , given that many senators need to travel across the country to return to the Senate , nearly half of them are age 65 or older , and one lawmaker has already contracted coronavirus . <p> But so far , there have been no new outbreaks in the Capitol . Just one staffer is known to have tested positive for the virus , forcing Sen. Lamar Alexander ( R-Tenn. ) into quarantine . And McConnell feels like his plan is paying off : Republicans have been able to show that they 're not just sitting on the sidelines , contrasting themselves with their counterparts in the House . <p> The Senate is also essentially a preview -- and a test case -- for what the rest of America is going to look like when everyone returns to work . Offices have been operating with a skeletal staff , strict social distancing protocols have been put in place and hand sanitizer is available everywhere . " I hope hand lotion does n't cause cancer , because we 've been using a lot of it , " said Sen. John Kennedy ( R-La . ) .
