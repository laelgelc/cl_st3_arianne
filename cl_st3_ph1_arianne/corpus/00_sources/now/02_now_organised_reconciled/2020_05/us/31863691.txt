The device ( which is a cap with wires ) is used so babies do n't have to be put under anesthesia which is super risky for them.Photo : Karen Warren , Houston Chronicle / Staff photographer <p> Aisha Atkinson was purposeful when choosing the name for her child , Aries Reign Williams . <p> It had to be strong , resilient and persistent . It also needed to start with the letter " A , " to match his parents ' first names . His middle name , Reign , was chosen to symbolize regality and wisdom . <p> " Ironically , this kid just embodies it without us even really having to teach him , " said Atkinson , 31 . " That name holds so much more meaning over his personality and the way his life will turn out . " <p> Aries is not a typical 2-year-old . His brain works differently since he was born 17 weeks early , at 23 weeks gestation , weighing only 1 pound , 11 ounces . <p> During birth , he suffered Grade III and IV-level brain bleeds , the most severe for a premature baby . Those led to a ventricle swelling in his brain and the development of the neurological disease hydrocephalus , and eventually , cerebral palsy in both legs and his left arm . <p> He spent 150 days in a neonatal intensive care unit at Children 's Memorial Hermann and underwent four procedures -- one to fix a small hole in his heart and three on his brain . There is a possibility he will eventually suffer from seizures and various neurological issues during his lifetime . <p> Because of his medical conditions , Aries will likely have to undergo regular tests and MRI scans for the rest of his life . And up until recently , he would have to be put under anesthesia for each . <p> CTOT is a wired hat that uses infrared light to map a child 's brain , said Dr. Manish N. Shah , a pediatric neurosurgeon at UTHealth and director of the Texas Comprehensive Spasticity Center at Children 's Memorial Hermann . It 's the first device of its kind that can be used on children . <p> When the cap is activated , doctors can see what happens deep in the brain when the child tries to walk or move . This visual aid helps doctors predict which patients are good candidates for surgeries to lessen the effects of epilepsy and cerebral palsy , Shah said . Using CTOT means Aries and other kids like him wo n't have to undergo anesthesia , which has major risks for young children . <p> " Every time we put a tube down the throat of a patient , we risk them getting pneumonia , aspiration or we could damage their vocal cords , " Shah said . " Some say anesthesia is detrimental to brain function . It has been determined that children under the age of 3 should n't be getting elective surgeries and elective anesthesia . " <p> But nothing about Aries ' medical situation is elective , his mother said , which made him a good candidate for CTOT . <p> In January 2018 , Shah approached Aries ' parents about Aries participating in a clinical study for the device . He is one of four children that have been tested so far . <p> " When Dr. Shah explained the dynamics of the device , it really made me think we needed to be supportive because it will impact ( Aries ) and so many other children who are born premature , " Atkinson said . " I started thinking of the future of how we can map the brains of children and adults . The revolutionary impact of the device is too phenomenal for us to turn it down . " <p> Shah developed the device with researchers Eva Sevick and Banghe Zhu at the Center for Molecular Imaging at the University of Texas Health Science Center . <p> CTOT was developed using the same technology as night-vision goggles worn by soldiers during the Gulf War , Sevick said . <p> " We know infrared light can travel through several centimeters of tissue , so we have been working to find ways to measure infrared light as it passes through tissue , " Sevick said . " We can make several measurements across the head and use that to map the oxygenation status . " <p> According to their study , published by the IEEE Engineering in Medicine and Biology Society , the device is able to image the whole brain 's activity within three seconds of data-acquisition time . In that time , a tomographic image forms , which shows CTOT-derived oxygenated and deoxygenated blood volumes . Those images spatially correlate with what can typically be seen by an MRI brain scan , according to the study . <p> The design is unique because the technology used to create it was classified military information for many years , Sevick said . A whole new type of medical imaging has been developed using the technology measuring near-infrared light propagation through tissues , including the lymphatic system . <p> According to the World Health Organization , 15 million babies are born premature globally every year . Premature birth complications are the leading cause of death for children younger than 5 . <p> A major concern for those children is their brain 's oxygenation levels , which is why the imaging process has to be done on a regular basis , Sevick said . <p> " While babies have a plastic brain , there 's a lack of therapeutic strategies for them , " she said . " We have n't been able to measure these kids , so this is a potential solution to a huge problem . " <p> CTOT took about three years to develop and two years to test , Shah said . In 2015 , he began working with Sevick and Zhu after he sent them the idea for a cap-based , whole-brain imaging device . <p> Then and now , there are no other scientists in the world who can take light from one side of the skull and turn it into a meaningful picture , Shah said . <p> With data derived from Aries and the other babies , the scientists are able to adjust the algorithm to ready the device for the next set of study participants . The goal for the device 's second generation is to shorten the imaging time from minutes to seconds , said Zhu . This is the beginning of the story , he added . <p> The device is just one tool that will help Aries live a full life , said Atkinson . For a child that had only a 20 percent chance of survival , Atkinson said Aries surprises her every day . <p> " This kid is by far the happiest kid on the planet , given everything he 's been through , " Atkinson said . " It blows my mind that someone can have so much happiness and joy within him . He just has this very resilient personality .
