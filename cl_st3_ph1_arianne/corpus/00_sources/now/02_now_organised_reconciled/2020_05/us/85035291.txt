<h> COP26 climate talks pushed back to November 2021 <h> The UN 's climate science advisory panel has warned that time is running out if a rise in global temperatures is to be capped at 1.5C <p> The UN 's climate science advisory panel has warned that time is running out if a rise in global temperatures is to be capped at 1.5C ( AFP Photo/Johan ORDONEZ ) <p> Paris ( AFP ) - Critical UN climate negotiations at which nations were expected to ramp up plans to combat global warming have been pushed back a full year to November 2021 , British politician Alok Sharma , president of the talks , announced on Twitter Thursday . <p> " Pleased to have agreed a new date for @COP26 , " wrote Sharma , Britain 's Secretary of State for Business , Energy and Industrial Strategy . <p> " COP26 will now take place between 1 and 12 November 2021 . " <p> Britain proposed the new dates for the Glasgow conference -- which had already been suspended -- in a letter to the UN climate forum , citing health concerns amid the coronavirus pandemic , and the need for more time to prepare the 30,000-strong meet . <p> The revised schedule was vetted and approved Thursday night . <p> Possible drawbacks of the delay were laid out in a briefing note , obtained by AFP , prepared ahead of the deliberations . <p> " A broadly shared concern relates to the potential loss of momentum in the UNFCCC process , " the note said , using the acronym for the UN climate body . <p> One 2020 deadline in the original schedule was the submission of revised -- and hopefully more ambitious -- " nationally determined contributions " , each country 's plan to cut greenhouse gas emissions . <p> Current national pledges submitted in annex to the 2015 Paris Agreement would allow the planet 's surface to warm at least three degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels , far above the ceiling of " well below " 2C -- and 1.5C if feasible -- agreed to in the landmark treaty . <p> The UN 's climate science advisory panel , the IPCC , has made it clear that time is not on our side , especially if global warming is to be capped at the safer level of 1.5C . <p> CO2 pouring into the atmosphere must be halved by 2030 , and reach " net zero " by mid-century , if that more ambitious goal is to be met , the IPCC concluded . <p> Economic fallout from the pandemic is projected to reduce global CO2 emissions this year by 4 to 7 percent , but will have scant impact on global warming 's long-term trajectory , according to a recent study in Nature Climate Change . <p> - Climate not on hold - <p> Most observers endorsed the postponement , but cautioned against inaction . <p> " Shifting the date of COP26 is understandable , but there can be no let-up in tackling the climate crisis , " said Andrew Steer , president and CEO of the World Resources Institute in Washington DC . <p> Trillion-dollar recovery packages across Europe , North America and East Asia offer a " once-in-a-generation moment to rebuild societies to be more sustainable , equitable , resilient and healthy , " he said in a statement . <p> Global warming continues to gather pace , other experts pointed out . <p> " The coronavirus pandemic has not placed the climate crisis on hold , as the continuing spate of extreme weather across the world makes clear , " said Alden Meyer , director of strategy and policy for the Union of Concerned Scientists in Washington DC . <p> Last week , south Asia 's first super cyclone in two decades ravaged eastern India , including Kolkata , while northern India was hit by searing heatwaves and locust swarms linked to climate change . <p> Scientists in the United States , meanwhile , warned of a more severe-than-usual Atlantic hurricane season . <p> Other important environmental conferences have also been pushed back . <p> The critical COP15 UN conference on biodiversity , originally slated for October this year , will almost certainly " not happen before May , " a source involved in its organisation told AFP . <p> A quadrennial meeting in Marseille of the International Union for the Conservation of Nature ( IUCN ) , which manages the Red List of endangered species , was postponed from June to January 2021.
