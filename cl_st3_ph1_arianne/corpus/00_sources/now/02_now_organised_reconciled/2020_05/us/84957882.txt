<p> Climate change is burning the candle at both ends when it comes to the prevention and treatment of cancer . <p> On one hand , climate change has led to a rise in extreme weather events , including hurricanes and wildfire , increasing the exposure of surrounding communities to carcinogens . At the same time , these events make it difficult for cancer patients to receive the care and treatment necessary to survive . <p> In 2017 , Hurricane Harvey flooded chemical plants , oil refineries and Superfund sites , carrying the carcinogens into the surrounding area . Meanwhile , patients undergoing radiation therapy and other treatment were cut off from local hospitals as the supply chain for medical resources was disrupted . <p> In an article for the American Cancer Society , Leticia Nogueira , K. Robin Yabroff and Aaron Bernstein argue that efforts to prevent and treat cancer should include addressing climate change . <p> ADVERTISEMENT <p> America is changing FAST . The hospital and pharmaceutical industry are the largest contributors to the U.S. health care system 's carbon footprint , said the authors , citing a study that found the pharmaceutical industry is 50 percent more carbon-intensive than the automotive industry . <p> Still , there are no estimates for the carbon footprint of cancer care , suggesting there is much to be done . <p> " Although some may view these issues as beyond the scope of responsibility of the nation 's cancer treatment facilities , one need look no further than their mission statements , all of which speak to eradicating cancer . Climate change and continued reliance on fossil fuels push that noble goal further from reach , " the authors wrote .
