If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <p> I have a two-year-old son , which makes him about the age many of this year 's graduating college seniors were on 9/11 . As I think about all that has been taken away from the class of 2020 , I ca n't help but imagine what it was like for them -- and their parents -- on Sept. 11 . The joy with which they bounded out of bed on that day , and the confusion as they were laid to bed that night . Hushed conversations they did n't understand , televisions abruptly turned off when they walked into a room , adults they 've never seen cry , fighting back tears . <p> Tragically , this would n't be the last time their worlds would be turned upside down . From the horrors of 9/11 that they were too young to understand -- though they certainly felt and were shaped by -- think of the events that followed . The introduction of the iPhone and the massive spread of social media , a deep Recession , school shootings and school shooting drills , countless standardized tests , a dawning realization that climate change and fights over climate change wo n't be wished away , the polarization of media , politics and religion , and so much else . <p> It is important to pause and think about how strong the class of 2020 must 've been to get through all of this . Some members of the class of 2020 had family members deeply impacted by the opioid crisis , others worked so unbelievably hard to get into their top college ; every student who survived these challenging times and managed to graduate deserved their college graduation . <p> Instead , the class of 2020 is met -- once again -- with one more impossible challenge . And what 's worse , when they look to us adults for help , they are -- once again -- often met with derision . <p> We know that there are much larger issues in the world than missing a college graduation : there is massive unemployment and too many people are sick and dying . Though there are larger issues than missed senior springs and graduations , it does n't mean that college seniors should n't be upset that another thing is being taken away from them . In fact , our failure to let students grieve says far more about us than it does about them . <p> Buy Photo <p> We can celebrate the class of 2020 by thanking them , not only for persisting through challenging times , but for continuing to fight for a better world . ( Photo : Amy Newman/NorthJersey.com ) <p> Take the common charge that this generation of students are snowflakes . When we begin thinking about all these young adults have been through so far , is it fair -- or even accurate -- to call them that ? Might it be the case that we attack them to avoid taking responsibility for not giving them the childhood they deserved ? That we attack them when they protest , or scoff when they seek counseling , because when they do this it forces us to confront our failure to shelter and nurture them in the ways we would 've liked to ? <p> By any accounting these last years have been rough for children and young adults , but instead of squarely facing this reality , we turn our heads away , indulging in the types of name calling that almost certainly ensure nothing will be done to ease the burden this generation has inherited from us . <p> Why do n't we face facts ? Because -- for many of us -- it would break our heart . I hate that my children have to do lockdown drills at school . I hate that they spend so much time on screens at school . I hate that their generation may be worse off than my generation , who is already on track to be worse off than my parent 's generation . Our youth have it rough , and instead of lashing out at them to protect our self-image , I suggest we take this moment -- college graduation season -- to muster the courage to acknowledge all they 've been through . <p> How might we do this ? The first step is simple . Do n't try to delegitimize their pain by comparing it to greater tragedies others are undergoing . That should be easy . The class of 2020 is experiencing a loss , and we should be grown up enough to make the attempt to be with them in their pain . <p> More , we can celebrate the class of 2020 by thanking them , not only for persisting through challenging times , but for continuing to fight for a better world . It is easy to mock misplaced idealism , but we should n't pretend that the class of 2020 does n't have reason to wish for a better world than the one they are living in . Though we do n't always have to agree with them , it is shameful to pretend that they do n't have very good reason to do everything in their power to fight for change . <p> When I look at my two-year-old and my three other children all under the age of eleven , I wonder what world they will be graduating into . When the class of 2020 is where I am now , will they use the difficulties they faced in their childhood and youth to denigrate the difficulties my children will go through -- perpetuating the next iteration of the snowflake myth -- or will they lead us into a different future ? <p> Because the class of 2020 had the courage to seek counseling and protest injustice , because they worked so hard did n't reap the reward of a normal graduation , will they be more willing to face the pain of the generations coming after them , thereby creating a world worthy of the infinite promise of each child ? <p> Given my experiences with the class of 2020 , I am confident that they will take the resilience they 've been forced to cultivate since 9/11 and use it to stay awake to the ways we can make our world better . Where our generation turned away -- calling them snowflakes , pretending that school shooting drills are the best we can do , not fighting to make sure their generation is better off than ours -- they will stand strong . <p> Though the class of 2020 wo n't have a proper graduation ceremony this year , though they 've already been asked to endure so much already , I hope we find ways to offer them our most sincere congratulations , and I hope they are willing to continue being the truly resilient and hopeful generation they 've already proven to be . We will need all the hope and resilience they can offer as we face the challenges ahead in ways that make it more likely that the class of 2040 will have a very childhood and coming of age than the one this class did . <p> I hope , in these polarized times , we can agree that our children deserve far better , and that we will finally begin demanding more in their name . Celebrating the class of 2020 is one way we can signal that we are waking up from our complacency , beginning work that should 've started long ago . <p> Jeff Frank ( Photo : Tara Freeman/St .
