And , over the past week , these efforts to address the disproportionate level of police brutality and systemic injustices that Black communities have endured have spread from the U.S. to Canada and Europe . <p> Universities may question what role they have to play as " apolitical " institutions invested purely in scholarly pursuits . Similarly , scientific societies may argue that their goal is to share , support , and advance ground-breaking research . However , in only examining the products of these institutions - the classes taught , publications outputted , and papers presented - neglects that there are people taking those classes , publishing those papers , and presenting their work . People who , in many disciplines , were and continue to be primarily white . <p> To say that a university is " apolitical " ignores the history of academic institutions implementing quotas on the number of Black students that could be admitted or denying them admission altogether and led to the establishment of Historically Black Colleges and Universities . These restrictions have limited the ability of not only Black academics , but also Indigenous students and other people of color to contribute to numerous scientific disciplines . And , as a result , scientific societies have not seen many contributions from minoritized academics . <p> However , universities and scientific institutions have acknowledged that working with non-white scientists requires active efforts , having established Offices , Initiatives , and Committees devoted to Diversity , Equity and Inclusion . Yet , these programs are not deploying their expertise to support students , staff , and colleagues affected by our current political context ( after all , manyothergroups have experienced violence from law enforcement ) . While silence may seem to be a neutral course action , it belies the leaders ' unwillingness to sit with their own discomfort and engage the community whose well-being they are responsible for . <p> It is understandable that universities and scientific societies feel that there is a risk in " taking a stance " .
