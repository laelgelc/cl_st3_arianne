UNITE ? <p> Rich countries are seeing exploding unemployment rates , and that does n't even take into account the fact that around six in ten workers globally work in the informal economy as day laborers , gig workers or in subsistence mode . " They have no rights , no minimum wage , no rule of law . For them , health and safety rights are just a myth , " Sharan Burrow , general secretary of the International Trade Union Confederation ( ITUC ) , told Global Translations . That reality is made even more precarious during a global health crisis . <p> Universal Social Protection : The International Labour Organization ( ILO ) says that every worker is entitled to legal protection , but that 's not the reality , including when the job ends . Overall , the ILO estimates that half of working people could lose their jobs within the next few months , but more than half of those will not be entitled to unemployment benefits . Burrow 's labor organization is asking for $37 billion from the governments of rich countries to fund five years of social protections in the poorest countries . <p> Burrow is especially furious that major global companies lack the financial capacity to ensure workers are kept on through the crisis , and is urging protests against the pandemic 's big corporate winners , including Amazon . She wants pension fund investors to push Amazon much harder on how it treats its workforce and what rights it gives them to organize . <p> Salary guarantee : Denmark 's left-leaning government convened employers and unions in mid-March to hash out a plan to pay 90 percent of wages for any worker sent home due to coronavirus impacts ( up to a maximum $3,800 per month ) . The U.K. also offers salary protection of up to 80 percent and up to $3,000 per month , including for independent contractors . Other countries with similar schemes include Australia , Austria , Germany and the U.S. , via its Paycheck Protection Program . <p> Small business inequality : While their rights may differ , independent contractors , gig workers and micro-business owners report similar working conditions and incomes as salaried workers . But people of color in that category are suffering disproportionately from the pandemic . The number of African-American business owners in the United States has plummeted more than 40 percent so far this year , compared to 22 percent for the overall population , according to the federal government 's employment survey . And due to the essential work they do , Latino communities are particularly exposed to coronavirus . <p> A message from Bank of America : <p> Bank of America is working to ensure small businesses are receiving much-needed relief through the Payment Protection Program ( PPP ) . Learn more . <p> WHO VALUES THEIR WORKERS ? <p> Minimum wage : Australia and Luxembourg have the highest minimum wages in the world , at $13 to $14 per hour , depending on exchange rate fluctuations . And Australia 's Centre for Future Work says now is actually the right time to boost minimum wages as a form of stimulus , in addition to recognizing that much low paid work is undervalued . <p> Pandemic bonus ! Some countries have put special measures in place during the pandemic . While American nurses are facing pay cuts , Ghanaian health workers recently won a 50 percent raise , and France and Japan pledged bonuses for doctors and nurses . An Iraqi governor said public health employees would be rewarded with free land , and the United Kingdom is paying $65,000 to families of medical workers who die of Covid-19 . <p> Migration halted across the world : While many employers are laying off staff , farmers are facing labor shortages due to Covid-19 , thanks to travel and immigration restrictions that make it more difficult to find people to pick their crops . That difficulty may soon extend into other professions : Around 30 percent of health workers in America are foreign born ; in Australia it 's nearly half . Migrants , themselves , are often trapped in untenable living conditions , which has led to major Covid-19 outbreaks in Saudi Arabia and Singapore . There 's more on the horizon , with over 30 million migrant laborers in the Middle East alone . <p> Edelman 's Trust Barometer survey found that more than half of workers surveyed are very worried about long-term Covid-related job loss . Half believe business is doing poorly , mediocre or completely failing at putting people before profits . As few as 19 percent of surveyed workers believe their business has been protecting them . <p> What really keeps Xi Jinping up at night ? Unemployment . China is now focused on joblessness rather than GDP targets , with real unemployment now likely in double digits ( there 's no verifiable number ) . China needs to find employment for 8.7 million new college graduates , a problem made more acute by the country 's gender imbalance .
