<h> Do you know how to identify poison ivy ? The extremely irritating plant is easy to spot in Massachusetts in late spring . <h> May 22 , 2020 10:39 am ET Updated May 22 , 2020 10:44 am ET <p> What most poison ivy in Massachusetts looks like right now . The reddish plants can be seen along roadsides , trails , and at the edge of your backyard . ( Shutterstock / K Steve Cope ) <p> NATICK , MA -- It 's never a bad time to learn how to spot a poison ivy plant -- but this time of year in Massachusetts , it 's particularly easy to identify . <p> As poison ivy leaves grow in spring , they take on a reddish color . This makes it easy to avoid among all the other green plants of spring . In summer , poison ivy leaves turn green , making them harder to pick out . <p> A good description of the plant from the UMass Amherst Center for Agriculture , Food and the Environment : <p> " The middle leaflet of the three tends to be larger than the two on either side , but each measures from 2 to 4 inches in length . At maturity , the leaflets are dull or glossy green . New leaf shoots in spring tend to be droopy and reddish-green in color while in fall , the leaflets turn a vivid orange , red and yellow . " <p> Poison ivy grows everywhere . Along roadsides , at ankle level along your favorite trail , and even among the grass in your backyard . Next time you 're outdoors , peer into the brush where a wooded area meets civilization . You 'll see dozens of reddish three-leaf clusters growing from stems between 1 inch and more than a foot high . <p> The plant is also quite a survivor . According to UMass , poison ivy can grow in either wet or dry soil , shade or direct sunlight -- and there 's even evidence that climate change and higher carbon dioxide levels help the plant thrive . <p> Most people who touch the plant will develop an icky , itchy rash . That comes from a resin called urushiol present on the plant 's leaves and stems .
