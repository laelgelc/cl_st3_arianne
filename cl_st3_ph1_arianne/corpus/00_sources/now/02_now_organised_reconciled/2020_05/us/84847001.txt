<h> Buchanan : What will be the new American cause ? <p> After the Great Pandemic has passed and we emerge from Great Depression II , what will be America 's mission in the world ? <p> What will be America 's cause ? <p> We have been at such a turning point before . <p> After World War II , Americans wanted to come home . But we put aside our nation-building to face the challenge of a malevolent Stalinist empire dominant from the Elbe river to the Barents Sea . <p> And after persevering for four decades , we prevailed . <p> What , then , did we do with our epochal victory ? <p> We alienated Russia by moving our NATO military alliance into the Baltic and Black Seas . We launched bloody , costly crusades for democracy in the Middle East that , invariably , failed . We exported a huge slice of our manufacturing capacity and economic independence to a coddled China . <p> Historically , blunders of such magnitude have undone great powers . <p> Even before COVID-19 , Americans had begun to realize the folly of decades of mindless interventionism over matters irrelevant to our vital interests . " Unsustainable " was the word commonly associated with our foreign policy . <p> But if our foreign policy was unsustainable during President Trump 's economic boom , with unemployment at record lows and a bull market to rival the Roaring ' 20s , can an interventionist foreign policy be sustained after the losses of this major depression we have induced to kill the pandemic ? <p> If the Democrats win in November , we know their priorities : national health insurance , carbon taxes , the Green New Deal , open borders , amnesty , reparations and wealth redistribution to reduce social and economic inequality -- an agenda costing trillions of dollars . <p> And Democrats will be looking at the defense budget as a slush fund to finance this new progressive era . <p> If the Republicans win , given the influence of hawks and neocons among the party elite , interventionism may get another run in the yard . <p> Having been exposed as naive beyond belief for their indulgence of China from the Bush I days to 2016 , some Republicans are looking to make amends by casting China in the Soviet role in Cold War II . <p> There is talk on Capitol Hill of refusing to pay off U.S. bonds that Beijing holds and of suing China for the damages done by the coronavirus , as China failed to alert the world the pathogen was loose . <p> Americans should think long and hard before defaulting on U.S. government debt and consider the consequences if we open a door to claims against sovereign nations for past sins . <p> Iraq was invaded in 2003 to force it to give up illicit weapons of mass destruction it did not have . Baghdad could have a case in international court against America for the unprovoked war waged against that country . <p> While the U.S. appears determined to bring back manufacturing -- especially of products critical to the health , safety and defense of our nation -- there seems to be no stomach among the public for a war with China . <p> But again , with the democracy crusades now repudiated , what is America 's cause , what is America 's mission in the world ? <p> Preventing climate change , say our liberal elites . Yet , even before the pandemic , global warming ranked near the bottom of national concerns . <p> The situation in which America will find herself after the virus passes and depression lifts will be almost unprecedented . <p> We will have the same treaty obligations to go to war on behalf of dozens of nations in Europe and Asia and at the same time , we will be running deficits on the order of $3 trillion a year with a shrunken economic base . <p> If Trump wins , borders will be tightened . The U.S. withdrawal from the Mideast will continue . U.S. manufacturing will begin to be repatriated . Transnational institutions will be downgraded , ignored and superseded . <p> The watchword will be what it has lately been : " America First . " <p> In a second Trump presidency , there would likely be even less concern for how other nations rule themselves . <p> Does it matter to us if Russia is led by an autocrat not unlike a Romanov czar , that Hindu nationalism wields the whip hand in India or that Hungarians have rejected Earl Warren 's ideas about liberal democracy ? <p> In recent decades , the U.N . General Assembly has seemed to resemble the bar scene in " Star Wars . " But is how other nations choose to rule themselves any business of ours , if those nations do not threaten us ? <p> In the 19th century , when the Hungarians had risen up against the Hapsburg Empire and sought U.S. intervention , Henry Clay opposed it : <p> " Far better is it for ourselves ... and for the cause of liberty ... that we should keep our lamp burning brightly on this western shore , as a light to all nations , than to hazard its utter extinction amid the ruins of falling or fallen republics in Europe . " <p> Not only President Trump 's preferences but also events seem to be driving us toward such a destiny . <p> To borrow from the title of historian Walter A. McDougall 's classic work , America 's future is as a promised land , not a crusader state .
