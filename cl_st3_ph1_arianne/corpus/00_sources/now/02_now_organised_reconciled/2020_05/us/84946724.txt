<h> Deep Water : ' Ocean , ' By Erin Covey-Smith <p> Maine poems edited and introduced by Megan Grumbling . <p> Share <p> These days especially , any number of distractions and worries too often conspire for our attention . But this week 's poem , Erin Covey-Smith 's " Ocean , " shares a moment of being brought back to senses and presence , and reminds us that , even now , that great body of water at our coast has the power to help us transcend what weighs on us . <p> Covey-Smith graduated with an MFA in printmaking from Concordia University in 2012 and found her way to the poetry world via her letterpress and book arts experience . She lives in Freeport , and her work can be found in the 2019 Littoral Books anthology " A Dangerous New World : Maine Voices on Climate Change . " <p> Ocean <p> By Erin Covey-Smith <p> Driving home , maybe-listening to the news , <p> thinking of the groceries I forgot to buy , and <p> something else that thought it was important . <p> And then -- the ocean , where it always , <p> unexpectedly , is : stolidly gray , unassuming , <p> a subtle sigh into horizon . It demands nothing <p> and snaps me to attention more abruptly <p> than postcard days of glinting sun , flirting surf . <p> The ocean swallows words as I drive past , <p> then challenges me to name it anyway . <p> Because this , this is no list I forgot to write , <p> no background news . Some would call it God -- <p> but I wo n't . God is too heavy , and this , <p> this weighs nothing at all . <p> Megan Grumbling is a poet and writer who lives in Portland .
