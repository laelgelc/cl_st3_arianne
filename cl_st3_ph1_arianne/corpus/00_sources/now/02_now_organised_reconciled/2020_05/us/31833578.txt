<p> Natalie Gerdes ' family owns Casamento 's , the Uptown restaurant famous for its oysters and tile decor . The restaurant switched to takeout when the coronavirus stay-at-home order began in mid-March , but it has seen a 75% drop in business , Gerdes said . <p> " We 're just trying to get by , " she said . " Some days are a struggle . " <p> Few people are ordering raw or chargrilled oysters these days , and those who do are mostly regulars , Gerdes said . " I 'm sure that a lot people would prefer to eat them here and have the experience , " she said . <p> Like farmers across the U.S. , oyster farmers are struggling to keep their businesses afloat while restaurants are closed or running at limited ... <p> More than almost any other food , oysters tend to be consumed in restaurants . And that means oyster farmers are struggling mightily to keep their businesses afloat while restaurants are closed or running at limited capacity . The pandemic is just the latest blow inflicted on an industry already reeling from environmental disasters . <p> Most Louisiana oyster farmers lease areas of the sea floor from the state . The farmers plant " cultch " -- a hard material for oyster larvae to latch onto -- and harvest the oysters when they mature . <p> The crop is sold to processors , who sell to distributors , who sell to restaurants . When the restaurant industry screeched to a halt , processors froze the product they had on hand . With freezers full of oysters , they stopped buying . <p> This left most Louisiana oyster farmers without a place to sell their crop , said Mitch Jurisich , chairman of the Louisiana Oyster Task Force . " We really have no work , " he said . " Demand is down like 99% . " On a normal year , about 5,000 sacks would be sold at the dock in Empire each week , Jurisich said . But only about 500 sacks per week are being sold there now . <p> Unlike most Louisiana oyster farmers , Boris Guerrero does n't dredge up oysters from the sea floor . He 's a manager at Grand Isle Sea Farms , which sells specialty oysters grown in floating cages . The company is also unusual in that it sells directly to restaurants . <p> With an already established presence online , Grand Isle Sea Farms was able to transition to selling oysters directly to consumers . Customers are more willing now to try shucking at home and are eager to share pictures of themselves doing so , Guerrero said . <p> Still , direct sales have not made up for the sales the company has lost from restaurants . And , it 's time-intensive to deliver oysters in small quantities , Guerrero said . The differences in how Guerrero does business also have n't protected his company from the environmental issues that have plagued oyster farmers across Louisiana . <p> While the Gulf Coast produces more oysters than anywhere else in the U.S. , production was down between 50% and 85% from historic levels even before the pandemic , according to a 2018 report by The Nature Conservancy . The Deepwater Horizon oil spill alone wiped out between 4 and 8.3 billion adult sub-tidal oysters in the Gulf of Mexico . <p> In 2018 , Louisiana was the only Gulf state producing as many oysters as before the oil spill . But recent flooding in the Mississippi River basin dented the progress . <p> Last year , immense rainfall from the Midwest flowed down the Mississippi River , through the Bonnet Carr ? Spillway and other channels and out into Louisiana bays . The spillway diverts water away from New Orleans when the river is high . Last year , the structure was opened twice in one year for the first time and released a record volume of river water . Flooding on the Pearl River further added to the onslaught of freshwater . <p> Too much freshwater kills oysters , which can not swim away like fish . <p> Data from the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries shows that last winter 's commercial harvest on private leases in the state dropped was down by more than 70% from the previous year . <p> " Last year was pretty terrible for us , " Guerrero said . Guerrero knows it 's bad when the sacks of oysters he pulls out of his floating cages sound hollow . " It 's just empty shells , " he said . <p> Guerrero is starting to see the telltale signs of oyster die-offs again this year . The spillway was opened last month , marking the sixth time the structure has been opened in the past decade . The increasing frequency of spillway openings has been attributed to a climate change-driven increase in rainfall in the Midwest . <p> The $2 trillion coronavirus stimulus package signed by President Donald Trump included $300 million to mitigate economic hardships faced by the fishing industry as a result of the pandemic . Louisiana fisheries were allocated $14.7 million of the $300 million available . <p> U.S. Rep. Garret Graves said the allocation was disproportionate to the value and size of Louisiana 's fishery . " How is it possible for Louisiana , one of the top fishing states in the nation , to only receive this much while other states with a fraction of a fishing industry get more money ? " he said in a news release . " This is a slap in the face . " <p> Graves said he has reached out to the House Natural Resources Committee to request an investigation into the amount states were allocated for fisheries . <p> " South Louisiana and the fishing communities are still hurting from the 2019 opening of the Bonnet Carr ? Spillway and face competition with the falsely labeled crawfish from overseas and imported shrimp taking precedent in the market over our domestic and sustainable seafood products , " he said . " The added pandemic has caused irreparable harm on our people . I look forward to digging deeper into this and finding out why Louisiana got the short end yet again . " <p> State Sen. Sharon Hewitt introduced a resolution to the Senate Thursday that empowers the Coastal Protection and Restoration Authority to work with the Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries to develop a plan to help the oyster industry recover . <p> Oyster farmers have criticized the state 's plan to rebuild the coast by using controlled openings in the Mississippi River to feed dying wetlands with a fresh supply of sand and dirt carried by the river . They see the impacts of the spillway as evidence of what is to come with the controlled openings , called sediment diversions . <p> Federal permitting for the coastal restoration projects requires that the state evaluate the impact to fisheries , including the oyster industry . Hewitt 's resolution includes a study of the impacts as well as the development of ideas to address those impacts . <p> " We do n't want to rebuild the coast at the expense of the oysters , " she said . <p> Louisiana Department of Wildlife and Fisheries Assistant Secretary Patrick Banks said oysters are more than just a staple of Louisiana cuisine . They also buffer the coast from waves , provide habitat for other species and improve water quality by filtering out suspended particles in the water as they feed . <p> The state 's plan to address the economic hardships of the oyster industry will not be prescriptive , he said . Instead , it will provide oyster farmers with options , such as leasing areas of coastal waters previously closed off to oyster farming . The resolution includes a December deadline for coming up with a plan to help the oyster industry . The plan will be vetted by the Oyster Task Force . <p> Short-term solutions can not address the blows the industry has taken from environmental disasters on top of the pandemic , said Brian Callam , director of the Louisiana Sea Grant Oyster Research Laboratory . The lab is currently working to develop an oyster that is more tolerant of freshwater . " But that 's not a golden bullet that 's going to be able to solve all the problems , " he said . <p> Still , the demand for oysters is unlikely to go away anytime soon . " Eventually , people will be able to leave their houses and they 'll want to have oysters , " Callam said .
