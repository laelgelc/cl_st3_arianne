If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <h> Orchid mystique : Tallahassee Orchid Society grows a legacy <p> If you are an orchid lover and have questions on how to grow or procure healthy orchid plants , come to the Orchid Club meeting in Tallahassee and explore the world of orchids . <p> The Tallahassee Orchid Society was established by a group of orchid lovers in 1965 with an intention of protecting these vanishing species . The society has been educating people and promoting orchid cultivation ever since . They are also determined to continue the legacy of maintaining orchid species passed on to them by fellow orchid growers who are no longer with us or hybrids developed several decades ago . <p> Several members in the club still own the plants once grown by founding members . You can also be a part of this orchid legacy by joining the Tallahassee Orchid Society . <p> Orchids are an enchanting flowering plants but growing orchids can present a challenge to even the greenest thumbs . <p> You must be patient and learn a few tips to grow them successfully . Online is one source but , for regular growing guidance your local club or local nurseries will know what works best in this area . <p> Paphiopedilum growing in the living room ( Photo : Hemanth V. Setty ) <p> Orchids can be successfully grown in the greenhouse . Most of the professional growers or hobbyists own a greenhouse where one can maintain a controlled environment . Not everyone can afford a greenhouse and the majority of the orchid enthusiasts either grow orchids inside their house or on a patio . <p> I have been successfully growing cattleyas , dendrobiums , vandas , bulbophyllums , phalaenopsis , encyclias , epidendrums and vanilla orchids inside my house and on the patio . There are few important tips that you must know before starting your hobby . <p> Most of the orchids are epiphytes and have aerial roots that cling tightly to the host plant in nature . They prefer to live on the shaded , west side of the tree , where it takes advantages of the greatest amount of humidity . Like most of the plants , orchids depend on their environment which influences their developmental process . External circumstances such as light , temperature , air and soil/medium and nutrition must be favorable for effectively growing and blooming of orchids . <p> Happy growing Vanda on the patio . ( Photo : Hemanth V. Setty ) <p> Light is one of the important factors which delivers the energy necessary for plant development . When growing inside the house one should consider the light intensity during temperate spring and in summer months . During summer months when the intensity is too strong , a southern/western expose is only good when the orchid plant can be provided with enough shade , such as from blinds , shades or curtains . It is not too difficult to gauge light requirements for every orchid . <p> Leaf color is good indicator , if it is dark green it means that plant is not receiving adequate light . If a plant is receiving sufficient light than the leaf will be light green in color . Too little light causes weak growth , and often is the reason it is not blooming when it should . <p> Temperature is another important factor . For most orchids , temperature between 60 - 82 F is ideal . If you are comfortable , your plants will also be comfortable . <p> Temperature around orchid plants depends on air movement and the place where your plants are either on windowsill or kitchen counter , bathroom , living room or patio . In most cases , day temperature of 75-90 F and 59-65 F night temperatures are ideal but many orchids can go much warmer , and do fine outside . Bring your plants indoors from the patio if the temperature drops below 55 F. <p> Phalaenopsis on the kitchen countertop . ( Photo : Hemanth V. Setty ) <p> Like light and temperature , humidity of air and soil plays an important part in successful orchid cultivation . You can use a hygrometer to measure the humidity . Most of the time the room or house humidity will be around 35% . If a windowsill is used provide additional humidity , especially during winter months . <p> You can either use a humidifier or a large bowl filled with water to increase humidity . But do not place the pot in the container with water , as it will drown the plant . You can likewise mist the plant with water to increase humidity . In summer months , provide additional air circulation by using either table or ceiling fans . Stagnant , heavy air is detrimental to orchids . <p> Like surrounding air humidity , moisture in the medium is also important . Too much water is harmful . Overwatering is the quickest way to kill an orchid . <p> A good rule of thumb is -- as little as possible , but as much as necessary . The best indicator is again the leaf . Yellowing of the leaves means too much water or roots rotting . It also correlates if air humidity is too high . Orchids do not want watering every day . Allowing for a short period of drying out is better than drowning them . Water once a week to 10 days in winter and once or twice a week in summer is ideal . <p> Phalaenopsis orchids must have only the roots watered , as water left in the center of leaves can cause crown rot and kill the plant . Pots can dry out at different rates , especially if different sizes or in different mixes or pot material . <p> Water quality also plays an important role in successful orchid cultivation . I use tap water for my orchids but , well water is also fine . Water thoroughly and allow to drain well . Do not use softened water , as the salt is toxic to the plants . Also , it should be at about room temperature . Tap water can be very cold in winter , and phalaenopsis can be damaged if too cold . Also , never use ice cubes . I have never seen them in the jungle . Save them for your lemonade . <p> Potting material and repotting are also a vital factor . The potting mixture used should allow air/water to move through it . At the same time , it should retain sufficient moisture and nutrients . I use a commercial orchid mixture which contains bark/charcoal/perlite , available locally . To that I add a little extra sphagnum moss to retain moisture . <p> I usually repot when I see plants pot-bound or decline in health . I do not have the issue of diseases and pests . I always quarantine new plants and wipe leaves once a month either with soap or 70% alcohol to remove any debris or bugs . Scale and mealy bugs are the most common pests and can be controlled this way . It is important to regularly inspect your plants for any issues . <p> By growing orchids , you are indirectly helping in their conservation . <p> Many orchid species in the wild all over the world are on the verge of extinction due to overcollecting , habitat loss and climate change . Your garden could become a survival place for orchids.There are several native orchids that grow in the panhandle of Florida , mostly on the ground , and not in trees . <p> Tallahassee Orchid Society members at their 50th Annual Orchid Show in 2016 , what an achievement . ( Photo : Tallahassee Orchid Society ) <p> You can become part of this orchid legacy by joining the club . Regular club activities involve invited guest speakers , member presentations , show-and-tell with member 's plants . The best part is interaction with fellow growers . <p> The club hosts an annual orchid show every year in April . Reputable Florida orchid and supply vendors participate in the show and bring ones that are great to grow . This year the 54th Annual Orchid Show was canceled due to the coronavirus pandemic . <p> The Tallahassee Orchid Society meet monthly on the second Thursday at 7 p.m. in the Jubilee Cottage at Goodwood Museum and Gardens ( www.goodwoodmuseum.org/ ) .
