<h> A stake on a farm in Harpersfield , N.Y. , marked the proposed path of the Constitution Pipeline back in 2015 . <h> Timothy Puko <p> WASHINGTON -- The Environmental Protection Agency is setting new rules aimed at speeding up Clean Water Act permit approvals that are often a sticking point for pipelines and other major infrastructure projects . <p> The changes implement a proposal the agency made in August . It sets a one-year deadline for states that have permitting authority under the Clean Water Act to take final action on a permit request . It also narrows the scope states have for rejecting permits , limiting it to only water-quality issues , EPA officials said . <p> The changes are part of a broad set of moves from the Trump administration seeking to limit how bedrock environmental laws can be applied to climate policy . Agency Administrator Andrew Wheeler , who plans to sign the new rules Monday , said in August that the change would bolster federal authority in granting these permits and help prevent states from overstepping theirs . <p> This rules change marks an uncommon move for an administration that has often weakened federal authority to give more power to states as it deregulates oversight of the U.S. energy industry . But these changes may have greater impact on states that are opposed to President Trump 's agenda , where leaders have tried to constrain the growth of fossil-fuel production and consumption as a tactic for slowing climate change . <p> Mr. Wheeler , when he proposed this rules change , singled out New York and its yearslong fight with Williams Cos . over the Constitution Pipeline and other stymied pipeline projects that have kept new natural-gas supply from reaching the Northeast . He accused New York and other states of using the Clean Water Act " to hold up these projects , " saying the policy changes were necessary to get states to follow the law and not overreach . <p> " EPA is returning the Clean Water Act certification process under Section 401 to its original purpose , " Mr. Wheeler said in a statement . " We are following through on President Trump 's Executive Order to curb abuses of the Clean Water Act that have held our nation 's energy infrastructure projects hostage . " <p> President Trump has promised to streamline government reviews and speed up energy projects . Interstate pipeline projects , in particular , have faced delays , with new lines to take natural gas to coastal consumers stalled for years under court and regulatory challenges as environmentalists and the public pressure government officials to slow fossil-fuel development as a way to curb climate change . <p> The new rules are likely to join a long list of deregulatory actions from the Trump administration that end up challenged in the courts . In October , 22 mostly Democratic-led states and the District of Columbia filed comments asking the EPA to throw out its proposal , saying they have " grave concerns " that it unlawfully curtails state authority that has decades of precedent in agency guidance and policy . <p> Laura Watson , the senior assistant attorney general in Washington , the state which has led the opposition , said in Senate testimony in November that her state was n't using this part of the Clean Water Act as a way to address climate change , but only water-quality issues . Pipelines and other infrastructure projects can cause leaks and disturbances into waterways , and Ms. Watson said these changes would effectively exempt some pollution and projects from state oversight . <p> " Taken together , it is plainly evident that EPA 's interest is not in protecting water , as the Clean Water Act requires , " she said in written testimony . " Rather , EPA wants to gift the industry a process that is so ' streamlined ' as to be virtually useless . " <p> Some corporate analysts are skeptical about these changes . They may have limited impact because states still have broad authority , both under the Clean Water Act and several other statutes , to slow permit reviews .
