<h> Ellie Reynolds : Virginia well positioned for a future of clean-energy transportation <p> By Ellie Reynolds <p> For The Virginian-Pilot <p> May 15 , 2020 12:05 AM <p> Ellie Reynolds , Environment America(HANDOUT) <p> This March , leaders in the legislature set a course to power the commonwealth with 100% clean energy by 2045 in passing the Virginia Clean Economy Act . With renewable energy steering our climate action plan , it 's time to face the largest source of global warming emissions in Virginia : the transportation sector . <p> At 45% of the commonwealth 's total climate pollution , transportation represents Virginia 's clear next hurdle to combating catastrophic climate change . On a warming planet our way of life becomes unstable , and extreme heat and disastrous flooding become the new norm . Residents of Hampton Roads could see up to 3 feet of sea level rise in the next 50 years , a rate within the top 10% in the nation . Extreme heat threatens localities throughout the commonwealth , with water shortages and heat stroke affecting rural and urban areas . <p> This fall , Gov . Ralph Northam can cut transportation emissions and improve the health of Virginians through a regional program called the Transportation and Climate Initiative ( TCI ) . <p> Advertisement <p> The product of a 12-state partnership and the first transportation program of its kind , TCI will help us reduce climate pollution and build the healthy transportation system we need . The program could fund equitable solutions for urban , suburban , and rural areas including electric vehicles , charging infrastructure and land use with the pedestrian and biker in mind . <p> Whether or not Virginia takes advantage of this opportunity will depend on action from the governor . The final memorandum of understanding is expected this fall , and Northam will decide whether to support the program and lead the nation to zero-carbon transportation or reject it and be left behind . <p> The Transportation and Climate Initiative will allow Virginia localities to provide clean transportation for the everyday health and well-being of pedestrians , bikers , transit-riders and drivers alike . With the opportunity TCI presents , Northam should implement a three-step plan outlined by Environment Virginia Research &amp; Policy Center . <p> First , ensure all new passenger vehicle sales are electric by 2035 . To drive demand for electric vehicles , we must ensure that owning one in Virginia is easy . That means establishing a reliable network of charging stations so Virginians can charge their vehicles while at home , at work , at school and en route . TCI could fund deployment of charging infrastructure in localities across the commonwealth . <p> Second , transition all transit and school buses to electric by 2030 . Adopting electric school buses would alleviate the stress of diesel exhaust on the group most vulnerable to it : children . Studies demonstrate that school buses " self-pollute , " or leak exhaust fumes into their own cabins . As a result , children on their way home or to school inhale as much or more exhaust than they would outside the bus . TCI could fund clean electric buses and their charging stations . <p> Third , double the number of people traveling by foot , bike or transit by 2030 . The commonwealth suffers from one of the highest pedestrian death rates in the country , with Fairfax County reporting 14% of the total 123 pedestrians killed on Virginia roads in 2018 . TCI could provide funds for protected bike lanes , sidewalks and bikeshare programs to make it safer and easier for Virginians to walk to their destination . <p> Virginians can and have envisioned a better , carbon-free way to get around . Gov . Northam can make their vision a reality by committing to the Transportation and Climate Initiative this fall . <p> Ellie Reynolds is the clean cars associate for Environment America , an advocacy organization whose Virginia affiliate is located in Richmond .
