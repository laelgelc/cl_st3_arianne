Photo by Amanda Edwards <p> Getty Images <p> The long Memorial Day weekend is behind us and if there was anything obvious besides the undeniable human nature for getting together , its that bicycling is witnessing a sudden renaissance as multiple factors converge , including warmer weather , ebike technology and the easing ( or outright ignoring ) of COVID-19 lockdowns . <p> Around the world , as people head back to work , fear of using mass transit means old bikes are being resurrected and new bikes are flying out of bike shops or landing at homes from delivery vans as people shun buses and trains for fear of contracting the coronavirus and its health-wrecking spawn , COVID-19 , despite often drastic actions taken by mass transit agencies . In hard-hit New York City , the DOT is reporting a 50% increase in cycling traffic over the same time in 2019 , despite large numbers of people still staying home . In Philadelphia , bike traffic is up 150% . Around the world , Milan , Berlin , London and many other major municipalities have seen ridership increases and in many metro areas , city planners are closing streets or modifying traffic patterns to accommodate the sudden surge of more daily riders , perhaps permanently . <p> While this perfect storm of circumstances is driving up sales for all kinds of bikes , it 's also powering up adoption of the newest iteration of the venerable two-wheeler , the electrically-powered " ebike . " <p> I 've been a bike commuter for the better part of 15 years now , and I live in a city - Portland , Oregon - that has one of the highest percentages of bike commuters in America and the world . I 've also been riding an ebike for the last three years . During my commutes , it 's not uncommon to get stuck in a Portlandia-style rush hour traffic jam of spandex , blinking LEDs and dinging bike bells . We complain , but with a smile , for we know the secret : You can usually commute faster on a bike ( even a regular one ) than in a car in Portland 's inner city . Add in electrical power , and it 's that much faster . <p> A spokesperson from ebike maker VanMoof , whose new S3 model I recently reviewed ( story below ) said sales at the Amsterdam-based ebike maker are up significantly during the epidemic . I talked with VanMoof co-founder Taco Carlier about the sales surge , which he said was a 600% increase over sales in April of 2019 . " It 's crazy right now in Europe , " Carlier said from Amsterdam , which is a world leader in bike commuting , with about 50 percent of commuting done on two wheels . He said the COVID-19 <p> VanMoof co-founder Taco Carlier <p> VanMoof <p> outbreak was a tragedy but he also noticed that the lockdowns and other safety measures have made cities more quiet and clean - trends he hopes continue as the epidemic hopefully wanes . " People saw the light . They realized how beautiful cities could be with a little less cars ... and they do n't want to go back to the old situation . " As for the sudden surge in ebike sales , Taco said that the company 's expectations of ebike adoption that they had predicted for the next decade seem to be suddenly compressed into just a few months . " The COVID tragedy is accelerating all the changes in micro-mobility , " he said . Carlier added that he thinks that ebikes now make up to 80 or 90 percent of the bikes being ridden in more rural parts of the Netherlands because they allow people to go farther than on a regular bicycle . " There is unlimited demand " for ebikes , Carlier said . " People are looking for better and healthier ways to commute . " <p> Eric Bjorling , Brand Director for American bike maker Trek , echoed Carlier 's sentiments on the sudden surge in sales of all bike types but especially ebikes . And somewhat ironically , Trek was beginning to spool up a major publicity campaign around the hashtag #GoByBike just as the pandemic gathered strength . The campaign was aimed at getting more people onto bikes - including bikes they already owned - to help combat climate change . Suddenly , everything changed . " It is one hell of a time for bicycles , " Bjorling said . <p> He said that most of the Trek staff in the United States continues to work from home , but many stores that sell the Trek brand have been deemed essential and continue to sell bikes , while bike manufacturing , especially in Southeast Asia , had seen some interruptions but is largely back on track . Trek electric bikes are made in Germany , but Bjorling said those facilities are back up and running after a prolonged closure . " Electric bikes have been on a rise for the past five or six years , " Bjorling said , and while they have been getting more popular over the last couple of years , demand has escalated in the last six weeks , and not just for ebikes . Kids bikes as well as mid-priced models designed for commuters were initially more popular as schools closed and transit systems shut down , but lately , Borling said more expensive models and electric bikes have begun to tick up in demand . Trek recently ran some studies that told them that many people - 85% of those that responded - now feel cycling to be a safer form of commuting than even mass transit . That kind of data is n't being lost on urban planners and city officials . " Cities are finally starting to wake up to the fact that biking is something people want to do , " Bjorling said . <p> A mid-90s Trek Y3 full-suspension mountain bike I recently found on Craigslist for $100 . It was so ... + close to brand new the nubs were still on the tires . <p> Roberson Photography <p> But back to the #GoByBike campaign , which Trek began working on in September of 2019 . The initial focus was going to be to hopefully inspire people to think about reducing car use in light of climate change , with a big social media push . Trek essentially shelved the campaign when the pandemic hit . Then in early April , Bjorling said their website traffic began to spike to record levels , bike use suddenly exploded , and they began to reconsider the message of the campaign . " We realized we actually had the perfect message , the perfect campaign , sitting right here for the times . So what we 're trying to do with #GoByBike is to keep the momentum going with what people are doing right now . " <p> Bjorling said the original goal of the campaign was to get drivers to replace one car trip with at least one bike trip , and then share it with the hashtag . He said in light of the pandemic and the sudden surge in riding , the campaign shifted from what might happen if humans did n't do something to stop climate change , to highlighting the positive things that were happening with bicycling in light of the pandemic : Suddenly , cleaner air in big cities , exercise benefits , views of mountains that had n't been seen in decades , and more people on bikes and less people in cars , of course . And Trek 's campaign is n't centered around just selling bicycles , they 're also encouraging people to dust off those older bikes and set them up for commuting , no matter what brand they are . " The best bike for you might actually be the one you already have , " Bjorling said , noting they are now seeing a lot of older bikes come in for service with little wear as owners spool them back up for commuting and getting around . <p> If there 's a positive legacy to the monumental losses of life and economic devestation due to the coronavirus/COVID-19 epidemic , it might be that for a brief moment , city streets were suddenly largely clear of car traffic , and many people may have reconsidered how they got to Point B and back to Point A each day , now and in the future . With the rapid evolution of ebike technology , this could be the big break bike tech has needed to dent automobile and transit-dominated commuting habits . In places like Japan , Asia , Portland and Amsterdam , there were already large numbers of people on bikes . Will they be joined by even more as warm weather arrives in the Northern Hemisphere , worksites reopen and commuters rethink riding mass transit ? Time will tell , but the answer may be in your garage , just some air in the tires and a bit of chain lube away from a small personal revolution in getting around . <p> Want to comment ?
