( AP Photo/Alex Brandon ) AP <p> Attorney General Dana Nessel on Wednesday , May 27 , announced Michigan is joining a 21-state lawsuit asking the federal court to review President Donald Trump 's administrative rollback of President Barack Obama-era Clean Car Standards . <p> The Trump Administration changes will allow auto manufacturers to build passenger vehicles and light trucks that get worse gas mileage and emit more carbon dioxide . <p> The EPA and NHTSA issued new rules that are scheduled to take effect June 29 guiding the auto industry and lowering standards for fuel efficiency and exhaust emissions previously established by President Barack Obama 's administration in 2012 . Those rules were set to expire in 2025 , but the EPA and NHTSA determined the rules hurt auto companies and needed to be tweaked . <p> " The final rule will increase stringency ( carbon dioxide ) emissions standards by 1.5% each year through model year 2026 , as compared with the standards issued in 2012 , which would have required about 5% annual increases , " a March 31 NHTSA statement said . Instead of being required to increase average fuel efficiency to 46.7 mpg by 2025 Trump administration changes give automakers until 2026 to reach 40.4 mpg . <p> " I will say that my interactions , at least with the Big Three are that what they want more than anything is they want predictability and they want stability , " she said . " And frankly , no one who 's affiliated with those companies wants to wake up and find themselves on the wrong end of a mean Tweet by President Trump . " <p> Nessel cited recent Michigan tragedies , including the state 's high number of coronavirus deaths -- 5,344 as of Wednesday -- and a failure on May 19 at Gladwin County 's Edenville Dam that led to mass flooding throughout Gladwin , Midland and Saginaw counties , in her opposition to the relaxed environmental standards . <p> " It 's been estimated that the increased emissions resulting from weakening mileage standards will result in 300,000 additional U.S. citizens with respiratory ailments , " Nessel said . " And in the midst of a global pandemic from a disease that attacks people with compromised respiratory systems , this rule is an additional slap in the face to public health . " <p> Nessel linked " the devastating effect of climate change " to the " increased number of rainfall events , " such as the one that contributed to Michigan 's recent floods . <p> " Instead of supporting common sense regulations of greenhouse gas emissions , the Trump administration is just doubling down on its support of the fossil fuel industry , " Nessel said . <p> During a March 2017 speech at Willow Run Airport near Ypsilanti , Trump said he would be attacking and attempting to loosen the Obama-era standards in an effort to make the U.S. " the car capital of the world again . " <p> If the U.S. Court of Appeals determines the rules violate existing laws , they could be reversed . If that were to happen , it 's likely the decision would be appealed to the U.S. Supreme Court .
