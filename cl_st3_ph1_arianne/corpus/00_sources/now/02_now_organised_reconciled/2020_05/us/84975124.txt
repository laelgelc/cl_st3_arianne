<p> Former Oregon House Majority Leader Mary Nolan and transportation policy buff Chris Smith are headed into a runoff for a seat on the Metro regional council representing North , Northwest and Northeast Portland . <p> The two emerged at top vote-getters in one of the most competitive metro races in decades . <p> Nolan , former city of Portland bureau director as well as former lawmaker , won 35% of the vote in results tallied as of mid-day Wednesday , not enough to claim outright victory . With 23% of the vote in a five-way contest , Smith made it into the runoff as well . He placed just ahead of Cameron Whitten , who finished with 20% of the vote . <p> Nolan , 65 , highlighted her legislative experience and support from the Democratic party establishment as well as her practical experience in Salem and city government as her top strengths . <p> She thanked voters for their support and for backing Metro 's region-wide homeless services measure as well . " I am looking forward to being a part of that oversight and direction for that program , " Nolan said in an interview Wednesday . <p> " I think that voters said that they are inclined to support someone who knows how to navigate those issues , " Nolan said , " and brings some policy expertise as well as coalition-building expertise . " <p> Smith , 60 , is a retired computer engineer and member of the Portland Planning and Sustainability Commission . He campaigned on what 's long been his passion -- transportation issues . He believes Metro should exert more authority over TriMet and the Oregon Department of Transportation . <p> " I look forward to engaging Mary Nolan in the runoff and believe we 'll be able to draw distinctions between our policies and qualifications , particularly around making hard choices to effectively address climate change , " Smith said in a statement . " Metro needs leadership that does n't compromise on our obligation to act on climate when considering investments in transportation and our future . " <p> Nolan said while she applauds Smith 's years of advocacy , she has practical policy making and coalition building experience . " I do think there 's a big difference , " Nolan said . " I have not just sat on committees , I 've convened the coalitions that are necessary to grapple with really big , and often contentious issues . " <p> Those issues -- like the homeless services bond and the upcoming transportation package -- are already facing Metro , she said . <p> Whitten , a 29-year-old community activist who most recently led the nonprofit LGBTQ+ community center , Q Center , talked about affordable housing and the need for climate action regionwide during this campaign . In a video message posted to Facebook , Whitten thanked his supporters and said he was just getting started . " This has been a transformative experience and it 's just the beginning , " he said , . <p> The five candidates competed to replace Sam Chase , a former chief of staff to the late Portland Commissioner Nick Fish . Chase declined to seek reelection and entered another competitive race , to fulfill the remainder of Fish 's City Council term . Chase failed to make the runoff in that race . <p> The eventual winner of the Metro seat will join the council as it faces interesting and complex work . The regional government is still expected to ask voters to approve a $7 billion transportation package in November . Voters on Tuesday also appeared to have approved a region-wide homeless services package . Metro is also managing a $652.8 million affordable housing bond approved by voters in 2018 and must endure significant financial blows to its budget with the Oregon Zoo , Convention Center and Expo Center temporarily out of business due to the coronavirus.
