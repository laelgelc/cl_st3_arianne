<p> Richard Norton had two thoughts when he saw a dam collapse in Gladwin County that sent major floodwaters rushing down the Tittabawassee River after several days of steady rainfall dropped about 8 inches on parts of northeast Michigan . <p> He felt compassion for the estimated 10,000 people being suddenly evacuated during the middle of a global coronavirus pandemic . And then he thought , " Here we go again . " <p> " These kinds of storms increasingly highlight how we need to be doing a better job of updating our infrastructure , " said Norton , a professor of urban and regional planning at the University of Michigan . <p> Mother Nature is conducting an unscheduled test of Michigan 's water infrastructure this week , and the results are not stellar for a state already soaked to the bone . Hydrologists characterize the past week as a " one , two , three punch " of rain that 's overwhelmed systems designed to keep water at bay . <p> " The fact that we 've been very wet for the past one , three and five years does come into play , " said Andy Dixon , a hydrologist with the National Weather Service in Grand Rapids . <p> " Even if we had n't been that wet , we 'd still be having issues . " <p> Across the Lower Peninsula , roads are closed by water spilling out of lakes , stream and rivers . Groundwater tables are high in many areas , particularly those with sand or gravel soil . The inland wetness coincides with record high water levels on the Great Lakes , which have been unruly neighbors of late to the owners of lakefront property , which is flooding and rapidly eroding in many areas . <p> Dixon said this week 's flooding might have been worse had there not been a mild winter , which produced a smaller snowpack that melted gradually rather than all at once . <p> Multiple Michigan rivers are hitting moderate flood levels this week , including the Grand River in Grand Rapids and Ionia , the Thornapple River in Hastings and the Saginaw River in Saginaw . The Tittabawassee River is the state 's only river currently experiencing major flooding . <p> Parts of Kalamazoo County are readying for some " nuisance " flooding along the Kalamazoo River near downtown , which is expected to crest Friday a couple feet under its historic 2018 flood level . Downstream , the river is already overflowing its banks in Saugatuck , forcing homes and businesses to fight back with sandbags . <p> In Kent County , flooding has closed 25 sections of roads and soaked flood-prone and low-lying areas along the Grand River such as Abrigador Trail in Comstock Park . <p> In Muskegon , the city got 3.35 inches of rainfall on Sunday , marking the second heaviest 24-hour rainfall total in May since 1904 , according to the weather service . <p> Rainfall overwhelmed sewers in Ann Arbor Tuesday , forcing the wastewater plant to release an estimated 600 to 1,500 gallons of partially treated sewage into the Huron River , which crested just shy of 16 feet on May 19 -- a daily record . <p> " At the time of this event , the flow into the plant was nearly triple the normal amount due to the significant rainfall that occurred in the vicinity over the past two days , " the city said . <p> Local officials urged people to avoid damaged roads that are impassable and closed . Gas and electric outages are expected . Floodwater is contaminated by sewage utility overflows and is considered unsafe to touch . Midland County officials said it could take nearly a week for the river to reach normal height . <p> Midland County Administrator Bridgette Gransden called the flood traumatic for a community recovering from flooding three years ago . " A lot of our folks in this community who had damage and loss have really just recovered and this is just going to have an impact not only on them financially , but emotionally , mentally , " she said . <p> Patrick Bak , a meteorologist with National Weather Service in Gaylord , said the water that overwhelmed the Edenville Dam fell in a slow-moving weather system that dropped steady rain in Gladwin , Arenac , Ogemaw and Iosco counties . <p> Between four and 8 inches of rain fell in some places north of the Saginaw Bay area between Sunday and Tuesday . Locals were speechless Tuesday watching the Rifle River rise in Arenac County . <p> Farms north of Au Gres are worried about their crops . <p> " There 's water where we never had water , " Devin Huber told the Michigan Farm News . " With all the water coming down , it just backed up . There 's not enough outlets . We farm about 2,000 acres , and I 'd say maybe 300 of it is above ground right now . " <p> Bak said the rain was just " too much for the ground to take . " <p> " The water is high everywhere , " he said . " You do n't have much wiggle room when there 's a heavy rain event like this before you start having issues . " <p> Michigan Gov . Gretchen Whitmer held press conferences late Tuesday and Wednesday afternoon . She issued an emergency declaration and eyeballed the flooding from a state police helicopter . She later sent the Federal Emergency Management Agency ( FEMA ) a formal request for assistance , calling state and local resources " insufficient " and limited by the ongoing pandemic . <p> Congressman John Moolenaar , R-Midland , issued a statement Tuesday saying he was communicating with FEMA " as we prepare to assess the need for future assistance . " <p> President Donald Trump said he spoke to Whitmer about the " big big problem " of dams breaking in Michigan , the Associated Press reported . On Thursday , Trump is scheduled to tour a Ford Motor plant in Ypsilanti that is making ventilators to aid the COVID-19 pandemic response . He sounded lukewarm about a flooding stop , but called the people of Michigan " great survivors . " <p> " Maybe we 'll do ' em both at the same time , " Trump said . " I do n't want to get in the way of the fix-up , though , because there 's a lot of water out there . " <p> Thankfully , Whitmer said Wednesday afternoon that the flooding had not resulted in any casualties yet . <p> " Experts have described this as a 500-year event . It 's going to have a major impact on the community and our state for the time to come , " Whitmer said . <p> Charlotte Jameson , a policy director for the Michigan Environmental Council , said the dam breach and flood highlight the need to invest in infrastructure that 's prepared to handle increasingly extreme weather linked to global climate change . <p> Jameson expressed concern about floodwaters mingling with containment ponds at the Dow Chemical Co. manufacturing complex in Midland , and called that a wake-up call . She noted that such toxic containment ponds , often filled with heavy metals around power plants and manure around factory farms , can threaten water quality if breached by floodwaters . <p> " In a world where we 're regularly flooding , what does that mean in terms of water quality and what people are being exposed to going forward ? " she asked . <p> " It took Flint happening for any sort of movement on lead service lines and lead in drinking water , " Jameson said . " I hope the silver lining in a disaster like this is we find some political will to take the steps necessary to avoid this happening again in the future . "
