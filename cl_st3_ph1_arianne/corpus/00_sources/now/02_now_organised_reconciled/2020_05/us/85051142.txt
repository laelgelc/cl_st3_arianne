<h> IU-South Bend grad finds her mission with coral reefs film <p> SOUTH BEND , Ind . ( AP ) -- At first , it was all about the dollars for Coral Dorsch . <p> Dorsch , a recent Indiana University South Bend graduate , initially planned to major in business . <p> " The reason I chose business is because of money , " she said . " That 's what you 're supposed to be doing going into college . " <p> But her true loves are art and expressing herself . <p> " I could do business , but it just was not feeding my soul , " Dorsch said . <p> So she changed her major to graphic design with a focus on cinematography . <p> Dorsch graduated from IUSB this month . She was supposed to walk across a graduation stage , but coronavirus restrictions disrupted mass gatherings like college commencements . <p> Dorsch visited with her grandparents , hung out with her mother and went to the park . <p> " My boyfriend ordered dinner and surprised me and we watched a movie , and at the end of the night we popped a little bottle of champagne , " she said . " It 's all about finding your own way to celebrate . " <p> Dorsch certainly found her way . She came to IUSB believing that climbing the corporate ladder was the path to success . She left with skills that aligned with her passion for advocating for the environment . <p> Dorsch made a short documentary detailing how global climate change is destroying coral reefs and the efforts being made on the island of Bonaire to save them . The film is called " Coral for Corals , " and was a part of Dorsch 's bachelor 's of fine arts show . The show , where the work of graduating seniors is displayed in the college 's art gallery , was another pandemic casualty . <p> People wo n't be able to see most of Dorsch 's display , which includes a book she wrote and designed about the demise of the coral reefs , but they can view the documentary , " Coral for Corals , " on YouTube . <p> Dorsch , a Mishawaka High School graduate , said she always worried about the environment . Her passion grew when a friend challenged her to embrace veganism . <p> " I quit eating anything that was not of the earth , and that transformed how I viewed this world that we lived in , " Dorsch said . " Being vegan sent me down a rabbit hole , and I began to look at all the things going on in this world . " <p> By the time Dorsch prepared to start on her senior project , she had studied endangered coral reefs . The project started as a play on her first name . <p> " Coral talking about corals . Ha , ha , super funny , " she recalled . " But then I started thinking and realized that it was a good idea because the issue was not getting a lot of exposure . " <p> Dorsch designed the book and created flyers and posters for the movie . It had always been her dream to make a documentary . <p> " I just wanted to go over and beyond and create something that everybody could remember , " Dorsch said . <p> That energy made an impression on one of her first professors at IUSB , as well as one of her last . Mark Bradford was an adjunct professor in the Leighton School of Business who taught an introductory course that Dorsch took her freshman year . <p> He eventually realized that Dorsch was passionate about the environment . <p> " All students start college a little shy , but by the end of the semester , it became clear that she had a lot of drive , " Bradford recalled . " A lot of college students are afraid to form an opinion , but Coral always had an opinion . <p> " It was clear that she was going places . The question was where . " <p> Bradford said the direction became clearer when he saw Dorsch 's documentary . <p> John Thompson , who instructed Dorsch in her last graphic design classes , said she was a fantastic student . " When she finds something that she is really passionate about , she puts all of her efforts into it . " <p> That 's why Dorsch jumped at a chance to go to Bonaire when her boyfriend 's family invited her to join them on holiday vacation . <p> She did research on the island and got her diving certification so she could see the condition of the reefs up close and shoot her own footage . She interviewed a scientist and a reef advocate , and then worked to edit the hours of footage that she turned into a 12-minute film . <p> " This is just a different way to inspire people to bring about change to their daily lives , " Dorsch said . " Hopefully , I have done that to some degree . "
