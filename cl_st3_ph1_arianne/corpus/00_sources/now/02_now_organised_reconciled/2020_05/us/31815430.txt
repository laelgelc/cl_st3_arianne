( National Park Service , via AP , File ) <p> In a closely watched move that could prohibit mountain lions from being shot by ranchers and other landowners from the Bay Area and Santa Cruz Mountains to Southern California , state wildlife regulators have taken a key step toward adding the big cats to the list of animals protected under California 's Endangered Species Act . <p> In a 5-0 vote Thursday evening , the state Fish and Game Commission granted mountain lions in six regions from San Francisco to San Diego " candidate status " to be listed as threatened in as soon as a year . <p> Following a meeting where more than 40 people testified by phone , commissioners agreed with a petition from environmental groups that lions in those six regions have diminished so significantly due to habitat loss from development , being struck and killed on freeways and being shot under state permits by landowners that they could face extinction there . <p> The vote does not affect mountain lion populations north of the Golden Gate Bridge , in the Central Valley or Sierra Nevada , where the animals are considered more numerous and less pressured by urban population growth and development . <p> " Wildlife officials deserve a big round of applause for moving to protect these amazing animals , " said Tiffany Yap , a biologist with the Center for Biological Diversity , an environmental group that advocated for the vote . <p> Opponents said they were disappointed . <p> " I 'm not surprised . I am dismayed , " said Kirk Wilbur , vice president of the California Cattlemen 's Association . " This is a largely political decision . There is n't sufficient science as far as we 're aware to show that the petition is warranted . " <p> State biologists estimate the mountain lion population in California at 4,000 to 6,000 animals . <p> But in six areas that were the subject of Thursday 's vote , scientists estimate there are only 225 to 510 lions . Those animals are increasing hemmed in by freeways and urban development , and are facing genetic bottlenecks as they breed with each other in dwindling numbers . <p> The decision is not the final word in what is likely to end up as a battle in court between environmental groups , the state Fish and Wildlife Department , and farming and ranching interests . Rather , Thursday 's vote gives state biologists one year to conduct a detailed scientific review , with a final vote expected afterward . <p> During that year , the lions receive protection under the state Endangered Species Act , which prohibits the killing of threatened or endangered animals . <p> From 1909 until 1963 , California offered a bounty for people who killed mountain lions . Sport hunting of the big cats , also known as cougars , was banned in 1990 when voters approved Proposition 117 . <p> That law , which is still in force , does allow farmers , ranchers and other landowners to kill mountain lions if they find them in the act of killing livestock or domestic animals . If they do n't see an attack firsthand , but find a dead animal later , it allows them to apply to the state Fish and Wildlife Department for a depredation permit . <p> Since 1990 , an average of 97 lions a year have been killed under such permits . One that the department issued in January sparked outrage across Southern California , when a rancher in Ventura County who had lost 11 sheep and lambs to a lion applied for , and obtained , a state permit to hire a hunter to kill the lion . The lion killed was P-56 , a radio-collared animal that had been the subject of research for years in the Santa Monica Mountains by national parks biologists , and which was believed to be one of two remaining adult males in the area . <p> Another controversy arose in the Bay Area in November , after the Midpeninsula Regional Open Space District , a public agency based in Los Altos , proposed allowing ranchers who lease its open space preserves for grazing to be allowed to kill mountain lions that kill their livestock , if other methods to chase the predators off did not work . After a public outcry , the district dropped the plan , which would have been unique among local parks agencies in the Bay Area . <p> The six areas affected by Thursday 's vote include San Mateo , Santa Clara , Alameda , Contra Costa and Santa Cruz counties , along with the central coast from Monterey to Santa Barbara . It also includes the Santa Monica Mountains and the San Gabriel Mountains in the Los Angeles area , the Santa Ana Mountains in Orange , Riverside , and San Diego counties , and the rural areas of San Diego , Riverside and Imperial counties near the Mexican border . <p> The California Fish and Game Commission voted on April 16 , 2020 to move mountain lions living in six areas between the Bay Area and San Diego , shown within the black boundary on this map , closer to permanent protection under California 's Endangered Species Act . <p> If the lions are added to the state endangered species list , the state will be required to draft a recovery plan . Highway developers will likely have to build tunnels in some places to allow the animals to pass under roadways without being killed . Rat poisons may be limited in those areas , and it 's likely that the state would not legally be able to issue depredation permits <p> " We do n't see any way a depredation permit could be lawfully issued , " said attorney Brendan Cummings with the Center for Biological Diversity . <p> Ranching groups agreed . <p> " They ca n't authorize any take now that it is a candidate species , " said Wilbur . <p> But the Cattlemen 's Association , the California Farm Bureau Federation and other groups say Proposition 117 , and its provisions to allow depredation permits , has precedence and that Thursday 's vote was illegal . <p> Environmentalists noted Thursday that there are tools to keep lions from livestock , including lights , fencing , pens and loud music . <p> " This is about not losing one of our irreplaceable animals . The world is watching us , " said Beth Pratt , California director of the National Wildlife Federation . " These animals are running out of time , and we do n't want them to disappear on our watch . " <p> Farmers and ranchers said that while many landowners do n't have lion problems , some do , and that alternative methods can be expensive and do n't always work . <p> " Mountain lion predation can be devastating , " said Sunshine Saldivar , an attorney with the California Farm Bureau Federation . " Given the small profit margins of most cattle ranches , such losses can jeopardize the continued viability of some ranches .
