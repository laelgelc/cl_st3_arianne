Questions were first posed to respondents in early April and then again between May 7 and May 12 . <p> For example , 52% of survey respondents report washing their hands after a trip to the grocery store , as compared to 63% in early April . Only 46% report going to the store less frequently , as compared to 51% in April , with far fewer people saying they 're trying to minimize the number of surfaces they touch while in the store -- 39% in May as compared to a whopping 50% in early April . <p> In addition , only 39% of shoppers say they 're using wipes and hand sanitizer while shopping as compared to 47% the month prior . The research also indicates that shoppers under 45 years old are reportedly less likely to take many of these risk-reducing precautions than shoppers in the upper age brackets . <p> But that does n't mean shoppers are n't concerned at all about COVID-19 . Many survey respondents say they 're concerned about the health of grocery store workers , as well as their fellow shoppers . About 24% of respondents say they 're ordering at least some of their groceries online , as compared to 16% in April , for example . <p> Just over 35% of survey takers say they 'd like to see employees wear masks , while over 25% want to know workers are frequently wiping down surfaces and slightly less -- just over 20% -- say they 'd like to see employees wearing gloves at work . Many of these preferences reflect survey respondents ' concerns not only for themselves and their fellow shoppers , but also for grocery store employees . <p> In addition to concerns about grocery store workers and their fellow shoppers , similar numbers of survey respondents say they 're worried about the availability of grocery staples and other non-food items . In fact , about 30% of survey takers pointed to the health of grocery store employees as compared to the 29% who said they 're worried about their stores running out of staples . <p> Concerns about food safety and our fragile food supply increased overall this month , according to the survey . Food safety concerns increased to 23% from 15% in April , with concerns about running out of healthy food at 16% in May as compared to only 9% in April . As far as threats to the meat supply are concerned ( a new question in May with no April comparisons ) , 21% of respondents say they 're worried about shortages . <p> However , the vast majority of survey takers said they 're confident that the food they 're buying is safe to eat , with a similar percentage ( 73% ) reporting confidence in the overall resilience of the U.S. food supply . At the same time , shoppers report being worried about food insecurity and their skyrocketing grocery bills , while one in four are worried about food waste . <p> Despite all the sourdough baking and Dalgona coffee drinking going on out there , two out of five respondents say they 're eating healthier now than they did before the pandemic . When we look more closely , however , we find that men and people under 45 were more likely to say they 're eating healthier whereas women said the opposite . They 're eating less healthfully than they did before the pandemic , which may or may not be connected to the fact that women are reportedly struggling more with mental health than men right now . <p> In addition , though consumers may be over-spending in plant-based protein categories , their actual eating habits seem to be more or less the same . Survey respondents report no change in their protein consumption habits , with about 50% indicating they now eat the same amount of meat , eggs , dairy and plant-based protein that they did before the pandemic . <p> Trust in expertise is changing too . Media coverage and headlines have fallen in trust with at least 29% of survey respondents , with 23% now reporting they trust doctors on TV and social media at least somewhat less than they did before . According to the researcher , " three in ten place more trust in government agencies , scientific studies , healthcare professionals and friends and family since COVID-19 began impacting the U.S. " Hopefully , government agencies will do better -- avoid giving bad advice and making bad decisions that could squander the public 's trust in the future . <p> I 'm a food and agriculture writer whose work has appeared in The Washington Post , Popular Mechanics , OneZero , New York Magazine , Slate , Mental Floss and SELF .
