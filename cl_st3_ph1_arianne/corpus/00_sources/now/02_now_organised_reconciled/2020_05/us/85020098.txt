<p> The project has faced fierce opposition , including from indigenous groups ( file picture ) <p> A top Canadian official has said this is a " great time " to build a pipeline because coronavirus-related restrictions ban large public protests . <p> Alberta 's Energy Minister Sonya Savage said people needed jobs and " ideological protests " would not be " tolerated " by ordinary Canadians . <p> She was referring to the Trans Mountain oil pipeline , opposed by indigenous groups and environmentalists . <p> It runs from Edmonton in Alberta to Burnaby , British Columbia . <p> Construction started in December and the project , an expansion of a 67-year-old pipeline , is expected to triple the current capacity from 300,000 barrels per day to 890,000 per day . <p> The pipeline has become a hot-button political issue for Canadian Prime Minister Justin Trudeau , who has said the project is in the national economic interest . It has faced numerous legal and regulatory hurdles . <p> " Now is a great time to be building a pipeline because you ca n't have protests of more than 15 people , " Ms Savage said on a podcast of the Canadian Association of Oilwell Drilling Contractors , when asked about the project . " Let 's get it built . " <p> " People are not going to have tolerance and patience for protests that get in the way of people working , " she said . " People need jobs , and those types of ideological protests that get in the way are not going to be tolerated by ordinary Canadians . " <p> Both Alberta and British Columbia have a ban on outdoor gatherings of more than 50 people imposed to curb the spread of the coronavirus . <p> Ms Savage belongs to the United Conservative Party ( UCP ) of Alberta Premier Jason Kenney . Kavi Bal , a spokesman for Ms Savage , said in an email to Canadian media : " We respect the right to lawful protests . " <p> The Trans Mountain project faces fierce opposition from the government of British Columbia , environmental campaigners and some First Nations along the route . They are concerned about oil spills , climate change , and the threat to the killer whale population off the coast . <p> Supporters see it as a necessary boost for Canada 's struggling energy sector which will help fuel the economy for years to come . <p> A lack of pipeline capacity in the landlocked province of Alberta has forced the provincial government to curtail production to reduce a glut in storage . <p> Mr Trudeau 's federal Liberals took the rare step in 2018 of buying the pipeline for C$4.5bn ( $3.4bn ; ? 2.6bn ) from energy infrastructure giant Kinder Morgan in a bid to ensure the project 's survival .
