Lawmakers had reached a deal to vote later today on a bipartisan FISA amendment from Reps . Zoe Lofgren ( D-Calif. ) and Warren Davidson ( R-Ohio ) . And the underlying bill -- which was negotiated by Attorney General William Barr and President Trump 's closest conservative allies -- has already ping-ponged between the two chambers . <p> But then last night , House Minority Leader Kevin McCarthy ( R-Calif. ) asked Democratic leaders to pull the bill from the floor after Trump encouraged House Republicans to tank it , throwing a major wrench into the plans and potentially blowing the whole thing up . Speaker Nancy Pelosi and Majority Leader Steny Hoyer are still weighing the request . But there are serious concerns in both parties about the bill 's fate following the addition of amendments and the last-minute opposition from Trump . <p> On the GOP side ... There are hawks who worry the new reforms go too far . Rep. Liz Cheney ( R-Wyo. ) , who supported the original FISA deal , argued on a GOP conference call that the amended bill would undermine national security . Meanwhile , Trump 's allieswho have been pushing to aggressively reform the law -- which they allege was improperly used to " spy " on Trump 's campaign -- may be reluctant to sign on to the new bill now that Trump has signaled he is n't satisfied with the final product . <p> On the Democratic side ... a new rift emerged yesterday over a change to the bill that was negotiated by Lofgren and House Intelligence Committee Chairman Adam Schiff . Sen. Ron Wyden ( D-Ore. ) lashed out at Schiff in a statement , accusing him of undermining the original language of his proposal and calling on lawmakers to reject the entire FISA reauthorization . The whole mess underscores just how tricky the FISA debate is -- and how it has created some strange bedfellows at times . Learn More <p> PREPPING FOR PROXY -- The GOP is launching an all-out assault on Democrats ' new proxy voting system , which lawmakers are poised to use for the first time today . House Republicans -- who have been crusading against the historic rules change -- are suing Pelosi in a bid to block the voting mechanism , though GOP aides say the courts are unlikely to intervene by today . <p> McCarthy and a group of 20 House Republicans filed a lawsuit yesterday challenging the system , which they claim is unconstitutional . It 's a legal move that top Republicans had been foreshadowing for days . " It is a brazen violation of the Constitution , a dereliction of our duty as elected officials , and would silence the American people 's voice during a crisis , " McCarthy said in a statement . <p> On top of that , GOP leadership has been encouraging House Republicans to not use the proxy voting system and instead submit statements to the Congressional Record if they ca n't be in the Capitol and want to register how they would have voted . " If Members are able to do so safely , they are encouraged to be present in D.C. and voting on the House floor , " the GOP whip 's office told members in a notice last week . <p> Democrats , however , dismissed the GOP attacks as politically motivated . They argue that the proxy system is the only safe way to have hundreds of lawmakers cast votes during the pandemic . " House Republicans ' sad stunt shows that their only focus is to delay and obstruct urgently-needed action to meet the needs of American workers and families during the coronavirus crisis , " Pelosi said in a statement . <p> Democratic leaders have been working hard to coordinate logistics ahead of the first-ever proxy votes today . So far , over 50 Democrats have signaled they plan to use a so-called " proxy " to cast a vote on the floor . Many of those lawmakers are from states with long travel times , like California , Washington and Arizona .
