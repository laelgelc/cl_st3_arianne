<h> Up to 3 billion people could live in too-hot conditions by 2070 : study <p> Scientists examine the effects of climate change and human population on future livability . <p> By <p> Alexandra Kelley <p> iStock <p> Story at a glance <p> A new report analyzes the development between human migration and climate change . <p> Results indicate human populations move very little , but climates are set to rise in the coming decades . <p> Rising temperatures can be mitigated with lower carbon emissions . <p> A new study underscores one of the potentially catastrophic effects of climate change : Approximately 1 billion to 3 billion people will be living in a climate too hot for humans to exist without air conditioning in about 50 years . <p> Weighing various scenarios of global warming and human population growth , scientists posit that with every 1.8 degree Fahrenheit , or 1 degree Celsius , increase in the average global temperature , approximately 1 billion humans ' environments will be too warm to be habitable without some type of air conditioning . <p> By identifying environmental niches humans have largely inhabited for the past millennia with stable temperature fluctuations , the study examines the distribution of population densities and analyzes how it has co-developed with climate change , revealing that human migration has remained " largely unaltered " in relation to mean average temperatures worldwide . <p> Our country is in a historic fight . " <p> Furthermore , the mean average global temperature is projected to rise by an estimated 7.5 degrees Celsius by 2070 , about 2.3 times more than the current mean global temperature rise . <p> These dramatic temperature changes will vary based on location , but in the worst case scenario , 3.5 billion humans will be exposed to temperatures greater or equal to 29 degrees Celsius . This includes swaths of Africa , Asia , Australia and South America . <p> Currently , only 0.8 percent of the global land surface , primarily concentrated in the Sahara , exhibits these temperatures . In 2070 , the study projects that 19 percent of global land surfaces will experience similar temperatures . <p> " It 's a huge amount and it 's a short-time . This is why we 're worried , ' ' said Natalie Mahowald , a Cornell University climate scientist who was n't part of the study . She and other outside scientists said the new study makes sense and conveys the urgency of the man-made climate change differently than past research . <p> The report further warns of large waves of migration resulting from climate-related problems , although these forecasts are " highly uncertain . " What is probable , however , is that population growth is likely to occur predominantly in hotter climates , further exacerbating the issue . <p> The authors optimistically note , however , that these results are based on unchecked , " business-as-usual " climate policies . Adverse effects to global temperatures and human displacement can be prevented with reduced emissions to keep the world below a 1.5 degree Celsius temperature change . <p> " It is not too late to mitigate climate change and to improve adaptive capacity , " they say .
