" <p> The researchers found that in the Mississippi Delta most marshes drown in a few centuries once the rate of sea-level rise exceeds about one-tenth of an inch per year ( 3 mm/yr ) . When the rate exceeds a quarter of an inch per year ( 7.5 mm/yr ) , drowning occurs in about half a century . <p> " The scary thing is that the present-day rate of global sea-level rise , due to climate change , has already exceeded the initial tipping point for marsh drowning , " T ? rnqvist said . " And as things stand right now , the rate of sea-level rise will continue to accelerate and put us on track for marshes to disappear even faster in the future . " <p> While these findings indicate that the loss of remaining marshes in coastal Louisiana is probably inevitable , there are still meaningful actions that can be taken to prevent the worst possible outcomes . The most important one , T ? rnqvist said , is to drastically curb greenhouse gas emissions to prevent sea-level rise from ramping up to rates where marshes will drown within a matter of decades . <p> The other one is to implement major river diversions as quickly as possible , so at least small portions of the Mississippi Delta can survive for a longer time .
