If you see comments in violation of our community guidelines , please report them . <h> Opinion : We Do n't Need an Economic Collapse to Curb Emissions <p> COVID-19 has caused a worldwide economic collapse . Yet some environmentalists are celebrating . <p> As factories remain shuttered and drivers stay off the roads , emissions are falling . Worldwide energy-related carbon emissions will drop as much as 5.4% this year , according to Goldman Sachs . <p> Many environmentalists can scarcely contain their glee . " This is an opportunity to talk about ... planned degrowth , " gushed one University of Vermont economist . " Suppose you were a policymaker , and you were thinking about what you would do to lower emissions -- you just got a pretty good instruction , " noted a think-tank climate change researcher . <p> Such callous disregard for the tens of millions of workers losing their jobs -- and the hundreds of thousands of patients fighting for their lives -- is revolting . Global shutdowns ca n't become the new normal . <p> Fortunately , humanity can cut carbon emissions without submitting to statist lunacy . For years , America has slashed emissions by transitioning its energy sector from dirty coal to cleaner natural gas . It 's time for the rest of the world to follow suit . <p> The rest of the world should be following America 's lead in reducing missions by transitioning its energy sector from dirty coal to cleaner natural gas , Langer writes . ( Photo : J. David Ake , AP ) <p> The coronavirus has wrought unprecedented physical and emotional destruction . Here in the United States it has already killed more than 90,000 Americans . Nearly half of American adults have reported declines in their mental health . Substance abuse and domestic violence are skyrocketing . <p> The financial carnage is equally severe . More than 26 million Americans have lost their jobs . One in four small businesses could soon go belly up . Food banks have been completely overrun . <p> Fringe environmentalists might accept this pain in exchange for lower emissions . But the rest of us wo n't -- and we do n't need to . <p> America has proven that it is possible to reduce emissions while maintaining economic growth . In 2019 , U.S. emissions declined 1.7% , even as the economy grew 2.3% . <p> Americans can thank natural gas for this progress . When burned to generate electricity , natural gas emits up to 60% less carbon dioxide than coal . <p> In the past decade , the United States has become the global leader in natural gas production , mostly because horizontal drilling and fracking have allowed producers to extract the fuel from previously inaccessible shale rock formations . Utility companies have switched from coal to natural gas -- which is considerably cheaper -- as their primary fuel source . <p> In fact , natural gas now accounts for over 38% of America 's electricity , up from about 24% a decade ago . In 2010 , coal provided about 45 percent of our power supply , but now generates just 23.5% . Thanks to this energy revolution , total U.S. emissions are hovering near 30-year lows . <p> Additional emissions cuts are surely on the way , thanks to initiatives like the Environmental Partnership , an alliance of top U.S. natural gas producers . The group is constantly implementing new emission-reduction strategies . Recently such efforts have included a leak and repair program to reduce emissions of methane , a greenhouse gas . <p> Radical environmentalists have made it clear that they 'll trade Americans ' economic well-being for emissions cuts . One indignant activist recently tweeted that " if weeks of suspended high-carbon economic activity can cut China 's emissions by a quarter , I do n't want to hear one ... word about how decarbonizing quickly enough to maintain a livable planet is ' unrealistic . ' " In other words , they 'd like the lockdowns to continue forever . <p> Fortunately , sober-minded Americans do n't have to choose between a functioning economy and emissions cuts . They can have both . <p> Andrew Langer is president of the Institute for Liberty . He wrote this for InsideSources.com.
