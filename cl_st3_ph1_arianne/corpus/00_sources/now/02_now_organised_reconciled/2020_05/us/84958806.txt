Newsom 's proposed budget cuts include canceling billions of dollars in climate change spending , a blow to environmental advocates who look to the state as a stopgap for the Trump administration 's weakening of federal protections . <p> California Gov . Gavin Newsom shared data Monday revealing that the severity of the coronavirus pandemic is declining and the state is ready to further relax the stay-at-home order . <p> The governor suggested sporting events without spectators may resume as early as June and people could be attending small church meetings and visiting hair salons in a few weeks . <p> " Yes , that includes getting a haircut , " said Newsom , who delivered an encouraging message during a press conference at Mustards Grill in Napa . <p> These activities fall into Stage 3 of Newsom 's four-phase reopening plan and , while he did n't provide a timeline for a jump into this next phase , he said multiple times " it 's weeks , not months away , " which suggests early June . <p> Counties will initially be given the opportunity to apply for approval to transition into Stage 3 and then Newsom will issue a statewide order offering the opportunity to all counties . <p> " The Bay Area is in a different position than other parts of the state , " Newsom said of the San Francisco region that has made modifications more slowly than other parts of the state . <p> Newsom is reopening the state 's economy on a four-stage plan that 's based on science and data and promotes social distancing . The state is currently moving from Stage 1 , with all residents except essential workers staying at home , into Stage 2 , when businesses with a lower risk of spreading COVID-19 will reopen . Gyms and hair salons fall into Stage 3 , and large gatherings at concert venues and convention centers in Stage 4 . <p> Newsom has given several sectors statewide permission to open , including curbside-pickup retail and office spaces where teleworking is n't possible , and most counties have introduced these modifications to their orders . <p> The governor emphasized counties have flexibility to make augmentations at their own pace and on Monday he announced a change in the reopening requirements that will make it easier for counties to move forward . Counties no longer need to have two weeks without a new coronavirus death and instead must meet new goals for testing , infections and hospitalizations , including less than 25 COVID-19 cases per 100,000 residents over the last 14 days . Newsom said 53 of the state 's 58 counties -- " give or take a few counties " -- are technically eligible to move into Stage 2 . <p> Twenty-four smaller , rural counties across the state have moved deeper into Stage 2 with state approval and are reopening office buildings and restaurants for dining-in service . Newsom said these counties with variances will be allowed to progress even farther into this stage , noting the possibility for in-store retail shopping is on the horizon . " Bottom line is people can go at their own place , " Newsom said . <p> Newsom said several indicators reveal the situation is improving in California , including a 7.5% decline in hospitalizations and a 8.7% decline in the number of patients in intensive care across two weeks . <p> What 's more , the state is better prepared than it was two months ago with increases in testing and contact tracing and 56.1 million masks currently in the state inventory . <p> MORE CORONAVIRUS COVERAGE : <p> Sign up for ' The Daily ' newsletter for the latest on coronavirus here .
