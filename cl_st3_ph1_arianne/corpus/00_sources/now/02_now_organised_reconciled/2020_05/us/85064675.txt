<p> Weeks before voters were poised to choose a Democratic nominee in Indiana 's 5th Congressional District , candidate Christina Hale showed signs that she was focusing on the general election , not just the primary . <p> Hale , a former state lawmaker , aired her first TV ad in the middle of May , criticizing " partisan bickering , " boasting about the passage of bills in the Statehouse with " bipartisan support " and emphasizing her support for lowering health care costs , a vague policy proposal that has appeal on either side of the aisle . <p> The ad that played out seemed an attempt to appeal to all voters , not just those who would vote in Tuesday 's primary , much different than some of the TV and radio ads seen on the Republican side in the final weeks of the election cycle . <p> For most of her political career , Hale has steered clear of more controversial , partisan issues , but the recent ad also could indicate her confidence in her ability to win the Democratic vote on Tuesday . <p> " The phrase ' end partisan bickering ' is usually reserved for the general election when candidates are emphasizing their effectiveness and trying to attract independents and moderates , " said Laura Merrifield Wilson , a political science professor at the University of Indianapolis . " This ad makes Hale look like she is already competing in the general election , a strategy that likely reflects her internal polling and prolific fundraising . " <p> Hale could be difficult to beat in the primary . She already had raised at least 16 times the amount of money that her fellow Democratic opponents had by the middle of May and has the backing of party insiders . Plus , because the coronavirus ruined candidates ' chances to campaign door-to-door and shifted voters ' attention off the election , pricey ad buys -- the kind that Hale can afford -- became all that much more important . <p> " There 's no doubt that she is the front-runner in just about every way you can think of it , " said Andy Downs , a political scientist at Purdue University Fort Wayne . <p> The former Democratic lieutenant governor nominee is running against scientist Jennifer Christie ; Andy Jacobs , the son of former Indiana congressman with the same name ; Ralph Spelbring , a former 6th Congressional District candidate ; and Dee Thornton , a Carmel corporate consultant . <p> For Democrats , the stakes are high . The winner will face the victorious candidate in the crowded 15-person Republican primary and attempt to hand a historically red district over to the Democrats as Republican U.S. Rep. Susan Brooks prepares to retire . <p> With no U.S. Senate seats and only two Congressional seats in Indiana , Democrats want this win . <p> On Tuesday , voters will have to choose : Do they pick Hale , a more moderate candidate with a significant fundraising war chest backed by the party in D.C. ? Do they choose Thornton , who lost to Brooks in 2018 and likely has some name recognition ? Or do they choose someone new , such as Christie , Jacobs or Spelbring ? <h> Shifting demographics <p> For more than two decades , Republicans have had little trouble winning the 5th District , which stretches from the northern parts of Marion County up to the city of Marion and includes all of Hamilton County . <p> But even before Brooks ' retirement announcement , the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee saw her seat as winnable , putting Brooks ' district on its list of 33 targeted seats in 2020 . <p> Brooks easily won in 2018 , but Democrats had watched optimistically as Thornton , who ran against Brooks in 2018 , gave the Republican her slimmest election victory to date . Meanwhile , Democrats were able to take control of the U.S. House in 2018 in large part due to suburban voters shifting to the left . <p> With no incumbent on the Republican side in the 5th District in 2020 , Democrats are confident they had a better shot at winning this year . Within hours of Brooks ' retirement announcement last year , election news analysis outlets such as Cook Political Report , Sabato 's Crystal Ball and Inside Elections with Nathan Gonzales changed her district from Solid Republican to Lean Republican in 2020 . <p> Some party insiders immediately started throwing around Hale 's name as a potential candidate , but Thornton saw the race as hers to win . <p> " We made significant progress in 2018 , " Thornton said on her campaign Facebook page when she first announced she was running again in 2020 . " We got the attention of the nation and with your help I am prepared to finish the job . " <p> It was the first time since 2012 that a Hoosier has made it on the inaugural list of candidates the DCCC threw its support behind . <p> Both Thornton and Christie criticized the DCCC for getting involved . <p> " I am deeply disappointed to learn that the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee ( DCCC ) has decided to play favorites and back a candidate before voters have been given the chance to make their voices heard , " Thornton said in a statement in January . " The DCCC 's decision to choose a specific candidate before the voters have had their say appears to be motivated by an age-old Washington tradition : money . " <p> Aside from the DCCC backing , Hale 's campaign has received money from the campaign committees of House Speaker Nancy Pelosi , former gubernatorial candidate John Gregg and former U.S. Sen. Joe Donnelly . <p> Hale had raised more than $1 million by the middle of May when not including personal loans , according to campaign finance reports filed with the Federal Elections Commission . Thornton raised the next highest with nearly $64,800 , followed by Christie with $34,700 and Jacobs with $12,800 . Spelbring has not filed with the FEC . <p> " The other Democratic candidates can still leverage other assets that do n't require money -- Dee Thornton surely has name recognition from her previous campaigns in the district , for example , " Merrifield Wilson said . " But funding is so critical to a successful campaign that this fundraising gap puts Hale at an incredible advantage that makes her hard to beat . " <p> Still , Christie is n't giving up . <p> " We have a well-organized grassroots campaign full of energy , motivated volunteers , and a focus on the most important issues of our time , " Christie told IndyStar . <h> Differences between the candidates <p> Christie , Hale , Jacobs and Thornton fall in line with the Democratic Party on most issues : all four say abortion should be a woman 's decision , are concerned about climate change and want to see firearm reforms to varying degrees . <p> Christie has labeled herself as the progressive candidate whose primary focus is addressing climate change . She supports universal basic income , free college and " Medicare for All . " <p> Hale , on the other hand , does n't focus as much on controversial social issues on her website , instead emphasizing her focus on lowering health care costs , strengthening the economy and helping Indiana 's children . She does n't support " Medicare for All , " and the only firearm reform mentioned on her website is universal background checks . <p> Jacobs and Thornton fall somewhere in between . For example , Jacobs supports free community college and supports a system where everyone has the option to enroll in Medicare but does n't have to give up private insurance if they do n't want to . <p> Thornton has focused on gun reform measures , such as raising the age to buy a firearm and banning bump stocks . She is critical of education vouchers and wants to move toward universal health care . But like Hale , she has n't pushed for free college . She also released a digital video ad that avoided controversial issues . <h> How to vote <p> Mail-in absentee ballots must be received by county elections office no later than noon on Election Day , Tuesday . Early in-person voting ends Monday . Hours and locations are determined by the county clerk 's office . <p> Polls are open on Election Day from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m ..
