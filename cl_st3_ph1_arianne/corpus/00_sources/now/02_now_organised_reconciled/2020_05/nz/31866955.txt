<h> A push for new coal mines amid ' alarming ' fast-tracking of infrastructure <p> The Morgan Gorge on the Waitaha River , the proposed site of a hydro scheme . <p> Supporters of an open-cast coal mine and a hydro scheme previously rejected by the Government are trying again . National Correspondent Charlie Mitchell reports on how economic recovery from Covid-19 will be cited as a reason to sidestep traditional environmental safeguards . <p> The letter is more in hope , than expectation . <p> Dear Prime Minister , it reads . <p> " We see mining jobs as resilient jobs , and we understand that with the appropriate authorisations , we could generate approximately 120 mining jobs in the Buller region within the next six to twelve months . " <p> Signed by seven West Coast leaders -- including the elected leaders of its four councils , the chief executives of two local iwi , and the head of the regional economic development agency -- it makes a case for the Government to support a string of projects in the region , citing the economic downturn created by Covid-19 . <p> The wishlist includes the Te Kuha coal mine ; a haul road across the Denniston plateau , enabling the development of a new mine ; and the Waitaha hydro scheme . <p> The letter does not make any specific requests of Jacinda Ardern , and notes none of the projects require financial support . It instead " highlights promising opportunities within two sectors for your consideration to support " . <p> The implied request is for the Government to relax bureaucratic steps that would allow the mines and the hydro scheme to proceed . Those steps would likely include access to public land , minerals permits , and potentially changes to RMA processes . <p> Although it appears to be separate from the call for ' shovel-ready ' infrastructure projects , it is a clear sign the economic recovery will be cited as a reason to sidestep traditional environmental safeguards . <p> While the Government looks to flood the economy with cash in a wave of infrastructure spending , some environmentalists see danger in the projects that could be fast-tracked . <p> Many hundreds of other infrastructure projects will largely bypass the Resource Management Act ( RMA ) , through a process that is already equal parts speedy and opaque . <p> To make the projects happen quickly , the Government is planning legislation that would significantly truncate usual processes . <p> The legislation has not yet been introduced , but it is likely to largely devolve decision-making authority to politicians , with limited scope for public scrutiny and expert opinion . Similar powers were used following the Canterbury and Hurunui/Kaikoura earthquakes . <p> The suspension of business as usual appears to have already emboldened those with projects that may struggle to succeed under regular processes . <p> NEIL SILVERWOOD/SUPPLIED <p> Te Kuha mountain with Westport in the distance . <p> The open-cast Te Kuha mine , proposed near Westport , would allow its owner , Stevenson Mining , to export 250,000t of metallurgical coal annually over the mine 's expected life of 16 years . <p> Part of the mine 's footprint would be on a 12ha section of conservation land , which its owner says is necessary to make the mine economically viable . <p> In 2019 , Conservation Minister Eugenie Sage and Energy Minister Megan Woods declined access to the land , largely due to the environmental damage it would cause to habitat for kiwi and other native species . That decision is now subject to a judicial review . <p> The Waitaha hydro scheme was scuttled last year , also for environmental reasons , when Environment Minister David Parker rejected an application to use the river . <p> The haul road would connect the Stockton mine to a planned mine on the nearby Denniston Plateau , which the mines ' owner , Bathurst Resources , said would be necessary to make a new mine economically viable . Environmentalists have long campaigned against mining the plateau , which contains unique biodiversity . <p> The letter to Ardern describes the economic benefits tied to the projects , with a particular focus on employment . <p> It notes of the Waitaha hydro scheme : " While Minister Parker declined to issue the Department of Conservation concession that would allow the scheme to proceed , there are compelling reasons which now warrant a reconsideration of the Government 's position and potential support of the project . " <p> RNZ <p> The Government is urged by one union leader in the construction sector to " crack on " with so-called " shovel-ready " projects . <p> The letter is a long shot , given the Government 's position on developing conservation land . Given two of the projects have been subject to a ministerial decision , a law change would likely be needed to reverse course . <p> Even one of the letter 's signatories , Buller District Mayor Jamie Cleine , acknowledged mining was unlikely to make a comeback any time soon : " Do I think they ( the Government ) will suddenly open the gates to mining on the West Coast ? No , " he told the Westport News . <p> The Government has itself quelled any notion it will change the law to allow mining on conservation land , or make special exemptions for the projects . <p> " I expect the Department of Conservation to continue to process applications for access to conservation land efficiently under the current law , " said Eugenie Sage , Minister of both Conservation and Land Information ( LINZ ) , when asked about the letter . <p> " There are no plans to weaken conservation law to make it easier for companies wanting to mine on public conservation land . " <p> She added that she expects LINZ to process any haul road application " in the usual way under existing law " . <p> It is also unclear whether the mines would be financially viable in the current economic environment . <p> International coal prices have plunged more than a third from the same time last year . <p> The-Press <p> The opening of State Highway 1 north of Kaikoura . <p> THE FAST TRACK <p> The request , successful or not , has highlighted an effort to push through projects that may otherwise struggle in normal times . <p> Despite assurances from the Government it wants to prioritise climate and environment friendly projects in its infrastructure binge , the ' fast-track ' legislation , as proposed , contains no environmental bottom lines . <p> A law allowing for fast-tracking consents is expected to be introduced to Parliament in June , but a Cabinet paper hints at what it could look like . <p> Some large , Government-led projects -- such as roads -- would be individually listed in the legislation as going through the fast-track process . This would likely include six larger transport projects already on the books , awaiting resource consents . <p> More Government-led projects -- specifically those led by the Transport Agency ( NZTA ) and KiwiRail -- would be able to " occur as of right " , effectively a standing consent to do things like road and rail maintenance . This could also be extended to local authorities , the Cabinet paper states . <p> The final piece is a fast-track consenting process for all other projects . A public or private project could apply to the Environment Minister to go through this process , who must then decide whether the project meets a list of criteria , including whether it would have a " significant public benefit " . The minister can reject the application for any reason . <p> If approved , the project would be referred to an expert panel , led by an Environment Court Judge , to receive consent . A project referred to a panel would be expected to receive consent , the Cabinet paper says . <p> There would be fewer information requirements than a typical RMA process , and no requirement for public notification . The minister , however , would be able to require certain groups such as iwi or environmental groups be notified . The panel must then issue a decision within 25 days . <p> The ' shovel-ready ' projects being chosen by ministers are likely to go through this process . <p> It remains unknown what the shortlisted ' shovel-ready ' projects are , and what criteria are being used to choose them . The Ministry for Business , Innovation and Employment has declined to release the list of projects , which prompted Forest &amp; Bird to seek intervention from the Ombudsman . <p> " There just simply does n't appear to be any transparency around how decisions are going to be made , " says Jen Miller , a Forest &amp; Bird spokesperson . <p> " We do n't know what the matrix is , or to what extent they 're putting weight on environmental outcomes , or the extent to which they 'll be transformative , if you like , in terms of reducing carbon emissions and that sort of thing . <p> " We do n't know . Nobody knows . " <p> The fast-track consenting process those projects would go through is " really alarming " , Miller says . " It does n't set any bottom lines and it gives the Minister extraordinary powers . " <p> Seven environmental groups have signed a joint letter to the Government proposing an environmental reference group to give input on the proposed projects . <p> Among them was the Environmental Defence Society ( EDS ) , which has argued the thrust of the Covid-19 recovery should be centred on climate change and environmental concerns . <p> " We urge the Government to place environmental and climate change objectives at the heart of the bill , to ensure they do not become subsidiary or secondary considerations , " the EDS wrote in a letter to Parker raising concerns over the planned legislation . <p> The group is nevertheless optimistic that ministers will not approve the likes of what the West Coast leaders are pushing for , says Gary Taylor , the EDS 's chief executive . <p> " I do n't think there 's a chance in hell of a coal mine or rejected hydro getting the nod and I think that consideration of environmental bottom lines will still happen during the consenting process . "
