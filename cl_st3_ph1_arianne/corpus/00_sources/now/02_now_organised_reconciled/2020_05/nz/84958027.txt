<h> Auckland house waste during Covid-19 lockdown increases 12 percent <p> Data released today shows Auckland households have produced much more waste during the lockdown - with extra landfill rubbish that could fill 66 double decker buses . <p> File image . Photo : Creative Commons <p> The city council said 1870 more tonnes of rubbish were collected in April compared to the same period last year , an increase of 12 percent . <p> There were also 326 extra tonnes of recycling , with glass increased by 26 percent and 27 percent more HDPE plastic , the opaque plastic used for milk bottles and cleaning products . <p> There were only 4 tonnes more aluminium cans recycled in April and 18 percent less PET plastic bottles , according to the council . <p> Councillor Richard Hills , chair of the environment and climate change committee , said the spike was natural with people staying in and eating at home during alert levels 3 and 4 . <p> " I 'm really proud of the efforts that everyone made over this time and the household waste increase reflects that we were staying inside . " <p> He said Auckland 's glass recycling was pressed here in New Zealand , so the increase was good news for local recyclers . <p> " We will continue to advocate for onshore recycling capacity for our other recycling material such as plastics , paper and cardboard . Processing this material locally means we wo n't need to ship it overseas , and it can instead be a valuable resource for Aucklanders , " Hills said . <p> Recycling and rubbish tonnages were down in January and February this year , which suggested that households under normal conditions were producing less waste , especially when accounting for Auckland 's growing population , the council said . <p> It said about 80 percent of the total waste to landfill came from commercial sources , not households , so the lockdown may have a net positive impact on overall waste across Auckland . <p> Hills encouraged people to keep considering ways to reduce their waste footprint . <p> " Rinsing your plastics , glass , and cans , and properly recycling can make a difference , but we also need to move away from a throwaway culture and single-use plastic more generally . <p> " Everyone needs to reduce their waste to achieve a sustainable future for Tamaki Makaurau. "
