The inventory , released to the Waikato Mayoral Forum today , shows the region has a wide emissions profile and identifies ... <p> For the first time , the Waikato 's three-yearly survey of greenhouse gas emissions has a breakdown for each territorial boundary . <p> The inventory , released to the Waikato Mayoral Forum today , shows the region has a wide emissions profile and identifies where best we can focus on transitioning to a low emissions economy -- and create more jobs -- by planting more trees , working towards a lower carbon footprint in the agricultural industry and accelerating alternative modes of transport . <p> Waikato Regional Council 's climate action committee chair Jennifer Nickel says the inventory paints an interesting picture of what is going on and where , and it means that approaches to curb and adapt to climate change will be varied across the region . <p> It shows that overall emissions from stationary energy and transportation have increased due to increases in population , emissions from agriculture have decreased due to an overall smaller herd size , and carbon removal rates by forestry have decreased due to rates of replanting not keeping up with the pace of harvesting . <p> It also shows that districts with high forestry are carbon neutral ( Taupo and Thames Coromandel ) , and that districts with high agricultural activities have the highest carbon footprint . <p> Cr Nickel says the next step is to use the information from the inventory to develop a climate action roadmap to support Waikato Regional Council and the territorial authorities to become leaders in carbon and water management . <p> " The decision to measure the carbon footprint of each of the territorial authorities was a unanimous one among the councils , and it will enable us all to work together to focus attention on the areas where the best reductions can be made . <p> " The data gives support for the development of our individual strategies but helps us all head in the same direction , together . Together , we can work together to achieve as a region . " <h> Some interesting facts and figures : <p> Activities within Waikato region 's boundaries generated approximately 12,646,982 metric tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent ( t CO2e ) between July 2018 and June 2019 . When forestry is included , the total net emissions for the region are 7,116,073t CO2e . <p> Agriculture remains the largest contributor to the total gross emissions for Waikato ( 69 per cent ) , followed by transportation ( 15 per cent ) and stationary energy ( 13 per cent ) . <p> Forestry removed a net volume of 5,530,909t CO2e or about 44 per cent of the total gross emissions . <p> Since the 2015-16 report , total net emissions ( including forestry ) have increased 349,291t CO2e ( 5.2 per cent ) to 7,116,073t CO2e , and total gross emissions ( excluding forestry ) have increased 247,439t CO2e ( 0.02 per cent ) to 12,646,982t CO2e . <p> Agriculture is the only sector to see a drop in gross emissions ( -4.2 per cent ) . <p> Transportation emissions is up 32.3 per cent and stationary energy emissions have increased 8.4 per cent due to population growth . <p> Carbon removal rates by forestry have decreased by 101,852t CO2e ( -1.8 per cent ) . <p> The net emissions per capita vary widely between the districts from -34.4 tonnes of CO2 ( carbon positive ) for Taupo district to 82.8 for Otorohanga district . <p> Thames-Coromandel district was also carbon positive due to high levels of sequestration from forestry . <p> Hamilton has the lowest gross emissions ( excluding forestry ) per capita at 6t CO2e . <p> Waitomo has the highest gross emissions ( excluding forestry ) per capita at 109t CO2e . <p> Waikato district is the highest emitter of agricultural emissions ( 1,721,291t CO2e ) , followed by Waipa ( 1,148,353 ) and Matamata-Piako ( 1,475,468 ) . <p> On a per capita basis , Waikato 's gross ( excluding forestry ) emissions are significantly higher than the national ( 26.7 v 16.5t CO2e ) ; agriculture emissions are high ( 18.2 v 7.9t CO2e ) while forestry sector removes more than double the national average ( -11.7 v -4.9t CO2e ) . <p> Stationary energy , waste and IPPU emissions are below the national averages .
