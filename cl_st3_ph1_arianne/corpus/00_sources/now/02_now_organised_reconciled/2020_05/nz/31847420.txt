<p> News from Here Technologies Commuter attitudes and behaviours around transport are divided in the face of increased congestion , according to new research from the location and mapping services platform HERE Technologies . <p> ' Where to from HERE : Mapping multi-modal movement in New Zealand ' surveyed 1000 respondents ( aged 18+ ) from Wellington , Auckland and Christchurch to find out how congestion is affecting commuter transport choices , what changes people are willing to make and what might be holding them back . <p> The study looked at behaviour and attitude towards congestion by city . It showed that Wellington residents are the most likely to sell either their first or second car to participate in ridesharing in order to ease congestion , with more the 60% of Welloingtonians surveyed responding that this would be a viable option . In addition , 42% are using public transport more , 14% have recently started walking more and 17% are willing to commit to walking more often . <p> Over half ( 51% ) of Wellington commuters said they would change their mode of transport depending on the live traffic updates they received . Yet , despite their willingness to try new travel modes , 45% still believe that driving is the fastest and most convenient . <p> In comparison , 62% of Christchurch residents would not be willing to sell one of their cars in order to participate in ridesharing . <p> Nationside , the survey showed that city commuters are taking public transport more often ( 44% ) and using rideshare services ( 28% ) so it seems that whatever they choose , commuters are likely to be on the roads , ensuring that congestion still needs to be addressed . Especially given just over a third of the country ( 37% ) have resigned themselves to the fact that congestion is now ' just a part of life ' , suggesting they are unaware of how specific modes of transport might be impacting their journey . <p> Almost half ( 43.8% ) are willing to choose their mode of transport based on real-time traffic updates , and potentially avoid adding to already-busy roads , where far fewer are willing to cycle or walk more often ( 21.7% ) or try working more flexible hours ( 15% ) . <p> Respondents also revealed how climate change impacts their travel choices . Almost a third ( 29.6% ) reported that concerns about the planet are not influencing their commuting decisions , with the remainder primarily taking public transport ( 37.8% ) or walking and cycling ( 37.5% ) more . <p> Within Auckland , residents were most likely to just accept traffic as a part of life ( 46% ) , with 48% using public transport more , and the same number are willing to give it a go . <p> While Auckland is said to have the worst traffic in New Zealand , its residents also are the least open to change , with almost half ( 46% ) still believing that driving is the fastest and most convenient option , and the same number is willing to accept their traffic in favour of keeping their routine and car . <p> People in Christchurch were the most varied in how they commute but least likely to sell their car ( 62% would n't even consider it ) and are using public transport less than other cities ( 29% ) . ? ? ? <p> Daniel Antonello , Head of Oceania at HERE , commented on the research : " Cities like Auckland , Hamilton and Christchurch ranked in the top 10 for congestion problems across Oceania last year , and Auckland is losing around $3.5million per day to traffic congestion . <p> More to the point traffic jams are the largest cause of air pollution and one of the largest contributors to excessive carbon footprints . They impact people 's happiness , where they choose to live , how productive we are as a society , and the success of our economy . <p> At HERE , we know that location technology and historical traffic data can help New Zealand 's major cities make more informed decisions on urban planning and infrastructure projects .
