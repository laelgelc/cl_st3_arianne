<p> Questions are being raised over whether the government is going too far , circumventing regular checks and balances to respond swiftly to COVID-19 . <p> Cabinet has given itself the power to implement multi-billion-dollar policies in response to COVID-19 without being scrutinised by government departments . <p> It on March 23 decided departments wo n't have to provide Regulatory Impact Assessments ( RIA ) for regulatory proposals responding directly to COVID-19 , until August 31 . <p> University of Otago public law professor Andrew Geddis maintains completing RIAs - even in a reduced way - is n't too much of an ask . He believes this would assure the public the government is at least going some way to adhering to regular decision-making processes . <p> Meanwhile economist , Cameron Bagrie of Bagrie Economics , has grave fears the government is using the COVID-19 crisis to push through ideologically-driven policy . Scrapping RIAs temporarily only adds to this narrative . <p> Around $25 billion of taxpayer money has been allocated towards the COVID-19 response so far , with more expected at next Thursday 's Budget . To put this in context , KiwiBuild was expected to cost $2 billion over 10 years . <p> What are RIAs ? <p> An RIA provides a high-level summary of the problem being addressed , the options and their associated costs and benefits , the consultation undertaken , and the proposed arrangements for implementation and review . <p> RIAs are typically published at the time the relevant bill is introduced to Parliament or the regulation is gazetted , or at the time of ministerial release . <p> There have been instances , such as the ban of new offshore oil and gas exploration , where the government has effectively made a decision before an RIA has been written . However the RIA still gets done and the relevant ministry 's advice gets put on the record . <p> A pointless exercise <p> Nonetheless , Cabinet on March 23 decided the RIA requirements " do not provide for proposals developed at pace to respond to emergencies " . <p> It said the process " consumes scare departmental and Treasury resources and time without adding value " . <p> On the flip side , it recognised scrapping RIAs risks bringing the process into " disrepute " . <p> Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern could n't on Tuesday tell interest.co.nz whether government departments had voluntarily offered Cabinet RIAs , even after decisions had been made , in an attempt to follow the normal process . <p> Finance Minister Grant Robertson could n't provide this assurance either but said Treasury is still commenting on policy , even if this is n't through the formal RIA process . <p> Treasury has n't published any RIAs nor advice on COVID-19 policies like the wage subsidy on its website . <p> Asked on Monday about the lack of an RIA in relation to the Small Business Cashflow Loan Scheme , which interest.co.nz revealed was mistakenly included in a piece of legislation passed last Thursday , Ardern pointed to the limited usefulness of financial modelling in the current environment . <p> " There is a high level of uncertainty around take-up rates , default rates on policies , regardless of how much time you would 've applied to debating the specifics , " she said . <p> Some normalcy should be the goal <p> Geddis recognised making projections in this uncertain environment government , processes of decision-making that have worked pretty well for us as a society . <p> " Trying to follow those as much as possible can reassure that the government is responding to this in
