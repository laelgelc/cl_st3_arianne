<h> Project choices not the smartest <p> Our council 's response to the Government 's request for a list of " shovel-ready " projects for potential funding is a poor use of the opportunity to fix some of our " hardy annual " regional problems . <p> I 'm not saying all of the nearly $200 million worth of projects put forward should not be on the list but , in my humble opinion , the region as a whole would benefit much more had we given a higher priority to others that have much greater capacity to make a real difference . <p> It is understandable that the council would want to see an end to its wastewater issues and similar community projects that have had a long time in gestation , eg the Olympic Pool upgrade . <p> However , some of the choices are nakedly political and would probably remain of doubtful value even after completion -- the restoration of the Gisborne-Napier railway link is a case in point . In spite of having spent $600,000 of government money on a feasibility study , it is still difficult to find an economic justification to repair the link at this time . That is probably good reason to rule it out altogether . KiwiRail 's response to the idea of reinstatement backs that view and as a consequence , l 'd be surprised if the Government supports it now either . <p> But politicians , being what they are , are unlikely to look a gift horse in the mouth and so we should n't be surprised that this more popular choice has priority over other more worthy ones in some quarters . Given the chorus for reinstatement has reached such deafening levels , votes become more important than logic for those who have one eye on the electoral calendar . We 'd probably do the same . <p> And anyway , it wo n't be ratepayers ' money being flushed down the dunny -- just an expensive misuse of taxpayer funds , which happens all the time , especially in election year . <p> What is not forgivable is for the council to ignore this opportunity to set in stone projects capable of delivering not only immediate , although temporary , job opportunities , but more importantly a restructured economy that will provide truck-loads of permanent ones . In so doing , it would help us recover from the Covid crisis and defend against the major threat of climate change . <p> Forgive me if l challenge the council to explain how the modern plans for the restoration of the Tokomaru Bay and Hicks Bay wharves are more important projects than the opening up of the Northern Tairawhiti via Minister Shane Jones 's offer to declare it " a special economic zone " . <p> Similarly , as popular as it might be in certain circles , how does the " aquifer replenishment " project take precedence over the need to build a much more expensive but more reliable freshwater storage system , while we have a government sympathetic to requests to help fund these normally " out of reach " developments ? <p> Actually , my government contacts suggest it is possible to do both . Why are n't we investigating that possibility ? <p> In the meantime , it is disappointing to observe that this council appears to be following its recent predecessors by allowing itself to become a pawn in the hands of those who want to manipulate our community for their own selfish reasons . Surely we know where that will lead . <p> This current crisis must be seen as a golden opportunity to show real leadership . <p> If a dummy like me can work out where the priorities should be , it should n't be hard for those sitting around the council table to show an even greater capacity for clear thinking . Now 's the time to show it .
