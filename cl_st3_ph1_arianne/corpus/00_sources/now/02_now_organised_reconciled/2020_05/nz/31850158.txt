<h> Green : the colour of spin <p> Gimmick-prone , sure , but the government leads by example in the march to environmental sustainability . <p> The Prime Minister 's announcement that , among other things , the public service VIP limousine fleet would be phased out in favour of greener machines has become the unfortunate flagship image of the government 's new sustainability drive . In the Press Gallery , one old hand remarked tartly , " But Helen , you 'll miss the rugby . " TV continues to illustrate the whole green push with shots of big beige and silver limos parked end-on-end outside various functions , with liveried drivers milling about while the pampered politicians cavort in imagined splendour elsewhere . <p> This is not an unfair image . Every Wellingtonian will have passed a limo-ringed restaurant or function centre and witnessed how little car-pooling goes on in the Beehive . <p> In other words , we are reminded that politicians are so much less sustainable than the rest of us . They fly incessantly , use more fuel-inefficient cars than the rest of us , more often , and in the case of our top politicians , often travel in convoy with flunkies and police . <p> Even the Greens were sprung a couple of elections back with a smokily flatulent campaign bus . <p> There 's no way round this . MPs have to come to Wellington most weeks . They have to get round the country to see the public . Alas for them , the process of trying to do it more greenly will be reductive . <p> Great fun will be had round water coolers imagining Foreign Minister Winston Peters greeting visiting dignitaries off their state aircraft , having pulled up on the tarmac in his self-drive Toyota Echo . Think of Trade Minister Phil Goff and entourage arriving at the next Doha Round in rickshaws . And if anyone deserves having to be seen in one of the new chartreuse Nissan Micras , it 's Trevor Mallard . <p> Nothing short of a fleet of dinky Smart Cars will convince the public that this is anything more than a shallow gesture . <p> More cynicism-inducing are the new green edicts for the bureaucracy . Why does the idea of the public service leading by example in the march to environmental sustainability make the eyes narrow and the hackles rise ? Perhaps it 's because the legions of policy advisers and functionaries in Wellington are already seen as generating so much wasteful activity , of being a drag-anchor on useful and necessary activity rather than an enabler of it . <p> The meetings , reports , working parties and draft protocol fuss-budgeting that will be occasioned by the new green edicts just make you want to curl up in the fetal position under your sofa . <p> Like Sir Geoffrey Palmer 's famous quango hunt , which failed so utterly that there were more quangos at the end of it than the beginning , this has " law of unintended consequences " written all over it . <p> If it was just as simple as curtailing officials ' physical meetings , making them all use the telephone and internet rather than the Koru Lounge , that would be just dandy . But the notion that several departments must achieve carbon-neutrality by 2012 seems somewhat tangential to their public purpose . The idea is that each department will over time find ways to offset whatever carbon it can not avoid emitting . This most likely will be by financing tree planting . <p> Do we really want Inland Revenue getting into forestry investment ? Should n't the departments of conservation and the environment be spending their budgets finding ways to make business and the citizenry behave greenly , rather than making token gestures themselves ? And how many heart and hip operations will it cost per hectare of forest , given the furiously carbon-happy nature of the health sector ? <p> It 's tempting to see this idea as a cute gimmick . If the government is committed to permanent forests to curb carbon damage , then that 's what it should do in its own right . <p> By extension , if the Treasury can not devise policies that curb poverty , should- n't it be made to devote a proportion of its budget to Barnardos , or even CYF ? <p> Still , though the thrust of Helen Clark 's Prime Minister 's Statement this week was overshadowed by Taito Phillip Field 's latest escapade , the slate of measures included some important beginnings to national climate change policy . A bio-fuels quota , commitment to permanent forests , environmental impact legislation , waste tax , car emissions clampdowns , a major science push to find ways of reducing livestock emissions and more intelligent public procurement policies are all inarguable measures to most of Parliament . <p> As the Greens complained , there is nothing about public transport , curbing our roading appetite and a host of other vital issues . But it 's a start - and a start the whole of Parliament has to make . <p> Were it not for the day turning into another Field Day , the speech might also have been seen as a credible Labour fightback against the easy run National 's John Key has been having lately . <p> After several weeks of Key setting the agenda - hungry children , heedless bureaucracy , gangs allowed to oppress poor neighbourhoods - the government was on the front foot again . <p> Key , whose job was to belittle rather than bolster the PM 's statement regardless of what was in it , did not have a terrific day . National lacking any eyecatching policies of its own , all he could do was play for laughs . <p> Long-term , however , this stuff should not be causing him any amusement . Already the polls show the Greens fattening up their vote , and though in time this will almost certainly be at Labour 's expense , the combined Labour-Green vote will be a tough total for National to beat . <p> In the spirit of sustainability and smaller environmental footprints , Parliament might take a lesson from Eden Park ( home of the Rugby World Cup , bring a cushion ) . <p> The proposed modular , temporary seating for the park 's upgrade might be equally appropriate for the debating chamber , where another MP is in the throes of being uprooted . <p> Parliament has always been a place where inmates are advised against getting too comfortable , but under MMP , the plumply upholstered fixed seating is becoming obsolete . Barely a month goes by without a new seating plan having to be devised , sequestering a disgraced MP , accommodating a reshuffle , tactfully repositioning a retiring senior . <p> But because the seating is fixed , disgraced MPs still have to sit cheek-by-jowl with the caucuses who despise them , and the smaller parties find themselves surrounded by enemies on all sides . Maori Party MP Te Ururoa Flavell is donutted in by National MPs , Brian Connell has never been more than a hair-flick away from fellow Nats since his disgrace , and if Taito Phillip Field dares to show his face , a place will have to be found for him on the perimeter of the far Labour backbench , where sit the very colleagues whose prospects he has damaged most - ambitious new MPs in a third-term government . <p> Modular arena scaffolding on moveable platforms is the only decent solution .
