<h> No new Covid-19 cases on eve of entry to level 2 <p> On the eve of New Zealand 's entry to level 2 the country has recorded no new Covid-19 cases for the second day running . <p> Jacinda Ardern and health chief Ashley Bloomfield are giving their final update during alert level 3 and said there were no new cases of Covid-19 , for the fourth time in total and the second day in a row . <p> The " encouraging news " meant the country had a combined 1497 cases - 94% were recovered , said Bloomfield . <p> Two people remained in hospital - neither in ICU . <p> There were 5961 tests yesterday . <p> " We 've reached the milestone of more than 200,000 tests which is just over 4 per cent of the population , " Bloomfield said . <p> He said the sense of anticipation for level 2 was palpable and understandable . <p> He reiterated the golden rules of keeping your distance , staying home if sick , call your doctor or Healthline and wash your hands . <p> Bloomfield said the numbers showed again " we 're on the right path " and we could n't afford to give our gains away so had to remain vigilent . <p> He 's also received a copy of the Waitakere Hospitals infections and it would be released at 2pm today . <p> This evening at 11.59pm , New Zealand will move into alert level 2 . <h> Announcement on funerals and tangi <p> Ardern said " the hardest parts " of the alert level framework were funerals and tangi . <p> There is currently a 10-person limit planned when level 2 kicks in , but Ardern signaled movement on this . <p> She has instigated calls between church leaders , funeral directors and iwi leaders to see if they could find a way to address legitimate health concerns while recognising funerals and tangi were life events which could n't be postponed . <p> They were " well on their way " to finding a solution and the Health Minister would have more information on that this afternoon . <p> The Government was still working through the details . She said they had always been agile and aknowledged the difficult times Kiwis were going through . She urged that " there will still be restrictions " . <p> They were seeing whether there 'd be " checks and balances " , involving funeral directors and the Ministry of Health . <p> Ardern said there had been other areas in their response that had been changed after consultation " and I do n't shy away from that " . <p> She accepted there 'd been consequences of the restrictions - she 's had friends who 'd had funeral and tangi during lockdown and alert level 3 . <p> " But ultimately we 've always said we 'd want to work through issues where they arose . " <p> But New Zealand would have to exercise caution because " we 're not out of the woods yet " . <p> Bloomfield said the consistency around the public health advice was around group size and the purpose of the gathering , especially where there would be mixing and mingling . <p> The public health advice was focused on balancing health risks but had engaged and listened to the specific concerns about funerals and tangi . <p> Ardern said no one wanted to see funerals broken up by police - but enforcement was ultimately up to police . <p> " I do n't think anyone in New Zealand wants to see a scene like that . " <p> Ardern said social gatherings had the biggest risk factors and countries were now having second waves which she called " a warning shot to us " . <p> This morning , relatives of a young butcher who died suddenly at the weekend made a heartfelt plea to the Prime Minister to allow them to attend his funeral . <p> Roy Green , 38 , died at the weekend after his business went into liquidation a week ago . <p> One of Green 's cousins , Bianca Rhind , has sent a letter to PM Jacinda Ardern pleading for the rules to be relaxed and to allow more than 10 people to attend their loved one 's funeral service . <h> Hairdressers <p> The Ministry of Health has told hairdressers the most important element of PPE was a mask , if they wanted to wear it . <p> Bloomfield said New Zealand 's situation was different to most other countries where restrictions were being relaxed because of our low levels of Covid-19 . <p> And it was up to hairdressers and other hands-on sectors if they wore PPE . <h> Migrant workers <p> According to official advice to Cabinet released in Friday 's document dump , there 's potentially 380,000 foreigners and migrant workers in New Zealand . <p> The advice said " repatriation of foreign nationals at such a scale is unlikely to be possible " and they would have to shelter in place in New Zealand . <p> Deputy Prime Minister Winston Peters said yesterday Civil Defence had " done their best " to help them out . <p> " But that said , if you 're here with no long-term legal authority or right to be here then perhaps you should go home . " <p> Ardern said they would need a longer term framework for migrant workers but said she did n't want to get ahead of any decision making . <p> When asked about Peters comments , she said in her view many foreigners would n't have had a chance to get home which was why the Government had a compassionate response . <p> Ardern acknowledged that as New Zealand moved out of the emergency response , that group of people would need a more tailored response . <p> The gap had been plugged for now but would need a longer-term response , she said . <p> She rejected that the Government had n't supported people in need . <h> A ' jobs Budget ' <p> Ardern said tomorrow 's Budget will be a " jobs Budget " , signalling how the Government intends to tackle the virus economically . <p> " Our number one priority is jobs ... That means doing all we can to support people staying in their current job or move to a new job if needed . <p> " And the reason for that is simple . It harks back to the sentiment of Norman Kirk , that all anyone ever needs is something to do , somewhere to live , someone to love and something to hope for . Employment helps form a foundation . It supports families , pays the bills , helps provide self-value and worth and when times are tough like this workplaces can provide an important support network . " <p> She said she would n't pre-empt anything Finance Minister Grant Robertson has to say tomorrow , but could set out what the Government was trying to achieve , and the values to make it work . <p> The Government 's plan was to invest . <p> " The first thing you will notice , is that we believe when times are hard , you do n't cut -- you invest . <p> " We will run the ruler over every line of expenditure , no question we need to ensure our expenditure provides value for money and supports our primary goal of jobs . " <p> The notion that the Government would make cuts to essential services New Zealanders needed more than ever was " not only immoral , it is economically wrong " . <p> That was why yesterday the biggest investment in health funding in two decades was made . <p> " It 's why on Monday we delivered pay equity for early childhood teachers . It 's why one of the first things we did when the virus hit was to increase benefit rates to ensure those who lost their job had more to help them through . <p> " Now more than ever we need our schools and hospitals , our public houses and roads and railways . We need our police and our nurses , and we need our welfare safety net . We will not let our team of 5 million fall when the times get tough , instead we will strengthen the blanket of support the Government can provide . We are rebuilding together , not apart . <p> " These foundations are essential . They are out base . But on top of them we must build the things that accelerate employment , empower businesses , and stimulate our productive economy . A relentless focus on jobs , economy and businesses is what 's required now for the wellbeing of all New Zealanders . " <p> In the coming month the Government would also launch a " comprehensive engagement programme " that will pose a simple proposition -- look what our team of 5 million achieved together in beating the virus , now what can we do together to get our economy moving again , to look after our people , and rebuild in a way that make things better than they were before . <p> " That will of course include the business community , but it will be broader too . " <p> " Prior to the virus we faced serious long term challenges -- persistent inequality and poverty , the threat of climate change , the need to diversify the economy , low productivity , limited domestic manufacturing and an abundance of low paid jobs . Do we return to those settings or is now the time to find a better way ? " <h> New Zealand to enter ' a very tough winter ' . <p> Ardern said the Budget " will be delivered within the most challenging economic conditions faced by any Government since the Great Depression " . <p> " The global COVID-19 pandemic has triggered a global economic shock not of our making , but like every country in the world , we are also not immune to its fallout . <p> " Let me be clear , the coming months and years will be some of the most challenging our country has faced in a very , very long time . " <p> She said the International Monetary Fund predicts the global economy will contract by 3% in 2020 , much worse than during the global financial crisis . <p> Around the world , unemployment will rise significantly , businesses will close and Government revenue will decline . <p> " And we will feel the pain here too . New Zealand is about to enter a very tough winter . <p> " But every winter is followed by spring , and if we make the right choices we can get New Zealanders back to work and our economy moving again quickly .
