<h> Curse of MMP <p> As Act flails its way to the polls , its trajectory seems to epitomise the fate of minor parties . <p> At the risk of being the kind of animist Don Brash disapproves of , I 'm starting to think there 's such a thing as the Curse of MMP . No minor party that has ever been in Government since we introduced the still-controversial system has survived the experience . The Alliance exploded into a thousand pieces . New Zealand First , although leader Winston Peters is still up and at ' em , seems unlikely to return to Parliament . United and Progressive are granted the courtesy title of " party " for old time 's sake , despite being reduced to one . The latter 's leader is retiring and the former 's vestigial leader will be lucky to be back this election . <p> It 's beastly unfair , because all these parties stepped up to do exactly what you 're supposed to do in politics -- get around the Cabinet table and try to change the world . Each managed to change at least a few nano-slices of the universe for the better . But instead of thanking them , the electorate told them to naff off . <p> It 's striking that the only small party to have maintained a viable vote across the whole MMP era is the one that has yet to succumb to the aroma of BMW leather , the Green Party . And its conundrum is that , although remaining electorally blameless , it has n't done much toward changing the world , either . Getting a home-insulation subsidy deal out of the Nats is not exactly wallpaper of history stuff . <p> Like the customer , of course , the voter is always right . But he/she is also maddeningly perverse . We voted for the MMP system because we were sick of single-party governments being able to do exactly as they pleased , without having to listen to anybody else . We punished them for not seeking more consensus . Now most Governments have had to accommodate other parties ' agendas , and we 're punishing those other parties for their temerity . Yesterday 's warm , fuzzy consensus is today 's filthy , cowardly compromise . <p> Even the small parties ' core supporters turn on them . However much of the party 's agenda its leaders manage to promulgate , they are sell-outs for cuddling up to National/Labour and relishing the baubles of office . <p> It has to be said , however , some parties do quite a lot of extra voluntary work to augment the impact of the Curse of MMP , which brings us yet again to the increasingly gory demise of Act . <p> Act has split and redivided like a demented amoeba since before getting into the Beehive , but amazingly there 's still enough left of it for the process to continue at an accelerated pace . At least this week 's splits and divisions were about actual policy rather than personality . <p> But what a policy . Act , in a shouty newspaper ad , announced this country is " pandering to Maori radicals " . In a television interview , Brash identified Pita Sharples as one of them . If that 's the sort of Maori radical we 're pandering to -- a kind , respectful , intelligent educationalist who is capable of winning an electorate seat and who has never been known to utter anti-Pakeha sentiment of any kind -- then we should probably keep pandering . <p> Brash 's major beef is that radicals like Sharples want to " change New Zealand 's constitution " to elevate Maori over everyone else . <p> It would help comprehension of this charge if we actually had a constitution . What we do have is an uncodified collection of statutes that neither the Maori Party nor any other Maori force is in any danger of being able to molest , because such changes are up to Parliament . In some cases it would take a 75% majority of Parliament to alter a constitutionally significant statute . As neither National nor Labour have threatened to do so , it 's probably safe to conclude that Brash is overanxious . <p> Brash 's other points remain equally open to challenge . His " bribe the tribe " critique of the Resource Management Act , which mandates consultation with local iwi , has yet to be backed up with hard facts . In how many approval processes have tribes received " koha " to " make the problem go away " ? Brash needs to bring this charge beyond the merely anecdotal if he expects voters to take it seriously . <p> Admittedly , one tangible Maori privilege he has raised is the granting of special unelected places on the Auckland Council . Sitting around a local authority table for hours on end , without the bloc voting power to so much as query the council 's toilet paper supplier , is the sort of privilege most people would be happy to waive . <p> As is endlessly pointed out to the good doctor , for a low-decile school to get more funding than a high-decile one , for instance , is hardly a privilege . It is simply a matter of compassion and brute common sense . The disadvantaged typically need extra resources to progress . As Maori are over-represented at the poor end of our socioeconomic statistics , it is both compassionate and economically rational to target more help towards them . <p> It 's true that a few years ago there was a rather inane push through the public service and among some lawmakers to insert Treaty of Waitangi provisions into anything and everything . But when it was realised this was backfiring as a gesture of goodwill and unity because no one could explain how the Treaty might be honoured or even interpreted in terms of the latest Road User Charges Bill or local authority drainage vesting amendment , and attempts to do so were likely to lead to ridicule , the fetish was discontinued . <p> Even if perceived inequities niggle with some voters , it 's inflammatory and rather petty to harp on about them when the biggest inequity is socio-economic , and not in Maoridom 's favour . It 's in all our interests to correct this imbalance , and that 's where Act 's considerable brainpower should be directed . <p> The resigned cynic might conclude that at least there 's a vote or two in a spot of hate-speech . But the ad and the ensuing publicity storm might not help Act 's brand , at a time when such issues as how to remedy debt , unemployment and business underperformance are somewhat more front-of-mind than the vague suspicion that a brown person with strong opinions might be , as the ad says ominously , " trying it on " . <p> Does Act , the brainchild of Sir Roger Douglas and the historical fulcrum of radically egalitarian fiscal policy , want to rebrand itself as the racist party ? <p> Before the announcement of Don Nicholson 's candidacy this week , I asked a source if he knew who the still-secret flash new candidate was . " I du n no . Someone in a white robe with a pointy hat ? " As it turns out , the new candidate is not known to have iffy views on race . The bad news is , he 's a climate change sceptic . Could Act do more to portray itself as the redneck conservative party if it tried ? <p> It is a matter of national shame that modern humans should derive emotional sustenance from living in a beautiful country and valuing historical artefacts . We must evolve beyond this soppiness immediately . <p> But the sincere long-term Act follower must by now be remembering Sir Roger 's faithful old ' 80s whiteboard with the frenzied , scratchy flat tax calculations all over it , and getting pretty misty-eyed.
