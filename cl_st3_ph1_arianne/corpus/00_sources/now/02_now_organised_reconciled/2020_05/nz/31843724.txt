<h> Climate change : E-bikes , tiny homes and embracing a life with lower emissions <p> My porch is made completely from rescued plastic and wooden pallets from the same factory . <p> OPINION : I try to live life by making as small an impact on the environment as possible . <p> Living a more restorative - not ' sustainable ' , as it 's too late to use that word - lifestyle has been something of a journey for me and I 'm still working towards repairing what I 've damaged . <p> I was raised in a lower middle class catholic family of nine in the United States , with one car that did n't always work . Suffice to say , I biked , walked , and car-pooled as my mode of transport . Our family was very dependent on the community to help us get to school , work and sports . <p> I experienced freedom for the first time in my life when I learned to ride a bicycle at the age of five . If there was one moment that put me on the path to living green , it was that - but I did n't know it at the time . <p> During university , I biked from home , which was 35 minutes by car . It was a two for one ; no need to beg for a ride and a free outdoor workout ! I knew it was better for the environment but I did n't do it for that reason ; I considered it a secondary benefit of biking . <p> While studying , I bought my very first car as I believed that 's what American adults needed to do to get around . I had it for about five years until I realised in my postgraduate studies in Colorado that I could just bike everywhere and save tons of money in petrol . Again , I did it for fitness , but also because I was operating on a tight budget . <p> I moved to Switzerland where it was n't necessary to own a car because of their excellent transport system , so I really embraced the bicycle commuting culture . It was here I saw the benefits that riding a bike had on population health , as the rates of obesity and cardiovascular disease in Switzerland are low . <p> Moving to New Zealand four years ago felt like I was plunged right back into the USA mode of " cars rule ! " all over again . I wanted nothing more than to avoid that lifestyle . <p> I view the bicycle as the remedy to our woes . <p> I am all for riding normal bicycles - something I did for years . However , I often found it difficult to bike to work and back and also go for an hour run after work with friends . I was too tired by the end of the day , but I still wanted an active social life . Life changed after I let go of my judgement about electric bikes and gave one a go . <p> As it turns out , the e-bike is the biggest fitness hack around . It allows you to not only go for runs with friends , but to get a bit of physical activity during transport without breaking a sweat ( if you do n't want to ) . My bike goes up to 47kph , so I can make it from Lincoln to Fitzgerald ( a distance of around 25km ) in 35 minutes during rush hour ! I can pedal to the front of red light queues and after two years , I 've paid for my bike three times over in the petrol savings alone . It 's a win-win-win ! <p> I think for most people , an e-bike is a legitimate car replacement , no matter the level of fitness . Pedal like a granny and go like Lance Armstrong ! It 's a realistic way for people of all abilities to bike up hills and through the nasty nor'westerlies all too common in Christchurch . <p> MARION VAN DIJK/STUFF <p> Life changed after I let go of my judgement about electric bikes and gave one a go . ( FILE PHOTO ) . An electric bike . generic <p> Getting an e-bike also means I did n't have to eat as much food to fuel my commutes . Instead , I used electricity which is rather efficient ( depending on the source ) in comparison to the distribution chain of the food I would need to buy to fuel my muscles . Even after I considered the impact of the battery , for the 10,000+ km I biked , I am certain I have a smaller footprint than if I would have biked on a normal bike and eaten that much more food to fuel myself . <p> Now I ride my e-bike everywhere and my car is currently for sale . For the most part , the car has has been a very expensive home for spiders and a toilet for birds . It 's no longer for me and does n't serve me , my wallet or my health in a way that is beneficial for our environment . Instead , I will use the money from my car to buy an electric cargo bike so I can carry large loads . <p> I only buy enough food for me to eat and I try to buy organic food no packaged in plastic and which is grown in New Zealand . <p> LIVING SMALLER <p> Two years ago , I decided to build a tiny house . I wanted to live in a warm house without moisture , without roommates and small enough that I did n't have to clean or service a 40-year mortgage . I wanted a place to call my own , but I did n't want to play by the big bank rules . The banks do n't identify my tiny house as an asset , but that does n't matter ; I paid it off and have a warm , pleasant place to call my own . <p> I installed a toilet which separates liquids and solids and dehydrates the solids with a small 10Watt fan , which runs constantly off of electricity in two small solar panels and a battery . <p> Catherine Elliot/Supplied <p> The Tasmanian oak would have cost me hundreds , but instead , it just cost me time to build . <p> I recycled seven long pallets and used the timber to make a six-by-two-metre shed frame for my bikes . <p> For the walls , I sourced 18mm ply top sheets from a local factory which has to pay to dump them . For the foundation , I used some 40 cinder building blocks a woman on the Neighbourly website was getting rid of for free . The only thing I had to buy were special screws to attach the free corrugated plastic roof I sourced from a local Menz Shed , the staples to attach the ply walls to the frame , and the sealant for the joins between the walls . <p> For the guts of the house , I could have gone to a large DIY retailer and bought the materials in a flat pack and ready for installation , but I did n't want to continue being a consumer of junk . Instead , I opted to buy plywood and build it myself . This not only allowed me to make things to a custom specification , but gave me the freedom to design boldly . <p> My porch is made completely from rescued plastic and wooden pallets . As the base , I used plastic pallets to prevent them from deteriorating and the top pallets are made of birch ply , so they have been treated with leftover natural linseed oil . <p> Catherine Elliot/Supplied <p> I rescued seven long pallets and used the timber to make a six metre by two metre shed frame for my bikes . <p> Inside , I used surplus wood from the Christchurch Justice Precinct building which had been donated to the Menz Shed . The Tasmanian oak would have cost me hundreds . Instead , it just cost me time to build and time to learn how to build it . It looks terrific and much nicer than something I could buy from a DIY retailer . The biggest cost is time , but that is a small price to pay when I consider how I saved a great deal of materials from being dumped into nature . <p> People give away free stuff all the time on Facebook Marketplace and other sites like ' Trash Nothing ' . If people open their eyes and ask , there are materials just begging to be saved from their ultimate demise in the landfill . It takes a bit of patience , courage and a keen eye , combined with having friends looking with you to find what you need . When we work collectively , not only do we save the planet , but we can save a lot of money and help companies get rid of their trash for free without adding to the waste at our landfills . <p> Everyone has to do their part and living smaller and sometimes taking the longer path is more important to nature than just getting a job done quickly . Living in tiny houses saves our rich soils from being covered with the ever-expanding housing developments . We will need rich soils for growing food locally . <p> Catherine Elliot <p> I rescued seven long pallets and used the timber to make a six metre by two metre shed frame for my bikes . <p> How we are living is not ethical and we need big , real change from everyday people . It 's about supply and demand . If we want governments and big business to be impacted , we have to vote every day with our dollar and stop buying petrol , stop buying new things and keep the things we do buy out of landfills . <p> We are in an emergency . Not just for the climate , but for the human species . We must listen to Greta Thunberg and the IPCC advice if we want to survive . Let 's be clear , there are no other options . We can be make uncomfortable changes now , or really pay for it with hunger in future . <p> What are we waiting for ? I choose to act now , as my life and those I love depends on it .
