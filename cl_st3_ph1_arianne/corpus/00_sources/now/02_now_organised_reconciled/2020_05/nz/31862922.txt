<p> Jones drew flak from all quarters after telling his listeners Australian Prime Minister Scott Morrison should " shove a sock down the throat " of Ardern . <p> Jones had taken umbrage after he thought Ardern had been critical of Australia 's efforts to tackle climate change . <p> Jones later apologised to Ardern , saying he never intended to suggest any violence towards her when he said he wanted to " shove a sock down her throat " . <p> It was one of many controversial episodes in a radio career marred by many polarising comments . <p> In an article in today 's Sunday Telegraph , Annette Sharp tells the " inside story " of Jones ' departure , with one media veteran saying " there is just no way he was going to leave of his own volition " . <p> Nine Radio boss Tom Malone is said to have paid Jones a visit at his property a couple of weeks ago . <p> The official version is that Jones invited Malone for lunch along with Jones ' agent Nick Fordham . <p> They said he had decided it was a good time to walk away , as he had a good relationship with the new Nine management team - unlike the previous leadership team , with whom he 'd had a lot of trouble , as it moved to sack him in 2019 . <p> " It was Alan who invited me down to his farm for lunch , " Malone said , according to the Sunday Telegraph . <p> " We were able to facilitate Alan 's request to stand down due to health reasons . " <p> However , sources inside Nine Entertainment say it was , in fact , Nine who called the meeting . <p> It 's been eight months since Jones made the controversial comments about Ardern , which led to advertisers walking away . <p> In fact , more than 100 advertisers have pulled their ads from the radio station , including Coles , Big W , Commonwealth Bank , Bunnings and McDonald 's . That boycott cost them as much as $80,000 a day . <p> Ad executives say Jones ' comment about the New Zealand Prime Minister have so far cost the company $20 million - about half of the radio station 's annual ad revenue . <p> It has also had a great impact in Nine 's ability to do business with premium advertisers across its multiple platforms . <p> It was the final nail in the coffin for the relationship between Nine and Jones , which had been damaged for some time . <p> Sources told the Sunday Telegraph Jones ' retirement had been in the works for about three months . <p> While it is true he had some health issues , those with inside knowledge say that was not the cause of the retirement . <p> " Alan is in rude health at the moment . A man who is normally very busy , has been taking it easy , and if you listen to him on air , he 's been in terrific form . Giving it to politicians left and right . He 's been blowing doors off . " <p> The same sources say he gave Malone some terms and conditions for his departure , including walking away on full salary and get paid out the balance of his $8 million contract . <p> He also told Nine he wanted his production team to either be paid out or offered new roles . Jones also wants his job to go to drive host Ben Fordham . <p> Another three are considering offers to remain at 2GB . Nine CEO Hugh Marks is confident the radio station that Nine fully acquired in August 2019 for $114 million will survive Jones 's departure : " Macquarie/Nine Radio can survive the loss of any of its talent , " he said in 2019 . Jones will now concentrate on his commitments to Sky News following word he has been negotiating to increase his Sky workload from two nights a week , as per his current contract , to four . <p> Podcasting opportunities -- as opposed to radio that might be in breach of his Nine contract -- are believed to be on the table at Sky . <p> As Nine seeks to tie up loose ends concerning Ben Fordham 's 2GB contract -- presumably he 's after a large pay bump -- Nine is banking on Fordham for a revival of revenue fortunes .
