<p> Planet of the Humans is directed and narrated by longtime Moore collaborator Jeff Gibbs . It makes particularly contentious claims about solar , wind and biomass ( organic material which can be burnt for energy ) . Some claims are valid . Some are out of date , and some are just wrong . <p> The film triggered a storm after its free release on YouTube late last month . At the time of writing , it had been watched 6.5 million times . Climate sceptics here and abroad reacted with glee . Environmentalists say the film has caused untold damage when climate action has never been more urgent . <p> For 50 years , I have studied and written about energy supply and use , and its environmental consequences . So let 's take a look at how Planet of the Humans is flawed , and where it gets things right . <h> Where the film goes wrong <p> Critics have compiled a long list of questionable claims made in the film . I will examine three relating to renewable energy . <p> 1 . Solar panels take more energy to produce than they generate <p> It 's true that some energy is required to build solar panels . The same can be said of coal-fired power stations , oil refineries and gas pipelines . <p> But the claim that solar panels generate less energy in their lifetime than that taken to produce them has long been disproved . It would not be true even if , as the film says , solar panels converted just 8% of the energy they receive into electricity . <p> But that 8% figure is at least 20 years old . The solar panels now installed on more than two million Australian roofs typically operate at at 15-20% efficiency . <p> 2 . Renewables ca n't replace fossil fuels <p> The film claims green energy is not replacing fossil fuels , and that coal plants can not be replaced by renewables . <p> To disprove this claim we need look no further than Australia , where wind turbines and solar panels have significantly reduced our dependence on coal . <p> In South Australia , for example , the expansion of solar and wind has led to the closure of all coal-fired power stations . <p> The state now gets most of its power from solar and wind , exporting its surplus to Victoria when its old coal-fired power stations prove unreliable on hot summer days . <p> What 's more , a report released this week by the Australian Energy Market Operator ( AEMO ) said with the right regulations , renewables could at times supply 75% of electricity in the national electricity market by 2025 . <p> 3 . Solar and wind need fossil fuel back-up <p> Some renewables systems use gas turbines to fill the gap when the wind is n't blowing and the sun is n't shining . However renewable energy storage is a cleaner option and is fast becoming cheaper and more widely used . <p> AEMO forecasts battery storage installations will rise from a low base today to reach 5.6 gigawatts by 2036 -- 37 . The costs of storage are also projected to fall faster than previously expected . <p> South Australia 's famous grid-scale Tesla battery is being expanded . And the New South Wales government 's pumped hydro plan shows how by 2040 , the state could get 89% of its power from solar and wind , backed by pumped hydro storage . <h> What does the film get right ? <p> Planet of the Humans makes several entirely valid points . Here are a few : <p> 1 . We need to deal with population growth <p> The film observes that population growth is the elephant in the room when it comes to climate change . It says politicians are reluctant to talk about limits to population growth " because that would be bad for business " . <p> As one observer in the film says , the people in charge are n't nervous enough . I agree . <p> An increasing population means increasing demand for energy and other resources , accelerating climate change . <p> 2 . Biomass energy does more harm than good <p> While the film unfairly criticises the environmental benefits of solar energy , it 's true that some so-called clean technologies are not green at all . <p> As the film asserts , destroying forests for biomass energy does more harm than good -- due to loss of habitat , damage to water systems , and the time taken for some forests to recover from the removal of wood . <p> The film calculates the sum total of human demands on natural systems as about 1,000 times what it was 200 years ago . It says there are ten times as many people now , each using 100 times the resources , on average . <p> Experts have repeatedly warned that human demand for resources is damaging the natural systems that all life depends on . <h> Get the message <p> Several other aspects of the film have been savaged by critics -- not least its claims about emissions produced by electric cars , which had previously been debunked . <p> Personal attacks on two prominent US clean energy advocates , Bill McKibben and Al Gore , also detract from the film 's impact . <p> It 's clear renewable energy has an important role to play in reducing greenhouse gas emissions and slowing climate change .
