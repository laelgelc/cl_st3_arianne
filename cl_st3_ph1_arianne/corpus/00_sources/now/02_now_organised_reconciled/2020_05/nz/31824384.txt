<h> Cameras on Auckland pest-free island catch lockdown-breakers <p> A couple have been caught having a glass of wine on one of Auckland 's pest-free islands during the lockdown by cameras set up to monitor the wildlife . <p> Ahead of the lockdown on March 26 , cameras were placed on Motukorea/Browns Island in the Hauraki Gulf after stoats were detected . <p> But unexpectedly , they 've since captured six people roaming the deserted island , including kayakers , day visitors , and even one person wandering around at 1.30 in the morning . <p> New Zealand Maritime Police Unit Sergeant John Saunders said these people were not only putting themselves at risk , but compromising the safety of emergency services . <p> " We 'll certainly be taking a closer interest in what 's happening around the island and those breaching the rules can expect action . Stay home and stay safe . " <p> Since mid-March , the Auckland Council biosecurity and regional park teams has been running a stoat incursion response on the island after signs of a stoat - or stoats - was detected during routine surveillance . <p> Auckland Council Environment and Climate Change committee chairman Richard Hills said finding people had been breaking the lockdown to travel to the island and hamper conservation efforts was " disappointing " . <p> " I 've been so proud of our community and how we have risen to the challenge posed by Covid-19 and led the world in our effort to eradicate it . <p> " I ask that people stay clear of these islands as no one should be travelling this far from their shores , in line with lockdown restrictions . Aucklanders are working together to swiftly move to level 2 and see a return to some normality . " <p> Auckland Council 's Head of Natural Environment Delivery Phil Brown said they still had not caught the stoat . <p> " We 're continuing to monitor and review the situation , but its capture is proving difficult . <p> " To date the stoat that likely swam to the island has proved elusive . " <p> Three stoat dogs and handlers visited the island before level 4 restrictions were imposed and the response programme was ongoing under " strict Covid-19 safety protocols since " . <p> Responding to biosecurity incursions in predator-free areas was considered an essential service where there is potential for loss of species . <p> " The most recent check last Thursday confirmed it is definitely still at large on the island , " Brown said . <p> " The presence of people on the island is not only breaching the Covid-19 rules , it hinders our conservation efforts greatly . " <p> Motukorea/Browns Island has a network of surveillance traps and monitoring devices in place to detect the arrival of animal pests on the island . <p> The trap network has recently been strengthened and expanded with additional traps and cameras . Rebaiting and weekly checks will continue , Brown said .
