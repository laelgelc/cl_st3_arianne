<h> Failed petition aims to spark more farming support <p> Petition organiser Terry Waite believe farmers are owed an apology at the very least and a backtrack on many of the restraints government has placed on them . <p> Te Kuiti-based electrician Terry Waite 's demand that the Government apologise to farmers for the way it has treated them -- especially over the last couple of years -- has failed . <p> Waite was so sparked up by what he believes is the Government 's poor treatment of farmers that he started a petition , asking people to support it so it could be presented to parliament . <p> The petition needed to attract 100,000 signatures . <p> He 'd tried to get his petition to ask : ' The NZ Govt to apologise to NZ farmers ' . However , the bureaucrats would n't allow that wording . <p> Waite remains undeterred and concedes that any apology is unlikely . But he says an improvement in government treatment of primary producers would be welcomed . <p> " The Government has treated our primary producers with disrespect with legislative and regulatory changes as well as additional taxes , which has resulted in suicides , " he told Rural News . <p> " Farmers are the backbone of our country and I believe they need a public apology from our Government . " <p> Waite says his motivation for starting the petition came from his experience dealing and interacting with farmers every day in his business , as well as his concerns about growing suicide rates and mental health issues in the rural sector . <p> " I have worked in the electrical service industry for around 30 years in the King Country . I work on farms every day and talk to many farmers , " he told Rural News . " Farmers are not happy and feel the Government is their biggest worry . " <p> Waite says some farmers are talking about walking away from the sector as it is all just getting too much , while others are getting paid to just grow trees . <p> " Food production is more important for our economy now than ever before , " he adds . " However , schools have started teaching our future generations that dairy farming is bad and that sheep/beef farming is bad . " <p> Waite says present government borrowing for the COVID-19 crisis will be funded by our primary producers ' future exports . <p> " NZ 's economy was built on the agriculture sector , farmers are still working and producing and there is no subsidy for them , " he adds . <p> " However , farmers feel more threatened by the Government than interest rates , commodity pricing or even then the weather -- and that is just appalling . " <p> Waite says this has seen a rise in farmer and rural suicide rates . He also blames mainstream media -- especially TV and urban-based newspapers -- saying they have not helped a growing anti-farmer sentiment in the country . <p> " If people actually knew how hard working and caring our farming people are , they might not be fooled by biased reporting , " Waite adds . <p> " NZ farmers are world leaders and other countries are adopting our farming practices . Even the UN says that food production should not be decreased for climate change policies . " <p> He cites as a prime example of the Government 's poor treatment of farmers , when a number turned up to protest that too much good farmland was being lost to forestry in Wellington last November . <p> " The Minister for Agriculture did n't even give them the time of day and the remarks from the Minister of Forestry simply reinforced the Government 's disdain for farmers , " Waite explains . <p> " I feel that farmers are owed an apology at the very least and a backtrack on many of the restraints government has put on them . " <p> Waite says many small towns in provincial and regional New Zealand need a strong agricultural sector because it is their lifeline . <p> He says with an election coming , someone in Parliament will push for change and work with farmers , not against .
