<h> Gisborne to Wairoa link ' not justified ' <p> The figures do n't stack up for reinstating the train line between Gisborne and Wairoa , KiwiRail boss Greg Miller says . <p> Gisborne District Council has identified the line 's refurbishment as a shovel-ready project that , with Crown funding , could bolster the economy in the wake of Covid-19 . <p> The council 's application to the Government 's Infrastructure Industry Reference Group says up to $23.3 million is needed for the project . <p> But KiwiRail , which would be responsible for the work , believes it could cost up to five times as much as that to reinstate the mothballed track . <p> It also says there is no guarantee of sufficient southbound freight to justify the proposed multimillion-dollar investment . <p> " Our view is it 's actually quite high risk to put this capital in there when there 's no certainty on revenue , " Mr Miller said . <p> He had provided that appraisal to the Government , which he said had been " proactively " seeking feedback from stakeholders in the line 's restoration . <p> Mr Miller said KiwiRail was feeling the pinch of reduced train services due to Covid-19 and would be focused on building freight volumes on existing lines . <p> " As a state-owned enterprise , we 've got to run a commercial business , and there are no subsidies for the freight network . " <p> A feasibility study , led by Berl economists and released late last year , found there was enough freight out of Gisborne to justify running a 24-wagon train on the line to Napier each weekday , as well as a daily 24-wagon log service , with each wagon carrying the load of one truck . <p> Mr Miller said KiwiRail had done its own research into potential freight volumes for the line . <p> He was yet to be convinced there was enough freight to justify the cost of restoring the line " at this point " . <p> KiwiRail 's research would not be published because there was a great deal of variability in freight volumes from day to day , Mr Miller said . <p> Storm damage led to the closure of the railway between Gisborne and Napier in 2012 , but the Wairoa-Napier section of the track reopened last June , thanks to $6.2m from the Provincial Growth Fund . <p> Mr Miller said the coastal train line between Gisborne and Wairoa presented more maintenance challenges than the track between Wairoa and Napier , and climate change was likely to exacerbate those issues . <p> I wish someone would make a decision either way . If it is going to reopen , then do it , if not turn it into a cycle/walkway from Wairoa to Gisborne . Swing-bridges over the washouts . This would be one of the best cycle/walkways in the North Island . It would bring in much-needed tourists , both national and international when our borders open again . Just look at what cycleways in other regions bring to their local economies . At the moment it is a valuable asset doing very little .
