<h> Greater challenges lie ahead <p> I am amongst those pleased with the positive mainstream response to the Government 's Covid-19 pandemic measures and am extremely hopeful we will soon see a similar resolve to deal with the even greater climate change threat . <p> On Sunday I listened to an audio recording of Gisborne 's Presbyterian St Andrew 's Church service . The Reverend Jin Sook 's " good shepherd " sermon included the parable of the king who had to choose whether or not to drink water from a poisoned well that had turned his people mad . When he eventually drank the water his subjects rejoiced because to them he had finally regained his reason . <p> This got me thinking . <p> I liken the story to present times . The water we drink and use is poisoned water , like it or not -- mostly polluted by acid rain , chemicals , plastic and other human-created waste products that end up in our rivers , lakes , reservoirs and seas . <p> The air we breathe , the water we drink and the soils that grow our food ; we degrade them all . Like the parable , we are all mad for allowing it to be . Our " kings " more so . <p> Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas and until last century , humans had only experienced a 172-300 parts per million ( ppm ) fluctuation in our atmosphere . When I was born in 1932 , the atmospheric levels were 308ppm . In 2009 when I began writing here it was 387ppm . Eleven years on it is now 418ppm . <p> This from millennium 's past : <p> The earth dries up and withers , <p> the heavens languish with the earth . <p> The earth is defiled by its people ; <p> they have disobeyed the laws , <p> violated the statutes <p> and broken the everlasting covenant . <p> Therefore a curse consumes the earth ; <p> its people must bear their guilt . <p> Therefore earth 's inhabitants are burned up , <p> and very few left . ( Isaiah 24 ) <p> Today globally 748 million people lack access to adequate clean water . Vast areas of our globe are experiencing multi-year megadroughts not seen for centuries . In New Zealand just now some are suffering extremely dry conditions too . <p> Here on this page , along with criticisms of the Covid-19 lockdown response , Craig Bauld has uttered these very obvious truths : " We all die . Some will die earlier " , and " It used to be that old people wished better lives for their children and their grandchildren " . <p> Could n't have put it better myself . After all , it 's our youngsters who will inherit the world . The least we can do is leave it habitable . <p> Last year the United Nations officially announced nature 's dangerous decline and unprecedented extinction rates . . . 200 species become extinct every day . <p> On the brighter side , some dramatic environmental benefits have been seen from the Covid-19 lockdowns around the world . <p> Last week NZ climate scientist James Renwick said : " We are hopeful there will be a reduction in carbon dioxide and greenhouse gas emissions this year , which certainly seems to be happening . " Also , " Yes , we are polluting the environment , but we have the power to stop that and we have the power to do so quickly . " <p> Let 's be honest , we are heading for near-term extinction at the moment . The longer action is delayed , the sooner our species becomes extinct . It 's as simple as that . <p> This pandemic has shown we can get by while greatly reducing our greenhouse gases , through minimising travel , air flights , tourism etc . <p> Humanity needs to take this chance to at least delay our own species ' inevitable extinction . <p> Meantime , as long as I 'm able I will continue my low environmental-impact lifestyle . <p> First , let 's get through this Covid-19 pandemic , constantly aware of the greater challenges we must face .
