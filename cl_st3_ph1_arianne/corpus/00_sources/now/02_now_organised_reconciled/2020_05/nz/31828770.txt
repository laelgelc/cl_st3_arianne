<h> GDC fiddling while Rome burns <p> In our rush to join the latest version of the " #MeToo " movement , which is the canonisation of our Prime Minister , we are overlooking our responsibilities to current and future generations that should require action on much more urgent matters . <p> While l get no pleasure in pointing this out to a populace that is frustrated from living under martial law for the past six weeks , someone has to do it . <p> I am well used to and unfazed by those members of our community who want to ridicule my ideas for economic growth , so l might as well have one more go at offering constructive alternatives to the dangerous " do nothing " policies of the current council . <p> In the process , it would be churlish not to acknowledge the high standard of leadership demonstrated by our Government , particularly the Prime Minister and senior members of the public service , during the lockdown -- but it would be a huge mistake ( one that l fear is about to be made ) if we imagined those efforts alone will be enough to get us on the road to a sustainable recovery when we emerge from our confinement . <p> As far as political leadership is concerned , the easy bit is over . The true test of our politicians ' abilities begins now . <p> What they do next will be crucial to how we deal with the wounds associated with this unfortunate interruption to our development as a community , region and nation . <p> For Tairawhiti , it will simply not be good enough to pretend that " more of the same " will satisfy the needs of a community that has lost some of its limited number of cornerstones for economic growth . <p> For example , while the destruction of our version of the tourism industry is not the fault of central government , it is the inevitable consequence of decisions made in mitigation against the pandemic . As such , it has to be accepted that at least for the moment , tourism is no longer part of the depleted number of opportunities we have at our disposal when plotting a course for the future . <p> In these impossible circumstances , we need leadership that can recognise the limited options available and explore them for all their worth . <p> That will not happen while we procrastinate in the face of this latest threat , or the threats posed by climate change . <p> Remember that old " bogeyman " that used to hog the pages of this newspaper and was given pride of place by those who claimed superior knowledge of the threats to our survival as a species ? Well , sorry to spoil the party , but it has n't gone away during this distraction . In fact , quantifiable evidence of its existence comes in the form of the current drought that it is waiting in the wings to destroy our hopes in ways that the virus could not . <p> All the more reason to develop a plan and a strategy for implementation that can help us restructure for a future endangered by both the effects of the pandemic and those destructive forces we have known about for some time . <p> Such strategies should be what you find in the local council 's Spatial Plan but , unfortunately , in spite of deluded comments from councillors and senior council staff that they are on to it , in fact , the cupboard is bare . <p> We have nothing that comes close to what will be required for the necessary restructuring . <p> And all the time this incompetence is on display , we are being swamped with offers of government financial help that are once-in-a-lifetime opportunities . How ironic is that ?
