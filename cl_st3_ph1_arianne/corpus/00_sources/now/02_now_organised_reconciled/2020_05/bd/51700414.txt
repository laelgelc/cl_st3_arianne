<h> The brighter side of pandemic <p> Emergency workers usually busy attending accidents on Thailand 's roads mill around ambulances parked at a service station -- fewer crashes and crimes a welcome outcome for several Asian countries during coronavirus lockdowns . <p> As Asia starts to assess the damage caused by the pandemic , some countries are realising there have been unforeseen benefits . <p> Vietnam 's nationwide isolation has seen a drop in crime , Hong Kong has hailed an early end to its annual flu season -- and now , Thailand is seeing a much-needed win in road safety . <p> " Accidents have gone down quite a lot , " said Banjerd Premjit , chief of the Por Tek Tung emergency medical team operating just outside Bangkok . <p> He credited Thailand 's virus-fuelled measures , including a ban on alcohol sales and a nighttime curfew . <p> Thailand has one of the highest rates of road fatalities per capita in the world , coming second only to war-torn Libya in 2015 . But this year Thailand saw a 60 percent decrease nationwide , with the death toll dropping to 167 from 386 people the year before . <p> Even Thailand 's coffin makers have seen a dip in demand , with one factory reporting orders are down by a third . <p> Regional neighbours with traffic-clogged megacities are reporting similar trends . Deaths from road accidents in Japan fell by nearly 20 percent in April , while Malaysia saw daily fatalities decrease from 17 to five , according to official news agency Bernama . <p> This year 's figures are even promising in India , which normally records 150,000 fatalities each year on its chaotic roads . <p> Police said the death toll in the state of Kerala sank by 90 percent during the lockdown , compared with the same period last year . <p> When the coronavirus hit Hong Kong in late January , residents scarred by memories of the 2003 SARS outbreak flocked to buy masks and immediately embraced social distancing . With millions practising better hygiene , doctors noticed the annual winter flu season came to an abrupt end in February -- nine weeks early with less than a third of last year 's deaths . <p> Scientists estimate China 's improved air quality averted more than 12,000 cardiovascular-related deaths , although warned in the Lancet journal their results should be " interpreted with caution " . <p> Global CO2 emissions from fossil fuels are set to drop by up to seven percent in 2020 because of the coronavirus pandemic , researchers reported Tuesday . <p> In early April , coronavirus lockdowns led to a 17 percent reduction worldwide in carbon pollution compared to the same period last year , according to the first peer-reviewed assessment of the pandemic 's impact on CO2 emissions , published in Nature Climate Change . <p> Though the scientist are saying the reduction is n't enough , the environmental gains seen during a global lockdown have scientists and celebrities calling for a " radical transformation " to save the planet instead of a return to normal .
