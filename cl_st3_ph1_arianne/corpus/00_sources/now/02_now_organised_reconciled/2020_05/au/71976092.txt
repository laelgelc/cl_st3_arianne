<p> An analysis of satellite images collected over 40 years has shown that a warming climate is causing hurricanes to blow harder . <p> The researchers found that " in almost every region of the world where hurricanes form , their maximum sustained winds are getting stronger . " <p> As the ocean warms , there is more energy available to drive these storms . The analysis found that the odds of a hurricane building to Category 3 or higher ( with sustained winds greater than 180 kilometres an hour ) is increasing by about eight percent a decade . <p> The research was led by Dr James Kossin , an atmospheric research scientist with the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration ( NOAA ) , and the Center for Weather and Climate , at the University of Wisconsin-Madison . <h> The findings matched predictions <p> In a prepared statement , Dr Kossin said the analysis matched up with long-held predictions of how climate change would impact tropical storms . <p> " Through modeling and our understanding of atmospheric physics , the study agrees with what we would expect to see in a warming climate like ours , " he said . <p> Dr James Kossin , atmospheric research scientist , has been investigating the impact of climate change on hurricane intensity and range .
