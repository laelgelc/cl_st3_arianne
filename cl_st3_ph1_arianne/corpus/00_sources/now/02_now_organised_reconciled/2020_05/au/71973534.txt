<h> Post-pandemic economy : Onus on our governments to stimulate the economy <p> May 16 , 2020 -- 12.00am <p> To submit a letter to The Age , email letters@theage.com.au . Please include your home address and telephone number . <p> Without government action , Australia is likely to have a prolonged recession . High unemployment and weak population growth , due to low immigration and birth rates , will undermine consumer spending and housing construction . Sluggish economic conditions overseas will weaken demand for our exports and the level of incoming tourists and students . Private sector capital spending could be subdued for years . <p> Illustration : Andrew Dyson <p> Governments will have to stimulate economic activity for some years . Vital investment must be allocated wisely , to projects subjected to rigorous cost-benefit analyses that account for new patterns of demand ( e.g. increased working at home ) , and to areas of social and educational need ( e.g. public housing , TAFE ) . Large projects that were planned before COVID-19 should be reviewed as some may no longer be warranted due to changed demand conditions . <p> The pandemic has exposed Australia 's reliance on uncertain international supply lines for key items that are crucial to public health . Our manufacturing sector has experienced difficulties in filling the gap . Economists such as Ross Garnaut and the Grattan Institute have pointed to major new industries using Australia 's advantages in natural resources such as iron ore and renewable energy ( solar and wind ) . Now is the time for governments to facilitate these industries . To do nothing is to condemn ourselves to grim unemployment for years . <p> Advertisement <p> Andrew Trembath , Blackburn <h> All groups must be supported during emergency <p> The job losses are catastrophic . The catalyst for social unrest in any emergency is when one or more groups seem to be unaffected by the emergency . This model fell over in the 1970s with the development of stagflation . We experienced inflation and rising unemployment at the same time . As did many other countries . This brought the post-War boom to a thundering halt . <p> Mr Wise 's " steady erosion of the ideas underpinning that model " included high levels of protection that saw us hiding behind a tariff wall , trapped in a small domestic market struggling to compete in global markets . We needed fiscal and monetary policies to reduce demand inflation pressures , and developed structural policies including micro-economic reforms to reduce cost inflation .
