<h> Groundbreaking research rewrites Australia 's ancient history <p> Climate change , not early humans , was likely responsible for the extinction of Australia 's megafauna , according to groundbreaking research that has rewritten the ancient history of our continent . <p> Over a decade of excavation at a fossil site in central Queensland by a team of Australian researchers has thrown the giant creatures that crawled and loped across the area more than 60,000 years ago into sharp focus . <p> An artist 's impression of several of the species whose fossils were discovered at the South Walker Creek site , including giant kangaroos , lizards and **28;197;TOOLONG Museum <p> Queensland Museum palaeontologist Dr Scott Hocknull led the research , along with colleagues from across the country , painstakingly excavating the South Walker Creek site south-west of Mackay . <p> Dr Hocknull said they uncovered fossils of 16 different species of ancient megafauna , at least one of which , a massive kangaroo , was previously undiscovered . <p> Advertisement <p> But intriguingly , there was no evidence of human settlement , meaning at this site at least , humans were not a factor in the animals ' disappearance . <p> Scientists have debated for years whether humans , a changing climate , or a combination of both caused the eventual extinction of practically all of the continent 's megafauna . <p> Dr Scott Hocknull with the fossilised leg bone of a giant kangaroo ready to be transported from the South Walker Creek site.Credit:Queensland Museum <p> Dr Hocknull said the possibility of humans having " some effect " somewhere else on the continent could not be ruled out . <p> " But the current understanding for the east coast is climate was a very key player in the extinction of these species , " he said . <p> " One of the key sites we have is aged at 40,000 years ago , which is actually younger than the expected extinction event around 45,000 years ago . <p> " This is very different from what you would expect if humans arrived on the continent and swept through from north to south driving species extinct through hunting . " <h> A land of monsters <p> When modern humans first arrived in Australia more than 60,000 years ago , monsters walked the land . <p> Arriving via sea crossings and land bridges with islands to the north of Australia , humans travelled into Cape York and Arnhem Land to find megafauna waiting for them . <p> Four species of massive crocodiles lived in the South Walker Creek area , according to the excavations , including one living on land rather than in water . <p> There was also Megalania , a seven-metre-long goanna . <p> An artist 's impression of Megalania , a huge goanna that lived until about 40,000 years ago.Credit:Queensland Museum <p> Dr Hocknull said combined with mammalian predators like the marsupial lion Thylacoleo carnifex , also found in the area , it would have been much more likely that those early humans would have tried to avoid megafauna entirely . <p> " If you think about it , dinosaurs went extinct 65 million years ago , and the largest predatory reptiles since then lived in Australia 60,000 years ago , " he said . <p> " If you 're armed with the weaponry of the time , you 've not actually encountered these species before . <p> " Not even in your prehistoric consciousness would you know how to deal with a six-metre-long land-dwelling crocodile with a hide like chain-mail armour . <p> " You would have to establish a relationship with the environment and these species in a way that was sustainable , and I think behaviourally , it would be much easier to avoid these animals rather than trying to specifically hunt them , especially at a rate that would drive them extinct . " <h> New species the heavyweight champion <p> One of the most exciting finds at the site for the researchers has been the discovery of an entirely new species of giant kangaroo , the largest ever found . <p> The two-metre-tall Procoptodon was the largest kangaroo-like species discovered until the team found this even-taller species , weighing in at more than 270 kilograms . <p> A size comparison of the giant kangaroo compared to a human.Credit:Queensland Museum <p> " Procoptodon looks strange to us , with its flat face and everything , but this species was essentially just a giant version of what we 'd think of as a kangaroo , " Dr Hocknull said . <p> " We 're still working to find out where it fits . We know it 's a species close to modern kangaroos , but a ridiculously huge one . " <p> The species is actually quite common in the fossils at the site because the majority of the fossils are the result of the previously mentioned massive crocodiles killing animals and eating them , with their bones falling into the clay of the ancient riverbanks to eventually become fossilised . <p> Loading <p> But the newly discovered species has not been found anywhere else in the country , meaning it probably filled an environmental niche similar to what Procoptodon inhabited in southern parts of Australia . <h> More discoveries to be found <p> In addition to the megafauna , the researchers have also uncovered a wealth of smaller animal fossils , plants and insects , which give more indications about what the ecosystem of the time was like . <p> Despite working on the site for a decade , Dr Hocknull said they barely scratched the surface of what it had to offer . <p> " We 're going to be working there for as long as the fossil deposits keep producing , " he said . <p> " We still have hundreds of bones that we 've already collected still to prepare , so there 's a lot of little secrets still to be unlocked . "
