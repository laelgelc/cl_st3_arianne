<h> Please explain : CHOGM to focus on Turnbull 's weak emissions policy <p> Australian Prime Minister Malcolm Turnbull might be enjoying a break from domestic ( read : energy ) politics at the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting ( CHOGM ) in London this week , but he is unlikely to escape scrutiny of his government 's feeble efforts on cutting emissions . <p> Alongside the important business of anointing a successor to the Crown , emissions targets have been thrust high on the CHOGM agenda for 2018 , by none other than the meeting 's host . <p> As the Guardian reports , the UK government has used the forum to announce plans to review its long-term emission reduction target -- raising the possibility it could implement a " net zero " goal for 2050 , up from the existing target of 80 per cent . <p> Whether it does this or not , the news makes Australia -- with its unshakable commitment to a 2030 target of 26-28 per cent on 2005 levels , and complete absence of any longer-term target ( Tony Abbott dumped the long term target when he dumped the carbon price ) -- look pretty pathetic . <p> As do the findings of a Christian Aid report , released on Monday , that named Australia -- alongside the UK and Canada -- as being in climate mitigation " deficit , " while some of the Commonwealth 's poorer countries -- Bangladesh , Kenya and Zambia , for example -- were in credit . <p> Small island states vulnerable to sea level rise -- and many of them Australia 's Pacific Island neighbours , like Kiribati , Vanuatu and Tuvalu -- were also more than doing their fair share , the report found . <p> The report , which assessed national capacity to make cuts and emissions since 1990 to calculate fair proportional efforts to address climate change , included the below table which translates each country 's mitigation deficit into TWh of renewable energy . <p> Meanwhile , in Australia , the federal government will try again this week to win the states over to its National Energy Guarantee which , according to broad independent consensus , wo n't help cut carbon emissions , wo n't incentivise a shift away from coal power , and might even dampen renewables investment . <p> As we reported yesterday , the latest indication from the Coalition on the shape of Turnbull 's NEG is that it will hold position not just on a weak emissions target , but on locking it in for as long as possible . <p> Contrast this with the UK , which -- on top of its plans to review its 2050 emissions target -- has set a clear target to exit coal , will ban the sale of new petrol and diesel cars , and is using an independent authority to advise it on all matters climate . <p> ( Australia has one of those , called the Climate Change Authority , but tends to ignore it . ) <p> But Turnbull wo n't be able to ignore climate change in CHOGM , which has also flagged climate inequality -- both in terms of its effects , and the efforts made to combat it -- as a major topic of discussion . <p> In its pre-meeting notes , the Commonwealth said that climate change would disproportionately impact poorer former British colonies . <p> " Without urgent action to reduce vulnerability and increase resilience , the impacts of climate change could push an additional 100 million people across the world into poverty by 2030 , " the CHOGM statement said . <p> " This is particularly relevant for the Commonwealth as 39 of our 53 members are small or other vulnerable states . <p> " The Commonwealth is well placed to take action , underlining our on-going commitment to tackling climate change , protecting the environment and increasing the resilience of our members .
