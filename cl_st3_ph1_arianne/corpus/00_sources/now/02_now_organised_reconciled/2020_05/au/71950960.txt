<h> Virus , climate response the same : minister <h> Energy Minister Angus Taylor <p> Energy Minister Angus Taylor sees no difference in the response to COVID-19 or climate change , <p> The federal energy minister sees no difference with how the coalition has dealt with climate change and coronavirus in regards to listening to scientific advice . <p> The Morrison government has credited its coronavirus response decisions to medical experts , with the nation 's top health officers front and centre . <p> Energy Minister Angus Taylor says it 's the same with climate change . <p> " We take enormous amounts of advice from scientists , " he told the ABC on Wednesday . <p> But Mr Taylor believes the two problems are different as climate change is a global issue to solve . <p> " Emissions know no boundaries , " he said . <p> " Contagion happens at a local level of course , so that is a difference . " <p> It comes as a new study reveals restrictions in place because of coronavirus resulted in global emissions declining by 17 per cent in April . <p> The preliminary analysis was published in Nature Climate Change on Wednesday and is the effort of a global team including researchers from the CSIRO . <p> It found the peak decline of a 17 per cent drop in carbon dioxide emissions from fossil fuel sources occurred on April 7 . <p> The decrease is compared to the same time last year , and resulted in a level of emissions not seen since 2006 . <p> China , the US , India and other high-emitters were all in high levels of lockdown at that point in April . <p> Daily emissions between January and April have declined by 8.6 per cent compared to the same period last year . <p> Data was used from 69 countries - including Australia - that make up 97 per cent of global emissions and have 85 per cent of the world population . <p> " Decreases in emissions in 2020 were largest in China where industry and communities first locked down , followed by the US , Europe , and then India , " CSIRO researcher and report co-author Pep Canadell said . <p> Decreases were the greatest in the surface transport sector , which includes cars and shipping . <p> It recorded a 36 per cent decrease in emissions , equivalent to 7.5 megatonnes of carbon dioxide . <p> " Across other sectors , the power sector ranked second and the industry sector , encompassing manufacturing and material productions , third in terms of their contributions to the global decrease in emissions in early April , " Dr Canadell said . <p> There was also a 60 per cent decline in emissions from the global aviation industry in April compared to the year before , amounting to about 1.7 Mt of CO2 . <p> If restrictions are lifted by the middle of June , the overall decrease in emissions for the year is expected to be about 4.2 per cent , compared to 7.5 per cent if countries remain at some level of lockdown until the end of the year .
