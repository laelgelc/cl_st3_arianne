<h> Kirstie Clements : Arguing with myself about fashion -- and still losing <p> The time we have all spent in isolation has certainly taught us some lessons in terms of fashion . <p> And what we can and ca n't do without . <p> Shopping your own wardrobe is the obvious answer when you ca n't go to a physical shop , but for anyone who is even vaguely interested in fashion , it does n't entirely dampen the desire for lovely new things . <p> I have bought nothing but track wear over the last couple of months . Instead , I 've spent many enjoyable hours internet shopping and popping lots of desirable , frivolous pieces into my trolley . <p> But then I never make it to the checkout . <p> I 'm struggling to know how to approach this time , and about how to be a more responsible consumer . You can take the ' buy nothing new ' approach . <p> Which is certainly the best option in terms of climate change and waste , but on the other hand , there are fashion businesses , with sound ethics , that have been hit hard financially and need our support . <p> Actress and activist Jane Fonda has gone the buy-nothing route . <p> Last last year she said , " When I talk to people about , ' We do n't really need to keep shopping . " <p> Given that Ms Fonda already has some very impressive clothes after decades of shopping , it 's no wonder she is happy to pull them out again . <p> I do love seeing her on the red carpet , recycling her Bob Mackie beaded gowns and sequinned tuxedo jackets -- and it sends a good message . <p> If you are buying good-quality pieces , which often come with bigger price tags , the longer they will last and the more you can re-wear them . <p> The " make do and mend " philosophy is sound but harder to apply to low-priced clothing . <p> Case in point : I bought a longline cardigan coat , not great quality , and the shoulder seam very quickly came apart . <p> I took it to be mended at the local dry cleaners , which cost $20 . <p> ( Need to add here that I can not sew myself , as I had a truly mean sewing teacher in fifth grade who terrorised me over my chain stitch sampler and I became convinced that sewing machines attracted poltergeists . ) <p> I then popped into Kmart for something and noticed a not dissimilar cardigan to the one I had just paid to mend . <p> It was $35 .
