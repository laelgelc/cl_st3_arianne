<p> The Australian Communications and Media Authority ( ACMA ) has found shock jock Alan Jones and radio station 2GB in breach of broadcasting rules over on-air comments about New Zealand prime minister Jacinda Ardern and climate change . <p> The watchdog found that 2GB breached decency rules in relation to comments made by Jones regarding the New Zealand prime minister . <p> The ACMA received more than 125 complaints about a broadcast of The Alan Jones Breakfast Show that went to air in 2019 . <p> Over the course of the broadcast Jones made several statements regarding Ardern that the ACMA has determined offended against generally accepted community standards of decency . <p> These statements by Mr Jones included : <p> " I just wonder whether Scott Morrison 's going to be fully briefed to shove a sock down her throat . " <p> " Now I hope Scott Morrison gets tough here with a few backhanders . " <p> " I hope he goes for the throat this morning . " <p> ACMA chair Nerida O'Loughlin says these comments were not appropriate for public radio broadcasting in today 's Australia . <p> " The repeated use of violent metaphors by Mr Jones and his apparent encouragement of aggressive silencing of Ms Ardern was highly offensive and did not meet contemporary community expectations , " O'Loughlin says . <p> " This was evident in the public display of outrage from the community , actions by advertisers and actions by then chairman of Macquarie Media who publicly stated any recurrence of this type of comment would result in the termination of Mr Jones ' contract . <p> " Acknowledging that the broadcast had caused offence to many in the community , 2GB did not oppose ACMA 's breach finding in relation to decency . " <p> Macquarie Media chairman Russell Tate , in a letter to clients , said the station " got it wrong " with Jones and would work to solve the issue . <p> Jones also made an on-air apology on the same day as the broadcast , an on-air clarification the following day and a written apology to the New Zealand prime minister . <p> The licensee also advised the ACMA it had counselled Jones . <p> " Given the imminent retirement of Mr Jones from 2GB and the actions taken by Macquarie Media and Mr Jones at the time , the ACMA is not taking further action against the licensee for breach of the decency rules , " O'Loughlin says . <p> The ACMA 's investigation also found a number of statements made in a segment about climate change policy were not accurate . <p> These included an incorrect assertion by Jones that biomass is a fossil fuel , and the incorrect presentation of figures relating to Australia 's and New Zealand 's percentage of energy use from solar and wind . <p> The ACMA found that these comparisons were not based on like-for-like data . <p> Jones will provide an on-air correction following due to the inaccurate comments he made about climate change . <p> O'Loughlin says broadcasters had a responsibility under their own industry code to use reasonable efforts to ensure the facts they are presenting are accurate . <p> " The factual error and inconsistent information were used to incorrectly portray that Australia generates more of its energy from renewables than New Zealand , " O'Loughlin says . <p> In addition to the on-air correction of the factually incorrect statements by Jones , 2GB has told ACMA it will incorporate the investigation findings into future training for staff . <p> Jones announced his retirement from radio earlier in the month , after receiving doctors ' advice that he needed to for his health . <p> Nine has since announced Ben Fordham will replace Jones on the breakfast radio show . <p> Have something to say on this ?
