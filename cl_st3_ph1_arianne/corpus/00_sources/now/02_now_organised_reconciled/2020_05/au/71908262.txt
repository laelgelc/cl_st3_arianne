<p> Few people , on either side of the political divide would disagree with Labor leader Anthony Albanese 's summing up of Mike Kelly 's career on Thursday . " He has brought a great deal of dignity , talent , capacity and commitment to this parliament , " he said . The member for Eden-Monaro , who reclaimed the formerly bell-whether seat for Labor by defeating the Liberal 's Peter Hendy in 2016 , has been forced to step down by medical issues that have dogged him since his army days . The former colonel , who ended his military career as the director of army legal services , gave 20 years of his life to the ADF . He first won Eden-Monaro in the Kevin 07 campaign , confirming the seat 's status as a bell-whether which always went to the party in government . Dr Kelly was also the first politician to shatter that tradition when , after having lost it in the 2012 poll , he was able to win it back in the knife-edge 2016 election . He was also successful in retaining it for Labor during last year 's " miracle election " which saw Scott Morrison returned as Prime Minister in his own right . The margin was tight , however . He scored 50.85 per cent of the two-party preferred vote . The other 49.15 per cent went to the Liberal 's Fiona Kotvojs . The difference was just 1.7 per cent . A swing of just under one per cent would now be enough to return Eden-Monaro to the government . Dr Kelly 's Thursday press conference showcased why he has been a well regarded local member and a highly respected MP . Affable , professional , courteous , and approachable are just some of the adjectives that have been used to describe him . His most notable achievement has been in carving out significant careers in the military and in politics without tearing others down . That , coupled with common sense and a centrist 's sense of moderation , made it possible for him to retain the support of swinging voters , even when there was a swing against his party . The ALP and the LNP are going to have to put a lot of thought into who they nominate for the by-election . Eden-Monaro is not an electorate that votes by the numbers ; it has to be won . It demands a candidate with strong local links and a commitment to the region . While there is talk of bringing in a high profile candidate , such as the NSW Nationals leader , John Barilaro , or Senator Jim Molan , the conservatives could do far worse than sticking with Fiona Kotvojs , last year 's candidate . She received 36,732 primary votes ( 37 per cent ) in 2019 compared to Dr Kelly 's 38,878 primary votes ( 39.17 per cent ) . Barilaro , the NSW deputy premier , is potentially divisive given his stance on brumbies in the high country and a penchant for Barnaby Joyce-like pronouncements . They were trounced by the Greens whose 8,715 primary votes ( 8.78 per cent ) helped get Dr Kelly across the line . Molan is also far from a perfect candidate . His admission his views on climate change were driven by lobbying , not by evidence , on QandA in February would haunt him in an electorate where the government 's bushfire response is a hot topic . The key question for both men is why would they risk the positions they already hold to contest a seat they might not win ? Eden-Monaro has far more than the usual number of swinging voters . That is why it is marginal . At this stage the ALP appears to be on the back foot heading into the by-election .
