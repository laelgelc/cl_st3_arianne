<h> Put away the doona - warmer winter expected despite chilly mornings <p> Despite the recent run of cold mornings , experts predict Queenslanders will not need their winter woolies too often over the next few months , with warmer-than-average temperatures predicted . <p> Tuesday morning saw temperatures 1.5 degrees below average for this time of year , following a cold snap at the end of April . <p> Experts say despite a chilly start to the week , we 're in line for another warmer-than-average winter . ( File image ) Credit:Bradley Kanaris/Fairfax Media <p> However , University of Southern Queensland climatologist Dr Chelsea Jarvis said despite what it felt like at the moment , Queensland 's autumn and winter would be warmer than usual . <p> " I suspect it has felt a bit colder than it actually is , because we had some quite warm temperatures leading up to the current cold snap , so it certainly caught me by surprise , " Dr Jarvis said . <p> Advertisement <p> " But the long-term forecast for the next three to four months is predicting daytime temperatures average to slightly above average , and night-time temperatures very likely to be above average . " <p> The much warmer than usual nights would be brought by increased cloud cover over winter , with more rainfall predicted than in the past . <p> " We do n't know if it 's going to be a lot more , but we do know it 's very likely to exceed the median rainfall figure , " she said . <p> " So the median rainfall for July to September in Brisbane is 69 millimetres , and we know we are very likely to exceed that . " <p> That mirrors the national forecast , which has predicted a wetter-than-average winter , in good news for struggling farmers in drought-hit parts of the country . <p> Oakey in the Toowoomba region was the coldest in the state on Tuesday morning at just 1.2 degrees , after a low of 1 degree on Monday morning . <p> Brisbane dropped to 12.3 degrees on Tuesday compared with 14.5 on Monday , and well below the May average minimum of 13.8 , with temperatures across the south-east similarly chilly . <p> Dr Jarvis said people shivering through a few cold mornings were experiencing the difference between weather - the immediate meteorological conditions happening outside our windows - and " climate " , the long-term trend of weather patterns . <p> " Those long-term trends we look at the overall picture of climate data , whereas weather is the next seven to 10 days , " she said . <p> " That 's where we do see those ups and downs , but over time , say over a three-month period , or a three-year period or even a 30-year period , those smaller ups and downs smooth out into the overall trend . " <p> The prediction for a warmer winter this year comes off the back of above-average temperatures experienced over the summer , which also saw widespread bushfires across the state and the country . <p> Loading <p> The 2019 winter was also warmer than average , with mean minimum and maximum temperatures nearly a degree above average . <p> And the reason for the warmer-than-average temperatures this winter was part of the overall trend of rising global temperatures , Dr Jarvis said , with countries around the world dealing with the effects of climate change . <p> " The past five or six winters in Queensland have been above average , both overall and for many individual areas of the state , and that 's just the reality of increasing temperatures , " she said .
