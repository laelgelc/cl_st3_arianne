<h> Coronavirus Government Global Briefing : May 20 <p> Welcome to Coronavirus Government Global Briefing , Mandarin Premium 's morning update on everything in local and global government responses to the COVID-19 outbreak . <h> World Economic Forum launches ' COVID-19 Risks Outlook report ' <p> The World Economic Forum 's Global Risks team has launched a new report that , based on the views of 350 senior risk professionals and published in partnership with Marsh &amp; McLennan and Zurich Insurance Group ( ZIG ) , is designed to create a holistic assessment of the headline risks , challenges and opportunities the world faces as a result of the pandemic . <p> In an article for the forum , ZIG 's Head of Sustainability Risk John Scott argues that the report , ' A Preliminary Mapping and its Implications ' , is designed to reconcile fears with the acceptance of uncertainties , and provide policy-makers and businesses a guide to the most likely fallout risks over the next 18 months . <h> How does risk operate , and how can we address it ? <p> That first area of concern , economic shifts , unsurprisingly tops the list of risks perceptions , but Scott highlights the interplay between indebtedness , for example , with social protection systems : <p> " High structural unemployment is likely to affect consumer confidence and the speed of economic recovery , as well as exacerbate inequality , mental health problems and lack of societal cohesion . It is also likely to widen the wealth gap between young and old and to pose significant educational and employment challenges that risk a second lost generation . " <p> Further , Scott notes how focusing on one global crisis creates means ignoring other existential global risks -- specifically in relation to climate change -- but that reboot economies and changing lifestyles could make it easier for businesses and governments to capitalise on low carbon opportunities . <p> Finally , Scott notes how increased dependence on technology through telehealth , online retail and home delivery could foster both greater mistrust or misuse of technology , specifically through new cybersecurity risks ; see , for example , every second headline for ' COVIDSafe ' . <h> Where are the opportunities ? <p> In Chapter Five of the report , " Implications for decision-makers " , there are a number of questions under five challenges for businesses , governments and societies worldwide : <p> Understanding interdependencies exposed by the crisis <p> i.e. " How can economic recovery and fiscal policies be used to accelerate nationally determined emissions reduction commitments and drive a low-carbon transition ? " <p> Managing institutional change in the midst of crisis response <p> Building back better <p> Building corporate , national and international resilience <p> Accelerating the evolution of public-private partnerships <p> Further , WEF 's risks paper has been published with a corresponding collection of essays , ' Challenges and Opportunities in the Post-COVID-19 World ' , which includes 10 essays on subjects including governance , geopolitics , technology , economics and work , health and psychological impact , and the public-private safety net . <p> In Chapter Four , " Geopolitics : Resilient and Sustainable Globalisation " Foreign Affairs Minister Julie Bishop and ANU colleague Anthea Roberts both dismiss critics who believe globalisation will return to normal or end completely once the pandemic is over . They warn that the biggest risks ahead include either an " over-correction or under-correction " in the degree of recovery , and call on governments to develop a new co-operative framework without clear global leadership : <p> " The risk of over-correction would involve a full swing back from the hyperglobalisation of the 1990s and early 2000s to a highly nationalist and protectionist order in the 2020s . Pro-globalisation supporters from the 1990s could be critiqued for presenting an excessively rosy picture of globalisation , " the two argue . <p> " There is also a risk of under-correction from assuming that COVID-19 will be a short and sharp disruption after which things will return to business-as-usual . " <p> The fill list of chapters includes : <p> " Global Governance : Planning for the World After COVID-19 , " Ngaire Woods <h> New York 's nursing home crisis <p> According to CBS News , Democrat Governor Andrew Cuomo announced on Sunday that no one should be prosecuted over the more than 5,300 coronavirus deaths in New York nursing homes . The statement comes just a week after mounting pressure saw him reverse a decision to send active patients back to homes . <p> Asked about recourse for aggrieved family members , Cuomo instead posited rhetorical questions for the 139 people who died the previous Saturday : <p> " How do we get justice for those families of those 139 deaths ? " Cuomo asked . " Who can we prosecute for those 139 deaths ? Nobody . Mother Nature , God , where did this virus come from ? People are going to die by this virus , that is the truth . <p> " Older people , vulnerable people are going to die from this virus . That is going to happen despite whatever you do . " <p> Perhaps unsurprisingly , Cuomo was quick to highlight his efforts to protect senior citizens in Monday 's address : <p> " Protecting our nursing homes and our seniors has been a top priority . Last week I put a requirement in place that staff at nursing homes be tested twice a week . <p> " You will always have people who say we should do more , that always happens and by the way now they 're complaining we 're doing more and they want it both ways . You cant have it both ways . " <h> How did New York get here ? <p> Unwise political communication aside , Cuomo 's comments follow extremely controversial directives across New York , New Jersey , Massachusetts , and California for long-term care facilities to admit discharged patients -- whether or not they were still infectious -- in an apparent effort to free up hospital space . <p> As the Foundation for Economic Education 's Jonathan Miltimore pointed out earlier this month , the decision attracted condemnation from health experts as a fairly obvious mechanism for spreading COVID-19 amongst facilities that both house vulnerable populations and where not built to properly quarantine infected people . <p> In late April , Cuomo was quick to defend the policy , even cutting off a journalist asking whether anyone had objected to it : <p> " They do n't have the right to object . That is the rule , and that is the regulation , and they have to comply with it . " <p> When Cuomo reversed that policy last week , and even enacted new testing requirements , Miltimore dismantled the New York Department of Health 's initial justification for actively denying nursing homes the ability to screen admissions as a " non-discriminatory " policy ; " the whole purpose of a quarantine is to discriminate the healthy from the sick so we can keep them separated and reduce spread of illness . " <p> Finally , Miltimiore -- a libertarian of sorts -- posits that Cuomo 's relatively-quick change of heart was driven by politics , and that the act of both making screening illegal , then reversing the ban in the face of public pressure , " exposes the problem with empowering politicians and bureaucracies with broad regulatory and police powers . The festival delivers three broadcasts each week until October , with events and activities to include music , storytelling , Indigenous performances , stand-up comedy , Zumba classes , Bollywood dancing and more . <h> Tasmania launches rent relief fund <p> Yesterday , Tasmania announced a ' COVID-19 Rent Relief Fund ' , which will ensure targeted support of up to $2000 or four weeks rent for tenants suffering extreme hardship . <p> Assistance will be made available to all tenants , including temporary visa holders , that are paying more than 30% of their income in rent and with less than $5000 in savings . <p> Payments will be made to landlords of eligible tenants , who will be required to pass it via via rent reductions , and will be administered by Communities Tasmania through Housing Connect . Applications will open from next week . <h> State wrap : Council funds , infection control and e-commerce <p> Over in NSW , the state government announced new legislative changes to provide councils greater flexibility to invest in infrastructure throughout the crisis , and confirmed that students will return to the classroom full-time from next Monday 25 May 2020 . <p> As the ABC reports , NSW will also allow regional travel in the state for any reason , including holidays , from June 1 .
