<h> CBD Melbourne : Turnbull and Grant follow crumbs in game of Canberra Cluedo <p> Malcolm Turnbull and his publisher Sandy Grant continue to investigate the mystery of who exactly received a leaked version of the former prime minister 's biography A Bigger Picture . It seems they are having considerable success . <p> When news first broke that pirated versions of the book had been shared far and wide , much scrutiny was focussed on Prime Minister Scott Morrison 's adviser Nico Louw . He had distributed a copy of the book to 59 people . Legal firm HWL Ebsworth 's defamation partner , Nicholas Pullen , who is working for Grant , has written to each recipient . <p> Malcolm Turnbull and Sandy GrantCredit:Shakespeare <p> We 're told Morrison was on Louw 's distribution list and recently received a letter ( his office declined to comment ) . Presumably Grant will let that one slide . Others have not been so lucky . Five recipients of the book reached settlements with the publisher this week . Louw , meanwhile , has been billed the equivalent of 59 digital copies ( more than $1000 ) . <p> But the money is n't the most interesting part . <p> Advertisement <p> Louw 's email gave Turnbull a list of the adviser 's contact book . But the wider attempt to trace the full spread of the manuscript beyond Louw has given the former PM a glimpse into the composition of other Canberra networks . Sources told this column that one copy was forwarded to News Corp commentators Andrew Bolt and Peta Credlin , and ex-PM Tony Abbott by another party . <p> Malcolm Turnbull 's A Bigger Picture on sale at the Parliament House gift shop in Canberra.Credit:Alex Ellinghausen <p> As Grant put it : " Following the distribution chain has been like getting our own guide book to the conservative side of Australian politics ... Malcolm has been going around saying it 's like Cluedo . " <h> ON THE SHELF <p> Turns out A Bigger Picture is now retailing for $29 a pop at Kmart and Big W. Beyond bricks and mortar , the book sits third on Amazon 's bestseller list , filed under " humour and entertainment " . Then again , it 's possibly the right category considering the book devotes a chapter to Tony Abbott 's decision to hand Prince Philip a knighthood . <h> THINKERBELL 'S NEXT FLIGHT <p> Eight months ago , PwC chief creative officer Russel Howcroft and Thinkerbell , the communications agency part-owned by the consultancy , had a dramatic falling out with the Minerals Council-associated COAL21 outfit . Now they 've landed another major lobby client . <p> The split with COAL21 came after the carbon capture and storage lobby group backed by BHP and Glencore handed over a motza for Howcroft 's assistance in a campaign designed to make us feel " proud about coal " . It did n't help the situation when confidential briefings about the campaign appeared in the press . <p> Late last year Howcroft , of course , popped up at the Prime Minister 's office to hold a workshop on how to better sell government policies , including those on climate change . <p> Now , according to details provided to members this week , Howcroft and Thinkerbell have been hired by the Business Council to design a campaign to push the safe reopening of the economy . In a Thursday missive to those members , BCA chief executive Jennifer Westacott wrote the " Good to Go " campaign would help " build momentum for the safe reopening of the economy while ensuring we keep our employees , customers and suppliers safe " . <h> MORDANT TO ADMIRE <p> Luminis Partners banker Simon Mordant 's association with Venice has certainly been a long - and at times bumpy - one . Sydney Museum of Contemporary Arts 's departing chairman is a major arts philanthropist and was twice Australia 's Commissioner at the Venice Biennale . <p> Then there was the 2017 funding snafu which pitted Mordant and fellow philanthropist Neil Balnaves against the Australia Council 's then chief executive Tony Grybowski . Ancient history now , of course . Because , for his contributions to the arts , Mordant has been awarded a knighthood in the Order of the Star of Italy by Italian President Sergio Mattarella . When CBD contacted Mordant on Thursday , he said he was " very surprised and very humbled " by the news . <h> HIGH AND MIGHTY <p> Parks Victoria has a battle on its hands as it prepares to forge ahead with a major cull of wild horses in the state 's High Country . Federal Court justice Michael O'Bryan paved the way for the cull last week , after dismissing claims it would impact on the Alpine National Park 's heritage value . <p> Of course , Parks Victoria officials are keeping the details of the plan under wraps and away from community groups who argue that brumbies are integral to the habitat and history of the region . In the meantime , a community group led by Anglers Rest cattleman Phil Maguire is preparing to take action with a muster ahead of the cull , part-organised online through the Facebook group Rural Resistance . <p> The high country community group appears to have pulled together supporters from across the conservative spectrum . Benambra MP and keen horse rider Bill Tilley joined the group during the week , and told CBD he planned to take part in a muster this weekend . Other members include News Corp columnist Piers Akerman and former New South Wales upper house Liberal Charlie Lynn .
