<h> Upper House inquiry to gauge environmental health of ' stressed state ' <p> Victoria 's rapidly deteriorating biodiversity , the threat of climate change and a bleak outlook for critically endangered flora and fauna will be examined in a parliamentary inquiry into the health of the state 's ecosystems . <p> Conservationists have welcomed the new Legislative Council inquiry , announced on Wednesday , saying Victoria was failing to slow the decline of the state 's biodiversity . <p> Mountain pygmy-possums weigh just 40 grams when they awake from months of hibernation.Credit:Zoos Victoria <p> " We have diverse natural heritage -- within a few hours you can go from a mountain ecosystem to a desert , " said Matt Ruchel , the executive director of the Victorian National Parks Association . <p> " But we are a stressed state in terms of ecological health -- we need institutions and laws that are up to combating that threat . " <p> Advertisement <p> The most recent environmental report card on Victoria , the " State of the Environment " analysis published last year , found the state 's environment was in worsening health in 51 of 170 categories . <p> Feral animals including cats , foxes and horses are growing in number and populating greater areas , while unique native animals including the mountain pygmy possum , Murray crayfish , spotted tree frog and Baw Baw frog are increasingly threatened . The devastating summer bushfires are very likely to have accelerated these trends . <p> Murray crayfish are extremely sensitive to habitat loss . <p> The state 's planning for the impact of climate change is poor , including coastal regions facing the likelihood of erosion from rising sea levels . <p> Sea levels along most of the Victorian coast are likely to rise by between seven and 18 centimetres by 2030 , under one scenario modelled by the CSIRO . <p> Loading <p> Upper House MP Cesar Melhem , chairman of the environment and planning committee , said it is " important that we hear from communities across Victoria so that the committee gets a complete picture of what 's happening to the state 's diverse ecosystems and what actions are needed to restore those under threat or in decline " . <p> Professor Mike Clarke , a zoologist from La Trobe University , said he believed the pandemic lockdown restrictions had increased public appreciation for outdoor and natural spaces near their homes . " I 'm hoping we can seize this opportunity to value our natural world appropriately , " he said . <p> Climate change means bushfires are increasing in frequency and severity and damp parts of the landscape are now burning with a devastating impact on flora and fauna , he said . <p> " Our environmental scorecards have very few good news stories in them at the moment and we need to turn that around , " Professor Clarke said . <p> More intensive monitoring is needed to understand the status of Victoria 's flora and fauna and track emerging risks , such as weeds and pest species that will move into unexpected landscapes and change the way ecosystems behave , he said . <p> The inquiry will be held by the Legislative Council 's environment and planning committee , with submissions invited before the end of July .
