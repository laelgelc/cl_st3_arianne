<p> It was with great sadness that I read Dr Mike Kelly would resign from the seat of Eden-Monaro for reasons of ill health ( " Eden Monaro MP to resign from parliament " , canberratimes.com.au , April 30 ) . Dr Kelly has been an outstanding Member of Parliament and will be greatly missed . He managed to get around and attend meetings more than most elected representatives . He has been particularly strong on climate change action . This has required courage in a largely conservative electorate where many people remain sceptical on the issue . His ill health ( renal failure ) was brought on by the dehydration he suffered while serving in the army in Somalia , East Timor and the Mid-East . Are we not looking after our soldiers when deployed overseas ? My grandson is in the army and I would not like to think that he would have to endure avoidable conditions , such as dehydration , that could lead to life-long illness . I wish Dr Kelly , and his wife Rachelle , all the very best in their post-parliamentary future . Watching Dr Mike Kelly retire made me reflect on my time working as a staffer with him over six years . One of my best memories as a young man is sitting down with Mike during a difficult time and receiving the following advice : " In life you will find some things you will describe as ' unacceptable ' . Unacceptable is a powerful word . If you find something unacceptable ... you must do something about it " . If you look across Mike 's life of service , you can always find him doing something about a range of unacceptable things , alongside his supportive family . Whoever steps up to be the next member for Eden-Monaro , I hope they look to Mike as an example . If they can follow his lead , they 'll do our region proud . Malcolm Turnbull 's decision to publicise his memoirs during the coronavirus pandemonium simply demonstrates his political tone deafness and sense of entitlement . The Prime Minister , and his ministers , are correctly ignoring him , focusing instead on this major crisis in a way that has been acknowledged on both sides of the political divide . They are doing so in conjunction with state and territory leaders . All parties are leaving politics aside . That said , it must be particularly galling to Turnbull that , at the very time he has chosen to protect his meagre legacy , including by attacking Scott Morrison , history has thrust greatness on his target . In many aged care homes today residents are incarcerated indefinitely with no access to family and friends . Their only crime seems to be that they are old . This is hard enough to deal with at the best of the times , without the added challenges of what seems to amount to kidnapping and confinement . Those forcing this situation on residents argue that it is the interests of the residents themselves . Has anyone asked the elderly if they would prefer to risk an earlier death in order to retain their precious connections with loved ones ? Or are they all automatically regarded as too stupid to make the decision ? Could we face a situation where the managers of these places face kidnapping or other charges ? If we do it might force those involved with aged care facilities in order to make a fast buck to move elsewhere . That would leave those who genuinely care about the elderly in charge of the residences . The ACT government seems incapable of adapting to improved circumstances . It is showing a distinct lack of trust in the Prime Minister and Chief Medical Officer 's advice and encouragement to allow students to gradually return to face-to-face learning . After making its case to follow NSW 's lead , Barr and Berry chose to cherry-pick some elements from NSW and completely ignore others . I realise many ACT parents still want to keep their children at home . I do n't have a problem with that . Nevertheless , having spoken with a number of teachers , administrative staff , and parents of high school students , it is debatable if the ACT government is even aware of the sentiment of many that our Year 11 and Year 12 students want to , and should be able to begin returning to face-to-face learning . The NSW model does not compel parents to send their children to school . It does take a measured , cautious and staged approach to opening up face-to-face learning . The territories are in a significantly better situation than all Australian states with respect to COVID-19 , yet , unlike NSW , the ACT government continues to sit on its hands . Virgin Australia is in administration because of a reported $6 billion debt and about 12,000 creditors . Its planes ca n't leave Australia . But , in a matter of life and death , a cruise vessel , which reportedly sourced scores of coronavirus cases and contributed to many deaths , has brought on a judicial inquiry . Nevertheless , in a confounding move , the vessel and its captain have been allowed to leave our shores . This is despite the prevailing view human lives are more important than money . Any contradictions between the law of the sea and Australia 's sovereign laws must be clarified . If there 's one compelling lesson our politicians can learn from the twin tragedies of bushfire and pandemic in 2020 , it 's that their approval ratings soar when they act and speak in the nation 's genuine interest . That means collaboratively , truthfully , candidly and without any political sniping . They could clinch the deal by ending the cynical use of faceless political staffers who are still being given the wink to do the dirty work , all the while insulating their masters from direct accountability for wrongdoing . The group that is suffering the most in the lock down environment is the same group that will suffer most from the impacts and the costs of the changing climate ; our young people . This is where the fresh ideas about the post-coronavirus recovery have to start . We ca n't go back to an economy based on unsustainable consumption , a disappearing biosphere , continued dependence on fossil fuels , growing emissions , flat lined wages , casualisation , and a massive chasm between rich and poor . We have to find a way forward that does not just involve digging things out of the ground and selling them overseas . All the science tells us that we have about 10 years to reduce global emissions by about 50 per cent . The other 50 per cent has to be achieved by 2050 . Business as usual is not going to deliver that , but it can be done . We have the tools , we know what to do . Do we have the will ? Our young people certainly need us to . If we do n't they will be living on a very inhospitable planet by 2050 . I 'm happy to support the young people when they take to the streets . They surely will if the government and its donors try to take us back to neoliberalism 101 . Re Choice 's claim private health funds are making inflated profits and counter claims this money will be returned to the members . Putting aside the over $1 billion the funds have already taken from the health system in profits , it needs to be pointed out members in not-for-profit funds have that money retained in the fund for their health needs . It defies logic that so many people still remain in funds which skim so much from their health dollars and have high administrative costs . There is a sign in Spence , and one in Holt , informing everyone the intersection is being signalled . This is a commendable effort by the ACT government to help motorists cope with frantic traffic flows . But the volume of traffic is not the only reason we have a build-up at intersections . It is also caused by drivers who hesitate to move despite having the safest clearance . Drivers glue themselves to intersections until no other car is in sight . In a busy area it does not take long for traffic to congest . I find this kind of driving behaviour very annoying .
