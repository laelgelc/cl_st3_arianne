<h> Opinion <h> WA gas has been a disaster for the climate -- do n't send it east at taxpayer expense <h> By Piers Verstegen <p> May 24 , 2020 -- 6.45am <p> The prospect of an east-west intercontinental gas pipeline has again reared its head after the Morrison Government 's national COVID-19 co-ordination commission promoted it as a way to power industry on the east coast with cheap gas from the west . <p> Loading <p> The proposal should not come as a surprise to anyone ; the commission is headed up by long-time gas lobbyist Nev Power . <p> On first sight the proposal might sound like a good idea . However , the reality is that here in WA gas is neither cheap nor clean . <p> While spending billions of tax payers dollars on a cross-continental white elephant might buoy the ailing gas industry , it will be a disaster for the climate and deliver an energy supply that Australia can not afford . <p> Advertisement <p> And its re-emergence flies in the face of a 2019 $5 million feasibility study from Acil Allen ordered by the Morrison government . That study showed that the pipeline would neither stack up commercially nor provide a good return on taxpayers ' investment . <p> In order to pay back the cost of building the 2900-kilometre trans-continental pipe , the price of the gas to consumers would have to be significantly higher than the price of sea-borne LNG . <p> Furthermore , since the Acil Allen study , the cost of both renewable energy and batteries for ensuring supply -- the most common purpose of gas generation -- has continued to fall even further . <p> The South Australian battery paid for itself within two years and several more are planned for installation while the cost of solar panels continues to decline . <p> Gas does not stack up against renewable energy now , and it certainly will not in five years ' time when the billions of dollars it would take to build a pipeline must be recovered in the form of even higher gas prices for Australian households and businesses . <p> But the biggest problem may not be the cost to consumers , but the cost to the climate . <p> " Gas companies , led by Woodside , have stridently opposed action on climate change in WA , using their corporate influence to prevent emissions reduction targets or regulation of pollution , leaving the state a global laggard on job-creating climate action . " Credit:Michele Mossop <p> Gas is repeatedly claimed by its backers to be a ' clean fuel ' and an ' ideal partner ' to renewable energy , but the experience in the west has been very different . <p> Thanks to the WA Domestic Gas Reservation policy the WA gas market is in an almost constant state of oversupply , providing a valuable lesson on what actually happens when you add a lot more gas into the energy mix . <p> The example of WA provides a cautionary tale ; WA has the highest proportion of gas generation of any Australian state but the lowest proportion of renewable energy and the fastest growth in carbonpollution , placing Australia 's Paris Agreement targets out of reach . <p> Instead of paving the way for solar and wind to enter the market by providing a reliable backup , theabundance of gas generation here in WA has made it more difficult for renewable projects to enter the market because of oversupply . <p> Not only is gas driving up WA 's emissions , but Australian taxpayers are being forced to pay the gasindustry 's pollution bill as the country spends billions offsetting gas emissions through the taxpayer-funded Emissions Reduction Fund . <p> If that is not bad enough , the industry is planning its biggest expansion yet . <p> The giant Burrup Hub proposal by Woodside , Shell , BP and others would produce around four times the emissions of the Adani coal mine -- over six billion tons of CO2 from direct and indirect emissions over its 50-year lifetime . <p> To offset the direct emissions of this project through the ERF would cost taxpayers over $17 billion dollars . <p> The gas industry does not just pollute the climate , it pollutes our politics too . <p> Loading <p> Gas companies , led by Woodside , have stridently opposed action on climate change in WA , using their corporate influence to prevent emissions reduction targets or regulation of pollution , leaving the state a global laggard on job-creating climate action . <p> Despite its excessive profits , the gas industry is the smallest employer of any sector in WA , producing fewer jobs per dollar of investment than any other industry . <p> Gas is dominated by foreign-owned corporations meaning its profits are going overseas while Australians pay its huge pollution bills . <p> The multinational companies behind WA 's huge gas projects pay virtually no tax or royalties for the gas they extract and export or sell at a tidy profit to Australian households and businesses . <p> With his direct commercial interests in WA gas projects , it is no surprise that Nev Power and his COVID-19 commission are pushing a taxpayer-funded pipeline that would subsidise the next round of profits for the gas industry . <p> But let us not be confused -- this will cost Australians dearly . <p> It will be a disaster for the climate and it will hold our country back from the opportunities in clean and affordable renewable energy that can underpin a recovery from COVID-19 and set Australia on a clean and competitive path for the jobs and growth of the future . <p> Piers Verstegen is the director of the Conservation Council of Western Australia
