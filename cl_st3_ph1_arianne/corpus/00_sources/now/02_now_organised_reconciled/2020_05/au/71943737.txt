<h> Court decision clears way for brumby culls <p> The Federal Court has given Victoria the green light to trap and cull brumbies in the high country , where scientists say they are trampling the habitat of critically endangered species in the wake of the summer bushfires . <p> The landmark ruling has implications for both Victoria and NSW , where the effect of the catastrophic bushfires on unique alpine ecosystems has placed pressure on state governments to tackle the soaring numbers of feral horses . <p> The Australian Brumby Alliance took Parks Victoria to court last year to prevent it trapping , removing or " otherwise interfering " with brumbies from the Bogong High Plains and Alpine National Park , arguing feral horses were part of the cultural heritage of the Australian alps , and their removal should be referred to the federal Environment Minister . <p> But in a judgment delivered on Friday morning , Justice Michael O'Bryan ruled he was not satisfied that prohibiting the removal of brumbies was appropriate , and their removal would not have a significant impact on the national heritage values of the Australian alps . He also ordered the association to pay Parks Victoria 's legal costs . <p> Advertisement <p> This decision clears the way for Parks Victoria to begin a contentious new culling program in the next fortnight . <p> The agency will deploy small teams of professional shooters , overseen by veterinarians , to cull horses in remote areas at night using thermal imaging and silenced guns . <p> Cowombat Flat exclosure plots show the difference in vegetation in areas where brumbies are excluded . Credit:Suplied : Parks Victoria <p> Previously , horses have been lured into yards and trapped , then captured or removed , although there has not been strong demand for rehomed brumbies . The feral horse population has soared in the past five years -- from about 9200 in 2014 to 25,300 in 2019 across the Australian alps . <p> Parks Victoria says there is a pressing need for feral animal control following the bushfires , with many parts of the alpine region heavily burnt and only tiny areas of green fringing waterways . <p> Injured or sick horses have previously been euthanised but this is the first time free-ranging horses will be culled . Even the location of the culls will remain secret to prevent confrontation with brumby lovers , who say the feral horses are an integral part of alpine cultural heritage . <p> Australian Brumby Alliance spokesperson Colleen O'Brien said she felt shattered : " We feel we are losing irreplaceable heritage based on flawed science . " <p> We feel we are losing irreplaceable heritage based on flawed science . <p> Colleen O'Brien , Australian Brumby Alliance <p> Ms O'Brien also said being ordered to pay costs was a blow to the organisations that funded the court case privately . " It sends a message to other organisations like ours not to take on the government , " she said . <p> Dr Mark Norman , chief conservation scientist at Parks Victoria , says the native plants and wildlife in the alps -- which represent just 0.3 per cent of Australia 's landmass -- occur nowhere else on Earth . In addition to rare plants and herbs , the mountain plains are home to unique and endangered Australian animals such as alpine frogs , skinks and spiny crayfish . <p> " They have taken millions of years to evolve and in the last couple of hundred years , we have introduced horses and deer and they are trashing the system , " Dr Norman said . <p> The hard hooves of horses and deer trample waterways and riverbanks , destroying the habitat of endangered species , he said . <p> Aerial reconnaissance by park rangers in recent months has observed large numbers of feral horses and deer in the remaining unburnt areas of the Alpine National Park . These include fragile areas of high conservation value , like moss beds and alpine herb and wildflower meadows . <p> Brumbies seen in the snow on May 7 along the Snowy Mountains Highway near Kiandra , NSW . Credit:Alex Ellinghausen <p> Invasive Species Council chief executive Andrew Cox said while Parks Victoria was taking control of feral horses in the Alpine National Park , the same could not be said for Kosciuszko . <p> " Sadly , NSW is still without a long-term plan to address the growing numbers of feral horses in Kosciuszko National Park , " Mr Cox said . <p> Burnt waterways and mossbeds at Davies Plain , Alpine National Park . <p> In February , NSW Liberal Environment Minister , Matt Kean , announced about 4000 feral horses would be removed from Kosciuszko National Park as part of an emergency response after large areas of alpine ecosystems were devastated by bushfires . Relocation is said to be the priority , but the government has not ruled the culling of some brumbies . <p> When the Australian Alps were added to the National Heritage List in 2008 , the listing noted the stories , legends and myths of the mountains are " part of Australia 's national identity " . <p> Loading <p> But Dr Norman says the romance does n't match the reality and native species are in a dire predicament in the wake of the fires . " Climate change is making it harder for endangered animals -- you 're getting pushed off the top of mountains by the warming climate and getting stomped on by invasive animals . "
