" <p> The Tories are brushing aside any such suggestion . <p> " Ontario families and businesses struggling to keep their head above water ca n't afford a carbon tax . It makes life more expensive , and our province more uncompetitive , " Doug Ford said in an emailed statement . <p> But a Canadian law firm dedicated to climate and clean energy says extricating Ontario from the cap-and-trade market with Quebec and California would be " more than an administrative nightmare " and ushers in more uncertainty for businesses . <p> DeMarco Allan LLP senior partner Lisa DeMarco said it would take years to untangle the agreement , an unknown amount of cash to reimburse companies who have bought into the market , and opens Ontario to lawsuits , especially from companies in California . <p> " It would be very , very difficult , " DeMarco told iPolitics . <p> Carbon pricing supporters largely agree with that assessment , but at least one industry group does not . <p> The Chemistry Industry Association of Canada says companies already lack certainty in the current cap-and-trade system because many of the regulations for the next phase of the market have not been set . <p> " We 're not asking for repeal but neither are we afraid of it , " said the association 's president and CEO Bob Masterson . <p> Ontario 's Environment Minister Chris Ballard has previously dismissed the idea of repealing the cap-and-trade system , but this week acknowledged that " anything is possible . " <h> How Ontario can get out of dodge <p> Brought into force in January , the market with California and Quebec was first announced in 2015 . In Ontario , electricity importers and fuel suppliers are among the companies that are required to take part in the system . <p> The agreement allows for any jurisdiction to pull out of the market , but it sets limits on the timing , meaning even if Ford takes government after the June election , it would be years before Ontario gets out of cap-and-trade . <p> Ontario is expected to give California and Quebec 12 months notice if it wants to bail on the shared carbon market . And it can only leave at the end of a compliance period . The current one wraps up at the end of 2020 . <p> Ontario 's system is aimed at lowering greenhouse gas emissions by putting caps on the amount of pollution companies in certain industries can emit . If they exceed those limits they must buy allowances at quarterly auctions or from other companies that come in under their limits . <p> The province has raised nearly $2.5 billion so far from the auctions and its already allocated close to $2 billion of that for spending on green initiatives like home retrofits and transportation projects . <h> Reimbursing companies <p> DeMarco said that in the event of Ontario pulling out of the scheme , the government would have to figure out how to " reverse engineer " the six auctions that have already happened and figure out whether companies need to be refunded for buying emissions allowances . <p> To further complicate matters , she said Ontario would n't just have to worry about its own companies but also those in Quebec and California that have bought or sold allowances in this province . <p> " It would be quite challenging , " she said . " If you just take the time it 's taken to create the system and reverse it then yeah , I think you 're looking at years . " <p> Ontario 's environment department says its already started selling emissions allowances for the compliance period that will start in 2021 . Of the 131,338,242 allowances sold so far , it said 8,576,000 are for the period after 2020 . <p> Whether the province would refund those purchases -- and at what cost -- is a big unknown , according to DeMarco , who pointed out that companies only bought into them because they were required to . <p> But Masterson said he believed that most companies would n't need to be refunded because they were purchasing allowances for the period ending in 2020 . <p> " You ca n't make the assumption that every entity that ever bought an allowance is going to come back and ask for a rebate , " he said . <h> Heading for the courts <p> DeMarco said walking out of the cap-and-trade market would likely also come with " legal consequences " especially because California is part of the equation . <p> She points out that while the system was a cost to some companies , it was a money-maker for others . The big difference between cap-and-trade and carbon tax is that companies can create " value from it . " <p> " In cap-and-trade you can do more than you are required to do and sell the excess for value , " she explains . <p> In the face of regulatory changes , DeMarco said American companies are " much more litigious than Canadians are , " meaning that Ontario risks entering a legal quagmire . <p> " American companies are much more likely to sue , " she said . " They regularly take their government to court , we do n't do that very often as Canadian companies . " <p> Ford is promising to join Saskatchewan Premier Scott Moe in fighting any carbon tax that Ottawa plans to impose on provinces that do n't implement their own carbon price . He has said he will go " all the way to the Supreme Court if we have to , if it means stopping this tax from being rammed down our province 's throats . " <p> Ottawa has already tabled legislation that would impose a price on carbon on the provinces . DeMarco said Ontario would end up with a more expensive system if the province trades in the current system for one from the Trudeau Liberals . <p> The Progressive Conservatives have said they do n't plan to release a full platform , but rather focus on a few policy planks . It 's not clear if the plan for exiting the cap-and-trade market will be part of that .
