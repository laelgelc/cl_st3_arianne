<h> Trudeau Trumpets Global Teamwork On COVID-19 Crisis At Key UN Meeting <h> The conference came as Canada is competing for a seat on the Security Council next month . <p> CPPrime Minister Justin Trudeau is shown in a screengrab of a United Nations webcast on May 28 , 2020 . <p> OTTAWA -- Prime Minister Justin Trudeau led a united call for global co-operation at a major United Nations meeting Thursday aimed at mitigating the devastating social and economic impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic . <p> And UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres tacitly endorsed Canada 's ability to convene larger groups of countries to serve the greater international good -- a key plank in Canada 's platform for a temporary seat on the Security Council . <p> The COVID-19 conference came as Canada is competing for one of two non-permanent seats on the Security Council next month against Norway and Ireland . Canada is running on a platform of trying to help rebuild the post-pandemic world , and Trudeau said Thursday 's video conference of more than 50 heads of state and government was an example . <p> Also on HuffPost : Trudeau says visit to U.S. for G7 meeting possible <p> Trudeau said the conference was the result of ongoing work with his conference co-host , Jamaican Prime Minister Andrew Holness , to mitigate the effects of climate change . <p> " Canada has a long tradition of convening , of gathering people together to deal with larger issues and this is certainly a role we hope to play on the UN Security Council , " Trudeau said . <p> " We demonstrated ( that ) today on an issue we 've been working on over the past five years in terms of development , financing and improving economic flows to countries in need . This is something that we can gather critical masses of consensus around the world . " <p> Trudeau said Canada , potentially as one of 10 rotating non-permanent members of the council , could help find common ground with its five veto-wielding permanent members who have been deadlocked for years on many issues , notably the carnage in Syria . <p> " I , of course , agree with the prime minister . I have never seen the council work being paralyzed by elected members , " Guterres said . <p> Trudeau , Guterres and Holness began the hours-long videoconference by affirming support for the international institutions that need to lead the recovery . <p> " Our citizens need to have confidence in international institutions that leave no one behind and are capable of overcoming global challenges , " Trudeau said in opening remarks <p> Remarks by dozens of heads of state and government followed , including Germany 's Angela Merkel , France 's Emmanuel Macron , and Britain 's Boris Johnson , along with representatives of the World Bank , the International Monetary Fund and the private sector . <p> U.S. President Donald Trump was noticeably absent as was China 's Xi Jinping . Trump has argued that wise leaders put the interests of their own countries first . <p> Guterres said the U.S. and China would support the various committees that were created in Thursday 's meeting to report back to him by July on the financial recovery . <p> Merkel reminded the gathering that Germany has trumpeted climate change during its past G7 presidency . Trump is hosting the G7 summit this year . <h> Boris Johnson : No single country holds ' keys to victory against our invisible enemy ' <p> European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen noted the pandemic might push some countries to focus on their own needs . <p> " This is wrong . We have to work together , and for each other . " <p> Macron , who has never shied away from criticizing Trump , said the " collective action " of the world 's multilateral system is crucial . <p> " No single country can find a solution to this crisis . There can be no isolated solution . We have to address issues collectively , such as health , the environment , justice . " <p> Johnson , sometimes viewed as a kindred political spirit of Trump 's , piled on . <p> " No single country hold the keys to victory against our invisible enemy , " he said . " If we are to defeat COVID-19 , achieve a global recovery , and avoid a future pandemic , then we must work together across borders . " <p> Ireland and Norway , Canada 's rivals for the two temporary seats on the security council , echoed the sentiment . <p> " This is no time to seek confrontation and to look inward , " said Norway 's Prime Minister Erna Solberg . <p> " Let us use this moment to ensure more global co-operation in order to build back a healthier , greener , fairer , gender-equal and sustainable world . " <h> Canada courting support for Security Council bid <p> The subtext of the looming Security Council vote was also on display . Trudeau has been courting the support of various large voting blocs in Africa , Asia and the Caribbean because European countries are expected to rally around Norway and Ireland . <p> Barbadian Prime Minister Mia Mottley , the current chair of the 20-country Caribbean Community , thanked Trudeau for his remarks , but laid down a clear marker for him and others . <p> " I hope that this high-level session will not simply be the record of glorious speeches but will trigger action that is needed badly . " <p> Trudeau said later he wanted to see firm action , noting that Caribbean countries that depend on tourism to support their economies need help to overcome the fiscal damage done by pandemic 's lockdown and closed borders . <p> Unless countries come together now to co-ordinate a recovery plan , the UN estimates the pandemic could slash nearly US$8.5 trillion from the world economy over the next two years , forcing 34.3 million people into extreme poverty this year and potentially 130 million more over the course of the decade . <h> 6 urgent areas of action <p> The UN said all countries face economic strain due to the pandemic , particularly developing countries which were already in " debt distress " before the crisis and ca n't afford to cushion the blow for their citizens or to undertake fiscal stimulus measures . <p> The conference addressed " six urgent areas of action " to mobilize the financing needed for a global recovery :
