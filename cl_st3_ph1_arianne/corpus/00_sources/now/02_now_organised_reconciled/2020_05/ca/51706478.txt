<h> Still rehabbing injury in Vegas , Winnipeg 's Cody Glass reflects on up-and-down first NHL season <p> Forward and Winnipegger Cody Glass is the only injured player on the Golden Knights roster , so he goes into an almost-empty facility for an hour each day to do rehab and receive treatment . ( Getty images ) Ethan Miller / Getty Images <p> His first season in the NHL included time playing on the top line of the Vegas Golden Knights , a severe knee injury , surgery , and eventually some lonely months far away from his family during the Covid-19 pandemic . <p> Winnipeg 's Cody Glass , a 21-year-old centre who was the Golden Knights ' first ever first-round draft pick , remains upbeat and looks at the season as a positive experience overall . <p> It has n't been easy , especially the last two months , which have been spent going through rehab on his right knee in Vegas , with many of his teammates dispersed to their off-season homes and his family back home in Winnipeg , unable to visit . <p> " That 's the tough part , not being able to see my family , especially with the border being closed and everything , " Glass said from Las Vegas on Thursday . " Hopefully this will all be over soon and I 'll see them eventually . " <p> Glass normally lives in the off-season with his dad , Jeff , and older brother , Matt , in Winnipeg . Jeff was a single father who raised the boys on his own and has been a huge influence in their lives . <p> " You never expected to see the world go like this and it 's hard to even take it day by day , knowing that your family is so far away and you ca n't really see them , " Cody said . " It 's kind of scary and it 's kind of hard to talk about really . " <p> First injured in a collision with Jay Bouwmeester of the St. Louis Blues on Jan. 4 , Glass worked his way back to a play a couple games in February , but then re-injured his knee and underwent season-ending surgery on March 7 . <p> The original prognosis was he would be ready for training camp in the fall , but it would take a lot of rehab , so Glass had to stay close to the Golden Knights ' facilities . <p> Injured players are still allowed to use team facilities under NHL and Nevada state health rules . <p> But Glass is the only injured player on the Golden Knights roster , so he goes into an almost-empty facility for an hour each day to do rehab and receive treatment . <p> " It 's nice weather here and Winnipeg was kinda tough nine weeks ago , so it 's not so bad , " he said with a chuckle . <p> " I 'm still rehabbing , still working out , getting better each day . It 's kind of a blessing in disguise going through this pandemic ... it sounds weird but if you have to go through an injury , it 's good to go through it during this time . " <p> Things started out so well for Glass as he scored his first NHL goal in his first NHL game on Oct. 2 , 2019 . <p> He wound up playing 39 games , many of them on a No. 1 line with a couple of established scoring stars of the NHL . <p> " I got a chance to play with ( fellow Winnipegger ) Mark Stone and Max Pacioretty and I played different positions , " Glass said . " I kinda got a feel for the league and that 's something going into the summer that I want to build on . Now I have a taste for it and I just want to keep improving and get better . " <p> Glass finished the season with five goals and 12 points , but more importantly he gained a bundle of experience to take into next season . <p> " I was n't very satisfied with my season , " the former Portland Winterhawks star said . " I know I can always get better and improve but for my first 39 games , it was an incredible experience <p> " Honestly , it was a nice first taste of pro hockey . I played in the AHL playoff run ( in spring of 2019 ) but it 's a lot different going into the NHL . People are a lot faster , a lot stronger and it 's something you have to get used to . It 's a learning experience at the same time as trying to play your hardest . " <p> Glass is a speedster who he did n't have difficulty adjusting to the pace of the NHL game , but there were certainly other challenges . <p> " Everybody there is just as strong or stronger than you , so going into a corner battle is a lot tougher , especially coming from junior , " Glass said . " It 's just an adjustment and it takes time to get used to . Maybe just gaining a few pounds and getting stronger on my feet is something I need to improve on . " <p> Glass is obviously looking forward to the day he can get back to Winnipeg to see his dad and brother . And family is not the only thing he 's missing . <p> " It 's awesome here , " he said . " It 's obviously a bit of climate change . It 's sunshine every single day and it 's really nice . <p> " But honestly , being from Winnipeg , those winter times , going to the outdoor rinks , that 's something I love and it 's something I miss . " <h> Glass says NHL return to play is a ' sticky situation ' <p> Given the nature of his injury and the recovery time required after surgery , it 's not clear when Cody Glass may be able to skate again , let alone participate if the NHL reboots its season in the summer . <p> There is a plan for a 24-team playoff in the works for the summer months and Glass 's Vegas Golden Knights would be participants . In fact , they would have a bye to the round of 16 if the plan goes ahead as it has been reported . <p> Glass was expected to be recovered in time to start the 2020-21 season with the Golden Knights , but two months sooner than that would be pushing it . <p> Either way , the 21-year-old Winnipegger will be happy to see hockey back , if it happens . <p> " It 's a very sticky situation right now with everything that 's going on , " Glass said . " Obviously everybody wants hockey back , but you have to do it in the safest manner possible , so we 're just taking our time . We want to finish the season and if we 're going to do it , it 's going to be done in the safest way possible . <p> " As long as we 're not spreading it or putting anybody at risk , that 's gon na be the best option for us. "
