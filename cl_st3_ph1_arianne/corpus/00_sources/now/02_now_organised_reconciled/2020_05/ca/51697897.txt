What 's cool about the Dry is that it is an instantaneous measure of demand and supply pressures . There is n't much market optimism or pessimism in it , and no real expectations embodied . By contrast , look at the daily lease rate for a Very Large Crude Carrier -- it starts spiralling up whenever the Brent futures market starts showing serious contango six months to a year out ( which is a lot of fun if you happen to own a VLCC at the time ) . The Dry can be blown off course now and then , owing to the long lag in getting bulk carriers on and off the market , and it is slightly correlated with bunker fuel costs , but its instantaneous slope says a lot about the state of the world .
