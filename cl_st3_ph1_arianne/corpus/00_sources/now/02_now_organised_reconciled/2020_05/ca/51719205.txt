<h> Attention local birdwatchers : It 's officially warbler season ! <p> It 's warbler season in our area . Locally , yellowthroat and yellow warblers can be found nesting in our wetlands and chestnut-sided , yellow-rumped and golden-winged warblers will be summering in local woodlots . David Hawke/OrilliaMatters <p> If you were to chart the highs and lows of excitement for birdwatchers , you would see a high peak in late March when the waterfowl migrate northwards , the ducks , geese and swans following the edge of melting ice as the lakes open up . <p> Then there is a quieter time as the nearby migrants return from the Carolina area of United States . <p> The next big hurrah happens now , as a group of small and very colorful birds , known collectively as warblers , finally arrive from their wintering grounds in the Caribbean islands and South America . <p> Just as the ducks follow the receding ice , the warblers follow the bursting open of the tree flowers . As maples , poplars , willows , birch and elm begin their reproduction cycle with a presentation of pollen-rich blossoms , insects of all kinds are attracted by their sweet inviting nectar . <p> As warblers eat only insects , they have to wait for the seasonal event to kick in as they make their way northwards to their own breeding grounds . <p> When the spring season is unusually cold as it has been for two springtimes in a row , the warblers have to delay their migration until the plants can catch up to the weather . <p> In the 1980s the first and second weekend of May was known as " warbler season " but recently this wave of arrivals has been pushed back to third and fourth weekend . <p> A few of the warbler species will stay here and begin nest building , while the bulk of them are heading to northern Ontario and the boreal forests . <p> Locally we can find yellowthroat and yellow warblers nesting within in the shrubs of our wetlands , and our woodlots will be the summer home of chestnut-sided , yellow-rumped and golden-winged warblers . <p> Love those names , eh ? As indicated by their monikers , this is a colourful lot and makes for a visual treat when you can see them . <p> In northern Ontario where many of these birds nest and raise their young within the black spruce forests , several species of wood warblers live in the exact same habitat , thus vying not only with others of their own ilk , but against other warbler species as well for food and nesting sites . <p> How do they do it , so that one species does not out-compete another ? Resource partitioning . <p> As these birds forage through the forest , each has an area of a tree to cover . <p> The Cape May warblers search for insect food amongst and within the very tops of the spruce trees . Blackpoll warblers search the outer tips of the upper third of the tree . <p> Blackburnian warblers search the interior of the upper tree branches . Chestnut-sided warblers search the outer tips of the branches around the middle of the tree . Yellow-rumped warblers search for food around the base and trunk of the tree . <p> By sharing , or partitioning , the tree into search areas these five warbler species can each survive by finding enough food within their respective areas or parts of the tree . <p> The added benefit is that the spruce tree is cleansed of harmful insects . And healthy spruce trees eventually make for lots of lumber , do n't you know . <p> It has been interesting to watch the gradual but steady acceptance of the lumber trade to understand the intricate relationships between ' their ' trees and so many other wildlife species . <p> As we all struggle to understand what climate change really means , and what effects it is having on the environment , the relationship between the flowering dates of trees , the emergence of insects and the arrival of insect-eating migrants is a revealing task . <p> Birdwatching has gone from an odd hobby of the eccentric to a recognized element of scientific study . So take a moment and be part of this annual cycle of life . <p> We welcome your feedback and encourage you to share your thoughts .
