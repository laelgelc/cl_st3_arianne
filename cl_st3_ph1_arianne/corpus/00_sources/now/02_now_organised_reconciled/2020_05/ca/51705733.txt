AFP PHOTO / ROMEO GACADROMEO GACAD/AFP/Getty Images <p> A Laurentian academic has contributed to a major study on the importance of carbon capture in mitigating climate change . <p> Michelle Kalamandeen , a post-doctoral researcher , is co-author of the recently published paper Long-Term Thermal Sensitivity Of Earth 's Tropical Forests , which appears in the prestigious journal Science . <p> The article discusses the role tropical forests can play in the fight against climate change and will also have applications locally for recovering carbon stocks in Sudbury , the university said in a release . <p> " An increase of carbon in the environment leads to the heating of the planet , which can yield devastating consequences , " Laurentian said . " The paper underlines the remarkable carbon-capture abilities of tropical forests , abilities which are under threat . " <p> Measuring over half a million trees across the tropics , these forests are resistant to temperature changes but only up to a temperature of 32 C , Kalamandeen and her fellow researchers found . <p> Any further increase could lead to these ecosystems releasing carbon dioxide in the atmosphere . <p> Tropical forests may seem surprisingly resilient to temperature changes , but they can " be pushed outside of this safe zone , " according to Kalamandeen . <p> Intact forests " cope much better with temperature increases than fragmented or degraded forests , " the researcher said . " The current pandemic has provided us with compelling proof of how closely our fate is linked with the health of our forests -- from the food we eat to where they 're sourced and how healthy those systems are , to our own individual health . We have a unique opportunity here to transform and reset our global climate system . " <p> By ensuring these forests remain intact and act as carbon stores , " we 're ensuring that both nature and people benefit , " she said . <p> Kalamandeen is continuing her research project , titled Landscape Carbon Accumulation Research ( L-CARE ) , along with supervisor John Gunn and Nathan Basiliko of the Vale Living with Lakes Centre . <p> " We were privileged to have her join our Laurentian University team as a post-doctoral researcher in 2019 , working in partnership with Cambridge University in the UK to assess the importance of carbon capture in the regreening of the Sudbury landscapes , using the same satellite images tools that she has perfected in this paper , " said Gunn . <p> With the publication of the Science paper , Kalamandeen has helped highlight the " significance of disturbance of tropical forests , the ' lungs of the planet , ' " he said . <p> Researchers at Laurentian 's Vale Living with Lakes Centre are " internationally recognized for their contributions to climate change science and this paper further increases their impact and will inform future actions of the global community , " said Tammy Eger , interim vice-president of research at the university . <p> Kalamandeen 's work in Sudbury is possible thanks to a $1.6 million grant from the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada and Ontario Centre of Excellence . <p> The full article in Science , a research digest from the American Association for the Advancement of Science , can be found at **31;2435;TOOLONG
