<h> Market News <h> British banks can withstand pandemic fallout on economy : BoE <p> LONDON ( Reuters ) - Britain 's top banks and building societies are robust enough to keep lending if the economy were to shrink by 30% in the coronavirus pandemic , according to a stress test carried out by the Bank of England . <p> The stress test was based on an economic scenario published by the BoE in its Monetary Policy Report ( MPR ) on Thursday , in which it said Britain was on course for the biggest economic slump in over 300 years . <p> Under the MPR scenario , Britain 's GDP drops by almost 30% in the second quarter versus the fourth quarter of last year and recovers as lockdown restrictions are lifted . <p> Britain has been in lockdown since mid-March and the government is expected to announce some easing of restrictions in the coming days . <p> The BoE 's pared down , " desk top " stress test showed that banks have the capital buffers to withstand even greater losses than those that result from the MPR scenario , the BoE said in its interim Financial Stability Report ( FSR ) . <p> Core capital ratios would drop from 14.8% at the end of 2019 to 11% in the second year of the test scenario , still well above their minimum regulatory requirements , it said . <p> " Overall , in the desktop stress test based on the MPR scenario , banks incur total credit losses of just over 80 billion pounds ( $98.86 billion ) . " <p> Companies could face a cash-flow deficit of around 140 billion pounds under the scenario , the FSR said . <p> But usable buffers of capital built up by banks are more than sufficient to absorb the losses under the MPR scenario and , with the support of the government 's lending guarantee schemes , would also be enough to help the corporate sector finance its cash-flow deficit , it said . <p> The BoE has already told banks they can tap 23 billion pounds in their counter cyclical capital buffers that would support lending of up to 190 billion pounds . <p> On Thursday the bank held off from further stimulus measures but said it was ready to take more action to support the economy . <h> DASH FOR CASH <p> BoE Deputy Governor Jon Cunliffe said that if banks once more failed to provide support , as in the financial crisis 10 years ago , the overall economic outcome would be worse and lead to greater losses for banks . <p> " On the basis of the scenario and of the desktop stress test , the economic impact of banks failing to provide support to the economy could worsen their own capital positions by around a full percentage point , " Cunliffe said . <p> The BoE reinforced its encouragement for banks to tap capital and liquidity buffers that are above mandatory minimum requirements , in order to keep credit flowing . <p> So-called Pillar 2A buffers cover particular risks at individual banks and the BoE said on Thursday they would now be set at a nominal amount in 2020 and 2021 , instead of a percentage of total risk weighted assets , to alleviate " unwarranted pressure " on banks . <p> The BoE set out further ways to ease regulatory burdens so that banks can fully focus on helping businesses and households . <p> The BoE 's Financial Policy Committee said it was postponing the launch of a climate change-related stress test of banks from the second half of this year until at least mid-2021 . <p> The BoE said it has also paused work on its stress test of insurers , saying it wo n't publish the results and will postpone the next test to 2022 . <p> The core banking system , along with market infrastructure like clearing derivatives withstood turbulence and instability in financial markets in March when there was a " dash for cash " as investors responded to lockdowns , the BoE said . <p> But the market moves brought back into " sharp focus " a number of vulnerabilities in the non-bank sector , Cunliffe said in a reference to open ended funds , some of which had to be suspended . <p> " The underlying issues in the non-bank sector will need to be addressed in due course , " Cunliffe said . <p> Market volatility also underscored why the Libor interest rate benchmark needs to be scrapped by the end of 2021 , the BoE said . <p> Reporting by
