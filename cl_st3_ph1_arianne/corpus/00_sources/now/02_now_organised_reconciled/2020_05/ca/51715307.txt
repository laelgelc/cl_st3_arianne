<p> Thank goodness for that prolonged dousing of rain Sunday or the forest fire hazard in the Sudbury area could be as bad as it is in the northwest , where a Restricted Fire Zone went into effect Saturday in four districts and chunks of two others . <p> As of Tuesday afternoon , the Ontario Ministry of Natural Resources was reporting no new fires in the northeast , leaving eight existing ones it was dealing with already . All eight were either being held , under control or observed . <p> On Saturday , a ministry fire engine , two water bombers a helicopter and fire ground crews responded to a call for assistance by the City of Greater Sudbury concerning a 3.2-hectare fire known as Sudbury 12 , which broke out four kilometres northeast of Windy Lake . The fire is under control and being monitored by one fire crew . <p> Sudbury 13 , meanwhile , also broke out Saturday about seven kilometers northwest of Alban and was dealt with by a ministry crew that had to access the blaze by boat . The fire was 0.9 hectares in size and is classified as out . <p> Sudbury 14 , in contrast , totalled 10 hectares and saw two ministry water bombers , five crews and a helicopter respond . The Saturday afternoon fire , which broke out about five kilometres northeast of Nairn Centre , is classified as being held . The municipality asked the ministry for help battling the blaze . <p> Sudbury 15 , which broke out Saturday evening off Highway 17 , four kilometres west of McKerrow , in the Municipality of Baldwin , saw three fire crews , water bombers and a helicopter respond after a request for assistance was received . The 4.8-hectare fire , which is now out , saw Ontario Provincial Police shut down the highway for a short time . The municipality had asked for ministry help with the fire . <p> Sudbury 16 , finally , was tackled by one ministry crew , which had to use a boat to access the Lake Penage-area blaze Sunday . The fire was 0.1 hectares in size . <p> With a forecasted high of 29C and an overnight low of 18C Tuesday followed by a high of 30C and an overnight low of 17C Wednesday , the humidex is going to remain high . Rain is in the forecast for Thursday when the high is expected to drop to 24C , followed by an overnight low of 16C . <p> Things really cool off on Friday with the rain continuing and the high only expected to reach 21C . The overnight low , meanwhile , is expected to plunge to 8C . <p> Isabelle Chenard , an information officer with the Ministry of Natural Resources and Forestry in Greater Sudbury , said three to seven millimetres of rain fell across the Sudbury district on Sunday , helping to improve forest fire hazard situation in the northeast . The return of sun and higher temperatues Monday , Tuesday and on Wednesday , however , will push the rating up . The rain expected Thursday followed by cooler temperatures will then drive the forest fire hazard rating down from high to moderate , she said . <p> " Areas around and south of Greater Sudbury are moderate to high hazard , " Chenard said Tuesday . " The rest of the region , it 's currently moderate to low , including north of Sault Ste. Marie , except for parts of the far North over to the Manitoba border ( in the Cochrane district ) . " <p> Chenard said that as of Monday , a total of 100 forest fires had occurred this forest fire season across Ontario , up from 66 at the same point one year ago , but well down from the 10-year average of 191 . <p> " We 've had 50 fires in the northwest and 50 fires in the northeast as of ( Monday ) , " she said . <p> Chenard said the ministry continues to monitor the forest fire hazard situation across the northeast and , if a Restricted Fire Zone is necessary for public safety , will consider implementing it . <p> Chenard said that anyone looking at doing open burning should check with local regulations and have a water source handy . <p> " You should n't be having a fire until two hours before sunset and make sure it is extinguished two hours before sunrise , " she said . " You need to burn in the cooler hours of the day . " <p> That rain that fell on Greater Sudbury Sunday ( 9.5 mm , officially ) brought the total May precipitation to 22.5 mm -- way down from the 83 mm average , said Peter Kimbrell , a meteorologist with Environment and Climate Change Canada in Ottawa . <p> " It 's definitely below normal , " he said in a telephone interview . " We anticipate precipitation on Thursday . Thursday is probably going to have the significant rain . " <p> Kimbrell noted that an average May in Greater Sudbury gets 0.4 days of temperatures 30C or higher . <p> " We 've already had one -- the 23rd , " he said , " ( Tuesday ) is likely going to be another one and then Wednesday , as well . " <p> Environment and Climate Change Canada issued a heat warning for much of the northeast Tuesday morning , including Greater Sudbury and vicinity , Chapleau , Gogama and Foleyet , and West Nipissing and the French River . <p> As of mid-afternoon Tuesday , the humidex had climbed to 33C and was expected to reach 35-36C before receding . The reason for this early heat wave is the result of hot , humid air in the American south being pushed north by a high-pressure system over the Atlantic Ocean . <p> A normal high for this time of year in Greater Sudbury is 20C . A normal low is 8C . <p> Kimbrell does not want to speculate on what kind of forest fire season the Sudbury area . <p> " I tend to shy away from long-term forecasts , " he said , " One month ago , I would have told you we were looking at an above average May . ( But ) the first two weeks of May were really cold and we had snow -- 16 centimetres May 7 and 8 . The average snowfall for May is 1.9 cm . " <p> Kimbrell is more confident forecasting short-term weather . <p> " In terms of the next 10 days , it 's going to cool down on the weekend , down to lows of 4 and 5C Friday and Saturday , and 7C on Sunday night , " he said . " On Saturday , the high will be 11C , and then we will see some moderation as we go forward into next month . Cool down on the weekend and then warm right up early next month . The hot weather ( that 's coming to Ontario ) will get close , but probably not into Sudbury . "
