<p> Doreen Vanderstoop has Margaret Atwood to thank for motivation when it came to penning her debut novel . <p> Well , indirectly at least . <p> Vanderstoop toiled on Watershed ( Freehand Books , 355 Pages , $22.95 ) off and on for roughly a decade while raising a family in Calgary and working as a freelance writer . Five years ago , she read a comment that Atwood had made while attending a literary festival in Barrie , Ont. , giving her a new boost of urgency and energy . By this point , the renowned author had helped popularize and spread the idea of cli-fi , or climate fiction , with her unsettling dystopian trilogy Oryx and Crake , The Year of the Flood and MaddAddam . On that day , Atwood made a statement -- or perhaps more of a challenge -- to her peers . <p> " She said ' Where are the Canadian writers who are writing about this really important issue ? ' " says Vanderstoop , in an interview from her Calgary home . " That was my clarion call to get it done . " <p> In 2015 , Vanderstoop attended workshops at Sage Hill Writing in Saskatchewan that kicked the novel back into high gear . <p> " It was really Margaret Atwood , with that one little statement , " she says with a laugh . " I thought ' I 've got to finish this thing and get it out there . ' " <p> On Saturday , Vanderstoop will hold a virtual book launch for Watershed from her Calgary home . But even before its official pub date , the book has shown early signs of becoming a runaway success , landing in the No. 2 spot last week in the Book Publishers of Alberta 's list of fiction bestsellers in the province . <p> Despite its decade-long gestation , Watershed is both timely and urgent as it imagines a semi-dystopian future in Alberta brought on by climate change . In the year 2058 , the glaciers have vanished and a catastrophic drought has plunged the prairies into despair . In southern Alberta , between Fort McLeod and Pincher Creek , Willa Van Bruggen oversees the family 's struggling goat farm and sinks her hopes into the promise of a new water pipeline . <p> Watershed , a new novel by Calgary author Doreen Vanderstoop.Calgary <p> Vanderstoop said inspiration from the novel came from various sources . Like the central family in Watershed , the author immigrated to Alberta from the Netherlands . While she grew up in Calgary , she maintained a strong interest in the surrounding rural areas of her adopted home and the impact weather had on the plight of the province 's farmers . From her childhood to the present day , Vanderstoop has also taken frequent trips into Alberta 's mountainous wilderness . <p> " There are certain facts that are irrefutable , one of them is that our glaciers are melting at record rates , " she says . " I 'm a big mountain person ; I love the mountains and we hike all the time . So that built into that concept , too : The notion that our reservoir in the mountains is depleting and at some point it 's not going to be there anymore . I 've probably condensed that time period in the novel in terms of when that 's going to happen . " <p> Whatever broad message the book might present in terms of the devastating impact of climate change , Watershed has also been lauded as a page-turner due to its fast pace and ability to tell a larger tale through an intimate one about the plight of an Alberta family . <p> The story also involves the spectre of " water terrorists " who threaten the pipeline with violence , and the malfeasance of corporate giants that control supply . Family relations are further complicated when Willa 's son Daniel begins work for the very company that is driving up the price of water and putting family farms such as the Van Bruggen 's out of business . Family secrets involving Willa 's father are also revealed . <p> " That tension created this dynamic in the family , of Daniel going off and making his own way in the world , " Vanderstoop says . " There 's a tension of what 's going on in the larger world and what people are trying to do on a micro level with their lives . The question is ' what do you do ? ' Do you become an environmental activist or do you try and do your best with the life you 're living in your own little space ? That was an interesting exploration as well . " <p> Born in the Netherlands , Vanderstoop came to Alberta with her family in 1968 . She has worked as a freelance writer and is also a musician and storyteller and current president of Storytelling Alberta , an organization that celebrates " the ancient tradition of oral storytelling " with performances at schools , libraries and festivals . <p> As a writer , she has published short fiction in Loft on Eight and Prairie Fire . Her 2014 story , The Clearing , ran in the latter publication in 2014 . It told the story of a girl in Banff in 1939 . With the 10-year journey to complete Watershed now finished , Vanderstoop says she has been thinking about that character and a sophomore novel . <p> " I wrote about her as a child and I would love to expand her story and turn that into a novel , " she says . " It would be interesting for me to now look back as opposed to having looked forward and dealt with climate change exploration and research and thinking about a future world . It would be really fun to do something different . " <p> Doreen Vanderstoop will hold a virtual launch of Watershed on Sunday , April 26 at 2 p.m . Visit doreenvanderstoop.ca.
