COULD put more than 60,000 Irish homes at heightened risk of coastal flooding by 2050 , according to a new survey of vulnerable coastal locations across the country . <p> A data and mapping analysis by Gamma Location Intelligence ( GLI ) has found that more than 70,000 addresses will be at risk of coastal flooding by the middle of the century , some 88% of which are residential . <p> Dublin , Louth and Clare are expected to be the most affected counties . <p> The analysis , published today , predicts that 21,513 residential addresses and 1,922 commercial properties in Dublin will be at risk . <p> Louth is expected to be second most affected county , with some 10,280 residential and 968 commercial properties predicted to be impacted , followed by Clare with 7,376 homes and 1,320 businesses at risk . <p> Limerick ( 5,426 properties ) and Galway ( 4,501 properties ) rounded out the top five areas expected to be most impacted by increased flood risk as a result of climate change . <p> The findings are based on a predicted global temperature increase of 2 degrees Celsius , which climate scientists expect to happen in the next 30 years in some scenarios . <p> Such an increase would cause sea levels to rise up to 1.75 metres and bring about more extreme weather events , leading to higher and more frequent coastal flooding . <p> The figures are relevant for coastal flooding only , and do not refer to other types of flooding brought on by heavy rainfall and high river levels in addition which could cause even more properties to be impacted . <p> The modelling also omits existing or planned sea wall defences . <p> #Open journalismNo news is bad newsSupport The Journal <p> Your contributions will help us continue to deliver the stories that are important to you <p>
