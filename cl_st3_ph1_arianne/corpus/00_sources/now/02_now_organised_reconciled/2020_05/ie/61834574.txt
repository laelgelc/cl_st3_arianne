<h> Dublin Zoo celebrates arrival of adorable Humboldt penguin chicks <p> Dublin Zoo is celebrating the arrival of two Humboldt penguin chicks , the first penguins to be born in Dublin since 2013 . <p> On March 29 , the first penguin chick hatched , the baby of parents Monica ( 7 ) and Joey ( 8 ) . <p> Less than week later , the second chick arrived on April 3 , born to parents Magdalena ( 18 ) and Patrick ( 16 ) . <p> Dublin Zoo is celebrating the arrival of two Humboldt penguin chicks , the first penguins to be born in Dublin since 2013 . Pic : Dublin Zoo <p> Magdalena and Patrick are longterm residents of Dublin Zoo , and first laid an egg together 13 years ago in 2007 . The veteran pair also happen to be the parents of new mum Monica , meaning that they became parents and grandparents in the same week . <p> Magdalena and Patrick are also the parents of Chandler and Phoebe ( there 's a running theme here ) , both of whom still live in Dublin Zoo . <p> The two newborns have been welcomed into the colony of 16 inhabitants of Dublin Zoo 's penguin habitat . <p> The two newborns have been welcomed into the colony of 16 inhabitants of Dublin Zoo 's penguin habitat . Pic : Dublin Zoo <p> Speaking about the new arrivals , Ciaran McMahon , team leader at Dublin Zoo , said : ' We are absolutely thrilled to welcome these Humboldt penguin chicks to Dublin Zoo . <p> ' They are important births for the species . Some of the parent names might seem strangely familiar to fans of certain show , but in the last number of years all new arrivals at Dublin Zoo have been given names from the country they are native to . ' <p> Mr McMahon added : ' While the Zoo is currently closed to the public , this goes to show that life goes on ! The penguin habitat is one of the most popular at the Zoo , and we look forwards to welcoming people back as soon as we can . Pic : Dublin Zoo <p> Although it may be a while before members of the public can visit the new penguin chicks in person , they can be seen at any time via Dublin Zoo 's live webcam . <p> It is estimated that some 32,000 Humboldt penguins remain in the wild off the coast of Chile and Peru , meaning that the species is classified as Vulnerable by the IUCN Red List . <p> The Humboldt penguin is under threat because they typically build their nests in Guano deposits , which are being harvested for fertiliser . In addition , climate change poses a threat to their continued existence due to alterations in currents and water temperature . <p> Although it may be a while before members of the public can visit the new penguin chicks in person , they can be seen at any time via Dublin Zoo 's live webcam . Pic : Rollingnews.ie <p> Furthermore , commercial fishing makes it difficult for Humboldt penguins to access their food supply , and the birds risk becoming entangled in fishing nets . They have also been captured by illegal traders for consumption and resale as pets . <p> For the last 12 years , Dublin Zoo has been providing Humboldt penguin conservation projects with annual financial support through Sphenisco . <p> Sphenisco works with Chilean and Peruvian scientists and activists , and the organisation is involved in research , monitoring and protection of breeding islands , campaigning for the creation of marine protected areas and environmental education .
