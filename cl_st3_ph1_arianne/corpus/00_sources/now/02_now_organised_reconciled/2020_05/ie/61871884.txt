<h> UCC study reveals impact of lockdown on air pollution and home energy usage <p> Mary Corcoran <p> POLLUTION from cars has dropped by 50 per cent in recent weeks while our electricity usage now replicate the weekend patterns every day . <p> That 's according to a new study by researchers at MaREI research centre in University College Cork ( UCC ) , which provides an insight into the impact of restrictions introduced to reduce the spread of COVID-19 on Ireland 's energy related pollution levels . <p> The study examined Ireland 's pollution levels prior to the introduction of the restrictions and compares it with those levels during lockdown . <p> It shows that pollution from cars has dropped by 50 per cent . <p> However , air pollution from solid fuel burning has not changed . <p> The researchers point out how nine out of 10 flights have been cancelled since restrictions commenced . <p> They say that assuming flight restrictions last for 12 weeks , and that there will be another 12 weeks with partial recovery , this could result in a reduction of 1 MtCO2 of heat trapping gases this year ; the equivalent of switching off Moneypoint coal fired power station for six months . <p> The demand for electricity has seen a decrease of between 5-10 per cent over seasonal expectations since early March as a result of the COVID-19 measures , however , the generation profile now replicates a weekend demand profile seven days a week , it said . <p> Dr James Glynn , one of the UCC based researchers involved in the project said that the COVID-19 lockdown measures across the globe are creating a " natural experiment for climate and energy researchers to observe how extreme demand scenarios can drive large scale fossil fuel demand destruction and collapse oil market prices " <p> He added : " By exploring the impact these restrictions are having on Ireland 's pollution levels , we are also seeking to understand feasible actions to mitigate climate change . " <p> Dr Glynn said : " We need to make long-term , equitable and sustainable changes to our lifestyles , to our economy and to our energy system . <p> " Climate change mitigation is a marathon event requiring rapid and sustained steady changes in how we consume energy , " he added .
