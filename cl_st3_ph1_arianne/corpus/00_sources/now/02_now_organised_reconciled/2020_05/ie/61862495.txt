<h> America takes the wind out of China 's renewable sails <p> President Barack Obama 's Clean Power Plan ( CPP ) has seen the US return to top spot in a league table of nations ' attractiveness to renewable energy investment . <p> David Sapsted <p> 16 September 2015 <p> Describing CPP as America 's " most comprehensive and far-reaching national energy legislation to date " , Ernst &amp; Young 's Renewable Energy Country Attractiveness Index ( RECAI ) said the plan had resulted in the US replacing China at the top of the latest rankings , while India had ousted Germany to claim third place . " While it 's unlikely to be plain sailing for the CPP , " said the report , " the plan 's unambiguous state-level emission reduction targets create a strong remit , and opportunity , to significantly increase the deployment of renewable energy capacity across the country over the next 15 years . " Ben Warren , chief editor of the report , added , " It is difficult not to get swept up in the excitement as the world 's media churn over the facts and figures of landmark initiatives such as the US Clean Power Plan and the various pledges being made ahead of December 's United Nations climate change conference in Paris . " It 's not only policymakers who are riding the climate change wave . Executives from 13 major US corporations have pledged to invest at least $140 billion to decrease their carbon footprint , while an encyclical by Pope Francis in June , urging the world 's 1.2 billion Catholics to help cut fossil fuel emissions , also helps to depoliticise the debate . " The report said that , while China continued to dwarf the US in terms of investment in new renewables , " the current economic slowdown , limited evidence of foreign participation in the market despite government efforts to open it up , and grid constraints contribute to its slip to second place " . Elsewhere in Asia , the report said that India continued to dominate the headlines with multi-gigawatt projects and multi-billion dollar investments . It added , " While various challenges are making many sceptical that India will achieve 100GW of solar and 60GW of wind by 2022 , there is little doubt that its ambitious targets have galvanised the market and prompted economic and political reforms that are creating the foundations of an extremely attractive , long-term market . " The drive in India has resulted in Germany slipping to fourth place . The report said that capacity deployment had fallen in Germany while a new auction regime for projects had raised fears of smaller developers being squeezed out . " Another European market slipping down the index this is the UK , " said EY . " Plummeting three places and falling out of the top 10 for the first time in its RECAI history , a raft of policy measures in recent months threaten to paralyse the historically attractive UK renewables market . " The report said that UK policy changes included ending or curbing support for onshore wind , for smaller scale solar farms and for domestic and community-scale renewables . EY said the moves seemed to suggest the government had " sentenced the UK renewables sector to death by a thousand cuts " , pointing out that 23 large-scale projects , representing around 2.7GW of energy capacity , had been abandoned.A Department of Energy and Climate Change spokesman in London accepted that the government had taken action to control renewables spending in a bid to keep consumer and business bills as low as possible . But he added , " The UK still remains an attractive location for investment in energy and we have continued to be the global leader in offshore wind investment . " Subsidies funded via bill-payers have contributed to renewables making a record contribution to our electricity generation in the first quarter of this year and have nearly trebled our renewable electricity capacity in the last parliament alone . " EY said the UK 's fall in the index had made way " for Latin America 's hottest markets to cement their position in the top 10 . Despite challenging economic conditions in Brazil , government proactivity in addressing key challenges such as low tariffs , and an increasing focus on its untapped solar market takes it up to eighth place . " Meanwhile , the success of renewables in Chile 's technology-neutral energy auctions and a continuing flow of large-scale project approvals justify its climb to ninth place . " The report said other notable index movements included Mexico 's rise to 19th place in the wake of a transition to a wholesale electricity market and tradeable renewable energy certificate regime that is attracting significant international investment ; while Turkey 's long-term investment and deployment potential has seen it jump to 15th place.Falling down the table , though , were Australia and Spain , now in 13th and 25th place respectively . " Australia 's government appears to have launched an all-out attack on the country 's wind sector while apparently softening toward utility-scale solar , " said the report . " Meanwhile , Spain also appears to be targeting its austerity measures , with plans to tax residential solar systems that specifically apply battery storage , while also preventing such systems receiving payment for selling excess power back into the grid .
