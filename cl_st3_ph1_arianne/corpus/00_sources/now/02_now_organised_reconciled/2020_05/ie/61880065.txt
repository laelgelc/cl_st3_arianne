<h> HSBC , IFC raise $320m extra for EM real economy green bond fund <p> HSBC Global Asset Management and the International Finance Corporation ( IFC ) have raised $324m ( $291m ) from European occupational pension funds and insurance companies for a bond fund aimed at supporting climate risk-mitigation investments in emerging markets . <p> HSBC Group and IFC have each committed $75m to the fund -- the HSBC Real Economy Green Investment Opportunity GEB Bond Fund , dubbed REGIO -- and today said that seven private investors had joined in for the latest close of the fund , taking the total so far to $474m , with others expected to commit later this year . <p> An HSBC spokesman said the private investors were a mix of European occupational pension funds and insurance companies . <p> The fund will target green and sustainable bonds from so-called real economy issuers in emerging markets with a view to increasing access to climate finance and promoting the development of sustainability-oriented capital markets by broadening the range of issuers . <p> This is to be achieved by the combination of the fund 's investment activities , supporting guidelines and eligibility criteria , and technical assistance provided by the IFC to bolster the supply of green bonds from real economy borrowers . <p> REGIO has some similarities with an emerging market green bond fund launched by Amundi and IFC in 2017 , but theirs is aimed at green bonds issued by financial institutions rather than companies operating in sectors such as manufacturing , agriculture , or services . <p> HSBC and IFC say their fund is the first green bond fund focused on the emerging market real economy . <p> " To achieve a close of this size in the current market environment proves the importance that institutional investors place on impact investing in emerging markets " <p> Nicolas Moreau , global CEO , HSBC Global Asset Management <p> Nicolas Moreau , global CEO , HSBC Global Asset Management , said : " We are at a tipping point in terms of climate change and investing in the real economy in emerging markets is critical to achieving the global transition to a lower carbon economy . <p> " To achieve a close of this size in the current market environment proves the importance that institutional investors place on impact investing in emerging markets . <p> " We hope that the green impact investment framework behind REGIO and its commitment to sustainable development is something that will be taken up by the wider industry . " <p> The spokesman told IPE the most recent commitment to the fund was made on 6 May . <p> Philippe Le Hou ? rou , CEO of IFC , said : " The success of this fundraising is proof that investors remain committed to fighting climate change , even at this time of global pandemic . <p> " Innovative solutions like this fund create tangible action on the ground at a time of great urgency . " <p> Asked about there being any signs investors were put off committing to the fund by concerns about the impact of the coronavirus on emerging market countries , the HSBC spokesman said : " This close is a very positive statement about the willingness of sustainably-minded , long-term investors , to commit to investing in emerging markets , despite the near term volatility . " <p> Financials drive EM green bond issuance <p> According to a recent report from Amundi and the IFC , emerging market green bond issuance increased by 21% in 2019 to $52bn , bringing the overall size of the market to $168bn . <p> Although emerging market bond growth continued to be led by China , other emerging markets drove the overall growth in 2019 with $18bn of issuance , nearly triple that in 2018 , according to Amundi and the IFC . <p> Excluding China , the leading issuers were India , Chile , Poland , the Philippines , the United Arab Emirates , and Brazil , they said . <p> Financial institutions remain the largest issuing sector in emerging markets , driving 59% of issues compared with 19% in developed markets , followed by non-financial corporates at 35% , sovereigns at 12% , government agencies at 5% ,
