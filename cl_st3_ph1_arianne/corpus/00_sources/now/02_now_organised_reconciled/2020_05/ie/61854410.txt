<h> Kildare Wildlife Watch : The tortoiseshell butterfly is a native species in sad decline <h> With Nuala Madigan of the Irish Peatland Conservation Council <p> Reporter : <p> Nuala Madigan of the Irish Peatland Conservation Council <p> 9 May 2020 <p> Email : <p> bogs@ipcc.ie <p> 0 <p> The small tortoiseshell butterfly . Picture : Nuala Madigan <p> As I work away from home Monday to Friday , one positive outcome that the stay at home element of Covid-19 has offered is the opportunity to watch the variety of wildlife that is visiting my garden during the day . <p> As an environmental education officer , I practice what I suggest others do in their gardens . I rainwater harvest , compost garden waste , and allow wildlfowers bloom -- even the dandelions ! I choose to plant pollinator-friendly plants such as lavender and I do n't use any chemicals . So far I have witnessed wrens , robins , blackbirds , starlings , bumblebees , hoverflies and butterflies , including the holly blue and small tortoiseshell . <p> April is the start of the butterfly recording season and this week I have decided to focus on the small tortoiseshell butterfly ( ru ? n beag as Gaeilge ) . This is a very common butterfly at this time of year and can be seen in flight from April through to September . It is easily identifed with its bright orange wings , each wing having black and white markings around their border . <p> This is a native butterfly and the adults can live more than one year by hibernating in dark sheltered locations over the winter . The female will lay eggs on the leaves of the nettle plant , and each year they can have two cycles . <p> Although I attempt to be environmentally friendly in my garden , I will be honest -- I do n't have a lush crop of nettles so the adults I witnessed were feeding on the nectar of the wildflowers in the garden rather than breeding . <p> The caterpillars are black and yellow and feed both day and night . The emerging caterpillars live in a communal web before dispersing and then pupating . Although butterfly populations fluctuate each year and this fluctuation is often weather dependent , small tortoiseshell numbers have recorded a decline in recent years . The reason for this decline is not fully understood at this time but could potentially be related to climate change .
