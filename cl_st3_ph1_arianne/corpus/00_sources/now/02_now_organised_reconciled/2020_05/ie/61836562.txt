<h> Why questions of food waste , climate mitigation , and agricultural production will become more urgent <p> Residents line up during a distribution to collect soap , vegetables , fruits and other staples distributed by volunteers in Clichy-sous-Bois , a suburb north of Paris . Picture : AP Photo/Francois Mori , File <p> A world that wants to reduce agricultural production in order to control climate change should first look at how much food it wastes . <p> The Food and Agriculture Organisation of the United Nations ( FAO ) estimated that in 2005 , one third of all food available for human consumption was wasted ( food that was fit for human consumption , but was not eaten ) . <p> In industrialised countries , food gets lost when production exceeds demand . Surplus crops are sold as animal feed . <p> Or unattractive foods are " discarded " . For example , carrots that are not bright orange , that have a blemish , or are broken , go for livestock feed . <p> Fresh products can be spoilt in hot climates due to lack of transportation , storage , cooling , and markets . <p> Failure to comply with minimum food safety standards can lead to food losses ( due to naturally occurring toxins , contaminated water , unsafe use of pesticides , or veterinary drug residues ) . <p> Poor and unhygienic handling and storage conditions , and lack of adequate temperature control , can also cause unsafe food <p> Poor farmers may harvest crops too early due to food deficiency or the need for cash . In this way , there is a loss of nutritional and economic food value . <p> Trimmings to make food more attractive are usually disposed of . <p> Errors during processing or packaging often lead to food being discarded . <h> READ MORE <p> Food on retail display may reach its " sell-by " date before being sold , and is wasted . <p> There are food losses in wholesale and retail markets in developing countries , which are often small , overcrowded , unsanitary and lacking cooling equipment . <p> Poverty and limited household income make it unacceptable to waste food in developing countries . <p> But one of the most important areas for food waste is at the consumption level in rich countries . <p> For example , restaurants serve buffets at fixed prices , which encourages people to fill their plates with more food than they can actually eat . Retail stores offer " one for free " bargains . Both lead to waste . <p> FAO calculations indicated that consumers are responsible for only 8% of food waste , but it could be as much as 19% , say Monika van den Bos Verma and colleagues from Wageningen University and Research , The Netherlands . <p> They found that once consumer affluence reaches a spending threshold of approximately ? 6 per person per day , consumer food waste starts to arise . <p> Measures to reduce food waste are likely to become as important as measures to control climate change , and they will need to be introduced in tandem . <p> One of the reasons why food waste will become more important than ever is that plant-based foods favoured in climate mitigation plans are more susceptible to waste . <p> The FAO estimated food loss and waste in the Near East and North Africa region at only 13% of meats , 14-19% of grains , 16% of oilseeds and pulses , 18% of dairy products , 26% of roots and tubers , 28% of fish and seafood , and 45% of fruits and vegetables . <p> Questions of food waste , climate mitigation , and agricultural production will become more urgent very quickly , if Covid-19 is as catastrophic as feared for millions who are already hanging by a thread , people who can only eat if they earn a wage , and whose savings have been decimated by lockdowns and global economic recession . <p> Covid-19 could push them over the edge . <p> The World Food Programme says the number of people facing acute food insecurity stands to rise to 265 million this year , from 135m in 2019 , due to Covid-19 . <p> The United Nations World Food Programme is the world 's largest humanitarian organisation , and its Executive Director David Beasley , UN World Food Programme ( WFP ) recently told the UN Security Council we are on the brink of a hunger pandemic , to add to the Covid-19 pandemic . <p> Before Covid-19 became an issue , he had warned 2020 would bring the worst humanitarian crisis since World War II . <p> It is caused by wars in Syria and Yemen , deepening crises in places like South Sudan , Burkina Faso and the Central Sahel . <p> Desert locust swarms in Africa ; natural disasters ; changing weather patterns ; economic crisis in Lebanon affecting millions of Syrian refugees ; hunger in the Democratic Republic of the Congo , Sudan , and Ethiopia ; add up to a perfect storm , said Beasley . <p> Famine was a very real and dangerous possibility for millions in conflict-scarred nations . <p> The current WFP numbers are that 821m go to bed hungry every night , and a further 135m face crisis levels of hunger or worse . Covid-19 could push an additional 130m to the brink of starvation . <p> Beasley urged the UN Security Council Council to help by seeking a global ceasefire in conflicts , to allow food be moved from from where it is produced to where it is needed . <p> " We could be facing multiple famines of biblical proportions within a short few months , " he warned . <p> Climate change is of course one of the causes of this global predicament . <p> It is a cause of world hunger , and it makes solving it harder . <p> The unusual proliferation of droughts worldwide at the moment illustrates the effect of climate change . <p> Some parts of New Zealand , such as Hawke 's Bay , have entered their seventh month of below-normal rainfall , which has reduced by 3% ( compared to normal April levels ) , the milk production in the world 's number one dairy exporting country . <p> Nearer home , much of central Europe has been hit by drought , damaging output of high-productivity agricultural areas . <p> Leading grain exporter Ukraine depends on suitable weather for the rest of the season to save its crops , with yield forecasts already officially reduced by 20% . <p> Moldova faces major crop losses . <p> Polish food prices will increase this year by 5.2% , mostly due to drought . <p> The Czech Republic is enduring its worst drought in 500 years . <p> Drought has hit Italy 's South , one of its main supply areas for pasta wheat , and farmers there decided not to transplant tomatoes . <p> Vietnam and Cuba are both hit by drought , and the lack of rain is the worst in 50 years in northeast Argentina .
