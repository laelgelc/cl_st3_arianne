<h> US President Donald Trump hints at the possibility of Brazil joining the military alliance between American and European countries as he hosted the far-right President Jair Bolsonaro for security talks at the White House . <p> Brazil 's President Jair Bolsonaro shakes hands with U.S. President Donald Trump at the conclusion of a joint news conference in the Rose Garden of the White House in Washington , US on March 19 , 2019 . ( Reuters ) <p> President Donald Trump heaped praise on right-wing Brazilian President Jair Bolsonaro -- and received plenty back -- in announcing a special relationship that he said could even see the Latin American country join NATO . <p> At a joint news conference on the sun-soaked Rose Garden lawn on Tuesday , Trump and the man dubbed " Trump of the Tropics " let the mutual compliments flow . <p> " I have always admired the United States of America . And this sense of admiration has just increased after you took office and the presidency , " Bolsonaro said . <p> Trump , stressing Brazil 's partnership in the US-led campaign to force hard-left Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro from power , said Bolsonaro was " doing a fantastic job " and had " brought the country together . " <p> For years Bolsonaro was little more than a marginal congressman , but his Trump-like campaign in 2018 , promising to combat corruption and crime , and to end politics as usual , propelled him to surprise victory . <p> His hard-right views , including frequent expressions of support for Brazil 's past period of military rule , horrified the left and drove deep divisions in Latin America 's biggest nation . <p> But at the press conference , his condemnation of what he called " fake news " -- opponents say he is referring merely to critical reports -- and references to deeply conservative social values pleased Trump . <p> " We have many views that are similar , " Trump said . <p> Brazil in NATO ? <p> Trump spent much of the news conference discussing efforts to squeeze Venezuela 's authorities , which he warned could face many more sanctions than those already imposed in an attempt to cripple the government 's finances . <p> Brazil , which shares a long border with Venezuela , is important in the strategy . <p> In Bolsonaro , an ex-soldier who idolises the former leaders of Brazil 's anti-communist military dictatorship , Trump has a ready ally against Maduro . <p> In return , Bolsonaro got one of the main items on his wish list : agreement for Brazil to be given NATO privileges . This " major non-NATO ally " status would ease Brazil 's access to US weaponry and other military links . <p> But in the sunny spirit of the occasion , Trump then went much further , stretching the diplomatic and geographical possibilities to a degree that surprised many . <p> " I also intend to designate Brazil as a major non-NATO ally or even possibly , if you start thinking about it , maybe a NATO ally , " Trump announced . <p> The North Atlantic Treaty Organisation already has 29 countries . None are from Latin America and none , like Brazil , are located in the South Atlantic . <p> " I have to talk to a lot of people , " Trump said of his idea . <p> Longterm relationship <p> Washington has had strained relations with Brazil 's long string of leftist governments but the rise of Bolsonaro , who talks enthusiastically of combating socialists and communists , has opened a new door f or the Trump administration . <p> A Trump-Bolsonaro bonding matters for Venezuela and for Washington 's push back against growing Chinese economic influence in South America . <p> It could also have large-scale implications for the future of efforts to slow or reverse global warming : Brazil is home to much of the threatened Amazon rainforest and Bolsonaro , like Trump , is a climate change skeptic . <p> Trump has withdrawn the US from the Paris Climate Agreement , an international deal on cutting carbon emissions . Bolsonaro is toying with following suit . <p> On Monday , Bolsonaro signed an agreement with US companies on technical safeguards to allow commercial satellite launches from Brazil 's Alcantara base . <p> But there are still major sticking points between the two countries , especially on trade , with Trump wanting Brazil to open up to more US products . <p> Whether what a White House official called a new north-south " axis " prospers may depend mostly on the personal relationship between its leaders . <p> Asked if he would manage to have equally close ties if a Democrat prevented Republican Trump 's reelection in 2020 , Bolsonaro said : " I do believe Donald Trump will be reelected fully . " <p> Trump interrupted him -- just to show yet again that they were on the same wavelength .
