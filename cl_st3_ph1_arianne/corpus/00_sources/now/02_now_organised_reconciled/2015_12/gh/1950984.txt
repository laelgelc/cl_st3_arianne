<p> The Ghana Cocoa Board has been promising a great harvest for the 2015/2016 crop season . <p> This was in defence against assertions that the country 's cocoa yield will drop given a poor level of investment in the sector , coupled with the then low purchasing prices given to the local farmers . <p> COCOBOD would later increase the producer price of cocoa to the tune of 21.74% for the 2015/2016 crop season effective October 2 , 2015 . <p> This saw an upward adjustment in cocoa prices from 350 cedis to 420 cedis on a 64 kilogram bag of cocoa and 6,720 cedis from the previous 5,520 cedis representing an increase of 1200 cedis according to the Producer Price Review Committee ( PPRC ) . <p> Acting project coordinator of the Peasant Farmers Association of Ghana , Charles Nyaba , the actual situation on the ground does not support COCOBOD 's assertion . <p> " Climate change is setting in , rainfall patterns have changed . The weather patterns have also changed . All these affect the kind of yield that we get . So @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ be able to make a committed investment to mitigate against these kinds of risk . But if you predict all these risks and yet you sit there then I will not be surprised that the crop yield will be one of the poorest we have gotten in a long time . " Mr. Nyaba says . <p> Mr. Nyaba added that the situation will discourage farmers from selling to government or even growing the crop at all . <p> " It is our plea that government does something about this in order to avert a downward trend in the sector .
