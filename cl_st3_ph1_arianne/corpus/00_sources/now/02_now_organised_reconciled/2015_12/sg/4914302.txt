<p> OSLO -- Two new consignments of crop seeds will be deposited next year in the " doomsday vault " built in an Arctic mountainside to safeguard global supplies <p> The vault -- which opened on the Svalbard archipelago between Norway and the North pole in 2008 -- is designed to protect crop seeds such as beans , rice and wheat against the worst cataclysms of nuclear war or disease . <p> It already has more than 860,000 samples , from almost all nations . Even if the power were to fail , the vault would stay frozen and sealed for at least 200 years . <p> " The seed vault is the back-up of the back-up , " said Ms Cierra Martin , a spokeswoman for the Crop Trust , the Bonn-based organisation which manages the Svalbard Global Seed Vault . <p> She said two deposits were planned for next year , but that the details of the deposits were as yet unclear .
