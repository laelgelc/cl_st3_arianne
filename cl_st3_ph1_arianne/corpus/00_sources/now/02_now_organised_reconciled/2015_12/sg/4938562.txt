<h> India close to deal on nuclear reactors <h> Westinghouse contract to build six reactors will give nuclear power programme a boost <p> NEW DELHI * India expects to seal a contract with Westinghouse Electric Company to build six nuclear reactors in the first half of next year , a senior government official said , in a sign its US$150 billion ( S$211 billion ) nuclear power programme is getting off the ground . <p> The proposed power plant in Prime Minister Narendra Modi 's home state of Gujarat will accelerate India 's plans to build roughly 60 reactors , which would make it the world 's second-biggest nuclear energy market after China . <p> The Modi government wants to dramatically raise nuclear capacity to 63,000MW by 2032 , from 5,780MW , as part of a broader push to move away from fossil fuels , cut greenhouse gas emissions and avoid climate change effects . <p> The United States signed a pact with India in 2008 , opening the way for nuclear commerce that had previously been stymied due to New Delhi 's nuclear weapons programme and shunning @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ reactor makers would get billions of dollars of new business faded after India adopted a law in 2010 giving the state-run operator Nuclear Power Corp of India the right to seek damages from suppliers in the event of an accident . <p> Indian officials have been trying to assuage suppliers ' concerns , including by setting up an insurance pool with a liability cap of 15 billion Indian rupees ( S$320 million ) . <p> A final hurdle - ratification of the International Atomic Energy Agency 's Convention on Supplementary Compensation for Nuclear Damage ( CSC ) - is expected within weeks , the Indian government official said . <p> The CSC requires signatories to shift liability to the operator and offers access to relief funds . <p> In a statement , Westinghouse said it expected India would move towards a framework that satisfies the CSC and channels accident liability exclusively to the operator .
