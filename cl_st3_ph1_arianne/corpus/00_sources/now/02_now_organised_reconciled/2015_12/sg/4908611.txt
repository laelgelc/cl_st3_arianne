<h> Paris has created a gigantic carbon ' take-back scheme ' <p> I wonder how many of the delegates in Paris realise that they have just created the mother of all " take-back schemes " . <p> As a consumer , you may have already come across this sort of deal : if you do n't want to dispose of the packaging of your new sofa , you can take it back to Ikea and it 's their problem . In many places , you can even take back the sofa itself when your kids have wrecked it . <p> For the Paris climate deal to succeed , something similar will have to happen , where companies that rely on fossil fuels will be obliged to " take back " their emissions . <p> The agreement reaffirms a commitment to stabilising temperature rises well below 2 deg C , and even retains the option of limiting warming to 1.5 deg C , if possible . But it also confirms national targets that do little more than stabilise global emissions between now and 2030 . <p> Protesters with a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a message to investment funds to move their money out of fossil fuels at the World Climate Change Conference 2015 in Le Bourget , near Paris , on Dec 2 . PHOTO : REUTERS <p> Given those emissions , sticking to within 2 deg will require us to take lots of carbon out of the atmosphere and store it in the ground . The parties to the agreement are , in effect , saying " we 're going to sell this stuff , and we 're going to dispose of it later " . <p> To stabilise temperatures at any level , be it 1.5 , 2 or even 3 deg , net carbon dioxide emissions must be reduced to zero . Most governments , environmental groups and business leaders now understand this . And it is acknowledged , albeit implicitly , in Article 4 of the Paris agreement , which calls for greenhouse emissions to be " balanced " by carbon sinks some time after the mid-century . <p> But we 're unlikely to hit " net zero " emissions before temperatures reach 2 deg , and even less likely @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ about 1 deg and rising by 0.1 deg every five to 10 years . We could slow the warming by reducing emissions , of course . But if we fail to reduce at the required rate - and the inadequate emissions targets indicate this is the intention - then we will be left with no option but to scrub the excess carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) back out of the atmosphere in future . <p> OWNERS OF FOSSIL FUEL ASSETS <p> That is why the deal is like a gigantic take-back scheme . The proof lies in what is not said in the Paris agreement . There is no explicit mention of a global carbon budget , for instance , which tallies total emissions since the industrial revolution . That is despite the fact that all governments have acknowledged , through the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change , the reality that stabilising temperatures requires a limit on cumulative CO2 emissions . Certain countries simply can not accept the suggestion that they may be obliged to leave some of their prized fossil carbon reserves underground . <p> And why should they ? @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ right , to ban India from using its coal . We simply need to ensure that , by the time global temperatures reach 2 deg ( or 1.5 deg if that is what is eventually deemed safe ) , any company that sells fossil fuels , or any carbon-intensive product such as conventional cement , is obliged to take back an equivalent amount of CO2 and dispose of it safely to ensure it does n't end up in the atmosphere . <p> Right now , that means reinjection underground : forests ca n't be relied on over geological timescales ( they might burn down , or even die out and re-release their carbon due to climate change itself ) . But there are plenty of other creative ideas for carbon dioxide disposal : someone just needs the incentive to do it . <p> And who better than the owners of the fossil fuel assets at the heart of the problem ? Logically , the cost of CO2 disposal should be borne by the seller of fossil carbon . If it is paid for out of general taxation , no one will @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ products they sell or buy , nor will companies have an incentive to minimise the cost of disposal . And relying on taxpayers to pay for disposal makes them vulnerable every time purse strings are tightened . <p> The idea of a " CO2 take-back " scheme was suggested by Mr Nick Robins , a United Nations sustainability adviser , at a recent event in Paris . It may have been meant as a whimsical aside , but it really is the only feasible way of stabilising the climate . The alternative - a global ban on fossil fuel extraction and use - is neither ethical nor enforceable . <p> FANTASY SCENARIOS <p> Enthusiasts for renewable energy would like us to believe they can make it cheaper than coal , so a global ban would be unnecessary . But there will still be cement , jet fuel , fertiliser - the list is endless . The idea that we will develop a cheaper substitute for every single application of fossil carbon , everywhere in the world , before temperatures reach 2 deg , is pure fantasy . As Professor Ottmar Edenhofer @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ put it : " As a Catholic , I believe in miracles , but I do not rely on them . " <p> Of course , if we include the costs of take-back , then high-carbon products will become more expensive , which is all good for renewables . But , unlike new taxes , take-back schemes are generally popular despite industry 's dire warnings about increased costs . <p> People understand that the main beneficiary of fancy packaging is the company selling the product . And , even at today 's prices , the main beneficiaries of our use of fossil fuels is not the consumer , nor even the firm with its logo on the pump , but those who hoover up the royalties , taxes and rents as fossil fuels come out of the ground . <p> Earlier this year , I suggested that something like a CO2 take-back scheme ( although not with nearly such a catchy name ) should be considered in Britain 's energy Bill , and was promptly taken out for a coffee by a well-spoken industry lobbyist who told me what a bad @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ suggested that I was onto something . <p> " Mandatory sequestration " has n't really caught on in the environmental movement partly , I 'm sure , because it is a bit of a mouthful for any campaigner . But stack up the net zero emissions point against the inadequate national targets , and you soon realise that all those shouting " 1.5 to stay alive " in Paris ( and there were plenty ) were actually advocating a crash programme of CO2 disposal . <p> #takebackCO2 - start tweeting it now . <p> * The writer is Professor of Geosystem Science and Leader of the ECI Climate Research Programme at the University of Oxford , United Kingdom . <p> * This article first appeared in theconversation.com , a website of analysis from academics and researchers . <p> A version of this article appeared in the print edition of The Straits Times on December 14 , 2015 , with the headline ' Paris has created a gigantic carbon ' take-back scheme ' ' .
