<h> China Promises Bamboo Boost For Africa <p> AsianScientist ( Dec. 28 , 2015 ) - by Keya Archarya - China aims to increase Africa 's expertise in novel bamboo products through a new knowledge exchange network announced at the COP 21 summit in Paris . <p> The country plans to team up with African states to start a partnership that would see knowledge about bamboo growth and products , such as bamboo-based biofuels and charcoal briquettes , transferred to other bamboo-growing nations . <p> The partnership , which was launched at an event on 9 December in Paris , will be overseen by the International Network for for Bamboo and Rattan ( INBAR ) , a China-based intergovernmental organization that seeks to use bamboo and rattan to reduce poverty and environmental damage . <p> Part of a 20 billion renminbi ( US$3.1 billion ) fund that China launched in September to increase South-South cooperation on climate change will be spent on the initiative . <p> Bamboo 's quick growth and easy care make it ideal for removing carbon from the atmosphere , and being a raw material @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ said . <p> " Bamboo should now become a South-South-North dynamic for climate change initiatives using China 's expertise in managing this sector , " said Hans Friederich , INBAR 's director-general . <p> The partnership also plans to include bamboo-growing countries from Asia and Latin America at a later stage , the event heard . <p> China 's innovations in bamboo growth and products have seen the sector grow into a US$30 billion industry , employing seven million people and reforesting over three million hectares of degraded land , the event heard . The country has developed technologies such as charcoal briquettes and construction materials made of bamboo . <p> In Africa , Ghana , Rwanda and South Africa have seen a boom in commercial bamboo growing , the event heard . <p> But some African representatives at the meeting were guarded about the prospect of Chinese interventions in local business . In Ghana , where bamboo charcoal briquettes are now used , there have been problems with some Chinese firms , said Yaw Kwakye , who runs the climate change unit at Ghana 's Forestry Commission . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ unregulated harvesting of rosewood in the northern zones with Chinese companies , " he told the event . <p> Other researchers , however , said the initiative could allow African countries to improve their plant science and better understand how to make the best use of their natural resources . Maria Vorontsova , a plant taxonomist at Kew Gardens in the United Kingdom , is working with INBAR to research bamboo species . <p> " There are bamboo species in Madagascar that I do n't even know what scientific name to give , " said Vorontsova . <p> She said African countries should create inventories of their plant diversity to better conserve them in the face of climate change . <p> -- -- -- <p> Source : SciDevNet ; Photo : Joi Ito/Flickr/CC .
