<h> NGOs Point to Mounting Dropout Problem <p> Khmer Times/Srey Kumneth and Pav Suy <p> Sunday , 20 December 2015 <p> 254 views <p> NGOs on Saturday aired their concerns about the possibility of a high school dropout rate , particularly in rural areas . The organizations said that government policies , lack of school facilities and unfavorable school environments are all factors leading young people to drop out . <p> The NGOs claimed that climate change was a factor compounding the problem . <p> At the " Youth Participation for the Improvement of Education Services " forum , the organizations said that as the weather heats up in Cambodia , more than 3 million students study in conditions without fans or even water supplies for toilets . <p> " This year , we can see that Cambodia is feeling the influence of climate change , " said San Chey , coordinator for the education monitor Affiliated Network for Social Accountability in East Asia and the Pacific ( ANSA-EAP ) . <p> According to Mr. Chey , the weather has become so hot between March and July that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ sweaty commutes , a lack of water to drink and classrooms that lack fans and toilets . <p> " They do not have the appetite to study , " he said . <p> On November 30 , the National Assembly passed the 2016 government budget , which involved a significant increase in the education budget to $502 million , up 28 percent from 2015 , when the budget was $453 million . The aim of the budget increase was to improve teaching quality and school facilities . <p> But Mr. Chey noted that so far the Education Ministry has allocated only a relatively small amount of the budget to local community schools under a formula in which schools with small numbers of students receive an accordingly small budget increase . <p> " Schools that are poor will remain poor according to this formula , " said Mr. Chey . <p> Koul Panha , executive director of election watchdog Comfrel , said that the government needed to focus on decentralization in order to lower the dropout rate . <p> " We need to get people involved in decision making to develop schools @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ work closely with teachers to persuade their students to study , " he said . <p> But Mr. Panha also agreed that the atmosphere at schools was a contributing factor to the high dropout rate . He added that major social issues such as poverty and corruption inside the school and education system itself were even more problematic issues . <p> " We have made the requests to the government , but they refused to listen to us , " he said .
