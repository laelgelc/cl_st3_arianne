<h> The new coalition on climate change <p> At the recent Paris conference on climate change , Microsoft founder Bill Gates took the opportunity to press French President Francois Hollande to include the need for research and development in the agenda . For years , Mr. Gates has been prodding governments to increase spending on research and development of new technologies . He has already put $1 billion of his own money into start-ups working on new kinds of batteries and nuclear reactors . His foreign aid operations and status as a technology icon have turned him into an influential global diplomat . <p> At his urging , Mr. Hollande and President Obama persuaded the Indian Prime Minister , Narendra Modi , to join their global climate goals . Mr. Hollande and Mr. Obama agreed to work with Mr. Gates to assemble a coalition of governments to double their spending on research and development and Mr. Gates volunteered to organize a group of billionaires to fund clean-energy start-ups . The group plans to finance companies that turn clean energy concepts into viable products . <p> Mr. Gates said that the coalition was focused on early-stage investments , but any breakthrough start-ups would be able to raise more capital to finance their technologies . <p> The idea for the partnership came about last January when President Obama met with Mr. Modi in new Delhi , and Mr. Modi told him that he needed new electricity to help raise India out of poverty , but coal was the cheapest source . He said India would use clean energy if there were technological breakthroughs that made it inexpensive . <p> Both Mr. Obama and Mr. Hollande said that Mr. Gates was helpful in convincing Mr. Modi to sign the climate accord in Paris .
