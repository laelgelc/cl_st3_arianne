<h> Peru Faces Tensions Over Water <p> Peru will face a " new normal " as greater agricultural and energy demands , population growth and climate change chip away at what is left of its glaciers , according to a recent article in the Yale Journal of International Affairs . Glacial retreat could ultimately lead to conflict in the country , the author found . <p> " Peru offers an early view of the challenges mountainous regions worldwide may face in coming decades , " wrote Peter Oesterling , the author . " The country -- if successful -- may also provide the world a model for effective policies to mitigate threats to environmental and human security . " <p> For people in Peru , glaciers are the essence of their existence . Most people live on the west coast , an arid region , and rely on glacier meltwater for day to day use , crops , hydroelectric power and mining . But since the early 1980 's , Peru 's glaciers have shrunk by more than 22 percent . The population is projected to grow by 35 million by 2020 , which will put pressure on the country 's existing land and water resources . Millions of households rely on the Ca ? on del Pato hydropower plant on the Rio Santa , but as water availability declines , the plant could lose 40 percent of its power generating capability . <p> The country 's mining industry also consumes a great deal of water . Eleven percent of Peru 's land is being mined for minerals . In addition to using water for mineral extraction , mining releases contaminated water back into the watershed . <p> " Peru 's trends in water use and supply are incompatible , " wrote Oesterling .
