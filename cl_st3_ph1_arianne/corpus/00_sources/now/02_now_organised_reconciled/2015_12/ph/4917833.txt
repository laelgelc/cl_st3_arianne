<h> The COP21 Global Climate Treaty <p> Today no one any longer questions the established science that greenhouse gases from burning fossil fuels , such as coal , are warming the planet . Or that both developed and developing countries must all lower their greenhouse gas emissions to ensure the world 's safety and economic stability . In Paris , ten days ago , 184 governments submitted their plans for reducing their domestic emissions after 2020 . These pledges will make up the core of a new agreement which is expected to require countries to meet at least once every ten years with even stronger emission reduction pledges . <p> The United States is pushing for legally binding provisions that would require governments to monitor , verify and report their emissions reductions to an international body . But a number of developing nations have called this a violation of national sovereignty . " Transparency is an enormously important part of this agreement , " said the American climate change negotiator , Todd Stern . " 184 countries have put forth targets . " <p> Mr. Stern said that the United States would like to see the creation of an international body of experts who would monitor and review how countries are following through on their emissions reduction pledges . Another way that some progress could be monitored would be by the use of satellites to monitor tree coverage in countries such as Brazil and Indonesia , that have pledged to reduce mass deforestation which is a major source of greenhouse gas emissions . <p> Thus far , international negotiators are optimistic because of the fact that , with the submission of individual climate pledges , negotiators are already further along than they have ever been in the 20-year process to form a climate pact .
