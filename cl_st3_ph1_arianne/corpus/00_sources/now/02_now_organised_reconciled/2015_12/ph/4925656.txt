<h> Flooding in the midst of drought <p> It does seem a bit difficult to grasp how our country can be threatened by both floods and drought at the same time . <p> We have just been visited by typhoon " Nona " which dumped heavy rains , blew down houses , trees , and electric posts , triggered landslides , and forced the evacuation of hundreds of thousands in the three days it took to cross the Visayas . But even as we took shelter from the rains , the United Nations weather agency warned that the ongoing El Nino phenomenon , which is causing fields in many countries to dry up and crops to fail , is the worst in over 15 years . <p> Typhoons are annual phenomena in our country . We are in the way of these storms that regularly develop in the Pacific and then move west-northwest towards the Philippines , then veer either to the north towards Japan or barrel west through our islands to the Asian mainland . <p> Around 20 of these powerful storms hit our islands every year @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ they hit our islands , they cross a line which is the eastern boundary of the so-called Philippine Area of Responsibility ( PAR ) , giving us due warning of their impending arrival . Our weather bureau has become expert in predicting the path , determining the strength of the winds , and estimating the volume of rain a typhoon is expected to dump on us . <p> Since super-typhoon " Yolanda " in 2013 , we have also learned to be wary of storm surges , the onrush of sea water driven by powerful winds inland . <p> The El Nino is an entirely different phenomenon . It is not an annual occurrence like a typhoon , although both El Nino and typhoons are born in the center of the Pacific Ocean . In El Nino , the surface of the ocean unaccountably heats up and the heat spreads in all directions -- east to South America and farther on to Africa , west and south to the Pacific islands of Papua New Guinea , Fiji , Samoa , Indonesia , and the Philippines . <p> Drought has now affected @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ typhoon that washes over the Philippines has not been enough to keep fields in many parts of our country from drying up . Our rice production is down and the government has moved to meet this emergency with big rice imports from Vietnam and Thailand this year and next . <p> Both these phenomena of typhoons and El Nino are related to global heating due to increased carbon emissions from factories , farms , and planes and vehicles around the world . This is why the just-concluded Paris Summit conference on climate change agreed to strive to limit the rise in global temperature to 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels . <p> The Philippines , more than most other countries , is at great risk from climate change . We have just been through typhoon " Nona " and we now face the El Nino drought .
