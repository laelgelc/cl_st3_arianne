This allows for the layering of solar cells so each coloured ray is directed onto a solar panel designed to convert this colour at maximum efficiency , the researchers say . <p> " The new device is a combination between a prism , which separates the different wavelengths of sunlight , and a lens , which concentrates the light that can be used to help harness a greater amount of energy from the sun , " explains Carlo Maragliano , the lead author of the research and a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ government-funded research organisation in Masdar City . <p> Traditional photovoltaic cells use silicon to transform light energy into electricity , but this can only absorb some of the sun 's wavelengths from the visible light spectrum , the researchers explain in a paper published in the Journal of Optics . Such cells are poor at absorbing the shorter blue and green wavelengths , converting only 15 to 18 per cent of this light into electricity , the paper says . And no light from the infrared spectrum is absorbed . <p> By splitting the light and layering the solar cells , around 40 per cent of the energy from the shorter wavelength colours can be turned into electricity , Maragliano tells SciDev.Net . Furthermore , making the device from plastic means it is cheap and can be mass produced , he explains . <p> " Layering solar cells improves the efficiency of the cells , and concentrating sunlight produces more electricity , " says Matteo Chiesa , a mechanical and materials scientist at the Masdar Institute and another author of the paper . <h> You might also like <p> All states @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ on oil , as stated in the national action plans of Gulf countries submitted to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change setting out their emission reduction strategies . She points out that the type of plastic used in the device is reported to warp under prolonged sunlight exposure . <p> " Weather conditions , like humidity and dust , could cause divergence of the light " that may weaken the device 's ability to improve solar cell efficiency , she says . <p> Nonetheless , the researchers want to stick with plastic because the low price would make the device a more attractive tool to improve solar power systems in developing countries .
