<p> Outside of sounds bites and vague promises chock full of get-out clauses , there has been no worldwide agreement with real regulatory teeth at the UN climate change conference in Paris , says Chris Nelson.Francois Mori / AP <p> Once , when the civilized world faced annihilation , two men met off the Newfoundland coast and forged an agreement to guide the planet away from the looming abyss . <p> That was August 1941 , as Nazi Germany , after conquering most of Western Europe , was trying to bomb Britain into submission while embarking upon the most savage invasion in history into the heartland of the Soviet Union . <p> The two men , Winston Churchill and Franklin D. Roosevelt , met secretly aboard a U.S. cruiser and hashed out the Atlantic Charter -- a document that gave American support to the beleaguered Brits and went on to pledge the restoration of national borders , along with self-determination for the countries of the world once the horror was defeated . It laid the groundwork for Hitler 's defeat , the expansion of international trade through the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ end to colonialism and the seeds of a future United Nations organization . <p> Two men did that . So imagine what 36,276 men and women accomplished in Paris these last weeks to deal with another global threat -- climate change . If you answered " not much , " then grab the first-prize ribbon . <p> Because there 's an unwritten law of international conferences -- the more who attend , the less gets accomplished . And boy , did the world 's gravy train experts turn up en masse in the French capital . And yes , indeed , to back up our new prime minister 's words with actions , Canada was back . Big time . So much so , we had more delegates merrily slurping on the public teat and posing for selfies than the Yanks , Brits and Aussies combined . <p> For the record , the Canuck count was 383 . That compared to 46 Australians , 96 from the U.K. and 148 from the U.S. When it comes to dealing with hot air , we 're again second to none . <p> Maybe @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ there and back if they 'd actually accomplished something . More multibillion-dollar boondoggles like the last government 's carbon capture publicity stunt ? Or will the vague promise of returning money to compensate average Albertans for higher gasoline and electricity bills actually transpire ? <p> These are not mere trifles to be considered at a later date . <p> This government has shown already a frightening inattention to detail in its proposed legislation .
