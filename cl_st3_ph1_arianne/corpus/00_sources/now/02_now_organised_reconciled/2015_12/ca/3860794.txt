<h> The fate of peace <p> Since Sept. 11 , 2001 the West - especially the U.S. - has put an incredible amount of energy , lives and money into fighting international terrorism . According to data gathered by the Mercatus Centre , between 2001 and 2015 the U.S. spent $1.68 trillion on combatting terrorism , including the wars in Afghanistan and Iraq . <p> Comparable statistics for Canada are difficult to find . However , in April the previous federal government allocated an additional $360 million for Canada 's role in the fight against the Islamic State ( also known as ISIS or ISIL ) , bringing spending for that operation alone up to $482 million . According to a report in 2014 by the Globe and Mail , Canada 's role in the Afghan War cost more than $18 billion . <p> Despite the massive expenditure of money and lives , terrorism is on the rise . Nine times as many people were killed in terrorism attacks in 2014 than in 2000 . <p> The majority of deaths , 78 per cent , are concentrated in Iraq , Nigeria , Afghanistan , Pakistan and Syria . For the people of those five countries , terrorism is a real and pressing danger . <p> It is a tragedy that any lives are lost in these brutal and senseless attacks , and the international community should continue to oppose the radical ideologies which spawn them and aid the people displaced by violence in Syria and elsewhere . <p> But it is important to retain some degree of perspective and relative scale . <p> Even in 2014 , 13 times as many people were murdered in non-terrorism-related killings than were killed by terrorists . In fact , until 2013 , more people were murdered in the U.S. alone than in terrorism attacks around the globe . <p> In Canada you are almost four times more likely to be killed or injured by lightning than a terrorist act ( 5.5 million @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ) .
