<h> News Brantford-Brant <h> BRANT COUNTY : Green bins urged to lessen landfill demand <p> A recycling advocate is bringing the message of the Zero Waste Brant campaign to county council in a bid to offer alternatives to an costly expansion in the nearly exhausted Biggars Lane landfill site . <p> Elizabeth Storjohann , a member of the Biggars Lane waste management advisory committee and the group Zero Waste Brant , will make a presentation to a meeting of the public works committee in the Wardens Room of the county 's Burford corporate office on Tuesday at 9 a.m . <p> She said she hopes to generate a demonstration of public support at the entrance to the building about half an hour before the meeting starts . <p> " No one wants a landfill located next to them . Taxpayers do n't want to pay the millions of dollars it will take to build a new landfill either , " Storjohann writes in an article that she has distributed . <p> " These two outcomes can be avoided if the county makes it easier for residents to separate @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ throw things away . Most of what is currently going into our landfill is either compostable or recyclable . This has to stop . " <p> Zero Waste Brant 's latest foray comes as the county has launched an environmental assessment to secure additional capacity at the landfill - a project that could cost as much as $3 million . <p> The county must secure extra space to meet the terms of reference of its certificate of approval from the Ministry of the Environment to operate the site , which has fewer than 10 years left before its capacity is exhausted . <p> Golder Associates Ltd. has been hired to undertake the assessment . <p> Storjohann noted that , over the past five years , her family of five has shrunk its household waste to one shopping bag every two weeks , while its recycling has grown by four times that amount . <p> She says she has turned one corner of her garage into a recycling depot that is emptied once every two weeks .
