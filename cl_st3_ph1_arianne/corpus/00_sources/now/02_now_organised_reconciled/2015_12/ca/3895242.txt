<p> The newly signed climate accord is being hailed around the world as a major step in the battle against global warming , though large Canadian carbon emitters are still determining what the impact will be on their bottom lines . <p> Alberta oil-sands producers and power generators were already awaiting more details from the government of Premier Rachel Notley , which unveiled its own climate-change framework last month . It includes an economy-wide carbon tax , a cap on emissions from the oil sands and a phase-out of coal-fired power . <p> There is no indication yet that the deal agreed to by 195 counties , including Canada , in Paris on Saturday will add new wrinkles to Alberta 's plan , which has caused some friction among oil-industry executives . The big winners are likely to be companies that are developing green energy and technology to reduce emissions from fossil fuels . <p> Cenovus Energy Inc. , a major oil-sands developer , welcomed the global solidarity on the issue .
