<p> Natural resources are of continuing importance to our economy . As I see it , prudent exploitation of such resources are particularly important in the rural areas of Canada . In various electronic and print media , resources generally are referred to as " commodities " -- i.e. , iron ore , coal , oil , aluminium , copper , tin and the like . <p> Since the worldwide economic slowdown , markets and prices for many of these commodities ( resources ) have become depressed . <p> However , there is one resource or commodity -- not yet identified -- that is more important than all the rest : water . It can be argued that water is the one resource whose access and availability is essential to the exploitation of all others . Consider the rancorous debate raging in university labs and in the broader community over the use and abuse of high volumes of water in the extraction of oil from the Tar Sands in Alberta . <p> We recognize that water , like all of our natural resources , is under the jurisdiction @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ management , safety and control of water supplies . Yet , water , as a commodity in its own right , is seldom considered apart from the development of other resources . <p> I am obliged to divulge that I have monitored discussions on the attempts to build the Keystone oil pipeline in the United States . I also have kept abreast of the severe drought and water shortage in some of the west and southwest states . Some have even suggested combining water and oil whereby it might be possible to engineer the Keystone pipeline and focus initially on the flow of water rather than oil . <p> In simple terms , this would amount to a Trans-Canada Pipeline ( TCP ) proposal to initially build a pipeline to bring fixed quantities of Alberta and Canadian water to the drought-affected west and southwest United States . The plan would permit California and impacted areas time to implement wide measures to sustain water management and adjust their economies accordingly .
