<h> OUR VIEW : Rapidway just start of Davis Drive transformation <p> The Viva Yellow bus route is up and running , meaning the bulk of the road construction on Davis Drive is complete . <p> While the streetscape is already greatly improved , work will continue into next year to add greenery and other decorative elements . <p> Even when all that is finished , however , the transformation of Davis Drive is n't complete . <p> You may have noticed concept art of a transformed Davis in the paper or on yorkregion.com over the last few years when we reported on the project . <p> That art includes buildings that do n't currently exist , often in place of existing buildings . <p> While there 's no guarantee those existing buildings will be replaced , there 's no question part of the reason behind the new bus rapidway is to encourage redevelopment along Davis . <p> That 's a very good thing , because Davis is n't a particularly attractive , pedestrian-friendly place at the moment . New sidewalks along the rapidway will partly solve that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ street-level retail , are the next logical step . <p> None of the Davis redevelopment makes sense without that step . There simply are n't enough people living on the Davis corridor to sustain the rapidway right now and there are n't enough destinations along the route to attract transit riders from elsewhere . Yes , some people will ride the bus to get to work , go to the hospital or do some shopping , but not nearly enough to warrant a rapid transit corridor . <p> A few more riders will be added to the mix next month once GO bus service comes to the commuter lot at Davis and Hwy. 404 , but even that is n't going to fill Viva buses . <p> Instead , the Davis rapidway must be viewed as the first step in a transformation of not only a street , but a culture . <p> Future developments in Newmarket and throughout the GTA will see more people living in less space . People in cities and suburbs in Europe and Asia have been living in higher density communities for years and it 's @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ need to protect wild spaces and farmland to help mitigate climate change , feed our society and protect vulnerable species . <p> It 's worth noting the proposed Hollingsworth Arena sale and redevelopment , which would also see two unattractive private properties redeveloped near the corner of Davis and Patterson Street , is exactly the kind of intensification and beautification needed . <p> Intensification only works with quality public transportation , however , as the number of cars ca n't sustainably increase in proportion with the number of people . <p> That 's where the Viva rapidways come into play . <p> Instead of driving to shopping centres , most people in future residential developments on Davis will either walk , bike or take public transit . The Viva Yellow will take them to Upper Canada Mall , Newmarket Plaza , 404 Town Centre or other existing or new developments . <p> To get to work , those people will either take a short trip to somewhere local or commute south using transit , as the Yellow will connect them with GO buses at Hwy. 404 , the GO train or @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ latter will connect to the Yonge subway line at Hwy. 7 , instead of Finch Avenue , in the not-too-distant future . <p> Public transit is improving in the GTA , but building a world-class system is going to take patience , money and vision .
