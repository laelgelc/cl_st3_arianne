Video by Ryan Jackson , Edmonton Journal . <p> Every year , there are a few stories that stand out as the most important in Edmonton -- stories that resonate deeply with us , and get us talking with our neighbours . From Dec. 26 to 31 , the Journal is counting down our picks for the Top 5 stories of 2015 in our city . <p> Today , #1 : NDP win election <p> There was no contest when it came to picking Edmonton 's top news story of the year . <p> On May 5 , Alberta voters ended the Progressive Conservatives ' 44-year stranglehold on political power in favour of Rachel Notley 's NDP , a party that went into the election with just four incumbent MLAs and had never before formed government . <p> How @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ age in Alberta is about 36 . Thus , more than half the province 's residents had never seen an Alberta government other than the Tories , who first came to power in 1971 . <p> In fact , the NDP is only the fifth political party to govern Alberta since 1905 . Until this year , the most significant NDP success in Alberta was the 1986 and 1989 elections , when 16 NDP MLAs faced off against PC majorities . <p> The time , the NDP ended up with 54 seats , while the PCs , who started the campaign with a 70-seat majority , were reduced to just 11 seats ( although that dropped to 10 when Jim Prentice resigned as party leader and gave up his Calgary seat ) . In fact , after four decades of power , the PCs did n't get enough seats to even form the official Opposition . That role went to the Wildrose , which won 21 seats under leader Brian Jean , who had only been on the job for just over a month . <p> It should n't have been @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , polls were showing strong NDP support and NDP rallies were packed . But pollsters were skittish of calling an NDP victory -- they had called a Wildrose win in 2012 only to see the Tories victorious again -- and four decades of voting patterns were hard to overlook . <p> What was a surprise , perhaps , was the sheer size of the orange wave . In Edmonton , the entire city went NDP . Even popular Liberal MLA Laurie Blakeman was turfed . <p> It was a stunning , and visceral , an indictment of the PC legacy . Voters had enough of allegations of Tory entitlement and corruption under former premier Alison Redford , and became fed up with Prentice 's inability to steer the party in a new direction . Crucially , Prentice 's decision to welcome 11 Wildrose floor-crossers to his party in late 2014 infuriated many voters who saw the move as the party 's way to dismantle the Opposition and eliminate any check on its grip on power . <p> A series of missteps in the spring -- Prentice 's quip that Albertans should @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ responsible for the province 's economic difficulties , the party 's refusal to raise corporate taxes despite many other tax increases its March budget , and the decision to call an election a year early -- seemed to be the final nails in the PC coffin . <p> In the months after the election , much has changed on Alberta 's political landscape . <p> Former NDP leader Brian Mason , who was named minister for infrastructure and transportation , admitted in August that the jump from the opposition benches to cabinet had n't been easy , explaining that learning to manage the expectations of the chorus of voices looking to bend his ear was a challenge . Yet Mason was one of only four members who had previously sat in the legislature , so the caucus had to learn how government worked -- and fast .
