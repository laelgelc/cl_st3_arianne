<h> VOICE OF THE PEOPLE Dec. 29 , 2015 <p> Prime Minister Justin Trudeau recently responded , " Because it is 2015 , " when asked why the new federal cabinet had gender parity . Well , it does not seem to be 2015 in Nova Scotia . <p> The government recently passed legislation that reduces the maximum allowed size of new net-metered solar and other renewable energy installations in the province by 90 per cent ( from 1000kW to 100kW ) . It also requires Nova Scotia Power to create a new rate that determines what net-metering customers will get paid for their solar-generated power . This new process means that an NSP customer could get paid less for the power they give to the grid than what they pay for power they take from the grid . The impact of this law will be to curtail installation of solar energy when we need to encourage it . <p> In the wake of the Paris Accord and the need for each province , community and individual to do more to address climate change , this is a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ activity in rural Nova Scotia , this is a lost opportunity . Supplying , installing and maintaining solar energy systems can provide good jobs spread throughout the entire region and keep our investments and people here . And since the federal government is soon to invest large sums for incentives for clean energy , the timing makes no sense . There was no consultation around these changes -- the government slipped this bill through the legislature with no discussion in the press and no awareness in the province . A 400-kW solar installation under discussion for a commercial building is now off the table . One homeowner I know has shelved plans to install solar panels in the spring for fear of losing money on the payback from NSP . <p> The government has justified these changes by saying that they want to keep power rates low , but this action makes no sense in that context . Solar power is not subsidized in Nova Scotia .
