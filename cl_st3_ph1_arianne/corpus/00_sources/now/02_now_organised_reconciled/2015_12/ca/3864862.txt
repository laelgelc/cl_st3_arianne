<h> Overpopulation to blame for climate change : Port Hope resident <p> The recent world climate conference is useful , but regrettably a late and inadequate response to a festering global environmental problem . <p> If all participating nations will live up to the general agreements , it will only slow global warming , it will not reverse it . <p> As early as the 1970s , a university course was given in Montreal titled energy and environment . The course clearly identified some preventive actions countries must take to reduce the unwanted effects of greenhouse gases . What happened since then ? Lots of talk , conferences , posturing and not much more . <p> Developing countries blame the West for the last 100 or so years of over polluting , conveniently forgetting that long before North America , England or Europe was industrialized or heavily populated , the damage began and lasted thousands of years in regions where these countries are now located . <p> The cheap fossil energy and food production resulted in an exponential population growth . Our planet has gone through several major @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in a phase of natural warming . The problem however is that human activity speeds up this process towards a critical tipping point . The increased demand for more energy , food , housing , transportation and consumer products is pushing the environmental degradation in the wrong direction . <p> Food production in itself constitutes large portion of the problem . Even poor arid countries with not much more than sheep herds contribute . The demand for meat , especially beef , weighs heavy in greenhouse gas generation . Rice production , the main food staple for many countries , and the harvesting of CO2-consuming trees add to the greenhouse burden . In Paris the 40,000 participants , who burnt much fossil fuels to get there , talked about a lot of things but so far not one word about the real root cause of our present predicament , which is world human overpopulation.
