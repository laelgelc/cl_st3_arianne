<p> A University of Alberta marine mammal scientist who specializes in polar bears has won a lifetime achievement award in honour of his 40 years of study dedicated to Arctic animals . <p> Ian Stirling was presented with the Weston Family Prize for Lifetime Achievement in Northern Research in Vancouver this week . The $50,000 prize is awarded annually to a leading northern researcher in natural science . <p> Much of Stirling 's work is focused on the dwindling global population of polar bears . Stirling 's research was the first statistically significant proof of the negative impact of climate change on polar bears , and he estimates that the majority of polar bears will be gone by the end of the century . <p> He 's also authored hundreds of scientific articles , as well as several non-scientific books about his work for the general public . <p> This past summer , Stirling logged a polar bear holding its breath for three minutes as it hunted for fish , while studying the effect of disappearing sea ice .
