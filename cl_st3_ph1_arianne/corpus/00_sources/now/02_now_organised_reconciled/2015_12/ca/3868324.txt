<p> PREMIER Greg Selinger went to Paris last week for the United Nations conference on climate change . In an odd twist , he spent part of the weekend pitching the benefits of climate change on the Port of Churchill . <p> Selinger , who flew to Paris Friday after the close of the legislative session , is working to convince port officials in Normandy , France about transporting Canadian forestry products through the northern Manitoba port . <p> " There is some real interest there , " Selinger told the Free Press in a telephone interview from Paris Sunday . <p> Selinger said with climate change extending the Arctic shipping season , there are opportunities for Churchill . <p> He admitted it was ironic he was there for a conference about fighting climate change , and pitching the port , which is more viable because of climate change . <p> He said ports on Canada 's east and west coasts are extremely busy and cost more to use than Churchill . <p> " We need to keep it on the radar screen , " he said . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is limited in what it types of vessels it attracts . <p> Omnitrax , which owns and operates both the port and the Hudson Bay Railway , is selling the assets because the amount of grain moving through the port has plummeted . <p> Fewer than 200,000 tonnes of grain were shipped through Churchill this year , compared with an annual average of 550,000 tonnes .
