<p> It took four years of preparation and two intense weeks of talks , sometimes lasting through the night , but nearly 200 countries finally reached agreement at the Paris climate conference . Canadian Environment Minister Catherine McKenna seems satisfied with the agreement , as do the small island states at risk of some of the worst effects of climate change . The agreement includes the hard goal of no more than two degrees of warming above preindustrial levels and refers to the softer goal of aiming for 1.5 degrees instead . And it contains the ratcheting-up clause McKenna wanted in the text , in which countries agree to revisit their targets every five years to set tougher goals . <p> After two weeks of negotiations with 194 other parties , the next step should be comparatively straightforward : McKenna will lead the charge to design a national plan to lower greenhouse gas emissions , one that includes all 13 provinces and territories .
