<p> CLARINGTON , ONT. -- The corn is gone . So is the rich topsoil that held the tall , green stalks . <p> A new road slices through wetlands . Fresh 11-metre holes , filled with cement , puncture the terrain . Groundwater is pumped out so excavators can dig in . Soon , eight kilometres of buried pipes will drain excess water off this patch of land . <p> For three decades , a sleepy rural hydro field sat atop a 64-hectare portion of the Oak Ridges Moraine -- a government-protected watershed arcing across south-central Ontario -- which provides drinking water to about 20,000 area residents . Across the province , about 250,000 drink directly from moraine aquifers . <p> The moraine is supposed to be off limits to most new development , like subdivisions , under conservation rules passed in 2002 .
