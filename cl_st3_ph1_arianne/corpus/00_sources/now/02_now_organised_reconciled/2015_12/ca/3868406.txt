ALAIN JOCARD / AFP/Getty Images <p> For the past few days , as a curious and possibly endangered citizen , I 've been reading up on the role cities in general , and Ottawa in particular , are expected to play in the mitigation of the coming , inevitable climatic disruption . It 's been rather like watching game tapes to prepare tactics for the upcoming contest , a contest of monumental proportions @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ several things . By the daily news coverage of the climate change summit in resilient Paris ; by the return of the federal government to the positive side of the climate change conference tables ; by the fact of Mayor Jim Watson , unable to attend the conference , being quoted as saying , via his spokesperson , that he " looks forward to speaking with those mayors who do attend ( Paris ) upon their return " ; by the emails I 've received lately from steadfast Ecology Ottawa ( ecologyottawa.ca ) regarding the advances and retreats in civic policy on climate change ; and by the conversations I had after the fact with many of my neighbours returning from the 100% Possible rally on Parliament Hill last weekend , one of the biggest the city has ever seen ( organized in part by Ecology Ottawa ) ; and by the fact of the minister of environment and climate change being an Ottawa MP , plus the symbolism of her new title . <p> In the midst of wading through a polysyllabic , largely incomprehensible paper by an Israeli academic @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ overheated future , I came across the word " resilient . " The author was talking about the Resilient City , a city that can spring back quickly from setbacks , that can get back in shape when it is bent out of it . By setback I mean that we might have been a little backward in coming forward . ( Take the politically inspired cancellation of the flawed but feasible Light Rail project after the 2006 municipal election . ) Ottawa is a city blessed with an abundance of resident , free-at-last scientists , a highly educated populace and a willingness among many to , as it were , lift our foot off the emissions pedal . With the right leadership , and a desire to capitalize on our home-grown , at-hand talents , I do n't see why we ca n't breathe easier . <p> Two of the major setbacks from which the city has suffered in the 25 years since the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change published its first report lie in the areas of transportation and buildings . Most of the reading I 've done points @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ carbon footprint , which is the Mission Possible that I 'm discussing here . <p> I recall my Dad giving me the advice when I was a teenager that if I found myself surrounded and about to be damaged , I should pick the biggest assailant first and try and overcome it . We are , eight years later , doing something about improving public transportation 's emissions with the expanded LRT . Our bus system , according to surveys of those who use it , is certainly not top of the class , and I would again ask for the example of Curitiba in Brazil , which has what is universally considered the best Bus Rapid Transit system in the world , to be our guiding light . We have begun to take the bicycle as a low emitter seriously . ( A friend just back from Copenhagen spoke of the racks of thousands of bikes in the main train station ; commute in on the LRT , bike to the workplace . ) <p> It is therefore in the building of low emission towers that we have the furthest @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . Many of the cities in the C40 Cities Climate Leadership Group , of which Vancouver is a member and Ottawa is not , are setting emission levels for new buildings . They are also actively researching best practice around the world in low emission building techniques . Might we one day see an emission test for buildings as well as cars ? With the results posted on the hoardings ? Annual awards for the new building with the lowest emissions that year ? A couple of suggestions from me .
