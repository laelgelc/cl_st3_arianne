<p> Food writers drive themselves a little crazy this time of year , each of us competing to be the first both to foresee the coming trends and to dismiss with a yawn those that have become the culinary equivalent of a pilled sweater -- tired and embarrassing . <p> To know that cauliflower was last year 's veggie darling , and that 2016 will see us peeling zucchini into noodley strips and thinking it 's pasta , is the ultimate triumph for the intelligent food lover . Oh , and the term foodie is so 1997 . Please do n't say it ever again . <p> Some of this trend-seeking merely reflects a desire to have fun , because eating is entertainment . Who does n't want to recognize and embrace the izakaya concept when it means you will be noisily welcomed into a teeming bar , where you can order sake ? The springy alkaline noodles in tasty broth have hit the big time in part because they combine delicious flavours and inventive ingredients in a comforting bowl that costs less than a pair of socks by Ted Baker . It 's also super fun to slurp the soup , and to be given permission to do so because that 's ramen etiquette . <p> But here 's what I 'm noticing about some key trends in recent years . Efforts that began , say , as a way to be like Gwyneth Paltrow through juicing , have become more substantial over time . Food pursuits that might have seemed disparate , even silly , when they first appeared on the scene -- kale smoothies , eating insects , creating dishes from food waste -- have converged , pointing to what may be the most important food trend of this year , or any year . <p> For 2016 , the trend increasingly will be to pay sharp attention to what and how we consume -- and in the most big-picture of ways . It 's about more than eating healthy by @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ people will want to eat food that 's clean and sustainable for themselves , and for the planet . <p> This means consuming the entire animal so we do n't waste , eating different species of fish that are more sustainable , and being alert to humane sourcing and traceability . Vegetarians and vegans take this a step further , reducing or eliminating animal products from their diet to shrink their environmental footprints and out of concern for animals . Even the meat-eaters among us are changing habits . Climate change has us worried , and more folks are turning to grains and lentils to meet our taste and nutrition needs . <p> Evidence of the new attitude is gaining momentum and will continue to do so . Inspired by grassroots movements percolating for the past decade or more , food giants are making enormous shifts in the way they purchase and sell food . McDonald 's has launched a program to use free-run eggs in their restaurants across North America , and also has a pilot project in Alberta to source sustainable beef for burgers . I 'm all for that . But even as we explore the latest hot sauce ( sriracha or sambal ? ) or newest technique for preparing vegetables ( charred ! ) , 2016 will see us be more mindful .
