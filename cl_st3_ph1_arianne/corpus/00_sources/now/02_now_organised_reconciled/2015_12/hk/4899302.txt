<p> On Monday 30 November 2015 , delegates from around the world gathered in Paris to attend the official start of the 21st annual Conference of Parties ( COP ) , also known as the 2015 Paris Climate Conference . The main objective of the COP series is to review the implementation of global commitments first discussed at the Rio Earth Summit in 1992 with the adoption of the UN Framework on Climate Change ( UNFCCC ) . This year , however , the focus is very much on the achievement of a legally binding and universal agreement on climate change to take effect by 2020 , with a goal of keeping global temperature rise to below 2 ? C. Though the goal seems simple , the complex negotiations will involve agreement by the member countries on the rules , mechanisms , county contributions , financial aspects , and stakeholder commitments as part of the climate deal . <p> This is not the first time this goal has been discussed at the climate conferences . Given this past history , many eyes are on this years ' conference to see if COP21 can do what COP15 could not . <p> As a signatory to the UNFCCC , Singapore has already submitted their Intended Nationally Determined Contribution ( INDC ) to the UNFCCC Secretariat in advance of COP21 . Singapore intends to reduce its Emissions Intensity by 36% from 2005 levels by 2030 , and stabilise its emissions with the aim of peaking around 20301 . Should a climate agreement be struck at COP21 , Singapore will be held to this commitment as a legally binding obligation . However , if no agreement is made then the INDC is not requisite for climate change action . <p> Singapore 's Environment and Water Resources Minister Masagos Zulkifli and Foreign Affairs Minister Vivian Balakrishnan are leading the Singapore delegation at the summit .
