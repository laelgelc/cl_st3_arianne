<h> African scribes urged to deepen people 's understanding of MEAs <p> Sifelani Tsiko recently in Nairobi , Kenya African journalists have been urged to constantly upgrade their knowledge and skills to help them to better educate and inform ordinary people on multilateral environmental agreements ( MEAs ) . <p> They should breakdown and interpret MEA concepts and issues which are central in regional and global efforts towards tackling the world 's environmental challenges . <p> This , it was said , will help people to better understand MEAs and enable them to make informed decisions and choices on their daily lives . Joe Ageyo , a veteran journalist and managing editor for Kenya Television Network ( KTN ) made the call at a capacity building workshop for African journalists on MEAs which was held recently in Nairobi . <p> " At one point , while attending CoP ( United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change ( UNFCCC ) Conference of Parties ) meetings in Europe , I thought I had got scoop when African negotiators walked out of the meeting , " he said . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , one of the negotiators said : ' We walked out because of Annexure . . . which industrialised countries had refused to agree on . " The workshop was organized by the Environmental Compliance Institute ( ECI ) and the Media for Environment , Science , Health and Agriculture ( MESHA ) with the support of the United Nations Environment Programme . <p> The goal was to assist the participants to get deeper insight into key environmental challenges in Africa and MEAs that address those challenges .
