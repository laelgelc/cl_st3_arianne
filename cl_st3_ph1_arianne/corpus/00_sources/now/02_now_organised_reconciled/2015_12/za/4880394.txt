<h> Netherlands donates $7 million to improve water management in Near East and Africa <p> Remote sensing satellite imagery will help to identify areas where water use produces poor crops . <p> The Netherlands and FAO are expanding their collaboration in the area of water management with a $7 million donation by the Dutch government to support the use of remote sensing technology in helping water-scarce countries in the Near East and Africa monitor and improve the way they use water for crop production . <p> The additional donation brings the total budget up to $10 million for the Dutch-funded project that uses satellite data to find land areas where water use is not translating into optimal agricultural production , identify the source of the problem and recommend different planting and irrigation techniques . <p> " The project uses some of the most advanced technologies and takes into account the ecosystems and the equitable use of water resources , " FAO Director-General Jos ? Graziano da Silva said at an event marking the extended agreement at FAO headquarters in Rome . <p> He highlighted the importance of the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Paris , noting the added stress that a concrete tool to take decisions about the best use of water and what kind of crops to grow -- but also about the growing season so that they can target their investments , " she added . <p> The data real-time . <p> Based on this information -- which incorporates rainfall and crop transpiration data , among other inputs -- experts can quickly assess problem areas where water and land productivity are low , meaning these areas use relatively high amounts of scarce natural resources for minimal yield . <p> Using remote sensing rather than country survey data offers a unified measuring tool that allows for easy comparisons between the productivity of land areas -- from the country level to the farm level . <p> By posing questions such as , why is one farm using the available water better than
