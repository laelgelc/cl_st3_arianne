<h> Peruvian farmer takes climate change fight to European courts <p> AFP / Cris Bouroncle Mountan guide Saul Luciano Lliuya stands at a lagoon formed under the almost disappeared ice and snow mass on the Churup glacier in the Huascaran National Park in Ancash , Peru <h> The soft murmur of water trickling from a glacier in the Peruvian Andes into the pale blue lake below is broken by the roar of a chunk of ice breaking off . <p> Huaraz ( Peru ) ( AFP ) <p> Saul Luciano Lliuya , who grew up in these mountains , has spent his whole life watching climate change break the glaciers apart . <p> Now the 34-year-old Peruvian farmer and mountain guide is suing one of Europe 's largest energy companies , RWE of Germany , for what he says is its role in causing global warming -- and putting his hometown and his farm at risk . <p> Luciano looks out his window , which boasts a view of Churup , a 5,400-meter ( 17,700-foot ) peak . <p> He still remembers when it was covered in snow @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ bald old man , sparsely covered with tufts of white hair . <p> " It 's a shame the glaciers are n't the same anymore . We 're losing them to global warming , " Luciano told AFP . <p> " Someone caused this , and those people have to be held responsible . It 's not right to remain silent . The world belongs to all of us , not just the polluters . " <p> Luciano lives with his wife and two kids , farming potatoes , corn , quinoa , barley and wheat in the northern region of Ancash as his family has done for generations . <p> The mountains that line the horizon are part of the Huascaran National Park , a UNESCO World Heritage Site that has lost 30 percent of its snow cover in the past four decades , according to the Peruvian government . <p> Luciano blames carbon-emitting industries for that . <p> A year ago , at a United Nations climate conference in Peru -- the precursor to high-stakes talks currently being held in Paris -- he met up with activists from the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ with a novel answer to the problem . <p> With Germanwatch 's help , Luciano is suing RWE , Germany 's number two energy producer and what the environmental group calls the " single-greatest CO2 emitter in Europe . " <p> -- The 20,000 euro question -- <p> Germanwatch announced the lawsuit late last month . RWE did not immediately respond , telling AFP it had only learned of the case through the media . <p> Luciano is seeking a relatively modest 20,000 euros in the German courts -- his calculation of RWE 's rightful share in the cost of protecting his community from floods caused by melting snow and glaciers . <p> It is a creative approach to dealing with the question of what economists call " externalities , " the consequences of an economic activity experienced by unrelated third parties . <p> Germanwatch said this is the first time a private individual affected by climate change has sued a company for damages . <p> Luciano 's lawsuit also highlights the tough questions besetting the negotiations in Paris , where there are deep divisions between wealthy countries that developed their @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ pollution and poor countries demanding financial help to do it more sustainably . <p> It remains to be seen what the courts will make of Luciano 's lawsuit . <p> What is clear is that the melting of the glaciers is creating new lakes and raising the water level in existing ones , putting communities like Luciano 's at risk . <p> The volume of water in the Palcacocha lake is 34 times higher today than in 1970 . <p> The US Agency for International Development ( USAID ) said in a report last year that the lake is so swollen it has become a permanent threat . <p> The lake 's last major flood was in 1941 , when it burst its shores and killed 5,000 people in the regional capital , Huaraz -- today a city of 100,000 people . <p> Luciano wants RWE to help pay for a series of projects to prevent floods , including building a dam .
