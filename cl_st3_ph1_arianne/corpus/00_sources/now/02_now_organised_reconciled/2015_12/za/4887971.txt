<p> I do n't need any excuse to visit my favourite city Paris and I care passionately about my planet 's environmental longevity . So why am I not at the COP21 climate change summit ? <p> Although it started well , my decision to give it a miss undergoes quotidian vindication . <p> The opening produced a record breaking number of world leaders . They were all on message and their rhetoric was both fiery and caring . There were so many of them , they had to be broken into two groups . Even then , there were some , like David Cameron , who voiced their displeasure at being kept waiting . <p> The French hosts cleverly learned a lesson from the abysmal COP15 in Copenhagen and brought these star performers in at the start of proceedings . By the time the leaders arrived in the Danish capital eight years ago it was too late to save anything . <p> On day three , Francois Hollande , riding his new wave of credibility , announced that his and other developed nations owed the Third World @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ? 2 billion for green energy projects in Africa . <p> Thus an opportunity has gone begging to report good environmental news . <p> Wait ! The way these COP things work , the sprint has now become a marathon . The late night meetings have started . There are so many consultations on the fringes that functionaries have begun seriously to refer to informal informal talks . <p> Journalists are expected to report this every step of the way . <p> They also have to identify the NEW THING . This time , it appears to be buildings . Heating and cooling buildings take up 40% of the world 's energy . Buildings emit a third of the planet 's greenhouse gases . So something has to be done about buildings . <p> Like something had to be done about coal . Yet coal remains as much used and as dirty as ever it was . Countries say how much they hate and fear it . Yet none have the political courage or muscle to declare ca suffit . <p> As an African , I would undoubtedly have a lead @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . And I would have been obliged to report it as though they had every expectation of getting something . As if .... <p> I would also have had to report the encouraging promises from participants . They have been unprecedented , the hosts report quite correctly . And that is where the rub starts . Surely there have been commitments before ? <p> Indeed I remember them from COP17 in Durban . And I recall all too vividly how participants went home and reneged on those commitments . There was little or no surprise at this . That 's the way these COP things roll . <p> What about those of us - and I am talking about the overwhelming majority of journalists , including the stick-to-the-facts agencies - who reported those ephemeral commitments as reality ? <p> The truth is we knew about the fickle nature of the beast even as we were writing about it . We knew that the deal struck after negotiators stopped the clocks to continue meeting until dawn had actually been cobbled together days before the deadline . <p> That made us complicit in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> I promised myself : never again . <p> So am not at COP21 because I am protecting something as important to me , and other journalists , as is drawing more and more energy from the sun and the wind : my credibility . <p> Jean-Jacques Cornish is the Africa correspondent at Eyewitness News .
