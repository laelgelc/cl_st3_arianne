<h> Building the post-animal bio-economy <h> At the intersection of food and medical science , cultured meat produced in cell culture is slowly moving into the mainstream <p> Flavour is still proving tricky with meat produced in vitro , but some commentators joke that cultured meat still tastes better than many vegan options currently on the market <p> You could start a fast food joint with the amount of grub that is being made from cell culture in a lab . <p> Burgers and eggs have already been successfully developed and chicken is next on the menu . <p> Cultured meat is meat produced in vitro , in a cell culture , rather than from an animal . Cells are taken from a cow , pig , chicken or whatever , and reproduced in a nutrient-rich sauce . <p> Once they have multiplied enough , they are attached to a spongy frame , usually made from something like soy protein . <p> They can be stretched and shaped , seasoned and cooked to make all manner of boneless , processed meats such as sausages , chicken nuggets or @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ some commentators joke that cultured meat still tastes better than many vegan options currently on the market . <p> The technology already exists . Finding the expertise is the tricky part . <p> " This is a very new area so while there is lots of enthusiasm , it is difficult to find people who are already passionate about this work , " says Isha Datar , CEO and president of New Harvest , a non-profit organisation working to promote breakthroughs in animal products made without animals . <p> " No one is currently funding this kind of research , mainly because it is at the intersection of food and medical science , " she adds . <p> " We spent most of 2015 trying to raise funds so that we could finance more early-stage research .
