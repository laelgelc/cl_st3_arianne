<h> Plan for Ireland to be fossil fuel free by end of century <h> More solar farms and bioenergy but no decision on nuclear power or banning fracking <p> A general view of the Bord na Mona Edenderry peat powered power station in Co Offaly . The Government 's White Paper on Energy has called for Ireland to cease its use of fossil fuels by the end of the century . Photograph : PA <p> Ireland will use no fossil fuels by the end of the century , according to a new Government energy policy paper . <p> The White Paper on Energy was published by Minister for Communications and Energy Alex White this morning . <p> Mr White said the policy builds on the ambition of the Paris Agreement on global climate change which was formally ratified last weekend . <p> The paper sets out the long-term goals for energy use in Ireland . Mr White said Ireland was still dependent on fossil fuels which provide 92 per cent of the energy share for the country at present . <p> He said by 2050 , the share @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ per cent and 95 per cent . Ireland would be wholly fossil free by the end of the century , he said . <p> The White Paper sets out a wide range of solutions to replacing fossil fuels as our primary sources of energy . <p> The Minister repeated several times that there were " no big bang solutions " , rather a range of measures . <p> The measures include a substantial expansion of solar power , primarily through solar farms ; the increased use of bio-energy ( including bio fuel crops ) , as well as tapping the potential of ocean and tide energy . <p> There are hundreds of solar farms already in operation in Devon and Cornwall and similar farms could be located in the southern tip of the country , particularly counties Wexford , Waterford and Cork , he said . <p> The main source of renewables for the foreseeable future will be wind-farms . Addressing the controversy over the location of some wind farms , Mr White said the vast majority of the projects have been established with no opposition from local communities . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ renewable energy . <p> There was no definite view expressed on nuclear power , or on fracking , which has been a very contentious issue . <p> On the latter , the Minister said he would await the outcome of an evaluation being conducted by the Environmental Protection Agency before making any decision on extraction by fracturing . <p> Similarly , on nuclear , he said he would await the outcome of an evaluation , although he expressed doubt if Ireland was large enough to accommodate a nuclear power station . <p> There are also commitments to increasing the number of electric vehicles in use . The paper refers the 2020 target of 50,000 vehicles but the Minister accepted that the take-up of electric cars has been " disappointing " so far , 700 in all . <p> He accepted there was no chance that target would be reached .
