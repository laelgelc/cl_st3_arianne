<h> Farmer writes : The joy of local <p> Kieran Sullivan talks about selling locally and why , if you 're sending off small numbers at a time , it can save labour and reduce your carbon footprint <p> Next up : finding a buyer for these 90 silage bales . <p> Kieran Sullivan talks about selling locally and why , if you 're sending off small numbers at a time , it can save labour and reduce your carbon footprint <p> With all the talk of climate change and carbon footprints in Paris last week , I am glad to report that the lambs we 're sending off for slaughter are doing their bit to reduce global warming . <p> By going to a local butcher , our lambs will generate very little transport emissions on their final journey . Admittedly , they clocked up a few miles when we brought them from the mart - it being a 90km round-trip to the nearest sheep mart in New Ross - but at least we did n't increase their carbon footprint by bringing them back to sell there @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the local butcher , then we 've saved a grand total of 80km worth of carbon emissions . Right , that 's our bit for the planet done , but what about our bottom line , you might ask ? <p> Cutting out the middle men <p> Well , supplying the butcher directly means there 's no middle men who have to be paid . The mart does a fine service and is the lifeblood of many farmers , so it 's entitled to cover its costs via commission ( do n't mention the levies ! ) . <p> The buyer at the mart this time of year for lambs would most likely be a factory agent . He too does a job and needs to be paid . <p> Dealing directly with the processor does n't mean you 'll receive what the above two would have got , but it does mean you have a little bit more leeway to achieve your margin . A working relationship with the local man also means there might be some give-and-take when it comes to carcases -- he might pay you for 23kg @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> At time of writing , lambs are making between ? 5.00 and ? 5.10/kg . You can do the maths yourself : that extra kilogramme the local man might give translates into an extra fiver per lamb . <p> As well as the petrol and carbon emissions saved , there 's also the time saved by having to travel less . The 90km road-trip mentioned above translates into half-a-day 's labour . For part-timers , this is the type of labour-saving that really matters . <p> Also , we 're inclined towards the whole sustainability thing in our house , so selling local means we 're in line with the farm-to-fork philosophy . <p> We 'll see in a few days how prices eventually went with the lambs . After that , it 'll be easier to decide whether we can continue to save the planet and make our target margins at the same time . <p> Final produce of the year <p> Next up for us will be finding a buyer for the 90 silage bales shown above .
