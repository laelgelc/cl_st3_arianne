<h> Farmer Writes : Cattle and sheep are our Google and Facebook <p> Co Longford dairy and beef farmer James Mimnagh wonders if enough is being done to protect agriculture in the climate change debate . <p> Methane from ruminants accounts for 15% of Ireland 's total greenhouse gas emissions . <p> Co Longford dairy and beef farmer James Mimnagh wonders if enough is being done to protect agriculture in the climate change debate . <p> Ireland has one of the most environmentally friendly agriculture industries in the world . <p> It is time the Irish public supported farmers and the agriculture industry in much the same way which we triumph about our IT and tourism industries . <p> Ireland 's dairy , cattle and sheep farmers are our Googles and Facebooks , in that we produce a fantastic , efficient product . Our litres of milk and kg of beef and lamb which our cattle and sheep produce are among the most efficiently produced food products in the world . <p> Let 's throw some perspective on Ireland 's agricultural greenhouse gas emissions . Agriculture accounts for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ production from ruminants accounts for 50% of agricultural greenhouse gas emissions ( GHG ) and therefore 15% of total GHG emissions . <p> Reducing methane emissions from ruminant animals such as cattle and sheep poses the best opportunity to reduce agricultural emissions . <p> Reducing emissions <p> How do we reduce these emissions ? Well , it is clear that methane emissions will increase as agricultural output increases , but what is important is to reduce methane emissions per unit of output , therefore producing milk , beef and lamb in as an environmentally friendly way as possible . <p> Farmers can and must reduce methane emissions by increasing efficiency on farm . A key efficiency which has been mooted occasionally is producing more young bulls , slaughtered at less than 16 months . <p> An even greater efficiency , which is applicable to dairy and suckler farmers , is calving heifers at two years of age . This is important as it can allow a cow to produce an extra calf and a years ' more milk in its lifetime .
