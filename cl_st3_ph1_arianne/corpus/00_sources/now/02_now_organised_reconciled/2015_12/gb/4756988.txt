<h> A Christmas message to the people of Blackburn and East Lancashire <p> IT SEEMS more difficult to enter into that spirit of joy this Christmas , because of the circumstances many around us have to face at this time . <p> Just recently the floods in Cumbria and North Lancashire have had a devastating impact on the occupants of many homes . And for a number it is not the first time . <p> But there are similar situations around the world , where natural disasters , Chennai to name but one recent tragedy , have sent a powerful message to the Paris Climate Change Conference . <p> But we can not blame the environment for every disaster . <p> The extraordinary refugee and migrant crisis this year with people fleeing their homes because of war and terrorism highlights the suffering we inflict on others . <p> Sometimes , however , the flight from our homes is for personal reasons , like Maggie Smith 's portrayal in Alan Bennett 's film , The Lady in the Van , an amusing and yet serious account of homelessness . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the Lancaster Night Shelter seems greater this year than previously . <p> All of which emphasises the importance of a home , a place where we belong and feel secure . <p> So in the face of so much suffering , what do we celebrate at Christmas ? Our circumstances may not be encouraging or positive , but then Christmas is not about us . <p> When God chose to enter this world as a human being , where He should have been at home , He was not welcomed , having to flee as a refugee . <p> Eventually in His prime he was arrested , tried and crucified . He was in the world , but the world did not recognise Him . As it says in John , chapter 1 , verse 11 - He came to His own and His own received Him not . <p> He was too difficult to have around and so the religious authorities got rid of Him .
