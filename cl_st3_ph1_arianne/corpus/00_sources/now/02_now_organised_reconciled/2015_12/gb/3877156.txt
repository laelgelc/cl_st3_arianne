<p> A coalition representing more than 100 countries , formed in secrecy six months ago , has emerged at key UN talks in Paris to push for a legally binding global and ambitious deal on climate change . <p> The " high ambition coalition " speaks for the majority of the 195 countries at the crunch conference and consists of 79 African , Caribbean and Pacific countries , the US and all of EU member states . But notable exceptions include major developing countries such as China and India . <p> The group is focusing on at least four key issues . They want an agreement at Paris to be legally binding ; to set a clear long-term goal on global warming that is in line with scientific advice ; to introduce a mechanism for reviewing countries ' emissions commitments every five years ; and create a unified system for tracking countries ' progress on meeting their carbon goals . <p> The move is significant because it forms a major new power at the talks that wants to come to as strong an agreement as possible .
