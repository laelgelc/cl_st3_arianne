Things were different in the 19th century , when the explorer and scientist Alexander von Humboldt was a super-celebrity for more than 50 years . Today , one may wonder how many members of the general public , outside his native Germany , have even heard of him . <p> When Humboldt died , in 1859 , the King of Prussia called him the greatest human being since Noah 's flood . Mountains and towns were named after him in South America ; Queen Victoria had made a special trip to visit him ; and the King of Siam kept a portrait of him in his palace . <p> On the centenary of his birth , in 1869 , 25,000 New Yorkers turned out to listen to speeches about him in Central Park . But this was no mere media celebrity ; his fame was based on real achievement , energised by an adventurous character and a colossal capacity for hard work . Born into a well-off family of Prussian aristocrats , he was soon chafing against his upbringing , being driven instead by the two great passions of his life , travel and scientific knowledge . <p> He read voraciously in all the sciences , experimenting on himself with chemicals and electrodes , and talked with unstoppable enthusiasm .
