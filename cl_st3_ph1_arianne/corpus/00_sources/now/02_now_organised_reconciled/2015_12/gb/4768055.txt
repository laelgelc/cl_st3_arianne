<h> From an economic slowdown in China , to the plunging oil price and Europe 's migrant crisis , it 's been another year of great unease in the global economy <p> As the old year draws to a close , it is , by tradition , a time for columnists to reflect on what 's changed over the past 12 months . There 's plenty to dwell on . No doubt , there is much I 'm missing ; by the same token some of what I highlight will eventually turn out to be of no more than passing significance . Nevertheless , at this juncture , these are what I would regard as the 10 most seismic developments of 2015 , at least for Western , industrialised economies . <h> Declining oil prices <p> Saudi Arabia 's decision to let " the markets decide " was admittedly taken in late 2014 , but against expectations it has stuck with this strategy throughout the past year and shows few signs of changing . The glut in supply may have become a much more prolonged affair than generally appreciated . For now , the oil price seems destined to be stuck in a range of $30 to $60 , providing a major economic boost for big consumers such as America and Europe . If permanently anchored in this range , there are obviously also equal and opposite negative implications for the big producers , including oil majors such as the UK 's BP and Shell . <h> Climate change breakthrough <p> It 's easy to be cynical about the outcome of the COP21 agreement in Paris , but this too may in time have profound implications for the future of hydro-carbons . If nothing else it symbolically marked the point at which the world collectively decided , in principle at least , to set a course for a carbon-free economy . The promised end to sanctions will , in time , provide another substantial boost to oil supply , helping to keep the price low . <h> China slowdown <p> This was the year in which the wheels began to come off the Chinese growth story . Attempts to rein in runaway credit growth in combination with a vigorous official crackdown on corruption sent a chill through the world 's fastest-growing , major economy , causing it to stall completely at one stage earlier this year . A consequent end to the commodities " super-cycle " has pushed some producer nations , including Brazil , into recession . Together with some easing of the austerity agenda , this has enabled the eurozone economy to start growing again . <h> Greek standoff <p> That same sense of abatement in the eurozone crisis was underpinned by Greece 's farcical - and fruitless - game of political brinkmanship over continued membership of the single currency . Elected on an apparently irreconcilable agenda of both rejecting further austerity and remaining in the euro , Alexis Tsipras 's populist , Syriza-led government went to the wire and then capitulated , agreeing a further austerity programme that was in some respects worse than the one originally rejected . For now , the euro has been saved . That there will be future , existential crises remains certain . <h> Migrant mayhem <p> As one European crisis subsided , another began . Misguided attempts to impose order on the Middle East has succeeded only in sparking an invasion of migrants and a wave of Jihadist terror on the West 's own doorstep . Angela Merkel 's decision to accept 1m Syrian refugees has brought the European Union to the very edge of destruction , with borders re-imposed and Britain threatening to leave entirely . <h> Rise in rates <p> After more than a year of preparing the ground , the US Federal Reserve finally succeeded in raising interest rates from the floor they have been on since the financial crisis -- albeit by just 0.25pc but the first such upward move in nearly 10 years . Nonetheless , this is expected to be one of the slowest and shallowest tightening cycles in Fed history . <h> UK continuity <p> The UK has looked like a haven of economic and political stability compared to almost everywhere else , with unemployment down to just 5.2pc , six years of economic expansion beneath its belt , the election @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ opposition reduced to little more than a squabbling protest movement . This could yet be shattered by the possibility of Brexit , the political and economic consequences of which are impossible to predict . But for the moment , " steady as she goes " seems to be the order of the day . <h> An amazing profit <p> You might consider my last choice odd , but for me it is far and away the most dynamic industrial development of the year -- Amazon made a profit . Amazon is a company known for sacrificing profit for long-term growth , but all of a sudden it has started to make lots of it from a quite unexpected source -- its cloud-hosting division , Amazon Web Services . This is essentially the engine room of the Amazon machine , a worldwide network of servers that enables the company to do what it does so successfully . By the by , it creates space for rent .
