<p> It was 11 o'clock on Saturday morning . Inside Le Bourget , negotiators and observers were waiting for the announcement of the final draft text of the climate agreement , trading rumours and speculation about its contents . As they waited , we stood outside the conference centre and prayed . <p> Outside Le Bourget , there are tall white pillars , each with a different country 's name and flag printed on it . From small island states to superpowers , here at least , every country is equal . <p> There is something very moving about praying ' thy kingdom come ' when surrounded by so many symbols of the world 's kingdoms and nations ; or praying ' thy will be done ' when metres away battles of political wills are going on ; or praying ' Amen ' and so accepting our own responsibility to bring about change.It was clear by this point that a deal was just around the corner . The Climate Generations area , where civil society groups had met throughout the conference , was closed and the displays were @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , people here were ready to go home . <p> Yet by bearing witness outside Le Bourget , even as the negotiations drew to a close , we showed our belief that the struggle is not over yet . And it will not be over now this agreement is finalised . It will not be over this year , or next . This a long journey , but an urgent one . <p> CAFOD believes that this draft agreement , with its target of limiting temperature increases to 1.5C , opens a new chapter for action on climate change . For poor people living on the frontline of climate change , it offers hope for a brighter future , but not yet the security that we 'll get there quick enough . So we need to redouble our efforts . We need to journey forward with hope and determination . <p> We left Le Bourget and headed to the Eiffel Tower , the ultimate symbol of Paris . As we approached , we unfurled a banner with the words ' United for Climate Justice ' . Underneath this message were printed @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ including CAFOD . <p> Our small group soon was surrounded by many thousands more . There were splashes of red throughout the crowd , as people who had dressed in red for the earlier ' red lines ' street action joined people from many different networks , nationalities and ages . <p> We sang and we chanted . We formed a human chain which connected separate individuals into a united whole . As we chanted ' Another world is possible ' , I heard the words of the Lord 's prayer again . For what else do we mean when we pray ' on earth as it is in heaven ' apart from that ? Another world is possible.Sarah Hagger-Holt works for CAFOD campaigns and has been with a CAFOD group in Paris .
