<h> Consumers and businesses will have to be more energy-efficient and switch to alternatives to fossil fuels <p> The Paris climate change summit will conclude at the end of next week . It aims to reach an international agreement on limiting emissions of carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases that are contributing to global warming . The main source of these emissions is the burning of fossil fuels -- oil , coal and natural gas -- that power industry , and heat and light our homes . <p> This is not the first international summit that has been held with this objective . The journey to Paris started more than two decades ago , at the 1992 Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro , Brazil . That led to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change ( UNFCCC ) , which has now been ratified by nearly 200 countries . <p> A subsequent meeting , in Japan in 1997 , led to agreement on the Kyoto Protocol , where the commitments made at Rio were translated into emissions reduction targets for countries to follow . Though most European countries , including the UK , took their commitments seriously , the Kyoto Protocol was largely ignored in the rest of the world . <p> The Kyoto Protocol <p> In Copenhagen in 2009 , when the world was struggling to recover from the financial crisis , an attempt was made to reach an agreement to follow Kyoto . But as most of the world was just emerging from a deep recession , it was not a good time to focus on the long-term challenge of climate change . <p> However , one important thing did emerge from Copenhagen : countries were asked to put forward their own plans for cutting the greenhouse gas emissions causing climate change . The " top-down " framework of Kyoto -- which resulted in targets that were widely ignored -- was replaced by a " bottom-up " approach , based on the plans that governments around the world have developed themselves . <p> So what will happen @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ agreement is likely to be reached , with most of the world 's countries signed up to it . But will it be followed and implemented by the countries attending the summit ? And what difference will it make to our daily lives and to the business world ? <p> I am optimistic that a Paris climate change agreement will have a much bigger impact than Kyoto . The US and China , which burn more than 40pc of the world 's fossil fuels each year , are now much more actively engaged in the discussions . <p> The agreement will be informed by the " bottom-up " development of national government plans , so there will be a more solid underpinning to any new treaty agreed . <p> Also , we are seeing major changes in technology that are delivering cleaner sources of energy and helping to meet the challenge of dealing with climate change . <p> One big area where technology is helping is through the development of renewable energy sources , such as solar and wind power . Bigger and better batteries are being developed so that electric cars can be charged more quickly and drive further without recharging . <p> These developments in technology will enable society to cut its dependence on fossil fuels and reduce the damage to the world 's climate . <p> But there will still need to be changes to our lifestyles and the way in which businesses operate . <p> The first change is that we are going to have to become a lot more energy-efficient at home and at work . Working at home -- when this is possible -- can cut out unnecessary travel and save both time and energy . In many of these areas there is a win-win : reducing cost and improving business efficiency alongside lower energy consumption . <p> A second big area of change is likely to be the wider use of electricity , both to power cars and heat homes . Electricity is the medium through which we can consume renewable energy . In the past year or so , we have all been very happy to see the oil price coming down and the cost of petrol and diesel falling . But if we are to take the challenge of climate change seriously , the price of fossil fuels that create damaging emissions will need to rise -- either because market prices will increase or because governments will impose taxes and levies . <p> For consumers and businesses , this will sharpen the incentive to be more energy-efficient and switch to alternatives to fossil fuels . <p> This may sound a rather intimidating agenda . But in fact it is all very manageable -- if we approach it sensibly . <p> In the Sixties , our lives were transformed by the transistor radio and we put a man on the moon . In the Eighties , Nineties and 2000s , we developed the personal computer , the mobile phone and the internet . Now we need to switch our attention to managing climate change in the 2020s and 2030s . It can be done .
