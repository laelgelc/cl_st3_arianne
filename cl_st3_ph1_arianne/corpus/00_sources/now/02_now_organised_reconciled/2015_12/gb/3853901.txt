" <p> If , for example , a relative of wheat is found growing high on a mountain top , while it may not look much like what we think of as wheat , but its location makes it likely to be adapted to living in circumstances where water is scarce . <p> The need to find these plants was urgent , Ms Haga said , because of the pressure agriculture was facing , and because many of them were in areas where they were under threat from problems such as habitat loss and could be lost forever . <p> She warned : " Agriculture @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ growth and climate change . <p> " This dual challenge of feeding another two billion by 2050 , that 's a challenge in itself . <p> " We also know that many people will eat more meat , which will put more pressure on plant production , so it 's a growing population but also a more demanding population . <p> " Then of course we have climate change .
