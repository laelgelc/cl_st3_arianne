<h> The forgotten death of the ' Gros Michel ' banana bears a stark warning about how global warming could drive us all to starvation <p> In the 1950s , the world happily chomped its way through two different varieties of banana . There was the Cavendish , the banana that we know and love today , and the Gros Michel , a smaller , sweeter variety . <p> The Gros Michel , however , was wiped out in the same decade by a single disease . The entire world trade in bananas , and many developing economies , are now based on a single variety , the Cavendish . In the 1850s men , women and children starved in ditches by the side of muddy roads as the potato crop failed . Ireland had got used to feeding itself on just one variety of potato . When disease took hold , the population had no resistance . There was no genetic diversity to act as a barrier to the spread of the pathogen , turning everything to grey , watery pulp as it went . Potatoes , and people , perished before the despairing eyes of an impotent world .
