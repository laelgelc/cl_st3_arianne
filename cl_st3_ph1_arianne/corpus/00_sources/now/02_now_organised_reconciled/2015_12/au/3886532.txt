<p> Among the various interests at the Paris climate talks , it is arguably the voice of business that has emerged most clearly . Many business leaders are now saying that if the world is intent on reducing greenhouse gas emissions , there must be a worldwide price on carbon and a framework for linking the 55 schemes that exist in areas such as China , the European Union , and California . <p> Momentum has been building since May , when six of Europe 's largest oil and gas companies , including Royal Dutch Shell and BP , issued a letter calling for global carbon pricing system . That month , leaders from 59 international companies also signed a statement calling for carbon pricing to feature in the Paris agreement . <p> Advocacy has continued during the Paris negotiations .
