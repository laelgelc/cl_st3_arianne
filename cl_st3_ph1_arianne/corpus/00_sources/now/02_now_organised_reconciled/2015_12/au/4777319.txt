<h> Our resolution and wishes for 2016 <p> At whatever time of day you end up reading this editorial , you may already be regretting or even breaching the commitments you made last night , perhaps in a heady bout of spirited conviviality or unduly solemn reflection , for 2016 . <p> At the risk of setting ourselves up for similar remorse or condemnation in 364 days , herewith a few things we 'd like to happen in the next 12 months . <p> Although the latest possible date for a federal election is January 14 , 2017 , Australians are all but certain to go to the ballot box this year . The Age 's wish is that the election is a contest of well-structured , well-explained policies , rather than a bare-knuckled encounter dominated by negativity and personal denigration , as was too often the case with the two most recent federal elections . <p> The free market for ideas is the most important market we have , with the potential to generate policies that might deliver the opportunity of a fair and decent future for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to participate in that market with verve , honesty and courage . <p> Advertisement <p> Refreshingly , Malcolm Turnbull has opened the floodgates to debate since his ascension . We applaud his readiness to countenance change and flexibility . Opposition Leader Bill Shorten sought to inject some ideas into the public domain in 2015 . We urge them both to lead and encourage constructive policy debate within their parties and the wider community in 2016 . <p> Across the Pacific , the United States will also hold an election for a new president . As the most powerful position in the world , it affects us all , and is of particular importance at a time when so many fundamental issues , notably climate change , economic growth and terrorism , are not constrained by national borders . We hope the Republican Party can find a legitimate contender to make it a proper contest . <p> Climate change will loom large this year , as nations seek to develop and implement policies that deliver on the promising accord reached in Paris recently . That accord will remain mere words should political @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to take the measures that scientific consensus indicates are the only hope of preventing a catastrophic rise in global temperatures . This masthead has long supported the widely held view that a market mechanism , based on a realistic price on carbon , is a core element of any policy suite to avert such a rise . We urge all parties to adopt this ahead of the coming federal election . <p> We urge , too , politicians to show greater leadership on the massive humanitarian crisis that is the biggest exodus of refugees since the Second World War . German Chancellor Angela Merkel has shown such leadership and enlightenment by allowing desperate people sanctuary and the promise of contributing to the German economy and society . <p> Notwithstanding its one-off increase in the intake of Syrian refugees , Australia 's overall treatment of people seeking asylum has for years been a disgrace , utterly unfitting for a nation that prides itself on fairness and decency .
