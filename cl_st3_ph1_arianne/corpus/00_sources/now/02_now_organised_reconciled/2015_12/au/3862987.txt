<h> Higher human impact on freshwater loss <p> Published : 11:58 am , Saturday , 5 December 2015 <p> The human impact on freshwater loss owing to activities including irrigation and dam construction is 20 per cent higher than estimated , research shows . <p> According to a study published on Thursday by Science magazine , the increase in the total loss of freshwater into the atmosphere by evapotranspiration , is 4370 cubic kilometres per year , equivalent to two-thirds of the annual flow of the world 's largest river , the Amazon . <p> ' Small things that we do on the surface of the Earth can have large global effects , ' said Fernando Jaramillo , postdoctoral fellow at Stockholm University . <p> ' Previously , the global effects of local human activities such as dams had been underestimated . <p> ' This study shows that , so far , the effects are even greater than those from atmospheric climate change . ' <p> The thesis of the study is that irrigation and dams have increased global human consumption of freshwater by increasing evapotranspiration , which @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ evaporation together with the loss of water by transpiration from vegetation . <p> Human impact increases the loss of freshwater into the atmosphere and , therefore , reduces the water available to humans and related ecosystems . <p> ' The human-caused increase in this loss is like a huge river of freshwater from the landscape to the atmosphere , ' said Gia Destouni , Professor at Stockholm University . <p> ' We have changed so much of the freshwater system without knowing it . <p> ' Our study shows we have already passed a proposed planetary boundary for freshwater consumption . This is serious , regardless of whether we have crossed a real boundary or if the boundary has been underestimated . ' <p> For the study the scientists had analysed data on climate , hydrology and water-use in hundreds of large watersheds of the world between 1901 and 2008.
