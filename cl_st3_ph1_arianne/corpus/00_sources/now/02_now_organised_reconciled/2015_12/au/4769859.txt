Photo : Getty Images <p> Why is it so difficult to agree on a universal and -ated solution to the refugee crisis facing the world at present ? According to the Refugee Council , there are almost 60 million people who are forcibly displaced globally . To put this number into perspective , if these people comprised a nation it would be the 24th largest in the world . Refugees remain in limbo , caught in the middle of political negotiations , a welcoming arrival in some countries and a closed door in others . <p> It has been a watershed year with the largest movement of refugees since the 1950s . Negotiations among world leaders throughout 2015 have produced little by way of a solution to the crisis . In fact , the political haggling and the rhetoric it has spawned have been nothing short of tragic . <p> The recent calls by the European Union to pay Turkey @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ refugees to Europe from the conflict in Syria is a perfect example of the ineffective negotiations . In the most crude and cruel of ways it places a price on the head of the 2.2 million refugees currently camped in Turkey . It suggests that by paying Turkey , the " problem " will be contained and somehow go away . <p> Australia 's approach also requires critical consideration . While the government is to be commended for accepting 12,000 refugees , the discourse surrounding the process of evaluation has been divisive : at times it has revolved around selecting those from particular religious groups ; others have placed an emphasis on the contribution that the families and individuals will make . On all accounts , we will not be burdened by these new arrivals who will provide a cost benefit to their newly adopted homeland . Evidence throughout Australia 's history has shown that both migrants and refugees have made a positive net contribution to the Australian economy . There is therefore little dispute that there will be economic gains . <p> Advertisement <p> There is however something missing in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ fail to consider the human rights of all people regardless of their " potential " contribution and as such we construct an artificial pecking order of social justice . In doing so we lose the very essence of what it is to be socially just : that is to be , inclusive , equitable and compassionate . <p> A refugee 's human rights are threatened , as is their safety . This is what drives them to leave , sometimes with warning and perhaps a few belongings and sometimes without any warning at all . They do not leave their homeland because they seek economic advantage . A refugee is therefore someone who is forced to flee and can not return until the conditions that forced them to leave improve . <p> The solution to addressing the refugee crisis and overcoming the disturbing pecking order of refugee assessment is found in creating a globally co-ordinated neutral body to evaluate and transport refugees from countries of crisis , war and persecution to countries of safety . This crisis calls for the establishment of an internationally sanctioned migration and refugee architecture similar to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ II period . <p> In the immediate post-war period , a number of countries came together to form the Intergovernmental Committee for European Migration ( ICEM ) , the first intergovernmental organisation tasked with the role of regulating migration flows . The newly-established organisation commenced operations in February 1952 and by 1989 had been renamed the International Organisation for Migration ( IOM ) . In its various guises , the IOM was tasked with the role of taking charge of the selection , recruitment , processing and transportation of migrants and refugees while not encroaching upon national sovereignty . As the co-ordinated transport of migrants and refugees out of Europe became less pressing over time , the organisation became a policy lever of migration for sending and receiving countries and lost much of its co-ordinating role in processing and transporting migrants to receiving countries . <p> The present-day crisis calls for a reinvigoration of such a body -- a neutral arbiter charged with the task of integrating three pillars : a social justice agenda which is at the heart of this humanitarian crisis ; an economic agenda , drawing attention to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and , a social integration agenda , creating a bridge between the refugees and the countries of settlement . <p> Ironically , while we are connected globally , countries outside of Europe are largely absolving themselves of responsibility in responding to the needs of refugees fleeing war and persecution . It is time we came together to end the suffering , co-ordinating our efforts to firstly change the architecture governing refugee mobility and secondly to break the divisive discourse surrounding the " burden " of accepting particular groups of refugees , while welcoming others . <p> Dimitria Groutsis is the Co-Convenor of the Migrants@Work Research Group at the Business School , University of Sydney <h> 45 comments so far <p> Surely another leftist organization prioritizing 60m displaced people over 24m Australians is a bad dream sure to invite hysteria on these pages . <p> Commenter <p> Bernie <p> Location <p> HV <p> Date and time <p> December 29 , 2015 , 9:32PM <p> Bernie , who caused this tide of human misery , the greedy right wingers or the compassionate left wingers.The world would be a better place without right wingers @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ December 30 , 2015 , 1:10AM <p> Bernie , the hysteria is right there in your own comment . <p> Commenter <p> Arthur Baker <p> Location <p> Sydney <p> Date and time <p> December 30 , 2015 , 2:52AM <p> 1 .
