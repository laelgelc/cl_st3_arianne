We could use biological methods like planting more trees or managing soils in such a way that they hold more carbon . <p> Then there are the chemical methods . We could find a way to accelerate the rate at which minerals naturally weather and draw carbon out of air . Or we could set up " artificial trees " that suck carbon dioxide from the air . <p> Proponents have produced fancy artist 's impressions of what such artificial trees might look like . But the key point to note is that no such system has been built at anything like this scale . Getting the concept off the drawing board and into the real world is going to take a lot of time and money , and even then may turn out not to be feasible . <p> None of these is a get out of jail free card @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that we can not be certain of . <p> All of the proposed methods have side-effects that may well balance out the good points . Planting trees sounds wonderful , but to make a material difference to the climate it would have to happen on such an enormous scale that it would severely restrict the ability for us to grow food and protect biodiversity . And artificial trees would use vast amounts of energy and money -- resources which humanity has alternative uses for . <p> We do not know which , if any , of the proposed techniques could be a deployed at a material scale . We need to understand whether these proposed techniques are technically possible , environmentally sound and socially acceptable . <p> While the ambition to avoid dangerous climate change has been stated and the recognition that negative emissions will be required to achieve that objective is understood , there is a disconnect between what is being done and what is required .
