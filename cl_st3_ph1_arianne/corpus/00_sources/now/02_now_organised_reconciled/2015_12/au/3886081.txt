<h> Bushfires with a known cause <p> Research published in a recent issue of the International Journal of Wildland Fire has found that the density of human populations can explain the pattern of fires ignited in New South Wales and Victoria . <p> " The higher the population density the more ignitions you get , " said lead author Kathryn Collins of the University of Wollongong 's Centre for Environmental Risk Management of Bushfires . <p> Ms Collins and colleagues gathered official data on more than 113,000 bushfires ignited between 1997 and 2009 in 144 bioregions across New South Wales and Victoria . <p> Of the fires with a known cause , 47 per cent were due to accidental causes , due to such things as cigarettes , escaped burn-offs and campfires , or sparks from equipment or powerlines . <p> Forty per cent were deliberately lit and 13 per cent were caused by lightning . <p> The cause of 31 per cent of fires was undetermined . <p> There was a pattern of increasing ignitions going from west to the coast as population increases . <p> Kathryn @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ignition density " , which was the average annual number of bushfires ignited per 1,000 square kilometres . <p> They then investigated possible factors determining this ignition density . <p> These included population density , natural vegetation cover , number of hot days , elevation ( which is known to affect the chance of lightning strikes ) , the number of lightning strikes actually received , as well as rainfall , wind speed and humidity records . <p> In five different models , Ms Collins and colleagues found that population density was the main driver of total ignitions at the bioregion level . <p> " Basically where you get people is where you get your ignitions , " Ms Collins said . <p> " So there was a pattern of increasing ignitions going from west to the coast as population increases . <p> " Most of the other factors I looked at were n't significant .
