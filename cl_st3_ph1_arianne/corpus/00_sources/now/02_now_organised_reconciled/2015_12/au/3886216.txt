<h> UN Paris Climate conference : two decades on and nothing to show <p> All activists have managed to do is pressure governments into posturing . In 23 years there has been virtually no diplomatic progress on climate change . Francois Mori <p> by Alan Oxley <p> At the end of this week the UN climate change conference in Paris is likely to agree on strategy to manage emissions of greenhouse gases . Its importance will be overstated . It will simply amplify aspects of a convention adopted 23 years ago requiring parties to develop voluntary national strategies to reduce emissions . <p> Environmental activists always wanted more -- a global system to regulate deep reductions of emissions . One was trialled in the short-lived Kyoto Protocol . It failed . <p> Developing countries , lead by India and China , would not join . There is a reality here which is swept under the carpet . They will not accept legally binding measure to reduce emissions because they will reduce economic growth . <p> So why will the EU and activists at Paris now go along with @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ago ? Because they have been told the charade can not continue . The credibility of the UN is at risk . There have been 20 full-scale UN conferences in 23 years , drawing thousands of attendees and costing billions of dollars , capped with the outright failure in front of the largest meeting of heads of government in the UN 's history in 2009 . <p> Why did this impasse drag on ? First , the EU sustained it - another EU blunder ? Second , environmental activists encouraged it . They campaigned that big business - big oil , big gas and big coal - were the problem . The reality is the activists are . They have had more money to spend on climate change than big business . A US Senate minority report released early this year showed how many hundreds of millions of dollars flowed to US activists . The same interests fund the campaign to halt coal exports from Australia . <p> Big business is in fact a higher priority target for activists than governments .
