<p> A prized tract of old growth forest in Western Australia 's south west will continue to be logged despite a local council 's decision to throw its support behind conservation groups who once offered to buy it . <p> Earlier this year , environmentalists offered WA Premier Colin Barnett $90,000 to buy back part about 1,600 hectares of the 8,000-hectare Mowen Forest , east of Margaret River . <p> The forest contains a mix of old growth and regrowth jarrah , marri and she-oak trees , which are logged by the state-owned Forest Products Commission and sold to various timber processors . <p> Premier Barnett rejected the offer , stating the area slated for logging had previously been logged and would re-grow again ; however , environmentalists kept up their fight , often setting up camp and chaining themselves to equipment used to fell trees . <p> Earlier this week , the Shire of Augusta-Margaret River endorsed a motion to support a petition put forward by local conservation group , Save Mowen Forest , calling for an end to logging in the area . <p> Shire President @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ on future logging , it was important that the council reiterated its opposition to it . <p> " We as a council have no real input into the how and when the forest is logged other than to express the concern of our community , " Cr Earl said . <p> " But we do have a responsibility to reflect the views of our community .
