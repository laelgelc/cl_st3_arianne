<p> Large fruit-eating monkeys and birds in tropical forests have been revealed as surprising climate change champions , whose loss to over-hunting is driving up carbon emissions . This is because their seed-spreading plays a vital role in the survival of huge , hard-wooded trees . <p> Tropical forests store 40 per cent of all the carbon on the Earth 's surface and the slashing of trees causes about 15 per cent of the greenhouse gases that drive global warming . <p> Long-lived , thick and hard-wooded trees are especially good carbon stores , but they have large seeds that can only be dispersed via defecation by large animals .
