<h> Package deal <p> - There is no end to the debate on the expansion of the UNSC <p> Krishnan Srinivasan <p> Foreign ministers vote during a UNSC meeting , December 18 , 2015 <p> The United Nations has always upheld the integrity of the sovereign State , as is seen in the number of its members - now 193 . Each nation is able to take its individual stand on any issue , and such is the case with the reform of the organization . The calls for reform mean different things to different nations ; any change a country wants is presented as a plea for reform , and the West and the global South make mutual demands that neither can satisfy . The countries that lecture the UN on the need to make merit-based appointments are often the same ones that press for jobs for their own undeserving candidates . In addition to the perennial problems of dysfunctional UN agencies , inadequate resources and lack of political will , the UN is always destined to face crises of expectations . <p> With the rise of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the international political landscape is shifting towards a new dispensation , and the UN needs to find relevance in a multipolar world . Multilateralism survives although the former or future big powers would never want to entrust global institutions with any real authority , since they see themselves as the best guardians of their own interests , and the UN , Bretton Woods institutions and World Trade Organisation are being supplemented by new institutions like the International Criminal Court , the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons and regional arrangements for deeper economic integration . <p> It is undeniable that there is waste and inefficiency in many UN agencies and organs . Some have resources barely sufficient to pay their own salaries while agencies dealing with Palestinian and Syrian refugees are desperately short of funds . There would be rationale in winding some up , amalgamating others , and establishing new ones to deal with energy , water and sharing the global commons . However , the developed , developing , least developed , small states , landlocked and emerging Asian nations each have their own agendas . The Indian government over the past 20 years has been obsessing about this , with the media following suit . This has reduced attention to the other issues mentioned earlier that are equally or more important for the UN to function effectively . Those reforms relate to decision-making , transparency , accountability , monitoring , evaluation , and the ' best bang for the buck ' , but sentiments in India are stirred only by the UNSC discussion . <p> Until 1993 , discussions about security council expansion were about non-permanent membership , which rests , since 1965 , at 10 . In the post-Cold War period , candidates started to put themselves forward for permanent membership to add to the existing permanent five , and 11 years ago , Japan , Brazil , Germany and India constituted a Group of Four to promote their claim . Any change will call for an amendment of the charter , which means the basic structure of the UN . This procedure will need the approval of 2/3rds of the UN membership , the approval of all the P-5 , and the ratification by due constitutional process of all UN members . The charter has been amended only a handful of times in its 70-year history , the last time in 1973 . Therefore , it is not just a matter of India getting a majority , or even a big majority , of the 193 members of the UN to support its case . The expansion of permanent members will have to be a package deal , and will have to comprise the number of non-permanent members as well and the question of the veto power . <p> It is because the issue is so complicated that it has been postponed from year to year . Initially the P-5 were inclined to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ contributions , along with a small increase in non-permanent membership . But this was not acceptable , especially to those who opposed any expansion on the unspoken basis that they would not be eligible under any criterion . The criteria of a new deal have been discussed , but they sometimes lead to unexpected results . If the size of a country is paramount , Canada should be a permanent member and Australia , Argentina and Kazakhstan strong candidates . If population by density , then Singapore , Malta , Bahrain , Maldives and Bangladesh would qualify . If contribution to peacekeeping , Bangladesh , Pakistan , Ethiopia , Rwanda and Nepal will rank high . If Germany is included , that will make as many as four European countries including Russia . If Germany and Japan are both taken in , they are the two losers in the last World War , leaving Italy with a claim . This borders on the facetious but these are among the arguments made . As for veto power , the P-5 stands to lose if the new permanent members have this power and the G-4 has let it be known that it will not insist on this for 15 years . <p> The important Western countries that offer bilateral support to India will do nothing to move the debate forward , because they know that on issues like human rights , sanctions , humanitarian intervention and regime change , developing countries will not be sympathetic to their position . The US is also under pressure from the Congress that aspirants must prove their credentials on non-proliferation , climate change and human rights . On a possible package deal , the situation is equally opaque . China is opposed to Japan for historical reasons and is lukewarm about India . Pakistan is aggressively opposed to India . Russia is seemingly of late opposed to Germany and Japan . If Nigeria or South Africa prevails , the Arabs will want an additional seat of their own . Twenty-three per cent of the world population is Muslim , and Indonesia would merit a claim as the biggest Muslim country . <p> Much is made in India of an unanimous general assembly resolution last September on the " Question of equitable representation on and increase in the membership of the Security Council " . This sets the stage to begin text-based negotiation , but this is only the start of a long process with no terminal date . Our ambassador pronounced this to be " historic and path breaking ... all of us are celebrating the fact that we can talk ... with an actual piece of paper . " Prime Minister Narendra Modi was less exuberant but noted " some movement " and " a significant step . " Unanimity in the UN does not mean that everyone agrees . The text to which our ambassador refers is of 25 pages in two parts ; one containing the collated views of some member @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ other members , including the United States of America , Russia and China . It therefore comprises diverse views from the G-4 , the G-69 ( 41 members ) of developing countries and the African group , which wants two permanent seats . The Uniting for Consensus group of 25 members rejected both the text and the process , calling the resolution a technical and non-substantive step . In other words , opinions were as far apart as ever and as of now there is no formula that commands the support needed . The positions of China , Russia and the US cast a gloom on the G-4 . If this resolution has served a purpose it was to create clarity as to who was on what side . <p> The G-4 have set out their arguments and may need to consider more compromises . They have already conceded on the veto ; perhaps they may agree to something less than permanent membership , such as extended non-permanent terms subject to five-year re-election . Germany may come under pressure to withdraw due to over-representation of Europe . Kofi Annan 's 2005 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ seat might be dusted off and revived . That plan involves no change in the present P-5 . <p> To give the impression that permanent membership is the unique objective of Indian foreign policy does not enhance our prestige , and making support for India the crucial factor in bilateral friendship is untenable . Territorial disputes with China and Pakistan diminish our cause . Permanent membership without a veto is not so attractive that we should spend excessive time and money on it . We should adopt the position of being willing to serve when called upon . The argument that the security council must reflect the changes in the world is valid , but no one can predict when the time for that has arrived .
