<h> Avian evolution linked to climate change : Study <p> New York , Dec 12 ( IANS ) New research led by the American Museum of Natural History reveals that the evolution of modern birds was greatly shaped by the history of our planet 's geography and climate . <p> The DNA-based work has found that birds arose in what is now South America around 90 million years ago , and radiated extensively around the time of the Cretaceous-Paleogene extinction event that killed off the non-avian dinosaurs . <p> The study said that birds in South America survived the extinction event and then started moving to other parts of the world via multiple land bridges while diversifying during periods of global cooling . <p> " Modern birds are the most diverse group of terrestrial vertebrates in terms of species richness and global distribution , but we still do n't fully understand their large-scale evolutionary history , " said Joel Cracraft , a curator in the museum 's department of ornithology and co-author of the study . <p> Cracraft and lead author Santiago Claramunt , a research associate in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for most modern bird families with information from 130 fossil birds to generate a new evolutionary time tree . <p> " With very few exceptions , fossils of modern birds have been found only after the Cretaceous-Paleogene ( K-Pg ) extinction , " said Claramunt . <p> " This has led some researchers to suggest that modern birds did n't start to diversify until after this event , when major competitors were gone .
