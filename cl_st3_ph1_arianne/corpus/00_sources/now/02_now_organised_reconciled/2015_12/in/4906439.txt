<h> Delhi December diary <p> - The season of smog , food and the arts in the capital <p> The Thin Edge - Ruchir Joshi <p> The weather app on my phone shows Delhi at an unseasonal , late-February-ish 24 degrees . Underneath the temperature , where it normally says ' Rain ' or ' Snow ' or ' Sun ' , for Delhi it simply says ' Smoke ' . Landing at T-3 , I do n't feel the jolt of pollution other people coming to Delhi complain about , but then I suppose I have Delhi hard-wired into my biology somewhere , and perhaps the programme automatically deals with the NCR 's foul environment . In any case , I 've returned here just as the ' season ' is reaching full speed and the many joys the Great Sprawl offers far outweigh minor concerns such as the lung-debilitating air quality . The ride from the airport to the place where I 'm staying is smooth by the city 's standards and soon I 'm enjoying the mild December sunlight on a south Delhi barsaati , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ long-sundered lovers at the local mithaiwalla 's - making their presence felt in my spice-deprived alimentary colonies . <p> The next day , I find the Calcuttan in me yet again being resentful of the variety and quality of entertainment Delhi so effortlessly provides . The newish jazz bar is full of cognoscenti and more than a few suspects from Cal . The reason for this is one of Calcutta 's most famous and talented guitarists is performing with a group he has put together . The joint has two floors so you can also look down from the top floor or , as on this standing room only evening , perch on the stairs . The guitar man is one of the most proficient and pahauncha hua our city has produced but he left our little backwater and settled elsewhere many years ago . Today , many of us in the crowd are from Calcutta and yelling our appreciation if not exactly in Bangla then certainly with Cal-inflected shouts and encouragements . <p> The sound-system is good , the abstract jazz-blues of high quality , the bar efficient at slinging @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ floor tables , the ones ignoring the music , are fully engrossed in coochy-coo , the audience attentive and happy , not excluding a baby belonging to one of the musicians , who has its head covered with outsized noise-cancelling headphones . All of it conspiring to make us ask ' why the roshogolla does n't Calcutta have anything that even vaguely approaches a club such as this ? ' <p> After the gig , we drive to a completely different galaxy of musical experience . In the centre of south Dilli is this massive dance club , several floors of it , and we wind our way to the top floor of the sound-factory . Here , with big openings overlooking a park , is where the south Delhi crowd lets down its hair . As the DJ goes through her repertoire , slamming one Punjabi pop track after another ( interspersed occasionally by international dance hits like " Lean On " by the Jamaican trio , Major Lazer ) , paunchy middle-aged men in tight t-shirts dance with women who are far too young for them , while other @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ night , I find myself with a young posse in a popular , unpretentious bar in Paharganj , in central Delhi . The drinks are super cheap , the laser lights change every now and then , and everybody 's attention is focussed on the live band playing Bollywood hits . Mid-week , various bands with varying talents come to this stage and do their thing with great seriousness and with enthusiastic crowd participation . On this night , there are about seventy mostly young men and about six women , including three in our group . No one hassles anyone and there is understated security carried out by unthreatening but extremely firm staff . The band plays under a television screen showing a wrestling circus , the musicians separated from the audience by one of those airport barriers . It 's simple , it 's great fun , and it seems , dare one use the phrase , so normal . <p> A few days later , yet another evening with more than one thing to do , this time connected to art . First we go to the large @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in typical landlord-builder sacrifice . The man has lived and worked here for a decade but has now moved out to a new studio . However , since he has the place till the end of the year , he has decided to do low-cost collaborations with other artists and turned the apartment into an impromptu art space . This evening , we see the photo and performance video work of a Japanese avant-garde group from the 1980s . The booze is unpretentious , home-made vodka-infused plums and Old Monk rum , the food ordered in from the local pizza-walla and the videos truly entrancing , shot with early high-end video-cameras , the performers crawling and sliding across a smooth floor as two scanner-type rigs move above them at different heights , the changing constellation of light and sounds transforming their bodies and movements . <p> From there we shift to another friend 's terrace . This man is another escapee from Calcutta who has made good in the brutal arena of the cultural capital of India . Today , a photo exhibition by him is opening in one of the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ decided to do a mini parallel show on the terrace for our benefit . We mill about eating , drinking and talking , examining the series of photographs on the floor , each picture is of a different Paris streetside dustbin and its contents . The show in Paris is to mark the climate change summit and in a small way to challenge the vacillating heads of state who have gathered . Here , in this strangely warm December , we stand around divested of our sweaters and jackets , wondering whether global warming has put paid to the north Indian winter for good . <p> Outside , on the road , the winter traffic is relentless as ever , the cars knocking into each other in the veins and arteries of the city like millions of metallic blood cells , circulating ceaselessly , jamming , flooding , screaming past each other . The talk all over Delhi is of the experiment about to start in January : only cars with an even number as the last digit on the number plate to ply on one day and odd numbered cars @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to reduce the crazily massed traffic and the risk is that this will lead to even more chaos and all sorts of new corruption . On WhatsApp , the jokes abound : two neighbours who 've not talked to each for ten years suddenly get in touch , one has an even numbered car , the other an odd numbered one ; oh , my darling how will we now meet for our trysts in the centre of the city , your car is an odd number and mine is an even number . An environmentalist friend is scathing in kaancha Bangla : ' eita korey kissu hobey na ! They have to ban most private vehicles and subsidize electric cars . But they will never dare do that . ' Other friends are more generous to Arvind Kejriwal and his ministers - wealthy south Delhi may not be receiving the tender loving care it did under Sheila Aunty but the outlying poorer areas have benefited in many ways under this Delhi government . Not that that means my Eco-Wizard pal is optimistic in any way , different kinds of fresh hell await us in the near future . <p> In December-time Dilli , life carries on despite the smog . Music , art , performance and food of different kinds , all jostle with each other . Speaking of food , I do eat a couple of expensive meals , but a dosai-craving returnee like myself can OD on quality idli- dosa-upma in no less than four different southy joints in close proximity . Each night is a feast that does n't explode your wallet . Again , the food is just one marker of the different Delhis that exist side by side . In Calcutta , we also have the grub , but alas not all the other stuff .
