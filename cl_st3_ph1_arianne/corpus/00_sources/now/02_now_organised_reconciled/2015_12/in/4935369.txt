<h> Assam 's Border Farmers fenced In By Changing Climate Conditions <p> Dhubri , Assam : Golok Das , a farmer in Assam , is happy with the harvest produced by his main six-hectare farm . But he 'd love to sell another four-hectare plot , just half a kilometre away , even though it 's equally fertile . <p> Why ? It sits on the other side of a barbed-wire fence marking the Bangladesh border , and that means he ca n't irrigate it . <p> The fence was built in 1987 to prevent illegal migration from Bangladesh . It traces a line about 150 yards inside the actual border , since no treaty agreement allowed a fence to be built on the border itself . <p> Large tracts of land , including some villages , were left on the far side of the fence into Bangladesh . In the Golokganj sector of Dhubri district , more than 8,000 farmers struggle with a fence between their homes and their land .
