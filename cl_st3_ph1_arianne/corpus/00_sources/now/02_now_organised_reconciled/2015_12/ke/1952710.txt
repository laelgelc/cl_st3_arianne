<p> Farmers in Nakuru have been urged to embrace fast-maturing drought resistant crops to cushion themselves from hunger in the wake of adverse effects of climate change . <p> According to Peter Waweru , the chief executive officer of the Sustainable Practical Program for Africa ( SUPPA ) , the changing weather patterns are inconducive for long season crops that flourish in high levels of rainfall . <p> " Farmers should grow maize which matures in three months , " he said Wednesday while responding to queries on ways farmers can avoid suffering in hunger during the droughts and heavy rains . <p> " Kenya is among the countries feeling the effects of climate change with rainfall patterns becoming increasing unpredictable , " he added . <p> Waweru who has been training farmers in Nakuru on how to adapt to climate change said traditional methods of farming are no longer productive . <p> He said the continued use of the outdated agricultural practices exposes farmers to hunger and poor nutrition . <p> " We encourage farmers to adopt agroforestry in which they grow trees in their farms . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the carbon gases in the atmosphere , " he said . <p> Nakuru County government through the department of Agriculture has continuously been advocating for the adoption of the better methods of farming to increase productivity .
