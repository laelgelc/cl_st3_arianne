<h> Climate Change Accord And The Tale Of Two Cities <p> Pollution alert : Smog in Beijing last week . The city was placed on a three-day red alert and all schools , offices , pollution-spewing industries and construction projects closed down and all travelling was curtailed . <p> Delhi , December 14 : After exhausting 24-hour long negotiations in Paris over two weeks , representatives of 195 countries , including many Heads of State and Government , have signed a climate change accord aimed mainly at capping global warming for the future at two degree Celsius maximum . A very happy and smiling UN secretary general Ban Ki-moon said : " The agreement caps a seven-year effort to get the world to act collectively on climate change . " The host of the extraordinarily amicable and cooperative global meeting , French President Francois Hollande , enthused he " is proud of nearly 200 countries that have adopted the Paris agreement " . And US President Barack Obama said the agreement offers " the best chance to save the one planet we have " . Asking for a warming cap of 1.5 ? C , many called the 2 ? C agreement a sinister watering down of a target that should have been achieved . Whatever else can be said about the agreement , one thing is for sure , the debates about what has been achieved in Paris will keep going on far into the future . And , as the old saying goes " The proof of the pudding is ( will be ) in the eating " ie actual implementation of the accord . <p> On the very same day when , led by Paris , thousands of Champagne bottles , accompanied by generous servings of caviar and canape , were being opened and rapidly emptied in many national capitals all over the world , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ environmental pollution in the two cities were being recorded at 20 to 60 times the limits set by the World Health Organization for serious health hazards , mainly long-lasting respiratory diseases . That day , close to 50 million residents of the two metropolitans , including newborn and very young children whose developing lungs are the most vulnerable to air pollution and who are very likely to be long-term victims of that calamity , were breathing what can only be described as poison gas . Ironically , and ominously , those deadly air quality readings , with very minor variations , had been recorded in the two cities for weeks prior to the signing of the Paris climate change accord . <p> No one really knew what all life-threatening chemicals , in what quantities , were being spewed out into the atmosphere by industries of all types , decrepit transportation vehicles , construction equipment and other nefarious generators of global warming gases . Agricultural and other waste being burned in and around the two cities were creating heavy smoke , which was turning seasonal grey fog into yellow smog . There @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ while driving around in Delhi on December 13 and for many days prior . The ever-present smog enveloping the city had cut down visibility to about 50 feet . Without an air-conditioned car , it was impossible to get from one place to another , and without air-conditioned shelters it was impossible to sleep or do anything , as this writer experienced first-hand . From all available reports in the international and national press and other media , Delhi was being rated as the most polluted city in the world ; the situation was no different in Beijing on the same days . <p> What were and are the national governments in India and China , and the metropolitan administrations in Delhi and Beijing , doing to remedy the situation ? In both cities , all sorts of educational institutions , offices and non-essential services were being closed from day to day , and people , especially the young and old , were advised to stay indoors ( as if the air-quality inside was or could be any better than that outside ! ) and to cut down inter-city travel as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ week , the government very reluctantly had to come out of its environmental calamity denial stupor , declare a red-alert and close all schools , offices , pollution-spewing industries and construction projects , and curtail all traveling within and around the city . In Delhi , the response was very different and deplorable to say the least . The democratically elected national government and city administration in Delhi were admitting that the air-pollution was rather bad , advising people to stay indoors and curtail all traveling as much as possible . But , more than anything else they were discussing a myriad proposals for long-term improvements in air-quality while their people -- young and old -- were dying and falling seriously sick every moment and every day as reported by doctors and hospitals around the city . <p> Ironically , neither the centrally managed authoritarian administrations of China and Beijing nor the democratically elected diffuse administrations of India and Delhi have as yet seen it fit or necessary to even issue cost-free surgical masks to all their suffering citizens living in the two cities before doing anything else .
