<h> Ross Henderson : On climate change and Keytruda <p> OPINION : It was a great week for the government . They announced the signing of a massive international agreement on climate change -- trumpeting it as a huge step forward for the whole planet . <p> At the same time , it 's all business as usual here in New Zealand and we do n't need to do a thing . Which seems a bit convenient . <p> Looking at the fine print -- which even the Green Party and our local environment crews seem to have missed -- it seems that a lot of the most important things we need to do to address climate change , pledging to curb carbon emissions , are n't legally binding in any way . <p> When you do n't get punished for inaction , why would you take action ? <p> The good news is that our government has finally figured out that climate change is real -- or at least , enough voters know it 's real that they have to look like they 're doing something . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ headlines about this great Paris climate change deal , they probably think they 've done enough . <p> But climate change is real . And even if it 's not directly threatening you or I right now , it will . <p> We only have to look at our Pacific neighbours who will very quickly be looking for somewhere else to live with even a tiny rise in sea levels . We 're a hospitable country , but we do n't want hundreds or thousands of climate refugees desperately hammering at the door -- for their sake as well as ours . <p> I 'm not advocating any extreme greenie policies about shutting down every mine and oil rig immediately and eating nothing but tofu for the rest of our lives -- this would be chaos , pulling the plug on vital , often well-paid , jobs which sustain their communities . <p> But the oil and coal are n't going to last forever , and our planet needs us to find different ways of living which do n't involve cooking the atmosphere . We need a plan to ensure our @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have a future -- because climate change threatens all of that even if it is n't obvious to us now . <p> Unfortunately , now they 've got a bit of paper to wave around , I suspect our government will just keep on pretending everything 's hunky-dory . <p> While we 're talking about our government 's love of talking big to hide its lack of action , my comrade John Sargeant had a bit to say about Labour 's support for the melanoma drug Keytruda last week , usually , and probably like most of you I stopped reading much of what John has to share . However last week I made an exception . <p> His mathematical argument is sound on face value -- if you fund one thing , you ca n't fund another -- but that ignores the real point of Labour 's campaign . It 's not just about this one drug , which could save many of the hundreds of people killed by melanoma every year in New Zealand . <p> It 's about the way this government has chronically underfunded our health system @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have made a huge song and dance about increasing healthcare funding at more than the rate of inflation . It 's a bit of an improvement , sure . But it 's done nothing to make up for the effective funding cuts over their first five years in office , and it ignores the inescapable reality that our population is getting older . Our healthcare needs are increasing . <p> Labour has independent research showing that National has underfunded our healthcare system to the tune of $1.7 billion over the past six years , and short changed Pharmac by $115 million . <p> And let 's not forget that once we 're fully committed to the TPP , the costs of many medicines is only going to increase . <p> Pharmac must be allowed to make independent , evidence-based decisions about the medicines they purchase . But that independence is threatened when their funding is squeezed . Penny-pinching starts to take a higher priority . <p> Defenders of the government have no answer to that except the usual pap about tightening our belts and living within our means -- even though @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ I have to wonder if some of the criticism of Labour 's campaign is a sign of nervousness among the right . After all , championing extended treatment with Herceptin , the breast cancer drug , was a major plank of John Key 's victory in 2008 . National did n't just challenge Pharmac -- they bypassed the agency to fund Herceptin directly within weeks of being sworn in . <p> Maybe what 's good for the political goose should be good for the political gander .
