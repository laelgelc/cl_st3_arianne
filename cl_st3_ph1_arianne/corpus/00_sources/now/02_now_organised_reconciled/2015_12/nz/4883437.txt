<h> Dunedin needs Dutch answers <p> A Dutch solution could well be needed for South Dunedin , writes Gill Caradoc-Davies . <p> The articles and maps in the ODT on November 20 and 21 about South Dunedin risks with sea-level rise made riveting and scary reading . <p> Even if the Paris talks exceed our wildest hopes and there is a determined and concerted international effort to combat the effects of climate change , the rate of change will be too slow for South Dunedin . <p> This juggernaut of rising water can not turn round in a hurry , and the winter 's floods suggest we need to start addressing the problem right now , because implementing a response might take more than a decade . <p> Think of the Christchurch rebuild . <p> Under threat are not only two or more high schools , one intermediate school , six primary schools and at least five , maybe more , child care centres , essential for returning-to-work parents . <p> Three sports fields and a racecourse at least are threatened directly , and four sports parks indirectly @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for the relocation of housing or essential services . <p> The old CBD and the area around Andersons Bay Rd at risk of flooding have not only shops and businesses , but also banks , pharmacies , doctors ' rooms and community group areas , for example . <p> There is no point in waiting for a governmental response .
