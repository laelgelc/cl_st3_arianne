<h> Week in review : between the Rock and a hard case <p> Prime Minister John Key started his strange week in politics by welcoming National MP Judith Collins back in to Cabinet . <p> It was a week of comedy highs and lows , writes PHILIP MATTHEWS . <p> OPINION <p> All we hear is radio ga ga <p> As Fairfax 's Tracy Watkins wrote back in October , Prime Minister John Key 's everyman schtick is a big part of his unprecedented electoral success . Apparently a sizeable minority of New Zealanders like the jokiness , the willingness to present himself as someone who does n't take it all too seriously and his skill as , in the notorious words of The Office 's David Brent , a " chilled-out entertainer " . The premise is that urban liberals might gag on their lattes when he takes part in rape jokes on radio or ponytail-pulling in Parnell but the masses do n't mind . It was in that spirit that Key reached out to all the young dudes on the Rock 's " morning rumble " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ website , where " John Key joins Tom in his cage " is just a click away from " Tom cracks eggs with his bum to make an omelette " . But at the end of a year in which prison violence and sexual assaults have been so newsworthy , is it really that churlish to feel that this kind of stuff is demeaning to the status of the Prime Minister , and to the rest of us ? <p> Children of the poor <p> It is almost Christmas and in the view of this columnist , the finest Christmas movie ever is the 50s cult classic Night of the Hunter , which you seldom see on television . Robert Mitchum is the crim with " love " and " hate " tattooed on his fists ; Lillian Gish is the woman who looks after orphans and delivers a classic line about damaged and threatened children : " You 'd think the world would be ashamed to name such a day as Christmas for one of them and then go on in the same old way . " It was apt @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Children 's Commissioner Russell Wills released the Child Poverty Monitor report which revealed that 29 per cent of New Zealand children lived with income poverty . Close to a third , basically . " We lack compassion in this country , " said the Wellington GP and public health spokeswoman Nikki Turner . " We can not see it from the view of the person who 's living in poverty . " <p> We 'll always have Paris <p> Okay , five more years of messing around and then we have to get serious . The United Nations climate change conference agreement will require 187 countries to really knuckle down and sort out climate change from 2020 onwards . And hopefully science will come up with something amazing by then and we will be off the hook . But to be fair , even reaching agreement on the 2020 target was a major tactical achievement after long days and nights of talks in Paris . <p> I do n't think there 's one word that can describe a man 's life <p> In journalism , we do this perilous thing in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , often in artificial situations , and pretend we can then explain them to the world based on that one meeting . I met Jon Gadsby a couple of times in such contexts : once with David McPhail and AK Grant , to talk about their series Letter to Blanchy , and once by himself . The second meeting was just last year . At both times the looser , easier small talk was more revealing and fun than the formal interview that could be reported on , especially as Gadsby was dreading questions about alcoholism , which he answered with honesty and empathy . My key memory is that he told very funny stories from a few decades in the insecure world of New Zealand entertainment . I only wish we had heard more of them .
