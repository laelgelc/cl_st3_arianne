<h> Largest patch of blue on the globe <p> It 's sometimes easy to forget in the suburbs of Dunedin that we are all adhered to the shaky side of a remote , isolated island chain , a spiky anchor at the far southern heel of the archipelagoes of Polynesia . <p> We are inhabitants less of the land than of the great bowl of the Pacific , whose blue orb is the largest single feature of a planet somewhat inaccurately named Earth . <p> Simon Winchester has not attempted an encyclopaedic approach to his work , for obvious reasons . <p> The Pacific Ocean contains multitudes . <p> He has wisely ( and entirely successfully ) collected , or perhaps curated , a series of snapshots of the intersection of humanity with the ecology and geography we make our lives within . <p> What made it most rewarding was the author 's dry humour , and sense of the absurd , leavened by a great sympathy for the human-scale trials and tribulations of those who have lived on and around that other vast ocean . <p> Could @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ he has . <p> Equally compelling and a worthy companion volume , Pacific is structured around a series of loosely connected chapters , dealing with events starting from January 1 , 1950 ( the reason for this can be found in the book ) . <p> Each chapter deals with an aspect of his topic , including nuclear testing and its dire effects , the genesis of Japanese consumer electronics , a history of surfing , the tensions on the Korean Peninsula , the decolonisation of Southeast Asia , the Pacific weather systems and climate change , Australia 's politics and society , volcanic vents in the abyssal deep , the Great Barrier Reef , and the militarisation of the Pacific by the United States and its new rival China . <p> In each of these sections , the author develops his themes in a discursive way , delving back into history , mulling on the future , diverting down curious byways . <p> He is just as adept at providing a potted explanation of El Nino as recounting the cultural politics behind the development of the Sydney Opera House . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and the author brings the eye of a novelist to his narrative . <p> It would be a strange person who would not be moved and inspired by the grand picture he has created . <p> Above all , what comes through is the sense of wonder , and love and respect , for the Pacific Ocean , its ecology , and its diverse peoples : Asian , Pacific , European , indigenous and colonial . <p> As he notes , this is an " Ocean of the Future ' ' . <p> And we are very much part of it . <p> * Victor Billot is
