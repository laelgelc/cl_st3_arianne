<h> POINT OF VIEW : Public-private effort best approach for climate change <p> I have long felt there was something going on with the climate , but have never been convinced that it was as catastrophic as climate changers argued . A recent Pew Research Poll showed that only 45 percent of Americans attribute global warming to human activity . <p> Conservatives in particular have been regarded as opponents of climate change . However , a recent poll by the Yale University Project on Climate Change found that more than half of GOP poll participants feel there is global warming and something should be done about it . <p> Earlier this year , the American Petroleum Institute , long a stoker of skepticism about climate change , acknowledged the threat of global warming . It would appear that the era of denial of basic climate change is coming to a close . <p> Up to this point , I am on board . However , as a conservative , I would much prefer to be thought of as someone who wants to take a more measured and practical @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ than see the government involved through the initiation of an abundance of regulations , I would like to see more of a partnership between private financial markets and government to fund new technologies -- combining public funding with private financing to control emissions and to encourage green concepts . This would provide a reasonable , balanced and sustainable approach to accomplish environmental objectives . <p> One such idea has been put forth by the Global Apollo Program . This project would be the worldwide equivalent of putting a man on the moon . Within 10 years , it would cut the cost of clean electricity to below that of electricity from coal . <p> The 12 largest economies , including the U.S. , China and the European Union , would participate . Business-people would oversee the consortium 's progress . Each country pays its own research efforts . Results would be made public , but patents protected .
