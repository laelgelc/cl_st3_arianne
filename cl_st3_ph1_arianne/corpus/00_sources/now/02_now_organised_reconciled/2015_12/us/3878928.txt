Nepal Slides <p> We can be grateful that the planet spared us any tremors of magnitude 9.0 or greater , the so-called megathrust earthquakes that result from subduction , when one tectonic plate moves violently beneath another . As it was , a magnitude-8.3 subduction quake off the coast of Chile , in September , registered as the year 's largest but caused remarkably few confirmed deaths ( thirteen ) . April 's devastating quake in Nepal , by contrast , killed nearly nine thousand people , even though it was less intense . This disparity underscores how capricious seismic energy can be , amplified not only by unstable bedrock but also frail economies . <p> Nepal 's extreme topography was certainly a primary reason for the destructiveness of the earthquake , which shoved a seventy-five-mile by thirty-five-mile block of crust about ten feet southward . This spring , in the seconds after the fault slipped beneath Nepal , the resulting reverberations shook loose colossal volumes of rock in landslides . The scale of these avalanches , which surprised even geologists who study past cataclysms , lends credence to an emerging theory -- that in mountainous areas , episodic landslides are the single most important mechanism of erosion , while rivers ( previously thought to be the prime movers ) just tidy up after them in the intervening decades and centuries . In other words , these landscapes may be shaped less by long periods of boredom than by short periods of terror . <p> 2 . In two separate instances , each lasting several days , seismometers on the ocean floor picked up very-low-frequency signals migrating along the upper part of this subduction zone . This is ominous because a series of near-surface slow-slip events is now known to have preceded the calamitous Tohoku earthquake , in 2011 . Chesapeake Bay , Too <p> California 's depressed state is a recent affliction , but the Mid-Atlantic Region , and in particular the Chesapeake Bay area , has been suffering from chronic subsidence , undiagnosed until this year . Back then , the area lay just south of the glaciers ' edge . The weight of the ice was great enough to cause Earth 's upper mantle to flow out from under it , warping the land into what geophysicists call a forebulge , akin to the area around a person on a water bed . Now , twenty thousand years after the ice melted , the dislocated mantle is still moving back into place ; areas that were once frozen over are slowly rising and the outboard bulges are collapsing . China Summons Islands from the Sea <p> Apparently undaunted by the prospect of inexorable sea-level rise , the Chinese are continuing a long tradition of bold geographic reconfiguration by scraping sediment from the floor of the South China Sea to create new land in the remote Spratly Archipelago . Uninhabited and long avoided by mariners because of its perilous reefs and shifting cays , the island group is scattered over more than a hundred and sixty thousand square miles of water . Its land area amounts to barely two square miles in total , at low tide . The islands ' greatest natural resource , apart from their fabulously biodiverse coral colonies and fishing banks , may be multi-millennium accumulations of guano . Nevertheless , six Asian nations lay aggressive claim to various shoals and atolls , and China is determined to maximize its acreage . <p> Aerial views of the dredging ships , with their hoses and bladders , resemble magnified images of protozoan parasites , which seems apt , given how much of the sediment is coming from living reefs . We can be reasonably confident that Earth will , as Darwin wrote , keep " cycling on according to the fixed law of gravity , " hurtling and pirouetting through space , its plates sliding and sometimes lurching , its mountains shaking themselves free of accumulated clutter , its mantle slowly repairing old dents . And we can expect that our Earth-moving interventions , intentional or otherwise , will not go unnoticed . Human challenges to geologic law end , inevitably , in unexpected forms of justice .
