<h> Most models overestimate how much climate change will increase rain and other precipitation <p> UCLA researchers have discovered a way to reduce uncertainty in global climate models ' projections of how climate change will alter rain and other precipitation . <p> Global climate models agree that climate change will cause precipitation to increase on average , with wet regions becoming wetter and dry areas getting drier . However , according to the findings published today in Nature , most models overestimate the amount that precipitation will increase due to climate change . The study , part of a joint project by UCLA and the Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory , shows that the global average of precipitation will go up , but by approximately 40 percent less than global models currently predict . <p> Not only will the research make the climate models more accurate , it will also decrease variation among the different models by an estimated 35 percent , according to the study .
