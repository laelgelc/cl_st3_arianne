<h> Renewables Grew to 15.5% of US Electricity Capacity in 2014 <p> By Monica Heger <p> Posted 10 Dec 2015 17:46 GMT <p> Illustration : Getty Images <p> Renewable electricity capacity reached 15.5% of the total installed electricity capacity in the US by the end of 2014 , according to a report by the National Renewable Energy Laboratory . Installed capacity exceeded 179 gigawatts , generated 554 terawatt-hours . <p> The NREL team found that hydropower made up the vast majority of renewable electricity generation in 2014 , followed by wind . <p> Although solar generation still made up a small mix of the renewables , at 6% , it accounted for 22% of total electricity capacity added in 2014 . <p> Solar was one of the fastest-growing renewables during 2014 , with California setting a record for solar power generation in March 2014 and nearly doubling its solar production in less than one year . <p> Renewables as a whole made up more than half of total electricity capacity added in 2014 , with natural gas accounting for 47% of addition , and coal 1% . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ from 98 GW to more than 179 GW in 2014 . Even when hydropower is not included , renewable electricity generation has more than doubled since 2004 . <p> With states implementing targets for renewables , such as California 's goal of hitting 50% renewable electricity by 2030 and Hawaii 's goal of 100% , as well global negotiations around climate change taking place this month Paris , renewables are likely to continue to grow . <p> In fact , according to the NREL report , investments in renewables grew in 2014 after two years of decline . In the US , investment in clean energy grew 11% to $40.8 billion in 2014 , with wind and solar the biggest beneficiaries of those investments . <p> Of course there are still a number of factors pushing back on renewables ' growth . The price of oil is falling and is predicted to stay low through 2016 , utilities have started pushing back against the proliferation of rooftop solar by proposing transmission usage fees on customers , and of course the US 's future political environment is uncertain .
