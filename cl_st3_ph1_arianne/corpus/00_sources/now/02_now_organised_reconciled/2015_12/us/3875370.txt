<p> Should the state be spending more to protect Connecticut 's forest lands ? <p> Connecticut is losing many of its forest song birds , a new report by the Connecticut Audubon Society warns , and the reasons why they are disappearing should worry anyone concerned about climate change and the environment . <p> The " Connecticut State of the Birds " report released today calls for a major and permanent increase in state funding for forest and open space preservation , not only to save habitat for woodland birds but also to help combat global warming . <p> The report 's experts say an average of 2,400 acres of Connecticut forest is being consumed by development every year , and that means the loss of tens of thousands of trees able to store carbon , help ease the flooding that comes with major storms , and protect our drinking water . <p> Included in the report are statistics from the Council on Environmental Quality that state government since 2005 has purchased or protected an average of only 580 acres of woodland a year . Other backyard favorites , such as the Black-capped Chickadees , may also eventually be pushed out of Connecticut by warming temperatures to cooler regions farther north , according to the society 's experts . <p> The primary focus of this week 's State of the Birds report is on the need to preserve Connecticut 's large forest tracts .
