<h> Bones of contention <p> In 2007 , Science published protein sequences from a 68-million-year-old dinosaur femur fragment . The bone came from a Tyrannosaurus rex -- its name , ' king of the tyrant lizards ' , recognising its one-time status as the largest carnivorous dinosaurs known . The paper , from palaeontologist Mary Schweitzer at North Carolina State University in the US and colleagues reported that amino acid sequences from dinosaur collagen found in the bone closely matched those found in modern chickens . The evolutionary link between birds and dinosaurs was not unexpected , but the results were still met with deep scepticism -- could proteins really survive this long ? <p> Although not the first report of ancient protein sequences , Schweitzer 's protein was 100 times older than anything else reported at that time . Within the next 18 months , two rebuttals were published . Critics focused on the quality and statistical analysis of the mass spectrometry data used to identify the protein sequences .
