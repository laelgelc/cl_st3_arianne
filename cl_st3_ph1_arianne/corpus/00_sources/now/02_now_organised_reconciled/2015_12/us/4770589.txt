<p> Fossil fuels ' days are numbered . That 's a good thing given the toll that burning coal and oil takes on our climate and health . But especially for the people of Beijing -- and Delhi and Tehran , among other cities currently choking on dirty emissions -- that final goodbye ca n't come soon enough . <p> The images are hard to miss and harder to ignore : women , men , children and even pets enveloped in an almost tangible haze , forced to wear face masks as they go about their daily lives -- bicycling , shopping , getting married . <p> The statistics are equally alarming : More than 4,000 Chinese die daily from air pollution . And that figure may even be an underestimate , as pollution levels have risen since researchers crunched those numbers earlier this year . <p> A tragedy is indeed unfolding , and threatening to escalate . <p> On multiple days this December , the air in Beijing measured at least 20 times dirtier than what the World Health Organization deems safe to breathe .
