<h> Climate <h> Opinion : White winters another victim of climate change <p> With record-breaking temperatures around the world this winter , DW 's Sonya Diehn misses Germany 's white winters - and reflects on the absurdity of negotiating a global temperature . <p> I remember well my first winter in Germany : it was cold , so cold . Living in Leipzig at the time , I was astounded to see people skiing along the forest path on the stretch of park I typically biked through to get downtown . <p> My second German winter was also harsh . By then in Bonn , I established my own personal limit of 0 degrees Celsius for bike-riding . <p> Since I am originally from the desert of southern Arizona , German winters took some getting used to . <p> I was enchanted with how snow transformed the drab surroundings into a " winter wonderland . Globally , the winter before had been the warmest on record until this year . <p> And next year could continue to break records - because the planet is warming . Since pre-industrial times , average global temperature has increased by about a degree Celsius . <p> Not a good thing <p> Some people might point out that warmer winters - especially in central Europe - are a boon . Less people will die due to extreme cold , and the growing season is longer . Global warming looks likely to make grape cultivation - and thus wine production - possible in Scandinavia , for example . <p> Although there might be a few bright spots resulting from global warming , the big picture does not look good .
