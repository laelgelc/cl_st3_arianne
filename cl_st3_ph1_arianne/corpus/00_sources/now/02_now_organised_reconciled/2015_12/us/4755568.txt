<h> Cultural impact : 2015 momentous for the arts in Charleston , on cusp of new era <p> Charleston has for years enjoyed robust offerings of music , dance , visual art , literary events and entrepreneurial creativity compared with other cities of similar size . But 2015 took things to a new level . <p> Mostly that 's because the Gaillard Center opened after a three-year renovation project that cost about $150 million . What used to be an institutional-looking municipal auditorium built in 1968 was transformed into a neo-classical , multipurpose structure housing a new concert hall with fine acoustics and an elegant design . The color scheme and large blue light fixture in the ceiling might not be to everyone 's taste , but no one can deny the comfort of the 1,800-seat space , with its excellent sight lines and relatively intimate feel . <p> The city , which paid half the cost of the project , and the Gaillard organization hope that the hall and its adjacent exhibition and meeting spaces will improve Charleston 's reputation as a cultural center of the Southeast @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ now is the premier venue in the region for classical music concerts and other unamplified shows , and it competes with the North Charleston Performing Arts Center and , to some extent , the Charleston Music Hall for amplified performances of popular music , Broadway-style productions and more . <p> The Gaillard presents artists on tour and it accommodates local groups who book the stage , such as the Charleston Jazz Orchestra and the Charleston Symphony Orchestra . <p> The CSO has been on an upward trajectory ever since its near-collapse in 2010 . This season , its first in the Gaillard , it welcomed new music director Ken Lam , who is determined to raise the bar still higher . <p> Lam , a former attorney , has been committed to music-making for a decade , during which he has gone from apprentice status to a position leading an important orchestra . Some of his success is due to fortuitous timing and relationship-building , to be sure , but most of it is attributable to a fiery talent impossible to miss . <p> Lam has said he will push the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to a wider variety of repertoire and presenting ambitious programs that include the CSO Chorus , guest soloists and other performers . Together , these signal a new era in the performing arts in Charleston . <h> Charleston jazz <p> In June , Leah Suarez stepped down as executive director of Jazz Artists of Charleston . Suarez had co-founded the organization in 2007 with the late Jack McCray , a beloved local jazz connoisseur and impresario .
