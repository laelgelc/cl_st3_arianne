<h> e360 digest <h> Paris COP21 : An Unexpected Move Toward Global Target of 1.5 Degrees <h> 10 Dec 2015 <p> It is the big surprise of the Paris talks : the growing acceptance of a call from small nations most vulnerable to climate change for the conference to declare warming should be halted at 1.5 degrees Celsius . <p> Even a few months ago , this seemed unimaginable . Two degrees was the only target on the table . But here it has gained momentum with more than 100 nations , including the United States and the European Union , agreeing that it should be in the final agreement . With more than a day of talks remaining , inclusion is far from a done deal . But it has strong support . <p> " 1.5 is alive , " said Monica Araya , a delighted policy analyst from Costa Rica , on Wednesday night . <p> Scientist Johan Rockstr ? m said in Paris that a 1.5-degree target " looks much more scientifically justifiable . Scientists here now say that 1.5 makes more sense for the planet . <p> One of them is Johan Rockstr ? m , director of the Stockholm Resilience Institute and pioneer in the science of establishing safe " boundaries " for human activity on the planet . " It could also trigger potentially dangerous feedbacks that accelerate warming by melting Arctic ice , parching rainforests , and releasing methane from tundra . Already washed by cyclones , they fear that two degrees would doom them , as warming gradually melts ice caps and pours ever more water into the oceans . <p> Michiel Schaeffer , of Climate Analytics , calculates that by 2300 , two degrees would deliver sea-level rise of 2.7 meters , enough to drown most atoll islands , while 1.5 degrees would limit the rise to 1.5 meters , enough to save many . <p> " It is great to see 1.5 degrees in the text , " says Myles Allen , a climate analyst at Oxford University . It would also encourage governments to " put in place policies that drive innovation and investment , " says Michael Jacobs , of the New Climate Economy Project and an advisor to the British prime minister Gordon Brown at the Copenhagen climate conference in 2009 . <p> That 's politics . But it is also science . The bottom line is that stabilizing temperatures at any level -- whether 1.5 or 2 or even 4 or 6 degrees -- will eventually require bringing the global economy to zero emissions . The sooner we get there , the less the warming . But without zero emissions , temperatures will keep on rising .
