<h> A platform of national reconciliation <p> By Joe Keefe <h> Friday <p> Feb 1 , 2019 at 3:15 AM <p> The 2020 presidential contest is under way and both announced and prospective candidates have begun visiting New Hampshire , host to the first-in-the-nation primary . As a former Chair of the New Hampshire Democratic Party long active in presidential politics , I will be looking to support a very different kind of candidate this time . I will be looking for a Great Unifier , and I believe the Democratic Party needs to offer a Platform of National Reconciliation . <p> This presidential election will be different from any in recent memory -- perhaps since the pre-Civil War election of 1860 -- because our nation is so deeply divided . One pundit recently described our national climate as a " cold civil war , " which strikes me as just about right . We have not only become a country of red states and blue states but increasingly one of red citizens and blue citizens . <p> Americans have been deeply divided before . Divisions plagued the thirteen colonies at the founding and abolishing slavery required a bloody @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Lincoln to carry the nation forward . Instead , we have the vile spectacle that is the Trump presidency , fanning the flames of disunity and division -- debasing minorities , attacking freedom of the press , undermining the rule of law , labeling inconvenient facts as " fake news , " pitting citizen against citizen , making war on truth itself . <p> We also have a Republican Party whose leadership has not only lost its backbone but seemingly its moral compass , tolerating and embracing the President 's aberrant behavior and dangerous repudiation of democratic norms . Despite Mr. Trump 's plain unfitness for office , opinion polls consistently reveal that the vast majority of Republicans and 35 precent to 40 percent of the public have become so partisan , so cynical , or have so lost faith in the democratic process that they are enthralled by an incompetent , narcissistic , authoritarian clown . This is an ominous development , but it is where Red America is today . <p> These extraordinary circumstances place a solemn responsibility on whomever the Democratic Party nominates as its standard bearer @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Donald Trump and the Republicans . Post Trump , the nation will need healing . If the Democratic Party fails to understand this , thinking it can simply push the pendulum in the opposite direction , substituting a more expansive , liberal Democratic agenda for the dangerous brand of ultra-right nationalism that the Republican Party has embraced , it will be making an historic mistake . We are too divided , too partisan , and need to step back from the brink . <p> I offer myself as Exhibit A. During my years active in Democratic politics I always had strong friendships with my Republican colleagues , including the two Republican chairs who served during my tenure as Democratic chair . When I lost a race for Congress against John E. Sununu in 1996 , commentators praised the race for its civility and lack of rancor . As a Democrat , I may not have voted for Warren Rudman , or Judd Gregg , or Gordon Humphrey , but I always regarded them as principled conservatives and genuinely decent human beings who would always act in good faith . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . It 's not just Donald Trump . If you could read my mind or hear my whispered curses when Mitch McConnell or Mike Pence appear on the TV screen , you would have a sense of how far I have travelled , and of how bitterly partisan things have become . I no longer accept their good faith . I fear they would sell the Constitution and the country down the river for partisan gain . <p> I 'm willing to admit that I have become part of the problem , but I 'm a relatively upbeat , tolerant person by nature ; many of my friends on the left are much angrier and more partisan than I am . <p> There is also a disturbing tendency in Blue America to write off all of Trump 's supporters as either stupid or racist . We forget that many Americans who voted for Trump also voted for Obama before him . In each election cycle , they voted for change because they are genuinely fed up with a system that truly is n't working for them . They are neither @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ -- and are understandably disdainful of smug elitists who view them as such . <p> That said , Red America does strike me as more close-minded , angrier and more bitterly partisan than is Blue America . There can be no doubt , for instance , that racism , xenophobia and other aberrances fuel its lunatic fringe . More concerning is that it 's not just the fringe : when the 2016 Republican Convention devolved into an angry mob chanting " Lock Her Up , " I was reminded of Europe in the 1930 's , and I feared for my country . <p> I believe we are witnessing two related trends that feed on one another : The first is distrust of and disdain toward the system , which thereby starts to lose its legitimacy as increasing numbers of citizens , bearing an anti-government animus that the Republican Party has recklessly fed for years , become more susceptible to the lure of an authoritarian demagogue ; the second is a growing distrust of and disdain toward one another . Our partisan divide has congealed into a deep cultural divide @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ election . It is not jobs or immigration , climate change or the opioid crisis . Until we have found a way to work together again , with good will toward our common future , we will not resolve any of these issues . <p> I am a liberal Democrat but if I were running for the Democratic nomination for president , I would announce on Day One that I intend to choose a Republican as my running mate , that I intend to offer key cabinet positions to respected Republicans , and that my presidency will be about healing and reconciliation -- about bringing the country together again , on a bi-partisan basis , to get things done . <p> Healing and reconciliation are not only what our politics need but what our system of government needs . All our great challenges -- again , from jobs to immigration , climate change to the opioid crisis -- require an easing of partisan gridlock so that politicians can return to the art of compromise , which is the only path to gaining majority support among the American public . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ if it is to recover from the debasement of Donald Trump to some semblance of its former self as a normal political party within a democratic system of government . <p> Most importantly , I believe healing and reconciliation are what the American people truly want , and that a Democratic presidential nominee who offers this will win -- and will be able to govern . <p> Given the perils facing our nation in the age of Trump , what we desperately need is a Platform of National Reconciliation , and the ideal presidential candidate is a Democrat who offers precisely that . <p> -- Joe Keefe is a former Chair of the New Hampshire Democratic Party . <h> Never miss a story <p> Choose the plan that 's right for you .
