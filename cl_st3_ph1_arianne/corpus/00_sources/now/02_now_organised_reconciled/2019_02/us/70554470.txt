<h> One man 's two-decade quest to suck greenhouse gas out of the sky <h> Klaus Lackner 's once wacky idea increasingly looks like an essential part of solving climate change . <p> The big metal container in Klaus Lackner 's lab does n't look like it could save the planet . It mostly closely resembles a dumpster -- which it sort of is . <p> As Lackner looks on , hands in the pockets of his pressed khakis , the machine begins to transform . Three mattress ? -shaped metal frames rise from the guts of the receptacle , unfolding like an accordion as they stretch toward the ceiling . <p> Each frame contains hundreds of white polymer strips filled with resins that bind with carbon dioxide molecules . The strips form a kind of sail , designed to snatch the greenhouse gas out of the air as wind blows through the contraption . <p> Crucially , that same material releases the carbon dioxide when wet . To make that happen , Lackner 's device retracts its frames into their container , which then fills with water .
