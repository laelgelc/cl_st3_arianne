What can ancient rocks tell us about 21st-century voting patterns , after all ? Well , a great deal , says Lewis Dartnell in this absorbing account of the planetary processes that have shaped humanity . <p> Consider the rocks laid down during the Carboniferous period 300m years ago when dying forests were transformed into the great coalfields of Northumberland , Yorkshire and Wales . Then compare a map of these areas with one outlining the 2017 UK general election results . The coalfields match exactly with large areas of Labour constituencies . " Support for the main leftwing political party in the UK almost perfectly matches regions of Carboniferous deposits , " says Dartnell . " Old geology is still reflected in people 's lives today . " <p> Labour 's union roots , in particular its links to the miners who hewed into those Carboniferous seams , explain this particular legacy . The underground tectonic plate on which Africa rests is moving north , sliding underneath the Eurasian plate on which Europe lies . " As a result , the southern Mediterranean coastline is relatively smooth and bereft of natural harbours because the African plate is being tipped down to be destroyed beneath the Eurasian plate , " says Dartnell . These have become sites of ports and harbours from which goods -- and ideas -- were traded , eventually producing the city states of ancient Greece and Rome .
