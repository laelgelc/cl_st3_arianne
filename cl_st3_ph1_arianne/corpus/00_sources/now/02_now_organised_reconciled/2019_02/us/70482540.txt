<p> Researchers from the United States and United Kingdom have developed a model of how rising temperatures will affect phytoplankton in the world 's oceans , which play a key role in how the waters appear , especially when viewed from above . <p> " There will be a noticeable difference in the color of 50 percent of the ocean by the end of the 21st century , " says MIT research scientist Stephanie Dutkiewicz , lead author of a study published Monday in Nature Communications . " It could be potentially quite serious . Different types of phytoplankton absorb light differently , and if climate change shifts one community of phytoplankton to another , that will also change the types of food webs they can support . " <p> If the model gets it right , the blue regions of the blue marble we live on will become an even deeper shade of blue as they shift to host less phytoplankton and other forms of life . This could include some of the waters far off the coasts of places like Florida , Mexico and Australia . " But you can see a significant , climate-related shift in some of these wavebands , in the signal being sent out to the satellites .
