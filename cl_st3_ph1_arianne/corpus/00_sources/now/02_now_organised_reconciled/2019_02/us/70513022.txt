<h> The poems of Edwina Trentham : A declaration of what matters <p> Hartford Courant <p> Feb 14 , 2019 6:01 AM <p> Poet Edwina Trentham reads her poem " When We Could Breathe . " <p> Poetry is at the center of Edwina Trentham 's life . For her , poetry is " refuge , healing , inspiration , history , a reminder that we have been through all this before -- suffering , joy , hope , despair . " She believes that preparing to write a poem is a political act , even if the poem is not overtly political , because " the act of writing poetry is , in and of itself , political , saying to the world , this is what matters , as much or more than fame , power , or getting and spending . " <p> Trentham , who lives in Moodus , attended the Bermuda High School for Girls , where the students read a great deal of poetry -- the English Romantic Poets mostly -- and they studied the King James version of the Bible , which is full of poetry . While there , she also founded the poetry journal Freshwater , which she edited for 15 years . She was a visiting instructor in the Graduate Liberal Studies Program at Wesleyan University for many years and has taught writing workshops at many different venues , including the Hill-Stead Museum , the Mark Twain House , the Clinton Art Gallery , and in many colleges and libraries . Her own poetry has been published in an array of journals and anthologies , and has won various awards . <p> Many of Trentham 's poems explore the way love and loss shape people 's lives . This difficult form is generally composed of a series of 15 sonnets , each one beginning with a line that is the same as the final line of the preceding sonnet , with the last sonnet in the sequence made up of the first lines of all 14 of the previous sonnets . She began working on this series when a voice in her head kept repeating , " There used to be these beautiful things called trees . " She imagined it as a voice from the future , telling a sort of bedtime story .
