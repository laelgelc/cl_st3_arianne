<h> Trump 's national emergency is a misuse of military funds <p> After six weeks of meandering negotiations and a lengthy government shutdown , President Trump announced a national emergency at the southern border . While this declaration may alleviate short-term obstacles to building the wall , it is self-defeating in every other way . <p> Damage military readiness ? Check . Allow future presidents to shun bipartisanship and the hard work of negotiating a deal to instead just call everything an " emergency " to get what they want ? Check . Complicate upcoming negotiations to avert almost $300 billion in sequestration spending cuts to the military ? Check . Any way you slice it , declaring an emergency and misusing military construction funding will prove remarkably short-sighted . <p> First , every military construction and family housing project with dollars diverted to the wall will stop immediately . The First Commandment of federal budget execution is " do n't spend money you do n't have " -- it 's literally illegal . Just yesterday , lawmakers heard from military families as they testified for hours about the disgusting state of family housing at bases across the country . Troops leave the military because of this . Plus , dozens of other projects like new training ranges and missile defense radar construction could also be affected . The administration might try to spread the pain around , but death by a thousand cuts is still a painfully slow death . <p> Second , numerous lawsuits will tie up this money , leaving the wall unbuilt in the meantime . While we all wait for the long legal process to play out -- remember the travel ban ? -- thousands of troops will remain stranded at the border instead of carrying out their training missions elsewhere .
