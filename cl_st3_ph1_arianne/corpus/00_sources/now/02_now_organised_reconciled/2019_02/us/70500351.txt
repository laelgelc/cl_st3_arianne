Portable and aesthetically pleasing , watercolors helped bring painters ' worlds to life . <p> Today , these works are precious and unwitting documents of a world altered by the ravages of climate change , overhunting , urbanization and other human activity . The Watercolour World , financed by the London-based Marandi Foundation , is a free online database of watercolors painted before 1900 . <p> It 's the brainchild of Fred Hohler , a former British diplomat devoted to art preservation . The nonprofit organization works with collectors around the world to find and digitize watercolors . It also is on the hunt for other pre-1900 watercolors that document animals , places , plants or events . So far , it has collected about 80,000 images . <p> The point is n't just to preserve them -- it 's to provide their documentary power to researchers . <p> " With the world at risk from climate change , rising sea levels , and worse , the project will provide scientists and environmentalists with an accurate visual account of much of the natural world as it used to be , " Hohler said in a statement . <p> That visual testimony can be strikingly different from today 's world . <p> For example , a search for " ice " shows chilly landscapes in the Hudson Strait , the Swiss Alps and Antarctica -- three places whose ice has waned with the warming climate .
