<h> Study shows survival rates of young salmon are in decline <p> Work is being undertaken on the river Erriff ( IFI 's National Salmonid Index Catchment ) as part of the Smoltrack project <p> A new study shows how salmon survival leaving freshwater is an important factor in declining European salmon populations , according to early results from a project examining early migration phase of salmon smolts from rivers across Europe . <p> The scientists discussed findings from the international Smoltrack project , the results of which reveal factors in the known decline of salmon stocks . <p> The project , which is funded by the North Atlantic Salmon Conservation Organisation , aims to help determine survival rates of young salmon as they move from rivers into the marine environment . <p> Work commenced in 2017 , and early results show that survival during movement from rivers to the sea is lower than expected . <p> While it was already accepted that salmon are impacted by a number of factors including effects of climate change , limited feeding opportunities and sea lice induced by fish farms , the low survival rate in river systems presenting in this research @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ smolts in some river systems could be attributed to changes in temperature and flow , while predators have also been shown to impact on numbers entering the sea . <p> Survival varies by catchment and , in a bad year , survival can be three times lower than in a good year . These results will be published in international journals on completion of the research . <h> Radio transmitter tags <p> The international project involves scientists from each participating country to tag salmon smolts with miniaturised acoustic and radio transmitter tags in rivers , and track their migration journey through lower parts of rivers , estuaries and coastal areas . <p> In Ireland , work is being undertaken on the river Erriff ( IFI 's National Salmonid Index Catchment ) and the river Bush ( Northern Ireland ) , river Tamar ( England ) , rivers Ulla and Minho ( Spain ) , rivers G ? ta and H ? gvads ? n ( Sweden ) and rivers Skjern and Storaa ( Denmark ) . <p> This information will help scientists to understand survival rates of salmon smolts during @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to warmer climates in Spain . <p> Already Smoltrack is providing new data on the initial migration of salmon smolts which will inform future management and conservation measures for this iconic species . <p> Dr Cathal Gallagher , IFI 's head of research and development , said : " We have seen a substantial reduction of salmon returning to Irish shores in recent decades .
