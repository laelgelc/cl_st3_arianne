File picture : Pixabay <p> Many South African politicians , economists and specialists in the energy sector are celebrating the news that a promising show of natural gas has been discovered in deep water south of Mossel Bay . It was found in an offshore prospecting area called Brulpadda ( Afrikaans for bullfrog ) , which is licensed to global energy giant Total . <p> The discovery , comes against the backdrop of rising fuel prices and an electricity utility in crisis , has raised hopes that it may be a game changer . The Conversation Africa 's Nontobeko Mtshali spoke to Robert Scholes and Rod Crompton about the significance of the find . <p> Is this an energy " game-changer " ? <p> It 's not yet clear how big the find is . In a press release , Total said it " could be around one billion barrels of global resources , gas and condensate light oil " . We simply wo n't know until more holes are drilled , and three-dimensional seismic surveys are completed . <p> Gas can be converted into liquid fuels . There are only a few gas-to-liquids refineries around the world . PetroSA , South Africa 's national oil company , built one in Mossel Bay in 1989 , which it still operates . It is the smallest refinery in the country . <p> This means the biggest benefit will probably be to that refinery . It has a capacity of about 40 000 barrels a day , and the Brulpadda find -- given its proximity -- could extend its life substantially . <p> How does this change the national energy strategy ? <p> The government 's energy policy and its Gas Utilisation Master Plan agree that South Africa could usefully increase the amount of natural gas in the mix . Switching them to natural gas could do this job more cheaply , more efficiently and with lower emissions , including of greenhouse gases . <p> So increasing the gas used increases the fraction of renewables which can be included in South Africa 's electricity mix , while still meeting a given electricity security and emissions target . <p> Will this gas be used in South Africa , or exported into the global market ? <p> It @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ chickens . It takes years to develop a gas-field to the point where it is producing gas . Many things can change in that period . The Brulpadda find is at great depth , both below the sea surface and below the seafloor . It will be challenging to develop in an area notorious for high winds and heavy seas . But the likelihood is that a modest-sized gas find on the South Coast would mostly be used in South Africa . <p> Compressing natural gas for long-distance export by sea is an expensive business . It needs major infrastructure , which South Africa currently does n't have . The country also does n't yet have a well-developed infrastructure for using gas , so the supply may initially be more than South Africa can consume . <p> But since there 's a captive market nearby , Total -- an international , for-profit company that will charge a market-related price for its gas -- will almost certainly first try to sell it locally , rather than incur the cost of transporting it elsewhere .
