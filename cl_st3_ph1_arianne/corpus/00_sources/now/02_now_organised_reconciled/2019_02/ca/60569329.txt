<p> I was asked by a reader how climate change will affect the future of wine . Rather than just give my impressions , I went back to a recording I have of the Tasting Climate Change conference that was held here in Montreal back in October 2017 . <p> Organized by Montreal sommelier Michelle Bouffard , the panel of experts presented a fascinating overview of the possible ramifications of our planet 's warming climate . A second edition will be held here on Nov. 12 , 2019 . <p> What I took out of the conference is that there is some good , but mostly bad news . I , of course , use " good " with the understanding that the wide-scale implications of climate change will be catastrophic for our planet . However , at least in the short term , many of the coolest growing regions have benefitted from warmer temperatures . <p> Gregory Jones , a specialist in climate change and a vineyard owner in Oregon , said that , " lots of wine is being produced at latitudes that 30 years ago we would never have dreamed . " Certain grapes , such as pinot noir , have a narrow climate niche that he said was an average temperature between 14-16 C. <p> While growing seasons that go above this envelope can produce good vintages , Jones believes that pinot 's tolerance for producing quality wine is limited to a two-degree rise in average temperatures . Jones has over 120 years of climate data on Oregon and 15 of the last 20 have been the warmest . Aside from an imbalanced ripening , wine cultivation puts even more stress onto water supply . <p> As cooler climate regions warm , they can be touched by new insects and diseases that previously could n't survive the winters . Also , warmer winters , especially in warmer growing areas , mean that the vines do not reach full dormancy , which causes stress and can affect the following year 's harvest . <p> While we all appreciate an early spring , it can be dangerous for wine grapes . In many areas such as Burgundy and the Loire Valley , warm soil temperatures too early in the season have caused the vines to start their growing season earlier .
