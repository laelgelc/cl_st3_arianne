<p> The long-term warming trend is reshaping how we garden , whether we like it or not . We do not like the overall trend , and support measures to curb this change , but we also accept that there is a certain level of warming which is at this point ' locked-in ' . <p> With the effects of climate change , we are more likely to experience hot summer drought and extreme cold conditions in winter that can kill many plant species which would need to adapt to our increasing hot and dry summers . <p> A dominant warming trend with the occasional cold snap . What does that mean for gardeners ? <p> Kevin Kavanagh is someone who has spent a lot of time thinking about this . After a career as a conservation biologist with both World Wildlife Fund Canada and Nature Conservancy of Canada , Kavanagh established South Coast Gardens in 2005 near the shore of Lake Erie in St William 's , Ontario . South Coast is a small nursery that specializes in native and rare species , some of which have historically been only marginally hardy in our climate . <p> In @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ climate change head-on , " I think we all can accept global climate change is happening . " <p> New Growing Zones <p> The opportunities he is referring to are those plants which we have n't historically considered hardy where we live based on the old USDA growing zone maps . Kavanagh explains , " There are published reports where they are looking at how the zones are shifting . In the past 30 years , zones have shifted northwards about 150-200 km . The new Canadian system is trying to match up with the USDA system , but for now you could try going a half-zone higher than what you 've tried in the past . In late autumn , place a screen of white fabric around plants without wrapping too tightly , a cheap set of white sheets will do . The white fabric is preferred as is deflects the winter sun which can burn plants while protecting against wind . <p> Create a microsite by planting simple barriers such as evergreens to the west and north of your home . Fencing works too , creating a protected area on the east side for planting tender varieties . <p> Mulchadequately , and water evergreens deeply in the fall . The freeze-thaw cycle can be particularly damaging with warm spells followed by cold snaps , something which is increasingly common . White plastic tree guards can prevent bark split or sunscald during early spring warm spells . <p> Experiment and take risks ! Experimentation is half the fun of gardening .
