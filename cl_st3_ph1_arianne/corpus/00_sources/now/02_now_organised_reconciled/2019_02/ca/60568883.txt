<h> Postdoc explores how the ocean moderates global climate <p> Research from Dalhousie Postdoctoral Fellow Erin Black provides insight into how carbon dioxide is affecting our world -- not in the sky , but in the sea . <p> " The ocean absorbs a significant amount of human-emitted carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) , " says Dr. Black . " Small critters in the ocean 's surface take in CO2 , just like we humans breathe in oxygen . This CO2 becomes part of their bodies in the form of organic carbon . I 'm trying to uncover where this carbon goes and how it is recycled . " <p> The International Postdoctoral Fellowship program -- a new initiative of the Ocean Frontier Institute ( OFI ) -- provides Dr. Black the opportunity to better understand how the ocean moderates our global climate . <h> Like ticking clocks <p> Dr. Black monitors organic carbon by tracking radioactive elements that occur widely in nature . She can determine how fast organic carbon travels from one part of the ocean to another because some of these elements like to stick to organic carbon and they also release pulses of energy , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Measuring how fast these clocks tick tells Dr. Black how conditions in a given area of the ocean are changing over time . " If we see ten counts of something over here and two counts over here , what does that mean ? It tells us about a process . " <p> One of the big questions is carbon sequestration -- how much CO2 is taken up by surface critters and how much of this carbon descends ( when they perish ) to the deep ocean , where it can remain for hundreds of years or more ? <p> To support this work , OFI 's postdoctoral program has paired Dr. Black with associate professor Stephanie Kienast of Dalhousie University , and research professor Bob Anderson , Lamont-Doherty Earth Observatory ( LEDO ) . Dr. Black sees huge value in the collaboration . <p> " You have three people that have specialties all working within the same vicinity so we can learn from each other . " <p> Drs .
