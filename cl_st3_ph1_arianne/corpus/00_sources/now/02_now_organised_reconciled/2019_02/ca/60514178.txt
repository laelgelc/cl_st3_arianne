<p> NEWS RELEASE UNIVERSITY OF GUELPH **25;2013;TOOLONG Healthier consumers , animals and ecosystems are the focus of three University of Guelph genomics research projects that received federal funding worth $1.5 million Monday . <p> The investment was announced Monday on campus by Kirsty Duncan , federal minister of science . In total , Duncan announced funding for 37 Genome Canada projects . <p> " Since taking office , our government has worked hard to return science and research to its rightful place . Monday announcement is another step in that direction , " Duncan said . <p> " Genomics research is driving innovation across many sectors , including health , forestry , agriculture , fisheries , mining , energy and the environment . These exceptional projects we are investing in today encourage strong research partnerships and will help our economy and communities thrive . " <p> U of G president Franco Vaccarino said that such funding support from governments and other partners is critical for university research . <p> " From plant and animal science to food science to human health and genetics , life science research at this University has already benefited people , animals and our planet @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ building on those strengths to help address important challenges , notably feeding the world while sustaining the planet . " <p> Prof. Bonnie Mallard , Department of Pathobiology , received $426,622 to adapt high immune response technology developed for dairy cattle to help improve beef cattle health and welfare . The technology earned Mallard a 2017 Governor General 's Award for Innovation . <p> She will work with the Semex Alliance , the Canadian Angus Association and the American Angus Association to adapt the test to combat bovine respiratory disease ( BRD ) in beef cattle . North American producers lose about $1 billion a year to BRD , the most common and costly disease of beef cattle raised on feedlots . <p> Integrative biology professor Mehrdad Hajibabaei aims to help protect Canada 's freshwater resources and biodiversity through improved genomic testing . He will use $840,000 from Genome Canada to validate environmental DNA meta-barcoding for ultimate use in monitoring the health of the country 's watersheds and rivers . <p> Canada is home to 20 per cent of the world 's freshwater supply , which is increasingly threatened by @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Working with Environment and Climate Change Canada and the World Wildlife Fund Canada , Hajibabaei aims to use DNA meta-barcoding to generate biodiversity data from watersheds across the country and monitor human impacts . <p> He is among the principals in the Centre for Biodiversity Genomics ( CBG ) , which uses genomics technologies such as DNA barcoding developed at U of G to identify species of living things planet-wide . <p> A third U of G project received $250,000 from Genome Canada 's 2017 Bioinformatics and Computational Biology Competition . <p> Under this project led by integrative biology professors Sarah Adamowicz and Paul Hebert , a team including U of G computing , informatics and statistics experts will create tools to help process huge volumes of DNA sequence data . <p> These bioinformatics tools will help in monitoring environmental health and managing natural resources , including identifying pests , invasive species and pathogens .
