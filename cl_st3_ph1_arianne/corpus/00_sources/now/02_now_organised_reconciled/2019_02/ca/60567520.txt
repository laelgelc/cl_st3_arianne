<p> Using dates of ice freeze and breakup , climate change could see thousands of lakes in the Northern Hemisphere eventually lose annual ice cover , says a York University biologist . Dr. Sapna Sharma led an international research team studying the effects of climate change on winter ice cover . <p> Researchers used data from more than 500 lakes across the Northern Hemisphere , including Lake Nipissing . They found 4.6 per cent of ice-covered lakes could see intermittent winter ice cover under 3 C of warming . The Paris Agreement 's central aim is to strengthen the global response to the threat of climate change by keeping a global temperature rise this century well below 2 C above pre-industrial levels . <p> " These long-term ice records allowed us to address how climate and variability differed before and after the start of the Industrial Revolution , " while asking if the ice dates reveal more rapid warming and if the warm extremes increase in frequency , says Sharma . <p> The focus of the inland lake research uses an interesting chronology for comparison . <p> Most studies of long-term climate have used quantitative , annual observations of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> In a rare find , the researchers found two inland waters having longer periods of observation -- Lake Suwa , Japan , with ice freeze dates from 1443 -- 2014 and Torne River , Finland , with ice breakup dates from 1693 -- 2013 .
