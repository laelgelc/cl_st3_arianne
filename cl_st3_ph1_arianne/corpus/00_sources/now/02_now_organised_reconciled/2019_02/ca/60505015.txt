<h> Nature Conservancy of Canada wants to protect Nova Scotia wetlands <p> Almost 15 per cent of the province 's total land area is comprised of freshwater wetlands that act as both a habitat for animals , and a protection for high-flood risk communities . <p> Feb. 2 was declared World Wetlands Day in 1971 as a way to raise awareness of the benefits of wetlands , and to champion their protection and conservation . <p> Wetlands have a number of ecological benefits . They store huge reservoirs of carbon . They are home to water fowl in the spring , and help feed them in the fall . <p> They 're also beneficial to recreation in the area , and provide Nova Scotians with places to play . <p> " The wetlands are really important , " said Andrew Holland with the Nature Conservancy of Canada . " They clean the water that us Maritimers enjoy at our beaches lakes and rivers . So wetlands protect wildlife , but they 're really important to all of us . " <p> Holland says wetlands are great at absorbing water , which is extremely helpful during periods of heavy rain @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and more of due to climate change . <p> In many ways , they act as a natural defensive barrier to flooding , which can wreak havoc on infrastructure . <p> " By preserving more wetlands and having a more strategic way of protecting nature , these can actually protect communities from some of these emerging weather patterns that we continue to see , " said Holland in an interview with NEWS 95.7 's Todd Veinotte . <p> " I think more and more communities are seeing the benefits of keeping wetlands in place so that they can help not only for recreational use and for wildlife and for birds but also for the benefit of our communities . " <p> The Nature Conservancy has been working on a number of wetland protection and nature conservation projects , including many in Halifax .
