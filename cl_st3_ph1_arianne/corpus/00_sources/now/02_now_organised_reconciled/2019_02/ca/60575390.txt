<h> CINDY DAY : Climate change fodder for farmers <p> Cindy Day had the pleasure of speaking to the New Brunswick Soil and Crop Improvement Association in Sussex , N.B. , Jan. 21. - Contributed <p> Thursday , Feb. 21 , might not have been the best day for a drive , but I was up before the crack of dawn and on the road as the snow started to fall . <p> I was headed for Sussex , N.B. I never turn down the opportunity to speak to farmers about the weather . Last week , members of the New Brunswick Soil Crop Improvement Association were holding their 40th annual general meeting and I had the pleasure of being their invited keynote speaker . <p> I should point out that yes , it " is " a thing . When I told a co-worker why I would not be in the office her reply was , " is that a thing ? " I thought it was odd , but how would she know ? <p> The soil and crop movement in New Brunswick began in 1977 through the formation of several local associations . The NBSCI boasts members from every corner of the province . <p> What do they do ? <p> They conduct educational seminars on a wide range of soil and crop topics . They promote new and innovative farming techniques like no-till trials on grain , soybeans and corn . They discuss pressing issues like waste water management . <p> That 's right , farmers do more than milk cows and cut hay . Farming has changed , technology has changed , and the agricultural industry is in step with it all . <p> Another change that 's having an impact on that industry is the weather . The climate is changing at an alarming rate and that is having a very real impact on the way our farmers feed the planet . <p> On Thursday , I presented scientific evidence of the warming , including : <p> Since the late 19th century , the planet 's average surface temperature has risen 0.9 . <p> The five warmest years on record have occurred since 2010 . <p> Global sea level rose about 20 cm @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ warmest January on record -- worldwide . <p> And then , farmers discussed anecdotal evidence that is also undisputable : spring droughts in the northeast , intense rainfall and frequent powerful wind storms are all signs of climate change . <p> New Brunswick has a total land mass of 7,344,000 hectares , of which approximately five per cent is considered farmland and only roughly two per cent is used for crop production .
