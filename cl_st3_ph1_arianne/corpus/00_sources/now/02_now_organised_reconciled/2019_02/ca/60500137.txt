<p> Having dropped one bombshell report this month warning that our food choices are destroying the world , the British medical journal the Lancet has now dropped a second that says we 're now in a perfect storm of undernutrition , obesity and climate change . <h> Announcements , Events &amp; more from Tyee and select partners <p> The Lancet calls it the " Global Syndemic . " The report 's authors say we 're facing three pandemics -- undernutrition , obesity and climate change -- that are interacting to form a " synergy of epidemics , " or syndemic , and it 's a global problem . <p> Here 's how it works . Undernourished children in low-income and middle-income countries suffer from stunting , which makes them smaller than well-nourished children . But it also makes them more susceptible to obesity ( which affects kids and adults in high-income countries even more ) . By 2015 , obesity was affecting two billion people around the world . They 've been set up to die of cardiovascular diseases , Type 2 diabetes and some cancers . <p> The report says , " Economic losses attributable to undernutrition are @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and Asia , or approximately $3.5 trillion annually . " Those climate effects , the report says , will end up costing five to 10 per cent of global GDP , while investing just one per cent of GDP could stop the increase in climate change . <p> The Lancet report is blunt about why we do so little , citing " the power of vested interests by commercial actors whose engagement in policy often constitutes a conflict of interest that is at odds with the public good and planetary health . " <p> For example , " The fossil fuel and food industries that are responsible for driving the Global Syndemic receive more than $5 trillion in annual subsidies from governments . Cutting subsidies to fossil fuel and the food industries would allow subsidies to sustainable energy and transportation as well as local food-production systems . <p> Health Canada 's recently updated food guide is largely in step with the Lancet report . But the meat and processed-food industries are fighting the changes , just as the fossil fuel industry has fought action on climate change . <p> To counter such lobbies , the report recommends " collective approaches to common challenges " by building new coalitions both nationally and internationally . <p> The report is vague on what " collective approaches " might be , but plenty of interest groups could form formidable coalitions : for example , teachers and parents ' groups could join forces with @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for better-fed , more active and fit youngsters .
