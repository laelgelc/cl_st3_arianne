<p> Black carbon originates from the combustion of fossil fuels , biofuel , and biomass . Its effect is especially dangerous in the Arctic . <p> According to the United States Environmental Protection Agency , black carbon deposits in the Arctic cover the snow , thwarting the Earth 's ability to reflect the warming rays of the Sun . At the same time , the deposits absorb heat speeding up the Arctic melting . <h> The main culprit <p> Now , a new study has found that fossil fuel combustion is the main contributor to black carbon . The research took five years to complete and raw data collected at five sites around the Arctic including in Alaska , Russia , Canada , Sweden , and Norway . <p> The researchers employed radiocarbon to determine fossil and biomass burning contributions to black carbon . What they found was that fossil fuel combustion was responsible for most of the black carbon in the Arctic , annually around 60 percent . <p> However , biomass burning became more significant in the summer .
