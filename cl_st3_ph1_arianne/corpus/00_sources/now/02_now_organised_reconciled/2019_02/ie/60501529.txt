<h> Shannon LNG : Are dolphins the key issue in this debate ? <p> February 1st 2019 <p> This week 's judicial review case of the planning application to construct a liquefied natural gas ( LNG ) terminal on the southern shore of the Shannon Estuary has raised many questions over whether such a terminal is appropriate in Ireland . <p> Both the political and environmental climate has changed considerably over the last decade , including Ireland 's obligations to reduce reliance on carbon-based fuels . <p> The potential negative impact on dolphins in the Shannon Estuary has been used by some as a justification to oppose planning permission . <p> In 1993 , the Irish Whale and Dolphin Group ( IWDG ) identified the Shannon Estuary as a home to a resident group of bottlenoses dolphins , providing a unique and extremely important habitat . <p> The Shannon population is considered genetically discrete and recent research by the IWDG estimates just 154 individuals occur in the population . <p> Impact on bottlenose dolphins <p> The IWDG contributed to the risk assessment of the proposed terminal on bottlenose dolphins and their habitat . The cumulative effect of a development , in combination with other current and potential activities , must be taken into account during assessments . <p> This is difficult to determine as a lot of assumptions are required as what is proposed compared to what actually goes ahead , as well as the scientific challenges of modelling cumulative impacts . <p> In the IWDG 's opinion , the proposed construction and operation of an LNG terminal will of course impact on the estuary . But will not have a " significant impact " on the dolphins and their habitat or compromise the conservation objectives of the Lower River Shannon Special Areas of Conservation ? <p> Significant impact is defined within the EIS process and guidance on what constitutes significant regarding protected sites is provided by the EU . <p> Will the proposed development enhance the habitat for , or improve the conservation status of , bottlenose dolphins in the estuary ? Not one development is significant but the cumulative effects of many could be ' death by a thousand cuts ' . <p> Although opponents to the construction of an LNG terminal will try and use the presence of a resident population of dolphins as an opportunity to undermine the development , this would be inappropriate . <p> There are much bigger issues to be discussed on a national level which should have profound implications for this and subsequent planning decisions in the estuary and elsewhere in Ireland . <p> Politicians and managers must weigh up all the pros and cons , costs and benefits @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Protecting nature conservation is essential , especially given recent stark warnings on the rate of biodiversity extinctions and is a legal obligation in the Shannon Estuary for a range of habitats and species . Failure to maintain their conservation status can result in huge fines . <p> Can we have economic development and maintain high nature conservation status in the Shannon Estuary and beyond ? Yes , but only in a spirit of respect and openness and with a much greater commitment to understanding the functioning and habitat requirements of our species and habitats . <p> We also need to ensure robust monitoring and surveillance is in place to ensure any deterioration in their conservation status is detected early enough to prevent any significant impacts .
