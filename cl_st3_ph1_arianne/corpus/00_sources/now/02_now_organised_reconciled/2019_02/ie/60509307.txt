<h> Offshore wind can blow many benefits our way <p> There has been cross-party support and agreement on Ireland 's response to the substantial threat posed by Brexit . <p> But it is disappointing there is no similar level of support and urgency on an issue that poses a much more significant long-term threat to our economy and society : climate change . <p> Ireland 's woeful record on climate change action is well documented . <p> It is a source of embarrassment and it is damaging our economy , society , and environment . The State is 90% off the 2020 target for reducing emissions and if on the current path there can be little prospect of meeting the 2030 targets . <p> Climate Change Minister Richard Bruton , has made promising noises since taking over the portfolio . A cross-government plan on climate action measures is due by the end of March . This has to be a game changer . <p> Meeting the climate action targets will require change in many areas -- for the economy and society . The highest average wind speeds in Europe are found off the west coast . <p> The Irish coast quickly reaches depths of 60 to 200 metres which facilitates floating turbines closer to shore and reduces the costs of installation and maintenance . <p> Off-shore wind energy is economically viable , with the cost of electricity generated from this source falling significantly in recent years . <p> An EU study predicted that electricity from offshore wind will be cheaper than generating electricity from gas . <p> As European governments commit to more offshore wind projects , the cost of electricity will continue to fall .
