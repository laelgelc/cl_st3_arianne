<h> The distraction of plastic pollution <h> What if our obsession with tackling plastic waste is just a convenient distraction from more serious environmental issues ? <p> Irresponsible disposal of plastic waste has become a major environmental issue , with plastic waste accumulating in large floating gyres in the ocean . The largest of them , the Great Pacific Garbage Patch , is as large as Texas . Image : Ricoh Carey / Shutterstock.com <p> Single use plastics are now high on the agenda -- with many people trying to do their bit to reduce usage . But what if all of this just provides a convenient distraction from some of the more serious environmental issues ? Consumers drive markets , and consumer choices will therefore create change in the industry . <p> Alternative products can often have different , but equally severe environmental problems . And the benefits of these small-scale consumer driven changes are often minor . So it 's not surprising that so many people think ocean plastics are the most serious environmental threat to the planet . But this is not the case . These changes could start to happen within the current century . <h> Lifestyle overhaul <p> This is not to distract from the fact that some significant steps have been taken to help the planet environmentally by reducing plastic waste . But it is important not to forget the need for large-scale systemic changes needed internationally to tackle all environmental concerns . This includes longer-term and more effective solutions to the plastic problem -- but also extending to more radical large-scale initiatives to reduce consumption , decarbonise economies and move beyond materialism as the basis for our well-being . <p> The focus needs to be on making the way we live more sustainable by questioning our overly consumerist lifestyles that are at the root of major challenges such as climate @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ choices -- such as buying our takeaway coffee in a reusable cup . And failure to do so could lead to massive systemic impacts to the Earth 's capacity to support life -- particularly the human race .
