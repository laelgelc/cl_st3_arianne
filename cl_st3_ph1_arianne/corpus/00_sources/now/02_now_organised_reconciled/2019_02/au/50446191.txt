<p> Congratulations to the Hunter Environmental Institute for securing Professor Will Steffen to speak in Newcastle this week . Steffen is a climate scientist of world renown , and his work has influenced national and international governments in planning their approach to the imminent warming of the planet . He was a member of the Australian Climate Commission until its dissolution in 2013 and is a member of the Climate Council . For me , his seminal work in looking at the pressures that humans put on the Earth at a global scale is outstandingly brilliant . We all understand there are boundaries to the demands we can make on the land or sea , and if we stray beyond these by using too much water or polluting too much , consequences follow . Steffen and his colleagues have designed a planetary boundary framework that provides a science-based analysis of the risk that each of the various human perturbations could destabilise the Earth at a planetary scale , and the limits of such changes that will still keep the world in equilibrium . This concept defines the environmental limits of climate change ; ocean acidification ; chemical pollution ; @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ; biodiversity loss ; air pollution ; and ozone layer depletion , within which humanity can safely operate . When we go above these ceilings for any of these then there is substantial risk of destabilising the state of the Earth in which modern societies have evolved . This bird 's eye view of possible futures for our world are of immense value to decision-makers in charting ways forward for the development of our societies on a global scale . Put in more general terms , there are two core boundaries -- " climate change and biosphere integrity , each of which has the potential on its own to drive the Earth system into a new state should they be substantially and persistently transgressed " . Steffen will conduct a seminar and workshops while he is in Newcastle -- a great opportunity to exchange ideas and experience with one of Australia 's leading climate scientists .
