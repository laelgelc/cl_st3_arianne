<h> Keeping prosperity in the ASEAN region through biodiversity conservation <p> The Visayan warty pig endemic to the Philippines . ( Photo by Ronnie Macdonald from Flickr.com ) <p> Cultures throughout the ASEAN Region , as well as other Asian countries , celebrate the Lunar New Year : in Viet Nam , it is called " Tet " while in China and other ASEAN Member States , the celebration is called " Chinese New Year " . This 2019 is also known as the " Year of the Pig " . On the occasion of the Lunar New Year , the ASEAN Centre for Biodiversity ( ACB ) joins the ASEAN Member States and China in observing this auspicious event . <p> According to the International Union for Conservation of Nature ( IUCN ) , the ASEAN region is home to 13 species of pigs , equivalent to an amazing 66 per cent of all the species of pigs in the world . Out of these 13 species , five are native to Indonesia and four are indigenous to the Philippines . One of the species , the Indo-Chinese Warty Pig has been assessed as extinct in the IUCN @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is critically endangered . The wild pig species is an excellent example of the wealth of biodiversity in the ASEAN region , which is home to more endemic birds and mammals than anywhere else on the planet . <p> The Chinese Lunar calendar incorporates the principle of harmony and balance of " Yin and Yang " -- a value that we hope to uphold in the region in conserving our nature 's riches and preventing biodiversity loss from threats such as over-exploitation , illegal logging and wildlife trafficking ; conversion and degradation of habitats for agricultural plantations ; and infrastructure development , pollution , and climate change . <p> May the Lunar New Year / Year of the Pig be a reminder of the need to protect the abundance of pigs and other biodiversity in the ASEAN region , which truly brings prosperity not just to ASEAN countries but to the rest of the world through food products , raw materials , and ecosystem services .
