<h> TRISH SEWE AND VICTORIA WANGUI : Marching to protect wildlife <p> Feb. 25 , 2019 , 12:30 am <p> By TRISH SEWE and VICTORIA WANGUI <p> March for wildlife <p> Every year hundreds of cities across the globe organise a Global March for Elephants , Rhinos and Lions . The event held in more than 130 countries brings thousands of people from various walks of life to advocate the protection of wildlife . Wildlife populations in Africa and around the world are declining rapidly due to threats such as habitat loss , human-wildlife conflict , poaching , climate change and unsustainable trophy hunting . The illegal trade in wildlife parts continues to deplete the species numbers . <p> In 2017 WildlifeDirect together with Kenya Wildlife Service and Stand Up Shout Out partnered with 30 organisations to plan the biggest march in the whole world , which generated global awareness about the plight of elephants , rhinos and lions . More than 2,000 people marched for 15km in Nairobi under the scorching heat . <p> Last year 's Global March was postponed to April 13 this year to coincide with the World Wildlife Conference . This year 's march is set to be even bigger and better and continues to advocate the strongest protections possible for endangered species that will possibly drive policy change at CoP18 . <p> Last year was one of the harshest for wildlife , and especially rhinos . In March the world lost the last known male Northern White Rhino and in July , 11 black rhinos in a botched translocation in Kenya . According to Save The Rhino , the black rhino population is estimated to be between 5,040 and 5,458 in the wild in Africa . Kenya 's black rhino population was at 696 as at the end of 2016 . Africa 's lion population has declined by 43 per cent , with an estimated 23,000 left , while pangolins remain the most trafficked mammal in the world . <p> This year 's march will advocate the closure of domestic markets , ban on trade in lion bones , pangolin scales , elephant ivory and rhino horn , canned lion hunting , live export of elephants and trophy imports . <p> Since 2013 , when the first Global March was held , it has generated immense global media attention and sparked public interest . This has brought to the limelight the trade in illegal wildlife parts , prompting governments to ban the trade in their countries . <p> China , one of the largest markets for ivory , banned domestic trade in ivory in early 2017 .
