<h> Struck by ' swarm ' of tremors , Palghar 's woes keep escalating <h> Last Updated : Thursday 21 February 2019 <p> What is more alarming than the repeated tremors that have rocked Maharashtra 's Palghar district is the discrepancy between the accounts of officials at various levels and other stakeholders . The Dahanu and Talasari talukas have undergone over a 1,000 " earthquakes " since November 2018 , data from National Centre for Seismology ( NCS ) shows . <p> Palghar provides the ' only available gap ' in this Western Ghats region , underscored by the fact that it is used by most trains from western India towards Mumbai . The tremors underline the weakness in this gap , environment expert V Subramanian said . <p> The former professor at Jawaharlal Nehru University , however , also clubbed the tremors with the " new normal " change in climatic conditions across the world , citing extreme weather events in Delhi , Assam and Kerala . <p> In fact , there is hardly any scientific explanation yet of the Palghar tremors from government sources . The lack of information is fuelling fear . <p> " It will @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ before it can be examined and any deduction can be made , " Subramanian said . <p> A string of reasons may be at play , including aggressive agriculture removing the protective layer of the soil faster than it can replenish itself . <p> " Like mountain ranges overground , which keep shifting , there are underground mountains 500 kilometres and more below the surface , " said Subramanian , who has taught geochemistry and climate change among other subjects . Movements in such ranges affect Earth . <p> The tremors in Palghar , registered as ' swarms ' , have raised fears of earthquakes among locals . " Abhi toh aadat si ho gai hai ( Now we have got used to it ) , " says Ramesh Surti , a waiter at a hotel near Amboli village . He claimed that there are three-four " earthquakes " every day accompanied with loud , clanging sound . <p> " such natural calamity ca n't be controlled but we have provided training , relief and rehabilitation , emergency services and held disaster management camps in affected villages , " a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , said . Most officials in the zone take a similar stance . <p> Villagers , however , quashed such claims of awareness drives , monetary compensations and rehabilitation measures like quake-resistant tents . Nothing has reached them , many of them told DTE . <p> " Sab bakwaas hai ( It 's all a lie ) , " Sheelu Patel of Amboli said about financial or material assistance by the district administration . " Only one family from our village was given a tent . How is that sufficient for 100 families ? " she asked . <p> Nowhere to go <p> On November 11 , 2018 , when the first quake was registered , 60-odd villagers of Amboli shelled out Rs 20 per head -- adult or child -- to take a truck out of the quake-hit zone to the safety of the distant Mahalaxmi ground where the villagers slept through the night . <p> The knee-jerk reactions to the tremors repeated over the next few days till they ran out of financial resources . " We decided to stay back in open spaces near our village . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ spend nearly Rs 100 or more every night to travel to Mahalaxmi grounds to sleep , " Sheelu said . <p> Her daughters refuse to spend the nights in their huts and instead sleep in the open on a make-shift bed of thatched grass , shivering in the cold . <p> Zilla Parishad school teacher Naresh Dauda and Jhadpi school teacher Magan Merya spend most afternoons together despite being from distant schools . Their respective schools , which usually go on through the day between 10 am and 5 pm , now open only between 7.20 am and 12.30 pm to accommodate the few students who show up . Most have dropped out though <p> The district administration claimed more than 1,200 tents were distributed in Dahanu and over 200 tents in Talasari . Though , not more than five tents were provided to any single pada , comprising nearly 100 families . <p> Efforts to find anyone who admitted to have received compensation did not succeed . Several villagers have bought tents on their own . <p> A series of tremors <p> The first tremor was felt by villagers @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 2018 , followed by a second distinct one on November 7 , 2018 -- the first day of Diwali . <p> However , official records show that a quake measuring 3.2 on the Richter scale , having a depth of 10 kilometre , occurred around 6.25 pm on November 11 , 2018 , at Palghar . <p> Further , tremors on December 25 , 2018 , led to cracks on the walls of houses . Followed by a modest-intensity earthquake measuring 3.2 on the Richters scale on January 1 , 2019 , that hit Jawhar Tehsil in Palghar district around 2.21 am . <p> Throughout February 2019 , tremors have been occurring with alacrity every day across Palghar as the district 's villagers fear an unthinkable escalation . <p> It is the epicentre -- Dhundalwadi village in Palghar district -- along with Haladpada , Dapchari , Amboli , Bahare and Wankas villages that bears the worst brunt of the tremor swarm . <p> An earthquake swarm is a phenomenon wherein repeat earthquakes of low magnitude , varying from 2.5 to 3.9 on the Richter scale , occur frequently . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a support to us .
