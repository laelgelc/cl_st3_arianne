<h> Kaani dialect on the verge of extinction <p> tn Feb 26 , 2019 , 04:38 IST <p> Kanyakumari : Global phenomenons such as climate change and urbanization are drawing the indigenous Kaani tribe living in the Western Ghats of Kanyakumari district away from their cultural roots , posing a threat to their unique dialect rich with its direct connect with the forest ecology . Forced to change their habitats , jobs and lifestyle the members of the indigenous tribe are moving away from their traditional way of life and it is showing in the way they communicate with each other . Their orally passed on dialect is on the verge of extinction . Chankaran , 70 , of Vellambi hamlet near Keeriparai said that less than 10% of the tribes in the 48 settlements can fluently speak Kaani pashai ( language ) . " Because of the cultural changes and the younger generation 's exposure to new languages , now Kaani is not used by many of them . The Kaani my 37-year-old son speaks is not as pure as mine and his children speak in a different way , " he said . <p> He observed that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in plantations . " My grandchildren 's language changed further when they left the hamlet to pursue higher education . They speak Kaani but with a mix of Tamil and English in which they are communicating with their peers . I do n't mind how they speak with peers , but I am sad about the mix while they talking to me , " he said . <p> The dilution of the language was apparent when the government tribal residential middle school , at Alamparai in Kanyakumari district put its students to test on International Mother Language Day ( February 21 ) . The Unesco Indigenous Local Knowledge ( ILK ) expert S Davidson Sargunam expert mentioned 50 commonly used words from Kaani pasha ( language ) to children from the tribe and asked them for their meanings . " I was shocked as they could tell the meanings of only 20 words . It is sad that a dialect rich in ethno-botany and ethno-biology with a major chunk of language directly referring to forest ecology , environment and lifestyle is fast vanishing , " Davidson told TOI . " Mingling with the world outside the forest , their dialect also gets mixed with Tamil and Malayalam , " Davidson said and observed that the tribes are also purposely avoiding the dialect as they feel inferior to others . <p> In their research article ' Indigenous Knowledge of Medicinal Plants Used for the Treatment of Skin Diseases by the Kaani Tribe of Kanyakumari District ' published in the International Journal of Pharmacy and Pharmaceutical Sciences @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the Center for Advanced Studies in Botany , University of Madras , and Davidson threw light into the aspect . Many medicinal plants have also started disappearing from the forests . Rapid deforestation , expansion of agricultural lands and destructive harvesting for commercial purposes are the main reasons for the disappearance of the medicinal plants . There is also a lack of interest from the youth to acquire the traditional knowledge from the ancestors . Some of their food varieties such as edible mushrooms and fresh water fish also face extinction due to climate change . <p> Intrusion of non-tribals in forest lands is also cited as a reason for the vanishing of the dialect .
