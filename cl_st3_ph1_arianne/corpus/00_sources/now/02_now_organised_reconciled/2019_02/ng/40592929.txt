<h> 2019 elections : No place for climate change <p> In less than nine days ' time , Nigerians will be going to the polls for the 2019 general election . The country is fired up with varied brands of election activities . As expected in an election year , the different parties and candidates in the elections are taking their campaigns to various sections of the society with firebrand style of promises about what they intend to do upon assuming offices . But a scrutiny of the rhetoric of most of the candidates leaves little doubt about how much the Nigerian political elite appreciates the enormous environmental and ecological problems which crisscross the country 's landscape as well as their related impacts . <p> Of particular concern are the main gladiators eyeing the central seats in the elections , the candidates of the All Progressives Congress , the Peoples Democratic Party , and the so-called third force candidates , the Allied Congress Party of Nigeria , Young Progressives Party , and others . Four of the media(ted) political debates and talk shows held in light of the presidential campaign are important indicators of how each candidate fared relative @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Presidential debate of January 13 , 2019 , the five candidates attempted to woo Nigerians with their words of power . Key issues that featured in the debate border on job creation , fuel subsidy , corruption , the economy , infrastructure and women participation . While the candidates of the two leading parties , the APC and the PDP took each other 's throat in exchanging blame about the failed past and offered promises of intended interventions in governance to turn around fortunes for Nigerians , the duo stopped short of addressing any environmental challenge that Nigeria faces . <p> A similar episode played out in the presidential debate which both the APC and the PDP candidates boycotted . The third force candidates who participated debated on equally key issues of job , economy , security and corruption without recourse to environmental/climate change concern . On the NTA-hosted The Candidates TV show , both the APC and the PDP presidential candidates had another chance to convince Nigerians why they should be trusted with the job of leading the country beyond 2019 . Below are some reasons why this failure is a big waterloo in this political season . <p> The Niger Delta region located in the South-South geopolitical zone of Nigeria is a hotbed environmental nightmare that should make up a campaign issue in an election year in Nigeria . Decades of oil exploration in this part of the country have transformed the once lush and vibrant habitat for human and diverse species into rusting earth and toxic water bodies threatening lives and ecosystems . In a 2018 study published in the Journal of Health and Pollution , it is estimated that " about 3.1 million barrels of crude oil enriched in manganese ( Mn ) , iron ( Fe ) , copper ( Cu ) , zinc ( Zn ) , lead ( Pb ) , nickel ( Ni ) , cobalt ( Co ) , cadmium ( Cd ) and chromium ( Cr ) were spilled from 1976 to 2014 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and possess life-threatening effect on health and the economy of the region . There are evidence that Nigeria is already having its own fair share in the climate change challenge . Recent years have witnessed erratic rainfall with uneven distributions across the country . The Northern front is growing desertification and the Lake Chad region is increasingly seizing to hold the bubbly life it once had . It is shocking that among those fronting to occupy our leadership seats , these issues seem not to matter . <p> The negligent treatment of the environmental/ecological concerns in this political season is a grave oversight . While some of the key issues being highlighted in the mainstream narratives are , no doubt , a serious concern for most Nigerians , their direct relationship with environment/climate change requires that any serious candidate should be expected to have some articulated positions on issues such as climate change , environmental clean-up , and disaster management plans . Most of the candidates emphasised creating jobs and growing the economy .
