<h> MP warns that climate change may lead to more wildfires <h> MP warns that climate change may lead to more wildfires Insurance Business <p> As insurers tally the costs of the damage wrought by the Tasman fires , some MPs are warning that a changing climate may lead to extended fire seasons in the coming years . <p> The recent fires in the Tasman district were among the largest in decades , ripping across 2,300 hectares and displacing around 3,000 people . Priyanca Radhakrishnan , Labour List MP based in Maungakiekie , praised the firefighters who fought to control the fire in what became the largest aerial firefight on record . <p> " These are men and women who are passionate about the important work they do , " Radhakrishnan told Stuff.co.nz . " They literally save lives and many of them do it voluntarily -- about 85% of FENZ Fire and Emergency New Zealand is made up of volunteer firefighters . " <p> However , Radhakrishnan tinged her praise with a warning : " The bad news is that such wildfires are likely to become more common . " <p> " Crown Research Institute SCION has been @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " said Radhakrishnan . " According to their research , drier conditions expected with climate change are likely to lead to a greater risk of longer fire seasons and large wildfires . Hotter , drier conditions increase fire risk . Wet , warm winters increase the intensity of fires . " <p> On the other side of the aisle , Stuart Smith , National MP for Kaikoura , told Stuff that , while he acknowledged the " incredible effort everyone involved in fighting the Tasman fires has put in , " there is still room for improvement . " As with most unprecedented disasters , there are issues that could be improved on , but before any comments can be made on these , there has to be thorough debrief of all those involved in the response , " said Smith . <p> " Overall , I consider the response to this fire , which will go on for some time as firefighters continue the difficult task of seeking out hotspots , has been timely and well-managed.
