<h> Companies taking notice of climate suits <p> International environmental lawyer Christina Voigt is a professor at the Department of Public and International Law at the University of Oslo , a legal advisor to the Norwegian Ministry of Climate and Environment and a negotiator in UN climate negotiations . She 's also advised oil giant Equinor . <p> Voigt got set on her career in climate law after doing her Masters at the University of Auckland . She was back in New Zealand last month to speak at a conference to mark Dame Sian Elias ' retirement . <p> Eloise Gibson asked her about the hurdles facing people suing governments and companies over climate change , and whether the law suits will change anything . <p> The interview has been edited for length and clarity . <p> Newsroom : What are you speaking about in Auckland ? <p> Christina Voigt : I 'll look at the international aspects of how the Paris Agreement can influence and guide climate cases that are being dealt with in domestic , national courts . <p> CV : It 's a rising tide , a lot of attempts everywhere in the hope that one @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ I think there 's going to be a watershed .
