<h> Otago University maternity research was assailed , not disproven <p> EDITORIAL : If B-movies have taught us anything , it 's that scientists are easily ignored until they get that wild-eyed rule-breaking behaviour going . <p> Remember when Ming the Merciless was cinematically strafing the planet with hot hail in 1980 ? And everybody just thought it was weird weather ? Only when Dr Hans Zarkov pulled a gun on Flash Gordon and forced him to into his spaceship was the problem heroically confronted . <p> This was a point the news media put to real-life Nasa scientist Dr Jim Hansen when he toured New Zealand in 2011 . He was the man who , in the late 1980s , snapped Al Gore , but not enough others , to attention on the dangers of climate change . " Scientists , " the extraordinarily mild-mannered Hansen acknowledged , " are not the best people for communicating . " <p> Pulling out weaponry in defence of research is probably not to be encouraged . But it does remain a torment for practitioners of the scientific method that their messages can languish unheeded because of the counter-tactics of those for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to be exactly the unedifying dynamic uncovered in the case of independent researchers from Otago University , who three years ago found evidence to suggest babies in midwife-led care were at risk of poorer outcomes than babies in doctor-led care . This finding was met by an alignment of the College of Midwives and senior health officials to try to discredit it . <p> The researchers were not saying a midwifery-led system was a bad idea . They supported it . But they found issues with the organisation of the system . <p> A Stuff investigation has found that , once armed with an advance copy provided by authors Diana Sarfati and Ellie Wernham , some ministry staffers worked on how to avoid " fallout " and shared plans with the midwives ' body to discredit the report and to spin the results to take the spotlight away from the safety of the system . <p> The message taken back to senior university figures was that the ministry felt the study was flawed . The academics stood their ground and the findings did hit the headlines , albeit to heavy @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ be accepted , by the public , on the take-it-or-leave-it view that people of good intent can disagree ardently . But the ministry drafted a misleading media release including the significant inaccuracy that the study had found no differences in the death rate for babies who had midwife-led or doctor-led care . <p> Good research is always infused with an explicit acknowledgement of its limitations , and that was the case here . <p> It should also be acknowledged that two University of Auckland academics published perceived defects in the Otago study . <p> Nevertheless , the findings did in themselves cry out for further research . This is the way things are meant to work . Tellingly , the ministry took the soothing line that the study would indeed be followed up . <p> It was n't . That is deeply concerning . <p> The researchers were left feeling beaten up by the experience , and it 's perhaps unsurprising that they were n't , themselves , sufficiently prepared for further combat . But there was clearly real disquiet -- if that 's not too mild a word -- @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ research was euthanised ; that it was sufficiently explained-away rather than disproven in a professional way . And on an issue as important as this , that surely was necessary . This was a case that needed , if not a Zarkov , then at least a champion of some sort .
