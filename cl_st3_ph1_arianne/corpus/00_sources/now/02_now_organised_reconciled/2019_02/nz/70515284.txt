<p> Research in 2011 looked at 21 weather stations across the country and what the projected change in conditions - temperature , humidity , windspeed , and rainfall - would mean for fire danger . <p> It compared fire risk under the average current climate to expected risk in the 2040s and 2090s . Fire danger levels were likely to increase in coastal Otago , Marlborough , South-eastern Southland and the west of the North Island , particularly around Whanganui . <p> FENZ rural national manager Kevin O'Connor said : " It 's important that we keep informed about predicted changes to New Zealand 's weather patterns that may impact future fire risk across the country . We monitor this with a risk profile modelling tool , which we will continue to develop ... <p> " Climate change is a challenge faced internationally by emergency services . Fire and Emergency NZ is a member Australasian Fire and Emergency Service Authorities Council ( AFAC ) .
