<h> Wrongful convictions and false confessions <p> The true crime documentary series , Making a Murderer , became a global phenomenon when it first aired three years ago featuring the case of Steven Avery and his nephew Brendan Dassey who were convicted of murdering a woman in Wisconsin . <p> Dassey was 16 at the time and his lawyers say he gave a false confession that led to his conviction . <p> She talked to Afternoons ahead of a stop in New Zealand in March on the Inside Making a Murderer tour . <p> For people who have n't seen the Netflix show , the case started about 15 years ago when a Wisconsin man , Steven Avery , was exonerated by DNA evidence of a rape he did not commit but spent 18 years in prison for . <p> Avery became a local celebrity and legislation was named after him but , two years after his release , he was charged with the murder of a young woman named Teresa Halbach . <p> " Brendan was questioned four times over 48 hours without a parent or lawyer present and eventually , as you can see on the tapes @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ fed to him , in which he helped his uncle in this rape and murder , " Nirider tells Jesse Mulligan . <p> Both Dassey and Avery were charged with the woman 's death and both were convicted . " <p> Nirider was drawn to the case while she was a third year law student in a class on wrongful convictions taught by Drizen , a world-leading expert on interrogation and false confessions .
