<h> Mercury says timing of renewable development moot <p> New generation development will get underway in New Zealand , but whether it is built within three years or five years is " relatively moot , " Mercury NZ chief executive Fraser Whineray says . <p> All the major generators hold development options and would n't have maintained consents for them if they had n't believed new generation would be required during the next five to 10 years , Whineray told journalists and analysts today . <p> " It 's only a matter of time , " he said . " It 's actually when ' , not if ' " it will be developed . <p> Last week , Meridian Energy said it believed economic growth and decarbonisation efforts would underpin modest on-going electricity demand growth . Longer-dated futures had risen and the company was re-working consents for an up to 140 MW wind project it has north of Napier to ensure the option is available when the time is right . <p> Mercury has consents to develop wind farms at Turitea south-east of Palmerston North and on the Puketoi Range east of Pahiatua . But commitments to 20-plus year generation developments take a lot of work with counterparties . <p> Recent high spot prices are now being reflected in longer-term electricity futures , with ASX contracts for 2020 and 2021 typically $15-20 a megawatt-hour higher than they were in October . <p> But Whineray suggested some of that increase may be reflecting short-term risks rather than more permanent trends in the market . <p> Liquidity in the futures market was still " seriously " low and that was increasing volatility during times of stress , he said . It also has interests in five geothermal power stations around Taupo . <p> The company is currently upgrading its Whakamaru and Aratiatia hydro plants .
