<p> Climate change will cause oceans to become a deeper shade of blue or green , scientists predicted today . <p> Rising temperatures will affect the numbers of tiny organisms called phytoplankton , altering the colour . The study , by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology ( MIT ) and involving the University of Southampton , predicted the changes over the coming decades . <p> This will be caused by the growth and interaction of different species of phytoplankton and the way it absorbs and reflects light . The study suggests that blue sea regions , such as the subtropics , will become even more blue , reflecting even less phytoplankton -- and life in general -- in those waters . <p> Some regions that are greener today , such as near the North and South poles , may turn even deeper green , as warmer temperatures " brew up " larger blooms of more diverse phytoplankton . <p> Rising temperatures will affect the numbers of tiny organisms called phytoplankton , altering the colour ( Victoria Jones/PA ) <p> The changes may not be visible to the naked eye but will be able to be detected via satellite . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ marine ecosystems . <p> Lead author Stephanie Dutkiewicz , at MIT 's department of earth , atmospheric , and planetary sciences , said : " The model suggests the changes wo n't appear huge to the naked eye , and the ocean will still look like it has blue regions in the subtropics and greener regions near the equator and poles . That basic pattern will still be there . But it 'll be enough different that it will affect the rest of the food web that phytoplankton supports . " <p> An ocean 's colour depends on how sunlight interacts with whatever is in the water . Water molecules absorb almost all sunlight except for the blue part of the spectrum , which is reflected -- meaning barren seas appear blue from space . <p> Phytoplankton contain chlorophyll , a pigment which absorbs blue but less green sunlight . This results in more green light being reflected , giving algae-rich regions a greenish hue . <p> Climate change is known to cause significant changes to phytoplankton in oceans . Different types of phytoplankton absorb light differently , and if climate change shifts one community of phytoplankton to another , that will also change the types of food webs they can support .
