<p> GLOBAL targets to limit climate change are unlikely to be met due to delays in changing the way people use land , according to new research . <p> The study , involving researchers from the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology ( KIT ) and the University of Edinburgh , suggests that efforts to make land management less damaging to the climate need to be stepped up dramatically if high levels of climate change are to be avoided . <p> The 2015 Paris Agreement to limit average global temperature increases to 1.5C ( 2.7F ) above pre-industrial levels relies heavily on changes in the management of agricultural land and forests around the world . <p> Many countries plan to prevent deforestation or establish new forests over large areas to absorb carbon dioxide from the air and to reduce greenhouse gas emissions from agriculture , changes which would remove up to 25% of the greenhouse gases released by human activity every year . <p> However , the new research shows that such changes in land use usually take decades to happen , far too slowly to help slow climate change to the agreed level . <p> Dr Calum Brown of KIT @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in the journal Nature Climate Change , said : " The 195 countries that signed the Paris Agreement in 2015 set out a range of actions they would take to tackle climate change . <p> " In most cases little progress has been made in implementing these actions and often the situation has worsened in the last three years . <p> " Our research suggests that many of the plans for mitigation in the land system were unrealistic in the first place and now threaten to make the Paris target itself unachievable .
