<h> Climate campaigners : Glasgow school pupils were quite right to walk out of class <p> HAVE you heard the one about the guy who 's suing his parents for giving birth to him ? <p> Nope , I 'm not trialling the opening line of a new stand up routine on you . Mumbai businessman Raphael Samuel is taking his parents to court for bringing him into this world without first seeking his consent . <p> While many of us are delighted to be here , Mr Samual believes it is a form of cruelty and enslavement to put children on the planet and force them to deal with lifelong suffering . <p> The 27-year-old 's parents are both lawyers so taking them to court is rather a bold move . <p> His mother , Kavita Karnad Samuel , seems like a pretty decent person to have as a mum . I certainly like her approach to the situation . <p> " Mum said she wished she had met me before I was born , " Mr Samual told the BBC . " And that if she did , she definitely would n't have had me . " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ I 'm very happy that my son has grown up into a fearless , independent-thinking young man . He is sure to find his path to happiness . " <p> Mr Samuel ascribes to a movement called anti-natalism , which suggests that the world is so full of misery and suffering that people should stop procreating and allow humanity to die out . <p> It is , perhaps tangentially , a form of environmentalism . Without humans , the burden on the planet would be eased and the flora and fauna could flourish without humans destroying it . <p> I thought of Raphael when I joined Friday 's protest in George Square , the Youth Strike 4 Climate . More young people , more concern for the state of the planet . <p> It 's just that the school and university students who took over the civic heart of Glasgow want to stop humanity dying out . <p> It was a hugely clever publicity move . A protest on a weekend day might barely have made a News In Brief but by walking out of class on a school day @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ found themselves discussed by political leaders . <p> Ms Sturgeon was full of praise : " It 's a cause for optimism , in an often dark world , that young people are taking a stand on climate change . " <p> Mrs May not so : " It is important to emphasise that disruption increases teacher 's workloads and wastes lesson time that teachers have carefully prepared for . " <p> I 'm with the First Minister . We , the adults , have used the planet 's resources with barely a thought for the next generations and it is our responsibility to take urgent action to rectify the damage done . <p> We have led consumerist lives , voted in politicians who have drained the life support system that is the planet and are leaving a horrendous legacy for our school age children . <p> How can we possibly chide them for taking an active stance , for calling us to action . <p> At the end of this week I 'm about to embark on three week 's of travelling that will see me take 22 flights @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ at myself and will spend the rest of the year trying to offset the massive carbon damage done by my desire to see other parts of the planet . <p> It 's not really justifiable . But that 's adulthood , is n't it ? We know what 's right but we tend to lean towards what is convenient and desirable . <p> I remember being a primary pupil and having an ardent interest in the environment . A child 's strong sense of justice and injustice is like nothing else . <p> But we grow out of it . We forget . We take the easy route .
