<h> Survival of coral reefs depends on pollution cuts : Study <h> Share Via Email <p> MIAMI : Some corals may naturally adapt to climate change , but their ability to survive could be outpaced by global warming unless cuts are made to greenhouse gas emissions , researchers said Wednesday . <p> Coral reefs carry an annual global economic value of $375 billion per year because they provide shelter for fish and marine life , protect shorelines and draw tourism to coastal areas . <p> But climate change , pollution , storms , bleaching and disease are endangering reefs worldwide , and up to 90 percent are in danger of dying off by mid-century , scientists have warned . <p> The study in the journal Science Advances looked at a kind of cool-water coral species known as tabletop corals ( Acropora hyacinthus ) in the South Pacific 's Cook Islands . <p> Some of these corals have genetic variants that make them naturally able to tolerate heat and rising temperatures . <p> But researchers discovered that their capacity is limited . <p> " These corals are n't going to adapt @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Bay , a postdoctoral scholar at the University of California , Davis . <p> " Keeping these reefs around requires curbing emissions . " <p> The study relied on computer models that simulated corals ' ability to survive under four different greenhouse gas concentration levels -- or Representative Concentration Pathways ( RCPs ) -- put out by the UN Intergovernmental Panel for Climate Change . <p> If little to nothing is done to curb carbon emissions in the next century and temperatures rise seven degrees Fahrenheit ( 3.7 degrees Celsius ) or more , tabletop corals will die off and risk going extinct , the study found . <p> " Under more severe scenarios , RCP6.0 and RCP8.5 , adaptation was not rapid enough to prevent extinction , " it said . <p> Under the other two more mild scenarios , which foresee that warming either does not exceed two degrees Celsius by 2100 , or that emissions increase for a few decades but then decline by 2040 , researchers found the coral would likely adapt and survive . <p> " Many existing coral populations have a bank of adaptations @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ said co-author Steve Palumbi from Stanford University . <p> " Those existing adaptations are an asset for them to survive longer and for us humans to benefit longer . " <p> More research is needed to determine how other coral species would react to various warming scenarios .
