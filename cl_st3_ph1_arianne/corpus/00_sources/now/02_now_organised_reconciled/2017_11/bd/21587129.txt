The advocates of traditional fuels are determined to push their case at the climate change talks in Bonn -- even though all the evidence says that the continued use of coal , oil and gas threatens severe climate change and the whole future of the planet . <p> According to a new report those lobbyists are not only endangering future generations -- they are also digging a deep financial hole for themselves , virtually throwing away multi-million dollar investments . <p> The report by Carbon Tracker , an independent financial think-tank specialising in the energy sector , looks specifically at the oil-refining sector and has some distinctly uncomfortable news for investors in the industry . <p> It says that by 2035 a growing tide of climate regulations , along with rapid advances in clean technologies , will lead to big cuts in the demand for oil and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ more than 500 refineries having to close . <p> " Many players will exit the market rather than haemorrhage cash " , says Andrew Grant , a co-author of the report . " Investors should beware that the risk of wasting capital extends to all new investments , including expansions or upgrades to existing facilities . " <p> Refineries hit <p> The thousands of delegates at the meeting here -- referred to as the Conference of the Parties , or the COP -- are discussing ways to ensure that average world temperatures do not rise to more than 2 ? C target , oil use will have to be radically cut back and the oil refining sector , which demands high levels of investment capital , will be especially hard hit . <p> The least profitable refineries will go out of business .
