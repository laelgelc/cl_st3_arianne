<h> Letters : Why Crossrail still deserves to be given the green light <p> ALTHOUGH former Labour rail minister Tom Harris may contend that any direct Glasgow Airport Rail Link is not a viable proposition without the Crossrail project , the underlying case for delivering planned Crossrail modernisation still remains intact , and robust , by integrating Scotland 's historically disjointed and handicapped networks as prelude to offering a more convenient , credible and competitive alternative to mass car commuting across Scotland ( " Airport rail link would be a poor use of public money " , The Herald , November 18 ) . <p> The core argument for Crossrail modernisation , initially reopening and electrifying the 1.8 miles of existing City Union Line , rests on delivering significant economic/employment , social mobility/urban regeneration and environmental benefits in their own right , with an airport rail link seen as a welcome " add-on supplement " rather than a pivotal justification for Crossrail , which is deliverable in incremental stages . <p> Pejorative comments suggesting that major transport projects are more determined by political dictat " rather than where real people @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ link ) was powerfully confounded by the reopened Borders line , and other restored rail routes , where actual patronage has hugely exceeded dismal theoretical modelling forecasts .
