<p> AS our governments reconvene for their yearly climate talks , society is being changed by people taking grassroots action all around the world . <p> For more than 25 years , the world 's nations have gathered annually to talk about how to halt the breakdown of the Earth 's climate , with this year 's UN Climate Change Conference currently taking place in the German city of Bonn . <p> But despite these two-and-a-half decades of well-meaning international discussions , fossil fuel use has never been as high . <p> Few of the key players have made concrete commitments to deliver the transformative change needed . <p> The real planning for a safe and just transition is taking place not in the conference halls , but through people organising in community halls , coffee shops and their living rooms . Germany -- despite its reputation as a leader in energy transition -- still relies on coal <p> to cover a quarter of its electricity demand , and plans mine expansions until at least 2040 . <p> On the fringes of the UN climate summit , social movements are @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ address this crisis with urgency . With a group of young people from Scotland , I travelled to Bonn to attend events such as a ceremony held by Pacific Islanders , the People 's Climate March and various other workshops . <p> We also joined 4500 others in a mine occupation organised by the German collective Ende Gelande , peacefully stopping the production of coal for the day . <p> Three months ago , the extent of my political involvement was to retweet well-meaning politicians or sign online petitions . Now I had committed to do more to create change , together with thousands of others , by taking direct action . Having attended training the day before and having formed an " affinity group " with friends , I felt supported and confident in our ability to undertake this mission . What was especially startling about this action was the diversity of its participants : young and old , experienced activists and novices like me , people from the global North and South . This was n't just Germany 's problem , it was everyone 's . <p> Several @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ group consensus to walk out of the mine together . But despite our peaceful intentions , police forces attempted to break our formation by pushing us , isolating and arresting individuals , and using pepper sprays and horses . <p> Singing , chanting and dancing helped us to keep our spirits high and face these intimidation attempts united . As we stood , arms locked and singing in front of the police line , the hypocrisy of the official climate talks was even more apparent . <p> As our governments busied themselves with interminable negotiations , their police were repressing the real action that their citizens were undertaking to protect our planet . <p> It is encouraging to know we can physically stop perpetrators of the climate crisis -- even for just a day . It seems evident we will not succeed solely by slow-moving , unambitious talks by our state leaders . <p> Pressure must come from acting locally and empowering people to organise in their communities .
