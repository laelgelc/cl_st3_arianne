<h> Writtle academic helps beech forest gain protected status <h> Essex 's own Writtle University College part of international effort to protect ancient beech trees <p> The efforts of a group of 120 international experts including a senior academic from Essex 's own Writtle University College have successfully had the world 's ancient beech forests across ten European countries added to UNESCO 's World Heritage List . <p> The project has led to the protection of 63 ancient and primeval beech forests , which gives them the same legal status as historic landmarks such as the Taj Mahal and Machu Picchu . <p> The European Beech Forest project was funded by the German Ministry of Environment and coordinated by the Centre for Econics &amp; Ecosystem Management , a partnership programme between Eberswalde University for Sustainable Development and Writtle University College , under the dual directorship of Professor Pierre Ibisch , of Eberswalde , and Professor Peter Hobson , of Writtle . <p> Professor Hobson said : " This is one of the most ambitious and most extensive World Heritage inscriptions since the UNESCO World Heritage List was set up . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Europe has less than 1 per cent old growth forest left and they are responding very rapidly to climate change ; there are many areas where the old growth beech forest is retreating but there are also areas that it is moving into . " <p> The designation was made when the UNESCO Commission was in session in Krakow , Poland , and is the culmination of six years of work , carried out by more than 120 experts from over 20 countries . <p> Professor Hobson added : " Unfortunately the UK , which has some of the most westerly examples of old beech forest , felt they were not in a position to commit or engage with the UNESCO designation . "
