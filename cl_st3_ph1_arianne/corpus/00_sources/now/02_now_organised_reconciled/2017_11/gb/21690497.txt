<h> Climate change ' affecting recovery of deforested areas in Amazon ' <p> Deforested areas of the Amazon Basin have a limited ability to recover because of recent changes in climate , a study shows . <p> Limited growth in a drier climate has restricted the amount of carbon that new trees can lock away from the atmosphere , reducing their ability to counteract the effects of global warming . <p> The research was carried out by teams from the University of Edinburgh , the UK National Centre for Earth Observation ( NCEO ) and Nanjing University of Information Science and Technology in China . <p> Scientists found forests replanted today would likely be able to recapture only two-thirds of the carbon that they have lost to the atmosphere in the past 20 years through deforestation and climate change . <p> Professor Mathew Williams , of the University of Edinburgh 's School of GeoSciences , said : " Owing to the impact of climate change , replanted and recovering forests may not grow to the sizes found in the undisturbed Amazon . " <p> The findings could inform conservation strategies @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in which many countries have committed to reforestation . <p> Scientists say that while replanting forests remains an effective way to counter the effects of climate change , the new research shows this may prove to be challenging . <p> Researchers used artificial intelligence to combine satellite data and climate records to reconstruct annual maps of forest changes due to deforestation and climate in the Amazon Basin . <p> The study is the first to differentiate between the impact of human activity in the Amazon and the impact of climate change to quantify the carbon-storing potential of new forests . <p> Further studies of tropical regions involving new satellites should enable scientists to work with affected countries to better conserve and manage their forests . <p> The research , published in Scientific Reports , was funded by the Natural Environment Research Council . <p> Dr Jean-Francois Exbrayat , of the NCEO , who led the study , said : " Technological advances are enabling us to build a clearer picture of the resilience of the Amazon and other tropical forests , which will help develop strategies to safeguard these environments . "
