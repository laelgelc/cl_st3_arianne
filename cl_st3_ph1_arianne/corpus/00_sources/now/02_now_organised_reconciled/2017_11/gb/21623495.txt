<p> Stumbling on submerged roots , attacked by bees and wading waist-deep through leech-infested water , the three researchers and their Pygmy guides progress at just 100 metres an hour through the largest and least-explored tropical bog in the world . <p> The group halt and unpack what looks like a spear , which is plunged over and over again into the waterlogged forest floor . Each time it brings up a metre-long core of rich , black peat made up of partly decomposed leaves and ancient plantlife . The deepest the steel blade reaches before meeting the underlying clay is 3.7 metres . <p> Leeds University forest ecologists Simon Lewis and Greta Dargie cheer .
