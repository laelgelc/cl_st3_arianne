<h> Irish CO2e levels soar in 2016 as renewable energy production falls <p> It will come as a massive blow to environmental campaigners and the Government alike to hear that CO2-equivalent emissions jumped in 2016 . <p> It had already been predicted that Ireland would fail to meet its greenhouse emission reduction targets , but new findings from the Environmental Protection Agency ( EPA ) have shown that our emissions actually increased in 2016 . <p> Publishing its findings online , the EPA found that the country 's total amount of emissions increased ' significantly ' by 3.5pc last year on the previous year , amounting to a total of 61.19m tonnes of CO2 equivalent ( CO2e ) . <p> Unlike CO2 , CO2e takes into account other greenhouse gas emissions from humans that contribute to climate change , such as methane and nitrous oxide , and distinguishes them from naturally occurring CO2 . <p> The last time Ireland 's emissions were this high was in 2009 , not long after the global economic crash . <h> Renewable energy use decreased <p> The greatest contributing sector to this sharp rise @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ demand for electricity , largely driven by gas , thereby increasing its emissions by 6.1pc last year . <p> While decreases were observed in coal and peat use , the use of renewable energy also dropped by 1.7pc , most likely attributed to poor weather conditions . <p> The overall impact is that there is an increase in the emissions intensity of electricity generation for the second year in a row . <p> Transport emissions saw a 3.7pc increase last year and 13pc over the last four years due to an uptake in the Irish economy , driven by a rise in diesel over petrol and biofuels . <p> Amount of emissions produced by each sector .
