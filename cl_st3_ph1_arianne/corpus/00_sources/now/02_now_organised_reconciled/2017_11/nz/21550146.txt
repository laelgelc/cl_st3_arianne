" <p> Shears , from the university 's Institute of Marine Science , and co-author Melissa Bowen from the School of Environment analysed three of the longest-running sea temperature records in the southern hemisphere for the study . <p> It included 50 years of daily sea temperature records taken at Leigh Marine Laboratory north of Auckland @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Marine Laboratory near Dunedin and 71 years of data from Maria Island , eastern Tasmania . <p> Satellite data , ocean currents and wind patterns were also included in the analysis . <p> Waters around Tasmania had the quickest rate of warming , at 0.2C per decade , but waters to the north of Auckland showed no change . <p> The research was undertaken to better under understand " tropicalisation " of oceans due to sea temperature rise from climate change . <p> Shears said everyone knew oceans were warming but until now it was not known how , or where around New Zealand that was occurring . <p> " It 's important to understand how that 's happening so we can predict what might happen to the ecosystems in the future .
