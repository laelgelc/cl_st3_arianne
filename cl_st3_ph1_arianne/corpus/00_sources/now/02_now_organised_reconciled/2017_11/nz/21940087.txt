<h> Motor industry in for radical change <p> The next 20 years will see the most dramatic changes in the history of the motor vehicle , forecasts the head of New Zealand 's biggest car company . <p> " The days of the internal combustion engine are numbered , " Toyota New Zealand chief executive Alistair Davis has told journalists during the media launch of two new vehicles in the South Island . <p> " And when that happens , it 's going to change everything - from how cars are built , to how dealerships operate . It 's going to be that radical , " he said in a presentation at Tekapo . <p> SUPPLIED <p> Is the future of the internal combustion engine in doubt ? <p> Davis told journalists the motor industry is already undergoing major change , but that it is nothing compared to the tsunami of change that is going to happen to the car industry over the next two decades . Climate change , safety , congestion and vehicle efficiency are all elements that will cause this change , he forecast . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ - because that will be responsible for a shift in motive power , " said Davis . <p> SUPPLIED <p> Electric cars such as the Nissan Leaf will change motoring , says Davis . <p> " It all started a few years ago with the advent of the hybrid , but that technology was always going to be a bridge to the future . Now we 're seeing the accelerating development of plug-in electric vehicles , battery electric vehicles , and hydrogen fuel cell vehicles . " <p> But this change in itself is creating a number of major issues the motor vehicle industry is having to wrestle with . <p> One is so-called " dirty " electricity , he said . While countries including New Zealand produce mainly clean electricity using renewables such as water and steam , really big markets such as China generate their power the dirty way by using coal . <p> " And then there are the issues surrounding how batteries are made .
