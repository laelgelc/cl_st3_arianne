<h> There is no urban-rural divide -- only a polluter-non polluter divide <p> OPINION : Agriculture is facing big challenges and the threat of fundamental , far-reaching change . <p> The challenges are many ; climate change , competitive markets , the clamour for sustainable farming and need for much better environmental protection . <p> If those are n't big enough for any industry to tackle , just over the horizon loom other significant threats , such as new technology which promises to replace farm-raised meat with meat grown in laboratories . <p> The technology is advancing quickly - lab meat prices are tumbling , the taste is improving and the product is now at the stage where rare-cooked lab meat even bleeds . <p> There is also the very real risk that it is undermining agriculture 's efforts to restore its social licence to operate . <p> Intensive farmers forget that the public has been endlessly patient with them on water . <p> That patience has now run out - as the recent Water New Zealand survey revealed , three quarters of those questioned are concerned about poor water @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ This is a significant majority . <p> It would be tempting for the commercial agri sector to again resort to the urban-rural divide myth to explain things away : <p> " Of course townies would say that , it shows how out of touch they are . " <p> What should be sobering for those trying to dismiss this groundswell of public opposition is the results include the views of the rural community , not just urban dwellers . <p> The survey result is further reinforced by voting returns from the recent election showing a swing to the left in rural electorates , particularly in the South Island . This should come as no surprise -- water quality and intensive farming 's role in its decline was one of the top election issues . <p> The public focus on this issue wo n't be diverted by the millions of dollars Fonterra has lavished on the professional ads on prime TV airtime to paint a rosy picture of corporate dairy farming . <p> Of course , not all farmers are to blame for poor water quality . <p> Many are already @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ environment and improve rivers , lakes and streams . They shake their heads in dismay when they are lumped in with those who refuse to accept such measures are necessary . <p> And sadly , there are farmers who remain in denial . Worryingly , some are in positions where they should know better . Federated Farmers ' Wairarapa dairy chairman made headlines in September when he claimed farming had no impact on water quality . <p> More recently , Federated Farmers President Katie Milne told TV3 's The Nation that the impact farming is having on waterways is being blown out of proportion . <p> The time for denial is over . It is time for corporate farming leaders to take concrete action so their industry can move ahead and confront its challenges . This has to happen now , not in another five , 10 or 20 years . <p> Abandoning their reliance on the urban-rural divide myth as an excuse for inaction is the first step .
