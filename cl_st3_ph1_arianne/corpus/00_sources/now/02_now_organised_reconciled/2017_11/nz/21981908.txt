<h> Scientists using ship log data to reveal more about the future climate <p> Last updated 14:17 , December 1 2017 <p> Christopher Furlong <p> Scientists plan to use ship data to better understand climate change . <p> Scientists are attempting to learn more about the planet 's future climate by using ship log data from the past 150 years to reconstruct weather patterns . <p> International group ACRE or Atmospheric Circulation Reconstructions over the Earth , is dedicated to discovering weather observations recorded in diaries , aboard ships and voyages of exploration . The aim is to detect various weather patterns from past events that may help reveal more about the planet 's future climate . <p> NIWA climate scientist Andrew Lorrey said testing links between short-term weather variability and long-term climate change required a large amount of data that meant going back much further than 50 to 60 years . <p> " If we had 150 years of weather data we could be more confident about our interpretations for the future , " said Lorrey . <p> Lorrey said the intention was to cast a wide net to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ effort takes weather observations from different places around the globe and pushes them into a supercomputer reconstruction of our weather . It 's designed to give us a better depiction of historic weather on a daily basis . " <p> NIWA leads two segments of the ACRE work -- one for the Pacific and one for Antarctica , the latter funded by the Deep South National Science Challenge . <p> For the Antarctic work , research has focused on sealing and whaling missions . Ship logs from these vessels can be used to help reconstruct past weather patterns . <p> NIWA climate scientist Petra Pearce said there was an enormous amount of data to mine . <p> " So far just for the Antarctic part of the project , we have about 150,000 images of ship logs sent to us from archives in the UK and Scandinavia . Each image has several days ' observations and each day has multiple observations so we will have millions of observations to key over the course of the project , " she said .
