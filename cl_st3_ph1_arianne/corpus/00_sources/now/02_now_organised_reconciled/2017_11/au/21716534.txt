<p> The countless cups of coffee downed during the UN 's COP23 climate summit in Bonn may seem insignificant . <p> But if coffee consumption has any message , it is that climate change is personal . <p> Worldwide , more than 2.75 billion cups of coffee are consumed every day . Half of all Australians love a good coffee to kick-start each morning . <p> Yet , as climate change influences weather patterns around the world , coffee yields and quality are already suffering , and pests and diseases are on the rise . <p> Fairtrade Australia &amp; New Zealand 's report on the climate risks to coffee , A Brewing Storm , found that the total area suitable for growing coffee will be halved by 2050 if global warming goes unchecked . By 2080 , wild coffee -- a crucial genetic resource for farmers -- could vanish entirely . <h> Hurting more than your wallet <p> This will hit the hip pocket of caffeine addicts everywhere . Many of these smallholders lack the capacity to plan more than a year ahead , much less for decades of climate change . An increasingly hostile climate also jeopardises the health of farmers and farm workers , particularly women and children . <p> Speaking at the first World Coffee Producers ' Forum in Colombia in July , former US president Bill Clinton called coffee an " anchor crop " . <p> Coffee is the second most valuable commodity exported by the developing world . <p> Take just one example . A third of Ethiopia 's export income is derived from coffee , and the livelihoods of around 2.5 million Ethiopians depend on it . Average temperatures in Ethiopia have already risen by 1.3 deg C since 1850 , and the thermometer is set to soar 3.1 degrees by 2060 and 5.0 degrees by 2090 . While the international community pledges to keep warming to under 2 deg C , experts say the berry borer 's numbers could explode well before 2 deg C , spread into new regions , and potentially overwhelm even the most savvy , well-connected producers . <p> A recent outbreak in Papua New Guinea 's Western Highlands threatened the country 's A$254 million coffee industry , which supplies Australians with one in 10 cups .
