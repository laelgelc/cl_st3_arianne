<h> Carbon Emissions Had Levelled Off . Now They 're Rising Again <p> But after a three-year stall in their growth , human-caused carbon-dioxide emissions have not , in fact , peaked , an international team of scientists announced this morning . <p> In 2017 , global emissions of CO2 from fossil fuels and industry will once again rise by 2 percent , the scientists project , to a record 37 billion metric tonnes . Those emissions had increased by only a quarter of a percent from 2014 to 2016 . Changes in land use , such as deforestation , will add around 4 billion metric tons of CO2 in 2017 , bringing the global emissions total to an estimated 41 billion metric tonnes . <p> The resurgence tightens the time constraint on the world 's efforts to keep global warming from exceeding 2 degrees Celsius -- a cap scientists increasingly believe is important to ward off climate change 's most catastrophic effects . <p> " What 's driving , really , the global trend is this pick-up in China , " says Corinne Le Qu ? r ? of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of one of several new emissions studies released today .
