<h> Rain Birds review : Harriet McKnight and the impact of climate change <h> Kerryn Goldsworthy <p> There 's a lot going on here , but the two plot strands are adroitly woven together in a tale of events in East Gippsland where the habitat of an endangered species is being destroyed . In parallel plots , two damaged characters collide , each with an increasingly worried and worn-out companion . Alan has early-onset Alzheimer 's and it is becoming impossible for his wife , Pina , to care for him . Arianna is a conservation biologist whose troubled past seems to have resulted in some form of PTSD . Her easy-going work partner , Tim , must deal with her intense personality and her erratic behaviour . Each of these situations comes to a head when a bushfire tears through the landscape and the town . This novel is an example of an emerging form in literature : the realist novel in which climate change is no longer science fiction but already an integral part of the real and familiar world .
