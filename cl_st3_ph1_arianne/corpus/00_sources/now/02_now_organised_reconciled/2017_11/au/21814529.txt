<h> What can albatross poo tell us about climate change ? <p> If there is one quality that scientists possess , it 's dedication . They train for years , mastering complex concepts and theories , all in pursuit of answering the universe 's questions . <p> Sometimes these questions lead to ... unexpected places . <p> Just ask Dr Julie McInnes of the Institute for Marine and Antarctic Studies at the University of Tasmania . She dedicates her scientific study to measuring the effect of climate change on how different species live and feed . <p> How , you ask ? By sampling fresh albatross poo on subantarctic islands , of course . <h> SHOO-BA-DO-WAH ! <p> This method of scat sampling is possible due to advances in DNA technology . Julie and her team can now better detect DNA in places where lots of different kinds of DNA are mixed up together ( like poo ) . Previously , scientists had to rely on sifting through albatrosses ' stomach contents , which is less accurate . <p> And while few scientists may be overjoyed at the idea of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ this new method can tell us about the birds ' ecosystem . <p> " One of the really exciting things about this study is finding jellyfish in the albatrosses ' diet . We did n't expect to find this . " <p> " With stomach content analysis , you ca n't actually find the jellyfish because they 're so gelatinous and are digested very quickly . " <p> " It 's a side of the food chain we have n't seen before . We 're finding that more and more species are eating jellyfish . " <h> LOOKING TO THE FUTURE <p> Julie 's work is n't just about finding out the best catering plan for seabirds . <p> The DNA analysis of 1460 scats from breeding sites around the Southern Ocean will be used as a snapshot of the albatrosses ' diet in the present day .
