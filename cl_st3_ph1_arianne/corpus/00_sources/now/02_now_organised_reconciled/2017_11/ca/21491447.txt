" TROY FLEECE / Regina Leader-Post <p> As Saskatchewan focuses on carbon capture technology and resists a carbon tax , a new climate change policy report is calling on the provincial government to consider a more multi-dimensional approach to combatting greenhouse gas ( GHG ) emissions . <p> The report -- titled Climate Change : The policy options and implications for Saskatchewan -- was released this month by the Johnson Shoyama Graduate School of Public Policy . It outlines the challenges , legal arguments and policy options surrounding climate change , as well as the impacts those policies would have on the province . <p> " If we 're going to be serious about addressing climate change in the way that the federal government has set out ... then there is no one policy solution to this , " said Dale Eisler , co-author and senior policy fellow . In October 2016 , Prime Minister Trudeau announced that provinces and territories must implement a direct tax on carbon emissions or adopt a cap-and-trade system by 2018 . <p> The Saskatchewan government has maintained that a provincial carbon tax will never happen , stating that their export-based economy could not withstand it , despite provinces and territories having the ability to exempt or reduce taxes on certain sectors . <p> Eisler said the report aims to broaden the discussion " beyond simply what often gets reduced to an issue over the carbon tax , yes or no " and exploring a combination of a carbon tax , cap-and-trade system and clean technology instead . <p> Carbon tax <p> The province estimates a carbon tax , which directly taxes GHG emissions , would produce $2.5 billion in revenue . According to the report , it is likely only the electricity sector and coal production would come under the restriction . <p> The advantage of this system is that it would monitor and measure progress in reducing emissions , says the report , but does not directly affect the behaviour of " individuals or households who may choose to absorb the additional costs and continue carbon intensive activities .
