<p> End-of-season estimates show that fewer than 3.6 million pink salmon have returned to the Fraser , less than half the median pre-season forecast of 8.7 million fish and not enough for a commercial or sport fishery . <p> There was a one-in-10 chance that the pink returns would be as low as 4.4 million , but the results were even worse . <p> " It was much less , obviously much poorer than expected , " Mike Lapointe , chief biologist with the Pacific Salmon Commission , said in an interview Thursday . The average pink run on the Fraser is closer to 12 million . <p> The estimated fry-to-adult survival rate of 1.6 per cent this year is half the average of 3.2 per cent . <p> The total catch of Fraser-bound pinks by Canada and the U.S. was just 159,000 fish this season , mainly Aboriginal fisheries but some test fisheries . <p> The dismal pink returns are in line with previously reported poor sockeye returns on the Fraser this season -- about 1.5 million fish compared with a median pre-season forecast of 4.4 million . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ years for sockeye ) , meaning the fish that returned this year were spawned in 2015 . The pinks travelled as far north as the Gulf of Alaska before returning to the Fraser this year as adults . <p> The bulk of pinks spawn downstream of the Fraser Canyon , but some have been reported as far upstream as Prince George , evidence that they may be expanding their range , Lapointe said . <p> He said no one really knows why ocean survival has been so poor . Food , predators , and especially ocean warming could be factors . " It 's smart to be humble and admit we do n't know , " he said . " All we know is they did n't come back .
