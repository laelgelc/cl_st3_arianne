Ontario is not , " drew much needed attention to the " Ministry of Wood 's " failure to act on woodland caribou conservation , in contravention of both federal and provincial policies . <p> As one senior federal employee put it recently , the MNRF " does n't seem @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " However , there is one caribou herd in Ontario on the brink of extirpation that has not received much attention , and where the most immediate threat is not the loss of habitat . <p> The first paragraph of Plotkin and Gray 's piece ends with the statement that woodland caribou " need large , intact swathes of Boreal forest to survive , and that habitat is disappearing . " Though efforts to maintain large , intact swathes of Boreal forest are laudable and critical , the statement is not entirely true . The qualifier is essential to understanding the plight of the anomalous Lake Superior caribou herd . <p> Maps showing the historic range of woodland caribou reveal that the animal inhabited areas as far south as the French River . This places the animal well within the Great Lakes St. Lawrence forest , the forest region directly south of the Boreal forest . This discredits the " Boreal forest " part of Plotkin and Gray 's claim . In actuality , what woodland caribou need is the means to avoid and escape predators ( primarily wolves ) @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for this , but not always necessary . <p> In 1981 a male woodland caribou was spotted on Michipicoten Island , a 184 square kilometre island located on Lake Superior . The island is covered by Great Lakes St. Lawrence forest , and does not have an abundance of lichen in areas accessible to caribou . <p> Article Continued Below <p> Having spotted the single bull , the MNR then transferred some of the caribou from the Slate Islands ( further north on Lake Superior ) to Michipicoten Island ( as well as other islands in the area ) . For three decades the population flourished . <p> Then , in 2012 , wolves reached the island when an ice bridge formed . The high density of wolves has resulted in the rapid collapse of the caribou population , just as Dr. Tom Bergerud , a leading caribou researcher , hypothesized in 1974 ; i.e. that the primary limiting factor for caribou is not availability of lichens ( assuming there is another food source , as there is on Michipicoten Island ) but rather predation . <p> Meanwhile , a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and caribou have disappeared entirely from the mainland of eastern Superior for the first time since the ice receded . The sometimes harsh and lonely shoreline has gotten significantly lonelier . <p> Though both Michipicoten and the Slate Islands are Provincial Parks , and though the provincial and federal governments have woodland caribou conservation plans in place , those at the MNRF have decided that the best course of action is to " study " the situation on the islands . They have used the ongoing study as an excuse not to act as yet another woodland caribou herd disappears from the landscape . <p> There are those who argue -- including provincial employees and those with ties to the MNRF study -- that the elimination of the caribou from these islands is part of a " natural process , " of Mother Nature returning to balance through predator-prey dynamics . <p> In the mouth of a scientist such language has zero merit . And third , there is no " Balance of Nature , " no eternal , harmonious , natural state ; ecosystems change , and what we call an equilibrium can and will shift . The shifts are usually slow , but can also occur rapidly , as we 're now experiencing around the world with species extinction and climate change . <p> Let us not then respond to the difficult challenges we 're now facing on the land by taking recourse in platitudes . It is a false comfort we find when we declare , " this is just Mother Nature at work , " throwing in a few " kumbayas . We have been building something . <p> I invite you to examine it , to see its contours clearly , and be prepared to defend your esthetic sensibilities to those who will have to inhabit that landscape . <p> For now , and for at least a short while longer , the caribou of Lake Superior are still here .
