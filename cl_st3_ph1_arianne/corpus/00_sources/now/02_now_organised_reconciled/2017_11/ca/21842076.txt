<h> A Tale of Two Cities : Swedish community sets climate example for Peterborough <p> Climate change is upon us , caused primarily by excessive use of fossil fuels which produce greenhouse gases ( GHGs ) . Extreme weather events are disrupting our lives and costing us dearly , but we can limit the severity of this planetary change . <p> The first article in this three-part series pointed out that Swedes , with a climate and population similar to Ontario , enjoy the same economic wealth that we do , using only two-thirds of the energy per person and generating just one-third of the GHG emissions . Part of their success is due to the transfer of power and decision-making ( including taxation rights ) from central to municipal governments . This has empowered local governments to invest significantly in biogas generation from waste water treatment , waste diversion from landfills and district heating . <p> Also the introduction of a carbon tax in 1991 , following the oil shortages of the 1970s and 80s , led to investment in alternative energy . The city has 90,000 inhabitants with 137,000 people in the Karlstad region , compared to Peterborough city 's 81,000 with 123,000 in the metropolitan area . Like Peterborough , Karlstad has a university , founded in 1967 ( Trent in 1963 ) . Karlstad has a strong hockey team , playing in the highest Swedish league , and Karlstad has a GE engineering plant focussed on wind power . <p> Sweden has used district heating to dramatically decrease its fossil-fuel use for the heating of buildings . District heating uses a central power plant to provide heat for a large number of nearby buildings , usually via hot water . Sweden 's first district heating system was introduced in Karlstad in 1948 , when a thermal power station was converted to a combined heat and power ( CHP or cogeneration ) plant . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ natural gas or nuclear fuel , about 40 per cent of the heat is used to power the electrical generator and 60 per cent must be discarded . In a CHP plant , this leftover heat is used for other purposes , such as residential heating or industrial processes , which improves the energy efficiency of the plant from about 40 per cent to over 80 per cent . In summer , the waste heat can also be used via absorption cooling for air conditioning . The whole city of Karlstad now uses a large wood-pellet furnace for CHP heating of most of its homes and buildings . This fuel is renewable as new trees are planted each year to replace the ones cut . ( Peterborough has forests nearby , and a system like this could create local jobs . ) All major cities and towns in Sweden now have district heating systems . Home energy costs are also dropping through other measures . For example , every new building in Sweden is required to have triple-glazed windows . <p> From 2007 to 2015 , the Karlstad municipality allocated @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ investment has clearly paid off . Karlstad cut carbon dioxide emissions by 3,205 tonnes per year , equivalent to emissions from 9,157 cars . These measures saved over CAN$1.2 million for municipal coffers . In addition , six out of 10 Karlstad residents travel regularly on city buses , with bus trips up 64 per cent from 2005 to 2013 . The buses are powered by biogas , and electric buses are being added . The biogas is methane , obtained by collecting and composting the city 's organic waste . Biogas also fuels Karlstad garbage trucks . <p> Karlstad has been voted one of Sweden 's most cycle-friendly cities , where about 20per cent of all trips are made on bike . Eco-certified Karlstad Airport is the first airport in Europe with a fixed supply system for bio jet fuel . Karlstad city reduced its GHG emissions from car travel on business trips by 32 per cent between 2009 and 2013 , largely by training staff to use the Internet to replace travel to meetings . <p> Like Peterborough , Karlstad has invested in renewable energy . We 're moving in the right direction , but we still have great potential for renewable energy and energy savings . <p> The last article in this series will look at Peterborough 's Climate Change Action Plan , to see where we can learn from the Karlstad example and make progress on our own .
