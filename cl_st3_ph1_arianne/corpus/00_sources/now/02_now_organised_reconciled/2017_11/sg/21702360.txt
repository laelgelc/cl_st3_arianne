<h> Climate change and neglect threaten Senegal 's Saint Louis <p> The echo of Koranic verse from a religious school in Senegal 's Saint-Louis is familiar , but just metres down the beach classes have fallen silent in another building abandoned to the ravages of climate change . <p> Listed in 2000 as a UNESCO world heritage site , Saint Louis has fallen into disrepair , and without urgent intervention more than 300 years of colonial history could be lost . <p> Beautifully coloured buildings with colonial facades line the streets of the city , once known as " Africa 's Venice " , but on closer inspection many are crumbling . Others that look on to the beach succumb to Atlantic waves . <p> And at the cathedral , large sections of plaster peel from the walls and float to the earth . <p> " It 's dramatic and it could become extremely serious , " said French Foreign Affairs Minister Jean-Yves Le Drian on a visit Tuesday to what was once the Senegalese capital , and where the first French chartered company set up shop in 1659 . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ River from the ocean , the effects of rising sea levels driven by climate change are clear . <p> Abdoulaye Mben Khali School was forced to relocate eight year groups to another establishment , Cheikh Ndar , itself just 50 metres away on the seafront and also under threat from the waves , explained Saint Louis governor Mariama Traore . <p> Meanwhile more than 150 families have lost their homes in recent years , several during the height of the storms of Senegal 's rainy season in September . <p> They are now living in tents near the city 's tiny airport . <p> -
