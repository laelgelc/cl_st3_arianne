<p> Vanguard had its proxy vote on Wednesday after a long campaign to get shareholders out to vote . There was a quorum for every fund , and new board members were approved . <p> The controversial " genocide vote , " Proposal 7 , was not approved -- as expected . The vote would have compelled divestment from companies with ties to Darfur or other companies that " significantly contribute " to genocide and crimes against humanity . <p> As the company is a cooperative owned by anyone with a Vanguard fund , the investment giant was concerned with not getting enough voters to ratify its agenda , a potentially expensive situation as each proxy vote campaign is costly . For this campaign , the first in eight years , Vanguard spent $18 million . <p> Proxy votes are not common for Vanguard , and the vote was the first for many of its shareholders , given the massive influx of cash into passive investing , and Vanguard specifically .
