The first is the flooding that took place in Bangladesh earlier this year which caused an estimated loss and damage to crops and infrastructure of between $3 billion and $5 billion . <p> Bangladesh is a country that sits in the delta of two of the world 's greatest rivers , namely the Ganges and Brahmaputra which flood during the monsoon season each year . <p> The people are well adapted to living with this by building their homes on raised hillocks and planting crops like rice and jute that can grow even in partly flooded lands . They are usually able to harvest their crops before the floods and then take out their boats and fish once the main flood waters arrive and inundate the entire floodplain . <p> We @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , of the abnormally large floods that occur every now and again . And this year 's flood was estimated to be a one-in-100-year event . <p> Hence it is reasonable to say that while Bangladesh is relatively well adapted to normal floods we are now facing a new-normal of climate change-induced events of much greater magnitude . <p> My second example is the series of severe hurricanes that hit Texas ( Hurricane Harvey ) , Florida ( Hurricane Irma ) and Puerto Rico ( Hurricane Maria ) in quick succession causing severe impacts in all those places . <p> Again the fact that the hurricanes occurred was not due to climate change but their relatively high severity was clearly due to the higher-than-normal sea surface temperatures at the time . <p> The estimates of the loss and damage in Texas , Florida and Puerto Rico is said to be over $300 billion , which they will ask the US Congress to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Congress is likely to pay some , if not all , of that bill for loss and damage . <p> This is because the United States , like many other federal governments , allows states to claim post-disaster compensation from the central government . <p> But who should Bangladesh send its bill to ? <p> The answer is actually quite clear in the mandate of the Warsaw International Mechanism ( WIM ) on Loss and Damage , which the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change adopted nearly five years ago in 2013 at COP19 . <h> " <p> Even if human-induced climate change does not cause these weather events , they get greatly exacerbated due to elevated temperatures -- and the actual loss and damage that results is now attributable to human-induced climate change . <p> The WIM is supposed to address the issue of loss and damage through enhancing our understanding and examining options for addressing loss and damage , including looking for innovative financing options .
