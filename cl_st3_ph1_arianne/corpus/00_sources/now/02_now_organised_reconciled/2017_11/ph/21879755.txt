<h> US eyeing ' clean coal ' alliance with PH <p> Washington ( Washington Times ) -- As coal output and sales continue to rise in the United States ( US ) , a senior energy adviser to the White House has signaled a global " clean coal alliance " that would include the Philippines . <p> David Banks , who represented Washington at a United Nations climate change conference in Germany last week , said the US " wants to help all coal-dependent economies to burn coal efficiently and cleanly . " <p> The plan , he said , would be driven by billions of dollars already signed off by President Trump for clean coal research , along with technology developed in Australia , India and China . <p> " President Trump still intends to withdraw from the Paris agreement , but in good faith we will continue to work with parties to the accord , " Mr. Banks said . " There are 1,600 new coal plants being built around the world , and demand for coal and gas is growing . It 's in the global interest @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ anyway , it must be done as cleanly as possible . " <p> He said the administration also wanted to try and change a World Bank ban on lending to projects that use coal . Such a change would allow Manila to borrow from the World Bank for future power plants . <p> The first discovery of coal in the Philippines was on Cebu island in 1827 and the country has un-mined reserves of more than 2.5bn tons . <p> Coal is used to generate more that 40 percent of all electricity in the Philippines , with a substantial amount used in production of cement . Local supplies are supplemented with imports from Indonesia and Australia . <p> Mr. Banks said the United States would remain " a global player " in climate change but also understood , " the reality of countries who have no choice but to generate electricity from coal . " <p> The US delegation said the proposed clean-coal alliance could include China , Australia , India , the Philippines , South Africa and Bangladesh .
