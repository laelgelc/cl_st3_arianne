Then , winemakers grew grapes cheaply for export to the U.S. and the UK , and compensated for the high acidity of unripe Riesling grapes by adding sugar to the crushed fruit . Some winemakers added so much , and sold the bottles at such low prices , that Riesling developed a reputation as cheap , sweet plonk . <p> Bitterolf says the trend has been reversed in the last decade , as producers focus on terroir and add little or no extra sugar . The warmer temperatures make that task easier . And Americans have grown to appreciate it : The German Wine Institute , an industry agency , says Americans imported about 1.5 million cases of Riesling in 2016 , part of a steady increase over the last 15 years .
