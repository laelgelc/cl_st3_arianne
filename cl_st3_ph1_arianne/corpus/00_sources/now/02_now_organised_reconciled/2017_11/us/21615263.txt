<h> Epic journeys and the changing nature of exploration <p> The job of explorers in the 21st century is not to conquer the far-flung reaches of the globe , but instead to protect them , the leader of numerous polar and mountaineering expeditions said Thursday during the season 's inaugural Aspen U forum . <p> The talk featured Eric Larsen , mountaineer and author of " On Thin Ice : An Epic Final Quest into the Melting Arctic . " The event , which took place Thursday evening , centered on Larsen 's 460-mile trek across the Arctic Ocean to the North Pole . A crowd of 120 people gathered in the Limelight Hotel to hear the story behind the expedition as well as Larsen 's intimate viewpoint on climate change . <p> Auden Schendler , vice president for sustainability at the Aspen Skiing Co. and Aspen U director , said the purpose of the Aspen U Speaker Series is to bring a " graduate-level , world-class speaker series modeled on old Aspen , where you can drink and hang out with the speaker . " <p> Larsen , whose @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ two-hour presentation , completed , with hiking partner Ryan Walters , a nearly two-month expedition to the North Pole in May 2014 . The journey required them to pull 325-pound sleds full of gear in temperatures of nearly 70 degrees below zero . The " very narrow margin of safety , " as Larsen put it , barely stopped there . They were trailed by polar bears , struggled through monstrous storms with complete white-outs and watched their food supply diminish in the final week . Larsen said the mental test was just as tough as the intense physical aspect of the excursion . He quipped , " Go home and draw an ice bath . Sit in it and stare at a blank piece of paper for 12 hours . That 's what it was like . His personal motto goes , " Life is just time between expeditions . " He has spent the past 20 years exploring peaks few people will ever see . He holds the American record for most trips to the North and South poles , and finished the first summer expedition to the North Pole . He also completed a " Save the Poles " tour , which involved traversing the South Pole , North Pole and Mount Everest within 365 days . Sea ice is measured by maximum and minimum " extents " each year , or the surface amount of sea ice located on each pole . According to a recent NASA study , sea ice on the Arctic reached the " second lowest minimum extent of the satellite era " in September 2016 .
