<h> Call of the wild : Minnesota women explore public lands , by bicycle <p> Provided by Women on Wheels for Wild LandsStarting at the Pacific Ocean on Sept. 9 , from left : Hannah Scout Field , Katie Ledermann , Alex Benjamin and Ariana Amini . They plan to finish their trek in South Carolina in December , exploring parks and other public land along the way . <p> Four young women from Mahtomedi and St. Paul have undertaken a journey of enlightenment -- by bicycle . <p> They 're on a quest to learn all they can about threats to public lands , crossing the nation from coast to coast on a 4,871-mile expedition that will end in South Carolina in December . <p> " We have a very big and fascinating country , " said Hannah Scout Field , of Mahtomedi . " It 's amazing how much you can learn about meeting people and seeing places and experiencing how generous every part of the country is . " <p> Field and two of her 2013 classmates from Mahtomedi High School , Alex Benjamin and Katie Ledermann @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ met as sophomores . Now they 've joined their friend Ariana Amini , of St. Paul , for what they call " Women on Wheels for Wild Lands , " a three-month tour of the southern United States . <p> " It feels good to be doing this trip for something beyond ourselves , " Benjamin said recently during a two-day layover in Austin , Texas . <p> The women , all of them now 22 , mapped their journey well in advance , calculating distances and arranging learning opportunities and personal encounters at every stop . They try to bike 80 miles a day even as they stop to explore federal and state lands , including parks . <p> The four travel about 80 miles a day . They carry all their gear , with no support vehicle , and hope to set an example for women . <p> Many people know that political decisions can shrink or restrict public lands , but the women are learning that other threats are looming as well . We think they 're here forever , they 're never going away , and that 's not the case , " said Benjamin , a chemical engineering graduate of Montana State University in Bozeman . <p> The federal government owns about 640 million acres of land , according to the Congressional Research Service . Four major agencies manage most of it : the Bureau of Land Management , National Park Service , Fish and Wildlife Service and Forest Service . The Department of Defense administers an additional 11.4 million acres for military bases and training ranges . <p> The states own another 197 million acres , with more than half of that acreage in Alaska . <p> The Minnesota women -- now accompanied on their journey by " honorary woman " Peter Kvale , 23 , of Duluth -- have been interviewing everybody from private citizens to park managers and city officials along the way . They 've experienced several flat tires and many drivers passing much too close , but they said they 're setting an example for other women . <p> " Women are capable of doing everything they want to do , " Field said .
