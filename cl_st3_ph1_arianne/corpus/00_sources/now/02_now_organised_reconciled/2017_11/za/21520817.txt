<h> Feeling hot , hot , hot : 2017 is set to be the " hottest year on record " <p> The United Nations ' World Meteorological Organization have revealed that 2017 is going to go down as a scorcher of a year . <p> According to WMO , Even if the cyclical weather events , which bump up global temperatures every three to seven years , are included this is still going to be one of the three warmest years ever . The other two warmest ever were caused by the El Nino phenomena . <p> This was revealed when WMO issued its annual State of the Global Climate report at talks in Bonn . <p> And , if you thought it was n't bad enough already , average temperatures from 2013 through 2017 are also likely to be the highest since accurate measuring began more than a century ago . <p> According to the agency , 30 percent of the world 's population now experience " extreme hot temperatures " for at least several days each year . <p> " The past three years have all been in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " said WMO Secretary-General Petteri Taalas . <p> 2017 has been one heck of a weather phenomenon with record-breaking hurricanes in the Caribbean and Atlantic , with one reaching as far as Ireland ; deadly monsoon flooding in the Indian sub-continent ; and a quarter of a million people in Somalia driven from their homes due to persistent drought , the WMO found . <p> Many of these events ... bear the telltale sign of climate change caused by increased greenhouse gas concentrations from human activity , " Taalas said in a statement . <p> " The impacts of extreme weather this year give us a taste of things to come under a warming climate , " said Richard Betts , a professor of climate impacts at the Met Office Hadley Centre in England , commenting on the report . <p> And there was more bad news . The icecaps are melting and could disappear by as early as 2030 in " high altitude regions " . <p> This all sounds familiar , does n't it ? Well , that 's because 2016 was another one of the " hottest ever " years .
