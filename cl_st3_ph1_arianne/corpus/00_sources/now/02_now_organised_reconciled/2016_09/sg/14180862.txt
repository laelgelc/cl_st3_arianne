<h> Protecting Mother Nature -- in Singapore and beyond <p> A LANDMARK climate change agreement adopted in Paris , in December last year -- which aims to hold the increase in global average temperature below two degrees Celsius , and to mitigate the adverse impact of climate change -- has now been formally ratified by China and the United States , ahead of the Group of 20 ( G20 ) summit . Notwithstanding the concerns over the lack of enforcement mechanisms , to ensure parties of the treaty actually follow promises to reduce their carbon emissions and pollution , ratification of the agreement by the world 's largest carbon emitters ( and also the world 's largest economies ) should encourage other countries to follow suit . <p> 180 countries have signed the Paris Agreement , but it will only go into effect if it is ratified by 55 countries , covering at least 55 per cent of global carbon emissions . China accounts for over 20 per cent of global carbon emissions , while the United States accounts for approximately 17.9 per cent . <p> Singapore signed @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ratified it . Beyond climate change , nevertheless , the protection of biodiversity and ecosystems is also important , and the National Parks Board is now working to conserve 46 local species -- 31 plant species , seven land animal species , and eight marine species -- to therefore bring them back from potential extinction . Living in isolated habitats , these species are vulnerable to external threats . Diversity within environmental ecosystems and of the flora and fauna they house is an essential component of Nature , yet this diversity has of late been threatened by human impact and destruction . <p> On the other hand , as Singapore conserves these species , it is working to rid itself of the Aedes mosquito menace .
