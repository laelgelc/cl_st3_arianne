<h> Relevant offers <p> Contamination of town water in Havelock North and Pahiatua has brought our attention again to the state of our water . <p> While environmental whistleblower Dr Mike Joy has long alerted us to cattle-transmitted , disease-producing pathogens in our streams and rivers , toxins in our drinking water has considerably higher stakes . <p> But there are higher stakes still . Contaminated water is one thing , not enough water is another proposition altogether . Globally , our relationship with the elixir of life is changing . While once we never imagined that the world 's water supply would run out , experts are now concerned that it could . <p> Over much of the earth the demand for fresh water now surpasses the sustainable yield of aquifers ( underground stores of fresh water ) and rivers . Satellite-based information shows that overexploitation of groundwater is depleting aquifers in China , India and the United States , and diminishing supplies of irrigation water are already helping to shrink the grain harvest in China and the Middle East . <p> This plundering of the planet @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and climate change is of serious concern . Not all ground water is replenishable , and with less rainfall projected in some areas of the world and the receding of glaciers as a result of climate change , the availability of fresh water threatens to further destabilize the world . <p> As has been said , if we persist in denying the seriousness of this crisis we could be fighting future wars over water . <p> It goes without saying that with a rising global population the demand for food , and the water that produces it , is also growing . Globally , food production accounts for 70 per cent of our water useage . The food we eat is both primary and secondary produce . <p> While grain is primary produce , the production of meat is secondary , because grain has to be grown to feed to the animals we then consume . Secondary produce -- meat - is far more resource-intensive than primary produce . Animal agriculture has a huge water footprint , contributing enormously to the current shortage of water . One of the ways of helping the problem is by making agriculture less thirsty , and the simplest and most effective way is to consume less meat . <p> Our appetite for flesh contributes to virtually every major category that threatens the planet -- climate change , deforestation , air and water pollution , soil erosion , biodiversity loss , hunger and the spread of disease -- and water depletion . To quote John Robbins in The Food Revolution : How your diet can help save your life and the world - ' You can save more water by not eating a pound of beef than you would be not showering for six months ' . <p> We need to remember that water is no longer an inexhaustible resource . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the simplest things we can do is to stop eating meat . Converting grazing land to food crops will mean more fresh water is available , more food can be produced to feed the hungry , and water supply will be sustainable into the future . <p> In this case , as in many others , the solution could be quite simple . We can help divert a future world water crisis with our knives , forks and chopsticks.
