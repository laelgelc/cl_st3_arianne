<p> Far above the Arctic Circle in Greenland lies an abandoned US military base , buried in the ice . <p> The problem , researchers suspect , is that eventually climate change will uncover the base , releasing large quantities of unpleasant waste , including low level radioactive coolant . <p> While that could be unpleasant , and lead to some international political tensions , it may not happen this century . In 1951 , the US and Denmark - which controlled Greenland at that time - signed the Defence of Greenland Agreement . <p> The USACE built Camp Century in 1959 about 200km inland from the US Armed Forces ' Thule Air Base on the northwest coast of Greenland . The reaction chamber of the portable nuclear generator was taken away . <p> Camp Century had been built on the main divide of the Greenland ice sheet , in the dry snow zone of the ice sheet , where almost no surface melting happened . It was assumed the base would be " preserved for eternity " by perpetual snowfall , the paper said . <p> Researcher William Colgan , a glaciologist from York University in Toronto and lead author of the paper , said the camp and its abandoned waste were now " safety nestled " under about 30 metres of ice . <p> " The original disposal intent of the camp was that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ it will continue to bury the base , and then the base will get pushed further , further , further , further , further , down into the ice sheet until eventually it will start to flow in with the ice and come out 10,000 years later at the edge of the ice sheet , " Colgan said . <p> He expects the ice sheet covering the base to start melting by the end of the century , and possibly sooner . <p> Waste buried under the ice is estimated to include 200,000 litres of diesel fuel , 240,000 litres of waste water , including sewage , and an unknown quantity of low-level radioactive coolant from the nuclear generator . <p> Colgan said the biggest ecological threat was thought to be posed by PCBs - polychlorinated biphenyls - long-lived chemicals , which could damage the Arctic ecosystem . <p> The paper said it was unclear whether the abandoned wastes remained US property .
