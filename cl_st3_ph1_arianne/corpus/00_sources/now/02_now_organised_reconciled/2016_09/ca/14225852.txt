He is , however , and expert in forestry and he addressed a small crowd during a Muskoka Lakes Association summer seminar in Port Carling on Aug. 21 . <p> " I have not seen a year like this , " said Munro , referring to the hot dry weather that lead to drought conditions and tinder dry forests . He said weather patterns will go from cool blue to hot red and we will need to begin to mitigate the effects . He said this would mean a need for assisted migration of things like Tennessee maple into the area . <p> He said tree species would n't be the only thing to disappear from local woodlots . Animal species , including moose who enjoy the cooler temperatures , would also likely migrate north . <p> " We need to be adaptive and reliant in the woodlot here , " he said , stressing the importance of maintaining healthy forests , being aware of the invasive species and troubled trees . <p> According to Munro , climate change and global economy could see woodlot managers warding off a greater number of invasive species .
