<p> It looks like a giant waterbed , covering an area the size of four football fields and surrounded by a chest-high concrete perimeter wall . <p> As Donald Trump might say , it 's huge ! And like the wackadoodle U.S. presidential candidate , this massive vessel , constructed next to West Fraser Timber 's Slave Lake Pulp mill , is also full of gas . In this case , biogas . <p> Technically , the big waterbed is known as an anaerobic digester . It 's a reactor in which micro-organisms called methanogens -- the same kind of bacteria found in our stomachs -- break down organic waste in the mill 's effluent , in the absence of oxygen , to produce methane . <p> None of this is visible to the naked eye , of course . All the biological magic happens in the about nine-metres deep , geotextile-lined basin that sits beneath the big waterbed 's multi-layered , flexible white geomembrane cover .
