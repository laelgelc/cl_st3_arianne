<h> Biotech mergers bad news for producers and consumers <p> The proposed US$66-billion merger between Bayer CropScience and Monsanto has critics saying it and other mergers between biotech giants will reduce competition in the sector and , in the long run , have a negative overall impact . <p> There 's one thing proponents and opponents of biotechnology can agree on -- continued consolidation in the seed and crop chemical industry is bad news for farmers and for society in general . <p> With two mega-mergers announced within days of each other this month , there is a rising chorus of concerns over the effects on market power and perhaps even more importantly , on innovation investment . <p> " Canola farmers are especially concerned about the effect of the merger on the offerings that will be available to them in the future , " the Canadian Canola Growers Association said in a release . " A reduction in competition could potentially increase crop input prices , curtail product choice and diminish the incentive for future innovation in canola seed and chemistry . Most farmers have embraced GMOs because they build herbicide tolerance into their crop . That allows them to spray their crops with the herbicides glyphosate or glufosinate , which kill everything in the field except the crop . <p> The network generally says no to GMOs , largely because of concerns over their safety but also because of the trend toward a small number of companies gaining control over the global seed supply . <p> That concern is shared by other industry observers now that plant breeding has largely been shifted over to the private domain . <p> Some even characterize the US$66 billion deal between Bayer CropScience and Monsanto as the " death of innovation , " as companies take the fast track to growth through mergers and acquisitions rather than relying on ' organic ' @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the third such announcement inside of a year in the seed and crop protection sector .
