<h> Brian Jones : We do n't snort oil , so do n't call us ' addicts ' <p> They 're talking about oil this week at Memorial University . An international conference called Petrocultures 2016 : The Offshore began Wednesday and concludes Saturday . <p> Brian Jones <p> The public is allowed to attend alongside oil experts from Norway , Texas and other petroleum-laden places . As can be expected of an academic event , some of the sessions gush with esoterica : " Oil that Sows Culture : Utopian Visions of Petrosocialism in Urban Space , " " Petropoetics : Phenomenology of Oil in Post-Soviet Russian Culture " and such . <p> I scanned it for a session I would be most interested in -- " Blaming the Consumer " -- but it was n't on the program . <p> The closest thing to it is a public forum Thursday night -- which has n't happened as of this writing -- that " will contemplate how our lives are directly and indirectly implicated by our reliance on oil . ... What would a post-oil world look @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Predictably , the forum 's description also refers to " our addiction to fossil fuels . " <p> Addiction implies there is an addict . I am an addict . You are an addict . Our friends and neighbours are addicts . There might be a hermit living deep in the woods who is n't an addict , but even he -- or she -- would need some modern tools and devices that inevitably entail burning oil at some point . <p> " Yes , as a matter of fact we do carry solar-powered TV sets . Where would you like it delivered ? " <p> There are popular ways to fight the addiction . Buy a bike . Use public transit . Send a donation to the David Suzuki Foundation , so it can redouble its efforts to shame other addicts . <p> Such noble ventures will not stop global warming , nor will they put a dent in worldwide use of oil . Assertions otherwise are mere petro-propaganda ( see : David Suzuki Foundation , et al ) . <p> The second part of the equation -- or , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . If people are addicted to oil , and must wean themselves off it , there have to be realistic , affordable , clean alternatives they can choose instead . <p> Those have not yet materialized . Driving an electric or hybrid car , or heating a house with solar panels , is not an option that average-income earners can afford . For most people , burning oil is a necessity , not a moral failing . <p> ( At this point , a cynic might ask : did the Texan oil expert cycle to St. John 's ? Did the Norwegian oil expert paddle across the North Atlantic ? Not being a cynic , I 'll suggest they likely burned jet fuel ... out of necessity . ) <p> Petroculture is indeed a dilemma , especially since the world will continue to rely on oil for the next half-century or so . Consumers need alternatives . Until those alternatives are available , there is no point insulting people by calling them addicts . <p> Consumers ' reliance on oil is not a personal fault , nor an immoral choice . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ be proper to discuss addiction . <p> " Man , I 'm telling you this for your own good -- you need to kick your oil habit and go thermal .
