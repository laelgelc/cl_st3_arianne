( Weather Underground via AP ) <p> People living in Nova Scotia 's drought-parched southwest are praying the rain forecast for the next few days will help dry wells and lawns . <p> Some residents in the Municipality of the District of Argyle have struggled with dry wells for up to six weeks , while others have dried up for the first time in 50 years , says warden Aldric d'Entremont . <p> " We have a lot of people with no water or very little , " d'Entremont told the Chronicle Herald . <p> The latest forecast is calling for up to 15 mm of rainfall across the region @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ at least that amount to replenish the water table in his area . <p> " I hope this time that it does n't shift or anything because we certainly need it badly , " said d'Entremont of the forecast . " It 's going to take a lot of rain . " <p> However , the municipality 's website warns that long-term weather patterns do not appear to favour any major rainfall . Nor does the seasonal outlook for September , October and November show any clear signs of precipitation . <p> In the meantime , Argyle residents are picking up four-litre jugs of drinking water from local fire halls and schools are also offering people showers . The municipality first started distributing drinking water 10 days ago . <p> The municipality will now be procuring water tanks for residents , but d'Entremont said that many people already have them . <p> " It 's not so bad for hygiene but inconvenient of course , " said d'Entremont . <p> As the drought grinds on , provincial parks in Lunenburg , Shelburne and Queens Counties are opening their showers and drinking @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ week until further notice , from 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. : <p> Graves Island Provincial Park , Lunenburg Co . <p> Rissers Beach Provincial Park , Lunenburg Co . <p> Thomas Raddall Provincial Park , Queens Co . <p> The Islands Provincial Park , Shelburne Co . <p> Valley View Provincial Park in Annapolis County has a potable water supply , but shower and toilet facilities are not available . <p> While these measures will offer short-term relief for parched Nova Scotians , prolonged drought and more extreme weather events could become the new normal for Nova Scotia as climate change takes hold . <p> According to Climate Change Nova Scotia , the province will see more precipitation but also higher rates of evaporation due to warmer temperatures , which will lead to a drop in water levels . Lower water levels may also increase the risk of ponds and rivers being contaminated by dangerous pathogens such as E-coli . <p> Coastal communities are especially vulnerable , as falling water tables and rising sea levels could lead to seawater contamination and render well water undrinkable.
