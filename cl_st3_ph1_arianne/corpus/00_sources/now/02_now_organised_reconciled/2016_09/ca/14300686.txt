<h> Mike Smyth : Christy Clark and John Horgan display an utter lack of leadership on pipeline politics <p> A tanker receives a load of oil at the Westridge loading dock in Burnaby , the western terminus of Kinder Morgan 's Trans Mountain pipeline . A proposed expansion of the pipeline is proving divisive among voters , making many politicians indecisive on the issue.JONATHAN HAYWARD / THE CANADIAN PRESS <p> Pipeline politics in British Columbia are stickier and more toxic than the stuff that actually flows through the pipe . <p> With an election just eight months away , no leading politician wants to get dragged down into the tar-pit issues of pipeline projects that are so controversial and divisive among B.C. voters . <p> That 's why both NDP leader John Horgan and Premier Christy Clark are twisting themselves into political pretzels as a federal approval decision looms for the $6.8-billion expansion of Kinder Morgan 's Trans Mountain pipeline . <p> The project would twin an existing pipeline connecting Alberta 's oil sands to a terminal in Burnaby and pump heavy crude oil to the coast for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ waters would increase from five per month to 34 per month -- or from 60 tankers a year to 408 . <p> Voters are split on the project . About half of Metro Vancouver residents ( 49 per cent ) oppose the pipeline while 33 per cent support it and the rest are not sure , according to an Insights West poll . <p> So , where do the two main political parties stand ? That 's where things get confusing , often deliberately . <p> Last week , Horgan suggested he was open to supporting the pipeline . <p> " I find it difficult to see how making Vancouver an export terminal for oil is in the interest of B.C. , " Horgan said . " But I can be persuaded . " <p> A flip-flop-flip ! Oh , how the Liberals love to see Horgan slip on an oil slick . <p> Keep in mind , this is the same John Horgan who signed a letter to the National Energy Board opposing the pipeline expansion because of climate change , oil spills and First Nations opposition . <p> As @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of flip-flopping -- and the powerful environmental wing of the NDP no doubt expressed internal displeasure at their wavering boss -- Horgan took to Twitter with a different message . <p> A flip-flop-flip ! ( Dix switched his position on the Kinder Morgan pipeline from " neutral " to " opposed " in the middle of the campaign . ) <p> Here 's the conundrum for Horgan : He wants to appear open to business , job creation , megaprojects and resource extraction , but he risks a backlash from environmentalists within his own party if he goes too far . <p> He is straining at the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ chain . The Liberals love it , because it makes him look weak and indecisive . <p> But the sharp-edged knife of pipeline politics cuts both ways . Clark 's " leadership " on the project has n't been clear and consistent either . <p> Despite the slings and arrows hurled at Horgan , Clark 's own position on the Trans Mountain expansion is n't much different : She is officially opposed to it , but could be persuaded to support it . <p> Clark has laid out five conditions that must be met for her to support the pipeline , including " world-leading " oil-spill prevention and response standards and a " fair share " of the project 's benefits for B.C. <p> But what does this actually mean ? When I asked the government which jurisdiction currently leads the world in oil-spill standards ( the ones we would have to surpass to meet Clark 's " world-leading " condition ) they would not name one . <p> Instead , the government pointed to a 2013 study on " world-class " oil-spill standards and what B.C. must do to meet @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 's current system . <p> But " world leading " and " world class " are two different things . " World leading " means B.C. must be the best in the world . <p> So , exactly what is Christy Clark demanding here ? Do n't bother asking her because she wo n't answer . <p> Ditto for her demand for a " fair share " of pipeline benefits . An official in the premier 's office told me a " fair share " agreement would have to be negotiated between the government and Kinder Morgan . <p> An official at Kinder Morgan told me an agreement would have to be negotiated among the provincial government , the federal government , Alberta and industry players -- not just Kinder Morgan . <p> So which is it ? What are the " benefits " British Columbia is seeking from the pipeline and where would they come from ? " Our five conditions have to be met , but we 're kind of agnostic about the timing . " <p> The timing is everything , though , is n't it ? The pipeline faces widespread opposition in Metro Vancouver , which contains 44 ridings in the election -- about half of all the ridings in B.C. <p> Clark could simply wait until after the election to take a final position .
