<h> Guest opinion : How to lessen risks of bad presidency <p> BRENT MEAD <p> 18 hrs ago <p> The season of electoral discontent is upon us . It is not -- or it should not be -- hard to understand why voters express historically high levels of disapproval with the two major party nominees for President , Hillary Clinton and Donald Trump . <p> Despite their different parties and platforms , I am incredibly bothered by the fact that the knee-jerk impulse of both candidates is to address various problems through a more powerful federal government . <p> Perhaps the discontent of voters suggests that they 're bothered , too . So I have a different solution . If Trump and Clinton are unacceptable candidates , then we should work to find ways to make the presidential election less consequential in our daily lives . That means reducing the power of the federal government and empowering our state and local decision-makers . <p> Federal agencies have plenty of power , and it significantly impacts Montanans ' ability to make a living . They can halt economic activity @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ fines of hundreds of thousands of dollars against family ranchers . Or raid Montana businesses in compliance with state -- but not federal -- law . <p> The reasons for federal policy vary , but they often ignore regional and local realities . Take Obama 's Clean Power Plan , which is the EPA 's misguided attempt to combat climate change . The plan 's top-down mandates hit Montana harder than any other state while ignoring the fact that Montana has stringent environmental protections in state law and in our state constitution . The plan could destroy more than 7,000 jobs in the state and cost Montana families $500 million in annual income . <p> Yes , we need to be responsible stewards of our abundant natural resources . But we need it do it our way . <p> That is the failure of centralizing more authority in Washington . Manhattan , Mont. , is vastly different from Manhattan , N.Y . When it comes to Washington , too often these two Manhattans are treated the same . Instead of further empowering federal officials to implement one-size-fits-all policies , we need @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ states the freedom to choose the path that best works for their citizens . <p> This requires more engagement , both in local government and with groups willing to step up and provide ideas to move the state forward . It requires that policymakers in Helena stop outsourcing their duties to D.C. and come up with actual solutions instead of slapping a " made in Montana " label on White House policy . Finally , it requires a sense of accountability for our decisions and their consequences . <p> Montana is a one big small town . We have the unique opportunity to get to know our elected officials personally . These local leaders will make poor policy choices from time to time . When those choices are made in Helena , we can call our local leaders to account for these decisions in a way that we can not ask Clinton or Trump , 2,000 miles away . <p> My organization , Montana Policy Institute , was founded on the idea that our state is a great place to live , work , and a raise a family , but @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to earn a living in Montana . When those choices are made locally , we can help each other , hold each other accountable , and change Montana for the better . <p> Moving policy decisions and debates down to the local level means that Montanans will have to weigh priorities and find a balance that works for our state . Montanans have struck that balance for more than 100 years and are fully up to the task . <p> When you are sick of presidential politics , think of ways to empower local communities . In the future , when we face choices like Clinton or Trump , it will matter a little less .
