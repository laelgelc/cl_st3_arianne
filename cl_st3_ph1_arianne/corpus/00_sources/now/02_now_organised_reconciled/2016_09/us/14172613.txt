<h> Future of shoreline depends on how LI confronts rising sea level <p> Long Island is changing , nowhere more so than on our coastline . Water and wind continue to sculpt our land . <p> We have not always responded well . We pump sand onto our beaches , watch it wash away , then pump sand again . We build too close to the water 's edge , despair when the sea rushes in , then build again . We protect one part of our shoreline with bulkheads and groins , see how that damages other parts of the shore , then remake those very same structures when they weaken . <p> That became imperative after superstorm Sandy in 2012 . Climate change is causing sea levels to rise and spawning more frequent bursts of extreme weather . We 've seen the damage that can result . <p> The threat of water claiming our shores may be a generation away , but what we do today will determine how well we can blunt that risk . <p> It 's against this backdrop that the 50-years-in-the-making flood @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ must be evaluated . It was pegged at $750 million in 2013 . It 's a lot of money ; let 's make sure we get it right . <p> The plan does have good elements , like its emphasis on re-establishing salt marshes to serve as natural defenses , while other aspects give us pause . But any debate over how to refine the granular details must be accompanied by an intense discussion of Long Island 's geographic future 30 and 50 and 80 years from now .
