<p> WASHINGTON : The way is now clear for a second term for the American president of the World Bank , Jim Yong Kim . <p> After a first term hailed by some Bank member states but marked by internal discord , Kim this month became the only candidate running for his own succession . <p> In keeping with an unbroken tradition , the US nominee will again fill the presidency at the World Bank , while the International Monetary Fund remains in European hands as Christine Lagarde , also unopposed , began a second term as managing director in July . <p> After nominations opened , no other country took the risk of trying to upset this established order -- unlike in 2012 , when the Nigerian Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala threw her hat in the ring to be leader of the development behemoth , which comprises 189 member states and employs 15,000 people . <p> Still , Kim 's record since taking office that year is not spotless . <p> A medical doctor and former president of Dartmouth College , Kim won plaudits for mobilizing the Bank against @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ against climate change , as well as for setting a goal of eradicating extreme poverty by 2030 all while expanding World Bank lending . <p> But he also had to contend with a high degree of internal dissent , stemming from an unpopular reorganization and a controversy in 2014 over bonuses granted to senior Bank officials . <p> The World Bank Staff Association last month denounced what it called a " crisis of leadership , " and in an open letter several former officials lamented what they said was the lack of a clear strategy . The Economist and Financial Times opposed automatically reappointing Kim and called for a more open process . <p> - ' Show of force ' -Despite all this , poor and developing countries still chose to support a second term for Kim , 56 , who will sit for interviews with the Bank 's board before being formally crowned next month .
