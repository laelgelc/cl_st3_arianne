<h> Sri Lanka and the Geneva Resolution - Was it an Example Used to Teach all Independent Minded Countries a Lesson . <p> Dr Palitha Kohona , Former Permanent Representative of Sri Lanka to the United Nations in New York responds to a series of questions posed by us . <p> Question 1 : Did a civil society led campaign cause the last government to lose office and a new Government to be elected in January 2015 ? Your comments ? <p> Answer . There seem to be two or three ideas mixed in this question . <p> To begin with , it is fanciful to suggest that a civil society led campaign led to the fall of the Rajapaksa government . Civil society may have played a role , but there were many more important factors that contributed @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ these were the disenchantment of important sections of the population with the performance of the government , influenced by civil society action , especially in the cities , a feeling of alienation among certain minorities , cost of living burdens caused mainly by international factors , allegations of nepotism , corruption and impunity which deeply disturbed the educated classes , the unacceptable behavior of certain individuals , etc . All played a role in the fall of that government . It is also important to remember that Mahinda Rajapaksa lost by a slim margin suggesting that a substantial part of the electorate still preferred him . <p> Secondly some elements of the international community may have welcomed the change . As we know , the international community consists , not only of a handful of Western countries which have a stranglehold on the global media , but of at least 193 members of the UN . The vast majority of them did neither welcome nor criticize the change . You will recall that the previous government also wanted time and space for this purpose . I recall that a representative group of ambassadors from New York led by the Japanese ambassador , who visited Sri Lanka in 2013 , agreed that Sri Lanka had achieved much in the short period since the end of the conflict than they had in their own countries and needed more time to realize the goal of reconciliation . Agitation in the North for immediate solutions are not going to help the reconciliation efforts as there is every possibility of a negative and unmanageable reaction in the South . <p> Thirdly , the UN Human Rights Council adopted resolution " Promoting Reconciliation , Accountability and Human Rights in Sri Lanka " in 2015 . Some have vehemently criticized the government 's decision to cosponsor the resolution . <p> It is a misconception to suggest that the UN HRC is tightening its grip on Sri Lanka . The HRC was never intended to have a grip on any country . The UN HRC has no powers of enforcement . Its resolutions carry a moral weight , particularly if the target country has cosponsored a resolution . But there is no mechanism for enforcing them . The UN HRC was intended to assist countries to improve their human rights performance not to act as a global human rights enforcer . The excessive focus on Sri Lanka and the constant hectoring on this issue intrigue me . We could compare the allegations concerning Sri Lanka and the situation in our wider region and I am certain that the focus on Sri Lanka alone would raise some interesting issues . <p> Question 3 : Do you believe that the Sri Lankan forces deliberately committed war crimes during the last stages of the war ? <p> Answer . See the report of USG Sir John Holmes to the Security Council on 26 March 2009 . <p> The LTTE must first bear prime responsibility for using civilians as a shield and as auxiliaries . LTTE spokesman Pullidevan is quoted as having said exactly this . <p> The government followed a strict policy of no civilian casualties . This was very clear . It would also have been self-destructive for the government to be careless about civilian casualties especially since its goal was to win the hearts and minds of the Tamil civilians and get the LTTE fighters to defect . 12,000 fighters actually surrendered in the end and the vast majority was rehabilitated and released .
