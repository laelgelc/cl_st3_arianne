<h> Omnibus savings bill passes lower house <p> The Turnbull government has fast-tracked $6.3 billion of budget savings through the House of Representatives a day after striking a deal with Labor . <p> The government 's so-called omnibus bill , which contains 20 measures ranging from cutting family benefits to climate change funding , was the first order of business on Thursday . <p> The bill and its amendments cleared the lower house with a shake of hands between Treasurer Scott Morrison and his Labor counterpart Chris Bowen . <p> The only dissenting voices came from the crossbench . <p> Labor 's frontbench lined up to boast about their efforts in securing what they saw as a fairer deal - supporting 20 of the 24 measures in the original bill - which the treasurer belittled as some sort of ' lap of honour ' . <p> ' The government is getting on with governing and the agreement reached on this bill demonstrates the ability of the government to get things done in this parliament , ' Mr Morrison told parliament . <p> The treasurer wants the bill passed @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Bowen argued Labor had led the debate on budget repair . <p> ' Today we lead the way as well , ' he told MPs . <p> Opposition families and social services spokeswoman Jenny Macklin warned Labor would oppose any future government move to further cut family payments or remove the energy supplement . <p> The amendments to the original bill allow the supplement to continue for eligible recipients , including those on Newstart and pensioners , drop the baby bonus and abolish a tax benefit supplement for families with incomes of more than $80,000 . <p> Greens MP Adam Bandt spoke against the ' dirty deal ' because it cuts half a billion dollars out of renewable energy . <p> Independent MP Andrew Wilkie also spoke against the bill , describing it as a ' blackhearted ' deal and accused Labor for being a ' pack of quislings ' for supporting it . <p> ' Just when the opposition should act like an opposition and stand up for the community and stand up for the environment , it 's rolled over to have its tummy tickled , ' he said @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ government has been served a fresh warning about the state of the budget . <p> The finance department has sounded the alarm in a briefing to its minister Mathias Cormann , casting renewed doubt over Australia 's path to surplus in 2020/21 . <p> ' The 2016 Pre-Election Economic and Fiscal Outlook reports a significant fiscal challenge for the government , ' the document , released to the ABC under Freedom of Information laws , said .
