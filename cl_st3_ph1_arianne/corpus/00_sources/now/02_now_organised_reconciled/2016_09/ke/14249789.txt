<p> Global warming is quickly becoming one of the biggest threats to long-term survival of wildlife . <p> The Kenya Wildlife Service yesterday said new hot spots of human-wildlife conflict have emerged as a result of the effects of climate change . Spokesman Paul Gathitu said Makueni is one of the hot spots . <p> He spoke a day after the Kenya Meteorological Service released a three-month forecast showing the possibility of human-wildlife conflict occurring as a result of scramble for scarce resources . On Monday , Kenya Meteorological Service acting director Peter Ambenje said with adequate preparation , the country can avoid some of the negative impacts by taking advantage of the short rains expected . <p> Gathitu said even though they have not seen the forecast , they will use it to mitigate human-wildlife conflict . <p> " They have not given us the report but we will consider it as we plan for La Ni ? a , " he told the Star on the phone . <p> La Ni ? a episodes represent periods of below average sea surface temperatures across the east-central Equatorial @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ weather patterns , which can lead to intense storm in some places and drought in others . <p> Human-wildlife conflict has been reported in Tsavo , Narok , Laikipia , Lamu , Kajiado and Meru . <p> The weather man said rainfall is expected to be distributed poorly as a result of ocean warming . <p> Ambenje urged urged government agencies to liaise with livestock farmers . " Close monitoring and contingency measures are necessary to cope with the situation , " he said .
