<p> With agriculture accounting for 32pc of total Irish GHG emissions last year , answering this question is also a critical element in achieving the national policy position on climate change . <p> It calls for an approach to carbon neutrality in the agriculture and land-use sector , including forestry , which does not compromise capacity for sustainable food production . <p> Ireland stands out among EU Member States in the high share of its total GHG emissions coming from agriculture . This reflects , partly , its limited heavy manufacturing base , but also the livestock-based nature of Irish farming . <p> Grazing livestock produce significant quantities of methane and nitrous oxide , which are only partially offset by the carbon sequestered in grassland soils . <p> The dependence of Irish agricultural production on grazing livestock has been shaped by three factors . The first of these is climate and soils . <p> Ireland 's temperate and moist climate , and a preponderance of soils with limited use , favour grass and tree cover rather than tillage or horticulture . Cattle and tillage farming are much more marginal , and depend on subsidy payments to be viable . <p> Record-high milk prices in 2014 , and the removal of milk quotas in April 2015 , has resulted in a significant increase in milk production . Farm payments under the EU 's Common Agricultural Policy ( CAP ) are now mostly decoupled from production , and do not discriminate in favour of or against particular land use uses , whether grassland , tillage , energy crops or forestry , provided certain conditions are met . <p> However , beef and sheep productions are protected by means of high border tariffs against lower-cost competition from outside the EU . If these industries had to compete at world market prices , their scale would be downsized considerably . Factoring in environmental damage costs would further reduce the level of animal production . <p> Irish farmers will continue to specialise predominantly in livestock farming . Sustainable production contributes to jobs and exports . However , the agricultural sector is covered by Ireland 's limits on GHG emissions . <p> EPA projections show that agriculture will account for 45pc of Irish GHG emissions in the sectors outside the Emissions Trading System in both 2020 and 2030 , without additional measures .
