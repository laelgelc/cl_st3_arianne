<h> Copious rainfall will boost agricultural yield in 2017 NMets <p> Akosile said : " The rains arrived around May and then increased toward the end of June and took some two weeks break , in what we refer to now as ' the July Break ' . <p> Prof. Clement Akosile , the President of Nigerian Meterological Society , says the copious rainfall so far recorded across the country will boost agricultural yield this year . He said this in an interview with News Agency of Nigeria in Lagos on Friday . He said that as of now , there was nothing significantly different from the usual that could negatively affect agricultural yield . Akosile said : " The rains arrived around May and then increased toward the end of June and took some two weeks break , in what we refer to now as ' the July Break ' . " We expect the rains to start coming continuously by September to October . " Except if there are some disasters or modifications in the rate that are unprecedented that there will be changes in the outcome of agricultural yields . " Akosile said that the effect of climate change on the rainfall patterns was calculated over several years and not just in one or two years . He said that the flooding caused by torrential rainfall in some parts of the country was as a result of changes in the landscapes and not direct consequences of climate change . He said : " As land structure is being modified for water passage , so water will be looking for its own way . " Besides , Akosile said that as people and governments were retrieving land from seas and oceans , especially in the coastal areas , the sea would surge and push inwards , thereby causing flooding . He said that lands and forests were being altered to build roads and solid structures without making pragmatic efforts to maintain the balance in nature for water passage . Akosile said that as the disruption continued unabated , even small rainfall would cause flooding and sack people from their houses , while carrying their belongings away .
