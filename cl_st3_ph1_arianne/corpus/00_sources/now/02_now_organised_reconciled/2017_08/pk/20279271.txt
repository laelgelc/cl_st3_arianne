<p> " In years with anomalously high spring air temperatures , elderberry fruited several weeks earlier and became available during the period when salmon spawned in tributary streams , " the shared . <p> " Bears departed salmon spawning streams , where they typically kill 25 to 75 per cent of the salmon , to forage on berries on adjacent hillsides " . <p> " This prey-switching behaviour attenuated an iconic predator-prey interaction and likely altered the many ecological functions that result from bears foraging on salmon . <p> " If these trends continue , by 2070 , the average onset of berry availability would occur during the average peak of salmon availability . " <p> They also added that coastal brown bears would continue to depend on healthy runs of salmon to " meet much of their energy needs " . <p> The researchers , from Oregon State University , Montana University , the US Fish and Wildlife Service and other institutions , said that climate change was altering the seasonal timing of life cycle events in organisms across the planet " . <p> But the researchers said climate-induced synchronisation could also create " novel interactions " . <p> The bears ' switch to berries could have knock-on ecological effects . " Climate change is altering the seasonal timing of biological events , effectively rescheduling the potential interactions among species , " the researchers added . <p> " Bears switched from eating salmon to elderberries , disrupting an ecological link that typically fertilises terrestrial ecosystems and generates high mortality rates for salmon .
