<h> add The News to homescreen <h> Editorial <p> August 6 , 2017 <h> Share <p> Advertisement <h> Taking the heat <p> We often think of climate change or global warming only in vague , abstract terms . But let 's attempt to paint a more vivid picture of the reality that could lie ahead . Try to imagine tens of thousands of people migrating from South Asia simply because it is too hot to live there . Also imagine millions of people around the world unable to develop because of protein shortages in food . According to a study produced by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology ( MIT ) , by the end of the century or the year 20100 , large tracts of South Asia may simply be too hot for people to live in . India , Pakistan and Bangladesh would be the worst affected countries with at least 1.5 million people hit by a situation which does not allow crops to grow normally and in which life can not be sustained because of the heat and drying up of water resources . While the Middle East will also face extremely high temperatures , the greater resources available to these nations would allow them to keep people safe . A study by Harvard University also speaks of protein loss on important staple crops including rice and wheat because of global warming . Experts admit they can not say why the nutrients are leached away but confirm this is an established phenomenon which could affect 150 million people mainly in Sub Saharan 2Africa and South Asia by 2050 . <p> The year 2050 is not that far away . Already , we have seen the consequences of severe heatwaves such as the one in 2015 . Yet despite these enormous hazards we do not talk enough about global warming and related environmental issues . Pakistan is one of the countries worst affected by the emission of greenhouses gases from other nations in the region , even though it produces very few gases itself . Policymakers in the country , along with other regional leaders , need to devote more time and energy to this serious issue . Experts from India , Pakistan and Bangladesh must come together to consider how cropping patterns have changed and what can be done in the future to try and ward off the threat of global warming and stifling heatwaves . Many in cities already suffer higher temperatures each summer than those experienced on average over past decades . This then is an issue that can not be taken lightly and needs to be taken up at the highest level with experts giving their opinion on a crisis that could lie not very far ahead .
