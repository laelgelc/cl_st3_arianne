<h> Email <p> UNITED NATIONS : In a notice delivered to the United Nations , the Trump administration has declared its intention to withdraw from the Paris climate change accord , but has said it will continue to take part in negotiations on the matter to safeguard its interests . <p> The notice delivered on Friday is the first one informing the world body that the administration plans to pull out of the 2015 agreement that has won the support of nearly 200 countries , according to the UN 's website . <p> The Paris accord was signed by former US president Barack Obama and was supposed to be ratified by the Congress . <p> President Donald Trump had announced in June that the US would leave the agreement . <p> US to continue participating in climate change talks , it adds <p> However , a statement issued by the State Department has now said the government will continue to participate in the negotiations , including talks aimed at implementing the Paris deal " to protect US interests and ensure all future policy options remain open to the administration " . <p> Under terms of the Paris accord , the US ca n't fully withdraw from it until Nov 4 , 2020 -- one day after the next presidential election in the country . The next US president could decide to rejoin the agreement if Trump does n't win a second term . <p> In addition , the US ca n't even formally notify the United Nations that it is withdrawing from the deal until 2019 . As a result , Friday 's notice is a largely symbolic statement with no legal weight . <p> International diplomats are still holding out the hope that Trump might change his mind , or reach some kind of compromise that would allow the United States to stay in . And they argued that Friday 's statement leaves some wiggle room for the US to remain in the accord . <p> In its statement the State Department said Washington was still committed to reducing its greenhouse gas emissions , added AFP news agency . <p> " We will continue to reduce our greenhouse gas emissions through innovation and technology breakthroughs , and work with other countries to help them access and use fossil fuels more cleanly and efficiently and deploy renewable and other clean energy sources , " it said . <p> While Trump has said he is open to a renegotiation of the Paris pact , the suggestion was swiftly shot down by fellow world leaders who said it was non-negotiable . <p> Speaking on a visit to Paris last month , Trump again raised the prospect of a change in policy by saying " something could happen " regarding US participation in the accord but gave no details . <p> Friday 's statement reiterated that Trump was " open to re-engaging " in the pact if the US could " identify terms that are more favourable to it , its businesses , its workers , its people , and its taxpayers " . <p> Andrew Steer , president of the US non-profit World Resources Institute , said the letter implied a level of ambiguity that could indicate a desire to remain engaged . <p> " The United States could engage constructively in those negotiations on an issue such as transparency , " he said . <p> " But a climate loner that is intent on withdrawing from the Paris Agreement will not be listened to if it aims to weaken or undermine the accord in any way . " <p> The United States is the world 's second biggest producer of greenhouse gases after China and its withdrawal was seen as a body blow to the Paris agreement .
