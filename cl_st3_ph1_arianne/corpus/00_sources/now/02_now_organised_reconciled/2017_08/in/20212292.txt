<h> ' Need greater water literacy to prevent conflict ' <p> Rosamma Thomas <p> TNN Aug 26 , 2017 , 20:31 IST <p> Rajendra singh , waterman . <p> JAIPUR : Rajendra Singh , also called ' Waterman ' for his work in conservation , was in Jaipur on Saturday as part of a cross-country tour promoting " water literacy " . " Human beings are part of nature . Anyone who does not respect the five elements can not genuinely feel any love for humans , " he told TOI in an exclusive interview . <p> Singh is spreading the word about the Bijapur Convention in Karnataka after Independence Day this year , where scientists , technocrats , academics and people interested in conservation from 101 river basins in the country agreed to create greater awareness about that dwindling precious resource , water . The convention also agreed that water conflicts between states must ideally not be taken to the judiciary but find resolution through dialogue . <p> Singh has been travelling between Kashmir and Kanyakumari , Guwahati and Goa since May this year spreading awareness about measures that should be taken to prevent flood and drought . " We risk having a Cauvery dispute-type situation over the sharing of the Mahanadi between Odisha and Chhattisgarh , " he said . <p> " I have about 40 years of work experience and have travelled across the country twice before . I have seen things change before my eyes . If you compare from the time of independence , we now have 10 times more land in the country likely to be affected by drought ; eight times more areas are prone to flooding . " Singh has been advocating for greater forestation and soil and water conservation to revive rivers . <p> In his childhood at Daula village of Baghpat district of UP , he said , he did not know water scarcity at all . " The Ganga and Yamuna flowed nearby ; there was never any water scarcity . Even so , I remember that when I dropped a pot of water , my grandfather had said that I would have to account for the loss before God . " He recalled the respect his grandparents felt for water sources . " When I was about 11 , we travelled three days by bullock cart from the village to Haridwar , where my grandmother wanted me to take a dip in the Ganga . She took off my clothes , wet them and placed them on my head - it was November , we were there for the Karthick Snan - that cold was enough to make any boy want to pee . Before I entered the river , she told me I should head off and relieve myself a little way away . That was the care she took to see that the little boy did not dirty the river . " <p> Water stress is beginning to case rifts between nations , he said , offering Syria as a case in point . " By 1990 , construction of the Ataturk Dam in Turkey was completed , and the water of the Euphrates , which would flow unhindered into Syria , was dammed . This is also the dam that has the largest reservoirs in the world . With the water flow of the river disturbed , traditional agriculture in Syria - which has among the most ancient sites of agriculture in the world - took a drubbing . There was mass migration to Europe . The beginnings of the problem can be traced to water . " <p> Recalling Gandhi , Singh said the earth can provide for every man 's need , but can not sustain greed . " What we need is decentralized management of our natural resources like water , with the community taking the lead in conservation . This current corporate-driven democracy is incapable of nurturing natural resources . We need to go back to the concept of ' shubh-labh ' - not just profit , but benefit for all . <p> Singh underlined the need for adaptation to climate change and measures to prevent pollution . " Our current agricultural practices are set to traditional rhythms of nature , but with rainfall patterns changing , farmers too must be enlightened about the need for changing crop patterns . We also need to be wary of cultivating water-guzzling crops like paddy or sugarcane in water-scarce areas .
