<h> Almost 100 volcanoes discovered under Antarctica ice sheets <p> Researchers say west Antarctica could be a hotspot for active volcanoes in coming years , as significant warming in the region caused by climate change has begun to affect ice sheets . Photo : AFP <p> London : Scientists have uncovered the largest volcanic region on the Earth -- consisting of almost 100 volcanoes -- 2km below the surface of Antarctica 's ice sheet . <p> Researchers at Edinburgh University in the UK revealed a staggering 91 volcanoes , adding to the 47 others that had been discovered previously , with the highest as tall as the Eiger , which stands at almost 4,000 m in Switzerland . <p> The newly discovered volcanoes range in height from 100 m to 3,850 m . All of them are covered in thick layers of ice . <p> These active peaks are concentrated in a region known as the west Antarctic rift system , which stretches 3,500 km from Antarctica 's Ross ice shelf to the Antarctic peninsula , researchers said . <p> " We had not expected to find anything like that number . We have almost trebled the number of volcanoes known to exist in west Antarctica , " Robert Bingham , from Edinburgh University , told the Guardian . <p> " We also suspect there are even more on the bed of the sea that lies under the Ross ice shelf , it is very likely this region will turn out to be the densest region of volcanoes in the world , greater even than east Africa , where mounts Nyiragongo , Kilimanjaro , Longonot and all the other active volcanoes are concentrated , " said Bingham . <p> Researchers said that any activity of this range may have crucial implications for the rest of the planet . " If one of these volcanoes were to erupt it could further destabilise west Antarctica 's ice sheets . Anything that causes the melting of ice -- which an eruption certainly would -- is likely to speed up the flow of ice into the sea , " said Bingham . <p> Researchers also pointed to an alarming trend that most volcanism in the world at present is in regions that have only recently lost their glacier covering -- after the end of the last ice age . <p> This could happen in west Antarctica , where significant warming in the region caused by climate change has begun to affect its ice sheets , researchers said .
