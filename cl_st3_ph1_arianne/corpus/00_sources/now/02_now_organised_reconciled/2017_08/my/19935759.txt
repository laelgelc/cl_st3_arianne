<h> Nation <h> On track to a clean , green link across the nation <p> PUTRAJAYA : Both the High Speed Rail ( HSR ) and the East Coast Rail Link ( ECRL ) can reduce CO2 emissions by millions of tonnes with more people leaving their cars behind in favour of public transport . <p> Natural Resources and Environ ? ment Minister Datuk Seri Dr Wan Junaidi Tuanku Jaafar said many people travelled by car for 12 or 15 hours just to reach Kelantan from Kuala Lumpur , especially during Hari Raya . <p> " Once this ( ECRL ) goes through , people will not travel by car any more . <p> " Can you imagine the millions of tonnes of CO2 not discharged into the atmosphere ? <p> " In terms of carbon emissions , Malaysia will fulfil our place in the world in the ( UNFCC ) United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change with these two tracks -- the HSR to Singapore and the ECRL . <p> " That 's why I support this project , " said Dr Wan Junaidi . <p> He was commenting on concerns expressed by conservationists , particularly on the ECRL , which is expected to cut through Peninsular Malaysia 's Central Forest Spine , a stretch that is a series of reserve forests in Perak , Kelantan and Terengganu . <p> In a statement , environmental group Peka Malaysia called on the Government to engage local experts to oversee construction of the rail line . <p> She also claimed that the Environmental Impact Assessment ( EIA ) study carried on the project was not enough . <p> Starting from Wakaf Baru in Kelantan and ending in Port Klang , the 688km link will see 22 stations . <p> Passengers will be able to complete the journey in less than four hours . <p> Dr Wan Junaidi said he had also asked for stretches of the rail line that passed through forest reserves and sanctuaries to be built underground . <p> " If it passes through a smaller piece of forest where it ca n't go underground , I would like for viaducts to be built so that wildlife can pass through , " he said . <p> Asked if his requests had been fulfilled , Dr Wan Junaidi said he was told that the rail 's design would include " many kilometres of tunnels " , adding that he hoped to see the details . <p> Other areas such as Malay and orang asli reserve land as well as villages , he said , should also be taken into account in the construction of the rail . <p> To a question if he would emphasise on strict environmental standards , Dr Wan Junaidi said he had alerted the Prime Minister on the possibility of a new law to govern noise control . <p> " Noise is not yet an offence under Malaysian law . But the new law will make it an offence to cause noise pollution . <p> " So , any design with roads or trains must accommodate the law before it comes into effect , " said Dr Wan Junaidi.
