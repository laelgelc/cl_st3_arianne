<p> What 's made of sugar , can carry four people and travel at 50 miles an hour ? A biodegradable car - whose inventors say could be the next step in environmentally-friendly motoring . <p> The lightweight electric car , created by students in the Netherlands , is made of a resin derived from sugar beets and covered with sheets of Dutch-grown flax . <p> " Only the wheels and suspension systems are not yet of bio-based materials , " said Yanic van Riel , one of the developers from the TU/Ecomotive team at the Eindhoven University of Technology . <p> The structure of the car they have called Lina has a similar strength-weight ratio to that of fibreglass and weighs only 310 kg ( about 684 pounds ) . <p> But the prototype has not yet passed crash tests , because the material " will not bend like metal , but break " , said the team 's leader Noud van de Gevel . <p> Demands to reduce air pollution and tackle climate change have pushed car companies towards alternative designs , but most still require a great deal of energy to make . <p> " Energy that is saved while driving the car is now spent during the production phase , " van de Gevel said . <p> The TU/Ecomotive team plans to test drive Lina later this year , once it is given the green light by the Netherlands Vehicle Authority . <p> Meanwhile , for more mainstream carmakers the shift from petrol and diesel engines to electric cars is set to speed up a shift to more lightweight materials in order to extend the driving range of battery-powered vehicles . <p> Batteries are heavier than conventional engines , and if that does n't change the challenge will be to shave weight elsewhere on the car . <p> That has big implications for the steel industry - the current go-to material for car makers with predictions that alternatives will increasingly take over . India 's Tata Steel however , claims that the likes of aluminium and carbon fibre will not replace steel because the alternatives are prohibitively expensive , and are harder to recycle . <p> In a report called ' Charging towards a sustainable future ' Tata argues that the shift to low emissions vehicles could see consumers look at the wider environmental impact of cars , and that advanced steel products - because it can be endlessly recycled - will be seen as more attractive than alternatives . <p> The ultimate mix of materials that will go into car body parts has yet to be determined , but producers of processed lithium - an essential element for batteries used in electric cars - are already agreeing long-term contracts with their customers to fund the investments needed to address a looming shortfall . <p> Demand for battery-grade lithium compounds is expected to skyrocket in the next decades in tandem with soaring demand for electric cars as governments and individual consumers try to reduce their carbon footprint . Although there 's plenty of lithium around , the problem is ensuring there is enough capacity to process it . <p> Battery makers and other end-users such as car manufacturers will need to sign multi-year deals that encourage large producers to invest more faster , industry sources say . <p> " We 've established the timeline for our own expansion based on the commitments our customers are making with us , " said Tom Schneberger , global business director at US-listed FMC Lithium , one of the top four producers .
