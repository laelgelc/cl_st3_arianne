<h> Defence Forces chief argues in favour of greater diversity <h> Organisations must also allow for mistakes in order to learn , says chief of staff Mark Mellett <p> The Defence Forces chief of staff has said it is vital that organisations promote gender equality and cultural , ethnic , religious and generational difference to deal with increasing global complexity . <p> Organisations that nurture difference and allow for mistakes as a form of learning will improve resilience as an " antidote " to complexity , Vice-Admiral Mark Mellett has said . <p> Writing in the current issue of Health Management , an online journal , he advocates developing a diversity and inclusion strategy that " promotes equality , values , difference , and embraces lesbian , gay , bisexual , transgender ( LGBTA ) and other communities " . <p> " Leadership , like innovation , is also about accepting risk-taking and mistakes , " Mellett says . <p> The Defence Forces chief of staff , who is the first Naval Service officer to hold the position , says achieving greater gender balance is a societal issue and that " improvement " in this balance at all levels " facilitates better decision-making and creative processes " . <p> He recommends " institutionalising a gender perspective " . This " analyses a person 's perceived value in a given context , their access to power , influence and resources , and mitigates against societal inequalities and unconscious bias " . <h> Recruiting women <p> The Defence Forces are still predominantly male -- only 6.3 per cent are female -- and a targeted campaign to recruit more women was initiated three years ago . <p> The forces comply with the Gender Recognition Act 2015 and have an LGBTA support network , Defend with Pride . <p> Mellett also advocates " bringing together diverse disciplines " , which requires an atmosphere of tolerance , a nurturing of different perspectives and a mix of humanities with the sciences . <p> Stressing the value of the arts , he says that this discipline encompasses the social and political sciences , " enhances the knowledge that builds and connects institutions , organisations and people " . <p> Mellett describes militaries as part of a bedrock that underpins sovereignty and contributes to a framework for civil society . <p> " We live in a time of extraordinary change and complexity , " he writes , with challenges all over the world to the shared values of civil society . <p> " Many have the characteristics of ' wicked problems ' , with extraordinary complexity , they may have multiple causes " and include " interstate and intrastate , hybrid and proxy wars , state competition , cyber warfare , terrorism and criminality " , he says . <p> " Other vectors like population increase and climate change feed this complexity , " while potentially mitigating " positives include the growth in technology , automation , robotics and the explosion in data " , which he singles out as the " most exciting of all " . <h> Change in mindsets <p> While answers to complex problems exist , they may " often lie outside organisational or indeed state boundaries " , he writes , and " achieving congruence ... requires innovation " . <p> However , he says that innovation is " not just about creativity " , but is a " systematic change in individual mindset and culture that permeates entire organisations with internal and external dimensions " . <p> " The more diversity stimulated in networks , the more potentially disruptive the innovation will be , " he says , and an innovation ecosystem might incorporate " state bodies , enterprise , academia and civil society actors " . <p> Collaboration between the Defence Forces and higher-education institutes has already shown benefits , he says . <p> " Driving innovation necessitates that the status quo is challenged , that cultures are open and inclusive , that there is no room for egos and , importantly , that there is an acceptance that mistakes will happen . " <p> Such " mistakes " are associated with risk-taking , and are distinguished from " violations , which are unacceptable breaches of the rules " . <p> Mellett says the future is about " how collaboration and knowledge-sharing is achieved , where ego is the enemy and empathy is the kingmaker " .
