<h> Threats to CAP highlighted at Tullamore Show <p> Brexit is seen as the ideal opportunity by many to make radical changes to the CAP ( Common Agricultural Policy ) post-2020 -- quite apart from the impact of the negotiations on the future of the single market for food and for the EU budget -- according to MEP and first Vice-President of the European Parliament Mairead McGuinness . <p> Speaking as she performed the official opening of the Tullamore and AIB National Livestock Show yesterday ( Sunday , August 13 ) , McGuinness said such radical change must be resisted , given the importance of farming and food production . <p> The MEP noted that a large number of stakeholder responses to the commission consultation earlier in the year on the future of CAP have come from environmental organisations taking the view that CAP is ' broken and must be fixed ' . <p> Speaking on the topic , McGuinness said : " Such views will influence the future of the CAP both in terms of funding and detail . The commission is also of the view that the policy must deliver more for the environment and climate change , but there is very little clarity about how this will be achieved . " <p> McGuinness added that more targeted payments are called for , for specific environment and climate action . Simultaneously , there is a unified call for simplification of the CAP . <p> " These two demands are not compatible . More detailed programmes with specific delivery requirements involve inspections and controls and add bureaucracy rather than simplification . " <p> McGuinness said there needs to be a much deeper debate on how to shape the future of farming which moves beyond time-limited schemes which might not bring about time-enduring change . <p> She added that in the last CAP reform round , a major source of division and debate was the different levels of payments between different farmers and between member states . <p> " These issues will arise again as we debate the next round of the CAP -- but it should not overshadow the challenges facing agricultural and food policy in terms of market prices , volatility and environmental pressures . <p> The future should be about ensuring that agriculture production systems are built on sustainability principles , both environmental and climate . <p> Economic sustainability is also important , the MEP said , noting : " Work must continue to highlight the critical role of Irish and European farmers in supplying high-quality food on a daily basis to consumers . " <p> CAP payments will continue to be important and the CAP budget must be defended against cuts , she added . <p> " The CAP makes shows like this here in Tullamore possible by sustaining a living countryside and a vibrant agriculture . I complement the show committee and all involved in making this massive show happen year in year out -- the level of commitment and the voluntary effort involved is incredible . <p> The EU is facing many challenges , including Brexit uncertainty and the impact it will have on the budget , as well as ongoing migration issues . <p> Concluding , McGuinness said : " Ensuring a supply of quality food and a living landscape can not be forgotten in an already crowded agenda . "
