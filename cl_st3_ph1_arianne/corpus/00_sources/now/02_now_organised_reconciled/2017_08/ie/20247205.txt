<h> Europe 's future power demand ' to peak in summers , not winters ' <p> By century 's end , climate change will likely shift the peak demand for electricity in Europe from the winters to the summers , as more people use air conditioning to stay cool , researchers have said . <p> Understanding how demand for power will change in the coming decades as the planet warms is crucial in the 35 countries of Europe , which makes up the world 's third largest electricity market . <p> " We find significant increases in average daily peak load and overall electricity consumption in southern and western Europe , " said the report in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences , a peer-reviewed US journal . <p> Portugal and Spain could expect demand for electricity to rise by between 3% and 7% . <p> Meanwhile , " significant decreases " are forecast for northern Europe , with a dip of 2-6% in Sweden and Norway . <p> It said that " the significant polarisation and seasonal shifts in peak demand and consumption have important ramifications " . <p> Power stations may need to adapt or be moved to meet costly peak-generating capacity , new transmission infrastructure may need to be built , and new energy-efficiency policies designed , said the report . <p> " Total electricity use seem to be smallest on days with a maximum temperature of about 22 degrees Celsius , and increases when this daily maximum temperature either rises or falls , " said lead author Leonie Wenz from the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research . <p> Heat waves can be deadly , and hot summer days can dampen productivity . <p> " There now is ample evidence that when it 's hot outside , air quality suffers , people are more stressed , aggressive , violent and less productive , mortality and crime rates rise , " said co-author Max Auffhammer from the University of California , Berkeley . <p> " All sectors of the economy are affected by thermal stress , from the residential to the commercial , agricultural to the industrial sector .
