<p> MORE than 80pc of young people globally would move overseas to advance their careers - with the US , Canada , the UK , Germany and Australia the most desirable countries to move to for job opportunities . <p> The Global Shapers Annual Survey of 25,000 people under the age of 35 , carried out by the World Economic Forum , also found that salary came top as the most important criteria when considering job opportunities , followed by a sense of purpose and career advancement . <p> Only 16pc of millennials surveyed across 186 countries said they are willing to sacrifice career and salary to enjoy life . <p> Similar results were found in an Irish-specific survey recently carried out by LinkedIn , with six out of 10 people saying they were interested in changing jobs , primarily because of pay . <p> Providing their views on technology , almost eight in 10 young people surveyed by the WEF believe technology will create jobs rather than destroy them . <p> When asked to name the next big technology trend , 28pc of survey respondents said that artificial intelligence will make the most significant impact , while education is seen as the sector that is most likely to benefit from the adoption of new technologies . <p> However , despite positive views on technology , only 3.1pc strongly agreed that they would trust robots to make decisions on their behalf . <p> The annual WEF survey also looked at young people 's attitude to media , big business and governments . <p> Just under one in three said they trusted the media , compared to almost 46pc who said they did n't . <p> The rise of ' fake news ' during the recent elections around the world may go some way towards explaining why distrust is growing among young people . <p> There are similar levels of distrust towards big companies , banks and governments , reflecting the fact that many are worried about corruption , the WEF found . <p> Meanwhile , the institutions deemed most trustworthy by young people are schools , international organizations , employers and courts of law . <p> Of all the issues affecting the world today , people under the age of 30 are most concerned about the impact of climate change and the general destruction of nature . <p> This is the third year in a row that climate change has been voted the most serious global issue , and - according to the WEF - suggests that young people are yet to be convinced by global efforts to tackle the problem . <p> Also featuring strongly on the list of issues that concern young people were wars and inequality , suggesting that those under the age of 30 are being affected by the current levels of global instability . <p> Poverty , religious conflicts and government accountability and transparency also ranked highly in the survey . <p> Adrian Monck , head of social engagement at the WEF , said that millennials are shaping the twenty-first century , <p> " The Global Shapers Survey captures their views and values worldwide , " said Mr Monck . <p> " It is an indispensable tool for anyone wanting to understand what 's important to the generation driving our future , " he added .
