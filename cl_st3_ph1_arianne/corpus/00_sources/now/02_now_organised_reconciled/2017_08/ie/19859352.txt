<h> Dramatic scenes in Newbridge as young actors get Visibly Vocal <h> 50 actors from all over Europe to join locals for performances <p> Irish students outside the Parthenon on a tour of the Acropolis during an exchange at Easter . <p> Crooked House Theatre Company in Newbridge will host 50 young people from all over Europe this week in a week-long theatre-making project called Visibly Vocal . <p> Teenagers from France , Spain , Denmark , Greece , Italy and Ireland will congregate in Newbridge for a programme of workshops and performances where they will work with professional artists to make short performances in public spaces around the town . <p> The project , a youth exchange , is funded by the EU Erasmus+ programme for Youth and Life-Long Learning , and is designed to bring European young people closer together to share their views and learn about issues facing Europe today . <p> Peter Hussey , Artistic Director of Crooked House , says organising such an event is a challenge , but they have hosted more than 20 such projects in the last 15 years . <p> Performing outside near the Acropolis last easter during the Indigo exchange to Athens . <p> " This one will be different in that the young people will perform on the streets and in the park on Saturday , August 12 from 7pm onwards . <p> The public is invited to follow a trail of short performances around the town , free . " <p> Visibly Vocal refers to young people becoming both visible in public and sharing their concerns to a wider audience . <p> " We can expect short performances about climate change , about migration and refugees , and about any issue the young people feel is important to them . " <p> The groups come from theatres with whom Crooked House has built strong relationships . " We have been working with Theatre Le Grand Bleu in Lille , in France , for several years , " Peter says , " and we regularly bring young people to Lille for projects . <p> " Our partners in Greece are Kinitiras Studio in Athens . This Easter we brought 15 Kildare young people there to work with 15 young Greeks on social issues relevant to the two countries . <p> " Likewise , we have been to the beautiful island of Bornholm , Denmark , where we worked with our partner there ( Cirkus Stjerneskud and Bornholm 's Dramaskole ) on various projects over the years . <p> " Our Italian and Spanish partners are new to us and we look forward to working closely with them . " <p> The Irish young people are from Newbridge , Leixlip , Kilcock and Celbridge . <p> Charlie Hughes-Farrell , from Kildare Youth Theatre , says that taking part has brought huge learning to himself and to his friends . <p> Cian O ' N ? irigh and Charlie Hughes-Farrell performing in Athens during the Indigo youth exchange last Easter . <p> " We get to really engage with people our own age from cultures different to ours : we find out how their education system works , how important or not family is , and what kinds of pressures face young people after school in terms of employment or college . " <p> The Irish young people are busy preparing their studio space ( above Johnson 's ) for the exchange . Lauren Kelly from Naas says " we work hard to make them feel as welcome as possible . When english is not their first language it can be difficult to communicate , but with theatre , we find that we often do n't need any verbal language to be understood " . <p> The benefits of international exchanges are not just for young people , adds Veronica Bagnall , one of the volunteer youth workers . <p> " We spend a lot of the project money on hiring local caterers , buying food and other supplies , and of course , the majority is spent on accommodation . " <p> All the young people are staying in the Gables Guesthouse , which is ideal , says Peter . " It does n't have a bar , each room has an en suite , and is situated along the beautiful Liffey Park and Strand , a short walk from the town centre where we are based " . <p> He also thanks the Patrician Secondary School , " without whom we would never be able to run the project . Patrick O'Leary , the Principal , and the staff , generously give us three rooms to use for workshops . " <p> Doug Morrison and some of the international group performing a piece they made during our Spanish exchange in June . <p> Ana Rodriguez Asensio is a volunteer with Crooked House on an Erasmus Study Abroad programme , supported by her university in Spain . She has a background in street theatre and performing arts . <p> One of the activities she organised is a kind of treasure hunt , where the young people have to form small groups and visit places in the town ( from Newbridge College to the Whitewater Shopping Centre ) looking for clues and making short performances at each stop . <p> On the other days the young people get to work with internationally established theatre-makers , an opportunity that they would never otherwise have . <p> These include Gerrgina Kakoudaki from Athens who is a director , young Spanish up-and-coming director Juan Escalona Correll , who directs verbatim and site-specific projects in northern Spain , choreographer Antigone Gyra whose work is recognised all over Europe . <p> The project is free to participants . Crooked House organises three or four such exchanges a year . <p> Young people can join KYT in September and be eligible to take part in the next one in Lille in November .
