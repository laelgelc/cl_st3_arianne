<p> New Zealand First candidate Sue Sara chose the first election forum in Nelson on Wednesday to announce she was standing in the city . <p> Sara , who worked for the past four years as office manager for NZ First MP Richard Prosser , said a dramatic rise in the number of constituents visiting the Stoke office over the past 18 months had driven her to stand . <p> " It 's sad that the majority is to do with housing and health and in some of those cases , those are interlinked , " she told a packed Trafalgar Pavilion . " So that was what made me think : ' I 've got to get out there . ' We need change and we need it now and I needed to get my voice heard . " <p> MARTIN DE RUYTER <p> Sue Sara has announced she will stand as the New Zealand First candidate for Nelson . <p> The forum was organised by Community Action Network on behalf of the Nelson Community &amp; Whanau Network . Each candidate was given a minute to answer questions in key areas including not for profit , housing , health , social wellbeing , education and the environment . <p> Osmaston said his party was proposing an end to the monetary system . The issues of today could not be fixed using the systems of the past , he said . <p> " We 're now looking at climate change , resource limitations , inequality , stress and technological unemployment , and within a money system , we can not fix those parameter problems . " <p> People had become " completely shackled by this monetary madness " . It had become too expensive to be healthy and people harmed the environment for one reason -- to get money out of it . <p> " We 'll remove the reason to harm it in the first place , " Osmaston said . " We 're talking about the end of crime , stress , violence , war , animal welfare issues , environmental issues -- basically every problem you can think of will be solved by this one change . " <p> The answers from Smith , Boyack and Lawrey reflected their better-known party lines . <p> " Our economy is strong , unemployment is falling , families are getting ahead and there is not a debate about whether we want to reduce poverty or clean up rivers or provide better healthcare , the real question for New Zealanders is who has the actual realistic capability to actually be able to deliver those things , " he said . <p> Smith outlined the achievements of the National-led Government over the past nine years . <p> " The single most important thing for our young people is actually a growing economy that is providing more job opportunities and , man , am I proud when I look at unemployment levels in countries like Greece and France and ... even Australia where Nelson 's unemployment is the lowest in New Zealand and New Zealand 's is one of the lowest in the world and that 's what you get from a National Government that knows how to competently manage and grow the economy . " <p> Boyack was the crowd pleaser and received bouts of applause : * when she said Labour would ensure not-for-profit groups had long-term funding streams ; * when she said Labour would not proceed with National 's tax cuts . " I do n't need an extra $20 a week and neither does Nick . We need to put that money collectively , from all of us in the room , back into our health system . " * when she said Labour would provide three free years of tertiary education ; * when she said there needed to be a change of government -- a Jacinda Ardern Labour Party government . <p> Lawrey had some catchy one-liners including the statement that he wanted his grandchildren to live in a society not divided into the " have yachts and have nots " . <p> Labour and the Greens have a memorandum of understanding so it was not surprising to hear Boyack and Lawrey shared similar positions on some issues including plans for a warrant of fitness for rental properties and an aim to put nurses into schools . <p> There were also similarities in answers to questions on the environment including the call for clean rivers . <p> Lawrey said all New Zealanders were green at heart . <p> " One thing that makes me really proud is when I look at other parties ' manifestos , " he said . " They 're going on about trains , rivers , medicinal cannabis -- these are things the Green Party has been talking about for 27 years and our success is seeing those parties adopt those policies so if you want more of that sort of thing , you need to keep the Greens going . " <p> He urged the forum attendees to " party vote Green and vote for me as well because the Green Party in government will help Labour do the right thing " .
