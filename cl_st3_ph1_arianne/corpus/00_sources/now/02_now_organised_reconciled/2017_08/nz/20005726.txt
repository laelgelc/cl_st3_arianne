<p> Journalist Kim Wall , 30 , boarded a submarine built by 46-year-old Peter Madsen and has not been seen since . <p> Kim Wall has reported from the depths of post-war Sri Lanka and from the capital of North Korea . While covering climate change in the Marshall Islands , she was quarantined and tested for exposure to radiation . <p> The 30-year-old freelance journalist , based in New York and China , is known among her friends as an intrepid reporter , skilled at exploring hard-hitting topics in obscure , and at times dangerous , locations . <p> So to her loved ones , a reporting trip off the coast of Denmark appeared to be a relatively safe destination . It was , after all , less than 50km from her Swedish hometown and in a country that ranks among the world 's safest . <p> REUTERS <p> Danish submarine owner and inventor Peter Madsen lands with the help of the Danish defence in Dragor Harbour , south of Copenhagen <p> But it was on this trip , aboard a submarine , that Wall vanished late last week . <p> She was last seen Thursday ( Friday NZ Time ) , departing from Copenhagen on the vessel along with its owner , a Danish inventor and amateur rocket builder named Peter Madsen , 46 . According to her family , Wall was working on a story about Madsen , who is well known in Denmark for using crowdfunding to build his own submarines and rockets . <p> Copenhagen police have arrested Madsen and detained him on a charge of involuntary manslaughter , although no body has been found , according to a police news release . As the search for Wall continues , police also said they would like to talk with anyone who may have seen her leave the submarine . <p> Police say Madsen deliberately sank the submarine on Friday ( Saturday NZT ) , when he was plucked from Koge Bay . Officials lifted the vessel out of the bay , where it had sunk 22 feet . <p> " The sub has been searched and there is nobody on board - neither dead nor alive , " Copenhagen police homicide chief Jens Moller told reporters at a news conference . <p> Madsen has denied the charge , saying he dropped Wall off in the harbour of Copenhagen late on Thursday ( Friday NZT ) after she completed her reporting . Madsen has since changed his story , police say , but they 've declined to explain further . <p> Madsen 's defence lawyer , Bettina Hald Engmark , told the Associated Press her client was " willing to co-operate " and had n't decided whether to appeal the ruling on his detention . After a two-hour private hearing on Saturday ( Sunday NZT ) , a judge ordered that Madsen be held for 24 days while police continue investigating . <p> Police had been searching for the submarine , named the UC3 Nautilus , since Friday , when Wall 's boyfriend reported that she had not returned to Copenhagen on Thursday night ( Friday NZT ) as planned . <p> Krisitan Isbak , told a Danish news outlet that he spotted the submarine , and Madsen in the vessel 's tower , after authorities asked for help in the search . He saw Madsen go down into the submarine and re-emerge shortly after . Then , the vessel began to sink , he said . <p> " There was no panic at all , " Isbak said . " The man was absolutely calm . " <p> Isbak described the scene further to the AP , saying " there was then some kind of air flow coming up and the submarine started to sink " . Madsen stayed in the tower until water began pouring into it . As the boat sank , he swam to a nearby boat , Isbak told the AP . <p> Madsen spoke to Danish television station TV2 , claiming the submarine sank after " a minor problem with a ballast tank " , which holds water to provide stability , " turned into a major issue " . <p> " It took about 30 seconds for Nautilus to sink , and I could n't close any hatches or anything , " Madsen told the station . " But I guess that was pretty good because I otherwise still would have been down there . " <p> Wall 's family declined to speak in detail about her disappearance . But her mother told The Washington Post : " We still hope for her safe return " . <p> " It is with a great concern that we , her family , received the news that Kim is missing after an interview with Peter Madsen in Denmark , " the family wrote in a statement to the Committee to Protect Journalists . " We sincerely hope that she will be found and that she is well . " <p> Wall 's disappearance resonated among journalists around the world , with many of them sharing the news across social media over the weekend . Her reporting covered topics such as identity , gender , pop-culture , social justice and foreign policy , and her work has appeared in Harpers , the Guardian , the New York Times , Foreign Policy , the Atlantic , and many other publications . <p> A native of Malmo , Sweden , Wall graduated from Columbia University with masters degrees in journalism and international affairs . She received a bachelor 's degree from the London School of Economics &amp; Political Science . <p> The International Women 's Media Foundation described her as a " beloved member " of its community and said it was " enormously concerned " about her disappearance . <p> " We ask that the Danish authorities urgently make every effort to locate Kim and provide everyone who loves her with more information , " the organisation wrote in a statement . " The global press freedom community is united in standing with Kim , her family and colleagues . " <p> The Committee to Protect Journalists echoed these demands , calling for an exhaustive inquiry into Wall 's " fate and wherabouts " . <p> " Denmark should not be considered a dangerous assignment for journalists , " wrote Nina Ognianova , the group 's Europe and Central Asia programme co-ordinator . <p> Christopher Harress , a reporter for AL.com based in Alabama , told The Washington Post he became good friends with Wall while attending Columbia University with her . She was in all of his classes , he said . <p> After she graduated in 2013 , Wall 's peers admired her decision to pursue a career as an independent journalist , move to China and chase stories around the world . She often relied on grants to fund her reporting trips . <p> " She did it all , " Harress said . " She 'd been to all these different dangerous places . " <p> So when Harress and Wall 's other classmates heard Wall was missing , they initially presumed she may have simply wandered to pursue a story on an island . <p> " Because it is such a safe country , we just thought off course she 's gone somewhere , " Harress said . " We just thought that was her , that was what she did . She just wandered places . " <p> But as the news reports took a more ominous turn , her friends became increasingly worried . <p> " I think we just fear the worst now , " Harress said . " She trusted somebody and then this is what happened . " <p> He said her disappearance had " shaken up " many of Wall 's friends and fellow journalists , and has " tested a lot of our perceptions of what can happen to someone in certain places " . <p> " You can go to Africa and be perfectly safe and then go to one of the safest places in Europe , " and this happens , he said .
