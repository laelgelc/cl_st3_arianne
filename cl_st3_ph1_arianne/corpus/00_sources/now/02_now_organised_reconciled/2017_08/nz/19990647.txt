<h> Criticism of farming gas emissions tells only half the story <h> It is imperative that political decisions on reacting to climate change are based on science , writes Waimate farmer Paul Studholme . <p> Last updated 07:41 , August 14 2017 <p> Marion Van Dijk <p> Animal emissions are part of the carbon cycle . <p> OPINION : I write because of frustration with the sweeping generalisations and half-truths critical of the farming industry in this country that are presented by the mainstream media and environmental groups as facts . <p> One in particular , repeated frequently , is this : Farming produces more than half the greenhouse gases in New Zealand . This is only telling half the story or one side of the equation . <p> What is referred to here are the gases methane and carbon dioxide emitted by cattle and sheep . This is part of the carbon cycle . <p> Putting it simply , as this is a part of a very large and complex process , plants , in this case pasture grasses and other plants used for fodder , absorb CO2 from the atmosphere by photosynthesis . The C is converted into carbohydrates and other compounds which become part of the plant above and below ground , while the O2 is emitted back into the atmosphere . <p> The leaves of these grasses are eaten by cattle and sheep , then bacteria and micro-organisms in the animal 's rumen break this material down into available nutrients . The resulting methane gas CH4 and carbon dioxide CO2 produced being emitted back into the atmosphere . <p> This means that the carbon component of these gases has not been added to the atmosphere but returned to where it came from . It has been in effect been recycled and the animal therefore is carbon neutral . <p> Methane has a much greater greenhouse effect than carbon dioxide . However this effect is relatively short-term as it breaks down over a period of time ( 10-12 years ) into carbon dioxide and water . <p> Since the late 80s , sheep numbers have more than halved in New Zealand . The greenhouse gas emissions of the sheep industry have declined correspondingly . <p> Looking at the big picture , historically there were long periods of time up until quite recently when there were vast herds of ruminants numbering many millions in Africa and North America . There were around 60 million bison in North America alone . <p> They produced quantities of greenhouse gases far in excess of today 's farmed animals , yet there were actually mini ice-ages in the period between the 16th to 19th centuries . What this shows is that the greenhouse gases emitted by ruminant animals in that period had no warming effect on the climate , rather there were other far greater influences . <p> The political parties of the left are determined to impose an emissions trading scheme on agriculture . It is imperative that decisions in this field are based on science , not politics . <p> There is a challenge here for our scientists and tertiary institutions to establish precisely the outcome of the carbon cycle where the carbon emitted is equal to the amount taken in by livestock . The sequestration of carbon in pastoral soils under grazing management by sheep and cattle , which is currently ignored in all calculations of carbon emissions by agriculture , also needs scientific investigation . <p> We should not stand by and have schemes foisted on agriculture by those who do not understand , or do n't want to understand , the detail of the natural carbon cycle as it influences the world we live in.
