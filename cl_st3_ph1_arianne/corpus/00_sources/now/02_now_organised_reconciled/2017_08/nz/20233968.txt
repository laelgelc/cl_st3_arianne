<h> Southlanders have their say : Environment <h> Robert Guyton - Columnist , councillor , forest-gardener <h> How would you rate the Government on the environment ? <p> Would an environmentally supportive government encourage new coalmines , knowing coal is the No 1 culprit in climate change ? Would a government that cares for communities and a farming industry threatened by climate change encourage the oil and gas industry to drill in our region ? Would a responsible government push for a doubling of the national dairy herd , knowing that intensive dairying is the No 1 contributor to the pollution of our rivers ? Would a government that wanted to protect the environment from harm , attempt to destroy the best protection we have : the Resource Management Act ? Southlanders will decide that for themselves . <p> SUPPLIED <p> Ernslaw One Ltd environment manager Peter Weir . <h> What is going right , and what is going wrong ? <p> Well , a country free of rats sounds great , does n't it , but talk is cheap and while the idea is popular , the reality is , rats are n't going to be beaten by talk . Nor will pouring money into killing them work either , in my opinion . The sheer number of pests -- insect and plant -- that are appearing in this country now shows the problem is increasing fast and wo n't be solved with heart-swelling slogans and promises of government money . The real issues that need to be addressed are matters of policy , not populism ; a government that cares for its people will take real action to improve water quality , take firm action to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by sending the oil and gas industry packing and the coal companies back to their own countries . <p> A government that genuinely values the environment will encourage diversity of land use and discourage the industrialisation of farming . And a government that represents New Zealanders fairly will give New Zealanders opportunities to have their say , rather than shut them out and cut their legal support , as has happened so often over the past nine years . Southlanders will recognise the real intent of a political party by its actions . <h> What action should the Government take ? <p> In my opinion , a genuinely committed government would take the leading role in reversing the harm to the environment that has resulted from too much focus on economic development . <p> The natural resources we all need to be healthy are degrading year after year ; an environment-focused government would show the way , lead by example and represent New Zealanders fairly ; replace polluting industries and land use practices with better forms that improve the environment ; our air , water , soil and landscapes . It would encourage a focus on diversity , community and health , not just for those who can afford it , but for everyone and everything . New Zealanders want to contribute to a healthy environment , not cause it to become unhealthy ; coal burning , drilling for oil and gas , intensively stocked agriculture ; these things cause us all harm and should be off the agenda for a truly environmentally responsible Government . <p> Brittany Pickett/STUFF <p> Pourakino Valley dairy farmer David Diprose . <h> Peter Weir - Ernslaw One Ltd environment manager <h> How would you rate the Government on the environment ? <p> We would take advice from the OECD 's 2017 environmental performance review of New Zealand and give the recent governments a C minus . Most of the indicators are going the wrong way . The Parliamentary Commissioner for the Environment has reinforced this view , as has the Ministry for the Environment 's own reporting on the deteriorating state of New Zealand 's freshwater . <p> Election <h> What is going right , and what is going wrong ? <p> The " going right " list is quite short , but air quality in urban areas is definitely improving because regional councils are giving effect to the national air quality standard . Hazardous substances management is much improved under the Hazardous Substances and New Organisms Act . In the forestry space , the Government is about to give effect to a national environmental standard for plantation forestry , which , among other things , will steer investors away from the worst of the worst erodible country in New Zealand ( which is where catchment boards previously incentivised it ) and towards landscapes that wo n't fall apart in storms two to five years after clearfell harvest . The forestry national environmental standard will lift the bar for forestry roads and culverts ensuring that all forest owners play their part in minimising sediment delivery to streams maintaining and enhancing freshwater , and it will give greater protection for biodiversity including ground nesting native birds . The current Government 's commitment to make 90 per cent of New Zealand rivers swimmable by 2040 is a great first step , but for those concerned that things will get worse before they get better , 2030 would have been a better deadline . 2040 lacks urgency . <p> Unfortunately , the " going wrong " list is quite long . New Zealand 's greenhouse gas emissions profile is , in the words of Vivid Economics of London in their report to a cross-party coalition of MPs named Globe New Zealand , " off track " . The Ministry for the Environment states that New Zealand 's greenhouse gas emissions are about 30 per cent above 1990 levels , despite the commitment made by our then-prime minister John Key to the UN in Paris to get our emissions down to 10 per cent below 1990 levels by taking urgent and bold action . On our current business-as-usual emissions trajectory , New Zealand does n't have a hope of meeting our Paris commitment . <h> What action should the Government take ? <p> Gazetting a national stock exclusion ( stream fencing ) regulation with immediate dates for dairy and progressive implementation dates for beef , deer then sheep before the election , with 100 per cent destocking by 2025 would be a very sensible step ( Environment Minster Nick Smith stated that this would be in place by July 2017 , and there now seems little time to progress this before the election ) . Public health records make a good case to include sheep farms as more of New Zealand 's near epidemic of outbreaks of campylobacter in rural areas are attributable to sheep than any other farmed species . The Havelock North disease outbreak was caused by contamination of well-heads by runoff from sheep pasture . <p> The next government would do well to progress Generation Zero 's proposed Zero Carbon Act , as recently endorsed by the Young Nats , to get New Zealand 's net greenhouse gas emissions to zero by 2050 , in an effort to keep global warming to less than 1.5 degrees Celsius by 2100 would be a good first step ( noting that the atmosphere is already 1.2C above pre-industrial times , even though it may not often feel like that in Southland ) . The proposed act recommends creating an independent climate commission with functions similar to , and the independence of , the Reserve Bank . The act proposed to treat long-lived greenhouse gases ( that is , carbon dioxide and nitrous oxides from the burning of coal and liquid fossil fuels ) differently to short-lived greenhouse gass ( methane -- in New Zealand largely from intensive farming ) . This policy could potentially see an emissions trading scheme with greenhouse gas offsets from plantation forests being restricted to dairy farming ( something that neither farmers nor forest owners would relish ) , with a much higher price being applied to combustion of fossil fuels ( effectively a carbon tax ) to incentivise switching to electric vehicles and biofuels . <p> The next government needs to progress the work that Smith started last year on water allocation and charging , perhaps adopting the key elements of Gareth Morgan 's The Opportunities Party policies on freshwater , which involve setting up an auction system for water abstraction above domestic and stock water needs and recycling the revenue back for freshwater and ecological restoration projects , applying the polluter pays principal on discharges of contaminants , and making a financial return to Maori who have a legitimate Treaty claim over the ownership of New Zealand 's freshwater . <h> How would you rate the Government on the environment ? <p> Environmental stewardship is not this government 's natural game , therefore it seems reluctant to get involved and lead decisively on environmental issues until it feels it has too . The result of the National-led government not front-footing the concerns sufficiently around the rural land and water has been that farmers have lost control of the freshwater quality conversation to a point where urban New Zealand now seems to by setting the agenda and therefore the direction of the discussion . This has been difficult for many farmers now to deal with as we now feel as if we have a target on our backs . My view is that this government because of its " hands off " approach is failing to support farmers enough which is confusing for many as farmers generally and historically continue to support this government . When all is said and done , the government of the day is entrusted with the guardianship of the environment . For this reason , I 'm giving them generous " average with a must do better " rating on the environment . <h> What is going right , and what is going wrong ? <p> The Government 's national policy on freshwater has got it right around community consultation in the form of ascertaining communities values around freshwater . The fact that for the first time the environment in particular freshwater quality is an election issue for many kiwis tells the story on the government 's lack of progress on these issues . Governments share an election cycle mind-set whereas environmental issues require " transgenerational thinking " This is where I see a National-led government by their actions value development over environment not quite understanding that our two largest bread winners tourism and agriculture both leverage off our natural environment . The promotion and encouragement of oil and gas exploration in western and central Southland does not fit with our environmental values as a province and could be counterproductive once again to our burgeoning tourism and agriculture sectors . <h> What action should the Government take ? <p> Start communicating with stakeholders . The Government needs to come out and explain clearly why we need to protect and enhance water quality and the go on to lead the conversation around the future community benefits of improving land and water . Transparency pre-election around the possibility of creating " special economic zones " which are designed to circumvent environmental safeguards . These enclaves act as their own independent state designed to create " regulatory relief " for developers with aquaculture in the south being mentioned as candidate for such a zone . We have committed through the Paris agreement along with 195 countries to reduce emissions . I 'm still not convinced National has a plan for us to meet our long-term commitments . Waiting on the science to arrive while continuing to pay the annual $1.4 billion fine is not a long-term approach . A comprehensive national strategy is warranted . <h> David Diprose - Wyndham dairy farmer <h> How would you rate the Government on the environment ? <p> The Government has been bold in starting to tackle the water challenge . They have put a framework in place with national policy statement on fresh water . They have placed commissioners in Canterbury to formula a model , that they now have running nationwide . The government have stepped up in some areas . In other areas , they send conflicting messages , which challenges the implementation of the process <h> What is going right , and what is going wrong ? <p> Right The government policy has created questions of what is important to us as Kiwis in regards to water , people 's values within their landscapes . Defining land use and the connectiveness of the waterways . Fencing of waterways for large animals is a good initiative and has positive outcomes for water quality . Communities are engaging to seek the solutions of tomorrow . <p> Wrong Regional management policies on local land systems , do not always create better water quality . Farm plans require regular updates to be effective . This adds a complexity to the business , which is reliant on data import and timing . Recording of data does not always reflect what has happened in the field and has little effect on environmental understanding . What it can create is a data base of management practice that could be used against the land user in a breach of rules . Riparian plantings are a feel-good factor on the landscape . Poor riparian planting planning without consideration for the local landscape could be disastrous for the biodiversity in the local catchment . <h> What action should the Government take ? <p> New Zealand 's land and water policy to regulate diffuse pollution to manage water quality , has been built around the assumption that " rule by numbers " would remove any doubt and provide clarity and certainty for both government and resource users . The opposite is unfolding . While we are wanting better waterways , the way of achieving this , has been a model generated first in Taupo/Rotorua Lakes , Canterbury , then through the rest of the country . In practice , fresh water objectives are first unrealistic and on a catchment level , unworkable . Policymakers should not be using science and predictive modelling to develop policy . A better understanding of nutrient transport paths 0would have a better outcome for the cumulative effects on a local water catchment . I suspect a predictive model on a catchment basis would create understanding and buy-in from local resource users . Catchment groups need to move from a participatory or medicated model , which is conducted by a science policy , to a local knowledge using science to impact the outcomes for that catchment .
