That is something we need to determine as quickly as possible . " <p> The Edinburgh volcano survey , featured in the Geological Society 's special publications series , examined the underside of the ice sheet for hidden peaks of basalt rock similar to those produced by the region 's other volcanoes . <p> Over the past century , explorers have reported sightings of their tips , which reach above the ice . <p> The survey team 's youngest member , Max Van Wyk de Vries , is a volcano fanatic who would n't stop wondering how many tips lie below the ice . <p> An undergraduate at the university 's school of geosciences , he set up the project with Dr Bingham . <p> They used ice-penetrating radar carried by planes and land vehicles to analyse measurements made by previous surveys and survey strips of west Antarctic ice . <p> Dr Bingham explained the results were compared with satellite and database records and geological information from aerial surveys . <p> " Essentially , we were looking for evidence of volcanic cones sticking up into the ice . " <p> After collating the results , the team reported 91 previously unknown volcanoes , adding to 47 others discovered over the previous century by explorers . <p> These newly discovered volcanoes range from 100 to 3,850 metres high . <p> All are covered in ice , sometimes in layers that are more than 4km thick . <p> Dr Bingham was shocked to find the active peaks concentrated in the west Antarctic rift system , which stretches 3,500km from Antarctica 's Ross ice shelf to the Antarctic peninsula . <p> " We were amazed . We had not expected to find anything like that number . <p> " We have almost trebled the number of volcanoes known to exist in west Antarctica . <p> " We also suspect there are even more on the bed of the sea that lies under the Ross ice shelf , so that I think it is very likely this region will turn out to be the densest region of volcanoes in the world , greater even than east Africa , where mounts Nyiragongo , Kilimanjaro , Longonot and all the other active volcanoes are concentrated . " <p> The volcanic activity could have crucial implications for Earth . If one erupts , it could further destabilise ice sheets in the region , where global warming has already had an impact . <p> Dr Bingham 's fear is that the Antarctic ocean 's meltwater outflows will cause sea levels to rise . <p> " We just do n't know about how active these volcanoes have been in the past . <p> " The most volcanism that is going in the world at present is in regions that have only recently lost their glacier covering - after the end of the last ice age . These places include Iceland and Alaska . <p> " Theory suggests that this is occurring because , without ice sheets on top of them , there is a release of pressure on the regions ' volcanoes and they become more active . " <p> Significant warming caused by climate change in west Antarctica has already affected its ice sheets . <p> If they reduce significantly , this could release pressure on volcanoes lying below . <p> This would lead to eruptions that could further destabilise ice sheets and enhance sea level rises , something Dr Bingham is keen to monitor .
