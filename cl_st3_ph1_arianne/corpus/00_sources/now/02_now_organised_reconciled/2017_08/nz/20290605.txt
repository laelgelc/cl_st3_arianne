<h> Climate change realities addressed by regional council <p> More severe weather extremes and significant coastal areas that could be affected by sea level rise are two of the key climate change risks for the Waikato region . <p> Now a new guideline to help respond to those risks has been adopted by Waikato Regional Council today . <p> Regional hazards team leader Rick Liefting says core council functions are all likely to be impacted by climate change . <p> These include flood scheme maintenance and management , catchment management , biosecurity and biodiversity , freshwater allocation , including discharges to freshwater , drainage and the provision of infrastructure along coastlines <p> " The climate is changing . While climate change is a natural process , increased greenhouse gas concentrations are projected to exacerbate the drivers of our climate in ways that we will need to respond to . " <p> While there is uncertainty over exactly what greenhouse gas scenario the world will be heading towards over future decades , the trend of change is " well accepted " , he said . <p> Mr Liefting said even if significant global action is taken now to reduce greenhouse gas concentrations , a degree of climate change is inevitable in our lifetimes . <p> " Climate change is often described as the biggest environmental challenge we face . The effects of climate change could carry tough economic and social implications for central government , councils and communities , due to increased risks to settlements , infrastructure and ecosystems from rising seas , storms and flooding . <p> " Serious consideration needs to be given to the potential future impacts of climate change . Particularly important are managing infrastructure and developments that will need to cope with climate conditions in 50 -- 100 years ' time . " <p> The new guideline is a high level document that provides a framework for the council 's integrated catchment management ( ICM ) activities to address projected impacts of climate change . <p> " The purpose of the document is to assist ICM staff and provide a consistent approach to planning for a changing climate in relation to the many and varied operational activities we deliver , " said Mr Liefting . <p> The key principles ICM will adopt are based on using robust information from central Government and managing uncertainty on climate change projections . " Managing uncertainty for the future is challenging , so we need to identify and understand the ' what ifs ' now to be flexible in managing climate change adaptation . Reviewing climate change information and using ' trigger points ' as part of our design and review process will help us ensure our projects are sustainable for the future . " <p> Councillors have previously been advised the region is highly exposed to the projected impacts of climate change with , for example : <p> * more than 800 kilometres of national and local roads within 1.5 metres of current sea levels <p> * 266 square kilometres of land already below sea level in the Waikato and Hauraki catchments <p> * in excess of $2 billion of commercial and residential coastal property within one metre of current sea levels . <p> Also , more severe weather extremes are expected with more frequent dryer ( drought ) conditions in the north and east and higher intensity rainfall events in the south and west of the region . <h> ALSO : <p> The relatively low take-up rates for some targeted benefits is a genuine scandal .
