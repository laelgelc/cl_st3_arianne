<h> Auckland Council unveils plan to combat climate change <p> This year Auckland has already experienced 90 coastal slips , including this slip at Stanmore Bay , which council is investigating . <p> A snapshot of Auckland Council 's response to climate change has been released . <p> With rising sea levels , air temperatures and rainfall Auckland Council has taken a number of measures to prepare the city for the impacts of climate change . <p> In December 2015 Auckland joined C40 Cities Climate Leadership Group , a global network of cities tackling climate change , at the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change in Paris ( COP21 ) . <p> CHRIS MCKEEN/STUFF <p> This week a large slip affected properties on Closely Road , in Auckland 's Bucklands Beach . <p> Last week council released an overview of its preparation , which included addressing coastal erosion , planting trees , enforced green standards for new buildings , intensifying pest management , and harsher building consents . <p> Auckland also had its plan Low Carbon Auckland , which set out a 30-year pathway and a 10-year plan to transform Auckland into a greener , low carbon city using sustainable resources . <p> A council spokesperson said while Low Carbon Auckland provided climate change direction for Auckland rather than adaptation of response measures . <p> Worldwide , city councils ' responses to climate change has become more overt in the last few months . In June 250 mayors in the United States backed a commitment for US cities to run on renewable resources by 2035 . In July 35 Australian city councils pledged to switch to renewable energy , build sustainable transport , and develop greener , efficient and more climate-resilient communities . <p> Victoria University 's climate change expert Kelli Archie said the localised effects of climate change made local scale planning and action vital in responding to impacts . <p> " Because we do n't have a cohesive national adaptation policy , local councils in New Zealand are often the ones tackling the problem , " Archie said . <p> " So far the approach has been piecemeal , but councils are moving forward . " <p> Auckland Council 's internal report outlined its work on managing changing coastlines , like realigning the southern section of Muriwai Breach , which since the 1960s had been eroded by about 65m of land annually . <p> Council was also dealing with 90 recent coastal slips , from minor slips to large sections of cliff faces , which had already cost more than $600,000 in repairs . <p> The report encouraged the public to visit and photograph king tides , the highest yearly tides , to raise awareness around changing coastlines , and to provide an insight into what Auckland 's normal tides could look like in 30 to 50 years time . <p> Council was reviewing its regional pest management plan to ensure it included managing new and emerging pests whose biosecurity risks and impacts would increase under climate change . <p> The report outlined that pests and diseases like Myrtle Rust or kauri dieback were expected to become more virulent because they thrived in higher temperatures . <p> One of the world 's 100 worst invasive species the Red-eared slider turtle would also benefit from increased temperatures . <p> Rain gardens were planted in areas to ensure vegetation and soil helped deal with heavy rainfalls , such as in Long bay on the North Shore . <p> Council 's new buildings attempted to be carbon neutral , such as a new community facility in Flatbush , and a proposed community recycling centre at Western Springs . <p> Council was rejecting subdivision consents based around the threat of coastal inundation on roads , footpaths and reserves , including a consent in Parakai in March 2015 . <p> Auckland Council 's controlled operations were also looking forward for example Watercare 's $1 billion underground sewage tunnel , the central interceptor project , had adopted a design that allowed for a 25 per cent increase in rainfall and sea levels . <p> Over the coming months council aimed to develop an integrated approach that encompassed both adaptation and mitigation , a spokesperson said .
