<h> Meeting funding challenge <p> A local government sector facing the high costs of a housing crisis and the effects of climate change needs a new funding model , the sector 's new president says . <p> Dunedin Mayor Dave Cull is that new president - he was voted in to head Local Government New Zealand late last month - and he says collecting rates from homeowners and charging for services is no longer a sustainable model . <p> Mr Cull won the presidency of the organisation that represents the country 's 78 councils , a group he says controls close to $20billion of infrastructure . <p> He saw off his only competitor , Nelson Mayor Rachel Reese , to take over from Hastings Mayor Lawrence Yule , who is now a National Party candidate . <p> Mr Cull is the first Dunedin head of the organisation that was formed in 1988 in the face of pending local government reform . <p> This week he told the Otago Daily Times about his new role , and what he hoped to achieve . <p> The president 's role was described recently by LGNZ chief executive Malcolm Alexander as a governance role , chief representative and chief media spokesperson for the organisation ' ' . <p> Mr Cull is chairman of a national council of LGNZ 's 15-member national council , a group that includes Auckland Mayor Phil Goff , Wellington Mayor Justin Lester and Christchurch Mayor Lianne Dalziel . <p> He is also chairman of the organisation 's governance and strategy group . <p> He said the role involved regular meetings with government ministers - an example was a meeting held last week with Environment Minister Nick Smith to discuss minimum rental housing standards . <p> Mr Cull won the role after seven years as a member of LGNZ 's national council , where he rose to the role of vice-president . <p> During that time , he came to appreciate the issues the organisation dealt with were council issues , he said . <p> But working on those matters from a council perspective meant working within set parameters and rules . <p> From an LGNZ president 's perspective you 're actually interacting with central government about the rules ' ' . <p> That meant there was a chance to change , challenge or reorder things , improving councils ' abilities to meet challenges . <p> One of the big issues facing local government - funding infrastructure - was whether it was new infrastructure for the likes of housing developments , or renewing items such as water treatment plants in small communities . <p> For quite a long time ' ' funding had been through rates and charges . <p> What LGNZ is saying to the Government is that is not a sustainable model any more . <p> That 's proven by the fact the growth-councils like Hamilton and Tauranga and Auckland are saying we 've reached our debt levels ' . ' ' <p> However , Mr Cull said everybody knew councils needed to be putting in more very expensive ' ' infrastructure to allow more housing development because of the country 's shortage of homes . <p> What we 're saying is we need to develop new funding mechanisms . ' ' <p> There had already been a call for GST from tourism , or a border levy , so international visitors provided some of the funding for tourism infrastructure . <p> At a local council level , those sort of issues could not be changed ; however , the combined voice of all councils might result in a better outcome , Mr Cull said . <p> Asked what he hoped to achieve in the role , he said as president he was keen to continue stating more assertively local government 's place in the governance of the country ' ' . <p> Councils owned about $20billion worth of infrastructure , not far short of what the Crown owned . <p> However local government had no constitutional place in New Zealand , and could be removed in the stroke of a vote ' ' . <p> He also wanted to drive decision-making towards the communities affected by it . <p> A tremendous amount of decision-making that affects local communities is made in Wellington . ' ' <p> Mr Cull said he wanted to better engage with the 78 councils who were members of the organisation . <p> Two big issues being ramped up were water and climate change , and they 're connected ' ' . <p> They could involve the quality of river water or storm water , quantity of water , iwi rights or funding of infrastructure . <p> The issues , as had been seen during national elections , were huge , and they 're comprehensive ' ' . <p> The appreciation of the need to pick up our game around water and climate change is right across the community , not just local government . ' ' <p> Asked about his ability to juggle his two roles , and whether he would have less time to concentrate on Dunedin issues , he said he was yet to get a full understanding of how much time the president 's role would take . <p> It would involve more visits to Wellington , although he did some meetings by teleconference . <p> It will mean more time out of Dunedin , there 's no doubt about that . <p> But the mayoral job is a seven-day-a-week job anyway . ' ' <p> Asked if he had time for both , Mr Cull said within LGNZ he would asking the 14 other members of the national council to do some of the work . <p> As with the city council it does n't have to be the mayor representing the council on every occasion ' ' . <p> If I accepted every invitation I would n't sleep . <p> I ca n't do everything I 'm asked or invited to do in any case . <p> I 'm used to prioritising . ' ' <p> He said the role could also bring benefits to Dunedin . <p> I ca n't see that it does any harm to Dunedin to have the mayor of the city with a national role and a national profile .
