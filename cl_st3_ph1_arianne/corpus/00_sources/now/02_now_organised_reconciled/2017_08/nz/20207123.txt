<h> An Inconvenient Sequel : Truth to Power review <p> Al Gore 's global campaign for climate change won him the Nobel Peace Prize , and the film he made about his campaigning won two Oscars . <p> A decade on , he is back with his follow-up - An Inconvenient Sequel : Truth to Power . <p> He 's been very busy since we last saw him , with cameras following him everywhere from the melting ice caps of Greenland to the UN Climate Change Conference in Paris , and the typhoon-torn Philippines . <p> Connecting the global dots is what made his first doco so compelling , and he does much the same here too to lesser effect . <p> However with plenty of material to work with , it still makes for thought-provoking viewing . <p> What New Zealand audiences in particular may find a little heavy-handed and even jarring here - I know I did - is how much more front and central Gore is here , painting himself far more heroically , the almost evangelical delivery distracting us from his central message . <p> But that aside , this film is still an important watch . <p> I remember how incensed I was , how shocked and galvanised I felt watching Gore 10 years ago , and while there is plenty of that still boiling , his follow-up does lack the sense of urgency and overall impact of that first terrifying film . <p> But this is not a drill , and this sequel serves to remind us of that again .
