<h> NZ First and National turn Wellsford Northland candidates meeting into a two-horse debate <p> JO MOIR <p> Last updated 21:23 , August 29 2017 <p> CHRIS MCKEEN/STUFF <p> NZ First leader Winston Peters wrapped up a day of scandal surrounding who leaked his superannuation overpayment with a colourful debate in Wellsford . <p> Take away the party loyalists , the campaign managers and the candidates and about a dozen people turned up in Wellsford for a Northland candidates meeting . <p> It was in stark contrast to the 130 people who filled the Mangawhai Heads golf club the night before - what a difference more than two days notice makes . <p> And then there was the fact NZ First did most , if not all , of the limited advertising for Tuesday night 's meeting . <p> There was barely a topic untouched at a Northland candidates meeting in Wellsford . <p> From the first question things seemed a bit skewed towards NZ First leader Winston Peters - by the third question it was blatantly apparent the room was full of his supporters . The exception being National candidate Matt King , his campaign manager and a couple of their mates . <p> Coincidentally he was a National contender ahead of the by-election in 2015 but King made a point of coming to his defence later in the night to say he did n't run this time round . <p> Everything from roading to housing and rivers and climate change was covered off - the moderator did n't seem too fussed as to when the night wrapped up . <p> An unusual moment came half-way through when the actual Northland Green Party candidate , Peter Hughes , arrived and took the floor to ask a question . <p> Peters was completely confused by that and insisted he join the panel but Hughes was happy to sit in the audience . <p> The biggest laugh of the night came when the candidates were discussing Wellsford being part of the Auckland super-city - something some locals are n't too thrilled about . <p> King said " that horse has bolted " and was n't interested in a binding referendum - he said endless referendums were Peters ' thing . <p> And right on cue Peters found another bottom-line - but this time with a caveat . <p> He told the audience if he discovered after the election that they had been " fast on the lip and slow on the hip " and not given their vote to him and his party then " I 'll know that you do n't give a rats about being cajoled into the super city " . <p> " But if I see that you 're serious , I 'll tell you this , I 'm not coming back here in 2020 and having you say I did n't keep my word , " he said . <p> And right there , a binding referendum for Wellsford to decide on whether they stayed part of the Auckland super-city , was a NZ First bottom-line , kind of . <p> As one gentleman in the audience put it - " I do n't like Winston that much but he 's actually talking quite a bit of good s ... " - if nothing else it proved there was at least one person in attendance who was n't part of the NZ First fan club .
