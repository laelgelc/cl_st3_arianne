<h> NZ Super dumps Genesis Energy shares as part of move away from carbon <p> HAMISH RUTHERFORD <p> Last updated 16:09 , August 15 2017 <p> REBEKAH PARSONS-KING/STUFF <p> Genesis Energy , owners of the Huntly Power Station , New Zealand 's largest thermal power station , has been dumped from the NZ Super Fund because of a move by the fund to cut its carbon intensity and exposure to carbon reserves . <p> The NZ Super Fund has dumped more than $950 million in investments as part of a move to cut its carbon footprint , including its holding in a state owned electricity company . <p> While the move has been welcomed by investment and environmental groups , NZ Super retains investments in hundreds of companies in carbon intensive industries . <p> In October 2016 the $35 billion fund , established to help cover future state pension costs , signalled a " fundamental shift " to its investment strategy , pledging to cut its investment in fossil fuels and target clean energy to prepare for climate change . <p> On Tuesday the fund declared that its global passive equity portfolio - which accounts for 40 per cent of the total fund - was now " low-carbon " . <p> Sales of high carbon industries reduced the overall fund 's carbon intensity by just under 20 per cent , while the exposure to carbon reserves was cut by 21.5 per cent , compared to what it would have been without the sale . <p> The move does not exclude all carbon intensive companies from the fund , or even the passive portfolio . <p> A scan of the remaining investments in the fund shows it has investments in at least 29 airlines , including Air New Zealand , 139 companies in the " oil , gas &amp; consumables " sector including Z Energy and close to 200 companies in the " metals and mining " sectors . <p> NZ Super said companies in high carbon sectors which had been independently judged to have " strong management engagement with the challenge of climate change " would be maintained in the passive portfolio . <p> The fund " also still holds high-carbon stocks periodically due to the discretionary decisions of active managers " . <p> Chief investment officer Matt Whineray said cutting exposure to companies at risk from climate change was not the same as other categorical exclusions , such as tobacco manufacturers . <p> " Reducing the fund 's exposure to carbon is a commercial decision based on long-term risk to our portfolio as a whole , " Whineray said . <p> " Companies have the opportunity to re-enter the portfolio in the future , if they improve their management of climate risk . " <p> Whineray claimed financial markets were under-pricing climate change risk over the long term . <p> " The global energy system is transitioning away from fossil fuels . For investors with very long horizons , such as the fund , reducing exposure to carbon emissions and reserves is a low-cost insurance policy . " <p> The move saw the fund exit 297 companies , a spokeswoman said . <p> Two were based in New Zealand , New Zealand Oil &amp; Gas and Genesis Energy , the electricity retailer and generator , 51 per cent owned by the Crown . <p> Genesis Energy , which owns the Huntly Power Station , the largest thermal power station in New Zealand , said it was not concerned by NZ Super 's move . The recent weather showed how important it was that New Zealand had a variety of energy sources . <p> " As the long , dry winter of 2017 has shown , it 's nationally important for Genesis ' thermal generation to be on tap when required -- to help ' keep the lights on ' for NZ businesses and households , " a spokesman said . <p> " Other investors , here and NZ , clearly continue to see and support the advantages of Genesis ' thermal ( gas and coal ) capacity . " <p> The company said almost half of its generation in 2017 had been from renewable sources , compared to 16 per cent the previous year . <p> " Our carbon intensity ( the ratio of ' kilotonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent ' to kilowatt hours ) has in fact fallen by two-thirds since our peak year in 2006 . " <p> NZ Super said the sales it outlined on Tuesday took it a " long way " towards hitting its target to cut its exposure to carbon reserves by 40 per cent and the carbon intensity of 20 per cent , compared to what it would have been without the sale , by 2020.
