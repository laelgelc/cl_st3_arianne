<p> Ruth Bennett woke up at 1 a.m. and looked through the doorway of her independent living apartment in Walker , about 20 miles west of Baton Rouge . It was Aug. 13 , 2016 , and rain had been pouring down for two days . Bennett was worried about her car flooding . <p> She saw water creeping up to the parking lot , but her car looked safe . She crawled back into bed . <p> Two hours later , one of the employees of Southern Pines Retirement Community was pounding on Bennett 's door . Not only had Bennett 's car flooded , the facility was surrounded by water . The employee told Bennet to grab what she needed , including her medicine . <p> " In two hours it had come from nothing , " Bennett recalled recently . " It was very frightening , especially when you 're old . " <p> The elderly and children are among the most vulnerable populations to extreme weather events . That 's in part because they often do not have the ability to mobilize quickly or on their own , and they 're at a greater risk physiologically and psychologically . These trends were noticeable in the aftermath of Hurricane Katrina . <p> Consider these key findings from several studies : <p> Seventy-four percent of the deaths related to Hurricane Katrina were among those age 60 and older , and 50 percent of these were among people over age 75 , according to a 2006 study . <p> In the year after Hurricane Katrina , displaced students in Louisiana public schools , on average , performed worse in all subjects and grades compared with other students . In addition , displaced students experienced a variety of problems related to attendance , academic performance , behavior and mental health , according to a 2010 report . <p> More than 5,000 children were separated from their families after hurricanes Katrina and Rita . The last missing child was reunited with her family 6 months after the storms , according the 2010 report . <p> Three years after Hurricane Katrina , serious emotional disturbance persisted in 11.5 percent of children and adolescents , compared with an estimated 4.2 percent pre-hurricane prevalence , according to a 2010 study . <p> Extreme weather events , like the Louisiana flood of 2016 , are becoming more likely because of climate change , scientists say . So doctors who work with aging people and children are getting involved in the climate change discussion . <p> " Pediatricians have served as advocates for children on the issues that affect their health , their prosperity and their future . And I ca n't think of another issue that would have a greater impact than the changing of the climate that we all live in , " said Dr. Samantha Ahdoot , a pediatrician and assistant professor at Virginia Commonwealth University at Inova Fairfax Hospital . <p> As water surrounded the retirement community where Bennett lives , it gushed into schools in Livingston Parish . Eighteen of the district 's 44 schools were damaged . For months , students from flooded schools were educated in gyms and on alternative schedules at nearby campuses . Three schools have not been re-opened . <p> Students from these schools continue to be taught on a temporary campus in Denham Springs , until the school district figures out how to move forward with the Federal Emergency Management Agency , said Rich Wentzel , the Livingston Parish Public Schools Superintendent . <p> Last week , Casie Richardson , a first grade teacher at Denham Springs Elementary , prepared her temporary classroom for a new class of students . " This is their home away from home , " she said of her classroom . <p> The Louisiana flood of 2016 reaffirmed that for her . Richardson 's own home was flooded during the storm . She , her husband , and their two young daughters were living with her in-laws when she returned to teach 20 days after the storm . <p> Richardson taught her class in a gymnasium alongside other elementary school teachers in the months after the flood . Thin partitions were all that separated the classes . She still remembers the first time it rained while she taught in the makeshift classroom . <p> " When you 're in a gym you can hear every bit of rain , thunder , commotion , " she said . " You could tell the kids were real antsy . " <p> Richardson 's partner teacher gathered the students together and read them " Thunder Cake , " a book about a grandmother who calms her frightened granddaughter during a thunderstorm by baking a cake . <p> " She could feel how tense she was and she could see it on the kids , " Richardson said of her teaching partner . " I just feel like experiencing it myself helped me give more of a compassionate side to what the kids were going through . " <p> Talking to her students helped Richardson to better understand how to talk to her oldest daughter about the flooding , she said . Richardson asked her students how they wanted their classroom to be decorated as they prepared to move onto the temporary campus . The students overwhelmingly wanted Richardson to keep the jungle theme that she had in her classroom before the storm . But more than anything they expressed gratitude for being able to have input in the new space . <p> " It made me think , ' I need to bring this over into my own child 's life and let her have an input in our own house , ' " Richardson said . " It helped me to realize that I need to make it her space . " <p> Richardson 's effort to give students input in her new classroom is a great example of how adults can help children cope after an extreme weather event , Ahdoot said . " Recreating a sense of normalcy and control is an excellent idea by that teacher , " she said . <p> The school district 's first focus was providing the basic needs for its students , Wentzel said . The district gave away 10,000 school uniforms and provided free breakfast and lunch to all students . The goal was to restore a sense of normalcy , he said . " It 's evident from the students ' test scores they did a really good job , " he said of the district staff . <p> Though some populations are more vulnerable to extreme weather events , there are ways to level out the playing field , said David Filiberto , a research associate at Cornell University . Filiberto 's research focuses on the impacts of climate change on vulnerable populations such as those with disabilities , the aging and indigenous peoples . <p> " Older adults should have an emergency preparedness plan , " he said , adding that preparations should include a means of evacuating and or sheltering in place . Older adults should also identify a person who can check on them before the storm , he said . <p> During the Louisiana flood of 2016 , Cookie Johnson stayed on past her shift at Southern Pines Retirement Community . She 's worked at the facility as a cook for 22 years and , normally , her shift ends at 2:30 p.m . But on August 13 , she stayed into the night . When the waters started to rise around the facility , she was there to help evacuate residents from the independent living apartments into the main building of the facility . <p> While her own home in Denham Springs was flooding , she stayed behind to help . " I did what I do , " she said . " I went into the kitchen and fed them . I kept them entertained . "
