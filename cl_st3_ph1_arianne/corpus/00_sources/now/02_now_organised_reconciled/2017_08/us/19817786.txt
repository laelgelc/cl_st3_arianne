( Photo : Staff photo by Carol Vaughn ) <p> SALISBURY , Md. -- Tangier Mayor James " Ooker " Eskridge disputed the reason for the island 's land loss in a television appearance Tuesday on CNN 's climate change town hall with Al Gore . <p> Eskridge was one of several audience members who had the opportunity to ask a question of former vice president Gore at the event , along with a priest serving in Appalachian Kentucky and a teenage girl whose home was destroyed by superstorm Sandy , among others . <p> " I 'm a commercial crabber and I 've been working the Chesapeake Bay for 50-plus years . I have a crab house business out on the water , and the water level is the same as it was when the place was built in 1970 , " Eskridge told Gore and host Anderson Cooper . <p> " I 'm not a scientist , but I 'm a keen observer . If sea-level rise is occurring , why am I not seeing signs of it ? " he said . <p> Eskridge went on to say , " Our island is disappearing , but it 's because of erosion and not sea-level rise . " <p> The last statement reflected an often-repeated sentiment among island residents about the cause of land loss there . <p> Eskridge also mentioned the need for a seawall to protect Tangier from further land loss . <p> " Unless we get a seawall , we will lose our island , " he said . <p> Eskridge was introduced by Cooper as the mayor of " a very small island in the Chesapeake Bay that 's now in danger of being swallowed by the sea . " <p> James " Ooker " Eskridge , left , mayor of the town of Tangier , gives Col. Jason Kelly , commander of the Norfolk District , a boat tour around Tangier Island . Kelly came to brief the town of a little more than 700 people on the status of district projects around their island . ( Photo : U.S. Army image/Patrick Bloodgo ) <p> Cooper noted that nearly 90% of Tangier residents voted for Donald Trump for president and that islanders are asking for funding for a seawall to be built to save the island from further land loss . <p> Cooper also mentioned Trump 's telephone call to Eskridge in June , in which the president reassured the mayor that the island would be around for hundreds of years . <p> Gore , who stars in the new documentary An Inconvenient Sequel : Truth to Power , after hearing Eskridge 's question , asked the mayor to what he attributes the erosion problem on Tangier . <p> Eskridge said it was due to wave action and storms . <p> " Has that increased any ? " Gore said . <p> " Not really , " said Eskridge , noting the erosion " has been going on since Capt . John Smith discovered the island and named it . It has gotten to our doorstep now , and we focus on it more . " <p> Gore told Eskridge he was " sorry what you are going through " and said " arguments about the science are n't necessarily going to be of any comfort to you . " <p> Global temperatures will probably rise to 2 degrees celsius or 3.6 degrees fahrenheit by the end of the century . Veuer 's Natasha Abellard ( @NatashaAbellard ) has the story . Buzz60 <p> Gore said scientists say the sea level in the Chesapeake Bay is rising and that Tangier has already lost about two-thirds of the island . <p> " The forecast of the future is another two feet of sea-level rise , " he said <p> " If I see sea-level rise occurring , I 'll shout it from the housetop . We do n't have the land to give up . But I 'm just not seeing it , " Eskridge said . <p> Gore said one of the challenges related to the climate change issue is " taking what the scientists say and translating it into terms that are believable to people and where they can see the consequences in their own lives . " <p> Virginia Eastern Shorekeeper Jay Ford , who was the Eastern Shore 's representative on Gov . Terry McAuliffe 's Climate Change and Coastal Resiliency Commission , had this to say about the exchange between Eskridge and Gore : <p> " We appreciate the vital need to raise awareness of Tangier 's plight and applaud Mayor Eskridge for working hard to advocate for his island . However , we do take exception with the assertion that sea-level rise is not a factor , which threatens Tangier . <p> " Sea-level rise is not up for debate . It is a fact , plain and simple . The water is rising ; scientists agree , the military agrees , and most importantly the agencies trying to help Tangier protect their island agree . <p> " Denying this basic fact has real world implications for coastal communities because the solution for erosion and the solution for rising waters driving erosion are two different things . " <p> A number of Tangier residents and others commenting on social media after the exchange took umbrage at Gore 's recounting of a joke in which a man trapped in his home by a flood turns down three different offers of rescue as the waters continue to rise , saying the Lord would provide for him . <p> After he perished , God told him the rescuers were his means of providing for the man 's rescue . <p> " I think Mother Nature is more persuasive than any of us . ... ( Climate change ) is , unfortunately , getting worse , and a few years from now it 's going to be easier still to convince people , unfortunately . But we 're running out of time . " <p> Al Gore , former U.S. vice president <p> " We have heaven-sent , so to speak , enough solar energy in one hour to provide what the entire world uses for a full year ; and from wind , we get 40 times as much energy as the entire world needs . We have the tools available now to solve this crisis , " he said . <p> Commenters on social media took Gore 's joke as making fun of islanders ' faith . Tangier is well-known as a bastion of Methodism . <p> Another commercial crabber , Keith Colburn , who appears on the television show Deadliest Catch , appeared later in the town hall . <p> Colburn said he has seen firsthand erratic weather resulting from climate change . <p> His question of Gore was , " How do we convince the skeptics that climate change is scientifically real ? " <p> Gore said trying to " figure out first where the person is coming from " is what he does . <p> Gore said people " who work up close to nature " such as fishermen and farmers are the group where the biggest change of opinion about climate change has been noted . <p> " I think Mother Nature is more persuasive than any of us . ... It is , unfortunately , getting worse , and a few years from now it 's going to be easier still to convince people , unfortunately . But we 're running out of time , " Gore said . <p> More pressing than determining why erosion is happening is " the development of immediate and long-term solutions for the viability of both the island and community of Tangier , as well as the issue of where the funding will come from to support these solutions , not just for Tangier but for the hundreds of other small rural communities nationwide at risk , " Smith said . <p> He said the current political climate " is trending toward cutting funding for the government programs that would be most beneficial to Tangier , so trying to find funding is becoming increasingly challenging . " <p> As result , agencies like the ANPDC are increasingly pursuing " additional avenues of funding and support form the private sector to fill the gap , " Smith said , adding the agency remains focused on working toward solutions to these issues and is committed to helping Tangier . <p> Ford said of Tangier 's recent stint in the limelight , " So many things about the way of life on Tangier have remained resilient in the face of adversity , and now the island itself has the opportunity to be a national example for coastal resiliency in the face of undeniable rising waters . "
