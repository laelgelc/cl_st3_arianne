<h> Prince Charles ' charity should be the ' TripAdvisor ' of responsible business , says Business in the Community boss <p> Tucking into a portion of Harry Ramsden fish and chips in the ballroom of Blackpool Tower might not seem an obvious way for some of the country 's biggest names in business to think about how to give something back to the community . <p> But late last month , at the behest of Amanda Mackenzie , Aviva 's former chief marketing officer who last summer was named chief executive of Business in the Community ( BITC ) , that is what happened . <p> The well-fed luminaries included John McFarlane , chairman of Barclays , Vivian Hunt , the UK head of McKinsey , and Carrie Longton , one of the founders of Mumsnet . <p> As they ate , Blackpool-born Andrew Parmley , the Lord Mayor of London , appeared from under the ballroom 's stage , rising on a platform as an old friend of his serenaded the group on the famous Wurlitzer organ . <p> The Lord Mayor of London introducing a very special evening in the Blackpool ball room to help the town thrive . **26;440;TOOLONG <p> " It was one of the most surreal moments of one 's life , " explains Mackenzie , who says the evening was finished off with a serving of ice cream from Blackpool favourite Notarianni , followed by a trip up the 518-foot tower . She led the 20-strong group to the fading Lancastrian seaside town to understand some of the problems communities like it faces . <p> " The next day we went to have a look at a house that 's been bought by the council , and converted back from 16 incredibly poor standard bedsits to a place that 's much healthier for people to live , " she explains . <p> " And we were fortunate enough to talk to a couple of ex-offenders and addicts who are now clean . They explained that having much better surroundings is now key to that . " <p> Blackpool was chosen as the destination for the trip because of its stark deprivation . A working class child born there , she explains , will be five times worse off than the average British youngster . <p> The town is the lead for BITC in trying to get businesses -- big and small -- to come together to help some of the most deprived parts of the UK . " For me , obviously every place is unique , but it 's about how you approach a place and look at the issues it faces , and see if there is a blueprint to help other towns . " <p> The mission is one Mackenzie is clearly passionate about . Since taking on the role running BITC , the Prince of Wales 's responsible business charity , just over a year ago , she has spent much of her time talking to more than 70 FTSE 100 chairmen and chief executives to try to understand what they want from the organisation . <p> Set up in the shadow of the Brixton riots and amid the urban unrest in early Eighties Britain , BITC began life in 1982 with 30 companies . Within three years , the year the Prince of Wales agreed to become president , it had ballooned to 108 . <p> It has a range of programmes -- focusing on ex-offenders , healthy high streets , gender equality and employee volunteering to name but four . <p> The Blackpool trip was one of its " Seeing is Believing " ventures , the Prince 's own idea to close the gap between the boardroom and communities , which has seen more than 9,000 executives take part since its inception in 1990 . <p> For Mackenzie , whose CV reads like a who 's who of consumer brands any marketeer would want to work for -- BT , Air Miles , Mars , British Gas -- leading the charity is a challenge she clearly relishes , but at the same time is somewhat daunted by , partly due to the challenges involved , not to mention the personalities . <p> She is without doubt a supporter of the Prince , who urged her to read a series of memos between him and her predecessors and BITC board members dating back over the past three decades . <p> " The original invitation to him , and the basis on which he accepted being president , is phenomenal , " she smiles . " It 's a beautifully written letter , capturing the spirit , which is as good today . " <p> The initial concept behind BITC -- whose current chairman is Antony Jenkins , the former Barclays chief executive -- was to convene business together to help communities , on the notion that strong companies need healthy communities , and vice versa . <p> But in the time the Hackney-based charity has been in operation , corporate responsibility has become something of a watchword , with companies spending money on lots of individual projects to varied ends . Mackenzie says : " In my old life at Aviva , I 'd say , ' what is our corporate responsibility programme ? ' and I knew what it was , it was ' Street to School ' , " which champions the needs of street children to get an education . <p> " But what I never did in my head was to work out what are the acts we 're doing responsibly , within our day-to-day work , and I think business needs to be clear that at a functional level , that every function can think about its responsibility , as well as then adding up the whole . If you have a company that acts with purpose , your employees stay longer , they 're proud ... and I think everyone , thank God , has moved beyond thinking our sole purpose is to make money . " <p> From her soundings with the FTSE 100 bosses she spoke to , she learnt that rather than acting alone , " they want to come together ; they like the idea of getting that compound impact of their strength -- and also the impact of our programmes . " <p> " One thing I 'm keen that we stop is random acts of responsible business , " says Mackenzie , preferring instead a joined-up approach that helps the communities or parts of societies that are targeted . " So if we bring this together and have a concerted effort , we can get a compound impact from it . <p> " As an organisation we need to be like the TripAdvisor of responsible business , curating and convening and helping businesses . " <p> Former United Nations Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon at Seoul National University - it was during his tenure at the UN that the Global Goals were introduced <p> Prior to taking the role , Mackenzie was on secondment from Aviva , working as executive adviser to Project Everyone , the Richard Curtis-founded organisation designed to spread the word on the United Nations ' global goals on poverty and climate change , of which she remains chairman . <p> " I went from a massive company with a huge team and an enormous budget by comparison , but it got me to be much more self-sufficient and resourceful ; and yes it did begin to prepare me for the way . <p> " But I 've always been struck by what makes an adventure . Am I going to have learnt something new , am I going to have achieved something , am I going to have made a difference ? So actually , in a way , I would still have applied those criteria . " <p> Her new world is perhaps much more varied than her old corporate life , but one she seems to be thriving in . Over the summer she spent time in North Wales with the Prince , visiting Moneypenny , set up by a brother and sister , which employs 500 people and has a community programme focused on giving traineeships to young women who have got into trouble one way or another . <p> HRH The Prince of Wales chats to workers at Moneypenny in Western Gateway , Wrexham <p> " He realised early on that business has massive purchasing power and is a mainstay of society , and clearly it 's quite hard to get business to realise that this is enlightened self-interest in many ways . <p> " I would say being responsible is n't a substitute for well-run businesses , but it is a way to do business , and the notion that somehow you do this as a substitute for good business is bonkers , " she continues . <p> Is there an element of executives getting involved to cleanse their souls ? " I sort of do n't mind the reason they come into it , because I think once they do , they will discover that . So , in a way , I do n't mind their original motives , but just want every one of our members to be committed . We 're a membership organisation so , by definition , everyone gets a vote -- arguably you should be holding to account your fellow members . <p> " And if you think about imagining all of the employees of all members are effectively enrolled in that as a movement , and think about their collective impact , that 's phenomenal . But we 're quite a way off that . " <p> How then will she measure her success ? " I would really want to be the go-to place for responsible business , not that we 'd be delivering everything , but that we 're the place to engage to really understand some of the best that 's going on and , inherent within that , we 'd have a really good proposition for small companies as well . <p> " I would hope that we 've got some compelling data against our 10 target cities that shows the powerful impact of this concerted effort and compound impact . And then I would love to think we 've got a series of towns , across the UK , and that we 've got some blueprints beginning to show the first evidence of systematic change . " <p> No small feat , but for the energetic Mackenzie , the task is firmly in hand . <p> HRH The Prince of Wales with gathered ' Business Connectors ' at a BITC event
