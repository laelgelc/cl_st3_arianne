<p> I 'm officially declaring this the Summer of Plastic . With the rising tide of plastic waste -- 38.5 million plastic bottles a day in the UK alone and production set to quadruple by 2050 -- the plastic pollution crisis in our oceans has become the breakout issue . <p> Activists are stepping it up from quiet beach cleans to strident zero-tolerance campaigns . In terms of severity , Surfers Against Sewage ( sas.org.uk ) rates plastic pollution up there with climate change . Its campaign , Wasteland , urges us to boycott throwaway plastics now . Meanwhile , Greenpeace continues to hound soft-drinks brands , mapping their alarming use of plastic and abject failure to take responsibility . <p> Actually , it 's the Summer of Microplastics . An estimated 5 trillion have rapidly accumulated in our oceans . Often described as " salt and pepper sized " flakes of plastic , microplastics do n't just resemble salt , they 're likely to be in it . <p> The latest study confirms concentrations of microplastics in 16 out of 17 brands of sea salt from eight countries ( with a potential market of 6 billion consumers ) . If the sea contains plastics , so do the products we harvest from it . <p> According to research by Ghent University , European seafood consumers are likely to be ingesting up to 11,000 tiny pieces of plastic per year . Microplastics have been detected in the edible tissues of commercial fish . But they have also been found in milk , beer and honey ( where plastic has been deposited in pollen ) . <p> Aplasticplanet.com finds that nine out of 10 of us want plastic-free aisles in supermarkets . But the plastic inside our products should be concerning us , too . If we got rid of those , would our plastic-free aisles be empty ? <h> The big picture : the first electric container ship <p> Ships , like cars , are racing towards driverless and electric versions . This time shipping is taking the lead with the unveiling of the Yara Birkeland . When it sets sail next year , the Yara Birkeland will be the world 's first driverless electric container ship to cross the ocean . Sadly , however , according to reports , its cargo will be fertilisers and chemicals , which you could argue somewhat undermines its green sensibilities . <h> Well-dressed : Gung-Ho <p> There 's wearing your heart on your sleeve , and then there 's wearing the story of species decline and reversal on your bow-tie or belted day dress . <p> Each design from London startup Gung-Ho takes an environmental issue -- say the decline of bees or the stag beetle -- and turns the species into a hand-drawn print . <p> The garments are handmade in London using 100% organic fabrics and with every purchase the brand donates to charities that work with that specific issue . <p> The founder , Sophie Dunster , explains that she wanted to bring more meaning to ethical fashion . ' As our style choices tell us so much about our personalities , this way we can not only wear what we love , but what we believe in , ' she says . <p> Given that ecology is about interconnectedness , it 's great to see ethical fashion that joins the dots .
