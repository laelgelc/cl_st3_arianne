<p> Since its first season aired in 2011 , Game of Thrones has become more than just a TV show . <p> Watched by millions every week and coinciding with the rise of social media , it has amassed an online fanbase dedicated to dissecting each and every scene , deconstructing them in a way never seen before - and which may never be seen again surrounding a television programme . <p> The rise of streaming sites , such as Netflix and YouTube , may threaten to put an end to the weekly ritual of traditional televisual drama , aired weekly and subject to water cooler , lunch canteen and group chat discussion in the future but those working on GoT find themselves subject to the largest ever MMORPG film club . <p> This means that the imagery in every scene has to be perfect , with costumes and props providing an insight into each character and foreshadowing future betrayal and deceptions . <p> Jon Snow 's sword , Longclaw . Credit : HBO <p> One man aware of this intense scrutiny is Tommy Dunne , the official ' weapons master ' for Game of Thrones . Working alongside a small team in a Belfast-based workshop , he is responsible for designing and manufacturing the hundreds of weapons used in the show - using modern production processes of casting and moulding . <p> He has also served as an extra in the show , fittingly appearing as the blacksmith responsible for reworking Ned Stark 's signature ice sword into the Widows Wail and Oathbreaker following his execution . <p> Having started his career working on the weaponry for Braveheart after he unknowingly wandered into a workshop looking for work , Tommy 's 25-year career has taken him around the world , working on the production of films such as Gladiator , V For Vendetta and Saving Private Ryan , as well as HBO World War II miniseries The Pacific and Band of Brothers . <p> With the finale of the show having aired and now available on digital download , LADbible caught up with Tommy and asked him what it 's like to work on the world 's biggest TV show . <p> LADbible:Hi Tommy , so what 's your process when it comes to the ' hero weapons ' and their creation ? <p> Tommy : Hi . With each weapon , we have they have metal versions , as well as safety and rubber versions . I like to make them in bronze to give them some weight that means if the weapon does need to break something on the show then it can do . <p> Although I have read the books , I did n't take any images from them as , in my opinion , we 're not doing George RR Martin 's story , we 're doing HBO 's interpretation and their condensed world . <p> With weapons like Needle , Longclaw and Oathbreaker becoming so iconic to their respective characters , how important do you think it is to distil a character 's personality in to their weapons ? <p> Really important . You try to integrate their personality as much as possible because if , for example , a character is vicious and evil then you want it to be similar to them . Or if they 're big like the Mountain then you need a larger weapon . <p> You put more time into the hero weapons because they 're more featured , and you have to think of people and how the character is so it 's the weapon that they 've always wanted and have fought with . <p> Is that something you get much guidance with in terms of the script ? <p> The script will describe the weapons but in terms like ' a family heritage swords ' - which we had with Samwell Tarly , for example , then you have to think where you want to go with that and how it 's going to be used - whether that 's for fighting or purely ceremonial . <p> Gendry 's warhammer and Jon Snow 's Longclaw on display . Credit : HBO <p> Once you know that then you have to figure out what that particular family is about and integrate crests . I 'm very lucky that I get a free reign with what I do . I 'm employed for my expertise so that 's what I give and that can mean giving it quite loud if I need to . <p> Was there any particular weapon that was difficult to get down ? <p> The only real time was with Roose Bolton . In that instance , it was actually GOT co-creator David Benioff who said to me that it would be really good to have an inverted man on the handle and I I immediately wished I 'd thought of that because it was great idea . <p> With my work , it 's very hard to just draw something and think ' there it is , that 's it ' because I might find something better . Sometimes it 'll happen that I 'll open a box and find a material - filigree which is an ornamental wire I 've not seen in year , or some bronze or brass - and then I 'll incorporate it into what I do . <p> Does it bother you that scenes get dissected frame by frame ? One example was when the show got some flak for Kit Harington 's sword ' wobbling ' as he pulled it out and that it was not realistic . <p> No , with stuff like that I think you have to look at the fact he was on a horse , so there are one or two tons of animal beneath him . If I 'm talking to the horsemaster and tell them I want a steel sword up there , they 're going to talk about insuring the horse and the actor . <p> You have to make sure everything is safe - that 's the world we 're in and I make no excuses for that . <p> With the more generic background weapons , do they get much reuse between different Houses and battles ? <p> The way I see it , you use the weapons to tell a story . So I would n't give a Lannister or a Tarly a sword that was n't of their House because they 're very recognisable to the audience in terms of lines and colour tones . But sometimes - say with the Dothraki - you might see characters take items and weapons and souvenirs , as that plays into the story that 's going on . <p> Ned Stark with Ice . Credit : HBO <p> With sell swords , you might have a more generic weapon or Night 's Watch can be a mix because they come in with their own weapon , but most Houses have something that differentiates them . <p> How does your process compare to how it would have been for medieval blacksmiths ? <p> Personally , I have n't ever tried traditional smithing from full scratch as it 's not my job . Of course it 's all possible , but it would take a lot longer and I 'd be wasting my time now you can mould and get bronze and stainless steel casts . <p> But the basics are still the same , a lot of the shapes of the weapons I make are doable and were doable years ago . Even though it 's now done on the milling machine , it could be done by hand . There is a crossover but now in the modern world we 're faster now . <p> And finally , I know you probably ca n't say much , but can we look forward to more weapons coming up in the next season ? <p> I ca n't say anything about what 's coming up , but there 's never been a year where we have n't made something . The show has been great for the longevity and the creativity . Every year a new character comes in and their weapon will be iconic to them .
