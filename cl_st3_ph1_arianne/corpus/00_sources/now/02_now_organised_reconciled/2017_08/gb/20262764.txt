<p> In a shift in opinion that coincides with years of lobbying and a government U-turn on Heathrow expansion , 45% favour and 19% oppose the principle of building more airport capacity to meet demand . <p> Even when explicitly asked about the environmental damage of air travel , significantly more people believe there should be unlimited flights . The number of people opposing the statement " People should be able to travel by plane as much as they like , even if this harms the environment " has dropped from 49% in 2008 to 35% in 2016 . The proportion of people willing to take fewer flights to combat climate change has also dropped significantly since 2011 . <p> The survey , conducted annually by the National Centre for Social Research , appears to show a peak in awareness and concern about climate change in 2010 , when David Cameron promised that his would be the greenest government ever . <p> After cancelling plans for a third runway at Heathrow in 2010 , the prime minister appointed a commission in 2012 to review airport capacity , which led to government approval of renewed expansion in 2016 . <p> Cait Hewitt , of the Aviation Environment Federation , said : " I suspect many people assume that because a lot of government policy documents talk about climate change , and we have goals and legislation , they are trusting government to deliver on these issues and do n't feel the need for personal action in the same way they have done in the past . People expect decisions on airport expansion will be taken with a view to meeting environmental and climate change commitments . " <p> Friends of the Earth said the figures were worrying . FoE climate campaigner Rachel Kennerley said : " Some trips can only realistically be made by plane , but aviation plays a big part in contributing to climate change . So it is worrying if a significant proportion of the British public think that people should be able to travel by plane as much as they like . <p> " But while many say they support unlimited flying , the reality is that only around 15% of the UK population take about 70% of all flights . If the government replaced air passenger duty with a frequent flyer levy , it would result in most of us being able to enjoy an annual holiday without incentivising frequent flying . "
