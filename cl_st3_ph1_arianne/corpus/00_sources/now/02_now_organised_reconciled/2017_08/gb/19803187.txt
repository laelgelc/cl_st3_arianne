<h> As digitalisation enters new territories , EU port authorities must invest in both hard and soft infrastructure to stay competitive <h> 2 Aug 2017 <p> For many years , digital technologies have been transforming logistics across a range of industrial sectors . Today , the rise of big data and the Internet of Things ( IoT ) are continuing to make digital devices more complex and widespread . The pace of change has been particularly rapid in the transportation sector as both suppliers and their customers demand quicker , cheaper and simpler services to connect them . <p> At the frontline of this innovation push are intermodal hubs , where cargo is transferred between different modes of transport . The European Federation of Inland Ports ( EFIP ) , which represents inland ports in Europe , believes their future competitiveness will hinge on the quality of their hard physical infrastructure and their adoption of soft digital solutions . <p> Intermodal ports " As trade and cargo volumes are expected to grow on a global level , the integration of intelligent transport systems within port communities will have huge potential to increase the efficiency and cost effectiveness of logistics " , said Alexander van den Bosch , Director of EFIP . <p> Over ? 1.5trn will be needed between 2010 and 2030 to develop transport infrastructure in line with demand <p> Since they are not directly involved in the supply chain , intermodal ports can play a neutral role in bringing parties together to discuss strategies for developing ' logistics 4.0 ' , which encapsulates cutting-edge supply chain technologies such as IoT devices . " As facilitators , intermodal ports can support the development of a port digital layer , and can initiate the development of independent IT platforms . This would enable shippers and forwarders to bundle their freight flows and integrate real-time data " , said van den Bosch . <p> Stakeholder statesTo make digitalisation smooth and effective , public and private entities need to collaborate . The collection of big data by an independent governmental body would allow containers and cargo to be monitored in real time . This could lead to better economic forecasting as an array of different parameters , including water levels in ports and fuel consumption in vehicles , could be combined . It would also help public bodies to gauge when and where to develop physical infrastructure . <p> The EU 's 2011 white paper -- Roadmap to a Single European Transport Area -- stated that over ? 1.5trn will be needed between 2010 and 2030 to develop transport infrastructure in line with demand . By 2050 , the demand for freight transport activity is expected to rise by 80 percent and passenger activity by 51 percent . To keep up , the EU will need to spend at least ? 550bn before 2020 , ? 215bn of which will go towards fixing bottlenecks . <p> " The debate about future EU budgets from 2020-2027 has begun " , said van den Bosch . " EFIP will join forces with 30 other European transport organisations to emphasise that investing in transport is the best plan for Europe . The EU 's tendency to develop projects through multi-fund approaches and blending should not be considered a silver bullet . The sector needs continuous , dedicated funding . " <p> Sustain the futureIn the private sector , transport and waste management companies will need to keep working towards a circular economy that emphasises waste reduction and utilises vast supplies of raw materials . Port authorities such as Strasbourg already host industries that collect , treat and ship waste materials . If these materials were incinerated and used to generate power , they could reduce fossil fuel use and create jobs . <p> " Inland ports are attractive for the recycling industry , due to their proximity to cities , industries and terminals " , van den Bosch said . " Intelligent transport systems bring additional opportunities to reduce carbon emissions and to support EU objectives on climate change towards 2030 . " In future , these will be key areas of growth , as will the more interlinked networks between inland ports and seaports . <p> " As economies shift their emphasis towards services , logistical hubs will need digitalisation and automation to stay afloat . As new technologies emerge to support this , they will change the industry in dramatic yet positive ways . "
