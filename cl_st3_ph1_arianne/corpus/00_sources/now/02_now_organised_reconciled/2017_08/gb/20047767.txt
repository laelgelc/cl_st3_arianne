<h> Newport student hopes to help coral with her 3D skeleton <p> Jessica Gregory , a product design graduate , has designed a 3D printed skeleton which replicates the coral 's natural skeleton . <p> The 23-year-old , who is now working with ocean conservationist Fabien Cousteau , said : " Coral is a complex and fragile organism . It is very important to humanity as well as the planet - coral reefs are one of the most diverse ecosystems in the world . <p> " But , due to human factors such as pollution , climate change and sedimentation , 30 per cent of the existing reefs could vanish in the next 30 years , in addition to the vast amount of reefs which have already been lost . <p> " Coralise is a project which aims to irradiate juvenile coral mortality , which stands at 99 per cent . There is a huge problem with coral dying . " <p> Miss Gregory , of Caerphilly Close , in the Rhiwderin area of Newport , said the structure she has designed is made of a ceramic material created from coral skeletons . This , along with a carefully designed organic form , attracts the young coral . <p> " The pattern on the product replicates the corallite structures created as coral grows its skeleton , protecting juveniles in developmental stages , " she said . <p> " Coralise is currently being tested but it seems like the coral will survive . <p> " I have been told the project could really help . " <p> Miss Gregory is currently studying a double masters in global innovation design , taught by both Imperial College London and the Royal College of Art . <p> " I have always been interested in nature and I am also interested in arts -- the course allows me to combine the two , " she said . <p> " My focus is on designing using nature for nature , helping with environmental issues .
