<h> First sighting of rare bird at Dee Estuary nature reserve <p> AN UNUSUAL bird has nested for the first time at the RSPB Dee Estuary nature reserve to the delight of staff . <p> The cattle egret was spotted at the Burton Mere Wetlands site near Neston and now visitors have the chance to experience the rare birds for themselves . <p> Little egrets are a familiar sight at the estuary with the hundreds of the birds living on the nature reserve but prior to this year the much rarer cattle egret had only managed two previous breeding attempts in the whole of the UK . <p> Cattle egrets are usually found around cattle and many people recognise them from nature documentaries where they often feature around herds of zebras and water buffalo in sub-Saharan Africa . <p> With bright white feathers these relatives of the more familiar grey heron are beautiful to see and fairly easy to spot . <p> Site manager at the RSPB Dee Estuary Colin Wells told the Globe : " We 've watched excitedly as little egrets rapidly colonised the Dee Estuary over the past couple of decades , with a total of 84 pairs nesting here this year . <p> " Their close relatives , cattle egrets , have been a regular visitor over the past six years since Burton Mere Wetlands opened , usually in the autumn when we have cattle grazing on the reserve . <p> " Egrets , along with other heron-like birds such as spoonbills , have gradually moved into the UK as their historic breeding areas in Europe are becoming hotter and drier due to climate change . <p> " Last year we had a record six cattle egrets at one point , but it was surprising to see at least two stay through the winter , even when the cattle were taken indoors to avoid the harsh weather . <p> " The birds felt so at home , we were delighted when they stayed to nest in amongst the established little egret colony . " <p> The pair successfully raised one chick , but they quickly left the area after the youngster flew the nest . <p> However , with cattle grazing the reserve again , one cattle egret has returned and there is hope more will follow . <p> Colin added : " Visitors have been getting brilliant close views of the cattle egret from the comfort of the Reception Hide . <p> " There 's no guarantee how long it will stay so if you fancy a look at one of these exotic birds close to home , come for a visit soon .
