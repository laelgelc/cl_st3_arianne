<h> UNBC Students Studying Plants in Port Edward <p> A group of students from the University of Northern British Columbia are spending time at Cassiar Cannery in Port Edward . <p> Professor Darwyn Coxson says a group of 20 students are exploring the different kind of plants that grow in the saltwater marshes . <p> " Looking to identify plants , we 're looking at structures of grasses and sedges . This could be valuable tools if they 're working in the resource industry , for parks , or a variety of employers . These are valuable skills to have in order to keep track of changes in natural environments . " <p> The students are spending some of their time in these marshes , where they 're gathering different types of species to better understand how they 're able to survive in this kind of ecosystem . Coxson says this is the best way for students to learn instead of being in a lecture hall full time . <p> " If we taught this course in the winter then we might have dry plants . It would probably be three hours a week in a busy schedule . Here , they 're in an intensive group , learning from their peers , they have fresh plant material . It 's a very hands-on small class learning environment . It 's really the ideal learning environment for student . Particularly , in the field of biology . " <p> Niel Pilgrim is a Teachers Assistant and says there 's one thing he 's taken away from here and that 's how fresh and salt water flow through the intertidal zone . <p> " Water runs down the mountain and all fresh water comes from the rain water . That makes the upper area of the salter water marsh fresher . As you continue to move further along , it gets very salty . It becomes fresher when you get to the water again . What 's happening is fresh water coming down and a lot of fresh water from the Skeena making the ocean itself here more fresh than it actually is salt . The centre part of the marsh as the tide comes up , it brings that salt with it and draws back leaving the salt deposited and as water evaporates , it makes it saltier in the centre of the marsh . " <p> Professor Coxson says this kind of classroom provides an opportunity for everyone to get an understanding of the North Coast marshes and provide information to future generations . <p> " When you look at scientific literature , you know what 's happening down in the United States and along the Fraser River . Surprisingly , there 's very little information about what the baseline information is here . Knowing that information is going to help us understand what happens with climate change , or industrial development . It 's critical knowledge for understanding our backyard . " <p> UNBC has previously spent time at the cannery and says it 's the marshes are very diverse as they 've discovered something in each visit .
