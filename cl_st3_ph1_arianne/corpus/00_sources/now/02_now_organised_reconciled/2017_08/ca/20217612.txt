<h> What is this ? <p> Dozens of watercraft filled Howe Sound Saturday afternoon to oppose liquefied natural gas and other industrial development in the Squamish area . <p> The flotilla , a recurring protest organized by the group My Sea to Sky , aims to draw attention to a waterway that activists say has only begun to recover after a century of heavy industry . <p> " It threatens the recovery of the herring and it threatens salmon as well , not to mention whales and all kinds . Everything , pretty much everything that lives in Howe Sound , " said organizer Tracey Saxby . <p> " Howe Sound is a treasure in our back yard . Imagine what Vancouver would be like if Stanley Park was n't protected . " <p> About 150 people took part in the floating demonstration that targeted in large part the proposed Woodfibre LNG plant . <p> Boaters formed a heart shape around a banner reading " protect Howe Sound " that they said they intend to send to politicians . <p> In addition to the region 's marine ecosystem , project opponents say they are concerned the LNG plant will generate greenhouse gasses and contribute to climate change . <p> " As our world is getting warmer , the oceans are actually drawing in more carbon , which is making the ocean water more acidic , so this is a really big problem as well , " Saxby said . <p> Global News has reached out to Woodfibre for comment . <p> But according reports earlier in the month , there are plans to seek long term contracts and begin construction in 2018 . <p> However as oil and gas prices plummet , some industry watchers are skeptical about the project 's future . <p> " If large international players like Shell and Petronas can not make a go of LNG , I do n't see how a small operator like Woodfibre can , " said Tom Gunton with SFU 's school of Resource and Environmental Management .
