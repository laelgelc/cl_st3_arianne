<p> Now that this year 's rains of biblical proportions have subsided , one can actually enjoy an occasional sunrise , assuming that solar eclipses do not become daily occurrences due to climate change . ( Since seeing people in rowboats on a West Island boulevard , I do n't take anything for granted anymore . ) <p> And since the lake in my backyard has evaporated into mud and confused landlocked carp , I am thinking about starting a vegetable garden . The difference between my idea and that of people who segregate little patches to growing such grocery produce , is that I want to do the whole backyard . The reason I am doing it in the back is because , hidden by a very tall hedge , I wo n't become a news item in the West Island Gazette : " Columnist is put on notice by municipality that his backyard is not zoned as farm land . " <p> I am keen on my project for several reasons , one being that I will no longer have to worry about cutting the grass . Whatever I do cut , I can eat . ( A pity we ca n't digest grass . ) So far , so good , but to be honest with you , my first choice in backyard application has always been to have my own Ferris wheel . And although I have not seen anything banning that in the local bylaws , I am pretty sure it 's illegal . ( But it 's okay for your neighbour to have a trampoline so you can watch his kids rise up , hover , and then and down , over your hedge all day . I once watched a poodle go up and down . ) <p> In preparation , I bought the most important items for suburban ( secretive ) farming : Overalls and a pitch fork so that I could take an American Gothic selfie . ( Some of you less art-appreciating types may have to look up what that means . ) <p> I have already started small in planting vegetables . My first emerging crop is a corner of squash plants . For weeks I have been admiring the tentacles of herbaceous , flowery vines creeping across my lot . I was eagerly anticipating a field of buxom gourds in multitudes of colour until my mother-in-law recently visited . She took one look at all of the squash thing 's flowers and casually said , " You are not going to get any squash out of that . You have a male plant . " <p> I was shocked . And how did she know ? Better yet , who knew that there are male and female vegetables ? What do you look for ? ( Do n't answer that . ) How am I supposed to tell when even the veterinarian at the shelter where I got my cat , told me I had a male and upon the second check-up revised that diagnosis to female ? In that light , I would n't have a clue as to what to check for on a vegetable . <p> My next crop was rhubarb but I was not so impressed with growing something where the leaves take up most of the land and you ca n't eat them . So I grew kale . Very hardy , very leafy , very productive but I have a new name for the vegetable I had preciously never tasted : The cardboard of the lettuce family . <p> I tried growing peppers and got only one ( obviously female . ) I grew carrots and ended up with holes after some rabbits ambushed them one night . I have the same trepidation with tomatoes . I would love to have tomatoes if they grew on trees . That would look really cool . <p> All this to say that I have come to a conclusion : Why complicate my life with all sorts of nebulous vegetables ? I can solve the whole grass-cutting , field-covering question in one fell swoop . I will plant corn . This idea has an added advantage way beyond the food part . I will knit sweaters out of the corn silk and hand them out as Christmas gifts ( with the proviso that they are not machine-washable because they fall apart in the spin cycle . ) I will tell my unsuspecting friends that the sweaters are pure silk .
