<p> Finding out how much the Alberta government is spending to administer its carbon tax program should n't take seven months of waiting and end with pages of blanked-out documents . <p> But that 's what happened when the Canadian Federation of Independent Business filed a freedom of information ( FOIP ) request . <p> The application went in on Jan. 18 , disappeared into the labyrinth that is the FOIP system and emerged this month with the important bits redacted . <p> For example , one internal memo from the deputy minister of finance to the deputy minister of the climate change office on Nov. 14 , 2016 , offers this tantalizing bit of information on the cost of the program : " The total operating cost of design , implementation and administration from 2017-18 to 2021-22 is expected to be ... " <p> The dollar amount has been blanked out by a FOIP officer , replaced with a reference to two sections of the FOIP Act ( 22 and 24 ) explaining why the dollar amount is secret . <p> For anyone experienced with FOIP requests , sections 22 and 24 are familiar . They raise the impenetrable brick wall that is cabinet confidentiality . <p> Here 's an excerpt from 22 : " The head of a public body must refuse to disclose to an applicant information that would reveal the substance of deliberations of the executive council ( cabinet ) . " <p> Section 22 is pretty much the default setting of government responses to sensitive information . <p> Understandably , the folks at the CFIB were not happy to hit the Section 22 brick wall . <p> " This government has rolled out the carbon tax full steam ahead , but time and again avoids being transparent about the costs , " complained Amber Ruddy , Alberta director of the CFIB . <p> Soon after , the United Conservative Party issued a news release slamming the government for " hiding " the cost . <p> But here 's where this story gets a little odd . <p> When asked by journalists to explain why the information was not released , government officials shrugged their shoulders and said the information was n't secret . <p> In fact , Finance Minister Joe Ceci had declared the cost during an all-party committee meeting last April when he was questioned by then-Wildrose MLA Derek Fildebrandt . <p> " How much money is being spent on administering the carbon tax rebates ? " asked Fildebrandt in a transcript of the meeting provided online by Hansard , the official legislative report of all the debates and discussions by MLAs . <p> " It 's expected to cost in total ... 2.5 million for the ' 17-18 fiscal year , " replied Ceci . <p> There 's the answer -- $2.5 million for the current fiscal year . <p> The CFIB 's Ruddy was understandably irritated and perplexed . <p> " Why did the freedom of information request come back redacted ? " she asked . " And I 'm still poking around looking for it now . Where exactly can I find it ? I have to search the Hansard of a committee ? ... This information , it 's important to have it available publicly so people can analyze it . " <p> Indeed , why would a FOIP officer redact the cost of administering the tax if Ceci was willing to discuss it publicly ? <p> Near the end of a frustrating day , I heard from a finance official who explained that the FOIP officer was required to redact the information because it was in a confidential document that had gone before cabinet for discussion last year . <p> However , on the other hand , when Ceci mentioned the $2.5 million price tag in April , he did so in a public venue at an all-party committee recorded by Hansard . <p> The Hansard record is a public document and as such would n't have been included in a FOIP response . The point being that FOIP officers should n't be digging through public documents to give to the public because the information is already , well , public . <p> But another point here is the information should have been more readily available to a member of the public . <p> It should n't involve a fishing expedition through pages of Hansard hoping to find an answer . <p> And yet another point . The United Conservative Party should n't have criticized the government Wednesday for " hiding " the $2.5 million price tag .
