<h> Letters : No point in making Lost Lagoon a salt water marsh <p> Re : A fresh look at an old salt-water flat ; Lost Lagoon : Experts Consider Feasibility Of Restoring 15-hectare site to its former salt marsh state , July 26 <p> The Vancouver Park Board even looking at turning Lost Lagoon into a saltwater marsh is about the worst possible waste of taxpayers ' funds we 've heard of for many a year . Such a project will have a multi-million-dollar pricetag , and for what -- a planned 10-year project period . <p> Most homeowners are already paying double-digit property tax increases . It is about time we had a few years when city tax increases stayed well within the inflation rate . We need responsible , focused leadership from the parks board , which must reverse the current parks poor upkeep problem and keep costs within taxpayers ' abilities . <p> Al Siddons , Vancouver <p> Leave Mother Nature alone <p> So we are considering playing God again . The wildlife in the Lost Lagoon ecosystem seems to be quite happy in fresh water . If global warming changes that , the flora and fauna will have time to adjust . For once , why do n't we just lay off ? <p> Bill Richardson , West Vancouver <p> Who knew ? It 's cold in Vancouver in winter <p> Re : City considering snow readiness plan , July 26 <p> City of Vancouver officials never cease to dumbfound anyone who is paying attention . Apparently , the councillors have just now become aware that Vancouver is located on the 49th parallel . Who was responsible for keeping this important fact from our elected controllers ? <p> The plan is to have more snow removal equipment and salt on hand for the winter . What is the discussion point of this -- that there is going be salt left over after winter and the municipality will require a yard sale to get rid of it ? <p> Vancouver city general manager of engineering services Jerry Dobrovolny says " climate change is real " when referring to last year 's unprepared snow events . So the bike lanes were cleared while pedestrians twirled around on icy sidewalks . Was that the fault of man-made climate change ? Poor planning , excuses for allegedly unforeseeable weather are what we have here . We need people in charge who understand that it is cold in winter and hot in summer . <p> Barry Craig , Vancouver <p> Market conditions do n't explain cancellation of LNG project <p> Re : Petronas cancels $11.4B plan , July 26 <p> There is no doubt in my mind that Petronas bailed on the Prince Rupert LNG project because of the election of the NDP . The timing is too obvious to ignore . I know Petronas is saying it was unfavourable market conditions , but what else are they going to say ? Petronas is still trying to do projects in B.C. ( hopefully ) and there is no upside to publicly pointing out that the NDP is hostile to industry . It would just make them even more hostile than they are now . <p> An $11.4-billion project could have provided funding for schools , hospitals , clinics , parks , rinks , pools , doctors , police , fire , etc . I hope the anti-capitalists are pleased with themselves . <p> Brock Bishop , North Vancouver <p> Sort out banned items from recyclables <p> Re : Disposal ban compliance on rise , July 20 <p> I just finished reading the article on the rise of the disposal ban compliance . I think it is a great thing that is being done to decrease the amount of recyclables being redirected to facilities that will sort them . What I would like to point out is that when a load is deemed to have banned items in it , the hauler is fined , but the banned items are not removed from the load -- it is simply pushed into the rest of the garbage . I was told by one of the inspectors a few years ago that there were no funds to pay for the labour that is needed to remove the banned items . So over the last few years that they have been issuing the fines , those items remain in with the rest of the garbage .
