<p> OTTAWA -- Ontario 's cap-and-trade program to reduce greenhouse gas emissions has provided the government with billions of dollars in new revenues , and on Monday the Liberals announced that towns and cities will get a cut of the action -- once they fill out the paperwork . <p> Environment Minister Chris Ballard -- who 's been on the job for two weeks following the resignation of his predecessor , Glen Murray -- made the announcement at this year 's Association of Municipalities of Ontario conference . <p> " We 're excited to be providing families and businesses with more opportunities to make low-carbon decisions , and to see the benefits in their homes , their places of work , and right across their communities , " Ballard said . <p> Under the terms of the new Municipal GHG Challenge Fund , any Ontario municipality can apply for up to $10 million to fund projects aimed at reducing greenhouse gas emissions . The government is making special allowances for small and northern municipalities that might not meet certain technical qualifications the program requires . <p> Ballard emphasized that the government intends to get this newly announced money flowing quickly : the application website opens today and will accept submissions until November 14 . Projects already underway can apply for provincial funding so long as they started after June 1 . <p> Winning applicants will be announced in February , just a few months before the 2018 election . The political context matters : Progressive Conservative leader Patrick Brown has promised to dismantle the cap-and-trade program should his party form a government , potentially imperiling the fund 's sole source of money . <p> Ballard denied the Liberals were simply trying to rope in more accomplices for their cap-and-trade program and improve its popularity prior to the election . <p> " Frankly , as far as I 'm concerned , this about seeing some action , getting some feet on the ground , " Ballard said . " This is about partnering with municipalities , the people who see first-hand the challenges of climate change . " <p> AMO president Lynn Dollin , who said earlier in the day that municipalities needed a sustainable source of revenue not tied to the political whims of the day , was n't willing to look a gift horse in the mouth . <p> " Municipalities will work with any government that wants to work with us , " she said . " No matter what stripe . " <p> It remains to be seen how many projects will actually survive the application process , and whether the government will wind up spending the full $100 million allocated . Even if it does , the size of the fund may limit what good it can do . Toronto passed a long-term climate change strategy in July whose total cost to 2050 is estimated at $60 billion -- and Toronto is just one ( albeit the largest ) of Ontario 's 444 municipalities . <p> The Liberals say today 's announcement is n't the last money that municipalities will see , and they also invited the federal government to increase funding to municipalities for emission-cutting projects . <p> Dollin acknowledged that Ontario 's towns and cities have an enormous backlog of potential projects that could be funded under the $100 million plan -- but she says the work has to start somewhere . <h> For over four decades , TVO has built a reputation based on trust , quality and depth <p> We are focused on delivering the best in digital education , current affairs and documentaries , helping to build strong communities with engaged citizens .
