<h> VOICE OF THE PEOPLE August 10 , 2017 <p> Re . Ellie Macklin 's " An Inukshuk Welcome " ( Aug. 5 ) , I agree " each summer there seem to be more than there were the year before , " but we part ways after this . I feel the multitude of structures constructed by tourists are not " whimsical and inspiring , " but more a blight on landscapes I leave the city to enjoy . I despair when I attempt to appreciate wonderful forests and lakes , only to turn a corner and see someone has disrupted the natural beauty with these constructions . <p> They are fascinating and beautiful structures in the context of Inuit culture . But respect for the Inuit is not being demonstrated here . It is merely an appropriation to selfishly stamp one 's identity upon a natural area . <p> To answer Macklin 's question , " What could be more Canadian ? " I would suggest not marring serene landscapes with what equates to graffiti screaming " I was here ! " I invite people to ignore Macklin 's advice to build an inukshuk and do what I do when they see one : kick it over . <p> Paul Wesson , Halifax <p> Hybrids best choice <p> Nova Scotia Power has announced construction of more charging stations for electric cars . Frequent power outages likely will increase due to climate change . What will be done during power loss to keep electric cars going ? I think hybrids are a much better choice . We 've driven one since 2009 with no issues . <p> Arline Palmer , Halifax <p> EV Gridlock ? <p> Where is the electricity coming from to charge electric cars we are all going to buy in the near future ? We already have power grid disruptions due to myriad problems . Now we want everyone to buy an electric car . <p> There is no infrastructure at the moment , a few stations here or there . Only about one per cent of people drive an electric car , but some nations are saying by 2040 everyone will have one . <p> Who is going to pay for all this ? Electricity companies like Emera ? No , it will be all of us , yet we have some of the highest electricity prices in Canada . The demand on our grid is immense . How are we to deal with so much more ? <p> Charlie Tischler , Bridgetown <p> NSHA APOLOGY <p> In response to the recent article Cape Breton doctor disputes Nova Scotia Health Authority hiring statement ( Aug. 7 ) , I would like to respond and apologize to Dr. Michael MacDonald and your readers . <p> Dr. MacDonald said some of the 16 incoming new physicians announced by NSHA started arriving more than a year ago and were recruited by local doctors . I would like to clarify that the number of doctors we announced in our release either started in July 2017 or will be arriving over the next six months . <p> However , the article prompted us to look at our list again and we have a correction to make . We announced 16 doctors , when in fact there are 13 doctors arriving . We counted a doctor who has been offered a contract , but has not yet signed , as well as a doctor who is starting , but going to another area of Nova Scotia , not Cape Breton . The third name was on the list in error . <p> I want to apologize for any confusion this may have caused and to emphasize this announcement was only intended as good news , to congratulate the community , the recruiters and the many doctors Dr. MacDonald refers to who have assisted in this recruitment effort with obvious success .
