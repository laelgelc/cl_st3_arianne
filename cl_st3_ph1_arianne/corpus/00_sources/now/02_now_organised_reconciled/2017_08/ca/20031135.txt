As NAFTA 2.0 negotiations begin , an old trade issue with a strange name has emerged to create unlikely allies across the political spectrum and staunch defenders in the oilpatch . <p> The " proportionality clause " originally appeared in the Canada-U.S . Free Trade Agreement of 1988 and became a major issue in that year 's federal election that returned Prime Minister Brian Mulroney to office . It was replicated six years later in the North American Free Trade Agreement ( although Mexico won an exemption ) . <p> The clause can be invoked if a government in Canada reduces U.S. access to Canadian oil , natural gas , coal , electricity and refined petroleum products without a corresponding reduction in domestic access to those products -- in other words , the ratio of energy exports versus supply must remain consistent , or proportional . <h> Right and left <p> The left-leaning Council of Canadians says the clause erodes sovereignty and gives the U.S. too much control over Canadian energy production . <p> The business-friendly Conference Board of Canada says the clause is an outdated restriction that is n't needed in today 's energy-rich world and should be cast aside . <p> " What happened with NAFTA is that Canadian oil and gas became North American oil and gas and we have very little control of it , " said Maude Barlow , honorary chair of the Council of Canadians , in an interview . <p> " We feel very strongly this is a chance to reopen what should never have been signed in the first place . " <h> Policy flexibility <p> She said the proportionality clause could allow the U.S. to prevent Canada from choking back oilsands production to meet its international environmental commitments , for instance , or to interfere if Canada tries to keep more oil and gas for domestic use during an energy shortage . <p> Michael Burt , a director with the Conference Board , said the clause is n't fair because it does n't cover Mexico , whose energy industry is being opened to private investment after decades of government ownership . <p> " Evidence would suggest that it has n't really been a binding constraint at any point in the 25 years of NAFTA , " he added . <p> " It 's not really adding any value and it 's taking away potential future flexibility in terms of policy responses , whether it 's to a crisis or to climate change mediation . " <p> While the Canadian think-tanks oppose the clause in submissions to the federal government , the energy industry itself prefers it to an alternative that could include government meddling in energy markets . <p> " It was put in there basically as a ' good neighbour ' clause , " said Nick Schultz , vice-president and general counsel at the Canadian Association of Petroleum Producers , who was involved in free trade negotiations in the 1980s as an Ottawa trade lawyer . <p> " It 's not the evil thing that people have long tried to characterize it as . It 's simply saying ... treat your customers fairly . " <h> Crises and NEP <p> He said the clause was created following global oil supply crises in the 1970s and the imposition of the National Energy Program in Canada in 1980 . The NEP aimed to promote Canadian energy security by favouring domestic over export markets but was blamed for throwing Canada 's energy industry into a deep downturn . <p> Schultz pointed out that proportionality protects energy consumers on both sides of the border -- as more U.S. oil products and natural gas replace western Canadian and offshore supplies in Eastern Canada , it will protect Canadian importers from arbitrary U.S. export cuts . <p> In U.S. President Donald Trump 's list of objectives for the NAFTA talks released in July , there 's no mention of proportionality . The one paragraph on energy policy lists general goals , including North American energy security and strong markets . <h> U.S. interests <p> The clause is receiving very little attention in the U.S. , said Robert Holleyman , the CEO of trade consulting firm C&amp;M International and a former U.S. trade negotiator in the Obama administration . <p> But that does n't mean it 's not important , he said . <p> " It ensures a certain level of North American market security because it ensures that U.S. dependence and reliance on Canada to supply some of our energy needs is largely predictable , " he said . <p> He said if Canada wants to drop the clause , it would likely have to offer substantial trade-offs in negotiations . The clause seems to be working and , therefore , should probably not be removed , he added . <p> Canada 's position on proportionality is unclear . It was n't one of the priorities mentioned by Foreign Affairs Minister Chrystia Freeland in a statement Monday . <p> Her office did not immediately respond to a request Tuesday to clarify Canada 's stance .
