<h> Named for our leaders : There 's a school for ( nearly ) every prime minister <h> Will debates over John A. Macdonald 's legacy end the tradition of naming schools for politicians ? <p> Joe Clark acknowledges party members ' cheers as he wins the Progressive Conservative leadership race in 1976 . Clark is the only living former prime minister who has a school named for him in Canada . ( Canadian Press ) <h> About The Author <p> Aaron WherryParliament Hill Bureau <p> Aaron Wherry has covered Parliament Hill since 2007 and has written for Maclean 's , the National Post and the Globe and Mail . <p> There are , now debatably , several schools named for John A. Macdonald , and the most celebrated names in prime ministerial history seem to have been rewarded with more than one namesake institution . <p> Wilfrid Laurier has his own school board in Quebec and a university in Waterloo , Ont . John Diefenbaker has a high school in Calgary and elementary schools in Toronto and Richmond , B.C. <p> A survey in 2013 found 12 schools named for Lester B. Pearson , three more than for Pierre Trudeau . <p> A manatee sticks its head out of the water at Miami Seaquarium in 2014 . In Florida , more schools are named for this noble beast than for George Washington . ( Wilfredo Lee/Associated Press ) <p> Those honours are perhaps to be expected . Unofficially , it seems that if you spent any time as prime minister , you get a school . <p> Alexander Mackenize , the second prime minister , has a high school in Sarnia , Ont. , the city he represented as an MP . John Abbott , who became Canada 's third prime minister when Macdonald died , has a college in Montreal . <p> John Thompson , who followed Abbott , is the namesake for a junior high school in Edmonton . Mackenzie Bowell , who followed Thompson , was honoured with a school in Belleville , Ont. , though it has closed ( according to a news report , some of its students were transferred to a school named for Macdonald ) . <p> R.B. Bennett , though remembered critically for his handling of the Great Depression , had a school in Calgary , though it has also closed . <p> Local connections likely motivate some namings . So there might eventually be hometown schools for Mulroney in Baie-Comeau , Que. , Chr ? tien in Shawinigan , Que. , or Martin in Windsor , Ont . <h> Getting past the unpleasantness <p> It 's also possible , if a pity , that no such naming will take place until each of those former prime ministers has died . Time heals most wounds , and obituaries tend to accentuate the most flattering bits . After that , it 's easier to appreciate a politician 's achievements and never mind the unpleasantness of , say , a sponsorship scandal or one 's dealings with Karlheinz Schreiber . <p> Clark might benefit from having been prime minister for only nine months in 1979-80 . If he 'd occupied the office for much longer , he or his government might have done more to embarrass themselves . <p> Were someone to announce plans next week for a Stephen J. Harper Public School , there would no doubt be snide questions about the school 's science curriculum . <p> Should schools bearing Sir John A. Macdonald 's name be changed ? 2:32 <p> Even 25 years from now , it might matter where such a school would be located . As late as 2010 , there was controversy over naming a school in Calgary for Pierre Trudeau . <p> Even while recent prime ministers have gone without the honour , schools have been named for former governors general Adrienne Clarkson and Micha ? lle Jean . <h> Will we stop naming schools after PMs ? <p> In Macdonald 's case , mind you , only time has actually allowed an argument against him to be built . And if his name goes , his supporters might ask why those who continued his policies are allowed to have schools . <p> Recent , current and future prime ministers might also be condemned for other reasons -- failing to act to combat climate change , for instance . <p> In these more cynical -- or realistic -- times , we might simply move away from naming schools for politicians . <p> Research published a decade ago in the United States found evidence communities were moving away from naming schools for presidents . In Florida , the number of schools named for manatees apparently outnumbered the schools named for George Washington . ( In fairness , the manatee is a very noble beast . ) <p> ' It may provoke a debate over whether that person is worthy of emulation . ' -- Report on Florida schools <p> " The difficulty with naming a school after a person is that it may provoke a debate over whether that person is worthy of emulation , " the authors wrote . <p> There are certainly other admirable figures whose names could be used instead : astronauts , doctors , teachers , hard-working and humble political journalists . Those whose mistakes and failings are less prominent . <p> There would still be history books for prime ministers . <p> Or , in maintaining the tradition of naming schools for them , we could decide that each prime minister , simply by holding the office , made a meaningful contribution to the country , as imperfect or outright flawed as that contribution may have been . <p> That they were a symbol of public service and democracy .
