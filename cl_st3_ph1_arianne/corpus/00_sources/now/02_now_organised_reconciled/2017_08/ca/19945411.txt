<h> ' Invented theatre to pretend that there 's a backroom cabal of social justice warriors ' <p> Jason Kenney entered the race to become the new leader of the United Conservative Party on July 29 . ( Richard Marion/Radio-Canada ) <p> A member of the committee writing Alberta 's new social studies curriculum is criticizing UCP leadership candidate Jason Kenney for calling the revamp a secret NDP effort to force its ideology on students . <p> Lindsay Gibson , an assistant professor of education at the University of Alberta , says that by making the curriculum redesign an issue , Kenney is engaging in " dog-whistle " politics . The term refers to a coded message intended for , and understood by , a particular demographic group . <p> " I just think that it 's invented theatre to pretend that there 's a backroom cabal of social justice warriors who are secretly designing curriculum and social engineering things , " said Gibson , who specializes in social studies education . <p> " It 's just not true . " <p> When Kenney launched his leadership bid on July 29 , he said the outline for the new social studies curriculum was " riddled with politically correct themes " such as colonialism , oppression and climate change , and lacked any study of Canadian , Alberta and military history . <p> " The NDP is preparing to attack Alberta 's model of successful school choice . They 're undermining parental authority and they are rewriting the school curriculum in secret , " Kenney told the crowd at his campaign launch . <p> " We all know what this means . More teaching fads and political agendas in our classrooms . " <p> Kenney said the NDP was trying to suppress schools from teaching military history , including Canada 's contributions in the two world wars . <p> Jason Kenney playing politics to win UCP leadership .
