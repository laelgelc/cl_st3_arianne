His wife , born three months after the war , lost her father after he was exposed to radiation while returning to Nagasaki after the bombing to see what had happened . <p> On Sunday , a commemoration of the first deployment of an atomic bomb over Hiroshima on Aug. 6 , 1945 , and the bombing of Nagasaki three days later , was held at 6:30 p.m. in the Toronto Peace Garden . The first bomb and its after-effects killed 140,000 . The second bomb and its aftermath killed 80,000 people . <p> Keynote speaker Douglas Roche , a former senator , MP and Canadian ambassador for disarmament , used the event to call on Ottawa to sign the Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons , the first UN treaty banning nuclear weapons . <p> " It 's important to use this as an occasion to stir the conscience of the modern public , not to mention government officials setting public policy , " Roche told the Star . " The goal of humanity must be a nuclear weapons free world . A nuclear war can never be won and must never be fought and the only guarantee that a nuclear weapon will never be used is their elimination . " <p> Article Continued Below <p> One hundred and twenty-two countries voted to approve the treaty on July 7 . But none of the countries known or believed to possess nuclear weapons -- the U.S. , Russia , Britain , China , France , India , Pakistan , North Korea and Israel -- support the treaty . It opens for signatures on Sept. 20 . <p> Roche also called for a silent vigil for two hours on Parliament Hill in Ottawa to highlight demands for a nuclear weapons-free world . <p> " There are two issues in the 21st century that have to be resolved if people are going to get out of the 21st century in one piece : climate change and nuclear weapons , " Roche said . <p> The former senator first became interested in the elimination of nuclear weapons when he was a member of Parliament and visited Hiroshima . <p> " When I went to Hiroshima for the first time and saw with my own eyes what had happened , the artifacts and museums , and I talked to people , it changed my life . The fact that there could be such devastation and such inhumanity and such suffering , " Roche said . <p> An exhibition about the bombings runs until Wednesday in the rotunda of Toronto city hall . The exhibition , hosted by the Hiroshima Nagasaki Day Coalition , showcases survivors ' artwork and displays about the history of atomic bombs . <p> " They painted how they died , how they suffered , what devastation of the bombing looked like , " Shibahara , the organizer of the poster display , told the Star . <p> Posters at the exhibition provide details about atomic bombs , the damage they caused , the work that went into the two cities to revive them by both locals and those overseas , and student initiatives in Nagasaki today . <p> One exhibit reminds us of Canada 's role in the creation of atomic bombs , as Dene hunters and trappers from Deline on the western shore of Great Bear Lake were paid to carry radioactive uranium to barges , to supply the uranium for bombs in the U.S. during the Second World War . Many inhaled uranium dust and later died of cancer . <p> " The artwork by survivors of the atomic bombing is a bit graphic but very persuasive about the atrocities of atomic bombing , " Shibahara said . " It 's important to advocate about the dangers of nuclear weapons to people . That is essential to transmit our opinions to Parliament .
