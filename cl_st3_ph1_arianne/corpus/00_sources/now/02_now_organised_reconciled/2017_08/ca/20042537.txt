<p> As talks to modernize the North American Free Trade Agreement kicked off Wednesday , a lot is on the line for New Brunswick businesses trading with the United States . <p> Fifty per cent of the province 's gross domestic product is tied to exports , and New Brunswick is the most extensive trade economy in the country , said Joel Richardson , regional vice-president of Canadian Manufacturers and Exporters . <p> He said the renewed NAFTA negotiations are " absolutely fundamental " to the provincial economy . <p> ' There 's thousands upon thousands of families in New Brunswick that are relying on manufacturing and exporting in the U.S. and selling to the U.S. ' - Joel Richardson , Canadian Manufacturers and Exporters <p> " We export more to the United States and the rest of the world than anyone else does as a percentage of our overall economy , " Richardson said . <p> " There 's thousands upon thousands of families in New Brunswick that are relying on manufacturing and exporting in the U.S. and selling to the U.S. So it 's tremendously important . " <h> Reduced trade barriers <p> Foreign Affairs Minister Chrystia Freeland said this week that Canada is looking for a progressive trade deal , with stricter environmental and labour standards , as well as a focus on climate change . <p> The country also wants freer movement of professionals , and protection of its supply management system for dairy and poultry , since Canada does not have free trade in these areas . <p> Richardson said businesses in New Brunswick and Atlantic Canada want to see reduced barriers to moving goods and people between the countries , to increase jobs on both sides of the border . <p> When NAFTA was signed in 1992 , it predated a lot of industries now on the rise in the region , including the high-tech and service industries , he said . <p> " Engineering firms travel more globally now than they did back then , " he said . " We would like to look at the inclusion of service industries , of high-tech firms in NAFTA , to really grow our business in that area . " <h> ' Buy American ' a worry <p> Richardson also said it 's concerning when U.S. President Donald Trump continues to push a " Buy American " strategy and said it 's important to remember that " we build goods together with the United States . " <p> " We import a lot of raw materials , food products , agricultural products , machines and equipment from the U.S. , " he said . " We manufacture it here in New Brunswick and then we re-export it to customers in the United States . " <p> Minister of Foreign Affairs Chrystia Freeland says Canada is looking for a progressive deal in North American Free Trade Agreement renegotiations in Washington . ( Aaron P. Bernstein/Reuters ) <p> Brad Howland , president of Easy Kleen , a Sussex-based manufacturer of pressure washers , said 40 per cent of his business is now with the United States , though he also exports to 15 other countries , including China . <p> Pressure washers are used in the construction industry , oil fields , manufacturing and mining . <p> Howland said Easy Kleen is now building five special units for a U.S. submarine fleet after having " very good success with the U.S. military and not very much success with the Canadian military . " <p> He said he 's less worried about the outcome of the negotiations in Washington than about the government 's ability to negotiate on his behalf . <p> " I 'm not confident at all in their ability to negotiate , " he said . " Our politicians are n't business people . They do n't know the story . " <h> Softwood lumber <p> While Freeland remained optimistic ahead of Wednesday , she also announced earlier this week that Canada could walk away from the talks if the U.S. pushed to remove a key dispute-settlement mechanism in the trade deal . <p> The so-called Chapter 19 regulates disputes between companies over dumping and illegal subsidies , including cases such as softwood lumber . <p> Negotiations on the softwood lumber dispute between the two countries are expected to run a separate , parallel track , after the U.S. and Canada failed to settle their dispute over duties in the weeks ahead of the NAFTA talks . <p> Softwood lumber trade is not part of NAFTA and is governed by separate agreements . <p> Negotiations on the softwood lumber dispute between the U.S. and Canada are expected to run a separate , parallel track , after the two countries failed to settle their dispute in the weeks ahead of the NAFTA talks . " They meet often . They are working hard . " <p> ' We will not settle for any deal but for a good deal for Canada . ' - Jim Carr , Minister of Natural Resources <p> He also stressed that Canada wo n't settle for anything " but for a good deal for Canada . " <p> At a news conference in Shediac on Wednesday , Fran ? ois-Philippe Champagne , the minister of international trade , pointed to Canada 's free trade agreement with Europe , and said the country is also looking to Asia and South America for potential new agreements . <p> He said Canada is entering the NAFTA talks with confidence . <p> " It 's about how can we make more together , build more together , then export to the world , " he said . <p> " Our economy is very much integrated in our supply chain . Trade is part of our DNA . "
