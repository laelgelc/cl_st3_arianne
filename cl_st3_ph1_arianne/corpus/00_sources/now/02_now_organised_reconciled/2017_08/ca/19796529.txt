<h> Book celebrates farmers , foods cuisine of Africa <p> RIVER JOHN -- Newly opened rural bookstore Mabel Murple 's Book Shoppe and Dreamery will host its first book launch in August . <p> Pottersfield Press will be introducing the latest book by Nova Scotia journalist and author Joan Baxter -- Seven Grains of Paradise : A Culinary Journey in Africa . <p> Baxter , who has lived and worked for more than three decades in several countries in Africa , says she is " thrilled " to be launching her sixth book in rural Nova Scotia . <p> She describes her book as a " celebration of African foods , farms , farmers , crops , cooks and cuisines . " <p> " Africa has much to teach the world about healthy eating . Of the ten countries with the healthiest diets on earth , nine are African , some of them among the monetarily poorest nations on earth . " <p> Readers travel from the fabled city of Timbuktu on the southern edge of the Sahara Desert , to the diamond fields of Sierra Leone , from the savannah of northern Ghana to the rainforests of Central Africa . <p> " It explores the riddle of a continent that is known more for hunger than for its rich and diverse foods and cuisines , and for having discovered and bred many of the staple foods and drinks consumed daily around the world . " <p> Baxter says the book does n't shy away from the real problems of food insecurity , hunger or malnutrition brought on by conflict , poverty , unfair trade and climate change . <p> " While the book focuses on the immense potential of family farming and locally produced food in Africa , it also documents the growing risks they face , " she says . <p> Sheree Fitch , who opened the store with her husband Gilles Plante in July , says she 's pleased to host the event because Baxter is a " local writer and a friend " and believes the book has " global significance . " <p> The event runs from 7 to 9 p.m. on Aug. 4 at the bookstore in River John on Allen Road . <p> Your comment has been submitted . It will be moderated prior to its display .
