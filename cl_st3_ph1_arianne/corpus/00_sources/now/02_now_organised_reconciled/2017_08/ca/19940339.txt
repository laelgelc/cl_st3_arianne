<p> North Bay Mayor Al McDonald will lobby the province next week for greater support for employment training for abused and at-risk women . <p> McDonald says he is slated to discuss the need for stable core funding for True Self-Debwewendizwin Employment and Training with Indira Naidoo-Harris , the minister of the Status of Women , during the Association of Municipalities conference in Ottawa . <p> The mayor says future provincial funding is up in the air for employment training programs for abused and at-risk women . <p> True Self , now 10 years old , is sponsored by Nipissing First Nation , but " the doors are open to anyone , " program manager Donna Forget has stated previously . <p> Program staff have all experienced domestic violence , drug addiction or mental illness , and Forget credits the " peer-based " delivery model for the program 's success . She says 78 per cent of the most recent participants will go on to full-time work or post-secondary education . <p> The program , which operates at 239 Main St. E. in North Bay , typically graduates 25 or 30 participants annually . <p> McDonald says True Self was originally told the way in which such programs are funded was being re-evaluated and that 2016-17 would be the final year for funding . <p> But the organization was informed in December its funding would be extended for another year to March 31 , 2018 . <p> McDonald says True Self originally received $500,000 over two years . But that amount has since been cut to $225,000 annually . <p> He says the program also receives funding from the District of Nipissing Social Services Administration Board , which has been reduced to $46,784 from $60,000 in 2016/17 . <p> Calling the program a leader in the community , McDonald says True Self is needed to help " bridge the gap " between the skills required by employers and those possessed by job-seekers . <p> " We are seeing increases in reported job vacancies , " he says , noting about 480 were reported in June . " Employers are looking for individuals with skill sets they require and ca n't find the individuals they need to grow their businesses/organizations . We have individuals willing and looking to work but they do n't have the skills required to fill the positions . <p> " If we want to grow as a community and region , we must bridge this gap . " <p> A city delegation also will meet with the parliamentary assistant to the minister of environment and climate change to talk about a proposed organics ban from landfills . <p> And the city also will discuss a number of issues with Energy Minister Glenn Thibeault during next week 's conference , which runs from Aug. 13 to 16 . <p> They include the ONTC and Ring of Fire , the future of the Atlantic Power facility in North Bay , as well as a proposed community energy park plan led by North Bay Hydro that will connect Memorial Gardens and the YMCA . <p> " North Bay Hydro is seen as a leader in cogens and a leader in innovation , " says McDonald , pointing to examples such as those at the North Bay Regional Health Centre and Merrick Township landfill . <p> He says alternative fuel sources for vehicles and other applications are the future . <p> " We would like to see North Bay as a leader in these types of initiatives . There are other opportunities today in the hydrogen field and we want to assist and support local companies such as Next Hydrogen to build the infrastructure needed to foster growth in this area , to benefit from emerging technologies they develop and to create the jobs of the future . " <p> Plans for the $4.5-million electrical micro grid that will connect Memorial Gardens and the YMCA were announced in May . The province is kicking in $1 million for the project .
