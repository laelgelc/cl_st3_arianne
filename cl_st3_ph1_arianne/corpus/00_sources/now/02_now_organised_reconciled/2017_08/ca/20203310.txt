<p> The gutting and teardown has begun at a federal building in downtown Windsor , but it 's just the first stage of a $10.8-million renovation to the future home of more than 300 civil servants . <p> The bulk of the new occupants at 441 University Ave . W. , will be about 300 Canada Revenue Agency employees vacating the Paul Martin Building a few blocks away at 185 Ouellette Ave. , next spring . Also making the move is a Public Services and Procurement Canada facility manager and four wildlife enforcement employees with Environment and Climate Change Canada . <p> The office structure being rehabilitated was once home to a Canadian Forces recruitment office , and , until 2014 , a Veterans ' Affairs office . Neither department will be among the new tenants . <p> The new federal investment is great news for Windsor and the downtown , said Mayor Drew Dilkens . <p> After Ottawa announced several years ago that it was vacating the Paul Martin Building , Windsor began lobbying to make sure the hundreds of federal civil servant positions there would n't be moved out of the city . <p> " This is extremely positive -- one of the priorities we had from the beginning was maintaining that federal footprint in Windsor , " said Dilkens . <p> Jones Group work crews were onsite handling the renovation of the front facade and interior of the Government of Canada offices at 441-467 University Avenue West on Thursday.Nick Brancaccio / Windsor Star <p> According to building permits being issued by the city , the work at 441 University involves a complete gutting of the building 's interior , providing a new fa ? ade along the street front and redoing the parking lot in the rear . The building will also get a lot greener , with energy-efficient lighting and mechanical units , as well as solar panels to be installed on the roof of the two-storey structure . <p> A CRA spokesman told the Star all of its employees at the Paul Martin Building will be making the move in 2018 . <p> John Revell , Windsor 's chief building official , said the work being paid for by Ottawa is a windfall for city hall . More senior levels of government do n't need municipal approvals for any work on their own buildings , but the feds usually apply for permits and pay for them " as a courtesy , " said Revell . <p> For an " extensive " project like the current $10.8-million upgrade , he said that means about $150,000 in building permit fees flowing into municipal coffers . Revell said that 's the annual pay equivalent for 1 ? new building inspectors . <p> A community outcry followed the previous Conservative government 's decision to shut down the VA office on University Avenue in January 2014 . The Trudeau Liberals opened a new VA-dedicated office in the Chrysler tower at 1 Riverside Dr. W. , last April , with 15 staff serving nearly 2,700 area military veterans . <p> With Windsor 's economy booming -- unemployment low and housing construction and prices high -- Revell 's department has been on a hiring spree the past two years .
