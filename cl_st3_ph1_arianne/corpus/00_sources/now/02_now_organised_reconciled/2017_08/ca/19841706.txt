The water gets so deep , large fish swim in the middle of the street . <p> Set to a mournful score by Jeff Beal , the film reveals many other climate-change horrors across the planet : giant icebergs exploding and collapsing into polar seas ; streets melting and buckling in India ; drought in Syria that sparked the migrant crisis there long before civil war ; and a rising threat of tropical diseases moving into the northern hemisphere . <p> The second half of the film takes an entirely different tack , showing how a great deal of progress has been made fighting global warming . <p> We see Gore persuading reluctant politicians in India , where hundreds of coal-fired plants are being built to provide electricity for the country 's vast population , to sign on to the landmark 2015 Paris Agreement to fight climate change . ( Canada 's Prime Minister Justin Trudeau takes no persuading at all ; he and Gore embrace as if they 're old friends . ) <p> He presents hard evidence that solar power , long considered too expensive to generate on a large scale , is finally in a position to compete with and even beat the conventional fuel sources coal , oil and gas . <p> Gore boasts of having trained 10,000 people in 135 countries over the past decade to spread the word about climate change . <p> The ex-politician has become much more of a showman in the past decade but also more convincing in his drive to save the Earth from burning up . He admits to an occasional bout of despair , and warns the fight against global warming will be long and hard , but he 's convinced real change is coming . <p> Recalling Room and Dogtooth without equalling either of those unsettling achievements , Brigsby Bear is an almost unbearably twee story about pursuing a dream . <p> You need to get past the film 's disturbing premise , treated as quirky character shaping by director Dave McCary , to even crack a smile . Bedraggled and bespectacled James ( Saturday Night Live 's Kyle Mooney ) , kidnapped at birth and held hostage for 25 years by an apocalypse-fearing couple ( Mark Hamill and Jane Adams ) , is suddenly returned to the world and his family . <p> James is ill-equipped to cope -- everything from cash to sex is a mystery -- but his biggest concern is the lack of new episodes of Brigsby Bear , the bizarre sci-fantasy children 's TV series his fake dad created for a devoted audience of one : his unsuspecting prisoner . <p> With the help of accomplices that include his kid sister ( Ryan Simpkins ) , a hammy cop ( Greg Kinnear ) and an eager new pal ( Jorge Lendeborg , Jr . ) , James sets out to make a Brigsby Bear movie . <p> What do you bet on a feel-good ending ? Far less likely is relief from the unfunny absurdity and trivializing of social ills . <p> If you 've been down this road before -- this is the duo 's third cinematic travel/culinary outing -- you can expect pretty much more of the same : a light , breezy travelogue with two engaging companions . <p> If you have n't , it may be a bit of a head scratcher . Be patient . <p> This time , comics Steve Coogan and Rob Brydon are off to Spain to sample the cuisine , trade barbs and do bad celebrity impressions . It 's hard to know what is real or improvised . <p> But Coogan sees himself ( literally in one scene ) as the Don Quixote to Brydon 's less distinguished Sancho Panza . ( He mentions his two Oscar nominations -- one for producing , one for screenwriting -- at least couple of times . ) <p> Brydon comes across as more balanced and content , with a loving wife and two youngsters at home , while Coogan , in pursuit of a married woman and facing the prospect of becoming a grandfather , is kind of a jerk . <p> Both are oddly likeable though the shtick does n't always hit the mark . <p> The scenery is strikingly beautiful and the food will make your mouth water . <p> The title evokes nostalgia for a time about 20 years ago when cellphones were n't ubiquitous , Blockbuster Video was still in business and U.S. First Lady Hillary Clinton had a penchant for pantsuits . ( Some things do n't change , it seems . ) <p> Filmmaker Gillian Robespierre 's second feature film is a family dramedy that 's heavy on the heavy and a little short on humour . <p> It 's still an engaging film in many ways , starting with a literate script riddled with witty repartee . The story centres on the Jacobs family , living a seemingly idyllic life in the Big Apple despite underlying tensions . <p> Younger sister Ali ( Abby Quinn ) finds some overwrought erotic poetry on the computer and suspects dad ( John Turturro ) of having an affair . Older sister Dana ( Jenny Slate ) is happily engaged until she meets a handsome rake for her past . <p> You ca n't go wrong with fine actors like Turturro and Edie Falco as the unappreciated mom . But it 's Quinn as the feisty kid sister who shines . Slate is OK as Dana -- but a little goes a long way . <p> Two generations of funny women , Goldie Hawn and Amy Schumer , team for this mother-daughter comedy about tourists kidnapped and held for ransom while on a South American vacation . <p> Humour is the more imperiled hostage , in the lewd and lazy screenplay directed by Jonathan Levine ( 50/50 , The Wackness ) , which wanders like Billy in The Family Circus . <p> After some bluster about bad ex-boyfriends and fearful moms , Emily ( Schumer ) and Linda ( Hawn ) find themselves sipping drinks and squabbling at a resort in Ecuador . They meet a zany pair of fellow travellers , played by Wanda Sykes and Joan Cusack , who are actually pretty funny together . <p> Emily and Linda also encounter some bad hombres , led by the creepy Morgado ( Oscar Jaenada ) , who considers them easy prey for a ransom scam . Has n't he ever seen a Hollywood comedy ? <p> Hijinks ensue , cautiously . Emily and Linda actually are in peril , from dangerous dudes and the encroaching jungle .
