The picture of the area denuded of trees in the July 14 Free Press raises again whether trees , the lungs of the earth , are more important than housing developments . <p> I recall driving out Waverley Street and admiring a field of sunflowers , now replaced by houses . When will we realize that we need nature more than real estate ? True , Gem Equities Inc. can make more money building houses than growing food on their property , but breathing clean air may be needed more in the future than a new subdivision . Future generations may have to prioritize food over elaborate shelter and long drives to work . Perhaps we do n't see that resource depletion and climate change are happening . Today we seek money and comfort but , in future , I hope sustainability and survival may be more important . Is it ignorance or selfishness that leads to an exploitation of nature ? Perhaps it is both , as we continue to place money above the well-being of future citizens . Save the trees and seek a healthy and sustainable future . <p> Barry Hammond <p> Winnipeg <h> Justin , are you listening ? <p> This letter is for Prime Minister Justin Trudeau . <p> You have made a personal , public and national commitment to reconciliation with Canada 's Indigenous peoples while simultaneously demonstrating a commitment to returning Canada to the world stage . I believe you have it within your grasp to not only merge these two goals in a practical act of nation-building but , at the same time , shine a bright light on Canadian scientific , economic , military and social skills and abilities . <p> As outrageously lofty as this sounds , the vehicle for achieving this progress is the community of Churchill . <p> Churchill has been both positively and negatively involved in all these areas over many decades and now faces the consequences of prior governments ' lack of vision . Happily and somewhat unfortunately , global warming offers the community , with your support , a chance to become a centre for Canadian leadership . <p> In the 1950s through the ' 70s , Churchill was an important military and economic community . During the Cold War , it offered an open Western hand to the Soviet Union through prairie grain shipments while others rattled swords . <p> During the same period , the adjacent Joint-Forces Military Base conducted tests on the practicality of operating equipment in Arctic conditions , and the airbase ultimately was able to accommodate the largest military and non-military aircraft from around the world . <p> Successful operations in the North required new lubricants and other technologies to undergo extensive testing . A rocket base was constructed to examine the northern radiation belts and saw the testing of Winnipeg 's Bristol Aerospace Black Brant rocket as well as American military armaments such as the Nike Hercules ( launched , but not discussed ) . <p> Unfortunately , Churchill , through its railhead , was also a transportation clearing house to the south for Indigenous individuals and families as a result of imposed medical , and other less-noble , reasons . <p> Today , as a result of global warming and other high-profile political , military , scientific and economic imperatives , the northern seas and archipelagos are on the forefront of change . <p> Re-establishing Churchill as our premier northern port through a high-quality railhead would ensure a national flexibility that we do not have . A modern military airbase would offer a visible NORAD presence and a much improved search-and-rescue response capability . Churchill , by rail , air and sea could offer practical support to augment Cambridge Bay 's High Arctic Research Station -- and the reconstruction , maintenance and management , and associated economic benefits that would accrue in partnership with our Indigenous neighbours , could only help to mend old wounds . <p> This is an opportunity in co-operation with Manitoba 's provincial government to make an important national , social and economic statement . <p> John Sherbino <p> Hartington , Ont. , ( Fort Churchill , Man. , 1954-1960 ) <h> Blaming the victim <p> Re : Should have listened to gut ( Letters , July 28 ) <p> I find it appalling that the Winnipeg Free Press chose to publish John Kennedy 's letter regarding the sentencing of Jackie Healey 's attacker .
