's waters are now threatened by the lack of sea stars . <p> She says the animals serve as important predators that keep sea urchin populations in check . Sea urchins eat kelp , and , with fewer sea stars , there are also fewer kelp beds to provide habitats for many forms of ocean life . <p> " It was so common to see these big beautiful stars at the ocean bottom , big piles of them " says Hildering . " In my last 50 dives , in the last year , I 've only seen three adults . " <h> ' Zombie-like ' <p> In the Vancouver Island area , specifically , the species worst impacted is the often metre-wide , 20-armed sunflower sea star . <p> Hildering says the progression of the disease on the sunflower sea star is " horrific " and most are lucky not to see it . <p> She says as the sickness takes hold the animals begin to contort . Stressed sea stars then release their legs from their bodies as a natural reflex . <p> " This you can imagine was almost zombie-like , " says Hildering . " Seeing the legs crawl away from the sea stars ... Then you would be left with these big piles of mush . " <p> The theory of climate change warming the ocean water and causing the sickness still does n't account for baby sea stars being spotted every so often . <p> Hildering 's hypothesis , based off prior research and reports from fishermen , is , at depths where the water is colder , there are likely still adult sea stars . <p> She says that when the animals reproduce they release their sex cells into the water column which leads to infant stars growing in shallow water . As the babies start growing in the warmer shallow water , they succumb to the disease . <p> Hildering says the ocean 's health is a clear indication of global environmental problems . <p> She adds , to reverse the current sea star situation , humans need to reduce carbon emissions , and , in turn , slow the warming of the ocean .
