<p> Parched conditions have led the province to issue 40 water shortage advisories across Alberta , with some towns south of Calgary banning outdoor use . <p> Of those advisories , 17 apply to river sections in the Calgary region , including the Bow and Elbow , in which voluntary angling moratoriums in most cases have been posted due to lower water levels and higher temperatures that are stressing aquatic life . <p> But for the Sheep and Highwood rivers and Fish Creek , it goes further to include no more temporary diversion licences being accepted , while in the North Saskatchewan River Basin , that 's been extended to restrictions on licensed water withdrawals . <p> For the past week , the town of Okotoks has banned nearly all outdoor use of water due to the sharp drop in the flow of the Sheep River and soaring demand amid the relentless summer heat . <p> Bans have also been put in place in the nearby towns of Black Diamond and Turner Valley , while softer restrictions are in effect in High River . <p> " Basically , it comes in as supply and demand , and we have a supply problem that goes a long way back , " said Okotoks Coun. Tanya Thorn . <p> Lawn watering , vehicle washing , filling of pools and other uses are prohibited for the foreseeable future , she said , measures last taken a decade ago . <p> The measure has succeeded , dropping Okotoks ' water use from 15 million litres a day to 7.2 million litres , said Thorn . <p> But she said that with climate change and predictions of an increasingly dry future , more needs to be done at the provincial and municipal levels in regulating and co-ordinating water use and encouraging conservation . <p> " Water management in southern Alberta needs to be significantly looked at -- in southern Alberta , we have the largest population and the lowest amount of water , and that issue is going to increase as we go forward , " said Thorn . <p> The provincial advisories have n't gone as far as restricting licensed water withdrawals or diversions . <p> Precipitation levels this summer have lagged in southern Alberta while temperatures were consistently scorching throughout July . <p> Those conditions have also led to a recent provincial crop report predicting a 10 to 20 per cent drop in harvest production compared with the long term norm , due mainly to relentless heat . <p> For the next two weeks , Calgarians should n't see any restrictions on water use , said Frank Frigo , the city 's lead river engineer . <p> Unlike smaller centres with limited water sources , Calgary is blessed with two rivers bolstered by decent mountain snow packs from last winter , he said . <p> " We 've had a fairly healthy and late-lasting snow pack -- it 's the lack of precipitation in the past six weeks that 's been noticeable but , other than that , we 're OK , " said Frigo . <p> One concern , if the region is caught in a prolonged drought , is Calgary 's relative lack of a significant water holding body upstream , he said . <p> And he said the lack of urgency in Calgary should n't lead to complacency among residents , who should do all they can to conserve water . <p> It 's a philosophy -- achieved through lower-flow bathroom fixtures and judicious outdoor use -- that 's already succeeded in halving daily per capita use from about 500 litres in the 1970s , he added . <p> But in Okotoks , Thorn not only echoed those sentiments but added a plea . <h> Comments <p> We encourage all readers to share their views on our
