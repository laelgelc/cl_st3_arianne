<h> News Local <h> Being tested for Lyme disease <p> Mike Blackmore was cutting the long grass at his home in Thorne when he noticed what he thought was a black fly on his arm . <p> " It 's black fly season now so I just thought it was just one of those pesky critters , " he said Tuesday . <p> " It 's that time of year when the black flies are out . I was wearing pants and had them tucked in to protect my legs , but my arms were exposed . Every now and then I would see a black fly on my arm . I would just flick it off or hit it . " <p> Blackmore said one of the " flies " did n't leave his arm so easily . <p> The next morning when he woke up he noticed a bullseye around three inches in diameter on his arm . It was the same spot he had difficulty getting the fly off . <p> " I knew right then it was n't a black fly . I had been bitten by a tick , " Blackmore said . <p> " I know about ticks , but I did n't think we had them in this area . " <p> A few hours later , Blackmore said he did n't feel so good . <p> " I started to feel dizzy so I went to the hospital to get checked out . " <p> Blackmore said he was told by the doctor on duty he had an ear infection . When he mentioned that he was bitten by a tick , Blackmore was surprised by the doctor 's response . <p> " The doctor told me there are no ticks in this area . I guess some people do n't believe they are around here . But they are and people need to protect themselves . " <p> Blackmore called his family doctor in North Bay . He is scheduled to undergo some blood tests to ensure the tick was n't carrying Lyme disease . <p> The North Bay-Parry Sound District Health Unit has received more than 50 ticks for testing this year compared to 30 in all of 2016 . ? As of early August , none had tested positive for Lyme disease . <p> The health unit only accepts and tests blacklegged ticks found on humans . <p> More than 360 Ontarians were diagnosed with Lyme disease in 2016 , on top of 440 in 2015 , according to the Ministry of Health and Long-term Care . <p> The danger of catching Lyme disease is acute in the Thousand Islands region and other areas abutting the St. Lawrence River , Lake Ontario and Lake Erie , says Dr. Curtis Russell , a program consultant with Public Health Ontario . <p> Russell blames climate change for the increase in ticks . <p> He says milder winters have allowed ticks to get more of a foothold during the past 30 years . <p> People are reminded to protect themselves against ticks by conducting tick checks , especially on children and pets . <p> Wear long-sleeved shirts , long pants tucked into your socks and closed footwear such as boots . Light-coloured clothing is best so you can spot the ticks . Use insect repellent containing DEET on your clothes and exposed skin .
