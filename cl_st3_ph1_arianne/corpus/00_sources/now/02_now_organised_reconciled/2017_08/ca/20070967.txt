<p> With all the outrage in the press over Trump 's Charlottesville comments , with CEOs abandoning him left and right , with Trump disbanding his business councils and with Trump-bashing having become a national sport conveying a comically dysfunctional White House that ca n't get anything done , it 's easy to miss the fact that Trump , and especially the Trump economy , just keep on winning . <p> Unemployment is down to 4.3 per cent , the lowest in 16 years , and black unemployment is at 7.5 per cent , the lowest in 17 years . Gallup 's Job Creation Index is at record highs , its " good jobs " index is steadily climbing and it reports that consumer spending is at a nine-year high . Inflation is down to 1.6 per cent . The stock market is at record levels with US$4 trillion in new wealth having been added . In nods to the strength of the Trump economy , the Federal Reserve increased interest rates three times since his election , compared to only once in the previous eight years . Economic growth has climbed to 2.6 per cent , well above that of the anemic Obama years . <p> So how did all these economic boons happen , especially since , without the help of Congress , Trump has been a president with one hand tied behind his back ? His high-profile legislative pushes -- to repeal and replace Obamacare and to pass massive tax cuts -- have gone nowhere . The answer : One hand -- enough to wield a pen -- has been all Trump has needed to fulfill one of his most consequential campaign promises : to stymie the regulatory state . <p> From Day One , Trump has focused on ending the mad promulgation of regulations characterized by the Obama years <p> From Day One of his presidency , Trump has focused on ending the mad promulgation of regulations characteristic of the Obama years , which led the U.S. economy to bear an estimated $890 billion in compliance costs . In President Obama 's first six months in office , his executive agency actions alone led to US$24.4 billion in new costs and a paperwork burden of 6,803,249 hours ; in Trump 's first six months , he imposed but $1.2 billion in new costs and almost six million fewer hours of paperwork , equivalent to 3,000 full-time employees . <p> But Trump is n't content to merely pass a few new regulations ; he wants to reduce existing regulations by as much as 80 per cent , a process that takes time since deregulatory rulemaking requires the same comment and publication process as rulemaking . Nevertheless , he has been on a tear , breaking records by passing 14 Congressional Review Act " resolutions of disapproval " that overturned $3.7 billion in past regulations , with at least a dozen new resolutions underway representing an additional savings of $55.1 billion in costs . These include Obama rules on water and fracking and on pipelines that hobbled economic development . To date , Trump has cut 16 rules for each new one he has imposed , exceeding his campaign promise of cutting two regulations for each new one imposed . <p> But Trump is not content to merely pass few new regulations ; he wants to reduce existing regulations by as much as 80 per cent <p> Going or gone are the Clean Power Plan , the Paris Climate Accord and dozens of other climate change programs , restrictions on energy development on federal lands and offshore , onerous limits on emissions at oil drilling sites , and rules demanding uneconomic energy efficiency improvements . Going or gone , too , are restrictions on how companies can manage their own operations , such as the government micro-managing rules that forbid restaurants to set policies on how tips should be shared among employees . Coming are the Keystone and Dakota Access Pipelines along with a streamlined approval process for infrastructure projects that will slash the time needed for regulatory approvals from the current 10 to 20 years to just two . <p> The business community has found the whiff of freedom bracing . To many if not most job creators , lightening the regulatory burden is as empowering as lightening the tax load . As seen by the National Federation of Independent Business 's Index of Small Business Optimism , which soared the day after Trump 's election and remains at recent highs , 60 per cent of small businesses now report that they are hiring or trying to hire , 13 per cent hired an average of 4.5 workers , and 19 per cent report that they plan to create new jobs , a figure not seen since 1999 . Businesses report that they 're paying higher wages and that they consider this a good time to expand . <p> This is the Trump economy at the fine grain level <p> The National Association of Manufacturers echoes this sentiment , its members ' optimism during Trump 's first six months reaching 91.4 per cent , the highest two-quarter average in the survey 's 20-year history . Nearly 60 per cent plan to hire more workers in the next year and 36 per cent plan to boost capital expenditures by at least five per cent . Not only did 80 per cent state they believe Trump 's actions on regulations to be headed in the right direction , most plan to deploy the funds saved from complying with regulations by investing in their business -- in capital investment ( 63 per cent ) and/or in hiring additional workers ( 38.4 per cent ) and/or in R&amp;D ( 34 per cent ) . <p> This is the Trump economy at the fine grain level . Individual businesses know that Trump wo n't be raising their taxes and even if Congress prevents him from lowering them , under Trump the weight of regulations will ease , giving them the moxie to invest in their businesses . The dividend then accrues to the nation as a whole through economic growth . <p> This is why the economy is winning . While all in the punditry are losing their heads while rushing to signal their virtue by denouncing Trump , businesses are keeping theirs , and focusing on what counts . A one-handed Trump is all they need to see an upside in investing in their future .
