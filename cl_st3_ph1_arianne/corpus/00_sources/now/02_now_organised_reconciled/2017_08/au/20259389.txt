<p> A GROUP of super rich kids have made a pact to use their wealth to change the world . <p> Their plan bucks the rising trend of " affluenza kids " -- a generation of spoiled brats who are self-centred , disrespectful and do n't know right from wrong . <p> The term " affluenza kid " was coined when privileged Texas teenager Ethan Couch killed four people in a drunken car crash . His lawyer argued he was left without a sense of responsibility because he was pampered by his family . <p> Clinical psychologist Heather Irvine-Rundle also suggested mollycoddling could be causing the rise of " affluenza kids " . <p> " We see these kids all the time , it 's absolutely an epidemic , " she told news.com.au . <p> " Some of the children we 're starting to see have a personality in which they 're much more demanding , and you start to see what 's called histrionic personality disorder -- that is , drama queen . <p> " They 're demanding , self-centred and ( have ) a lot of emphasis on appearance , as well as extreme difficulty making relationships based on values and connections around shared senses of love and trust . " <p> But one group is proving wealthy people can be selfless rather than selfish , and have banded together to invest their money to improve the lives of others . Together , they have billions of dollars that could change the world . <p> The group is also inviting other affluent families to join their cause , The ImPact . <p> Some of the members are among the richest in the world , including heiress to the Hyatt Hotels fortune Liesel Pritzker Simmons and Jason Ingle , the great-great grandson of Henry Ford . <p> They are among many turning their family 's wealth into a good deed . <p> The ImPact is like Bill Gates 's The Giving Pledge , a commitment made by wealthy individuals to invest the majority of their wealth into helping people . <p> The ImPact encourages young family members to continue philanthropic work started by their parents and grandparents . <p> The ImPact chief executive Abigail Noble told CNN the average wealth of families signed up to the cause was $700 million . <p> Each family invests in their own philanthropic project and compare portfolios with other members of The ImPact to see how they 're tracking . <p> Liesel Pritzker Simmons is part of The ImPact network . Picture : TwitterSource:Twitter <p> AOL founder Steve Case and his wife Jean have joined The ImPact and their project is the Case Foundation , a charity invested in people and ideas that can change the world . <p> " This idea of locking arms with other members and working together even if you 're not investing in the same things I 've found to be really quite powerful , " Ms Case told CNN . <p> Even younger members of families are stepping up to manage these types of philanthropic portfolios . <p> " They reject the old way of doing things , which is just about financial return , and see almost a different way of capitalism going forward where private capital is used for public good , " she said . <p> A Bank of America survey showed 93 per cent of millennials believe social impact is key to their investing decisions . <p> Justin Rockefeller is the co-founder of The ImPact network and wants to continue the family legacy . Picture : Nicholas Hunt/Getty ImagesSource:Getty Images <p> Paolo Fresia lives in Hong Kong and started his own philanthropic project addressing the world 's <p> biggest issues . <p> " Inheriting wealth passed down through three generations is a great privilege , " he wrote on The ImPact website . <p> " However , it also comes with a responsibility to invest capital so that it can be used to tackle the world 's pressing challenges -- from climate change , to income inequality , to promoting respect for human and labour rights . " <p> Co-Founder of The ImPact , Justin Rockefeller , is the global director of family offices and foundations at software company Addepar , and a philanthropist . <p> " I am part of this amazing family , with a rich history in both capitalism and philanthropy , " he told the Financial Times . <p> " I want to build on the family legacy and the best way I know how to do that sits at the intersection of philanthropy and capitalism . Impact investing continues both family traditions , but with a new spin on it . "
