<p> The international team of researchers analysed leaves from more than 7600 species across the world . The data was teamed with new theory to create a series of equations which predict the maximum viable leaf size anywhere in the world based on the risk of daytime overheating and night-time freezing . <p> The researchers will use these findings to create more accurate vegetation models . These will be used by governments to predict how vegetation will change locally and globally under climate change , and to plan for adaptation . <p> Dr Law said it was exciting to be part of a team that has developed new theories in ecology . <p> " Not just observing patterns and changes , but really being able to explain why they happen , " she said . <p> " It 's the first step towards better predictions of the future . " <p> The study is published as the cover story in the latest edition of Science .
