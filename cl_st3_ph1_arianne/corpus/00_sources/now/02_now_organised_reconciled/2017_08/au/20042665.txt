<p> Temperatures across southern Queensland and northern NSW have been up to 13 degrees Celsius above the August maximum on a number of days this week , and while climate change may the words on everyone 's lips , it is not the whole story . <p> Coffs Harbour , on the north coast of New South Wales , reached 32.8C on Wednesday , well above its average maximum of 19.8C . <h> Key points : <p> This year has so far been warmer than average " by a long way " <p> Southern Queensland and northern NSW have this week had temperatures well above average <p> Climatologists expect extreme weather to become more common <p> It was a similar story across most of southern Queensland and northern New South Wales . <p> The last time Coffs Harbour saw a day this hot in August was in 2009 when temperatures reached 34C . <p> " So we 're not as hot as 2009 but nonetheless , it 's a warmer year than average by a long way , " Bureau of Meteorology extreme weather desk senior forecaster Scott Williams said . <p> " In a place like Coffs Harbour where the records go back to the 1940s , I could only find four daily temperatures above 30 . " <h> Why so hot ? <p> For starters there is always natural variability in temperature , or what climate scientists call " noise " -- some days are just going to be hotter than others . <p> But this does not completely explain 13C above average . <p> For that we need to look at the big drivers of the Australian climate . <p> The consensus among climate scientists the ABC spoke to is that it is hot because of what has not been happening in the atmosphere . <p> The weather across the country has been very stable in the first half of winter . <p> With few cold air intrusions to mix things up , pressure and temperatures have been able to build . <p> El Nino Southern Oscillation ( ENSO ) and another ENSO-like driver called the Indian Ocean Dipole ( IOD ) have been neutral for a while , and that means they are not to blame for the hot weather . <p> This means other climatic systems that usually sit in the back seat are getting their chance at the wheel .
