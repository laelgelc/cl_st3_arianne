<h> Los Angeles tests cooling pavement paint to beat the city heat <p> LOS Angeles is testing a novel paint treatment on roads road in a bid to reduce high temperatures in populated areas . <p> AFP <p> AFP <p> August 15 , 20171:20pm <p> Special cooling paint is being trialled on roads in a bid to reduce street-level temperatures and help tackle global warming . Picture : Paula Bronstein <p> CAN a splash of grey pavement paint help combat global warming ? <p> In Los Angeles , where summer temperatures regularly surpass 38 degrees , workers are coating streets in special grey treatments in a bid to do just that . <p> The City of Angels , home to four million people , is the first major city to test the technology . Normal black asphalt absorbs 80 to 95 per cent of sunlight , while the grey " cool pavement " reflects it -- dramatically lowering ground temperature and reducing urban street heat , advocates of the method say . <p> Densely populated urban areas including Sydney , Melbourne and Brisbane are bearing the brunt of the UHI , which occurs when natural permeable surfaces including grass , plants or bushland are replaced with concrete , asphalt and infrastructure that can trap heat . <p> Los Angeles is the first city in California to test the novel paint treatment on a public road in a bid to reduce temperatures in densely populated areas . <p> If you can fry an egg on the pavement , it 's too hot.Source:Flickr <p> " We 're hoping to inspire other cities to experiment with different ways to reduce the heat island effect , " Greg Spotts , assistant director of the city 's Bureau of Street Services said . <p> " And we 're hoping to get manufacturers to come up with some new products . " <p> " Potentially there could be a huge market for cool pavement products , and in fact , it 's part of a much larger economic trend where solutions for climate change could be the next great investments for the future , " Spotts added . <p> The city will also monitor how Angelenos react to the newfangled asphalt -- and how quickly the notoriously thick LA traffic dirties the grey coating . <p> ' RIGHT APPROACH ' <p> George Ban-Weiss , an assistant professor of Civil and Environmental Engineering at the University of Southern California , said cool pavements show promise in reducing heat , but " may have some environmental penalties . " <p> " Recent and current research is working out whether the environmental benefits of cool pavements outweigh those penalties , " Ban-Weiss told AFP . <p> Still , " the city of Los Angeles is taking the right approach and installing and assessing several cool pavement test sections before committing to widespread adoption , " he said . <p> Mr Ban-Weiss noted that heat mitigation strategies like planting trees along streets and using cool roofing materials were more " no-brainer " remedies . <p> Alan Barreca , an environmental science professor at the University of California , Los Angeles , said the pavement cooling technology could be more equitable than current methods like airconditioning . <p> " Not everyone has the resources to use air conditioning , so there 's concern that some low-income families will suffer , " he said . " That bothers me on a moral dimension . The pavement would provide benefits to everyone ... It can protect people who have to be outdoors , " he added . <p> Plus , he added , " lower temperatures -- due to the pavement -- mean less reliance on airconditioning . So , that means less greenhouse gases . "
