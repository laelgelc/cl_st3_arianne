<h> Get used to Donald Trump - he is likely to serve full term <p> US President Donald Trump is likely to serve a full term , says Matthew Goodman , an Asian economic expert at the US Centre for Strategic and International Studies Photo : Pat Scala AFR Thursday the 24th of August 2017 . Pat Scala <p> Donald Trump is likely to serve a full term as US President because the " smoking gun " that could persuade Republicans in Congress to remove him still does n't exist , a visiting expert on US politics says . <p> Matthew Goodman , Senior Adviser for Asian Economics at the Center for Strategic and International Studies in Washington , said it was possible that one would emerge from the investigations into the Trump presidency but " I think the bar is very high in our system to remove a President against his will " . <p> The question is how much damage the real estate mogul can inflict on US prestige in the three-and-a-half years he has left to serve , Mr Goodman - who served the Barack Obama and George W Bush administrations - said in an interview . <p> At one end of the spectrum is a " nuclear holocaust " caused by a meltdown in relations with North Korea 's volatile leader Kim Jong-un , which Mr Goodman said is unlikely because - despite inflammatory rhetoric - the substance of Mr Trump 's North Korea policy is " within the zone " of plausible responses to a rogue state . <p> The US has shanked its drive under Donald Trump 's leadership and will probably spend the next three and a half years hacking around in the woods before a new leader takes over Patrick Semansky <p> At the other end is a " happy ending " in which " we get a new leader in a few years and people say , ' welcome back ' , and we rejoin the TPP ( Trans-Pacific Partnership ) and we get back in the game of expanding and defending the international order , " he said . <p> He said this was a powerful metaphor and " there is some truth in it " . But he preferred a golf metaphor . <p> " We are a strong golfer but even strong golfers sometimes shank the ball into the woods , and we are going to be hacking around the woods for the next three and a half years . " <p> Having pawned its crown , the US can ; t redeem it at full value , Paul Keating says Peter Morris/Lowy Institute <p> But just as the object of golf remains to get the little white ball in the hole , the object of policy remains the advancement of national interests . <p> " The US ... will get back on the green eventually , " Mr Goodman said . <p> " You can change leaders , you can change foreign policy but you can not change national interests , and the US has a strong national interest in leading the international community on a variety of things and leading and building on the rules-based international order , and I think we will be drawn back into that . " <h> TPP <p> President Donald Trump reacts before speaking at a rally at the Phoenix Convention Centre . Alex Brandon <p> Mr Goodman said Mr Trump 's decisions to pull out of the TPP trade deal - even though it had strong provisions on issues close to his heart such as labor rights and state owned enterprises - and the Paris agreement on climate change were examples of the damage his freewheeling style could inflict on the US . <p> Trade inquiries into Chinese steel and aluminum imports to the US based on a national security law could also backfire by harming the steel industries of allies like South Korea or by encouraging adversaries like Russia to use national security as an excuse for protectionist trade policies , he said . <p> Mr Goodman said the US regain its leadership in the Asia Pacific by encouraging TPP partners , including Australia and Japan , to move ahead with the deal - hopefully leaving room for the US to return in a few years - and updating the North American Free Trade Agreement to include TPP rules already agreed by the US , Canada , and Mexico . <p> It would also help to engage constructively with other Asia Pacific Economic Co-operation leaders at their meeting in Vietnam in November , he said . <h> Tax reform ' hard ' <p> Mr Trump had n't done some of the worst things he said he would do - such as imposing huge tariffs on Chinese and Mexican imports - but his poor relations with Senate Republican leader Mitch McConnell and their failed attempt to repeal Obamacare had squandered political capital that would be needed for tax reform . <p> But deep cuts in company taxes would n't pass Congress without loopholes being abolished . These would provoke a fierce backlash , Mr Goodman said , and overcoming this would require a huge investment of political capital . <p> " That 's why people are so interested in this relationship because if it is poisoned it 's going to be very difficult to achieve a set of tax changes that are necessary . <p> " It 's going to require all hands on deck and everyone rowing together in the same direction and if they 're not it 's going to be very difficult .
