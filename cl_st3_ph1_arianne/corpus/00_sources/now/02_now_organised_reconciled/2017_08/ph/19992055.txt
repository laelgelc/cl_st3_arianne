<h> Shaping Asia 's energy future with natural gas <p> President Trump 's decision to withdraw the United States from the landmark Paris climate accord must not become a distraction from urgent global efforts to combat climate change . <p> Countries in Asia were among the most committed supporters of the Paris goals . Now is not the time to break stride , but to reinforce the resolve . <p> Energy demand is set to double this century , with the world 's population reaching 11 billion , up from 7.5 billion today . <p> ADVERTISEMENT <p> As the world changes , so will the energy system that powers it , driven by the need to reduce carbon emissions and -- crucially for Asia -- tackle air pollution that blights so many lives . <p> Put simply : we will need more and cleaner energy if Asian countries are to continue to thrive in the coming decades . <p> For Asia 's population , renewable energy will be essential to meeting growing demand while tackling climate change and air pollution . <p> But renewables chiefly produce electricity . <p> And there are parts of the economy , such as industries that produce iron , steel , cement , plastic and chemicals that can not be electrified yet -- certainly not at a reasonable cost . <p> That 's one reason why Shell 's Scenarios team believes that 75 percent of overall global energy demand will be met by traditional sources like oil , gas and nuclear in 2050 . <p> The coming decades will see a big change in the way energy is produced , used and made available to people . <p> And I see a combination of renewables , such as wind and solar , and natural gas -- the cleanest-burning hydrocarbon -- playing an increasingly important role . <p> ADVERTISEMENT <p> Natural gas is one of the few energy sources that can be used across all sectors of the global economy , including fueling transport , heating and lighting homes , and powering industries . <p> Reserves are abundant and available in many regions . The environmental benefits are also clear . <p> In power generation , for example , natural gas emits around half the carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) emissions and less than one-tenth of air pollutants compared to coal . <p> In the Philippines , domestic natural gas production fuels 20 percent of electricity generation . <p> With domestic supply forecast to decline , according to the Department of Energy , the government is introducing policies to promote LNG imports for power generation . <p> Policies of successive governments will determine the extent to which gas will play a key role in the coming decades . Their decisions must reflect the commitments made at the UN Paris climate summit . <p> Government-led mechanisms that put a price on CO2 emissions will stimulate the development of low-carbon and renewable technologies . <p> Beyond policy choices from governments , a lot will also depend on the action of energy companies . <p> Governments , companies and consumers have the power to shape a new energy future , where renewables and natural gas play critical roles . <p> Now is the time to step up the drive to deliver on the environmental pledges made in Paris .
