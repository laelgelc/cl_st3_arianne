<h> The climate shift : Warming to warning <p> Polar ice caps in Antartic are said to be formed some 33.6 years ago . Several million years ago , the Earth had undergone natural climate phase . Layers of snow piled up and compressed together forming gigantic layers of ice . <p> In Photo : Comiso <p> Scientist gradually studied patterns and sequence of weather conditions in that area . They invested much of their time generating new knowledge through numerous explorations and research . <p> Polar ice caps are home to numerous magnificent colorful creatures often times seen in television cartoons . Certainly , polar bears and penguins topped the most adorable animals in the minds of the children . Polar animals , as well as its environment , became one of the most interesting fields of study . <p> However , one of the things they found out is that the disturbance in the natural phasing of the earth 's climate can be felt mostly in the cold region . The effect of anthropogenic activities on climate became very alarming . <p> Global warming is a natural phenomenon wherein the Earth , through greenhouse gases , trap sun 's energy and radiate it in all directions . As a result , it makes the planet livable . Without these gases , the earth will be approximately 30 degrees Celsius colder . <p> Still , human-induced global warming caused by burning of coals and fossil fuels adds up to the greenhouse gases in the atmosphere , trapping more and more heat , eventually making the global temperature much warmer . <p> Due to this , melting of polar ice caps occurs . It is important to note that global warming is a " symptom " of climate change . <p> The United Nations ' Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change had discussed the changes in the behavior of the animals , for instance , the earlier breeding season for birds or forced migration of animals due to inadaptable weather condition . Animals that are not capable of adapting to the new and fluctuating weather condition are very vulnerable to extinction . <p> It is not only animals that are extremely affected by this abrupt shift in climate . This imbalance creates a lot of negative consequences for humans . One of which is the sea-level rise . Coastal countries , like the Philippines , may experience severe flooding . <p> In one of the plenary presentations at the 17th Science Council of Asia Conference , hosted by the National Research Council of the Philippines ( NRCP ) in June at the Philippine International Convention Center , Dr. Josefino C. Comiso , a senior scientist from the National Aeronautics and Space Administration-Goddard Space Flight Center , proposed several actions on how to combat climate change . <p> Research efforts vs climate shift <p> Comiso pointed out that gradual shift of energy source from coal and fossil fuel to other renewable-energy sources , like geothermal , solar , the wind and biofuel , can accelerate the recovery of the planet . <p> In the 2016 Power Situation report of the Philippines 's Department of Energy , the country still gets most of its energy supply from coal-power plants but the growing trend to shift from renewable energy is undeniable . <p> Comiso added that the popularity and potential of new technologies and innovations , like smart cars , powerful batteries , cheap solar cells , hydrogen fuel , nuclear fusion and flywheel storage system , can hasten dramatically the shift to a more sustainable future . <p> It was in 1997 when the NRCP Division of Earth and Space Sciences ( NRCP Division 12 ) started to shift its focus from mining studies to natural disasters and climate change . <p> Research programs were veered toward these fields , concentrating on mitigating and adapting to the various impacts and its long-term effects , like the continuous rise temperature , the changes in the period of wet and dry season . They include the changes in the precipitation pattern , drought , heat waves , intense typhoon and the sea-level rise and tremendous flooding . <p> Since then , the members of NRCP Division 12 who are acclaimed geologists , geophysicists and meteorologists committed to contributing through various research undertakings in the resolution of the climate change-related problems to protect the lives of the Filipino people and prevent the continuous deterioration of the country 's environment . <p> Restoration and resilience strategies <p> Comiso also proposed restoration and resilience strategies , which are not very new but still applicable these days . <p> Planting trees , for one , has countless benefits for the environment . This reduces flooding and landslide , as well as it sequesters carbon and conserving soil . <p> A single mature tree can absorb carbon dioxide at a rate of 48 pounds a year and release enough oxygen back into the atmosphere to support two human beings , according to Mike McAliney in " Arguments for Land Conservation : Documentation and Information Sources for Land Resources Protection , Trust for Public Land " in Sacramento , California , in December 1993 . <p> Trees are also considered as natural air conditioners . They help stabilize the temperature , making the surroundings much cooler by as simple as blocking direct sunlight to a more sophisticated chemical process of cooling . <p> This is the most basic activity people can do to help solve the problem of climate change . People engaging in this activity as young as possible will help them appreciate nature more and understand the balance and processes of the Earth . In addition , there are a lot of health benefits in joining tree-planting activities . <p> Reforestation would also lead to the protection of biological diversity . Rehabilitating the natural habitat of plants , animals and microorganism reduces the harmful effects of climate change to them . It is important to ponder on that by simply veering away from the people-centric mind-set and considering that people share the same Earth with the other living creatures will really make a difference . <p> Human activities , good or bad , create a ripple effect , natural cycles and processes keep the balance .
