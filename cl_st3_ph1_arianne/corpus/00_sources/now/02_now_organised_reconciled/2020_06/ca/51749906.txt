<p> Stephen Poloz was a busy man during his last months as governor of the Bank of Canada . Oil prices tanked , interest rates were cut and the country was plunged into a deep recession as the COVID-19 pandemic swept the planet . <p> If that were n't enough to keep the governor occupied , there was his quest for some lucrative post-retirement work . Happily for the former governor , he scored big . <p> Within 48 hours of stepping away from his seven-year tenure as head of Canada 's central bank , in early June , Poloz was appointed a director of Enbridge Inc. -- a pipeline company that 's one of Canada 's most important players in the energy field . Then , on June 9 , CGI Inc. -- the Montreal-based technology firm -- announced that Poloz would also be joining its board . <p> According to reports , Poloz will be paid a combined $607,000-per-year for the two part-time board positions , though they could be reduced due to pandemic-related pay cuts . <p> The bank and Enbridge both insist that all the rules were followed and that the appointments were vetted and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ doubt the lawyers did their jobs , and everybody followed the rules -- lax as they are -- but I still believe the whole thing stinks . <p> I ca n't think of a more important job in the country than being head of the central bank . It 's a position that requires a deep understanding of monetary policy , an ability to follow global economic trends , deft management skills and an ability to work closely with key shapers of economic policy in government and the private sector . <p> For that , the governor is well remunerated -- perhaps not at the stratospheric pay levels of chartered bank executives , but as much as $544,800 a year . That 's more than the prime minister gets . And the governor is provided with a guaranteed , inflation-indexed pension for life . <p> That should be enough to assure us that the governor of the Bank of Canada avoids being involved in a personal , private-sector external job search while being in charge of the nation 's monetary policy . Enbridge was more forthcoming . " Discussions with Mr. Poloz began several months ago , " the company stated . Earlier , an Enbridge spokesperson told another journalist that " director recruitment occurs over a long period of time , often more than a year . " <p> And what was happening during that period ? Well , last November , the bank issued a report listing its research priorities related to the threat of climate change -- noting that carbon-intensive sectors like oil and gas could be negatively affected by significant changes in pricing that could occur in a transition away from a carbon economy . I 'm sure Enbridge took note . Were they already in talks with Poloz ? Who knows ? In any case , Poloz has long been seen as a friend of the oil patch , which is thrilled with appointment . <p> Since the pandemic hit , the bank has been forced to undertake extraordinary measures to backstop the economy @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in April . Eligible issuers are companies based in Canada that meet a series of criteria . Guess who 's on the list ? Enbridge . CGI is not . <p> The bank said that it has begun to buy corporate bonds but is not involved in day-to-day management of the purchase program . Assets under the program are handled by TD Asset Management , and the bank does n't intend to publish the names of individual issuers and securities . <p> While there is no indication of any favouritism towards Enbridge , why should the question even have to be posed ? The whole point of conflict of interest rules is not just to avoid real conflicts , but also the appearance of conflicts -- to make sure there is complete transparency , to reassure us that the public interest is above all individual , personal and corporate interests . <p> This unseemly kind of transition has happened before . In early 2015 , John Baird quit as a cabinet minister in the Harper government , and then as an MP . Within weeks , he had joined Barrick @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ lobbied him as foreign minister . Then Baird , a former transport minister , joined the board of Canadian Pacific Ltd . All was within the rules , though the timing of the appointments led tongues to wag . <p> Canada 's ethics regime needs to be tightened to make sure senior officials and politicians do n't conduct personal job searches while in positions of public trust . Period . <p> Even without tighter rules , it was all so avoidable for Poloz . It 's so easy to just say , " Sorry , I ca n't discuss this now . Call me in six months when I 'm no longer at the bank . " Or " I 'm flattered but I have my hands full with my current job . And it would look bad if it were known I was having these discussions . " <p> All Poloz had to do was look to past experience . One of his predecessors , David Dodge , left the bank after a seven-year term in February 2008 . Three months later , he was appointed chancellor of Queen @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ career in public service . Then in July , he joined the board of Canadian Utilities Ltd . In September 2008 , he became an advisor to Bennett Jones , the Calgary-based law firm . Well-timed . Elegant . <p> It 's unfortunate Stephen Poloz did n't take that example .
