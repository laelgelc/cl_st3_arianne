Canada will continue playing a leadership role in galvanizing investment and support from new actors , like the World Bank , to unlock support for much needed services for refugees in critical areas like health and education . <p> Second , refugees would be fully accepted as part of societies , everywhere . At a time when many countries are facing difficult economic situations , we risk seeing an increase in anti-refugee/migrant narratives : for example that refugees are a burden to taxpayers , that they steal jobs from locals , or that there are too many non-white refugees , etc . <p> No society , including Canada , is immune to this rhetoric . That 's why it 's more important than ever to ensure the debate around refugees remains positive and factual . We are grateful for this dedication and the sacrifice they have made to support their new home . <p> In developing countries , a " new normal " would mean refugees are no longer dependent on external aid . Refugees take pride in their ability to provide for their families , and many -- often women -- open small businesses . We must continue and increase programs that build the entrepreneurial skills of refugee women , a fundamental step in changing the narrative on refugees from being burdens to valuable members of communities . <p> Third , when they can not return to their homes or find safety in a neighbouring country , refugees would be able to rebuild their lives elsewhere .
