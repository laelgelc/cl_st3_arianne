<p> Viruses are naturally occurring entities . Viruses have existed on Earth long before humans and vastly outnumber humans . There are more viruses on Earth than there are stars in the universe or cells in the human body . <p> As a cellular microbiologist who has studied the origin and development of infectious diseases and their prevention , understanding where infectious agents come from is as important , if not more important , to understanding how to combat the rampant spread of diseases within the human population . <p> Fortunately , only a small fraction -- about 200 -- of this vast array of viruses can infect humans . Some of the better-known human viral infections include measles , varicella , polio , human papilloma virus , influenza and rhinoviruses , which are typically responsible for the common cold . <p> An even smaller number of viruses are responsible for the deadliest human infections that we have experienced . Population growth , global travel and climate change that cause the disruption of habitats further provide opportunities for cross-species transfer . <p> Cross-species transfer <p> Many of the viruses that have affected us over the past 20 years have emerged from non-human reservoirs . Reservoirs are the source of viruses and other pathogens and can be located in animal populations or the natural environment . What is of note is that the original reservoir for many of these viruses are bats . <p> Bats have been shown to be the natural reservoir of numerous deadly human viruses . The specialized immune systems of bats allow multiple different types of viruses to persist within these hosts .
