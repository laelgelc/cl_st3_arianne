<h> Driving a Virus Away <h> Can cars be a weapon in the fight against COVID-19 ? <p> American culture has long been based on mobility and independence . In the early days , we relied on boats and horses , but cars quickly took center stage . We have built a massive interstate highway system , talk with joy about the " new car smell , " and romanticize road trips . Many of us name our vehicles and selling an older car can feel like we are giving up on a friend . <p> Of course , the same automobiles cause pollution , contribute to climate change , cause thousands of fatalities each year and generate traffic jams and road rage . Europeans have questioned our car-centered lifestyle claiming that it reduces our connection to others , and the environment . But we seem singularly unwilling to give up the freedom they represent . <p> Many Americans literally spend hours a day in their cars , commuting , driving children to and from activities and running errands . We locate shopping malls in the midst of huge parking lots , and can eat , drop off mail , and pick up dry cleaning without ever leaving our cars . While this unwillingness to leave the comfort of our vehicles could be construed as lazy , it is also an efficient means of completing errands in a culture which places high value on productivity . Despite the cost of maintaining and driving a car many Americans ca n't imagine being without one . <p> Are we just being selfish when we refuse to alter our dependence on cars , or is there another reason ? The truth is that decision making involves cost benefit analyses , whether we are conscious of it or not . So , what accounts for our connection to our cars ? In a busy , connected worlds , cars give us a space of our own . They are climate controlled and self-contained . We personalize their colors and accessories and control what we listen to while we are driving . We sleep in them , sing while driving , advertise our social @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ with bumper stickers and even messages painted on the windows . <p> Maybe without realizing it we have been retreating into our own little rolling " Mental Health Pods " for years . Many commuters say they resist carpooling because they feel they need the time in their car to switch gears from home to work , or to decompress before getting home . Driving alone can afford people the time to think about issues and solve problems . Parents often report that teens find it easier to talk about difficult subjects in the car , in part because they do n't have to make eye contact . Changing clothes in the back of the minivan between after school activities is a suburban rite of passage for many school age students . Bereaved individuals sometimes sit in their car and cry , so they wo n't upset the people around them . <p> In the age of COVID-19 cars have also become our own little Self-isolation units . Many cities have created drive through testing sites , so patients do n't even have to get out of their car . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cars queuing up to pick up staples and banks and pharmacies that already had drive-through facilities have enabled people to conduct business at a social distance . Other retailers have followed suit . It is now possible to place online orders for everything from groceries , to take-out food , to clothing and then pick up your items curbside without ever entering the business . Doctors , Veterinarians , Hair Stylists and other offices that provide in person services have moved their waiting rooms into the parking lot . Checking in by phone when you arrive and staying in your car until you are summoned by text message has rapidly become a new normal . <p> Certainly , cars have also served as a refuge from real life in other eras . From family Sunday drives , to teenage drive-ins we have used our cars to carve out space and time from our routines . However , during this viral lockdown people are also using their vehicles to escape crowded apartments , or family homes packed with people who are working or studying . In my private practice , I @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and homeschool children consider leaving someone else in charge for an hour while she takes her lunch and drives somewhere else to eat , relax and regroup . When we work in an office , we often take a break to eat somewhere else so why not do the same thing in reverse ? <p> This is not to say that everyone falls prey to the lure of the automobile . Research suggests that teens today are waiting longer to get a driver 's license and in densely packed cities people may choose to give up cars and turn instead to public transportation , motorcycles or even bikes and scooters . But for many of us a car is the first big purchase we make , driving is the last vestige of independence as we get older and more infirm , and our car is a place where we can truly be ourselves . Our vehicles have also become a key form of personal protection , from an invisible virus .
