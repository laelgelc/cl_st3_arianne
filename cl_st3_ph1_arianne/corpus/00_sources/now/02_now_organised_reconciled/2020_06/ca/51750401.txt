<h> You can make a difference . <p> June is called National Indigenous History Month , and claims World Oceans Day , but it also marks the 10th anniversary of the federal designation of the Gwaii Haanas National Marine Conservation Area Reserve . <p> Longtime stewards of this area , at the southernmost part of Haida Gwaii , the Haida Nation has been watching over Gwaii Haanas for generations . The islands and marine waters within this protected area are teeming with ocean life -- from breaching whales to wave-riding dolphins and undersea kelp forests . Visitors from around the world are inspired by the region 's natural beauty and the rich presence and history of the Haida . <p> The Gwaii Haanas National Marine Conservation Area Reserve and Haida Heritage Site is not just an ecological hot spot . It is also a prominent example of Indigenous-led , co-operatively managed marine protected areas ( or MPAs ) in Canada . <p> Leading by example for the past 35 years , the Haida have changed how we approach co-governance , conservation and sustainability . In 1993 , the Canadian government and the Haida Nation signed the Gwaii Haanas Agreement to co-operatively govern the terrestrial portion of Gwaii Haanas as a national park reserve , and the Gwaii Haanas Marine Agreement followed in 2010 . <p> The two nations now co-operatively manage Gwaii Haanas through the Archipelago Management Board ( AMB ) , established under the two Gwaii Haanas agreements . This governance structure is unique in the equal decision-making power it grants to Canada and the Council of the Haida Nation , and its grounding in both Canadian and Haida law .
