<h> Hundreds of fish dead after liquid manure spill near Cousins Pond in P.E.I. <p> Likely more than 500 fish , all of the brook trout like the one pictured here , perished in a fish kill near Cousins Pond in P.E.I. - SaltWire file photo <p> More than 500 fish are dead after liquid manure entered streams near Cousins Pond earlier this week . <p> Provincial conservation and environment officers responded to the report of a major liquid manure spill the evening of June 3 . <p> The spill impacted approximately four kilometres of streams between Irishtown and the North Shore at Cousins Pond . <p> Officers constructed silt fences and a series of three dams to stop the flow of manure into the water . By 7 p.m. they had stopped the concentrated manure from entering the stream . <p> " It 's pretty fair to say that any time you have over 500 dead fish or or other wildlife , it 's quite concerning , " said Brad Potter , Manager of the Fish and Wildlife section at the Department of Environment , Water and Climate Change . <p> " It 's certainly concerning to the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ larger and to Islanders in general . I mean , this is not what I would consider an every day occurrence . " <p> The fish , all of them brook trout in various life stages , were collected as legal evidence with the help of the staff at the Kensington North Watersheds Association . The fish will be sent for analysis to determine cause of death . <p> " Some of these fish were very , very small , " said Potter , adding it 's likely more than 500 fish fell victim to the spill . <p> " As for the longer-term impact on other species , that 's not something we currently know , " he said . " These are complicated situations , and this one is under active investigation from the enforcement group . " <p> Clean up efforts continue and provincial biologists will return to the site later in the season to determine the long-term impacts of the spill . <p> " This is certainly unique in timing , because it 's the first part of June , " said Potter .
