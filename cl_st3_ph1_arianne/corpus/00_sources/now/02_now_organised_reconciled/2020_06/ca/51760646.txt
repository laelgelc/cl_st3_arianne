<p> After more than four years of campaigning , Canada has fallen short in its bid to win a seat on the United Nation 's Security Council , failing to secure the necessary two-thirds support in a unique round of physically distant voting on Wednesday . <p> Canada won only 108 votes from the member-states of the UN General Assembly , while Norway took 130 and Ireland grabbed 128 . The three countries were competing for two seats in the Western Europe and other states group . Norway and Ireland will hold the seats for the 2021-22 term . <p> There was some speculation that members would need a second round of voting to decide the race , but Ireland secured just enough votes to prevent a runoff with Canada . <p> The results on Wednesday mark the second consecutive time that Canada has lost a bid to sit on the UN 's decision-making body , and comes after a high-profile campaign from the Trudeau government to win the spot . <p> Canada also failed to win a seat 2010 , under the former Conservative government , falling short of the support gleaned by Germany and Portugal . In other years , all delegates would show up to UN headquarters in New York City and vote in person , with each round of voting taking a couple minutes . <p> But this year , because of the pandemic , delegates had designated times to arrive , drawing out the length of the voting process .
