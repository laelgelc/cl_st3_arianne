<h> Climate-justice stories in every community , waiting to be told <p> " EVERY CITY HAS ITS GEORGE FLOYD , " Rev. Jesse Jackson said after attending Floyd 's June 4 funeral service in Minneapolis . No stranger to white supremacist violence , Jackson was 26 when he witnessed the assassination of civil rights leader Dr. Martin Luther King on a motel balcony in Memphis . The murder of Floyd , Jackson added , " photographed by a terrified and courageous 17-year-old girl , has made it impossible to ignore police violence against Black people . " <p> Indeed , the media has taken notice . But police brutality is not the only violence Black Americans and other communities of color must endure . It is factually indisputable that the poor and people of color in the US and around the world suffer first and worst from climate-driven catastrophes such as Hurricane Katrina , whose fifteenth anniversary comes in August . The same is true of standard air and water pollution , such as the lead-laden drinking water provided to residents in Flint , Michigan , the majority of whom are Black .
