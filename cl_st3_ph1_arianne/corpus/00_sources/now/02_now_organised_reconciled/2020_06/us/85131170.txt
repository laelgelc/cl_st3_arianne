<h> Climate change brings fires , floods and moths to Siberia <p> by Marina Lapenkova <p> Russia 's Siberia region , best known as an icy tundra , is being transformed by climate change <p> Best known as a vast , cold tundra , Russia 's sprawling Siberia region is being transformed by climate change that has brought with it warmer temperatures , forest fires and growing swarms of hungry moth larvae . <p> Spanning millions of square kilometres east of the Urals to the Pacific Ocean , the area has been particularly hard hit this year by extreme weather , which scientists say is the result of global warming . <p> Photographs of wild flower fields in local media last month were a rare site so early in the year in the normally chilly region -- and ice cream sales were up 30 percent . <p> " This winter was the hottest in Siberia since records began 130 years ago , " said Marina Makarova , the chief meteorologist at Russia 's Rosgidromet weather service . <p> " Average temperatures were up to six degrees centigrade higher than the seasonal norms .
