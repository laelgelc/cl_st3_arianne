Wages for those who can find work may be depressed and inconsistent . <p> As distressing as this sounds , we have been here before and there are solutions . In 1930s , our nation faced extraordinary unemployment and a structural breakdown of our economy . Despite the bleak outlook , through bold action and leadership , the country rallied to get people back to work and rebuild the economy . In addition to market reforms and other strategies , the 1930s New Deal devised the Work Projects Administration . <p> In the seven years prior to full reemployment as a result of World War II , the WPA pumped billions into the economy , employed over 8 million , and focused on widespread infrastructure improvements , including roads , bridges , utility systems and buildings of all description across the country . It is estimated that WPA efforts resulted in 40,000 new buildings and improvements to 85,000 then-existing buildings , many of which are reportedly still in use today . Creating a climate adaptation " new deal " could be a pathway towards economic dignity for the millions unemployed who we must transition from reemployment relief to sustainable jobs . We know climate change realities require substantial investments in adaptation infrastructure and technology , the re-training of displaced workers and further conversion to renewable energies . At the same time , we could invest to stabilize our communities through affordable housing , meaningful community health programs , deployment of free and rapid wi-fi infrastructure to provide connectivity to all communities and hiring new and more teachers . <p> To achieve the goals of climate adaptation and a true economic recovery , we need our leaders to be bold and visionary . We must invest smartly in our collective and sustainable future by investing in infrastructure and technology , re-tooling industries and re-training our work force . The question is , do we have the leadership , courage and determination to get there ?
