Each time the administration considers an international body to be an obstacle to the president 's short-term political whims , it simply walks away . And while the pattern is predictable , it has never been more dangerous . <p> Global challenges , including pandemics , require coordinated global responses . No individual nation or leader can overcome them alone . This is precisely why the WHO and the United Nations were created in the first place . They are far from perfect , but trying to go at it alone is not only costly and inefficient . It is also doomed to failure . <p> The United States traditionally understood this better than most and set a precedent as an international leader that galvanized international coalitions to advance common interests instead of undermining them . Even today , this administration tries to have it both ways , touting its global response to the novel coronavirus while simultaneously attacking the institutions that make an effective global response possible . More fundamentally , in this already fragile moment for global public health , it makes no sense to undermine the actors working in places where nation-states can not fill the void . President Trump claims to operate under the banner of " America First , " but his strategy will ultimately hurt Americans . Daunting transborder issues such as COVID-19 and climate change necessitate cooperation and foresight , two traits this administration seems incapable of embodying . <p> There is no doubt that UN institutions have their faults , but they are only as good as the effort put into them by key member-states -- including the United States .
