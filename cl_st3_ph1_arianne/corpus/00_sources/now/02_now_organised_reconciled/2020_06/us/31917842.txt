( Courtesy of the Hermitage ) <p> The Hermitage is bordered on three sides by the Lafayette River , and North Shore Road , which leads to the museum , floods regularly . Gardens on the east and west of the property are also often inundated , and the grounds have lost several acres to the water since it opened in 1937 , said Jennifer Lucy , marketing and design manager for the Hermitage . <p> The installations are scattered inside and on the grounds and range from the satirical to the serious . <p> One artist team created the photo series " One Foot Under " to show life in which people have adjusted to living with one-foot of water everywhere . One photo shows children wearing floaties and carrying inflatables along with their books as they board @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ with water and fish swirling about their legs and barnacles growing on the legs of their dining table . <p> An interactive installation , " Linked , " is a series of 10 rain chains between 30 and 70 feet long that are hung along a wooded path in front of the museum . Rain chains are often strung along roofs to move water from gutters . With " Linked , " each chain consists of colored lights , acrylic cups and bells . Visitors will go into " carbon footprint stations " that are located at each end of the path . Visitors can use their phones to connect to a software program and answer questions to measure their personal impact on the environment , such as how often they drive . Their answers will be calculated and they will be visually represented on the first chain . That person 's " footprint " will move to the next chain as other visitors coming behind them answer the same questions .
