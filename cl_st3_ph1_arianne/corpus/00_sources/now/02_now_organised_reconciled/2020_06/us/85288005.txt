<h> Viewpoint : We must challenge environmental racism , too <p> The Black Lives Matter movement has made great strides in bringing to light the racism embedded within our criminal justice system . Moved by horrific images of police brutality , the public and political leaders can no longer deny the problem , and real policy reform is underway . <p> Yet institutionalized racism extends well beyond the criminal justice system . One far less visible manifestation is the suffering black communities experience in the form of environmental injustice . <p> Black people are far more likely to live in areas of poor air quality . They are also disproportionately exposed to toxins associated with power plants , industrial production , and waste disposal . This inequality is true even when we control for income , evidence that this is not just a matter of economic status . It is environmental racism , plain and simple . <p> The consequences are profound . In 2019 , 235 black people were killed by police . At the same time , well over 13,000 black people died prematurely due to air pollution alone . As with criminal justice reform advocates , these activists need the support of political leaders and policy makers to address immediate health threats . But even broader environmental challenges , like climate change , have racial implications . As we saw in New Orleans during and after Hurricane Katrina , people of color are more likely to experience the effects of climate change due to the more vulnerable areas in which they live , while also being less able to recover after disaster hits . Unless action is taken , we will see similar racial disparities as climate change worsens . <p> Criminal justice reform is desperately needed . But we must recognize that racism is embedded in every major institution . Environmental racism is truly a killer . We must stand with advocates of criminal justice reform , but we must not stop there .
