<h> The Golden State has the highest gas tax in the nation , and one of its worst-performing highway systems . <p> In the middle of a severe recession , the California state government is doing what it does best : raising taxes . <p> On July 1 , the state 's motor fuel excise tax will rise by 3.2 cents to 50.5 cents a gallon . That heavily regressive levy secures the Golden State 's status as the nation 's top taxer of gasoline . This coming increase -- the third in four years , according to the Los Angeles Times -- is the first time the tax will go up as the result of an automatic inflation-adjustment mechanism added in 2017 . <p> That law requires that every July , beginning this year , the state 's gas tax rate be adjusted to match any change in overall consumer prices , as measured by the California Consumer Price Index ( CPI ) . <p> Inflation rates have plummeted around the globe during the pandemic , and California is no exception . Because prices went up about 6 percent over that time , the gas tax is set to increase by the same amount . <p> That 's prompted a group of Republican legislators to propose suspending the gas tax increase . <p> " Unemployment continues to rise and all the ways California was unaffordable prior to the pandemic still exist -- suspending the gas tax increase is the least that could be done , " Assemblymember Marie Waldron ( R -- Escondido ) told the Los Angeles Times . <p> Democrats have dismissed the idea of delaying the tax increase , arguing that the state needs all the money it can @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ falling across the board . <p> Twenty-two states and the District of Columbia index their gas taxes to inflation .
