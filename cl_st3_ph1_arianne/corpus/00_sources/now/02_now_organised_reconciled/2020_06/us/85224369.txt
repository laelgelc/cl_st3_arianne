<p> A federal judge on Saturday approved Pacific Gas &amp; Electric 's plan to exit bankruptcy , a crucial step in efforts to overhaul a utility whose equipment was involved in some of the worst wildfires to ravage California in recent years . <p> The approval will allow PG&amp;E to take part in a $20 billion state fund to help cover liabilities from future wildfires started by the company 's transmission lines and other equipment . <p> But even with that financial shield , reorganized finances and new management , PG&amp;E will face daunting challenges . The company 's operations stretch across a 70,000-square-mile service area that appears increasingly vulnerable to wildfires because of climate change . And it is not clear whether the company , which has been repeatedly cited for negligence , is up to the enormous task of making its transmission system safer . <p> But ending the bankruptcy process would at least clear the way for payments to victims of the wildfires .
