While leaves may look green and healthy , farmers can face nasty surprises when they go to harvest their crops . <p> This also poses problems for plant breeders , who have to wait months or years before knowing how crops respond to drought or temperature changes . Not knowing what nutrients or growing conditions the crop needs early on also hinder crop productivity . <p> New research using machine learning and to help predict root growth and health with aboveground imagery was published June 14 in Plant Methods . <p> " One of the great mysteries for plant breeders is whether what is happening above the ground is the same as what 's happening below , " said Michael Selvaraj , a co-author from Alliance of Bioversity International and the CIAT . <p> " That poses a big problem for all @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ canopy , height , other physical features that take a lot of time and energy , and multiple trials , to capture what is really going on beneath the ground and how healthy the crop really is , " said Selvaraj , a crop physiologist . <p> While drones are getting cheaper , and hardware for capturing physical images through crop trials has become easier , a major bottleneck has been in analyzing vast quantities of visual information . This shows scientists exactly how plants are responding to stimuli in the field in real-time . <p> Using the technology , breeders can now respond immediately , applying fertilizer if a particular nutrient is lacking , or water .
