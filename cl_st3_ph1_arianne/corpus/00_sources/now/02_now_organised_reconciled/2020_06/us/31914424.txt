This question has haunted alien hunters ever since the Nobel-winning physicist Enrico Fermi posed it to some colleagues over lunch 70 years ago . There are billions of sun-like stars in our galaxy , and we now know that most of them host planets . But after decades of searching , astronomers have n't found any that appear to host life . This is the so-called Fermi paradox : Our galaxy seems like it should be teeming with alien civilizations , but we ca n't find a single one . <p> Researchers working on the search for extraterrestrial intelligence , or SETI , have proposed a number of solutions to the Fermi paradox over the years . But the most persuasive answer is also the most obvious : Perhaps intelligent life is just far more rare than we thought . <p> How rare ?
