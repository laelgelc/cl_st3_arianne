To that end , I @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ literal blurring of reality -- but executed within the parameters of a single photographic image . To achieve this , I used a 30-second shutter -- the camera 's max -- and held a covering ( drink coaster made of leather ) to the front of the lens . I 'd lift it for a period of time that seemed appropriate for the lighting conditions , then cover it again , reframe the camera , lift again , etc. until my 30 seconds were up . I often combined these coaster lifts with movement of the camera itself in order to " draw " light through the frame . Results were always highly unpredictable . <p> Additionally , I focused on details , using a macro lens to explore the minute miracles of life all around us . Examining the forces of nature inches from one 's face is the same experience in remote forests and grassy backyards alike . While some of these images were made in solitude miles from other people , others were made in my neighborhood park among the socially distanced masses .
