<p> June 4 ( UPI ) -- Mangroves provide hundreds of millions of dollars in protections against coastal storms and flooding every year , but they could soon be gone if carbon emissions are n't significantly curbed in the coming decades . <p> New research suggests that if CO2 emissions are n't drastically cut by 2050 , mangroves are unlikely to survive the sea level rise that 's expected to accompany a warming climate . <p> Advertisement <p> For the new study , published online Thursday in the journal Science , researchers analyzed sediment layers from the end of Earth 's last deglaciation period , 10,000 years ago . Based on the analysis , scientists ran simulations to estimate the effects of sea level rise on mangroves under two scenarios , low and high carbon emissions . <p> The models showed that under a high carbon scenario , featuring 6 millimeters of sea level rise per year , mangroves would be highly unlikely to grow fast enough to keep up with rising seas .
