I 'm going to try to cut through the noise to help discerning readers looking for objective information . <p> Here are some of the more common comparisons made on climate change and the coronavirus over the last few months and corresponding reality checks . <p> Comparison : Pandemics and climate change are bothmassive risks that much of the world is ignoring or downplaying . <p> Reality check : True . <p> They 're both gray rhino risks . As I wrote in this column early in the pandemic , a gray rhino is a metaphor coined by risk expert Michele Wucker to describe " highly obvious , highly probable , but still neglected " dangers , as opposed to unforeseeable or highly improbable risks -- the kind in the black swan metaphor . <p> Comparison : They 're both existential crises of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> The pandemic will define our generation uniquely , while climate change will wear on for many . <p> The word " crisis " implies a finite beginning and end , which certainly fits the bill of the pandemic . At some point , just like past pandemics , this coronavirus will likely recede , become normalized or be resolved with a vaccine . <p> I do n't use the word crisis to describe climate change because humanity is going to be living with impacts of a warming world indefinitely even if we do drastically reduce heat-trapping emissions . It does n't have finite parameters that typically define crises . <p> It 's like saying a cheetah is a turtle , only faster . Yes , they 're both animals , but otherwise they have inherent differences that means the turtle will never be faster than the cheetah . <p> Climate change is , by definition , a slow-moving , centuries-long problem whose impact on the world is uneven and secondary .
