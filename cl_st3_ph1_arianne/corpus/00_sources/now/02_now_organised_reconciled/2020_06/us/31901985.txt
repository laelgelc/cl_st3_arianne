Fertilizers and other nutrient-rich waste seep into ditches , streams and rivers that eventually empty into the Gulf . <p> The equivalent of 3,100 standard-sized shipping containers per year of nitrogen washes off Midwest cropland into the Mississippi and Atchafalaya rivers , according to the report . <p> Excess nitrogen causes algae overgrowth and the depletion of oxygen that can kill , weaken or displace marine life . <p> Use of fertilizers has grown as prices have fallen . They can increase water-holding capacity , diversify crops , plant cover crops and implement other practices that stabilize soil and retain nutrients . <p> But farmers need financial and technical support to change the way they operate , the report says . The UCS recommends a variety of federal policy changes , including crop insurance policy incentives for healthier , less fertilizer-dependent soil and the expansion of the U.S. Department of Agriculture 's farm stewardship programs . It also says Iowa , Illinois and other Midwest states that are major contributors of agricultural pollution should offer financial incentives to farmers that cleanup local waters and improve soil health .
