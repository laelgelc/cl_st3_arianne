<h> Air conditioner bumps the electric bill by 42% , increasing the risk of energy poverty <p> A new study by Ca ' Foscari and CMCC combines OECD and NASA datasets for 8 countries to show that the share of households ' spending dedicated to cooling is greater to what estimated in previous studies . Credit : Gaia Squarci <p> A new study published in Economic Modelling by researchers at Ca ' Foscari University and CMCC shows that owning and using an air conditioner greatly increases the electricity bills of households , with important implications for the energy poverty of the less well-off . <p> Previous studies , mainly focused on the US , estimated an increase of household spending for electricity bills of about 11% . This new study , analyzing the socioeconomic characteristics of households in eight other OECD countries ( Australia , Canada , France , Japan , the Netherlands , Spain , Sweden , and Switzerland ) and climate data from a NASA dataset finds that on average , using an AC increases electricity bills by 42% with respect to the households that do not have an AC unit . <p> The actual increases will @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ households will have to face in the future . That additional spending is thus a new factor influencing the energy poverty of the poorest households , a situation arising when the families spend more than 5% of their annual income on electricity . <p> According to BPIE , in 2014 , the population affected by fuel poverty in Europe ranged from 10% to 15% , depending on the member state . This new study shows a more worrying situation . <p> " The concept of energy poverty is usually related to ensuring adequate heating during the coldest months , " explains Enrica De Cian , professor of environmental economics at Ca ' Foscari and leader of the Energya team which drafted the study . " Our data , however , indicate that we should widen the concept to include the increasing role of cooling during the summer months . Poorest households already spend a consistent share of their budget for basic goods such as food and electricity .
