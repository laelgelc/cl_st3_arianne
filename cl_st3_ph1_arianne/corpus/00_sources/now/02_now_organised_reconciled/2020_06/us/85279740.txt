It ignores political boundaries of all kinds . It travels easily from place to place . It attacks people in hospitals , and bars , and voting lines , and ICE detention centers , and political rallies , and nursing-homes , and so on . In the past six months , the coronavirus has revealed the most important and troubling features of our society . <p> Advertisement <p> They span the entire country . They reverberate to the very core of life in America . They touch our citizens and our communities and our economy and our standing in the world order . Taken together , they point clearly to the cracks in the foundation of our nation . <p> The virus has , for example , allowed us to recalibrate unexpectedly large inequities across the population . This is perhaps the most obvious example of what the virus has revealed : People of color are more vulnerable to the virus , even though the germ does not discriminate . The virus has showcased multiple injustices that have not diminished as much as many had thought over past decades -- inequities in access to quality health care , high-paying jobs , jobs where working remotely is possible , financial security , food security and so on . This has been made particularly clear in diverse and large urban neighborhoods as well as lightly populated rural communities spread across wide swaths of the middle of the country . <p> The virus has exposed serious problems with the publication and communication of timely social and epidemiological scientific research into critical questions of immediate consequence . How many lives can be saved by quick action informed by prudent testing ? How long does immunity born of infection last ( if it exists at all ) ? Does this or that potential therapeutic actually do any good ? And so on .
