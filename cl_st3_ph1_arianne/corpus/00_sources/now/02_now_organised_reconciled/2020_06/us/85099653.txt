By late afternoon , the air mass will have destabilized sufficiently for convective storm cells to initiate over @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ over the D.C.-Baltimore metro region . <p> The air mass will more strongly destabilize than occurred Wednesday , and there are no elevated " inversion " ( warm , dry ) layers that can impede the rapid upward growth of convective clouds . One of those layers led to widespread suppression of convective clouds here yesterday afternoon . <p> The amount of available convective energy ( buoyancy ) this afternoon will probably exceed 2,000 J/km , which is moderate for the Mid-Atlantic in summer . Anything over 1,000 can mean general thunderstorms . When we get into the 2,000-3,000 range , we anticipate some isolated , strong to severe storms . As a point of reference , the June 2012 derecho had values that exceeded 5,000 . <p> Also , wind shear -- the increase in wind strength with altitude -- will help convective cells become stronger and more long-lived and band together into small clusters . It 's not strong enough shear to produce rotating thunderstorms ( called supercells ) but enough to push a few storms into vigorous territory . <p> An approaching disturbance in the upper atmosphere @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ formation of storms today . <p> The drop in temperature with altitude ( called lapse rate ) is particularly large just above the surface this afternoon , and this is a setup that can lead to strong gusts .
