<p> Punching two more holes in the sides of the Mississippi River could reduce New Orleans ' flood risk while easing the environmental harm caused by frequent Bonnet Carre Spillway openings , a new study by Tulane University says . <p> Two river diversions the state plans to build upriver from New Orleans would cut the amount of water flowing through the spillway by 60% and reduce the duration of openings by about a third , according to digital simulations based on flows from the three spillway openings in 2018 and 2019 . The Ama and Union diversions , which would channel river water through levees and into coastal marshes and bays , are identified as priority projects in the state 's 2017 Coastal Master Plan , but they remain in the conceptual phase . <p> " The study 's big takeaway is that even medium-sized diversions upriver ... would help quite a bit to relieve the pressure on the Bonnet Carre Spillway , " said Ehab Meselhe , a professor in Tulane 's Department of River-Coastal and Science Engineering and a lead author of the study . <p> A pelican takes flight on Queen Bess Island in Barataria @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 2018 . <p> ( Photo by Brett Duke , NOLA.com The Times-Picayune ) <p> The Ama diversion would funnel water into Barataria Bay through a gap in the river 's west bank near the Davis Pond diversion and just downriver from the Bonnet Carre . The Union diversion would divert water into the northern part of the Maurepas Swamp , near Burnside . <p> Both diversions could have the added benefit of spreading land-building river sediment and nutrients into wetlands that have been rapidly eroding , Meselhe said . A second phase of the study will explore the diversions ' potential impacts on coastal wetlands and bays . <p> The aim of most diversions is to reconnect the Mississippi to coastal marshes and swamps that were cut off from the river by levees . Starved of the river 's sediment , freshwater and nutrients , much of Louisiana 's coast is rapidly eroding and sinking . <p> The Tulane study was requested by Restore the Mississippi River Delta , a coalition of conservation groups that include the Environmental Defense Fund , Lake Pontchartrain Basin Foundation and National Wildlife Federation . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Carre 's impact on wildlife , fisheries and coastal communities . <p> Managed by the Army Corps of Engineers , the spillway was built in 1931 as a release valve meant to be used only on the rare occasions when the Mississippi threatened to overtop levees in the New Orleans area . But the spillway 's use is becoming increasingly routine as climate change triggers stronger and more frequent rainstorms upriver . For the first time in its history , the spillway was opened in three consecutive years : 2018 , 2019 , and 2020 . Last year also marked the first time the spillway was opened twice in one calendar year . <p> In Louisiana 's court filings , the state suggested the Corps could reduce spillway use if it helped the state build the two diversions and upgrade the Davis Pond diversion . <p> If the Corps agreed , the three proposals could be combined into one or more joint federal-state projects , with the federal government paying 65% of the construction cost and the state paying the other 35% . <p> The Army Corps of Engineers open @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Friday , April 3 , 2020 . <p> STAFF PHOTO BY SOPHIA GERMER <p> The Tulane study did n't delve into the potential use of the Morganza Spillway above Baton Rouge . Use of the Morganza , which is designed to channel Mississippi River water into the Atchafalaya River basin , has been controversial . The Corps says water inundation in the spillway could threaten wildlife and farmland . In 2011 , the last time it was opened , about 90,000 acres of crops and pastures were impacted . <p> Meselhe said modeling the Morganza 's flows into the vast Atchafalaya basin would need a study of its own . <p> " It 's a bigger , more complex system , " he said . " But it definitely needs to be looked at . " <p> Meselhe 's study also examined the planned Maurepas Swamp diversion in St. John the Baptist Parish , but determined it would provide only marginal reductions in the Bonnet Carre 's flows . The relatively small Maurepas diversion 's aim is the revival of a cypress-tupelo swamp on the edges of Lake Maurepas.
