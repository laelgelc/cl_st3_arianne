<p> KUALA LUMPUR ( June 1 ) : Malaysian small and medium-sized enterprises ( SMEs ) should consider investing in the agricultural , tourism and services sectors in India 's northeastern states as the country is gradually opening up its economy in the wake of Covid-19 pandemic , says the co-chairman of the Asean-India Business Council ( AIBC ) Datuk Ramesh Kodammal . <p> He said this was important as Northeast India is being developed as a gateway to Asean , which will see growing connectivity taking place between the republic and the 10-member bloc in years to come . <p> " Northeast India is something special to us , as it shares international borders with Myanmar , and perhaps Malaysian SMEs seeking to become globally competitive should leverage on this by investing in agricultural products . <p> " Assam , being one of the Indian northeastern state , is rich in bamboo , and Malaysian SMEs could perhaps tap the potential of the commodity , " he said during a webinar titled ' India-Asean Business Opportunities and Challenges ' , held virtually via Zoom on May 20 . <p> Indian Minister Dr Harsh Vardhan -- who had held @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in 2018 -- had said ( in that same year ) that India has the potential to become a significant player in international bamboo export . <p> Speaking at a meeting held with the representatives of International Bamboo and Rattan Organisation ( INBAR ) in New Delhi in 2018 , Vardhan emphasised the thrust provided by the Government to develop the bamboo sector to address various issues such as livelihood , climate change mitigation and land degradation . <p> Ramesh suggested the setting up of a special council in the northeastern states to facilitate businesses besides having a one-stop centre to assist in answering all the questions pertaining to trade and businesses . <p> " With a combined population of 1.9 billion people ( India and Asean ) , and existence of bilateral trade agreements such as the Asean-India
