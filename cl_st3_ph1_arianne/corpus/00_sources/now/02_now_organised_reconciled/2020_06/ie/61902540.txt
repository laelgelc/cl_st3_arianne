<h> ' Ireland must at least stabilise herd to benefit from Oxford science on methane ' -- EPA <p> The Environmental Protection Agency ( EPA ) has responded to the latest Oxford University research on methane emissions from livestock -- and has outlined what it could mean for Ireland in terms of tackling agriculture 's carbon footprint . <p> Over recent months the Irish farming community has been absorbing and discussing information from global experts -- including Dr. Frank Mitloehner , professor and air-quality specialist in the Department of Animal Science at the University of California , Davis -- on how biogenic ( produced by living organisms ) methane from livestock " is not the same " as fossil-fuel related methane from oil , gas etc -- in terms of its origin and fate . <p> Earlier this year , professor John Fitzgerald , chairperson of the Climate Change Advisory Council ( CCAC ) , and Dr. Frank O'Mara from Teagasc also both acknowledged that how Ireland treats methane from livestock -- from a scientific point of view -- is " probably not appropriate " . <p> As for the aforementioned international authorities on agricultural emissions , they have @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ comes to accurately accounting for methane emissions in the carbon cycle , including : <p> An acknowledgement that the carbon that is emitted by animals is " recycled carbon " ; <p> An acknowledgement that methane emitted by livestock is destroyed in the atmosphere " after 10 years " as it is a " short-lived climate pollutant " ; <p> An acknowledgment that stable methane levels ( in other words constant herd sizes ) will have " no impact " on global warming ; <p> And an acknowledgment that where methane is significantly decreased , it can lead to a significant " cooling effect " . <p> However , these same scientists -- including Prof. Myles Allen and Dr. Michelle Cain ( both Oxford University ) -- also stress that where herd sizes increase it means that new additional methane is being added to the atmosphere and therefore new additional global warming . <p> They affirm that while methane is in the atmosphere it is heat-trapping and a potent greenhouse gas . <p> With a number of farmers , farm leaders and commentators now drawing on this new scientific evidence @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the EPA has cautioned that there are a number of key caveats when it comes to applying such compiles greenhouse gas figures for Ireland , including for agriculture , according to standards and guidelines that are agreed internationally . <p> From there , Ireland 's figures are submitted on an annual basis to the European Union ( EU ) and the United Nations ( UN ) where they are reviewed . <p> To date , Dr. Cotter says the figures for Ireland have been found to be " transparent , consistent , comparable , complete and accurate " . <p> On those circulating questions regarding the methodology used to calculate methane emissions from cattle and circulating commentary regarding different results . <p> Advertisement <p> " EPA analysis indicates that should alternative accounting for short-lived methane be adopted , as set out in this research , Ireland will have to at least stabilise the national herd to see any benefit . <h> Carbon sink calculations <p> The EPA representative also acknowledged that land-use activities -- for example , forests and grassland -- can remove
