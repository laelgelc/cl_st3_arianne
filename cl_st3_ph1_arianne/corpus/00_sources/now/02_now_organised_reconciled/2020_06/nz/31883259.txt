<h> Rare iguana species discovered through The Blue Lagoon faces a new threat to its survival <h> A critically endangered Fijian iguana -- first identified after a screening of the 1980 film The Blue Lagoon -- may need to find a whole new island to live on . <p> 5 June 2020 <p> It was during a screening of the 1980 film The Blue Lagoon that the Fiji Crested Iguana was first identified . <p> The movie was partially shot at Nanuya Levu , Fiji and , when John Gibbons from the University of the South Pacific watched the sweeping landscape shots of native flora and fauna on the island , a large colourful iguana caught his eye . <p> Dr Gibbons , who was studying Fijian banded iguana at the time , travelled to Nanuya Levu and identified the new species of lizard to science as the Fiji crested iguana . <p> With its emerald green skin and distinct white markings , the Fiji crested iguana -- also known as the Samure or Vokai , locally -- became one of the country 's most treasured species and is a traditional totem to the people of Yadua Island . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ species . <p> The critically endangered creature used to be found on 14 Fijian islands , but now more than 90 per cent of the remaining Fiji crested iguana population live on just one island , Yadua Taba -- a protected sanctuary . <p> Eroni Matatia is a ranger for the National Trust of Fiji , and like his grandfather before him , he is the protector of the iguana on Yadua Taba . <p> " When the National Trust of Fiji took over and established Yadua Taba -- Fiji 's first wildlife sanctuary -- my grandfather was the first ranger and he had that job for close to 20 years , " Mr Matatia said . <p> " I feel like my family has benefitted from protecting these iguanas , so we owe these iguanas -- we owe them .
