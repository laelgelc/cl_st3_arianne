<h> A vision for workplaces that work for all of us <p> After the lockdown , it is now clear to many that " work " has been conceptualised and operationalised in a way that excludes many people and causes avoidable stress . So how do we deconstruct those ideas ? asks Jess Berentson-Shaw <p> This week public servants in Wellington were chastised and then begged to return to work in the central city due to the financial perils experienced by small businesses . In one article the head of Business NZ appeared to be indicating that without the coffee addictions of public servants the Wellington economy would fall apart . That 's quite the niche version of " private profits are the public service 's responsibility " . <p> It 's a pretty staid story : regardless of the wider public benefits ( or costs ) people in government should persist with upholding current systems and practices to ensure short term financial benefits for some . <p> The more interesting and future focused story in a Covid-19 world is the enormous public value ( value in the more expansive meaning of it ) that comes from a shift @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ be more in line with what people really value . Now is the time for people in business and government , all of us , to explore these stories in depth . <p> Opening a window to a less frantic way of living <p> Covid-19 has thrown open many windows of possibilities , showing how the world we have shaped can be shaped differently should people decide . One area where change has happened on a significant scale for many is how we do our paid work . For those not in essential work , working away from the office gave us some insight as to what it felt like when one thing that matters ( paid work ) is in greater harmony with other things that matter to us in life . <p> ... lifting the stress people experience when paid work and unpaid work comes into conflict , are some of the greatest investments people in business and in governments can make . <p> In our family , while schooling the kids was pretty challenging ( bless you teaching profession ) , there were real benefits in terms @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and what we were able to get done in the rest of our busy life ( the boring but essential work of washing , cooking , and keeping the family cared for ) . All while continuing to be productive in our paid work . The time saved , the stress removed from commuting , rushing between meetings , picking up kids , being stuck in traffic , could be put to use on family walks or bike rides . And once the kids were back at school , the dreaded anxiety of kids being sick on a work day was removed as we knew we could meet their needs while doing work from home . Giving caregivers more time with children , lifting the stress people experience when paid work and unpaid work comes into conflict , are some of the greatest investments people in business and in governments can make . Research has shown it improves multiple outcomes for children , adults and all of us benefit . When we are all on Zoom no one is excluded as the person who is n't physically present . <p> I suspect there is a lot of research to be done to help reveal the best ways to organise ' work ' for meeting the different needs and outcomes that matter to different people in society .
