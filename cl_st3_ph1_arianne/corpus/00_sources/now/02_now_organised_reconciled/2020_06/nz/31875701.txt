<h> Our dying coastal waters <p> Efforts to protect our coastal waters from further degradation are piecemeal , slow and amount to a drop in an ocean of problems . How do we bring life back to the sea ? <p> The health of our coastal waters is on the rocks -- can marine parks save it , or is it too little too late ? <p> Climate change , coastal development , over-fishing and increased ship traffic are all having devastating effects on our marine life . <p> A government report last year found that 90 percent of seabirds and 22 percent of marine animals are at risk or threatened with extinction . <p> Sixteen percent of fish stocks are overfished -- only a 3 percent decrease between 2009 and 2018 . <p> Another report in March this year revealed more dire statistics for Auckland 's Hauraki Gulf Marine Park ; crayfish are functionally extinct ; the snapper population is less than 20 percent of what it used to be and currently less than half a percent of the Gulf is a protected area . <p> Last week , representatives from Fisheries New Zealand , the Department of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that to 30 percent . <p> But apart from a short-term aim of getting to 5 percent by the end of 2022 , there 's currently no timeline . <p> Professor Simon Thrush at Long Bay beach .
