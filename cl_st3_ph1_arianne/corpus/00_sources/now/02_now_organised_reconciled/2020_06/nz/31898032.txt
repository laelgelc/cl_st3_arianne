<h> Think smart , while there 's still time <p> If we have exited this lockdown without showing that we learned from the experience , we are acting irresponsibly . There are probably more colourful adjectives to describe our current local government long-term strategy , but I 've already used them on numerous occasions and see no point in stating the obvious one more time just for the heck of it . <p> However , it is important that someone is prepared to keep reminding our leaders that this crisis has been the main reason why we now have real options we did n't have even six months ago , and we should be taking advantage of them . <p> That fact alone is enough justification for us to insist our council does n't waste the opportunities we have to consider -- but time is running out and we need to act decisively and quickly if we are to benefit from these extraordinary times . <p> As the Government has rolled out its plan for recovery , we are able to examine the detail and pick out the measures that have application for the Tairawhiti region . <p> Some @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ offers of assistance that have been around for some time now , but that should n't detract from the necessity to re-examine them in the context of changed circumstances . Others represent new offers of government assistance that we would be mugs not to accept . <p> The problem as I see it is that in spite of these " once in a lifetime " opportunities for restructuring our economy , that could include massive injections of government funds into this tragically vulnerable but extremely beautiful part of the country , our council appears fixated on dealing with issues that really are of no consequence in the greater scheme of things . <p> There is no question in my mind that the most important items on the council agenda at this time should be those that are identified as " must-have " and " must-do " projects . They are also the ones that should be in the Long-Term Plan . <p> In that context , it would seem to be a " no-brainer " that we select projects that qualify as " shovel ready " because they will have @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ probably , more importantly , become influential in our mitigation against the ravages associated with climate change and our survival against that threat . <p> Yet , our list of " shovel-ready " projects being considered by Government for approval includes not a single one in that category . <p> Sure , there are projects on the list that , when completed , will become necessary parts of the local infrastructure , eg , raising the stopbanks and the Olympic pool complex redevelopment , but even they offer no long-term benefits in terms of permanent job creation or in being an integral part of a restructured economy . <p> It really does bother me that our leaders just do n't seem to get it . <p> I presume they have their reasons why they regard the pandering to pressure groups that demand action on what can only be called " as yet , economically-unproven projects " , as being more important than accepting Shane Jones 's offer to pay for the opening up of the Northern Tairawhiti.
