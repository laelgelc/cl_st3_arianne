<h> Kiwifruit spray under regulatory spotlight <p> A spray that helps kiwifruit to fruit at the same time is due to get its safety reassessed again . Farah Hancock reports . <p> How do you get an orchard of kiwifruit to fruit at the same time ? One way is to spray a hydrogen cyanamide in August . <p> The chemical , most commonly used in a spray called Hi-Cane , fakes the effect of a good winter frost for the vines , signalling it 's time for them to get flowers under way . <p> Fake frost means kiwifruit orchards can be planted in warmer places where fruiting may not happen - or at least not as uniformly or abundantly - without the chemical helper . <p> It 's used from Northland to the Bay of Plenty and even in Nelson , where our southern-most orchards lie . <p> Hydrogen cyanamide products are currently up for reassessment by the Environmental Protection Authority ( EPA ) . Depending on the outcome , there 's a chance the rules around its usage may change , or it could even be banned as it is in Europe . <p> NZ Kiwifruit @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Zealand 's almost 3000 orchards will be using the chemical . Its ability to compress the flowering season saves labour costs and ensures all fruit picked are uniformly ripe . Even in places with cooler winters like Nelson it 's used for this reason . Instead of having an orchard with fruit ripening over three or four weeks , you have ripe fruit all in the same week . <p> Removing the chemical from growers ' tool boxes could render many parts of the country unsuitable for growing the iconic export crop . <p> Research by NIWA published in 2017 mapped the country for where the green Hayward kiwifruit could be grown based on temperature with and without the use of the chemical . Climate change effects were thrown into the equation . <p> Lead author Dr Andrew Tait said as air temperatures increased there was the likelihood of more mild winters . <p> " This could put significant stress on the kiwifruit industry in the Te Puke area , particularly if hydrogen cyanamide is banned .
