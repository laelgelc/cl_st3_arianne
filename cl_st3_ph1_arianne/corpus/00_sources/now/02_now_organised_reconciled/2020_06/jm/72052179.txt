<h> BPO sector loses nearly $6 billion to COVID - Vaz <p> The Business Process Outsourcing ( BPO ) sector has lost approximately US$42 million ( $5.88 billion ) in revenue due to restrictions caused by the COVID-19 crisis . <p> This was revealed by Daryl Vaz , Minister without portfolio in the Ministry of Economic Growth and Job Creation during his contribution to the 2020/21 Sectoral Debate in the House of Representatives on Tuesday . <p> At the time of his presentation , Vaz had responsibility for land , environment , climate change and investment . Since Wednesday he has been shifted to take charge of water and housing . <p> Vaz noted that the local BPO sector was closed on April 22 as Jamaica 's COVID-19 cases rose sharply following an outbreak at the Alorica call centre in Portmore , St Catherine . Up to Wednesday , the Alorica cluster was responsible for 236 of Jamaica 's 678 confirmed cases of the coronavirus . <p> " Members will recall that following the increase in cases due to the Alorica call centre , a decision was taken to close all call centres . Despite this , we managed @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and are moving to see how best to retrieve approximately 7,000 jobs due to the measures imposed to curb the spread of the virus , " Vaz said . <p> He noted that the work-from-home arrangements were extended for approximately 40 per cent of the call centre agents for three months . This will end on August 31 this year . <p> The minister said the BPO task force which he chairs , approved new protocols developed by the Ministry of Health and Wellness that will govern operations within the sector going forward . <p> These include measures related to sanitary and hygiene practices , which must be adhered to at all facilities ; furnishings and equipment ; social distancing , risk reduction , transportation , ventilation , the handling of persons who are ill , the wearing of masks , among others . <p> Inspections are undertaken by the public health inspectors using the BPO inspection tool and the public health institutional health inspection form that has been developed with a view to minimizing the risk of transmission . <p> Meanwhile , Vaz said the growth trajectory for the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> " It has been highlighted that we can experience a return to this trajectory if the conditions such as training , promotion , policy and health guidelines are adhered to and if we work to secure new business and/or scale-up existing business , " said Vaz . <p> " All being equal , we expect to see a rebound in the sector by the end of the 2021 fiscal year , with a move towards the original projection of 50,000 jobs by March 2022 , " he added . <p> Despite the optimism , Vaz said the Global Services Association of Jamaica ( GSAJ ) has indicated that growth will be contingent upon the recovery of the United States , which is Jamaica 's main market . <p> He said there is a proposal for about 20 to 30 per cent of the Global Services Sector workforce to be supported in a virtual Special Economic Zone .
