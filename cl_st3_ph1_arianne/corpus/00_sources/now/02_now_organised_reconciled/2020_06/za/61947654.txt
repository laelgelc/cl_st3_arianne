<h> Do poorer communities have fewer plant and animal species ? <h> A new study confirms that the " luxury effect " is real <p> Wealthier areas in South Africa have more bird species . Photo : Brent Meersman <p> Wealthier suburbs in South Africa are more likely to benefit from the " luxury effect " , enjoying a greater number of plant and animal species , than less affluent places . <p> An international team , with researchers from South Africa , Italy , and Hungary , compared 96 studies undertaken in cities around the world , and found that biodiversity -- the variety and abundance of species from plants to mammals -- increased in wealthier areas of a city . <p> " You get more species in places where there are richer residents than poorer ones , " explains co-author Arjun Amar , an associate professor at the University of Cape Town .
