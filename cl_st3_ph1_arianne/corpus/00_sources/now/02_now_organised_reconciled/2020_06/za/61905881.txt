<h> We do what we do because of you <p> Why do you do what you do ? This is a question asked when you start a Nieman fellowship at Harvard University . Everyone had a different answer , but we were united by the compulsion to tell stories and interrogate the way the world works . For me , the question sent me straight to thinking about my parents , volunteers in Eswatini , then Botswana and finally in the overly hot Limpopo province . <p> They worked on inclusive community development . This meant a childhood spent finding ways to occupy myself on the periphery of community meetings , and seeing the nuts and bolts of how things such as boreholes were drilled . <p> The Mail &amp; Guardian was ever-present in this time . My mother , Mary , collected editions -- sometimes with bits blacked out by censors -- and kept them as collectors ' items . Although we did n't have much money , each week came with an investment in quality information . In Limpopo , Friday and the paper were a ritual . If I close my eyes now I can still picture my dad , John , sitting back in his chair with the paper opened mid-air for hours on end . <p> This was a paper setting down the first draft of history . It was a paper questioning that history , first by challenging the violence of the apartheid regime and then brutality of the neoliberal , trickle-down economics that came after 1994 . <p> We live in an unequal world . A world where inequality is perpetuated by vested interests , where our extractive and polluting status quo harms people 's lives and fuels deadly climate change . These interests write the rules and help manufacture the elective process . Good journalism is crucial because it exposes what those in power do . It equips you , the resident and the voter , with the information you need to change the system ( if you want to ) . <p> For 35 years , the M&amp;G , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ sought to change this world by shining a spotlight on issues . But we also live in a world where the courts ensure consequences and , when they do n't , the public rises up and demands it . <p> I stepped into this newsroom in 2011 as an intern , first walking past walls decked with journalism awards ( intimidating ) and then towards people who greeted me with smiles , curiosity and genuine affection . Colonialism tore out natural resources and sought to break humans . From villages in the Steelpoort valley of Limpopo to the sand dunes of the Eastern Cape 's Wild Coast and veld of the Klein Karoo , I sought to document this reality and question why it continues . <p> There are few newsrooms that take this kind of reporting seriously . The M&amp;G is one of them . It costs money to send skilled reporters and photographers to remote areas .
