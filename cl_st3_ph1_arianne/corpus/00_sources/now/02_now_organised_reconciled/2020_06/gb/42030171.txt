<h> Environmental impact of grazing cattle ' overestimated ' <p> Scientists say the climate impact of grass-fed cattle herds may have been overestimated . <p> Grazing cattle may not be as bad for the environment as previously thought , according to a new study . <p> Scientists at Rothamsted Research in Hertfordshire say their findings , published today to coincide with World Environment Day , show the climate impact of grass-fed cattle herds may have been overestimated . <p> The study , conducted on the institute 's experimental beef and sheep farm in Devon , found urine from animals reared on pasture with white clover had just over half the amount of nitrous oxide previously assumed to be released by grazing cattle . <p> They say nitrous oxide -- a greenhouse gas 265 times more harmful than CO2 -- can account for up to 40% of beef supply chain emissions and the findings from the study could help the farming sector achieve its ambition of becoming net-zero by 2040 . <p> The experiment involved measuring the nitrous oxide levels in urine from three 30-cow herds of cattle grazed on different systems . <p> These were : land that had @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ farmers and a high-sugar grass and white clover mix . <p> The scientists found the urine from cattle grazed on the white clover mix contained 0.44% nitrous oxide once the additional nitrogen captured from the air by clover was accounted for . <p> This is almost half of the 0.77% figure used by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) in its calculations . <p> " These differences might not sound like much , but when used in calculations of the climate impact of beef , they have a considerable effect , " said the study 's lead author , Dr Graham McAuliffe . <p> Study co-author and nitrous oxide expert , Dr Laura Cardenas , said the inclusion of white clover in pasture was unlikely to be a " silver bullet " . <p> However , its inclusion , along with increased levels of legumes in
