<p> When the phrase " natural wine " started appearing on more progressive wine lists around a decade ago , several assumptions were made . One of the most prevalent was that the adjective " natural " gave the bon viveur licence to order bottle after bottle of this frequently seductive drink and that the following morning there would be little or no consequence . The more sceptical drinker found the notions of cleanliness and purity associated with " natural " discouraging ; the word suggested an insipid wine devoid of character or flavour . For many of us , experience of the odd sore head has disproved the first assumption , while the esoteric funkiness and rewarding palate of natural wine has dispelled any idea that its varieties are likely to be boring . Today , to drink natural wine is to go on an adventure . <p> In our digital age , in which authenticity and rough-hewn traditions of handicraft are now highly valued , natural wine speaks to an idea of a much in-demand sense of integrity .
