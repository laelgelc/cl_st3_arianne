<h> Net loss : Author salutes the last salmon fishermen as centuries-old industry fades into history <p> He might be the east coast 's answer to Para Handy , the fictional Gaelic skipper of the puffer Vital Spark whose tales entertained generations of Scots . <p> Sandy Geddes is the imaginary skipper on a River Spey salmon netting operation who , faced with depleting stocks , brings on a group of inexperienced recruits to learn the ropes . He is the star of The Summer Crew , a book capturing in gentle comedy Scotland 's inshore salmon netting industry , paying tribute to a way of life , gone but not forgotten , in riverside communities . <p> Their hilarious tales partly written in the Doric dialect of north-east Scotland are inspired by author John Bennett 's experiences as a student in the ' 80s when he worked as an " oar " , rowing the coble -- or flat-bottomed boat -- that " shot " the nets . <p> Author John Bennett <p> John , 30 , who hails from Kingston on Spey , had to delay the book 's launch in March when he , his wife @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ his six-year-old son Hamish was unaffected . <p> Twelve weeks on , John is recovering at his home in London . He told The Sunday Post : " I met some fantastic people and characters while I was working on the nets . <p> " The summer crews were a mix of permanent employees and part-timers hired to fish the summer grilse run . <p> " Many fisheries stretched back over a thousand years but most were bought out or closed around the turn of the millennium , with the culture , tradition and skills of the salmon fishers disappearing almost overnight . <p> " At one point , up to 15,000 salmon would have been caught on the River Spey each year but in 2018 it was 3,000 .
