<p> The recent incident of a female wild elephant in Kerala falling prey to crude bait meant for wild boar sheds light on the widespread issue of human-wildlife conflict in the state . <p> Scientifically decimating crop-raiding nuisance animals , or vermin , involves a complex and prolonged official process in Kerala . Small scale farmers across the state are finding wild boars as a major threat to their crops and livelihood . <p> In the absence of easy permissible measures to ward off the threat , farmers look for alternatives that are often illegal . In a situation like this , other animals , especially elephants , become unintended targets of crude practices to drive away crop-raiding wild boars . <p> This was the case with the recent deaths of two wild elephants in Palakkad and Kollam districts of Kerala .
