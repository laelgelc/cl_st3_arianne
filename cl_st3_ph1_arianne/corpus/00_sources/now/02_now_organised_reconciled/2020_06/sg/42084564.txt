<h> The coronavirus has accelerated changes in energy supply and demand <h> One of the immediate impacts of the coronavirus crisis was a fall in global energy use . While demand will likely rebound to previous levels , the overall energy mix is changing . Renewables are playing a bigger role as traditional sources such as coal become increasingly uneconomic . This trend was already in place when the pandemic hit , but is now proceeding at an accelerating pace . <p> While there was a drop in oil demand in the first quarter of 2020 , the coronavirus ' impact on oil demand is likely to be mixed . Namely , the investment in renewables capacity and related technologies seems poised to slow but not stop . Moreover , power generation and energy consumption from fossil fuels appears ready to decline even more quickly . These developments are clearly positive for the fight against climate change . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ now <p> Much of the sudden impact of this virus on the energy sector is due to a reduction in demand . High unemployment , greater use of remote working arrangements , and social distancing immediately curtailed the amount of energy that society has been using . If social distancing continues in some respects and the overall economic recovery is slow , many experts believe demand will stay suppressed beyond 2020 . It 's even possible that some of these trends in demand reduction -- such as the shift to remote working -- could be more structural , and perhaps even permanent . <p> Indeed , there have been significant changes in energy usage since the coronavirus outbreak first occurred in early 2020 : <p> -- The International Energy Agency ( IEA ) estimates that energy demand will plummet by 6 per cent for the entire year , primarily driven by the second quarter . The lockdown policies were the clear and obvious driver here : passenger car miles driven , public transportation usage and air travel all plummeted , at least temporarily . Shipping and trucking also took a hit , but to a much smaller degree , since goods still need to be transported . <p> Like the thermal coal industry , the oil and gas industry was also experiencing headwinds before the coronavirus hit , with a greater number of global banks and insurers already restricting some financing and insurance aspects of oil and gas operations .
