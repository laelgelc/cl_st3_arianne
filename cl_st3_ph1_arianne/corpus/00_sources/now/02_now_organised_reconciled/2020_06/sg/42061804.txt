<p> TOKYO , June 18 , 2020 /PRNewswire/ -- The journal Scientific Reports published a research paper entitled " Anthropogenic Anoxic History of the Tuvalu Atoll Recorded as Annual Black Bands in Coral , " written by Dr. Nobuko Nakamura , Research Fellow at the Ocean Policy Research Institute of the Sasakawa Peace Foundation ( Researcher , Graduate School of Science of the University of Tokyo , at the time of the project ) , and a research team led by Professor Hajime Kayanne from the Graduate School of Science of the University of Tokyo , on April 30 , 2020 . <p> The findings of this paper are based on the research conducted in Tuvalu as part of the project " International Eco-Engineering Program for Sustainable Sea Level Rise ( 2008 -- 2013 ) , " through the Science and Technology Research Partnership for Sustainable Development ( SATREPS ) . The paper was published as part of the foundation 's FY 2019 ocean policy research project entitled " Research toward establishing a collaborative framework for SIDS environmental change monitoring and research . The highest elevation in Tuvalu is about 5 meters ( 15 feet ) above sea level . The nation is threatened by sea level rise caused by global climate change . <p> Although global satellite data can track widespread environmental changes , understanding long-term changes at specific locations is difficult due to incomplete or nonexistent historical records . The timing and process of ecosystem deterioration around Tuvalu is unknown because of the shortage of water quality data from before 2009 . <p> Certain species of coral have annual growth cycles that leave patterns in their skeletons , similar to growth rings in tree trunks . Domestic waste running into the oceans increases the amount of nutrients in the water , which then leads to out-of-control growth of algae . Sudden blooms of algae decrease the oxygen levels in the water . <p> " Healthy coral ecosystems play a pivotal role in island resilience against sea level rise . The ecosystem shift from coral to algal overgrowth due to pollution greatly reduces the potential of island formation . The restoration of healthy coral reef ecosystems is strongly recommended for increasing island resilience against sea level rise , " said Professor Hajime Kayanne from the UTokyo Graduate School of Science , a co-author of the research publication . <p> Collaborators at the Univ . of Tokyo and Dentsu Inc. in Tokyo , and the National Institute for Environmental Studies in Tsukuba contributed to this research .
