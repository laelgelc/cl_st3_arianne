<h> Opinion <h> Barry Jones , still hauling around his loaded cart of knowledge <p> When Barry Jones was at primary school in 1942 , he felt the Second World War had taken away the most engaging teachers and it was important to bring his own learning resources . <p> A student forever : Barry Jones.Credit:Matt Irwin <p> And so , walking to Ripponlea State School , the boy who would become one of Australia 's Living National Treasures pulled behind him a little red cart containing what he considered essential reference books , including Pears ' Cyclopaedia and Cassell 's Encyclopaedia . <p> " This caused some derision and a boy called Menzies used to kick me in the ankles , so provoked was he by the red cart , " Jones recalled in his autobiography A Thinking Reed . <p> Seventy-eight years on from grade 4 in Ripponlea , Barry Jones has produced a new edition of his own monumental reference book , the Dictionary of World Biography . <p> Advertisement <p> He has , in short , never stopped learning , however many kicks he might have got to the ankles along the way , particularly @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ made up for the lack of a killer instinct . <p> The Dictionary of World Biography , he says , is his magnum opus : the most important and best work a writer might produce . <p> " It demonstrates my preoccupation , even obsession , about making sense of the world to myself and sharing those insights with others , " he writes in the foreword . <p> This latest edition - He still has three bound copies of that first draft . <p> From there , the work grew in both physical size and intellectual scope , and Jones has no plans to sign it off .
