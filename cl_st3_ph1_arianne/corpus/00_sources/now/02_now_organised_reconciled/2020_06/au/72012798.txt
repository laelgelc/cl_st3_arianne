That 's according to new research that surveyed 2,131 Australians about their news consumption in relation to climate change . <p> It also found the level of climate change concern varies considerably depending on age , gender , education , place of residence , political orientation and the type of news consumed . <p> Young people are much more concerned than older generations , women are more concerned than men , and city-dwellers think it 's more serious than news consumers in regional and rural Australia . <h> 15 per cent do n't pay attention to climate change news <p> More than half ( 58 per cent @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a very or extremely serious problem , 21 per cent consider it somewhat serious , 10 per cent consider it to be not very and eight per cent not at all serious . <p> Out of the 40 countries in the survey , Australia 's eight per cent of " deniers " is more than double the global average of three per cent . We 're beaten only by the US ( 12 per cent ) and Sweden ( nine per cent ) . <p> While most Australian news consumers think climate change is an extremely or very serious problem ( 58 per cent ) , this is still lower than the global average of 69 per cent .
