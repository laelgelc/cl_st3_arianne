<p> At the beginning of 2020 , Australia 's national conversation was dominated by the catastrophic bushfires raging throughout the country . The fires killed at least 34 people , burned through more than 11 million hectares and destroyed nearly 6000 buildings . In March , the first scientific assessment of the role of climate change found that global warming increased the risk of hot dry weather , raising the risk of bushfires by at least 30% . <p> Some analysts -- myself included -- wondered whether the particularly intense bushfire season might present the kind of national crisis that Australia needs to shake it from its climate malaise . The Liberal-National Coalition government found its credibility and authority under challenge as it struggled to craft a coherent and effective response to the fires . <p> Australians are more likely to view environmental threats as more critical than " traditional " security issues such as military conflict between great powers and foreign interference in Australian politics . <p> Then came the novel coronavirus . At the top of the list of perceived critical threats was drought and water shortages , with 3 of 4 people ( 77% ) . <p> Yet , the coronavirus and other potential epidemics ( 76% ) and a severe downturn in the global economy ( 71% ) were ranked more highly as critical threats than environmental disasters such as bushfires and floods ( 67% ) .
