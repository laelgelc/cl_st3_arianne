PHOTO : AFP <p> Covid-19 , coupled with consecutive climatic shocks , is wreaking havoc in Africa . Even before its outbreak , access to safe and nutritious food supplies have been adversely impacted both by natural calamities and the prevalence of multiple epidemics . According to the Regional Economic Outlook Report by the International Monetary Fund ( IMF ) on Sub-Saharan Africa , this is the world 's most food-insecure region , with climate change one of the primary contributors of food insecurity . At present , nearly 650-670 million people in Africa have to face food insecurity , with more than 250 million being classed as severely food insecure . <p> According to Oxfam , nearly 40 million people are at a risk of increased hunger and poverty . Since the past 15 years , nearly 15 million people have been facing hunger on an annual basis . With the former involving heavy reliance on lockdowns , curfews and the need to observe social distancing measures , the latter is concerned with increasing mobility so agricultural inputs and food supplies reach both the farmers and the markets unhampered . To mitigate the impact of Covid-19 on agriculture , governments and donors should set action plans and map out future policies that provide fiscal stimulus packages to bolster Africa 's food security . The proliferation of government relief can be done through the provision of multipurpose cash transfer grants that help facilitate farmers in purchasing food , seeds and fertilisers , thereby increasing their food supply . <p> Furthermore , steps such as subsidising farm inputs can be undertaken to help strengthen prevalent food systems and make them more resilient to any climatic shocks to be experienced in the future .
