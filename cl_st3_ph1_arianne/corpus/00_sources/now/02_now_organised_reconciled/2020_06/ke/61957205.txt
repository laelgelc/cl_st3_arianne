<h> Formations like the El Ni ? o of the Pacific Ocean could emerge <p> About 19,000-21,000 years ago , ice-sheets covered North America and Eurasia , and sea-levels were much lower , with Adam 's Bridge exposed so that the Indian subcontinent and Sri Lanka were contiguous . This period , the peak of ice age conditions , is called the Last Glacial Maximum . Researchers analysed simulations of this past climate and predicted that the ongoing climate change could reawaken an ancient climate pattern of the Indian Ocean . <p> They find that this could be similar to the El Ni ? o phenomenon of the Pacific Ocean bringing more frequent and devastating floods and drought to several densely-populated countries around the Indian Ocean region . If current warming trends continue , this new Indian Ocean El Ni ? o could emerge as early as 2050 . The results were published in Science Advances . <h> Study on shells <p> By studying microscopic zooplankton called foraminifera , the team had published a paper in 2019 which first found evidence from the past of an Indian Ocean El Ni ? o .
