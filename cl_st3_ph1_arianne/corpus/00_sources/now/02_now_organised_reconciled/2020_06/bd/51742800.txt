<h> OP-ED : Picking up the straws of governance <p> The dreaded coronavirus found a way to wreak havoc in urban centres and densely populated cities of our planet . We could safely say this to be essentially an urban phenomenon . <p> Let me begin where the storm originated . We can trace the origins of the virus in the large , thriving Chinese city of Wuhan that then spread quickly to other cities of the world . Although originating in Asia , the geography of the pathogen 's occurrence reveals that it has primarily targeted the flourishing cities of the wealthy West , with glamorous New York showing maximum fatalities . <p> In the countries of the Indian sub-continent , nearly 75% of the total number of positive cases has occurred in the top 35 urban agglomerations , with 32 metropolitan cities in India , Pakistan , and Bangladesh accounting for about 65% of cases . <p> After nearly 60 days of lockdown , as governments relaxed the restrictions and cities started to come back to life , the question that haunted city-dwellers ' minds was : What will city life be like in the future ? @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ been affected , and we are confronted with new challenges . <p> The nature of work , the mode of travel , our social relations and lifestyle , all may undergo a change . Can our cities of the sub-continent learn some fundamental lessons this debilitating virus has offered us and plan for a better future for our cities ? <p> Some takeaways <p> One of the biggest gains from the lockdown is the drastic reduction in urban pollution levels . This was the result of the near-absence of movement of motorized vehicles on roads . <p> So , in the interest of a cleaner environment and health of the people , the first priority should be to move away from an auto-dependent mobility pattern and adopt a strategy that promotes non-motorized transport . This would mean redesigning street architecture to accommodate more bicycles and walkways . Some narrow and congested streets must be pedestrianized , like for instance , the narrow and winding streets in the old sections of Dhaka , Delhi , and Lahore . <p> Secondly , we must aggressively encourage the use of public transport by @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ poorer sections . Intermediate transport like autos and cabs are required to connect commuters to the last mile . <p> For quite some time , they may have to carry a limited number of passengers , and there will be no carpools . What is needed is a well-planned multi-modal transportation system with convenient connectivity to different modes of transport . <p> One of the most painful outcomes of the lockdown is the loss of jobs , particularly for migrant workers who had made cities their home to earn their livelihoods , rendering a variety of services . <p> Suffering from the loss of jobs and the loss of dignity caused by the lockdown and some inept handling by the authorities and employers , they started trekking back to their homes . This will lead to two consequences . <p> One , a drastic reduction in the workforce in cities seriously affecting the economy , and secondly , pressure on the rural economy to generate more employment . How many will return to their original places of work at a future date is uncertain . All this point to the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to ensure that the workers at the bottom of the pyramid are treated as a dignified labour force who contribute substantially to the urban economy . <p> The policy should include matters relating to their shelter needs as they move into the city , the job opportunities depending on the skills and preferences of the individuals and the safety and security of the migrant population . <p> As cities and urban centres are major contributors to the national economy , we must plan to maximize the economic potential of urban agglomerations and other promising towns . The aim must be to promote local economic growth and create employment . <p> The time is now appropriate to initiate action to de-congest large cities by shifting some businesses and offices to smaller cities and towns . It is , however , essential to build infrastructure in and around the new areas where certain activities are proposed to be shifted or new activities created . <p> Urban health has now emerged as a key issue in managing cities . While scientists may be grappling to find a new vaccine for the present virus @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ may break out . Our health system must be equipped to deal with new types of diseases from hitherto unknown sources . <p> A new concept being developed is called " One Health " -- which aims to integrate the management of the health of wildlife , livestock , and humans -- has been advocated by the One Health Institute at the University of California , Davis . It seeks to regularly check for possible infections from livestock and wild animals that are farmed and traded and high human risk populations . We may consider adopting such an approach . <p> The case for decentralization <p> In the light of new developments , we need to review the prevailing urban planning practices . The traditional land use and infrastructure development planning must yield place to more comprehensive planning that prioritizes health , environment , employment , and equity . <p> The recent Cyclone Amphan that hit the coastal areas of West Bengal and Odisha , must serve as a warning to the authorities and the people to be prepared to deal with more than one disaster at a time . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to survive the effects of climate change and disasters . Architects will have to redesign existing buildings to provide for more ventilation and natural light and reduce the use of air conditioners . Builders will have to rethink raising huge skyscrapers . Any city plan will have to be inclusive with space for the poor , the vulnerable , and the migrant sections who are subject to the maximum stress during times of crisis . <p> Finally , a word about the management of lockdown in three countries of our sub-continent : India , Pakistan , and Bangladesh . While we may take pride in having restricted the impact of the virus in terms of the number of cases and deaths , the credit should go in large measure to the lower levels of government -- district and city administrations that have borne the brunt of implementation of the guidelines . <p> A key lesson from the virus : The greater the decentralization , the more effective will be the governance .
