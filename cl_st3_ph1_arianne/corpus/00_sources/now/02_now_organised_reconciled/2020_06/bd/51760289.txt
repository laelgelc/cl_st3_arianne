<h> India 's virus lockdown fuels timber-smuggling in Kashmir forests <h> India 's coronavirus lockdown has paved the way for a rise in illegal logging and timber-smuggling in forest-rich Indian-administered Kashmir , with guards unable to patrol and local incomes plummeting , the region 's forest officials said . <p> The damage to forests was particularly high during the first few weeks of the strict lockdown , which began on March 25 and has been eased since late May . <p> " There was a spike in incidents of timber-smuggling because of the lockdown , but it does n't mean our staff completely failed to act , " said Mohit Gera , principal chief conservator of forests , speaking by phone from his office in Jammu . <p> " In the first few weeks , timber smugglers took advantage as our staff could not reach far-flung areas and our workers have also been busy with helping the government in the fight against COVID , " he told the Thomson Reuters Foundation . <p> Jammu and Kashmir has so far reported about 4,500 cases of COVID-19 infection and at least 50 deaths . <p> Forest department workers seized 4,342 cubic @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , as well as confiscating 13 other private uses , as well as people who recently turned to felling trees because they lost their work during the pandemic , officials said . <p> On World Environment Day , June 5 , the forest department launched a mobile app and toll-free number so that local people could report timber theft in real-time , Gera said . <p> In early April , Kashmir 's Wildlife Department put out a circular asking people living near forests not to visit them , in a bid to avoid transmission of the novel coronavirus . <p> " We felt the need to issue the circular after we learned that a tiger was found
