<h> Uganda becomes first African country to submit REDD+ results , paves way for payments <h> Last Updated : Monday 29 June 2020 <p> Uganda has become the first African country to submit results for Reducing emissions from deforestation and forest degradation ( REDD+ ) to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change ( UNFCCC ) . <p> REDD+ is a mechanism developed by the parties to the UNFCCC to reduce emissions from deforestation and forest degradation . <p> The results submission to the UNFCCC has paved the way for potential results-based payments to the country , the Food and Agriculture Organisation ( FAO ) said on June 24 , 2020 . <p> Results-based payments comprise the final REDD+ phase . It provides financial incentives to developing countries that prove they stopped deforestation during a certain period of time . This is done through rigorous UN-backed technical evaluations . <p> Uganda claimed it reduced emissions by 8,070,694 tonnes of carbon dioxide for 2015-2017 in a document prepared by its Ministry of Water and Environment , the REDD+ Secretariat and National Forestry Authority . <p> Arresting deforestation <p> The REDD+ results showed deforestation reduced to 28,095 hectares ( ha @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ) from a 50,147 ha per year average over a 15-year reference period ( 2000-15 ) . <p> This meant a 44 per cent reduction in the country 's rate of deforestation annually between 2015 and 2017 , compared to estimates for 2000-15 and the protection of 20,052 ha every year on an average during 2015-2017 . <p> These estimates were drawn on data collected by the National Forest Authority . <p> One concern was that Uganda 's forested area could have disappeared within the coming century , if deforestation was not reigned in . <p> The country 's forest cover had depleted to 8 per cent from 24 per cent in the 1990s , largely attributed to human encroachment , including for charcoal , timber and agriculture . <p> The document submitted by the government will help Uganda receive funds through the Green Climate Fund 's forest conservation scheme . <p> Brazil , which received $96.5 million under the results-based payments , was the first recipient country . <p> REDD+ in Uganda <p> In Uganda , the REDD+ program forms a part of the country 's National Climate Change @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a climate-resilient and low-carbon development path for sustainable development . <p> The country had launched its REDD+ programme in 2013 . In 2017 , Uganda presented its first forest reference emission level of historical average emissions from deforestation between 2000 and 2015 as required under measurement , reporting and verification for REDD+ activities . <p> The REDD+ strategy , launched in 2017 , included developing plans and options for forest management to reduce carbon emissions and maintain valuable ecosystem services such as biodiversity , water supply , soil protection and wealth creation for sustainable livelihoods . <p> The Government of Uganda then decided to assess the country 's performance in reducing emissions for 2015-17 and to further improve estimates of forest change and associated emission factors . <p> Significant for Africa <p> The submission of the results are a significant development on REDD+ for Africa , said the FAO . <p> This will encourage other African countries to reduce carbon emissions by decreasing deforestation and forest degradation . <p> Achieving REDD+ results in the continent is challenging , as there are other development priorities -- with agriculture , mining , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ FAO . <p> Africa had the greatest annual rate of net forest loss , at 3.9 million ha , across the world in this decade . The rate of net forest loss also increased in Africa from 1990 till this year , according to the FAO 's Global Forest Resources Assessment 2020 . <p> Supporting African countries through all three REDD+ phases -- readiness , implementation and result-based actions -- by providing tools and analysis of how to design , implement and measure the results of REDD+ action is key to reversing these trends .
