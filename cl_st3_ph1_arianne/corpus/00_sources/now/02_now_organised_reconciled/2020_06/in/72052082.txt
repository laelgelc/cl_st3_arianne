<h> UK can reduce 37% of its CO2 emissions by restoring ecosystems : Report <h> Last Updated : Thursday 25 June 2020 <p> The United Kingdom can reduce its carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) emissions by 134.7 million tonnes -- 37 per cent of its mitigation targets -- if it restores its degraded ecosystems , according to a report published on June 24 , 2020 by non-profit Wildlife Trusts . <p> Landscapes in the UK can store carbon and absorb a third of the island nation 's emissions , according to the report , titled Let nature help : How nature 's recovery is essential for tackling the climate crisis . <p> The UK 's peatland soils store around 3.2 billion tonnes of CO2 , but release 23 million tonnes every year , according to the report . The country 's grasslands and woodlands store two billion tonnes and one billion tonnes of carbon respectively , the report said . <p> A hectare of seagrass stores two tonnes of CO2 a year , said the report . The UK , however , lost half of its seagrass meadows since 1985 . <p> Similarly , one hectare of saltmarsh captures @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ sediments for centuries . The country , however , loses nearly 100 hectares of saltmarsh a year . Coastal realignment could restore much of it . <p> Restoring grasslands , planting more woods and allowing natural regeneration could lock up more carbon and would be one of the most cost-effective , nature-based solutions , the report added . <p> Restoration projects <p> Wildlife Trusts point to nature restoration projects such as the re-wetting of 3,700 hectares of fenland in Cambridgeshire that will lock an estimated 325,000 tonnes of CO2 each year . In Essex , up to 60 per cent of coastal marshes were eroded in the last 20 years . <p> The report called on the government , industry and local authorities to increase investment in nature 's recovery and climate change mitigation . <p> We are a voice to you ; you have been a support to us .
