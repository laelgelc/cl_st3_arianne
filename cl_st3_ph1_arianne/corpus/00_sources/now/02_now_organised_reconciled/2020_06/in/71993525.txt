They are protected in India under Schedule I of the Wild ( Life ) Protection Act , 1972 . <p> There were just 250 dugongs in the Gulf of Mannar in Tamil Nadu , the Andaman and Nicobar Islands and the Gulf of Kutch in Gujarat according to the 2013 survey report of the Zoological Survey of India ( ZSI ) . <p> The survey was conducted under the leadership of then ZSI director K Venkataraman , Basudev Tripathy deputy director of ZSI , Kolkata , told this reporter . <p> In 2010 , we had counted 250 dugongs in the Gulf of Mannar , the Andamans and Gulf of Kutch through a boat survey , K Sivakumar , a senior @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Wildlife Institute of India ( WII ) , Dehradun , said . <p> This year , we will count the dugongs with the help of underwater drone cameras , he added . <p> There is no doubt that dugongs are an important part of the marine ecosystem and their depletion will have effects all the way up the food chain , Sivakumar noted . <p> Dugongs are mammals , which means they give birth to live young and then produce milk and nurse them . <p> Once the female is pregnant , she will carry the unborn baby , called a foetus for 12-14 months before giving birth . Female dugongs give birth underwater to a single calf at three to seven-year intervals . <p> Dugongs graze on seagrass , especially young shoots and roots in shallow coastal waters . They can consume up to 40 kilograms of seagrass in a day . <p> Human activities such as the destruction and modification of habitat , pollution , rampant illegal fishing activities , vessel strikes , unsustainable hunting or poaching and unplanned tourism are the main threats to dugongs . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ was the most important factor behind dwindling dugong populations in many parts of the world , Sivakumar said . <p> Hundreds of dugongs inhabited waters off the Odisha , West Bengal and Andhra Pradesh coasts two centuries back .
