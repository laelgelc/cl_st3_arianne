Photo : Jitendra <p> In the middle of a conversation about shrinking pasture in the Masai Mara National Reserve , human-animal conflict and changes in life style , James Vetingio , 38 , a pastoralist in the reserve , became perplexed after getting to know that the Sahiwal cattle breed has its origins in the Indian Subcontinent . <p> " Sahiwal is our lifeline . It has changed life of the whole of Masai Mara , " he beamed at me while the others smiled . <p> I was sitting along with pastoralists among thousands of cows , mostly hybrid Sahiwal . Smoke was billowing out from hearths where milk was being heated and bread being prepared from cornmeal . <p> All pastoralists were surprised with the information about the Sahiwal . Apparently , the breed has adapted so well to Kenya , they never realised it was ' foreign ' . <p> " Our herds have 40 per cent pure Sahiwal and 40 per @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ native , " said Saimon Liaran , 62 , another pastoralist , who has a herd of around 300 cows . He sells around 50 litres of milk every day . <p> The Sahiwal migrated to Kenya around 80 years ago from the Subcontinent and is now considered as the backbone of the country 's milk production . <p> The breed is the main source of earning for many dairy farmers and is also helping adaption in the face of climate change . Drought used to happen once in four years earlier but is now an annual feature . <p> The National Drought Management Authority ( NDMA ) reports of 2017 show the region faced droughts or received deficit rainfall in 13 of the last 16 years . <p> The number of drought-affected people has also been increasing . <p> According to a report titled Ending Drought Emergencies in Kenya , there were one million drought-affected people in 2013 . This year , in July , the prices of corn flour , a staple , have risen by 25 per cent . <p> Dairy Kings <p> However , food is not a cause of worry for the pastoralists of the Masai Mara . <p> " We started selling milk when pure Sahiwal cattle joined our herds . Our native breeds hardly give milk . It is not more than half a litre during the lactation period , which is used for our consumption . But Sahiwal cows give up to 5 litres , which we consume and also sell , " said Vetingio . <p> Life and times have changed in the Masai Mara plain , 300 kilometres ( km ) south of Nairobi , after the introduction of the Sahiwal breed . <p> The famous Masai ethnic group used to rear around 3,000 native cattle called Zebu and Boran per family . Now , they have reduced the number to around 200-300 . They replaced these cattle with Sahiwal cows . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , which in turn changed their food habits and improved economic conditions . <p> They started sending their children to private English-medium schools . Polygamy , a regular feature of Masai society , meant that earlier each Masai man had 7-10 wives , to manage thousands of native breeds . Today , most Masai men have one wife . <p> " The Masai depended on their native breeds of cattle primarily for meat , blood and milk , in that order . But the Sahiwal changed their priorities . It gives them lots of milk which they consume and sell . It also gives lots of meat , " said Joseph Oyango , farm manager , Narok Pastoralist Training Center , a government organisation in the Narok County . <p> The Sahiwal requires relatively less management in an arid climate zone . <p> " The Sahiwal has adapted to Kenya 's climate and requires lesser management than native cattle . Like other native breeds , it grazes in the open but gives more milk , " said Oyango , whose Centre provides training to pastoralists on managing farms . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 's Southeast and has proven quite effective even in the time of drought .
