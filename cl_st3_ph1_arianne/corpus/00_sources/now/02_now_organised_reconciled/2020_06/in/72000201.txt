<p> The desert locust is a deadly agricultural pest that has been on a feeding spree across North Africa , West Asia and South Asia . Lore and mentions of locust swarms exist in the Mahabharata , the Bible and the Quran . But it has been largely absent from the collective memory of urban societies until recently , when locust swarms were found in New Delhi and Jaipur . <p> Hari Sharma , a globally renowned agricultural scientist formerly associated with the International Crop Research Institute for the Semi-Arid Tropics ( ICRISAT ) , says South Asia could have prevented the debilitating locust infestation if India and Pakistan had heeded warnings sent as early as February this year from the UN 's Food and Agricultural Organisation . <p> In this interview for The Wire with Kunal Shankar , Sharma discusses the issue of locusts and whether climate change has altered the agricultural pests ' behaviours , leading to more virulent attacks .
