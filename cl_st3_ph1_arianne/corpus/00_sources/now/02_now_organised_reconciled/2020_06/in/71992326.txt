<h> Full reservoirs as monsoon sets in : Why India should watch out for floods <h> Last Updated : Friday 05 June 2020 <p> The onset of the monsoon has raised fears of dam-induced floods as India 's major reservoirs are already full . Live storage in 123 reservoirs was 55.837 billion cubic metres ( BCM ) , according to data released by the Central Water Commission ( CWC ) on June 4 , 2020 . <p> Live storage in reservoirs in the corresponding period last year was 32.801 BCM , with the average for the last 10 years at 33.706 BCM . <p> The current live storage available in 123 reservoirs was 170 per cent of the same corresponding period of last year and 166 per cent of the last 10 years ' average . <p> This does not bode well for flood management in a country where extreme rainfall events are becoming a norm . <p> It is , thus , crucial to carefully manage the release of water from dams , as the monsoon reaches the rest of the country in the coming days . <p> The rule curve -- an international practice of dam operation @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ any lapse may lead to dam-induced floods , of which there are several instances in the country . <p> Measures to regulate when and how dams should be filled and emptied are mentioned in the rule curve . <p> It also ensures dams are full only towards the end of the monsoon , providing them a cushion during times of excess rain and curbing chances of floods in downstream areas . <p> Mismanagement occurs when sufficient water is not released initially and -- as rains intensify and reservoirs bulge -- operators suddenly begin releasing water . <p> Dams in Kerala <p> The Kerala floods in 2018 and the Karnataka and Maharashtra floods in 2019 are recent examples of dam-induced flood disasters . Floods increased in these states as the release of water from overflowing dams in the region occurred at the same time as rainfall . <p> The CWC recently revised the rule curve for four major dams in Kerala , lowering permitted water levels in the Idukki , Kakki , Banasura Sagar and Idamalayar reservoirs . <p> Water level in the state 's biggest dam at Idukki should be @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ according to the new norms . <p> The current water level at Idduki dam -- the overflow from which was one of the main causes for the floods in 2018 -- is at 35 per cent of full reservoir level ( FRL ) . This was 18 per cent during the corresponding period last year . Current levels are more than the average of the last 10 years which was at 19 per cent . <p> The levels , in fact , are more than the previous two flood years during the same time . The FRL in Idukki dam is 732.43 metres and at present , there is water up to 712.385 metres . At around the same time last year , it was 704.67 meters and in 2018 it was 708.17 meters . <p> One of the reasons for this was reduced power consumption due to the lockdown to curb the spread of the novel coronavirus ( COVID-19 ) . The hydroelectric dam , as a result , is underworked .
