<p> An estimated 2,100 U.S. military veterans were bound for the frigid Northern Plains on Saturday in a mass show of support for Native Americans and their allies battling the Dakota Access oil pipeline . <p> The vets , organized under the banner " Veterans Stand for Standing Rock , " include 219 vets from California , many of whom departed in seven charter buses on Friday . <p> " Here 's a chance to use my energy as a vet and as a Native American , " said Robin Gage , 60 , a former member of the California National Guard who organized transportation for the California veterans . Her grandmother was a member of the Choctaw Nation , from Louisiana . <p> " Quite frankly , I do n't like bullies , " said Gage , referring to North Dakota 's deployment of militarized police against opponents of the 1,172-mile , $3.8-billion pipeline , which would transport up to 570,000 barrels of oil a day . <p> The pipeline is nearly finished but for a section under the Missouri River .
