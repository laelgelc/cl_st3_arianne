<p> As 2016 draws to a close , it is being recalled as a year of violence and instability , a year of civil war in Syria , a refugee crisis in Europe , terrorism across much of the globe , killings of and by police in American cities , disruptive election results in Great Britain and the United States , unabated climate change -- the list goes on . The world seems a scary place , but almost all previous years were even scarier . The average human being lives a richer , longer and more peaceful life now than at any time in history . <p> The day-to-day , or even year-to-year , events that dominate the headlines are often disturbing -- but taking a longer view shows them to be blips in a trend of general improvement in the human condition . Max Rosen , an economist at the University of Oxford , has launched an online publication , " Our World in Data " ( https : //ourworldindata.org/ ) , to provide statistical documentation of the upward trend . In the United Kingdom , one country that has reliable statistics over a long period , the average person born in 1801 could expect to live to the age of 35.9 . By 2011 , life expectancy in the UK had more than doubled , to 80.8 . Worldwide , the average life span was 29 in 1820 , and 71.4 in 2015 . <p> Part of the reason for longer lives is a steep decline in infant and child mortality . In 1800 , 43.3 percent of children worldwide died before reaching the age of 5 . By 2015 , the rate was 4.3 percent , and many countries have rates below 1 percent . Many of the gains have been recent . In 1990 , 7.6 million children died before the age of 5 .
