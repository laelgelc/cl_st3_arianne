<h> Montana Congressman nominated to lead Department of the Interior <p> President-elect Donald Trump selected his secretary for the Department of the Interior today , according to several reports . While Washington Congresswoman Cathy McMorris Rodgers had been a rumored front-runner , it 's Montana Congressman Ryan Zinke that will be getting the nod . <p> Zinke majored in geology while playing football at the University of Oregon and later earned an MBA and a Masters in Global Leadership at the University of San Diego 's business school . He served 23 years as a Navy SEAL before entering politics in 2008 . <p> Zinke , a Republican , has split with his party on several occasions over the status of public lands . While it 's true that some shale-oil-producing areas lacking pipelines have vented and burned off large amounts of methane -- notably North Dakota 's Bakken Formation -- the Keystone pipeline would have conveyed diluted bitumen and crude oil , not gas . <p> Given Montana 's extensive coal mining , Zinke has been a staunch supporter of the coal industry , emphasizing that Montana 's coal burns cleaner than some other coal deposits that contain more sulfur . He has also pushed back against the stoppage of several logging projects in Montana because of the endangered Canadian lynx . <p> Zinke 's opinion on climate change is difficult to pin down . " <p> As Interior Secretary , Zinke would oversee 75 percent of America 's public lands as well as a host of agencies including the National Park Service , United States Geological Survey , Fish and Wildlife Service , and the Bureau of Indian Affairs . He 'd be in position to help roll back Obama Administration rules regarding oil , gas , and coal production on those federal lands -- including offshore oil leases . Zinke 's nomination is subject to senate approval .
