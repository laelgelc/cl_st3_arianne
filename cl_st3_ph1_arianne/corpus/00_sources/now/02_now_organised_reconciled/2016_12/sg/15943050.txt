<h> Trump 's picks signal more US policy changes <h> He chooses hardliner for envoy to Israel and climate change sceptic for Interior Secretary <p> WASHINGTON * United States President-elect Donald Trump has signalled that more changes in American policies are in the offing with two new nominations . <p> He named Montana lawmaker and former Navy Seal Ryan Zinke , who questions whether humans are largely the cause of climate change , to be his Secretary of the Interior . <p> He also named a bankruptcy lawyer aligned with the Israeli far right , Mr David Friedman , as his nominee for ambassador to Israel , elevating a campaign adviser who has questioned the need for a two-state solution and has likened left-leaning Jews in America to the Jews who aided the Nazis in the Holocaust . <p> The only major departments Mr Trump has yet to assign are the departments of agriculture and veterans affairs . <p> INTERIOR SECRETARY <p> If confirmed by the Senate , Mr Zinke will head an agency that employs more than 70,000 people across the country and oversees more than 20 per @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and Yosemite . <p> As a one-term US congressman , Mr Zinke took several stances favouring coal , which is high in carbon emissions when burned . Coal output suffered during the administration of Democratic President Barack Obama as the development of competing fuels such as natural gas and solar and wind power soared . <p> Mr Zinke , 55 , pushed to end a moratorium on federal coal leases on public lands by 2019 , saying it had resulted in closed mines and job cuts . <p> He also helped introduce a Bill expanding tax credits for coal-burning power plants that bury carbon dioxide emissions underground to fight climate change , a measure supported by coal interests and some moderate environmental groups . <p> Mr Trump 's potential Cabinet is filling with nominees from top fossil fuel-producing states .
