This year , green bond issues are projected to reach US$100 billion , up from only US$2.6 billion in 2012 . <p> That 's far from enough but a good start , and more is likely to come . Citibank , Goldman Sachs , and Bank of America have pledged more than US$300 billion for climate finance in the next decade ; Bill Gates and other high-profile investors recently announced a new US$1 billion fund targeting new disruptive clean technology ( cleantech ) ventures . <p> The role of governments will change to focus on enabling the private sector to invest and do more , faster . This includes removing barriers , getting out of the way when necessary , and being more creative in using public capital . <h> 2 . We need better technologies . <p> Proven , scalable cleantech technologies are already available , and they keep getting better . The real problem is deployment . Solutions that look good on paper , offering attractive paybacks , often end up being too expensive and too risky . <p> Take rooftop solar . The price of a module is often less than 20 to 30 per cent of total installed cost , while 50 to 60 per cent goes into ' soft costs ' like finding customers , permits , financing , installation , and maintenance . To drive this cost down , an entire new industry of solar marketing specialists , installers , financiers and operators needs to emerge . <p> The same goes for energy efficiency , green buildings , sustainable agriculture , or distributed energy . Good technologies are already out there , but we ca n't get them to market . Instead of more technology pilots , let 's put our money into supporting local businesses and entrepreneurs that can de-risk and scale deployment . <h> 3 . Big companies tend to be locked into existing technologies , business models , and mindsets -- no matter how hard they try to change . <p> On the other hand , start-ups are faster and leaner . They can experiment more , understand local market needs better , and excel at ' disruptive innovation ' that radically challenges how we approach or solve problems , including how to get cleantech to market . They start small , but can grow quickly and become big -- really big . <p> The world 's largest producers of solar PV , electric cars and wind turbines were all founded by entrepreneurs less than 20 years ago . Seven out of 10 Chinese renewable energy companies were set up after 2010 , while three-quarters of India 's solar PV was installed by young entrepreneurs . <p> No country has scaled cleantech successfully without also developing a strong base of local entrepreneurs . <h> 4 .
