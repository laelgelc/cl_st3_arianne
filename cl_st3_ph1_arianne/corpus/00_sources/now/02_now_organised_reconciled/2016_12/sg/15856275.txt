<h> Why It Matters <h> Enforcement key in haze fight <p> Indonesia 's latest move to institute an immediate - and total - ban on the cultivation of peatland has been lauded as a big win in the global fight against climate change . <p> For South-east Asia , it may signal the beginning of an end to mass forest fires , often over peatland in Kalimantan and Sumatra , which cause the toxic haze that engulfs much of the region each year . <p> The ban , declared by President Joko Widodo on Dec 1 , has the potential to reduce carbon emissions by up to one gigaton annually , according to Indonesia 's Peatland Restoration Agency chief Nazir Foead . Conserving peatlands can also ensure that endangered species such as the Sumatran tiger and orang utan can continue to live , and thrive , in their natural habitats . <p> Peatland in Indonesia has been under threat for decades , mainly as a result of demands from palm oil and pulpwood industry giants . But observers said that despite that moratorium , protected forests were still being cleared using the slash-and-burn method because of weak enforcement . Mr Joko has been tough on culprits who clear land illegally since he was elected President in 2014 - having taken many individuals and big plantation firms to task for using fire to prime land for cultivation . <p> Indonesia , however , must follow through in its efforts by rigorously enforcing the blanket ban under the latest presidential decree , to ensure it is not toothless legislation that looks good only on paper . <p> A version of this article appeared in the print edition of The Straits Times on December 12 , 2016 , with the headline ' Enforcement key in haze fight ' .
