<h> Human rights investigation into climate change impacts proceeds <p> Published by Greenpeace Southeast Asia <p> Saturday 10 December 2016 <h> Share <p> The Commission on Human Rights of the Philippines ( CHR ) today initiated the next steps in the world 's first-ever national investigation into human rights harms resulting from climate change ( 1 ) , despite apparent opposition from some fossil fuel companies . <p> The CHR announced that it will start holding public hearings from April 2017 , which will be webcast due to the global significance and relevance for other countries . Commissioner Cadiz , who is leading the national inquiry , acknowledged that some 20 companies have responded to the Petition . He also mentioned that independent expert submissions have been received by the Commission . The Petitioners will be submitting a consolidated reply on February 14 , 2017 . <p> Petitioners welcomed the CHR 's announcement as indicative of its continued commitment to proceed with the unprecedented investigation in a transparent and inclusive way despite surmountable challenges presented by some of the corporate respondents . The independent non-profit Business &amp; Human Rights Resource Centre invited the companies to share their responses . Only 11 companies volunteered their positions , with some challenging the investigation ( 3 ) . <p> " As long as companies and governments fail to act on climate change , every day is human rights day .
