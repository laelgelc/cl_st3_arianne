<p> Donald Trump has made a huge impression on the leaders of the Canadian business community . He is seen as a dramatic change agent , for good and for bad . <p> Unfortunately , Canadian business expects the good to be largely for the United States and the bad largely for Canada . The C-Suite believes Mr. Trump 's policies will be strongly supportive of business growth . The political environment for business in America is seen as vastly better than it was pre-election . As a result , Canadian business leaders think the U.S. economy has its best growth prospects in a decade . <p> This is not because president-elect Trump is expected to govern differently than he pledged to do in the campaign . On the contrary , business leaders think he was dead serious about his promises . Led by the expected approval of the Keystone XL pipeline , oil patch executives see him as very pro-fossil-fuel development . With the heavy reliance Canadian companies have on the United States and with expectations the U.S. economy is going to be strong , maintaining that access is business 's primary demand from the Trudeau government . <p> This sets up a couple of collision courses for business . One is with the Canadian government . Executives think the policy environment for business in the United States will now be better than it is in Canada . The Canadian government is trying to balance interests -- social equity and the environment on the one hand , and economic performance on the other . Many businesses want to be unshackled in response to what Mr. Trump is planning for American business . This immediately conflicts with climate-change policy .
