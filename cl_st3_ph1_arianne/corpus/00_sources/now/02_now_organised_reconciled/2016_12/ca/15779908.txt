<h> Solar power lights school at off-the-grid Vancouver Island alternative bastion <p> LASQUETI ISLAND , B.C. -- The solar-power energy system that lights up False Bay School on Lasqueti Island passed a major test this fall when the tiny two-classroom building suddenly went dark . <p> That was when Principal Reid Wilson discovered the school 's back-up power source , a diesel generator , had broken down and the West Coast school had been running totally on solar energy during the dark , wet , dreary October days . <p> " It was just kicking out tons of power to the point where we virtually did n't run our generators at all , " he said , standing in the school 's solar-panel storage shed as rain pelted the roof . <p> " We did n't even know ( the generator ) was broken for a while , " Wilson said . <p> School was cancelled for two days to fix the machine . <p> Wilson said what started as an over-the-fence conversation between neighbours about renewable energy a decade ago , turned into a project to bring solar @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Lasqueti Island , named by a Spanish naval officer in 1791 , is located off the east side of Vancouver Island in the middle of the Strait of Georgia . <p> The island is known as a counter-culture enclave of about 450 people , attracting artists , tinkerers and hermits .
