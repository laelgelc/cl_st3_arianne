<h> What 's wrong with globalization and how to fix it <p> Of all the radical initiatives put forward by U.S. president-elect Donald Trump , the notion of blowing up international trade deals is the most popular . <p> But tampering with globalization is risky . Should Trump have his way , the resulting dislocation would shake the global economy down to its socks . <p> Is it really worth the effort ? <p> After all , the original governing logic behind globalization was magnanimous : " A rising tide lifts all boats . " From its inception in the dark days after the Second World War , free trade was seen as a central pillar for European recovery and the preservation of world peace . <p> Why was free trade so highly regarded ? <p> Protectionism and vicious national economic competition were widely believed to be major contributors to the Second World War . The Treaty of Paris ( 1951 ) established the European Coal and Steel Community and set an important standard in co- operation . This was soon followed by the Treaty of Rome ( 1957 ) , which established the European Atomic Energy Community . <p> With the success of these early supranational agreements , the road was cleared for more globalization initiatives . That accelerated the development of the European Economic Community and set the stage for the Canada-United States Free Trade Agreement of the 1980s . <p> Regrettably , globalization changed for the worse after the fall of the Soviet Union . In the early 1990s , the monetarist- inspired Washington Consensus launched a new era , with a new set of rules for globalization . <p> Originally , globalization was as much about strengthening human rights as about trade . From that point , Wall Street and corporations gained the upper hand . We see the results now . <p> During the presidential campaign , Trump said China was responsible for " raping " the U.S. economically , engaging in " the greatest theft in the history of the world . " <p> But China did n't set the rules of international trade , which allowed multinational corporations to exploit Chinese workers . The administration of former U.S. president Bill Clinton granted Communist China " most favoured nation status " with no human rights or political reform preconditions . Corporations gained free access to developed markets for their products while paying next to nothing for labour . <p> With these changes in the 1990s , globalization became a race to the bottom . Manufacturing flooded out of " rights-biased " developed economies like the United States , Canada and the European Union to " rights-denying " emerging economies like China , Bangladesh and Mexico . <p> Fixing globalization wo n't be easy , but @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ renewed commitment to improving workers ' wages and conditions would level the playing field . <p> Those would be good first steps toward creating a sustainable global economy -- rather than blowing up international trade deals , as Trump has threatened . <p> Robert McGarvey is chief strategist for Troy Media Digital Solutions Ltd. , an economic historian and former managing director of Merlin Consulting , a London , U.K.-based consulting firm .
