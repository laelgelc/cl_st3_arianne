<h> Supporting refugees and beating back bipartisan cruelty <p> Hundreds of days of protests by refugees on Nauru , landmark court decisions , the Nauru Files , politicians ' offices occupied , parliament interrupted , suicides in detention , damning international reports and many more people becoming active in the campaign for refugee justice is the story of the refugee campaign this year . <p> The significant growth of campaign groups and the development of new ones means we are in a better position to end the indefinite and cruel mandatory detention of asylum seekers and refugees . <p> The movement needs to grow , and fast , because there is a global refugee crisis . <p> Refugee crisis <p> A 2014 UNHCR report , released last year , said there were 19.5 million refugees in the world and 60 million people displaced by conflict . By 2050 , climate change will have created 150 million more refugees . <p> The majority flee to neighbouring countries such as Lebanon , which has 1.2 million refugees in a population of 4.4 million , or Ethopia where 252,000 refugees arrived in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ being forced to suffer the most . <p> Western superpowers started the wars in Afghanistan and Iraq . Australia is supporting , including with arms , the Sri Lankan regime 's genocide from which Tamils are fleeing . First World countries are the greatest climate vandals , one of the triggers of the Syrian civil war . <p> The rich nations have a moral responsibility to look after the victims of the wars and dictatorships they have helped create . But the world 's elite are only interested in protecting their wealth and power : they hog global resources while pushing anti-immigration laws and deploying their navies to stop boatloads of desperate people from being able to claim asylum . <p> The documentary Chasing Asylum , seen by thousands this year , viscerally portrayed the detention experience that is literally killing people , including Omid who committed suicide this year by self-immolating . <p> Reza Berati was beaten to death by guards . This is happening , with a range of new groups forming or significantly developing their campaigns this year , including Grandmothers against Detention , Mums for Refugees , Doctors4Refugees , Teachers for Refugees , Love Makes a Way , Amnesty International , Students for Refugees and People Just Like Us . <p> Doctors4Refugees launched a High Court challenge to the law criminalising those speaking out about the horrors of detention , and the movement won a Border Force Act exemption for doctors and health professionals . <p> A poll commissioned by SBS before the federal election found that asylum seekers were the most important issue for people under 25 . <p> Before the election , the Turnbull government claimed -- falsely -- that for the first time in a decade there were no children in detention . The protests worked and Baby Asha was moved to community detention . She is still in Australia . <p> The Palm Sunday protests in March were bigger in the major cities and were also organised in regional centres , often leading to the formation of new refugee rights groups . <p> This year also saw the start of the daily Nauru protests , which have been taking place for more than 200 continuous days , often with new banners each day . This pushed some conservative crossbench Senators to indicate they may not support it . <p> In early December , activists bravely disrupted national parliament , causing a bipartisan furore . They effectively brought their point home about the bipartisan cruelty to refugees ( see story on page 4 ) and won widespread praise for their successful banner unfurling from the parliament 's rooftop . <p> Humane policy <p> The movement for refugee rights is finishing off the year in a stronger position . This has led to some discussion about what a humane policy would look like . After more than 20 years of refugee bashing we need to be wary of those arguing for compromises . <p> The movement can not accept boat turn-backs . Alternatively , they would be forced to wait in third countries , such as Malaysia or Indonesia where , even if they are assessed as UNHCR refugees , they would be without citizenship rights . <p> We can not accept compromises that absolve Australia of its responsibility to help refugees . As long as the conditions exist for people to try to flee from war , genocide and climate change , people will seek asylum . <p> Equally , we should reject the US refugee swap deal that hinges on boat turn-backs .
