<h> Climate talks cast shadow over Turnbull <p> But Malcolm Turnbull is discovering the dangers of the lights being out , especially in South Australia and Victoria . <p> The prime minister faces a perfect storm when it comes to electricity prices , blackouts and climate policy . <p> SA was hit by what is known as a " black system " event on September 28 and an unrelated series of power outages on December 1 . <p> The state relies heavily on the uninterrupted service of the transmission network that connects it to Victoria . <p> The blackouts were not only unwelcome by families and businesses but sent shock waves across federal politics . <p> But the issue has gained momentum this week before the release of a report by chief scientist Alan Finkel and the bungled handling by the government of a coming review of its " direct action " climate policy . <p> Finkel was commissioned by the government to put together a national blueprint on energy security and reliability . <p> The Australian electricity market is going through rapid change , with more renewable energy @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ being taken offline , prices under pressure , rooftop solar gaining in popularity and consumer demand changing . <p> Renewable energy is necessary to address carbon pollution , but also presents a challenge in terms of reliability of electricity supply and the cost for businesses and consumers . <p> The climate change policy highway is littered with the bodies of political leaders . <p> Turnbull lost the Liberal leadership in 2009 over his support for a deal with Labor on an emissions trading scheme . <p> Julia Gillard 's carbon tax deal with the Greens and independents to form minority government in 2010 led the way to her demise . <p> Kevin Rudd 's attempts to put new life into the issue at the 2013 election ended in tears , with Tony Abbott coming to power on a platform of " axe the tax " . <p> Now a recycled Turnbull - who is still a staunch supporter of climate action - faces the heat from all angles . <p> His environment minister , Josh Frydenberg , set off alarm bells this week when he suggested the government , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ intensity scheme " similar to that taken to the election by Labor . <p> Conservative senator Cory Bernardi said it was " one of the dumbest things " he had heard and Abbott chimed in saying : " The last thing ministers want to do is reopen questions that were settled for our side back in 2009 . " <p> Turnbull quickly went into defensive mode , despite such a scheme likely to be the centrepiece of the Finkel report and having been backed by CSIRO , the Climate Change Authority and the Australian Energy Market Commission . <p> " We will not be adopting an emissions intensity scheme . We wo n't adopt an ETS . We wo n't adopt a carbon tax , whatever way you like to call it , " Turnbull said . <p> Treasurer Scott Morrison talked down the review as mere " housekeeping " and was surprised by all the fuss . <p> He has accused the Labor opposition of applauding the end of coal-fired power and setting an economically damaging renewable energy target of 50 per cent by 2030 . <p> But @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ climate policy in the hands of the states - with no national co-ordination or direction - might keep coalition climate sceptics at bay , but is not sound leadership by Turnbull . <p> The Climate Institute 's John Connor says it is not a political game . <p> " We are talking about the stability of our energy system ... in a world that is turning to clean energy and is taking decarbonisation seriously , " he says . <p> The energy industry and its investors are worried about a lack of national leadership . <p> Australian Energy Council chief Matthew Warren says the situation in SA shows the energy system is " visibly deteriorating " . <p> Large power stations are being shut down without a plan to replace them and investment has been paralysed by a decade of policy uncertainty , he says . <p> " Australians want a reliable and affordable transition to a decarbonised energy future . It is not an unreasonable request , " Mr Warren says .
