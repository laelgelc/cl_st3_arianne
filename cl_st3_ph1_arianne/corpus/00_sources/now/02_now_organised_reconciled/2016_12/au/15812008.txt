<p> No one tells the story of Australian gardening like Richard Aitken , who takes postcards , family photographs , prize certificates , magazine advertisements and other seemingly random pieces of ephemera and weaves them together with more high-brow imagery from the State Library of NSW . <p> The pictures in all their diversity are the perfect accompaniment to Aitken 's wide-ranging reflections on the twists and turns of gardening through the ages . Gardens can be scientific collections , artistic expressions , environmental statements and eccentric whims and Aitken touches on it all . By looking at everything from Indigenous environmental management to topiary kangaroos in the suburbs to the modern-day inclination to find meaning through growing food , he examines how the contemporary garden came to be .
