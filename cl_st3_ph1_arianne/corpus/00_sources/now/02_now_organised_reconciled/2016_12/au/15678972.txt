<p> SINCE Donald Trump 's triumph many politicians and commentators have claimed his victory proves something they have been saying for years -- about immigration , elites , political parties or trade . <p> We should take all such claims with a large grain of salt . If Hillary Clinton had won , would these astute observers of politics and society have been as quick to admit her victory proved their beliefs on these topics false ? Unlikely , and for good reason . <p> Only 55 per cent or so of registered voters cast a ballot , and a little more than half of those voted for Clinton . According to statistician Nate Silver , if just one more in every 100 had favoured Clinton she would have secured a victory as great as that by Trump . <p> What should astonish us about Trump is not that he narrowly won the election , but that , even putting his personal failings aside , he could be a serious candidate without offering any policies that might provide solutions to his supporters ' problem . <p> The promise Trump @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ by them -- mainly white , working-class and rural folk , mostly without education beyond high school , who are concerned about Mexicans , Muslims and Chinese . President Trump vowed to make America like it used to be . <p> But Trump 's plans will not achieve this . They are more likely to harm his supporters . They include tax cuts for the wealthy , ending climate action to cut coal consumption , abolition of Obamacare , abandonment of free-trade deals , and imposition of high tariffs of up to 45 per cent on imports from China . <p> Let 's look at each of these . <p> Coal is a killer of poor people . The International Energy Agency estimates the pollution resulting from the burning of coal and oil causes about three million premature deaths each year , and indoor air pollution caused by cooking stoves and heating accounts for a similar number . <p> Even if coal is responsible for the minor share of this burden , which the World Health Organisation says causes more deaths per year than HIV/AIDS , tuberculosis and road @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ consumption of coal . Add to that the problem of climate change , which was recognised in the Republican National Platform until 2008 . It is clear why the price of shares in Peabody Energy , the world 's largest private-sector coal company , have fallen from $80 in 2008 to $2 this year , when it filed for bankruptcy . Making Peabody Energy great again will require more magic than the market has managed . <p> The chance of tax cuts for the wealthy helping Trump 's supporters is no more hopeful . Inequality of family incomes in the US decreased from the 1950s to 1980s , but rose sharply after the Reagan tax cuts . As measured by the Gini coefficient ( the measure used by the CIA in its World Factbook ) , inequality in the US is on par with Mozambique , Jamaica , Uruguay and Peru , while the most equal societies are the high-taxing European and Scandinavian countries . Tax cuts might stimulate economic growth , but rather than the new wealth trickling down , it gushes up .
