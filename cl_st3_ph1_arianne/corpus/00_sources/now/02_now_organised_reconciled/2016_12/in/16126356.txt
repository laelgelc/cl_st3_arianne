With winter setting in , millions living in rural and suburban India has many reasons to worry about , one of it being air pollution . <p> The surroundings suffer from choking smog and acrid smell in the air , most of which are the result of smoke from chulhas and conventional cook stoves used by millions at their homes . However , the effect of air pollution is different in Eastern India as compared to the rest of the country , where millions still depend on coal-based heating and cooking medium . <p> With multiple cities far from reaching the minimum air quality standards recommended by the World Health Organization , traffic is being forcibly reduced , construction projects are put on hold , in order to combat the crisis . Yet one of the pollution crisis 's major contributors , emissions from cooking , has not made headlines as other pollution sources have . Over 85% of Indian rural households use biomass as cooking fuel , according to reports . Biomass burning not only causes indoor pollution but destroys a key resource for soil rejuvenation . <p> According to The Energy and Resources Institute ( TERI ) , nearly 40% of India 's air pollution comes from domestic fuel burning . Unsustainable wood harvesting also contributes to deforestation , reducing carbon uptake by forests . <p> Earlier this year , an online database established with support from the Global Alliance for Clean Cookstoves , an initiative by the United Nation Foundation , began tracking the impact of household energy consumption on more than 640 districts across India . The data showed that almost 30% of the country 's outdoor air pollution is due to household energy combustion .
