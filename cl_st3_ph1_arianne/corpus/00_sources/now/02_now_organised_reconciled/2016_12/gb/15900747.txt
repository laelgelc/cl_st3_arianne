<p> On Christmas Day 1911 , Captain Robert Falcon Scott and his seven companions huddled in tents over a celebratory supper in the wastes of Antarctica . Outside it was minus 21.6 Celsius . The Christmas provisions had been loaded surreptitiously ; the extra weight on the sledges the men were dragging to the South Pole was unrecorded . Not that it would have added much . The main course was pemmican -- a revolting-sounding concentrate of fat and horsemeat , on this occasion thickened with biscuit and flavoured with onion and curry powder . That was followed by a " hoosh " ( stew ) of cocoa , arrowroot and more biscuit , then cocoa with raisins , and finally dessert : two half-inch squares of plum duff each , and four tiny slabs of toffee and crystallised ginger . <p> Scott nevertheless recorded the event with relish : " I knew that supper was to be a ' tightener ' , and indeed it has been .
