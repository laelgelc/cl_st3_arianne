<p> When the river Eden swept into Angela and Ian Burrow 's house uninvited one night last December , they resigned themselves to spending the festive period in a rented flat paid for by the insurance company . But not in their wildest , darkest fears did they ever anticipate a second Christmas Day away from their smallholding outside Crosby-on-Eden , five miles from Carlisle . <p> Yet that is their miserable reality , along with at least 700 other families across Cumbria , whose homes are still uninhabitable a year after being battered by the brutal triumvirate of Storms Desmond , Eva and Frank last December , which flooded 16,000 properties across the UK . <p> Record volumes of rain fell on land already saturated from a soggy autumn , causing rivers to swell so much that the Burrows found a pregnant cow at the golf course at the bottom of their garden which had been carried 20 miles down the Eden from her farm near the village of Great Salkeld . <p> The Burrows ' still uninhabitable home in Crosby-on-Eden .
