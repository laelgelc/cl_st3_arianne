<h> Want to fight climate change ? Try diet change <h> World Meatless Lunch encourages less meat intake among Filipinos to combat global warming <p> Adobo , menudo , sinigang , lechon , sisig and other famed Filipino dishes may whet many appetites . For the Department of Environment and Natural Resources , though , these meaty delicacies also contribute to global warming . <p> On Monday , November 28 , secretary Gina Lopez feasted with the academe , health and nutrition experts , food and beverage companies , students , businesses , environmental and animal advocates , and religious groups for the World Meatless Lunch Day . <p> First observed on October 1 , 2012 , this year 's theme -- " Fight climate change , one plate at a time " -- points to a simple proposition : that the way to a less warm world is through the stomach . Advocates find it cool . <p> Its proponent is Luntiang Lunes , a local offshoot of the global Meatless Monday campaign that aims to wean Filipinos off meat even for one day every week . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ local campaign , touting the health and environmental benefits of devoting to vegetables and fruits in lieu of meat . <p> The organizer , Nora Andaya-Castillo , said : " What we do is that we should do Monday , it 's a good way to develop a habit . That 's why it 's called Meatless Monday . " <p> The Meatless Lunch event featured an assortment of vegetarian and vegan dishes that deceptively look meat-based but are guaranteed guilt-free : adobo , kare-kare , hamburger , nuggets and cookies , to name a few . <p> There are now a number of restaurants in the Philippines catering to vegetarians and vegans such as Blissful Belly Vegetarian Cuisine and Shop , Kisig Lopez and Agico Vegetarian Cafe . They serve veggie versions of Filipino fares such as adobo , tapa , sisig and kare-kare . <p> A website called Happy Cow aggregates a list of vegan and vegetarian restaurants around the world . <p> There are also imported foods such as meat-free nuggets and burgers from British food company Quorn .
