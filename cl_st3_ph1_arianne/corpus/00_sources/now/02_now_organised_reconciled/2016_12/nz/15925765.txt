<h> Tuatara population bolstered <p> Orokonui Ecosanctuary 's population of endangered native species was bolstered by the arrival of 17 juvenile tuatara yesterday . <p> The 3 to 4-year-old tuatara , reared at the University of Otago , joined 87 adult and juvenile tuatara released at the ecosanctuary in 2012 . <p> University of Otago department of zoology Prof Alison Cree said the national tuatara population was about 60,000 . Orokonui was the only fenced ecosanctuary in the South Island where tuatara roamed free from the risk of predators . <p> Ecosanctuary general manager Chris Baillie said research by Prof Cree and her research assistant Scott Jarvie on the 2012 tuatara release group indicated the tuatara were thriving at the sanctuary . <p> Huirapa Upoko David Ellison , of Karitane , with a juvenile tuatara . " <p> Creating safe habitats for tuatara would also increase the survival chances of more critically endangered reptiles which favoured similar conditions , Prof Cree said . <p> The 17 tuatara were released one by one in a newly cleared area within the sanctuary close to the area where the 2012 cohort were released . <p> John Williams , of Dunedin , was given the honour of helping the first tuatara into a burrow after volunteering to clear bush before the release . <p> Mr Williams said he had never seen a tuatara before his hands-on encounter . <p> The juveniles would take a further 11 years to reach maturity . <p> The prehistoric characters were likely to survive at the ecosanctuary for as long as 100 years , Ms Baillie said . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @
