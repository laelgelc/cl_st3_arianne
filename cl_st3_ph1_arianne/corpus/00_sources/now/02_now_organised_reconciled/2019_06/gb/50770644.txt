<h> A greener Europe <p> By keeping global warming in the public eye the protests helped Green parties in last month 's European elections . They won 74 seats in the European Parliament , compared with 52 before . <p> Their surge , and the boost for liberal parties in the centre , will change the dynamic of EU politics . The cosy centre-left/centre-right majority is now gone . <p> Eurosceptic and anti-EU nationalist parties also increased their numbers - which could act as a brake on EU ambitions , as they demand a repatriation of powers from Brussels . The greater fragmentation may make it harder to achieve consensus . <p> Pro-EU politicians are cheered by the fact that voter turnout increased , after decades of decline , to about 51% . So , despite the nationalist rhetoric , they argue that on certain issues , such as climate change and migration , there is an appetite for " more Europe " . <p> But are the EU 's plans for 2019-2024 ambitious enough on climate change ?
