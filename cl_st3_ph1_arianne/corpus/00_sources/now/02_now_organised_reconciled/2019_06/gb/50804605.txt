<h> Why has the UK introduced a net zero target on greenhouse gas emissions ? <h> Why has the UK introduced a net zero target on greenhouse gas emissions ? <p> The UK has become the first major economy to set a net zero target on greenhouse gas emissions for 2050 . <p> -- What has happened ? <p> The Government has signed into law a new target to cut greenhouse gas emissions to " net zero " by 2050 , after it was approved by the Commons and Lords this week . <p> It strengthens the target the UK already had to cut its greenhouse gases by 80% on 1990 levels by mid century , which was approved by MPs under the Climate Change Act in 2008 . <p> ( PA Graphics ) <p> -- Why do we need to aim for zero emissions ? <p> It means cutting emissions to as close to zero as possible and using methods to take greenhouse gases out of the atmosphere to " offset " the remaining pollution -- for example , by planting trees which absorb carbon dioxide . <p> -- What happens if we do not do it ? <p> We have already experienced 1C ( 1.8F ) of warming , and commitments by countries to cut their emissions leave the world on track for rises of 3C ( 5.4F ) by 2100 . <p> Temperature rises of more than 1.5C are set to increase extreme weather events such as heatwaves and flooding , cause greater losses in crop yields and wildlife and raise the risk of large-scale irreversible impacts such as melting ice sheets which will push up sea levels . <p> While the UK 's current emissions make up only a small percentage of the overall global output , the country led the industrial revolution and has made a major contribution to greenhouse @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ emissions to net zero by 2050 will meet the UK 's commitments under the Paris Agreement to limit global warming and provide leadership for other countries to follow suit . <p> -- How are we doing so far ?
