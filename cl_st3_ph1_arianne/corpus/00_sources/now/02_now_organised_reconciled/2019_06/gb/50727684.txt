<h> Dalston church extension raises concerns with community <h> Dalston church extension raises concerns with community <p> A FORMER university lecturer has raised concerns about plans to extend an historic village church . <p> David Wilson , who lives in Dalston , has highlighted his fears of the removal of 17 gravestones , and several trees , one of which is hundreds of years old , if building work gets the green light . <p> The planning application , which was submitted to the city council in March , aims to extend the church to add a library , a function room , and an entrance . <p> It also states plans to convert the existing vestry to form a new accessible toilet , office and kitchen , plus to make alterations to the existing disabled toilet to provide a vestry and an accessible toilet . <p> David , 68 , who used to lecture at the University of Cumbria , expressed his views to The Cumberland News that the proposed development should not go ahead . <p> He said : " The Bishop of Carlisle says Dalston 's fine churchyard trees are of ' no particular value ' @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ historic graves . <p> " Although residents including children have adorned these impressive and healthy trees asking for their protection , the office of the Bishop of Carlisle has addressed a letter directly to members of the Carlisle city council 's development control committee , asking for approval of a proposal that angry villagers consider unnecessary and damaging . <p> Mr Wilson claimed that Dalston parish council also opposes the application . <p> Since plans were submitted , there have been more than 30 comments made online - comprising of 24 objections , five in support , and one neutral . <p> David continued : " The Bishop 's letter of support , published online on May 20 , says the trees will be replaced by others ( i.e. saplings ) that are ' more suitable ' . <p> " Meanwhile Historic England has been courted and is complying with the church authorities , referring to the trees as ' poor specimens ' and failing to accept the visual impact on the north side of the church and its setting , also making no mention of the removal of gravestones @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ The proposal for this extension is causing anger and alienation among many in Dalston who would otherwise be sympathetic with the aims of Dalston Parochial Church Council ( PCC ) . <p> " This anger and alienation would increase and be consolidated over the longer term were the proposal to succeed . " <p> David added that Dalston is already well supplied with a range of community and meetings facilities . <p> " At a time when the retention and planting of trees , not just in conservation areas like this one , are seen as crucial in the mitigation of climate change , the proposal to fell them for the sake of bricks and mortar for this unnecessary scheme seems perverse and out of touch . " <p> In a letter to the planning department to voice his support for the development , Dalston and Burgh councillor Trevor Allison said : " The project is important for the future of St Michael 's and this is recognised at the highest levels in the church . <p> " Objectors to the project argue there are adequate facilities already in the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " It is clear that this is seen only as competition for the existing facilities .
