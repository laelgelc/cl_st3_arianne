I only mention it because some days you could be forgiven for thinking there are only two , one in London and one in Edinburgh , and that they do nothing but obsess about the constitution . The Department for Infighting , and Reviling the EU , the Whitehall version could be called . DIRE , really . <p> For many of us punters , the Brexit experience has been like getting stuck in the story of an interminable box set -- Lost , perhaps , minus the sunshine and charismatic leads -- where we all woke up on June 24 , 2016 , trapped in a new reality only to become increasingly freaked out as the implications of our situation unfolded . Our lifeboat is a referendum and you can choose from two kinds : a People 's Vote or indyref2 . The fightback is on . <p> And so the saga continues . It 's infuriating but also secretly thrilling because politics has n't been this adrenalin-fuelled since the Poll Tax . <p> But while we punters tweet and Facebook and march , and while our political masters spend hours of each day embroiled in Brexit , many of them pushing for that so-called confirmatory referendum on the EU ; while Scottish ministers use ever more of their political capital ( and quite soon parliamentary time too ) advancing the case for independence , there has @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a feeling that the country is drifting and the bridge is deserted . <p> Of course our constitutional arrangements matter . But with so much attention on that , just take a look at what is piling up in the government 's inbox . Homelessness . Poverty . Climate change . Social care . The list goes on . And unfortunately none of it is going to be solved by a referendum . <p> This is the big stuff that will happen regardless of whatever constitutional set-up we have . The Trussell Trust yesterday released figures showing that last summer , demand from families for emergency food parcels rose by one fifth ; they think this summer will be even busier . Let 's just let that thought sink in for a moment : 6,500 children , in this advanced economy of ours , given food parcels because parents who normally rely on free school meals struggle to feed their children during the holidays . <p> Some of those children will be stuck in bed and breakfast accommodation , or sleeping on the sofa at granny 's house . Homelessness applications are up for the second year running , after many years of decline , with nearly 36,465 Scots seeking somewhere to live .
