<h> Western Rajasthan faces climate change perils : Report <p> The desert districts are on the brink of a humanitarian crisis with the rise in surface temperature <p> JAIPUR : The desert districts are on the brink of a humanitarian crisis with the rise in surface temperature in Rajasthan . The excessive loss of water due to evaporation is leading to mass migration of people , livestock and desertification of the cropped land . To mark the world environemnt day , a Central Arid Zone Research Institute ( CAZRI ) , Jodhpur shared its detailed report on the impact of rising temperature in the arid region says that one degree rise in temperature would increase the evapotranspiration by 35 mm to 96 mm . It translates into the additional water requirement of 1.57 lakh MLD water per annum to feed the irrigated land . <p> The report studied the temperature , rainfall , surface heating , evapotranspiration and its sociological impact in districts including Barmer , Bikaner , Churu , Ganganagar , Hanumangarh , Jaisalmer , Jalore , Jhunjhunu , Jodhpur , Nagaur , Pali and Sikar . The additional water requirement will put more groundwater zones into a dark category . " Rajasthan at present has 595 groundwater zones of which 268 zones are under the categories of grey and dark zones where the water has been utilised more than 65% . The drought every alternate year takes 10-15 years for any zone to hold water more than 50% . Globally evapotranspiration trends are projected for 5% to 10% increase with the increase in temperature by 2 to 5 degree Celsius . <p> The rise in temperature has already started costing the farmers of these districts . The report says that with the rise of one degree temperature in Ganganagar would require about 276.19 MCM water and Hanumangarh would need 245.33 MCM water . " The additional water means , additional power and other resources making the sustainable living difficult . " In no time , the men would be leaving their soil with their families and livestock which will eventually push them into poverty , " said Dr Zahida Shabnam , a historian on Rajasthani culture and tribes .
