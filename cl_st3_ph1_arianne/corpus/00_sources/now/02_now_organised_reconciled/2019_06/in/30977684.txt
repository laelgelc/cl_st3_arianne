<p> To minimise the consequences on human health and to make the health system more resilient to climate change , the Maharashtra State Public Health Department will study the impact of climate change on health . A committee has been formed that will study the adverse effects of climate change seen on environment and its effects on communicable as well as non-communicable diseases . The study will be started from the most polluted cities in the state , said the officials . <p> The impact of climate change has been considerably enough to threaten human health both directly and indirectly through increasing temperatures , reduction in water and food supply , extreme weather events such as floods , droughts and population migration . Children , elderly and people from economically deprived class are among the most susceptible of the damaging effects due to climate change . According to experts , there is an also indirect effect of climate change such as mental health problems and involuntary migration that too are important and should be considered . <p> Dr Pradeep Awte , State Surveillance Officer , said , " An action plan will be made and accordingly the effects will @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ be recommended . Initially surveillance will be conducted of the air pollution in the cities of the state that is considered to be polluted . The respiratory data will be evaluated and used to compare its heath impact on the people with the number of respiratory disease . There is a noticeable amount of impact of climate change on the health of people that includes communicable and non-communicable diseases . " <p> Dr Awte said , " Soon nodal officers will be appointed and the surveillance will be started . Seeing the cumulative trend of impact of climate change on human health , implementation of alleviation measures such as through early monitoring , disease investigation , vector and disease control can be done .
