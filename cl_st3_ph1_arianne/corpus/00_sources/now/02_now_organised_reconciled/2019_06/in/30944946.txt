Modi , now firmly in power and conscious of the limits of such an endeavour , will probably not pander too much to this constituency . <p> His victory will also give a fillip to elites in India 's political and financial capitals who have thus far felt sidelined by a consensus that privileged the state as the Indian economy 's primary actor . And with such a diversity of actors in the mix , it is foolhardy to believe everyone wants the same from India 's foreign policy . What is clear , though , is that none of them want more of the same . <p> Second , the traditional view that our foreign policy is crafted to preserve room for India 's development , while it gradually stakes out a greater political role in the world , has also been challenged by these elections . To be sure , Indian diplomats will not abandon overnight the country 's economic priorities for all else . Students of international relations in India will be ill-advised to ignore this change . <p> India 's external environment will naturally affect the conduct of its diplomacy during Modi 's second term .
