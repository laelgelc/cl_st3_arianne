<h> ' We need to treat the mountains gently and with respect ' <h> Last Updated : Sunday 16 June 2019 <p> Like his father , the late Sir Edmund , Peter Hillary has summited Everest and forged a new route to the South Pole . Peter is an accomplished mountaineer , speaker and expedition leader . He spoke to Down To Earth about the recent ' traffic jam ' on Everest in May which claimed 11 lives <p> What is your first response to what happened on Everest in May ? <p> Everybody looked at that photograph ( of a crowd on the top of Everest ) with a certain amount of disbelief . People like my father and I suspect , Tenzing Norgay also , would look at it with a certain amount of regret because they were very fortunate in a way . They were there first and had the mountain to themselves . <p> But having climbed Everest on many occasions and summitted it twice , I can tell you it is a wonderful experience . And for all the people who go there , have a life-changing experience . Climbers on one day had not climbed and had waited for better weather to climb on the next day . Hence , they ended up with a larger group of people going up and got jammed up . But I would hate to see rules and regulations become part of climbing Everest . I would rather like people to use common sense and good management to avoid situations like what happened in May . <p> Many media reports have criticized the Nepal government for the mess , saying that it has issued too many permits to climbers . Would you agree ? <p> I do n't think that is necessarily the case . We need better management of people on the mountain .
