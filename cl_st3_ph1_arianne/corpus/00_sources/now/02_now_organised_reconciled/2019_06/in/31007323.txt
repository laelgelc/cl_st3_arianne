<p> The thought of a book was the last thing on Dhruv Bogra 's mind when he was traversing through mountainous terrain on a bicycle . But when Grit , Gravel and Gear came out in April , the 51-year-old Country Manager for Forever New in India and the Middle East , knew that taking a two-year sabbatical in 2016 for a solo journey from Alaska to Argentina , was worth the effort . <p> What was the key moment when you decided to pick up this arduous journey ? <p> I was on a backpacking expedition in the Chugach mountains of Northern Alaska in 2015 and was fascinated to discover a land that had barely been tread upon . Initially the plan was to cycle in Alaska but soon it became an ambitious plan from North to South America . <p> How did you prepare for it ? <p> It stretched over a year . Not only did I have to train physically and psychologically for the journey but also plan each and every mile over two continents on multiple country-specific maps . It took me many weeks of pitching my tent on the terrace garden of our home to get used to living in a tent instead of sleeping on a comfortable bed in air conditioning . I was doing all this even as I worked long hours and travelled 10 days a month on work . <p> Were you keeping a journal of your trip ? <p> Through Grit , Gravel and Gear , I not only wanted to share a bicycling journey of endurance , mental strength and discovering freedom but also introduce bicycle touring as a form of travel and discovery for people in our country . I wanted the book to become a metaphor for life , filled with exploration , happiness , struggle , hardship , passion , sacrifice and perseverance . I layered it with stories of cultures , people , their craft and beliefs , geology and a commentary on the environment . I chose those geographical segments of the journey that I felt would appeal most to an adventurer , a cyclist , an aspiring tourist and a leisure reader . Eventually we chose to retain all the stories . <p> What were the challenges ? <p> One of the most challenging aspects of travelling on a bicycle is managing basic needs - food and water , in the remotest parts of the world with no support . The other was physically hauling more than 40 kgs of gear , with the bicycle weighing another 20 kgs , up a mountain or a hill . <p> What has been the learning experience from this ride ? <p> The 400 days on the bicycle taught me that we need to challenge our limiting beliefs @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ challenge the system , fiercely protect our animals and forests , that global warming and climate change are real and that the culture and languages of our indigenous people need to be saved . <p> What are your future plans ? <p> I intend to continue my love for exploring the remote corners of the world on my bicycle .
