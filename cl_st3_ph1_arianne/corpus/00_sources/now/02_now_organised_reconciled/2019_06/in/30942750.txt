<p> HYDERABAD : The women of Deccan Development Society sanghams have bagged one of the most coveted UN awards - Equator Prize for 2019 . Since the inception of the awards in 2002 , out of a total 223 awards , only nine awards have been bagged by India so far . " It is a matter of great pride for the entire DDS family that the non-literate , dalit , under-privileged women from DDS sanghams have received this award and are the only Indian group to have done so in 2019 , " said Deccan Development Society P V Satheesh . The award cites DDS sangham women as ' an outstanding example of a local , nature-based solution to climate change and sustainable development . ' This year , a total of 847 nominations were received by the UN from 127 countries all over the world . Of them , only 20 were given this prestigious award . The women of DDS sangham are among them . Over the last 30 years , in their own unsung manner , about 5,000 dalit women of DDS sanghams have made significant contribution towards environment combating climate change , food and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ establishing iconic village level indigenous seed banks and many more . The UNDP Equator prize comes as a crowning glory for all their efforts , Satheesh said . The $10,000 prize will be officially declared on June 5th , the World Environment Day and will be handed over to the DDS community members in New York in September 2019 in a special ceremony at UN Headquarters . On June 5 , 2019 , in a special celebratory event being held in Pastapur village where the Deccan Development Society is situated , five selected women leaders from various sanghams will be felicitated for their signal contribution in the three decadal journey of DDS sanghams from fighting for equality to receiving Equator Prize . The leaders to be felicitated include Maisanagari Ratnamma , leader of Women Sanghams in Algole who was the first rural dalit women recipient of the Vriksha Mitra award from the Ministry of Environment , Government of India in 1993 . Begari Tuljamma from Pastapur , a representative of over 30 DDS sanghams that carried out the revolutionary community designed , community controlled localised Public distribution system in which @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and halted the desertification of farm lands in the Zaheerabad region will be felicitated . <p> Nagwar Sunandamma , representing the Indu DDS Sangham , who was instrumental in turning a 90-acre stony life-less hillock into a lush-green forest planting over 200 thousand trees and contributing to the fight against climate change , and Yerrolla Kanakamma from Machnoor village who for over 25 years has protected a patch of land on which she and her sangham established a sanctuary for rare local medicinal plants , and turned it into a village medicinal commons will also be felicitated . Nadimidoddi Anjamma from Gangwar who is known for saving and conserving nearly a 100 traditional seed varieties and has been a community seed keeper for over 25 years and Chilkapalli Ansuyamma , an amazing green finger who has designed , implemented and inspired several thousands of sangham women to transform wastelands into neighbourhood forests and planted millions of trees are among those who will be felicitated.
