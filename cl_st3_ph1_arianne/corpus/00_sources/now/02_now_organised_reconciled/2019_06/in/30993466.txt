<h> India 's bid to overcome ' Chinese wall ' on countering terrorism <p> That China has much to hide regarding its counterterrorism policies became apparent when it carefully staged a controversial visit by Vladimir Voronkov , a Russian diplomat currently heading the UN Office of Counter-Terrorism ( UNOCT ) , to its restive Xinjiang province . China 's global reputation has already suffered a setback because of its repressive policies toward the Uighur community . Beijing routinely uses the rhetoric of terror to inflate and vilify the threats from the Uighur Muslims . Therefore , despite protests by the United States and Britain , China was desperate to gain international legitimacy for its notorious policies by arranging Voronkov visit . <p> In this backdrop , the Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi 's recent call for a global conference to tackle terrorism assumes significance . The international community has failed to agree on a single definition of terrorism , leading to confusion and application of double standards . China 's official use of the term ' terrorist ' has been reserved almost exclusively for describing those tied to Xinjiang . However , some Chinese government-affiliated experts now characterize the revolts by ethnic Tibetans as terrorism . <p> Even since Xi Jinping has become the president , he has further tightened political control and centralized power , giving indication a resurgent Communist Party . But increasing control does not always mean that he has become more powerful ; it also means increased insecurity . China 's major security concern is the prevailing instability and lawlessness around its periphery . The proximity of these locations to Xinjiang has given rise to fears that Uighur separatists could use these areas as staging grounds for attacks against China or link up with Islamic radicals already operating in Afghanistan and Pakistan . But it is totally unjustified to use Chinese civilian deaths to conflate the peaceful religious practices of Muslim citizens with terrorism . Authoritarian regimes are not expected to follow universally recognized human rights standards . But the brazenness with which China terms human rights violations as counterterrorism operations defies all logic and rationality .
