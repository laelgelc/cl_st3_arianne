<p> OTTAWA -- Canada 's price on carbon will have to be five times what it is now if the country is to reach its Paris Agreement greenhouse-gas emissions targets just by charging for those emissions , Parliament 's budget watchdog says . <p> The current $20-a-tonne federal levy on fuels is set to rise to $50 a tonne by 2022 . It applies in provinces that do n't have equivalent carbon-pricing systems of their own . The Parliamentary Budget Office says that may not be enough . <p> In a new report Thursday , the PBO says an additional levy that goes beyond fuels would have to start at $6 per tonne of emissions in 2023 and rise to $52 by 2030 . <p> Combined with the current federal fuel charge , that would add up to $102 per tonne . The PBO estimates that would mean an additional cost of 23 cents for a litre of gasoline . <p> The report was released the same day Environment Minister Catherine McKenna said the government would enforce the federal carbon tax in Alberta starting next January .
