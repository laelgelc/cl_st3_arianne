In fact , the traditional , centre-left and centre-right alliances will continue to choose the EU 's leaders and to govern Europe -- and the Greens will hold the balance of power . <p> Many fascinating nuggets are buried in the results . Turnout was up almost everywhere , the highest in 25 years . Secondly , both a regional and a generational divide clearly emerged . The old East European nations shifted hard right , most of the Mediterranean nations moved left , as did much of Northern Europe -- but the Greens and the UK Liberal Democrats were big winners . Their efforts to organize their own voting bloc in the European Parliament seem unlikely to be stable , leaving a centrist EU leadership in charge . <p> The poorest performers were the traditional giants in the UK , Germany and France . French socialists and conservatives together polled less than 15 per cent ! British Labour and Conservatives together won less than one quarter of their voters . In Germany , the Greens won more votes than the Social Democrats , who took less than 16 per cent for the first time in history . These are unprecedented collapses in European politics . <p> Are their lessons for Canada is this very mixed outcome ? In Italy it is pro-union and deeply anti-capitalist , in France it has attracted many former Communist voters , frequently on Islamophobic , anti-immigrant messaging .
