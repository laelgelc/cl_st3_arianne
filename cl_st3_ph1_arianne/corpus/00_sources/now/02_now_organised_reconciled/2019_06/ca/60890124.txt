<h> Merlin Nichols : For the sake of science , we need climate change clarity , not hysteria <p> It would be good to have some clarity . But we do n't have it and given the present social and political environment we are not likely to get it any time soon . Such a rare commodity should be high on the agendas of academics , scientists , politicians , law courts , universities , and us ordinary people who depend on the experts to provide the raw material for our opinions -- if not our opinions . <h> related <p> We have been taught since elementary school that science is based on fact , and that fact is immutable . Find the fact and the truth of the matter follows . We have been taught that those who practice the art of science are a cut above the ordinary folk in their ability to wring a fact or a solution or even a reasonable explanation out of the rubble of antiquity . <p> The rest of us have trouble distinguishing fact from fiction . They are just as susceptible to error as we unwashed masses . They filter their observations through the same sieves and view the evidence through the same lenses that the rest of us use : prejudice , greed , envy , competition , opinion , belief , and not a little respect for the one who is paying the bill . Indeed , it is difficult to escape the clutch of humanity even for the sake of science . <p> Too often the conclusions of what we call scientific investigation are shaped and coloured by these filters and lenses . <p> Real science is the stuff of observation , measurement , and replication . When we move beyond the scope of observation , measurement , and the ability to replicate we enter the realm of metaphysics , of speculation , of philosophy , and , dare I say , religion . That is where we are today with much of what we call science . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , about development of species , about the vast reaches of the cosmos , we are definitely beyond the scope of science and into the realm of philosophy . And that 's OK as long as we recognize the limitations of our musings . <p> Those pursuing the " hard " sciences candidly admit their limitations as scientists and the limitations of the tools at their disposal . They see their boundaries more clearly than academics in other disciplines . <p> Frequently , there is a disconcerting gap between the reporting by the researcher in the scientific journals and the story reported to the public through the various media . But it is the media version that gets the public 's attention . It is the media version that captures the imagination of the masses and becomes part of the language of society . <p> The academics pick up from the scientists those ideas that suit their established opinions and make them popular in undergraduate classes in philosophy , anthropology , and other " soft " sciences . Once upon a time , not too many months ago , it was called global warming and the culprit was carbon dioxide , an essential component of all plant life . Green plants absolutely require carbon dioxide for proper growth and the byproduct is pure oxygen , which animals require for life . Remarkable ! Incidentally , the level of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere is currently below that required for optimum plant growth . <p> It seems now that the globe is not warming .
