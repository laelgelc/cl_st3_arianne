<h> Yarmouth do-gooders make a difference with Boomerang Bags <p> YETT member Virginia Smith with the first installation of Boomerang Bags at the Dayton Red &amp; White on the outskirts of Yarmouth . - Sandra Phinney <p> World renowned anthropologist Margaret Mead once said , " Never doubt that a small group of thoughtful , committed , citizens can change the world . Indeed , it is the only thing that ever has . " A group of concerned citizens in Yarmouth are doing just that -- and having a positive impact in their community -- one project at a time . <p> The Yarmouth Environmental Think Tank ( YETT ) formed in September of 2014 . Spurred on by viewing the documentary film titled Revolution by Canadian filmmaker Rob Stewart , Argyle resident Virginia Smith ordered and distributed several of these films to the local high schools in the area . The film examines the looming environmental collapse and what people , especially youth around the world , are doing to avert it . <p> Smith also gathered a group of like-minded adults together to watch the film . That meeting spawned the formation of YETT . Smith @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ We considered that our job was to address the changes we could make in our individual and family behaviours toward reducing our impact on climate change . " <p> In short order , the group started meeting once a month , and continue to do so , every third Thursday evening at the YMCA in Yarmouth . For the past four years YETT members have engaged in projects that put into practice their beliefs about conservation and the importance of protecting the environment . <p> " Two major issues we have discussed in our sessions have been carbon dioxide buildup in the atmosphere , and plastics , " Smith says . " We had a project to reduce CO2 locally by trying to get the town and surrounding areas to discourage idling . " <p> YETT did this by providing car window stickers to the public for a Reduce Idling Campaign . Smith adds , " The local Credit Unions really helped out by putting a ' No Idle ' reminder on their television screens . " <p> As well , members attended a QUEST workshop in Yarmouth . Robinson explains Boomerang Bags started in Australia six years ago as a way to tackle the problem of single-use plastic shopping bags . <p> In a nutshell , people get together and make bags from recycled clothing or fabric remnants . The bags are then distributed to merchants or grocers with signs that encourage people to use one and bring it back . <p> Robinson adds , " The movement is now all over the world . " <p> As stated on boomerangbags.org , the movement is not only about @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ starting conversations , fostering sustainable behaviours , and having fun in the process . <p> With this in mind , YETT members organized a public session last fall in the local library to introduce the idea of making Boomerang Bags . They brought in sewing machines , ironing boards , irons , material , and sewing notions . <p> Eventually , they rounded up more donated equipment and were able to leave everything set up in the top floor in the historic Killam Brothers Building on Water Street , where they now meet the first Saturday of each month from 2-5 p.m . <p> Research shows that every minute of the day , one million plastic bags are used in markets and stores throughout the world . Plastic by the ton-loads ends up in landfills , is blown all over villages and cities , and floats in our waterways and oceans , strangling marine life and causing other environmental problems . <p> YETT is happy to be part of this movement that is making a difference .
