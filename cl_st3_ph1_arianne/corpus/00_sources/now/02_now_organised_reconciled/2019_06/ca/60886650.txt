<h> Island Voices : Fixing climate change begins with a village <p> Victoria council is calling for the introduction of free transit as part of an effort to deal with climate change . <p> Victoria city council has passed a resolution calling for the introduction , over the next several years , of free transit as one way of committing itself to the Climate Emergency Declaration that the Capital Regional District board declared in February . <p> The essence of that resolution is that , in order to begin curbing our local greenhouse gas emissions we begin the process of lowering the greenhouse gases from transportation . They are proposing , over the next several years , to improve and expand our current bus system with the ultimate aim of providing free transportation and lessening our dependence on the use of personal vehicles . <p> With the loss of revenue from bus fares , the transition would not be cheap . <p> This initiative is receiving strong support from young people , but considerably less from older residents , some of whom have registered concerns for the possible impacts on their municipal taxes . <p> I am one such @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ frequently . I do not use the bus that often . Considering the increased assessed value of my home , I am also concerned about rising taxes . <p> And yet , I , like many older people here , have major concerns about the state of a planet whose ecosystem is increasingly stressed on so many levels , almost entirely because of human activities that , instead of being curbed , are increasing . I do not wish to leave this legacy to my grandchildren or to anyone else 's grandchildren . For this reason , I recognize that a massive effort , on economic , social and educational levels must be undertaken if we are to stand a chance of survival as a species and even as a dynamic , biologically diverse , planet . <p> There are arguments that it is wiser to focus our efforts on preparing for climate change by building up defences against it rather than taking measures that would decrease our greenhouse gases . That does little , if anything , to address the challenges we face and is , in fact , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ considering the root causes and offering long term solutions . <p> Using what tools councils have available to them and moving ahead on this agenda beginning with a movement toward free transit , as well as investing in our collective futures by abstaining from investing in the petrochemical industry , will require careful thought and preparation by our local governing bodies . <p> This may require such elements as a medium-term financial and other factor cost-benefit analysis , as well as encouraging people to support this movement away from dependence on their vehicles . But I , for one , am prepared to begin this journey myself , and support our local governments in making these moves . <p> There will be , of course , those naysayers who diminish the importance of what is being proposed by claiming that Canada 's contribution to global warming is of little importance , ignoring the fact that Canada , together with the U.S. and Australia are among the top 10 largest emitters of greenhouse gases in the world . <p> However , we on Vancouver Island have an opportunity to break new @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ by Mahatma Gandhi when he was working to create an independent India , which at the time appeared , to at least some of his own people , to be a hopeless cause . He was approached by a person in one village who asked him : " Gandhiji , it seems to me that my contribution to your cause is of little importance . Why should I bother ? " Gandhi 's response was : " What you are doing may seem unimportant , but it is very important that you do it . " That 's how India won its independence , village by village . We can , and must , do the same with our planet today . <p> David Stott has worked as a community development professional , establishing and overseeing six small to medium sized organizations and 15 small-scale projects and enterprises , first in support of international development initiatives and later , beginning in 1987 , primarily in the fields of sustainable food production , community gardens and affordable housing .
