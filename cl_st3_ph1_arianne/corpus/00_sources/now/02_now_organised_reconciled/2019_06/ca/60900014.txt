Ours is a system of representative and responsible government ; organized , disciplined and competitive political parties are central to our political life . <p> Parties organize and shape public opinion . They provide a forum where citizens can contribute to politics . They recruit political leaders . They perform the roles of government and opposition . One party takes the initiative in terms of proposing legislation and spending , while other parties offer constructive criticisms . <p> In this way , voters have an alternative government in waiting at all times . No one would claim that parties perform any or all of these functions perfectly ; indeed , they are even less admired than politicians . Parties are seen by many to reinforce the worst tendencies of politicians . <p> Here are some of the perceived flaws of politicians : <p> Nearly three-quarters of respondents in a recent poll believed that politicians regularly break their promises . A related complaint is that politicians are not candid about the difficulty and cost of solving major problems , such as climate change or poverty . More than 70 per cent of respondents agree with the statement that their elected representatives quickly lose touch with their concerns . There is a perceived gap between the " bread-and-butter " concerns of voters -- such as economic security -- and the more abstract issues , like the SNC-Lavalin affair , that dominate parliamentary debates and media coverage . <p> The public does not like the excessive partisanship , bickering and negativity that dominate legislatures and media coverage . Rather than the naming , blaming and shaming , voters want politicians to work collaboratively to improve government performance . Illegal or unethical conduct by a small number of elected representatives adds to the negative stereotype of politicians in general . <p> Politicians rightly complain that the media and academics contribute to the lack of knowledge and disillusionment with the politics . Here are some steps they could take to strengthen public trust : <p> Political parties could select leaders who do not follow a " win-at-almost-any-cost " approach . They could improve their processes for vetting party candidates and they could strive to elect more women , knowing that a critical mass of women changes the tone and content of public debates . <p> The leaders of political parties could treat party members as more than just reliable voters and donors , by engaging them in policy development . Leaders could promote a code of responsible conduct for members . This might lead to a more constructive form of partisanship that does not automatically dismiss the criticisms and ideas of political opponents . <p> Leaders and parties could spend less time and money on perpetual campaigning and more on preparing to govern . Finally , politicians could practise more honesty in political messaging , especially by avoiding campaign promises that are too good to resist and impossible to deliver . <p> Politicians will never be loved .
