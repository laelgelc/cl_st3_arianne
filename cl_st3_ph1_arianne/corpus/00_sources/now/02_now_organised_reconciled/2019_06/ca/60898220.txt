( Photo : Mark Albins , Oregon State University ) <p> By KATIE WILLIS <p> A new model is providing insight into the impact of a venomous predatory fish that has invaded more than 7.3 million square kilometres in the Atlantic Ocean and Caribbean Sea , wreaking havoc among native fish populations . <p> Scientists have speculated that the invasive lionfish are so successful in the Atlantic because prey do n't recognize @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the case , according to University of Alberta marine biologist Stephanie Green , who led a study on the lionfish . <p> " We found that reef fish enter the ' danger zone ' -- close enough to be eaten -- around invasive lionfish at similar rates to native predators . But for those prey that stray too close to lionfish , they are up to twice as likely to be captured than by predators that are naturally found on Caribbean reefs , " said Green . <p> The researchers suggest the lionfish 's success as a hunter is likely due to a combination of its particular stalking pattern , mouth suction and forward momentum as it strikes -- characteristics that are unlike native fish predators . <p> The ranges of many predators are expected to grow due to climate change and future invasions . The new model -- a systematic way of looking at behavioural traits -- is designed to help scientists and conservationists better understand how predators select their prey . <p> In the case of lionfish , the scientists hope the model can help them identify areas @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ hunting strategy of lionfish as the invasion spreads . <p> " As invasions take hold , scientists have few tools to help them predict what the effects will be , and as a result , we often do n't understand how invasive predators have changed environments until it is too late , " added Mark Hixon , a professor at the University of Hawai'i and co-author of the study . <p> Hixon and Green hope their approach can be used by researchers in the Mediterranean who are keen to understand how fish in that sea will be most affected by the recent invasion of lionfish . <p> Green is also adapting the model to examine predator-prey interactions for albacore tuna with respect to climate change . <p> " We hope that using knowledge of species behaviours can help scientists and managers predict who will eat whom when predators and prey encounter one another in new settings , " she said .
