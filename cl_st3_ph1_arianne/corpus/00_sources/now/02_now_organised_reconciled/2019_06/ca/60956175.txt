The federal government describes small modular reactors ( SMR ) , as the " next wave of innovation " in nuclear energy technology and an " important technology opportunity for Canada . " <p> Here 's what you need to know about them . <h> What 's a small modular reactor ? <p> Traditional nuclear reactors used in Canada can typically generate about 800 megawatts of electricity , or about enough to power about 600,000 homes at once ( assuming that 1 megawatt can power about 750 homes ) . <p> The International Atomic Energy Agency ( IAEA ) , the UN organization for nuclear co-operation , considers a nuclear reactor to be " small " if it generates under 300 megawatts . <p> Designs for small @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ been submitted to Canada 's nuclear regulator , the Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission , for approval . <p> Such reactors are considered " modular " because they 're designed to work either independently or as modules in a bigger complex ( as is already the case with traditional , larger reactors at most Canadian nuclear power plants . ) A power plant could be expanded incrementally by adding additional modules . <h> How do nuclear reactors generate electricity ? <p> In thermal power plants , the heat turns water into steam , and the steam pushes turbines that generate electricity . That part of the process is the same whether the heat is generated by nuclear power , burning fossil fuels such as coal or natural gas , or even concentrated solar energy . <h> What are the advantages of SMRs over traditional nuclear power plants ? <p> Canada 's federal government says SMRs are designed to have lower upfront capital costs and enhanced safety features compared to traditional reactors . <p> Because of their small size , most could be completely built in a factory and installed module by @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cheaper , according to the World Nuclear Association , which represents the nuclear industry . Upfront costs , especially , would be lower , since modules could be added as needed instead of being paid for all at once . <p> Another feature that is predicted to lower the cost is that it 's easier to cool SMRs because of their larger surface area-to-volume ratio . That means their safety systems do n't need to be as complex . Most can rely on " passive " built-in safety features in the event of a malfunction , rather than special systems that need to be activated . <h> Are there other differences between SMRs and traditional nuclear power plants ? <p> Some SMR designs are effectively scaled-down versions of traditional nuclear reactors , but some also incorporate next-generation nuclear technologies and designs . <p> For example : <p> Molten salt reactors that use molten salt -- salt that has melted into a liquid at high temperatures -- instead of water as a coolant and dissolve the fuel in the salt . That allows them to operate at regular atmospheric pressure instead @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Liquid metal fast reactors use liquid sodium or lead as a coolant . Both molten salt and liquid metal fast reactors can reuse and consume fuel from other reactors . <p> High-temperature gas reactors use an inert gas such as helium as a coolant and can operate at a higher temperature , making them more efficient . <h> What could SMRs be used for ? <p> Remote communities that currently rely on polluting diesel generation . <p> Resource extraction sites , such as mining and oil and gas . <h> What challenges do SMRs face before they 're built ? <p> Licensing costs for an SMR are " potentially a challenge " as they are n't necessarily cheaper than they are for a large reactor , notes the World Nuclear Association . <p> Also , while small modular reactors should theoretically be cheaper than traditional reactors , their actual costs wo n't be known until some designs are actually built and operating , noted Scott Montgomery , an affiliate faculty member at the University of Washington , who lectures and writes about global energy , in an article in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ SMRs are designed to produce less nuclear waste than larger reactors , disposal remains an issue . <p> Canada does n't yet have a permanent nuclear waste repository , although the Nuclear Waste Management Organization is currently working to select a site . <h> How close are SMRs to operating in Canada ? <p> Not that close . <p> Natural Resources Canada released an " SMR roadmap " in November , with a series of recommendations about regulation readiness and waste management for SMRs . <p> In Canada , about a dozen companies are currently in pre-licensing with the Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission ( CNSC ) , which is reviewing their designs . <p> Ultra Safe Nuclear 's Micro Modular Reactor Energy System is designed to fit in a standard shipping container .
