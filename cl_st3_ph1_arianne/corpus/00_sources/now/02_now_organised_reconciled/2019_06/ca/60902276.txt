<h> Uneven road to recovery from sea star wasting disease <p> A newly released study says a combination of warm waters and infectious diseases has been determined as the cause of a die-off of populations of sunflower starfish across the Pacific coast . ( The Canadian Press/Handout-University of California Davis , Janna Nichols ) <p> At the time , it sounded like a plot twist straight out of a science fiction movie . <p> A mysterious disease was sweeping through an entire species of sea creatures , causing their bodies to dissolve and turn into gooey blobs before disintegrating , limbs falling off . <p> But this was not a sci-fi movie . It was the grim reality that impacted 20 sea star species on the West Coast in 2013-14 , decimating populations . <p> Five years later , there is both good and bad news for the future of starfish around the Vancouver Island area . <p> " It 's a mixed bag , " said Alyssa Gehman , a postdoctoral researcher at the Hakai Institute and UBC . <p> Two of the most impacted sea stars , the ochre and the sunflower stars , will be familiar @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the Hakai Institute , ochre sea star populations at many sites in the Salish Sea fell by at least 75 per cent as a result of sea star wasting . <p> However , scientists are starting to see unusually high numbers of juvenile ochre sea stars , leading to hopes they can replenish their populations . <p> It 's a very different story for the sunflower sea star , which was hit even harder . <p> The Hakai Institute found a 96 per cent decline in sunflower sea stars around the central B.C. coast and so far , there have been signs of recovery in just one area . <p> " The heartstring side is where the sunflower sea star is , just a really gorgeous , massive and really powerful sea star . They moved through systems and ate so much of the things in the sub-tidal that all these animals had escape responses from them , " said Gehman . <p> According to a research paper published by the American Association for the Advancement of Science , sunflower stars are an important predator of sea urchins . In @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ west coast , urchin populations have exploded , causing kelp to diminish . <p> As for the cause of the sea star wasting outbreak , there are theories , but no conclusive answers . Scientists are also still trying to figure out why some species were more impacted than others . <p> Gehman has been looking into what role climate change may have played in the outbreak and she said there has been fairly strong evidence that temperature is a factor . <p> " We do n't yet know exactly where in the process but in the lab when we 've done experiments . If you put six stars into warmer water they die faster , disease spreads faster , if you put them into cooler water that actually slows the disease down , " she said . <p> Ultimately , understanding sea star wasting could help humans as well . <p> " The models I use are exactly the same as what you use to study human diseases . By studying how the environment changes the relationship between hosts and their diseases we can actually expand on the literature @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ affects disease in general and that can influence diseases in human populations as well , " said Gehman . <p> For Gehman , studying sea star wasting has been both rewarding and disturbing with particular concern for the sunflower stars . <p> " They are really quite magnificent so to have so many of them die is just a tragedy .
