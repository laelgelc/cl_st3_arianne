<p> Four days in ( at the time of writing ) and so far I 've been on safari in a secret location , watched a disastrous magic show in somebody 's apartment and slow-danced with a man who murmured terrifying facts about climate change in my ear . It can only be the Fringe , Montreal 's annual cornucopia of theatrical oddities , surprises and -- all the more fun for it -- dramatic missteps . <p> The Man Behind the Curtain takes place in Sam 's Apartment ( 3638 St-Laurent Blvd . ) That 's not a modish name for a venue . It really is Sam 's apartment , Sam ( Jameson ) being one of the performers , along with Erik Leisinger . They 've invited Vancouver 's very own legerdemain legend Erin the Magician to perform an impossible feat in Sam 's living room . Things do n't quite go according to plan , but the ramshackle results are kind of an illusion too . Suffice to say , the open-air playing space is expansive enough to double as an African hunting reserve where three crisply written , splendidly performed monologues are delivered against various well-chosen backdrops . There are some startling transitions between close-up action and expansive vistas , filled with herds of imaginatively created " animals . " <p> Wacky puppetry abounds this year , notably in the eye-boggling Multiple Organism ( La Chapelle , 3700 St. Dominique St. ) , in which two women use objects and projections to present a love story between two toothbrushes . Things get amusingly scatological ( a toilet leads us down the rabbit hole to a magical place ) , and the transformation of a naked torso into a goatee-sporting character had the audiences repeatedly cracking up . <p> The unpromisingly-titled puppet show The Carrot : Sex , Sh*t and Death ( Studio Multim ? dia du Conservatoire , 4750 Henri-Julien @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ shows so far . It 's a series of seriously messed-up sketches on the lot of human-and-carrotkind . For all the comic madness , the final image is an almost Beckettian tableau evoking our mortality in an uncaring universe -- but with carrots . <p> Lear in Limbo ( Le P'tit Impro , 3713 St-Laurent Blvd. ) also begins with puppets , accompanying a folk-song summary of Shakespeare 's bleakest play . Unfortunately , this charming opening gives way to a rather soapy drama paralleling Lear 's predicament with that of a Shakespearean scholar who rejects his puppeteer daughter 's life choices before succumbing to Alzheimer 's . The name-recognition gathered an enthusiastic , full-capacity crowd , and the recurring puppet scenes were a delight . Ultimately it was a rather banal riff on that earth-shattering classic , unimaginatively staged . <p> More effective was Tomorrow ( Freestanding Room , 4324 St-Laurent Blvd. ) , a terse multimedia treatment of Macbeth , or at least choice bits of it . It 's earnest and sincere , sometimes cloyingly so . But the musical component soars , the eight young actors ' voices finding a searing emotional range . <p> Edging With Mike ( Le P'tit Impro ) is a one-man show about the joys of knife-sharpening . Surprisingly , it 's a subject that allows our host and demonstrator , Mike O'Brien , to delve deep into science and philosophy .
