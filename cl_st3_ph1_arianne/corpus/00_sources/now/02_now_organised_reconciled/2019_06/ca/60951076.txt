<h> Up to 12 times more smoke due to climate change , warns prof <p> FIRE AND SMOKE -- U of A wildfire Prof. Mike Flannigan spoke to the 2019 Clean Air Forum in Strathcona County Thursday . His research suggests that global heating will double the amount of land burnt each year in Alberta by 2100 , resulting in six to 12 times more forest fire smoke . <p> Alberta can expect to see twice as much forest fire and up to 12 times more forest fire smoke a year by 2100 because of global heating , says a University of Alberta wildfire expert . <p> About 70 people were at the Strathcona County Community Centre Thursday for the 2019 Alberta Capital Airshed Clean Air Forum . This year 's session focused on wildfires . <p> St. Albert does n't usually see wildfires near its borders , but it does get the smoke from them , said Alberta Capital Airshed president Brent Korobanik . Centre for Disease Control . She estimated that a typical " very smoky " day with 100 ? g/cubic meters of particulates in the air would cause four extra deaths and 140 more asthma-related doctor 's visits per million people . <p> Forum-goers learned that particulate levels in parts of Edmonton hit a record 1,880 ? g/cubic metre on May 30 due to forest fire smoke . <h> More heat , more fire <p> U of A wildland fire professor Mike Flannigan showed forum-goers stats and charts on how Alberta was seeing more forest fires now than it did several decades ago . <p> " This is because our weather is changing , and it 's ( changing ) because @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ three things to happen , he explained : fuel , ignition and weather .
