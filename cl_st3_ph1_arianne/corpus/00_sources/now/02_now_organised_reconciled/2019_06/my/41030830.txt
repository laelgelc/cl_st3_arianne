<h> Single-use plastic ban at government events : Sort out practical implications <p> The government should consult those affected on the alternatives to bottled water and plastic-wrapped food at various events , says Kenneth Lee . <p> Recently , the Malaysian government reportedly proposed to ban single-use plastics -- including the distribution of bottled water and plastic-wrapped food -- at government-organised events from 2020 . <p> The proposal came amidst growing concern about the environmental damage caused by the rising amounts of plastic waste , both globally and in the country . Many surveys and reports have pointed to Malaysia being one of the biggest consumers of plastics globally . According to a 2015 study , the country reportedly ranks eighth among the largest producers of mismanaged plastic waste in the world <p> If that is not bad enough , China 's ban on solid waste imports in 2018 has left many countries around the world looking for an alternative dumping ground -- and Malaysia finds itself becoming one such site . <p> Our waste imports often contain the kind of plastic that is contaminated , mixed and of low grade . To put things into perspective , one tonne is equivalent to 1,000 kilograms . <p> It is commendable that the Ministry of Energy , Science , Technology , Environment and Climate Change is going all out on issues and policies pertaining to plastic waste in the country . At the microscopic level , the ministry must also be lauded that government agencies are starting to prepare for Malaysia 's roadmap towards zero single-use plastics by 2030 . <p> But the effort towards zero single-use plastics is not just about pushing the idea out without carefully considering or consulting relevant stakeholders on its practical implications . Questions on what practical alternatives to bottled water and plastic-wrapped food -- and how the policy will affect existing practices -- need to be thought through . <p> There are many types of plastic items .
