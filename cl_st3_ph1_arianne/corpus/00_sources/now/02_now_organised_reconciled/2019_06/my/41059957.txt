<h> Lynas must remove its radioactive wastes from Malaysia <p> LETTER Recently , the general manager of Lynas , Ismail Bahari , announced that its wastes can be used as a fertiliser or soil conditioner ( Condisoil ) . He said that the Water Leach Purification ( WLP ) " residue " contains a very low level of radioactive material , equivalent to naturally occurring levels . Hence , the waste produced need not go to waste and can provide the agriculture sector with an alternative supply of nutrients essential for plants . <p> This description of Lynas 's WLP which is a naturally occurring radioactive material was also repeated by the Australian High Commissioner to Malaysia three days ago . <p> In February 2015 , the Department of Environment approved Lynas 's proposal to construct a landfill within its premises to store its Neutralisation Underflow ( NUF ) waste which had been classified as scheduled waste . This approval required Lynas to submit an EIA to the DOE which it did on Feb 15 , 2019 . <p> NUF is historically categorised as radioactive waste materials under AELB . However , the Atomic Energy Licensing ( Radioactive Waste @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 274 gazetted on Aug 16 , 2011 , conveniently created new provisions which allowed the reuse and recycling of radioactive materials . <p> This in effect gave a licensee a carte blanche to manage radioactive wastes upon approval from the AELB . The P. U. ( A ) 274 regulations do not clearly set limits or standards for exemption of radioactive wastes based on radiation exposure that is allowed for individuals unlike the International Atomic Energy Agency 's General Safety Guide ( IAEA-GSG ) . <p> AELB has arbitrarily set its own safety standards for radiation exposure which are not according to the international standard . The AELB standards have been used to exempt and clear Lynas 's radioactive wastes for reuse and recycling . <p> Lynas ' Radioactive Waste Management Plan ( RWMP ) labels its radioactive wastes as " residues " as it was planning to commercialise them and cites the P.U . This came to fruition when an application by Lynas in July 2013 resulted in AELB releasing the NUF from its control as Lynas was said to have duly complied with the requirements of the AELB and DOE . <p> However , the DOE Scheduled Wastes list has no category for the kind of wastes that Lynas is producing i.e. radioactive wastes containing uranium and thorium . Due to the omission of radioactive contaminants in the DOE regulations , the operating licence does not require any monitoring or discharge limits for them . All of them , except lead , are radioactive . The most dangerous ones are the radium elements . In an undisturbed ore , the entire elements in the decay chain stick together , as in a family . The radium elements need stronger containment and will have to be kept in isolation from the environment for a minimum of 200 years . <p> There are three streams of wastes in Lamp . The bulk waste contains gypsum , magnesium hydroxide and silica which should be disposed of in the normal way with regard to the pH and concentration of non-radioactive heavy metals . The second stream is thorium-uranium and the third stream is radium-mesothorium . The AELB has lumped all the three streams together . These different streams are created in different processes and have to be seen as different entities .
