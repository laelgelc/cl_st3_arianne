In February 1984 , the National Geographic ran a multi-page report documenting the impacts of the 1982 El Nino . <p> But El Nino is not a new phenomenon . Centuries ago , Peruvian fishermen observed a periodic warming of the ocean along the South American coast - usually a rich fishing ground - which emptied their nets of fish . <p> As this usually occurred around Christmas , they named the phenomenon El Nino , a reference to the birth of Jesus Christ . <p> Mr Rodney Martinez , international director for the International Research Centre on El Nino , or Ciifen , says the term has since been @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ was not a bad thing , he says , although it may cause confusion since the same name was also used to refer to phenomena of different scales . <p> " For Peruvians and Ecuadorians , El Nino is a local event that happens when there is a sustained warming of the ocean that brings heavy rainfall in some places , " he tells The Sunday Times at his office in Guayaquil , Ecuador . <p> Today , El Nino is better known by the international community as a global phenomenon , with a footprint spanning the tropical Pacific Ocean . It can also affect weather beyond the Pacific basin through links known as teleconnections . <p> On a broader scale , El Nino refers to the warm phase of the El Nino-Southern Oscillation , one of the world 's largest natural climate cycles that influences sea surface temperatures and rainfall patterns . <p> The cool phase , known as La Nina , has an opposite effect - causing more rain in the west , and drier conditions in the east . <h> THE CLIMATIC SEE-SAW <p> The Pacific Ocean @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ it is not a uniform body of water , and the differences influence the climate of various regions . <p> Under normal conditions , the western Pacific is much warmer than the east . <p> The reason lies in the way the winds blow . <p> In the tropical Pacific Ocean , winds mainly blow from east to west . These predictable trade winds , as they are known , keeps warm water confined around the maritime continent . <p> The waters warm as they move across the Pacific Ocean , eventually pooling around the maritime continent . Says Dr Xie : " Evaporating warm water fuels the rain clouds in the western part of the tropical Pacific . That explains why Singapore and Indonesia are blessed with a lot of rainfall . " <p> Things are different in the east . <p> When the trade winds blow , they push water away from the South American coast . This causes water from the depths of the ocean to well up towards the surface . <p> Deep waters are cooler , and they contain many nutrients from sinking organic matter @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ stimulate the oceanic food web . This upwelling is one of the reasons why some of the world 's richest fisheries can be found along the South American coast . <p> But cooler waters also cool the atmosphere above it , causing air to sink and creating an area of high pressure . This makes it harder for rain clouds to form . <p> When an El Nino hits , on average every three to four years , it disrupts this status quo . Scientists believe the weakening of the trade winds may be what kick-starts this process , although the exact trigger is still being studied . <p> As the trade winds weaken , the pool of warm water in the west is no longer confined to that region , and a tongue of warm water now extends from west to east . <p> As the warm water moves towards the central and eastern Pacific Ocean , the rain clouds follow . <h> Reducing impact of extreme events <p> More warm water in the east pushes the cooler waters to deeper depths , and what upwells to the surface @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and is why El Nino impacts can stretch over months . <p> As the oceans warm and heavy rain falls , native wildlife in the eastern Pacific can be severely affected . <p> In the Galapagos Islands , birthplace of Charles Darwin 's Theory of Evolution , the population of the world 's only equator-dwelling penguins was almost wiped out in the aftermath of the 1982 El Nino . <p> The event had cut off the supply of nutrient-rich deep , cool water , causing a collapse of the oceanic food web and organisms reliant on it , such as the penguins . <p> Says Charles Darwin Research Station sea bird researcher Gustavo Jimenez : " On average their numbers range between 2,000 and 4,000 penguins , but after that event , we counted just 300 .
