<h> Revealed : Singapore 's strategic secrets for staying ahead <p> The great military strategist Clausewitz once said that leaders must " bring to the task the quality of intuition that perceives the truth at every point . " In other words , they must imagine many different outcomes -- and ensure that they are prepared . <p> How can leaders do this when the world is increasingly volatile ? When fake news and ' deep fakes ' can cloud our judgement of reality ? When even the most certain opinion poll can shift within minutes to the opposite outcome ? <p> This is the challenge facing a unit at the heart of Singapore Government . Nestled in the Prime Minister 's Office , the Centre for Strategic Futures must think of the many possible outcomes for Singapore in the next 50 years . The problem , Ho explains , is human nature . Economists refer to ' bounded rationality ' , with human brains only able to consider a finite amount of information and make a decision . Climate change is a whole series of interconnected problems , for example . <p> We also suffer from another flaw known as ' hyberbolic discounting ' . This simply means that " people are reluctant to invest in the long term , " Ho explains . " They would prefer to focus on the short term because of the obvious payoffs " . This is true in politics where there is no credit given for something achieved in the future , and no credit at all for problems that were avoided . <p> Ho has added his own theory to the discipline , which he terms " black elephants " .
