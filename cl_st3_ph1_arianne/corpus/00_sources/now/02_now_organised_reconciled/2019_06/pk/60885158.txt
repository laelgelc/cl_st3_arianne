<p> Mountains do n't speak , but when hurt by humans they respond in equal terms . Last week , the Mountain Everest took lives of as many as eight climbers . Given heavy traffic of climbers the victims were stuck at the ' death zone ' , and as they waited their supplemental oxygen ran out . The area above 26,000 feet is called the ' death zone ' where air is thin and survival without oxygen is extremely difficult if not impossible . They were among the hundreds of climbers who waited for their chance to stand at top of the world . The government of Nepal , for which the Mount Everest is a gold mine , had issued record 381 permits costing $11,000 each for the current spring climbing season . But relatively small window of suitable weather triggered rush to the peak causing hours-long traffic-jams on the last leg of journey to the top . While Nepal is ' generous ' in issuing permits China on its side of the Mount Everest is quite circumspect in offering this opportunity to the so-called ultimate mountain climbers . It was enlisted in 1979 as Unesco World Heritage site . But commercial mountaineering has turned it into the world 's highest rubbish dump . Its pristine glory has been vandalized and all around on it there is ugly footprint of unwanted human intrusion . There are remnants of fluorescent tents , discarded climbing equipment , empty gas canisters and human feces . It is not true that the governments on both sides of this highest mountain are oblivious @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ They have put in place some measures to discourage climbers from dumping garbage on way up or down the peak . Nepal charges $1000 for each kilogram of material that is taken on way up but is not brought back . The record however suggests that quite a big number of climbers prefer to pay the fine instead of taking the trouble of returning it . But on the Chinese side , the rule is stricter ; the climbers are required to bring down the same amount they took with them . <p> Given growing human propensity to challenge the impossible the Mount Everest and many other high peaks are expected to receive ever more climbers . And , it is also quite likely that mere fines would n't stop them leaving behind their ugly footprints .
