' In her article , Kate argued that representatives of the world 's youth should be invited to a General Assembly to reimagine the UN Charter for the 21st Century . <p> The submissions were judged by Lord Jim O'Neill , Chair of Chatham House , Gideon Rachman , Chief Foreign Affairs Commentator of the FT , and Jayathma Wickramanayake , the UN Secretary-General 's Envoy on Youth . <p> Kate was presented with her award at the Chatham House London Conference on June 14 , by Lord Jim O'Neill . <p> The judges praised Kate 's article for the boldness and imaginativeness of her proposal to address flaws in the UN system . <p> It is 2019 , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ purpose . Therefore if I was UN secretary-general for a day , my first action would be to convene a General Assembly of the young -- for it is the youth who have the greatest stake in the future -- and I would ask them to revisit the UN charter with fresh eyes , writes Kate Anderson . <p> The duty of the UN , as defined in 1945 , was to " save succeeding generations from the scourge of war " . In 2019 , while this scourge still prevails , global conflagration is no longer the most pressing threat ; instead , the clear and present dangers to humanity include climate change , a rise in dictatorships , autocracies and pandemics . What is needed is a global body that truly represents the people . As of 2018 , 42 per cent of the world 's population are under the age of 25 . This is the largest number of young people ever recorded and it creates unprecedented potential for political , economic and social progress .
