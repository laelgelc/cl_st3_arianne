Picture : Howard Crowdy <p> SOCIAL BOOKMARKS <h> Electric cars and more cycle lanes in Government plan to ' save our planet ' <p> By Michelle Devane <p> THE Government 's " bold and ambitious " climate action plan will nudge people and businesses to change behaviour , the Taoiseach has said . <p> Leo Varadkar said the plan represented a call to action in the fight to save the planet . <p> The Government published its plan to tackle climate breakdown yesterday , outlining how Ireland will reduce its overall carbon emissions by 20% by 2030 and become carbon-neutral by 2050 . <p> The plan includes more than 180 measures , including incentives to encourage people to switch from petrol and diesel vehicles to electric vehicles . <p> A nationwide charging system for electric vehicles is to be set up and legislation would ban the sale of petrol and diesel cars from 2030 . <p> A retrofit programme for homes is also set to be implemented . <p> Mr Varadkar said : " This plan represents the sum of our hopes for the future @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ our planet . " <p> The actions in the plan include : <p> : : Eliminate non-recyclable plastic and impose higher fees on the production of materials which are difficult to recycle ; <p> : : Establish a microgeneration scheme , allowing homeowners to generate their own electricity and sell what they do not use back to the national grid ; <p> : : Move to 70% renewable electricity by 2030 ; currently only 30% of Ireland 's electricity comes from renewable sources ; <p> : : Bring 950,000 electric vehicles on to Irish roads , deliver a nationwide charging network , an electric vehicle scrappage scheme , and legislation to ban the sale of petrol/diesel cars from 2030 ; <p> : : Expand network of cycling paths and park and ride facilities ; <p> : : Deliver a plan to retrofit 500,000 homes , with large groups of houses being retrofitted by the same contractor to reduce costs , smart finance , and easy pay-back . <p> However , Labour 's Cork East TD Sean Sherlock criticised aspects of the proposal , saying Fine Gael had abandoned the international @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> He said : " The Oireachtas committee on climate action endorsed the findings of the world 's leading international experts who said that all countries need to reduce their greenhouse gas emissions by 45% by 2030 . For Ireland , that means reducing our emissions from 60 million tonnes at present to 33 million tonnes by 2030 . <p> " If the overall target is anything less than reducing our carbon dioxide emissions to 33 million tonnes by 2030 , the Government 's plan has fallen at the first hurdle .
