And how will it be paid for ? <p> The agreement was drafted by the four parties in Dublin City Council 's new " voting pact " or new " ruling group " : Fianna F ? il ( which has the most seats ) , the Green Party , the Labour Party , and the Social Democrats . <p> Close-readers will notice the different shades of language used in those action points -- from promises that they " will put in place " or " will ban " this or that , to promises " to champion " or " to seek to " do something . <p> Since local councillors are often dependent on funding from the central government , or buy-in from city managers , what they are able to get done can depend on whether those players are on board , too . <h> A Taste of the Plans <p> Plans to tackle climate @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ emissions by 2030 and the achievement of a " carbon-free city " by 2050 . The council will publish an annual audit of its carbon footprint , the agreement says . <p> How will the council accomplish these things ? Concrete measures include putting in place a strategic policy committee for climate , working with the central government on district-heating systems , starting with the Poolbeg Peninsula , and retrofitting council buildings to make them more energy-efficient . <p> Under the housing heading , the agreement recognises that everyone in Dublin is " entitled to a suitable , affordable and sustainable home " . It commits to not selling off public land to developers -- but with a caveat : unless the financial benefit to the council outweighs the social and economic benefit of having social housing and other public uses on the site . <p> Some of the action points essentially promise to implement existing rules : applying the vacant site levy to empty sites , or using compulsory purchase orders . <p> The agreement says councillors will also seek to take waste-collection services back under the control of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ councillors numerous times this month . <p> In terms of transport , the plans seek to introduce a street warden to more strictly enforce rules on cycle lanes and parking on footpaths . <p> The agreement also says councillors want to work towards " free public transport for all " and establish a democratically-elected Dublin Transport Authority . <p> In terms of arts , heritage and sports , the agreement says the council will seek to appoint a " night mayor " who will " enhance " the city 's nighttime culture . <p> It also says councillors will " work to deliver 5% of cultural , creative community space within the city " , and to establish further options for studios in the city , including 40 artists ' studios in the planned Poolbeg West development . <h> Lack of Progress ? <p> Some councillors , like Patrick Costello from the Green Party , say that they 're frustrated with what they saw as a lack of achievements by the last council . <p> " We found that the conversations we were having with parties were n't very policy-driven @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ during the last council 's five-year term involved Sinn F ? in ( which had the largest number of seats at 16 ) , Labour , the Green Party and some independents . <p> The results of the local elections last month shifted the constellation of councillors sitting in the chamber in City Hall , and with it , the ruling coalition .
