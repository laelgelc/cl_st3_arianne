<h> Fasihah Hasan : Angry youth will save SA <p> Fasihah Hasan came to the fore during the #FeesMustFall movement . Picture : Tracy Lee Stark <h> Millenials have a more urgent and immediate purpose to change what is wrong in South Africa . <p> Young radicalism is the future of democratic social justice , if the new wave of young political activists becoming lawmakers is anything to go by . <p> And for " born-free " politician Fasihah Hassan , who was acclaimed for her role during the nationwide youth revolt for free tertiary education , an angry and impatient youth is the best thing for South Africa 's democracy . <p> Born to United Democratic Front ( UDF ) activist parents at the dawn of democracy in 1994 , Hassan says she and her peers have a different and necessary perspective on the ills that face the country . It 's the more urgent and immediate sense of purpose that drove the #FeesMustFall movement . <p> " The world is not a fair place and it 's our job to make it more fair . But once you understand that the world is unfair you understand that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ needs to be channelled , " she says . <p> Speaking just days before her inauguration as an ANC member of the Gauteng legislature , Hassan says making the transition from student activist to politician , to policy maker has not been an easy road to travel . <p> " It has been a long and difficult journey which people do n't see . People see the front of the house . They do n't see what 's going on behind the scenes back in the kitchen , so to speak . But I seriously believe the kind of student activism we were doing before and during #FeesMustFall has played a huge role in shaping how I think and deal with the issues . " <p> As an ANC Youth League branch leader herself , Hassan is not afraid to criticise the organisation that moulded her , saying it was necessary for change in the ANC to be driven from within . <p> " The difficulty with the older generation is that they see the issues , but they would rather only bring it up internally . Whereas with us @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in the ANC , it does n't prevent me from criticising where I have seen wrong . " <p> And with the layers and layers of internal structures , age groups and the gender factor , a young woman might not have risen so quickly into leadership in the ANC 10 years ago , but the ANC elders are beginning to hear young people , Hassan says . She admits that some of the youth disillusionment at the ANC evidenced in the last two elections could be attributed to the weakening of the ANCYL over the past decade . <p> However , she says channels for young people to be heard within the mother body have improved and have put young women in more meaningful platforms , such as the Young Women 's Desk . <p> " When we talk about renewal , it 's not just about the president , it 's from the bottom up . And in terms of us renewing youth structures , I do n't think a lot of people know about the Young Women 's Desk , but it is an incredible structure of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ league . " <p> So , what can young people bring to policy and law making , now that #FeesMustFall heroes have entered parliament and legislature ? A sense of urgency , she says . <p> " I am part of this millenial generation and often hear criticisms of ' you guys just want instant gratification . You guys are so impatient -- you do n't want to work for things ' . And my honest answer to that is that impatience is what forced change in the #FeesMustFall movement and brought us free education . It was because we were not willing to accept that this is okay . " <p> Young people also tend to have a more nuanced understanding of issues such as globalisation , technology and climate change and how it affects them , she says . <p> " If you look at climate change , when we take the land back and our climate changes and the land is not arable , that piece of land will mean nothing to us . It 's not this far-fetched elitist concept .
