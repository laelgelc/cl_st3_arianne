<h> Penguins could become less resistant to parasites as fish resources shrink <p> Cape Town -- Dwindling fish sources could see birds such as penguins become less able to resist parasites . <p> Dr Marcela Espinaze , from Stellenbosch University , knows more than the average person about the ticks and fleas that are naturally part of an African penguin 's life . <p> According to her , while healthy birds can keep the parasites on them in check , changes in their environment can weaken their resistance . <p> Espinaze researched the topic as a PhD student in conservation ecology . She analysed five penguin colonies along the south-western Cape coast : land-based colonies in Simon 's Town and at Stony Point near Betty 's Bay , and island colonies on Dassen Island , Robben Island and Dyer Island . <p> The types of fleas and ticks that were recorded did not surprise Espinaze as they were in line with the parasites normally associated with penguins . <p> Overall , chicks were found to be more infested than older adults . <p> " This might have to do with the fact that they have a weaker immune system @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ on the mainland tended to have more ticks and fleas per animal compared to islands . <p> " Soft ticks and fleas are nest parasites and this pattern is related to the larger number of nests that are currently found at the mainland colonies , " said Espinaze . <p> In her research she picked fleas and ticks off 210 adult penguins and 583 chicks , and also investigated the parasite contents of 628 nests . <p> " Each type of nest creates a different type of microclimate and influences the temperature , ventilation and moisture experienced by the resident birds <p> " However , this microclimate is also important for the development of nest parasites , and drier and warmer nests tend to have more parasites , for instance . <p> " If birds are healthy enough and environmental conditions are normal , then the birds are quite able to keep the number of parasites found on them in check through their usual preening . <p> " When nature is out of sync , as is being experienced currently because of dwindling fish sources , pollution and climate change @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and less likely to resist parasites , " Espinaze said .
