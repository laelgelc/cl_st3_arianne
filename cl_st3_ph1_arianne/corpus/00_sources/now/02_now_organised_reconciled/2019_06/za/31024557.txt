<h> How SA 's coastal forests could help turn the tide on climate change <p> 27 June 2019 - 10:59 By Sage Seef <p> Erica Smithwick , professor of geography and director of the Centre for Landscape Dynamics at Pennsylvania State University , measures trees to quantify carbon stocks in the Dwesa-Cwebe nature reserve in the Eastern Cape . Image : Supplied <p> SA 's indigenous coastal forests are a massive carbon sink that could play a key role in teaching the world how to combat global warming . <p> Geography professor Erica Smithwick , of Pennsylvania State University in the US , analysed the carbon content of forests in the 5,450ha Dwesa-Cwebe Nature Reserve in the Eastern Cape . <p> Her research , published in the journal Carbon Management , showed that coastal indigenous forests are able to store a considerable amount of carbon . <p> " As we think of pathways for reducing atmospheric carbon dioxide concentrations , one of the available approaches is to use the natural world as a sponge , " she said . <p> In 2011 , Smithwick tagged and measured trees in the reserve .
