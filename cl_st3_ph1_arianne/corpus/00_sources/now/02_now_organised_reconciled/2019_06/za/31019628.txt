<h> Simplifying languages used by experts could help climate change <p> Pretoria - Simplifying languages used by experts to relay messages about sustainability issues such as climate change would go a long way in eliciting positive responses from the public . <p> This was the plea by Tshwane mayor Stevens Mokgalapa yesterday during the closing of the fifth African Capital Cities Sustainability Forum at the CSIR . <p> He urged academics to ensure that the manner of unravelling climate change phenomenon , for example , was easy to understand . <p> The easy to understand language , he said , would make climate change issues resonate with people . <p> " That will make it clear to people that climate change is about keeping the city clean . Switching off plugs when you are not using them ; that 's where the academics come in , " Mokgalapa said . <p> The exclusion of young people from programmes of sustainability was also at the centre of discussions among more than 400 delegates . <p> Mokgalapa called for cities to include young people in sustainability programmes with a view to bridge the disconnection between the older generation and them . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the needs of the people was highlighted as one of the significant issues . <p> MMC for Finance Mare-Lise Fourie , who participated in a panel discussing finance and governance for sustainability cities , said cities were the custodians of public assets . <p> " We are not here because we are important but because we serve the needs of the people . " <p> She highlighted the importance of incorporating good ethical values into governance by municipality leaders . The values for good governance included transparency in handling the public purse and competence in doing work needed to be achieved . <p> One of the panellists , Carl Barnadac , who is deputy regional director at the French Development Agency , said his organisation worked together with municipalities to come up with sustainability solutions . <p> Through partnerships , he said , they were able to foster dialogues such as transformation and the inclusion of other people . They also worked with cities to stimulate entrepreneurship . <p> Dr Benni Lekubu , senior academic at Unisa 's College of Law , said the elephant in the room was ethical @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ offices are these people ethical ? Are they responsible to focus on their constitutional obligations ? " he asked . <p> Mokgalapa said African cities needed to devise solutions to their own problems .
