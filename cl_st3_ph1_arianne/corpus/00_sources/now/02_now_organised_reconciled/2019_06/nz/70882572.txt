<p> A graduate who grew up " surrounded by the ocean " hopes a Fulbright award to study it further , will help provide ways to adapt to warming seas . <p> Mitchell Chandler from Nelson is about to start a PhD in physical oceanography in America , having become one of eight graduates in New Zealand to be granted the Fulbright Science and Innovation Graduate Award . <p> The 22-year-old from Stoke hoped to focus on ocean currents or ocean warming , or to combine the two , when he began his studies at Scripps Institute of Oceanography at the University of California San Diego in July . <p> " I just find it fascinating how it 's changing . We know so little about the ocean , there are always opportunities to learn there . " <p> Chandler heads to San Diego having graduated with a BSC ( Hons ) in geophysics from the University of Auckland in 2018 , and a BSc in oceanography from the University of Otago the year before that . <p> supplied <p> Nelson oceanography graduate Mitchell Chandler , pictured with his sister Holly , and parents at Parliament on Monday , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Award . <p> He traced his love for the environment , and a specific interest in science and water , back to his time as a student at Nayland primary school , when he took part in the Poorman Valley Stream eco group . <p> Becoming an " environmental house captain " at Nayland College , he went on to be awarded the inaugural national Sir Peter Blake Environmental Leader Award in 2010 , and in 2014 was one of 12 students who travelled to New Zealand 's subantarctic islands with the Sir Peter Blake Trust , to look at research for a climate change research station . <p> Studying an ocean current along the Fiordland coast that " no one had really looked at before " while at the University of Auckland , added fuel to his passion . <p> " Pretty much everything that we were finding out was new , and that was really exciting to me , " Chandler said . <p> " I think it 's obvious that we as a human race are causing issues with the environment . <p> " For New Zealand @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ it exerts a very strong control on our day-to-day life . So being able to understand how it 's changing , can then lead on to how it might impact us . " <p> Quantifying the changes could predict how they may change in the future , and help come up with strategies to either mitigate or adapt to them , he said . <p> Chandler was presented his award at a ceremony at Parliament on Monday . <p> The annual Science and Innovation graduate awards are offered in partnership with the Science and Innovation group at Ministry of Business Innovation and Employment ( MBIE ) and are valued at up to US$40,000 ( plus NZ$4,000 travel funding ) for up to one year of study or research in the US . <p> Students undertaking multi-year Masters or PhD degrees had the opportunity to apply for additional funding of up to US$30,000 towards their second year of study .
