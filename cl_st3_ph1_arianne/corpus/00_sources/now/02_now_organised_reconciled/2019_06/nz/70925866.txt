<h> We need a radical shift in our education system to tackle climate change <p> Students are interested in the issue of climate change - but should it be part of the school curriculum . <p> OPINION : A recent Stuff article by Marty Sharpe explored the question of whether or not climate change should be made a stand-alone subject in the school curriculum . It made me reflect on whether teaching about climate change is enough . It also made me think about the assumption that education is a panacea for all ills . The issue is so much more complex . <p> We need to address the myth that " teachers teach , and students learn " . Good teaching and learning is a dialogical and active process . Simple knowledge transfer will be ineffective in terms of driving the kind of learning that is going to make a difference for our planet or students . <p> For learning to be meaningful students need to be engaged and interested . We know many students are interested in the issue of climate change . There is a sense of urgency with this issue . <p> Our current curriculum provides some opportunities for students to learn meaningfully about climate change . Inquiry based research by students allows them to follow their own paths in researching the issue , and engage in group problem solving , values exploration and decision making . These learning processes are important - but they are not enough . <p> It seems to me that if we are to develop a future citizenry with the capabilities to take action on climate change we need a radical shift in education that goes beyond the status quo . If we are going to address the complex and multifaceted issues associated with climate change and human survival , we need to rethink education . <p> Western education systems are based on the assumption that with enough knowledge and technology we can solve all problems and manage the Earth -- including climate change . We ca n't . At least not within the current system . We have gone so far down the track with climate change now that we absolutely need to radically alter our relationship with the Earth . And this requires the kind of education that empowers students to critique the current social order and economic system and re-imagine ourselves . <p> Empowering education fosters in students an ability to think critically and deconstruct power relations in wider society . In doing so students can begin to question the myth of human supremacy that is at the root of our current environmental issues and that is embedded in Western thought . This needs to be the focus of any education about climate change . <p> Yet at schools human superiority is assumed , and the technological/managerial mentality is encouraged .
