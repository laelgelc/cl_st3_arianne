<h> Debate over methane target in new carbon legislation <p> The government 's decision to set a target for methane reduction is not based on science , the National Party says . <p> In its Zero Carbon Bill , the government proposes that biological methane emissions should be reduced by 10 percent by 2030 , and - provisionally - by between 24 and 47 percent by 2050 . <p> National Party climate change spokesperson Todd Muller said setting the latter target went against the advice of the Climate Change Chief Executives ' Board and the Parliamentary Commissioner for the Environment . <p> " This target around where we want to anchor the trajectory of this country over the next 20 years . . . we have to make sure that 's underpinned by science . <p> " The National Party strongly believes that what 's been landed certainly does n't deliver against that . " <p> However , Climate Change Minister James Shaw has defended the longer-term target , saying it was based on the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change 's scenarios for living within 1.5 degrees of global warming . <p> " That 's where a lot of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and the Parliamentary Commissioner for the Environment . . . the reports were solid but they were based on different assumptions . <p> " And that 's why we did n't use them because they did n't actually meet that requirement of living within 1.5 degrees . " <p> Mr Shaw said a nominal level had to be set to give the agricultural sector and the wider economy some certainty . <p> The Zero Carbon Bill requires the Climate Change Commission to report back by 2024 with a specific number .
