<h> Reversing the trend of degradation <p> Environment Minister David Parker recently told a Forest &amp; Bird meeting about the Productive and Sustainable Land Use package unveiled in the Budget . Here are excerpts from his speech . <p> The Productive and Sustainable Land Use Package is a key Budget initiative committing $229 million over four years . <p> This will help us tackle the environmental issues New Zealanders in rural and urban areas care about . It will support the primary sector cornerstone of our economy to both transition and increase the value of its exports . <p> It will help us reverse the trend of degradation and manage the transition as efficiently as possible . <p> Our water , land and climate are all under pressure . This year the Government is making significant decisions on the framework for addressing these issues . <p> My ministerial responsibilities include water , and I am personally committed to improving water quality and ecosystem health . <p> It is more cost effective to stop catchments deteriorating than to restore them once they are degraded . Many communities are ready to protect waterways but some need help to make this possible @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in lakes , rivers and wetlands most at risk . <p> This funding is for people on the ground to facilitate , leverage and accelerate community led action . It is not for grants to plant trees . There are other local and central government funds already available for that . This funding is for trying new approaches and overcoming hurdles preventing action . <p> Improving the way we use land can have significant benefits for the health of our waterways and contribute to our climate change goals while supporting our communities and businesses . Reducing polluting outputs can also reduce the waste of costly inputs . <p> My colleague Damien O'Connor and I agree that the future for the primary sector in NZ lies in more value rather than more volume . <p> Many in the primary sector are already responding to market signals and meeting consumer and public demand for more sustainable , high value products . <p> For those progressive farmers and growers , the transition to sustainable land use is already underway .
