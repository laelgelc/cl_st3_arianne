<h> Hort 's turn to feed the world <p> It will be horticulture the world turns to in the quest to feed the world in a sustainable way , a Trans-Tasman conference in Melbourne has heard . <p> More than 3500 people are attending the HortConnections Conference in Melbourne this week which is a 20% increase on last year 's " fantastic " attendance , says James Whiteside of AusVeg . <p> The conference is jointly hosted by the Produce Marketing Association Australia-New Zealand and AusVeg and a strong contingent of New Zealand delegates are attending . The trade fair will have over 195 companies exhibiting . <p> The event was jointly opened on Monday evening by PMA ANZ chairman Michael Franks , who is the New Zealand based chief executive of Seeka , and Bill Bulmer , chairman of AusVeg . <p> The conference theme is Growing our Food Future . The programme will challenge thinking on how the industry will look in 20 years time , Whiteside told the first session . <p> Delegates will look at questions on how to respond to the challenges of feeding our fair share of the world and how we will @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ says , <p> This includes climate change , sustainability and all things environmental , but also rapidly changing consumer sentiment and power . <p> " It is going to be fascinating to look at who sees these opportunities first . How will our food be grown ; how and where will the capital needed to deploy these changes be found and what will the successful farm look like , " he says , <p> He says it is horticulture that the world will turn to . <p> " Our future is not in beef production , it is not in chicken or dairy . " Other sectors have environmental and logistical challenges that much of horticulture should be able to dodge . <p> There will be a " relentless wave of environmental and social issues that are going to push consumers our way " . <p> These are the opportunities we need to be preparing ourselves for , he says .
