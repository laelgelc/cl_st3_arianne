<p> There is a dirty little secret in economics today : the United States has benefited -- and continues to benefit -- from the global slump . The US economy is humming along , even while protesters in the United Kingdom hurl milkshakes at Brexiteers , French President Emmanuel Macron confronts nihilist yellow-vested marchers , and Chinese tech firms such as Huawei fear being frozen out of foreign markets . <p> But relatively strong US growth amid sluggishness elsewhere is not what economics textbooks would predict . Whatever happened to the tightly integrated world economy that the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank have been advocating -- and more recently extolling -- since World War II ? <p> The US economy is in a temporary but potent phase in which weakness abroad lifts spirits at home . But this economic euphoria has nothing to do with Trump-era spite and malice , and much to do with interest rates . <p> Borrowing costs are currently lower than at any time since the founding of the US Federal Reserve in 1913 , or in the UK 's case since the Bank of England was established in 1694 . For starters , American consumers , whose real ( inflation-adjusted ) wages are finally increasing after decades of stagnation , are seeing all sorts of bargains .
