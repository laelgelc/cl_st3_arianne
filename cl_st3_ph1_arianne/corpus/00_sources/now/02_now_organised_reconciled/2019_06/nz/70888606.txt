<h> India 's sixth largest city is almost completely out of water <p> The city of Chennai , India 's sixth largest metropolis , is almost entirely out of water . <p> Millions of people are running out of the resource in Chennai as the Chembarambakkam reservoir continues to dry out . Three other reservoirs that have traditionally supplied the city with water have also all dried from the extreme heat . <p> The Tamil Nadu state government are struggling with the insufficient water supply after low groundwater levels and difficulties collecting rainwater led to the severe shortage . <p> Residents are currently receiving water through truck deliveries , standing in line for hours in extreme heat to fill their containers and cans , CNN reports . <p> Many hotels and restaurants have reportedly closed due to the scarcity . <p> Private tankers , delivering water from unaffected outside areas of the state , are in high demand . Many are unable to supply water on time , and the cost of frequent deliveries to businesses are expensive . <p> Wealthier Chennai families are relying on exorbitant private water tankers due to strict rationing , CNN reports . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ far unknown , but the latest census in 2011 showed the city 's population was 4.6 million . <p> According to a report in May 2019 , 712,000 families live below the poverty line in Chennai . In 2011 , 29 percent of the population lived in slums . These numbers are indicative of the significant number of people who are unable to afford private water access . <p> Climate change is also having devastating effects on the country , as drought becomes more common and monsoon rains become more erratic . In a largely agricultural country , the weather could severely impact farmers ' harvests and many family livelihoods . <p> Around 80 percent of India 's groundwater is used for irrigation , watering thirsty crops such as rice and sugarcane.
