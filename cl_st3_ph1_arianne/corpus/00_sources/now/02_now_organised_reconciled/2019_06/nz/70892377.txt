<p> Terror in Christchurch and concern about climate change have been the motivation for the 2019 Mahara Gallery Matariki programme which seeks to harness art 's capacity to support emotional healing and renewal . <p> The Gallery 's Matariki exhibition opens on Saturday 22 June and is supported by a programme of 10 workshops or events in late June and July . <p> " Matariki is the time for remembering those we have lost , for reconnecting as a community , and celebrating rebirth and renewal , " says Gallery Director , Janet Bayly . <p> " The year has so far delivered events that have been confronting in the case of the Christchurch massacre and challenging in the case of the climate change marches . <p> " In putting together a programme for Matariki , we looked for a way to harness art 's capacity to support emotional healing and renewal . " <p> Janet Bayly says thirteen artists will be exhibiting in the gallery and some will be involved in the 10 workshops or events during June and July in which the community is invited to participate . <p> " Many of the artists who responded to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in Christchurch and the effect that it 's had on their work , " says Janet Bayly . <p> " Otaki artist Ramila Parbhu , who uses recycled materials to create her works , told us that the Christchurch shootings had left her with ' an overwhelming sense of sadness ' and that art is her form of healing and grounding in the world . <p> " We are thrilled to have a quilt from the wonderful Woodville-based fabric artist , Ron Te Kawa .
