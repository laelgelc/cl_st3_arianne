<h> Palace not fazed should UN probe push through <p> MALACA ? ANG expressed confidence that the call by the special rapporteurs of the United Nations ( UN ) for an independent investigation into alleged human rights violations in the Philippines will have no impact on the country 's membership in the international body . <p> There will be " no " impact even if the UN body acts on this call , Presidential Spokesperson Salvador S. Panelo told BusinessWorld in an ambush interview last Monday . <p> Asked what the Philippines will do in case the investigation pushes through , the spokesman said : " Wala ( Nothing ) . " <p> The Philippines is a signatory to the UN 's Universal Declaration of Human Rights ( UDHR ) . <p> " They can do their worst , " he added , " Wala ' yan . Walang maniniwala d'yan ( That 's nothing . No one will believe that ) . " <p> Last Friday , 11 UN Special Rapporteurs called on the UN Human Rights Council to conduct an independent investigation into the alleged rights violations in the Philippines . <p> In a statement issued from @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Human Rights Office of the High Commissioner , they said : " Given the scale and seriousness of the reported human rights violations , we call on the Human Rights Council to establish an independent investigation into the human rights violations in the Philippines . " <p> The Special Rapporteurs and Working Groups , as explained on the statement 's footnote , " are part of what is known as the Special Procedures of the Human Rights Council ... Special Procedures ' experts work on a voluntary basis ; they are not UN staff and do not receive a salary for their work . " <p> The experts said they have recorded " a staggering number of unlawful deaths and police killings in the context of the so-called war on drugs , as well as killings of human rights defenders . " <p> They also noted that only " very few independent and effective investigations have taken place . " <p> University of the Philippines Political Science professor Maria Ela L. Atienza , in an email to BusinessWorld last Sunday when sought for comment on the matter , said : @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in the area of human rights , used to be one of the leading and respected voices from the developing world in the UN in the areas of human rights , women 's rights , and environmental issues , including climate change . " <p> However , she added , this has " changed " when Mr. Duterte came to power in 2016 and launched his war against illegal drugs . <p> " The government , including all secretaries appointed in the Department of Foreign Affairs , has defended the war on drugs , " Ms. Atienza said . <p> She also noted that the President has " lambasted the UN and human rights advocates and accused them of interfering in the nation 's internal affairs . "
