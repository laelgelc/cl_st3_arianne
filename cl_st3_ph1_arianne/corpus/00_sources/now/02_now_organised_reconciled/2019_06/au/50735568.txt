<h> Marine protected areas missing the mark <p> Protected marine areas are often in the wrong locations to stop threats to biodiversity , according to international research led by a University of Queensland team . <p> Dr Caitlin Kuempel said protecting large tracts of ocean can be incredibly effective against biodiversity loss and there has been a lot of progress . <p> " But if we do n't place them where they can reduce threats to marine habitats and species -- arguably their primary purpose -- we 're not getting the immediate benefits needed to protect our oceans , " Dr Kuempel said . <p> Researchers classified human impacts on oceans as ' stoppable ' or ' unstoppable ' based on whether effective marine protected areas could reduce each threat . <p> " We found that areas with relatively low levels of stoppable threats had 6.3 times the strict protection of areas with high levels . <p> " Stoppable threats included stressors like fishing , human-made structures and direct human impacts , while unstoppable threats included threats that require action on land or additional policies , like nutrient pollution and climate change .
