<p> More than a dozen species of seabirds are in decline off Australia 's southeast coast - likely because of warming ocean temperatures , new research has found . The study revealed sightings of almost half the 30 most abundant seabirds - including the wandering albatross and flesh-footed shearwater - had fallen in the region over a 17-year period between 2000 and 2016 . Seabirds are critical in maintaining healthy marine ecosystems , acting as predators that feed on a range of prey . " Our findings are worrying , not just because 13 of our more common species are declining , but because we do n't know for sure what is driving these declines , " lead author and University of NSW honours student Simon Gorta said in a statement on Monday . Researchers at the UNSW Centre for Ecosystem Science suggest that warming ocean temperatures are likely contributing to the trend . The east Australian current has strengthened off the country 's southeast , which has led to warmer and less productive waters in the region . That is potentially driving birds to hunt elsewhere , Mr Gorta said .
