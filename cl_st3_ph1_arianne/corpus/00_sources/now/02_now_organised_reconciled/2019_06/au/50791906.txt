<p> OH , for the good old days of rugby league . We used to go to a game of league with great expectations that our team would win on their merits , their skill and , at times , the bounce of the ball . You would see centres and wingers scoring tries from set moves and skilful footwork not being encumbered by some slow-barging biped in the back line . Winning a ball from a set scrum was an art requiring the participation of all your forwards , and in which both teams had a chance to win the ball . The referee was a genius who could detect a forward pass and even determine if the ball went forward or backward from a player 's hand . Sure , they may have got a few wrong , but it all evened out in the end . A win , loss or draw was mostly attributed to the performance of the competing teams and the competence of the referee to enforce the rules that we all understood . Raymond Parkes ( Letters , 18/6 ) suggests that before anyone comments on climate change they read Ian Plimer 's book Heaven and Earth - Global Warming - The Missing Science . May I alternately suggest that in an attempt to save time and actually learn about the topic people instead read Ian Plimer 's Heaven and Earth - Checking the Claims by Ian G Enting , a lengthy analysis of Plimer 's book which ran to a staggering 64 pages of errors and misrepresentations .
