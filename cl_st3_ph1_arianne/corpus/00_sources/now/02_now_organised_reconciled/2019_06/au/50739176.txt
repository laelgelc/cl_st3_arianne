This offers a strategy to assist the threatened species survive climate change . <h> Koalas driven to drink by climate change <p> A long-held view that koalas get all their hydration from eating leaves has been overturned by new research published today from Dr Valentina Mella and colleagues at the University of Sydney . <p> The study in PLOS ONE offers hope in the fight to conserve this threatened species , with researchers finding that koalas will regularly use artificial water stations , particularly during hot and dry conditions . <p> " Drinking stations could help koalas during heat and drought events and might help mitigate the effects of climate change , " said Dr Mella from the School of Life and Environmental Sciences . <p> Dr Mella also said drinking stations could prove a useful strategy to support other arboreal folivores such as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ some monkeys on other continents . <p> Koala populations along Australia 's east coast have been declining due to lost habitat from deforestation , diseases such as chlamydia , attacks from feral animals , fire and vehicle collisions . <p> The Australian Department of Environment estimates that combined koala populations in Queensland and New South Wales declined from 326,400 in 1990 to 188,000 in 2010 , a drop of 42 percent . <p> However , koalas are also particularly vulnerable to the effects of climate change , suffering heat stress , Dr Mella said , and because the trees they rely on are affected by temperature and rainfall change . <p> Dr Valentina Mella with koala joey in Gunnedah . <p> Koalas ca n't simply eat more leaves to compensate for reduced water content in their favourite food . This is because koalas are limited in their food intake by leaf toxins . <p> " It is predicted that increased CO2 emissions will increase the level of phenolics and tannins in eucalyptus leaves , " Dr Mella said . " This means koalas will need alternative strategies to find water @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ stations . " <p> Dr Mella has been conducting field work in Gunnedah in western NSW where , in 2009 , a heat wave killed an estimated quarter of Gunnedah 's koala population . <p> " We were n't sure if the water stations could be used to mitigate the impact of extreme weather events , " Dr Mella said . " But our results clearly show koalas will regularly use these stations to supplement their water needs . " <p> During the first 12 months of the study , Dr Mella and her team recorded 605 visits to 10 pairs of water stations , with 401 of these visits resulting in koalas drinking . <p> They found that the total number of visits and total time drinking doubled during summer compared to other seasons . <p> " Frequent access to water may be fundamental for koalas to assist thermoregulation when temperatures are high , " Dr Mella said . <p> Initial findings about koalas ' drinking behaviour were announced in 2017 with videos that showed widespread use of water by the iconic mammals , particularly during drier periods . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ campaign at the University to support further research into koala conservation that raised more than $150,000 . <p> Dr Mella 's study has influenced the direction of state and national koala research , with water supplementation supported by the NSW Office of Environment and Heritage , through the NSW Koala Strategy and used by the North West Local Land Services as a central concept to koala management on private properties . <p> " We need to monitor how effective these are -- as the stations can also attract feral animals and predators . Fortunately , we have n't seen any deaths from predators near the drinking stations in Gunnedah , " Dr Mella said . <p> To mitigate this risk , her team has now developed drinking stations that are inaccessible to ground-based predators . <p> " Our next steps will be to see if disease , such as chlamydia , influences koala drinking behaviour , " Dr Mella said . " And we will also monitor individual koalas to examine these drinking behaviours over a longer time period .
