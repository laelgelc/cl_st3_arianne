<h> Four-year project captures islanders ' songs of global warning <h> By Kathy Evans <p> Can appealing to the wisdom of Pele ( the Hawaiian goddess , not the footballer ) and the spirits of our region 's seafaring ancestors through song get us out of this global ecological mess ? <p> Tackling climate change , experts agree , involves a nuanced dance between the technical scientific world and the ineffable , immeasurable world of culture . Music , in particular , can convey meaning in a way that rational analysis can not . <p> When filmmaker and music producer Tim Cole studied the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change 's fifth report in 2014 , his response was to set about transforming bleak facts and data about the impact of rising sea levels in the region into empathy and compassion . He wanted to show that climate change is more than a policy problem ; it 's also about loss and disruption . <p> BaoBao Chen during filming on the Small Island Big Song project . <p> He and his Taiwanese partner , project manager BaoBao Chen , spent the next four years recording songs by 100 musicians representing @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ homes are on the frontline of global environmental destruction . The result , Small Island Big Song , is a multi-platform album of remarkable resilience , rather than doom and gloom bombast . <p> Advertisement <p> Opening with a traditional chant sung on a headland in Taiwan , it heads next to Rapa Nui ( Easter Island ) then across the Mozambique Channel to Madagascar , Papua New Guinea , Singapore , Malaysia , Aotearoa/New Zealand and the Solomon Islands , to name but a few . In that way the album also pays homage to the ancient Taiwanese migrants who pushed out their boats 5000 years ago , embarking on a seafaring journey across half the earth 's surface and creating an extraordinary cultural diaspora . <p> A map showing the island homelands of artists who took part in Small Island Big Song to raise awareness of the impact of climate change . <p> In a kind of reversal of tectonic drift , the island land masses have become fused into a supercontinent of sound . The result is both contemporary and ancient . <p> " What we are hoping to do is remind us all that there were stories before the current ones and even stories beyond that too , that we are trying to reach back to find in this musical lineage , " says Cole , speaking from the mountains of Taiwan , where he and Chen are visiting relatives . <p> Cole and Chen kept things as close to nature as possible , turning up at remote locations in rainforests , beaches , cliffs and volcanoes with just a mic , a laptop and a camera , seeking out indigenous elders to guide their endeavours . Everything was constructed from the grass roots up . <p> " We just said ' share a song that you are proud to represent your heritage and we just let it go completely ' .
