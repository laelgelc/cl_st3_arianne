<p> More Americans say " made-up " news and information is a very big problem in the US than say the same of climate change and other global threats , including illegal immigration , racism , and sexism , according to a new Pew Research poll . <p> While 50% of Americans say made-up news and information is a very big problem in the country today , 46% say the same of climate change . <p> Meanwhile , 49% say violent crime is a very big problem , 40% say the same of racism , and 38% feel similarly about illegal immigration . Drug addiction and health care costs topped the list of respondents ' concerns . <p> A large majority 68% of Americans believe made-up news greatly impacts Americans ' confidence in the government , and 54% said it 's having a major impact on Americans ' confidence in each other . <p> But responses to the issue , which is sometimes described as " fake news , " are divided along partisan lines . <p> Concerns about what is sometimes called " fake news " is greater among more conservative respondents , and President Donald Trump has @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to shift public attention from challenging stories about him and his administration . While 62% of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents say made-up news is a very big problem in the US today , just 40% of Democrats and Democratic-leaning independents say the same . <p> Almost three times as many Republicans as Democrats blame journalists for creating " a lot " of inaccurate or misleading news and information . <p> The Pew poll surveyed 6,127 U.S. adults between Feb. 19 and March 4 , 2019 and has a margin of error of plus or minus 1.6 percentage points .
