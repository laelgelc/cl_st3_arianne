Photo : Skoll Foundation/Gabriel Diamond <p> Rice that is ready to cook and rice grains grown as seed for the next crop look remarkably similar . That can tempt the unscrupulous , who pocket big profits by packaging table rice for sale as seed , according to counterfeiting experts . <p> " When you look at rice seed and rice for the table , it 's almost impossible to distinguish between the two , " said Bright Simons , a Ghanaian entrepreneur who has developed technology to detect fake seeds , medicine and other products . <p> Almost 10 percent of seeds sold in Africa and South Asia are fake . This brings a huge cost for farmers , who can see harvests slashed , their families go hungry and bank loans remain unpaid if they are tricked , Simons told the Thomson Reuters Foundation . <p> " If your yield is cut by 90 percent , essentially you 're bankrupt , " he said in an interview .
