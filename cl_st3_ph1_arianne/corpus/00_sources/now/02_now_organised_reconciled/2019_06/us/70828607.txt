( Photo by Emily Rasmussen , Long Beach Press-Telegram/SCNG ) <p> Chalk-drawn waves of blue , varying in shades and lengths , stretched across part of Long Beach 's Marine Stadium on a grey , gloomy morning . <p> While the artistic waves set an upbeat tone for 500-plus attendees on Saturday , June 1 , who came to the city 's first Climate Festival event -- which aims to educate the public about climate change -- they also held a more ominous meaning . The chalk-drawn waves gave a real-life representation of how areas around the city , like Marine Stadium , could flood amid rising sea levels . <p> The festival provided the venue to reveal the draft of the city 's Climate Action and Adaption Plan , which aims to address sea-level rise and other climate-change-related issues that loom ahead . <p> The plan , which @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ culmination of about 50 community events that engaged nearly 9,000 residents -- paired with scientific analysis from 13 independent scientists with organizations ranging from Cal State Long Beach to the Aquarium of the Pacific . <p> The end result includes dozens of actions that would mitigate climate change , in addition to adapting to the effects it would have on the city 's infrastructure and residents . Saturday marked a 45-day period of public comment of the draft , before a finalized plan is ready to head to the City Council for approval to implement . <p> These are some of the 19 priority mitigation actions : <p> Increase frequency , connectivity and safety of transit options ; <p> Increase bikeway infrastructure ; Increase density and mixing of land uses ; <p> Non-priority mitigation actions include increasing construction of zero-net emissions buildings , providing water efficiency rebates or incentives , and requiring solar panels on new construction . <p> The CAAP draft plan comes a few weeks after the Aquarium of the Pacific opened Pacific Visions , a two-story new wing , which carries a strong message about climate change @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ humans have on the earth and its oceans , and what can be done to address it . <p> The plan found that transportation was the largest source of greenhouse gases coming from the city . But it also addressed other contributors , such as energy and waste are also factors , said Alison Spindler , planner for Long Beach Development Services . <p> The main goal is to take a holistic approach of climate change , by mitigating the causes of climate change and also addressing the effects , Spindler said . One example , she said , is planting trees . <p> They not only cool neighborhoods during extreme heat , but they also clean the air and encourage more people to walk , Spindler said . <p> " Looking for those things can have co-benefits across different sectors is an important part of it , " she said .
