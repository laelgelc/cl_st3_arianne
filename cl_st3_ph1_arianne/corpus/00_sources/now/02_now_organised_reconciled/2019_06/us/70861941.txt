<h> For Immediate Release <p> Research suggests that microbes in the soil , roots and leaves have important impacts on plant health and productivity . Now , new interdisciplinary research at North Carolina State University and three Danish universities will examine the roles of plant-associated microbes and their interactions with plants . The goal is to help make crops more resilient against environmental stresses while reducing the need for chemical treatments and irrigation . <p> The new Collaborative Crop Resilience Program ( CCRP ) is funded by the Novo Nordisk Foundation , the parent organization of NC State partner and biological solutions leader Novozymes . NC State will receive approximately $8 million of the project 's $30 million in funding over six years . <p> NC State Chancellor Randy Woodson said that the grant-funded program represents a milestone for the university . <p> " The CCRP positions NC State at the forefront of discovery in plant sciences and in translating these discoveries into solutions for farmers , life-sciences companies and consumers , " he said . <p> The project will take a holistic view of plants and their interactions with microbes in the soil , roots and foliage . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ they interact with the plant -- plays a critical role in plant health and well-being , similar to the way the human gut microbiome influences human health . <p> Wheat , one of the world 's most important crops in both production and value , will be the featured crop in various studies -- including heat , cold and drought tolerance -- occurring in Denmark and North Carolina .
