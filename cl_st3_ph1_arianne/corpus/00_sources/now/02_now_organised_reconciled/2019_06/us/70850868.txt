Yet what links these stories together is our country 's increasing inability to agree on what counts as the truth , which referees deserve respect , and whether any common vantage points remain from which effective , evidence-based public policies might be made . <p> President Donald Trump 's 10,000 lies certainly threaten democracy and the rule of law , but the fractured media and polarized electorate pose perhaps equally grave dangers to our capacity to confront public health challenges ranging from measles to climate change . After all , neither the press nor the public can make sense of policy proposals without some confidence in what counts as " the facts . Yet if self-interested and false claims have always infected public discourse , our era 's epidemic of dangerous distortions is more virulent for having taken root at the highest levels of government , in powerful media outlets and across multiple internet platforms . <p> Distrust of science is especially evident in the disdain the president and his allies display for the scientific consensus that humans are causing global warming , reflected most recently in administration plans to prevent federal reporting on climate change effects that may occur after 2040 . Such willful blindness erodes our ability to confront rising temperatures , melting ice caps and ocean degradation , all of which are causing human misery across the globe . <p> We see no easy solutions to these public health challenges . Scholars differ on the mechanisms by which partisanship influences beliefs , but no one doubts that heightened political polarization is related to increasing disagreements about matters of fact . <p> As we grapple with the rising epidemic of falsehoods , viable solutions may seem beyond reach . Progress can begin , however , by prioritizing the post-truth problem and being clear about its many causes and implications . The rise of social media , a changed journalism environment , the influence of dark money in politics , political polarization , and perhaps even campus trends questioning the idea of " objectivity " are fueling our descent into public uncertainty . <p> Because misinformation is spread rapidly via social media , many call for internet providers to police their platforms .
