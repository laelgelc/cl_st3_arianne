Photographer : Joshua Roberts/Bloomberg News <p> BLOOMBERG NEWS <p> Corporate lobbying reduced the likelihood of passing the Waxman-Markey clean-energy bill a decade ago , resulting in an expected $60 billion in climate costs to society , according to economists from UC Santa Barbara and the University of Chicago . <p> The economists use Waxman-Markey , the most promising U.S. climate bill to date , to demonstrate a way to quantify the social impact of lobbying . Their method also shows how bills can be better written , they say , to withstand lobbying campaigns . <p> " We calculate that lobbying lowered the probability of enacting the Waxman-Markey bill by 13 percentage points , representing an expected social cost of $60 billion ( in 2018 dollars ) , " write Kyle C.
