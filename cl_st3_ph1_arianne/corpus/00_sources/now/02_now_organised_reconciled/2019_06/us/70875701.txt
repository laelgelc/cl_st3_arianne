<p> In December 2016 , a new Lake Ontario water management plan went into effect , crafted by the International Joint Commission , the U.S.-Canadian agency governing the Great Lakes . It replaced a plan that had been used since 1963 . <p> Plan 2014 , as the new regulations were called , acknowledged the water would be a little higher , and there might be some more erosion and flood damage on the New York State side of the lake than there used to be . But the conditions along the lakeshore would be better for wildlife and the shipping season would last longer , according to the plan . <p> The plan anticipated $20 million in lakefront property damage from flooding and erosion every year , versus an average of $18 million under the old plan . <p> It has n't worked out that way . In 2017 , officials declared a state of emergency over the high Lake Ontario water level . New York State committed $100 million to repairing the damage .
