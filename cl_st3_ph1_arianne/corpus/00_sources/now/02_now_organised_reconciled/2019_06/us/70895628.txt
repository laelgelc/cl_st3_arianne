<p> Gov . Gavin Newsom on Friday proposed creation of a $21 billion wildfire recovery fund to help PG&amp;E and other California utilities pay for damages to victims from future wildfires , but the fund does nothing for the thousands of California residents burned out of their homes in 2017 and 2018 . <p> Nearly 6,200 North Bay homes were destroyed and 40 people died in the October 2017 conflagrations , which exacted the heaviest toll in Sonoma County . No action by lawmakers last year or anything in the governor 's new plan directly addresses losses by present fire survivors . <p> " It seems like we 're getting left out , " said Brad Sherwood , a block captain in Larkfield Estates , where 163 homes were lost to the Tubbs fire . <p> Newsom 's plan would require utility investors and ratepayers to contribute to the fund , which would " provide ready dollars " to pay future claims and allow power companies to " stabilize their financial health " in order to provide safe and affordable energy , his proposal said . <p> PG&amp;E , the state 's largest utility , would be prohibited from @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and 2018 wildfires and exits bankruptcy with a reorganization plan that is " neutral to ratepayers " and concluded by June 30 of next year , the proposal said . <p> PG&amp;E equipment was deemed responsible for causing 17 of 18 of the Northern California wildfires that broke out in October 2017 , according to Cal Fire .
