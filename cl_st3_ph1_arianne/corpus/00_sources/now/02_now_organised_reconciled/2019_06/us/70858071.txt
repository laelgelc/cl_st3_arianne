<p> Alabama Gov . Kay Ivey ( R ) has signed a bill that will require people convicted of certain sex offenses to undergo " chemical castration " as a condition of parole -- a requirement meant to keep perpetrators from committing similar crimes . <p> Ivey signed the bill Monday -- the last day under Alabama law that she could have done so after the state legislature passed it on May 31 . Gina Maiola , a spokeswoman for Ivey , said the law will apply to people who commit sex offenses after Sept. 1 of this year . <p> The " chemical castration " law says a judge must order anyone convicted of a sex offense involving a child under the age of 13 to start receiving testosterone-inhibiting medication a month before their release from prison . Most offenders will have to pay for their treatment , which will be administered by the Department of Public Health , until a judge decides the medication is no longer necessary . <p> Under the law , a judge -- and not a doctor -- will tell the offender about the effects of the treatment .
