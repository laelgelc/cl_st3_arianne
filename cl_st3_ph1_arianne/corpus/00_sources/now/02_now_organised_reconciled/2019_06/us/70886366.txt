<h> Scientists chart course toward a new world of synthetic biology <p> Synthetic or engineering biology involves genetically engineering not only yeast and bacteria but also plants , animals and humans . The benefits could be immense , ranging from gene therapy for disease to improved crops and better medicines . <p> Genetically engineered trees that provide fire-resistant lumber for homes . Modified organs that wo n't be rejected . Synthetic microbes that monitor your gut to detect invading disease organisms and kill them before you get sick . <p> These are just some of the exciting advances likely to emerge from the 20-year-old field of engineering biology , or synthetic biology , which is now mature enough to provide solutions to a range of societal problems , according to a new roadmap released today ( June 19 ) by the Engineering Biology Research Consortium , a public-private partnership partially funded by the National Science Foundation and centered at the University of California , Berkeley . <p> The roadmap is the work of more than 80 scientists and engineers from a range of disciplines , representing more than 30 universities and a dozen companies .
