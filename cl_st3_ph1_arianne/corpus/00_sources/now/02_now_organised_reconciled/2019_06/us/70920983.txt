<h> Keep the Faith : The End of the World : Destruction or Renewal ? <p> By The Rev. Gary R. Shahinian <h> Saturday <p> Jun 29 , 2019 at 5:57 AM <p> When you think of the end of the world , what images come into your mind ? Visions of doom , destruction , and despair ? Where do we get these ideas ? Is the physical earth we presently experience going to be permanent or is God going to obliterate it completely ? <p> Because of their twisted interpretation of the Bible , many evangelical Christians look forward to the end of the world because they believe they will be " raptured , " that is , removed from the world before the apocalypse wreaks its devastation upon the earth 's remaining inhabitants . <p> According to this view , the end of the world is not a one-time event . In fact , it comes in three stages . <p> First there is the aforementioned " rapture " when all true believers suddenly disappear from the world . This occurrence precipitates a series of horrifying episodes that leads to the rise of a tyrannical world @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ up as the world 's savior but is really an instrument of Satan . During this period occurs the " Great Tribulation , " a time of catastrophic events and wrathful judgments upon the earth by God never experienced before . These judgments serve as a precursor to God 's ultimate destruction of the world . <p> Seven years after the rapture , when the world is at the height of its chaos , the second stage occurs when Jesus Christ returns to earth to defeat the Antichrist and to set up his literal 1,000-year kingdom , ruling the world with an iron fist from Jerusalem . This is the second stage . <p> But wait , this is still not the end . Despite this period of time being referred to as the " Millennium , " there is yet a final failed rebellion instigated by Satan to overthrow Christ 's kingdom at the end of the 1,000 years . Only after Satan is conclusively defeated does the end of the world arrive . This third and final stage involves the unbelieving dead being raised and their final judgment @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have already attained their resurrected state via Christ 's return during the rapture and will immediately achieve everlasting glory in heaven . God then finally destroys the world as we know it and creates a " new heaven and a new earth . " <p> The above scenario has been reinforced in our culture by Tim LaHaye 's best-selling Left Behind series of 16 books ( 1995-2007 ) , which have sold over 80,000,000 copies . Three of the books have been made into full-length feature films : Left Behind : The Movie ( 2001 ) , Left Behind II : Tribulation Force ( 2002 ) , Left Behind : World at War ( 2005 ) , and Left Behind ( 2014 ) , a remake . <p> Is this really what the Bible teaches ? Do n't be alarmed . The answer is no . God 's ultimate intention for us and the world is not destruction but renewal . Nevertheless , prophecies , even misinterpreted ones , can be self-fulfilling . That 's because how we see the future influences how we act now . Disagreement among Christians @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ academic matter . What we think will happen in the future determines our present outlook . <p> If we believe that God intends to destroy the world , then we wo n't have much regard for creation right now . We wo n't concern ourselves with climate change , pollution , recycling , and the environment . We wo n't care about overpopulation , poverty , war , extinction of species , and all the social , political , and economic problems in the world because Christians will be suddenly snatched from the world and not need to deal with these issues . <p> But what if the rapture is an illusion ? What if Christ is not going to remove his followers from the world in an instant ? What if instead Christ gives us the responsibility to care for the physical world for future generations ? <p> A favorite New Testament passage that Christians who believe in God 's destruction of the world love to quote is 2 Peter 3:10 : <p> But the day of the Lord will come like a thief , and then the heavens @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ elements will be dissolved with fire , and the earth and everything that is done on it will be disclosed . ( New Revised Standard Version ) <p> I can see why conservative evangelicals quote this passage to reinforce their view that God intends to destroy the world because the last verb has not always been translated properly . The New American Standard Bible and the Revised Standard Version translate the verb as " will be burned up " and the King James Version says " shall be burned up . " The New International Version is somewhat better in its translation , " will be laid bare . " We must remember that our English Bibles are all translations from Hebrew ( the Old Testament ) and Greek ( the New Testament ) . Sometimes no exact word for word correspondence exists between two languages . This is why translation is such a difficult and very often an inexact science . <p> There 's a big difference , however , between " to be burned up " and " to be disclosed . " Is God 's coming judgment @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ annihilation of the present created order ? Or will it be a smelting process from which the world will emerge purified ? The Greek verb used here , heurethisetai , is a metallurgical term applicable to smelting and refining . When a metal was placed into the fire , it came out purified of its dross and impurities so that its genuine identity became " disclosed " or " laid bare . " <p> With this understanding of the verb , we get an altogether different idea of the end of the world . God does not intend to destroy the world , but rather , to purify or renew it , just as a metallurgist plunges the gold into the fire so that it may emerge in a purer , more refined form . <p> God 's aim is always a redemptive one . Even God 's judgments are always in the context of redeeming or saving . So will they be in the future . The end of the world , therefore , should not conjure up images of doom and gloom , but rather , joy and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ new earth , where righteousness is at home " ( 2 Peter 3:13 ) . <p> The Rev.
