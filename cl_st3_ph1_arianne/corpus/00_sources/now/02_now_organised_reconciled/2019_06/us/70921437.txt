Population pressure , drought and climate change have made China the world 's biggest victim of desertification and it could take 300 years to reclaim just one-fifth of desert land , according to a recent report . For nearly 2,000 years , the Tarim basin , comprising mostly the bone-dry Taklamakan desert , spired by the lofty Kunlun , Tien Shan and Pamir mountains to the south , north and the west , had been a central artery of the ancient Silk Road . But the cities , nourished by thriving trade between the east and the west , and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and monasteries , were ruined when international commerce discovered sea routes . <p> Nevertheless , the land corridor between Asia and Europe is back in the limelight after Chinese President Xi Jinping decided to give the ancient route a 21st century facelift . With the BRI , the revival of the Tarim basin -- a critical node in Xinjiang that links China with Central Asia , South Asia , West Asia , Russia and Europe -- has been inescapable . <p> In the past , India was connected with the Silk Road via Yarkand , not far from Kashgar in the Tarim basin . But currently , the route to the Indian subcontinent , marshalled by Pakistan , passes through the Karakoram highway south of Kashgar . <p> Two major passes from the Tarim basin also lead out to Central Asia , via Kyrgyzstan . The Irkeshtam pass , west of Kashgar , heads in the direction of Osh -- a famous Fergana valley city , once frequented by Babur , the founder of the Mughal Empire . Bishkek , the capital city , is around 400 km away , and can be reached via Naryn , a city that derives its name from Naryn river which flows through the city before joining the mighty Syr Darya . <p> In the Chinese perception , the importance of the Tarim basin has magnified as more routes from the hinterland funnel into the Xinjiang Uighur Administrative Region , before channelling overseas . The Chinese have been especially focussed on reinforcing transportation links between Xinjiang and Tibet , two giant border regions , where calls for separatism have been countered by bullish state-led economic activism . <p> Unsurprisingly , the now famous Qinghai-Tibet railway is acquiring another node to the northwest in the direction of the Tarim basin . Golmud , a major junction on the railway , is also the starting point of the new enterprise . Since the end of 2014 , the Chinese have been constructing the Golmud-Korla railway . These include oil and gas as well as lithium -- the silvery-white alkali metal that is the life-blood of electric car batteries -- as well salt sourced from the basin 's 43 vast salt lakes . <p> From the Qaidam basin , trains will tunnel through the Altyn-Tagh -- a mountain range that separates the eastern Tarim basin from the Tibetan plateau . But the new railway corridors and expressways passing through the Tarim basin require ecological protection . For several decades , the lower Tarim river , in proximity of the new transport corridors , had been in the eye of a major ecological disaster . The upper part of the 2,030 km river was heavily dammed for agricultural purposes . <h> Environmental damage <p> Such was the resulting imbalance that in the 1970s , the flow in the last 321 km of the river stopped completely , drying up streams and lakes .
