<p> Ottawa lived through three of the country 's Top Ten weather events of 2017 -- a list of weather that it 's usually better to miss . <p> There were spring floods that drowned hundreds of houses , the summer that " went missing " in a long stretch of cool and wet days , and finally a little good news -- record-breaking warmth and sunshine in September . <p> Environment Canada 's David Phillips selected the 10 biggest weather news stories , and says this about our local Big Three : <p> A man was all set for the weather as he makes his way through the water on Rue Saint-Louis on Saturday . Water levels in Gatineau have started to come down in the flooding but major damage is starting to show Saturday May 13 , 2017 . Ashley Fraser / Postmedia <p> * The flooding was easily our worst weather of the year : " The Quebec government claimed the spring downpour was the worst ever recorded in the province over the past 55 years . In Montreal , April rains totalled 156.2 mm -- the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ had their wettest spring in history -- 400 mm or more with records dating back to the 1870s . Spring flooding occurred in hundreds of communities , forcing 4,000 people to leave their homes . " <p> * Then came what he calls a " bummer of a summer " : " Total rainfall from April to July was well above normal in most places , including the Great Lakes and St. Lawrence River Basin , where it was the wettest in 70 years of observations . Cool , overcast weather and frequent spring showers continued throughout the summer . " He blames the jet stream , which was off its usual course . Lake Ontario reached its highest level since 1918 . <p> * But September warmed us up : " From September 22 to 27 , over a thousand heat records fell ( across Central Canada ) as humidex values shot up close to or above 40 , prompting a week-long stretch of heat warnings . <p> " In the lead up to the five-day scorcher , there were two weeks of delightfully sunny , warm and rain-free @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ weather in the entire year . " <p> And Environment Canada , which publishes the list , adds some context : <p> " Extreme weather events demonstrate that the world is seeing the effects of climate change , " it says . <p> " Environment and Climate Change Canada 's scientists ca n't directly link a single extreme weather event to a warmer world , but it would certainly be consistent with our expectations of increased climate unpredictability . What we do know is that Canadians are becoming more vulnerable to extreme weather and that our climate is consistently changing and warming . <p> " Devastating forest fires , the increased frequency of floods , heavy rains , ice storms , temperature changes and some of the most powerful hurricanes in history are examples of how our climate is changing and how people are experiencing extreme weather . <p> " As the Top 10 Weather Stories of 2017 shows , our communities must become more resilient -- not only for what lies ahead , but for the changing climate that is already on our doorstep .
