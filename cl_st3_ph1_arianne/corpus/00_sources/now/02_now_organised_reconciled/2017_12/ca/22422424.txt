When the Assiniboine Park Conservancy started looking at ways it could rejuvenate its aging zoo in 2008 , it decided to seize the opportunities a northern zoo in a winter city could provide . <p> Instead of focusing on exotic species , why not focus on the species that exist here in our province ? <p> Since opening in 2014 , the $90-million , award-winning Arctic species exhibit has become not only the jewel of the zoo , but also of Winnipeg . <p> It 's a destination tourist attraction @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and a source of civic pride . The polar bears are , undoubtedly , the biggest draw . But the polar bears at the zoo do n't just come here to be admired . Journey to Churchill is also the site of important conservation work and research . <p> Opened in 2012 , the Leatherdale International Polar Bear Conservation Centre is the raison d'etre for the exhibit , which aims to educate visitors about a variety of northern species -- which , in addition to polar bears , includes harbour seals , muskox , arctic foxes , snowy owls , caribou and wolves -- as well as the greatest threat to their survival : climate change . Most of the polar bears that populate Journey to Churchill are rescued bears from northern Manitoba . Bears , who come to the zoo , only do so under specific circumstances as outlined by Manitoba 's Polar Bear Protection Act . <p> They must either be orphaned cubs who will not survive on their own , or at-risk bears who would otherwise be euthanized .
