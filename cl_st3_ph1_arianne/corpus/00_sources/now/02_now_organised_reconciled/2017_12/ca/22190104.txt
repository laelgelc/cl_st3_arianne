<p> The city of Ottawa lags on investment in energy conservation and climate action . When the city 's 2018 budget is finalized this week , the city 's renewable energy strategy , Energy Evolution , is set to again be under-resourced . <p> With a goal to be the greenest city in the world by 2020 , Vancouver is actively working to reduce its carbon footprint by 33 per cent . Montreal has just boosted its carbon reduction target from 25 per cent to 35 per cent -- by 2020 . <p> The mayors of Atlanta , Cape Town , Paris and Vancouver have pledged to lead a 7,000-city push to combat the threat of climate change . In the United States , state and municipal leaders are rebelling against Trump 's anti-environment , pro-coal policy stance , and leading climate change-fighting mayors met last week in Chicago . Montreal and Vancouver were two of the signatories there among nearly four dozen mayors from five countries , who committed to track their carbon emissions quarterly . ( Ottawa emissions are reported only every four years . ) <p> These @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ demonstrating solutions that clean the air , and create local jobs and business opportunities , all the while driving down their carbon footprint . <p> So why the limited ambition in Ottawa , the nation 's capital and one of the wealthiest and best-educated cities in Canada ? <p> To be fair , kudos where due . The mayor and council have shown leadership on Light Rail , a major step towards greening our public transportation . And each year the city 's Building Engineering and Energy Management Unit implements energy efficiency and conservation measures within existing buildings owned by the city . These are investments that significantly reduce operating costs , typically paying back the investment in 5 1/2 years and thereafter providing significant annual savings . The investment in LED street lighting alone will save taxpayers $6 million a year , forever . <p> But none of that activity will come close to putting Ottawa on a trajectory for doing its part and meeting its goal of reducing greenhouse gas emissions 80 per cent by 2050 . Much more action is needed to encourage and equip all sectors in Ottawa to participate in the low-carbon economy . The clean technology sector is the fastest growing sector of the international economy , worth an estimated $1 trillion US a year and growing . <p> Some will argue that Ottawa has so many priorities , that we simply ca n't afford to spend more on climate change .
