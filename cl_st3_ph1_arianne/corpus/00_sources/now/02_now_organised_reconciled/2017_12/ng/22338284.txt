<h> The NGO Bill versus the environment <p> Very quickly , let me state that considering the existing legislative contradictions in our governance superstructure , what the formulators and proponents of the so-called NGO Bill are doing is tantamount to leaving the log in your eye to remove the speck in your brother 's eye . That is paraphrasing the famous words of Jesus Christ . <p> It is now common knowledge that the National Assembly is currently considering the enactment of an Act to " Provide for the Establishment of Non-Governmental Organizations Regulatory Commission for the Supervision , Coordination and Monitoring of Non-Governmental Organizations , Civil Society Organisations etc. , in Nigeria and for Related Matters " . This is popularly known as the NGO Bill . It has gone through First and Second Readings and is presently at the committee phase at the House of Representatives . <p> Last week , there was a public hearing in the House . It is interesting to note that at the event , almost everybody in the auditorium spoke against the bill . Nobody stood up for it . <p> My @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ are patriotic in their new-found passion , there are more crucial things to do to achieve the same end . I respect our lawmakers and our political leaders , but a blind man can notice that sometimes , most of them fail to rightly situate the national interest when defining their priorities . <p> Let us start with the Disclosure of Sources of Funds , which is one of the issues addressed by the proposed NGO Bill . <p> Section 25 of the bill makes a provision to the effect that funds pledged by donors must be disclosed before commencement of the implementation of the project including the identity of the donors , the mode of disbursement and the conditions attached to the funding by the donor . Section 29 also provides that the assets transferred to build the capacity of an organisation should be done through the commission which will identify the operation criteria . <p> The irony is that there is a weightier matter in our own national budget implementation laws , which our legislators are still pussyfooting around up till this moment . <p> It was just @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in ThisDay newspaper entitled , " FG to track donor funds through annual budget " , stated that the Minister of Budget and National Planning , Udoma Udo-Udoma , had hinted that donor funds would henceforth be captured in annual budgets . " The minister did not elaborate . Interestingly , the writer immediately followed the report with a comment , " It is still hazy how the government intends to pursue this objective . " <p> The question then is , why would lawmakers be hasty in chasing after NGOs on the issue of fiscal transparency while the country 's own fiscal governance is punctuated by dubious opacity ? Which one is more atrocious : obfuscation of donor sources by NGOs or keeping Nigerians in the dark concerning funding sources in national budget ? <p> Meanwhile , we must not forget that NGOs already have laws , legislation and regulations that guide every detail of their operations , whereas the National Assembly is not under any legal mandate to put donor funds under separate budget heads . <p> Secondly , the environment sector will suffer when environmental NGOs are @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Federal Government is not bothered about efficiently managing ecological funds . Most of the work done in the sector as of the today are by civil society organisations ; the government makes little impact . And , these grassroots organisations are like foot soldiers , they depend on shoe string budgets , and they get the job done . My fear is that in seeking to implement jackboot laws against NGOs , the government will destroy an entire sector . <p> The first test of government presence should be when one takes into account almost zero number environmental health officers employed in local government areas . In fact , how many people in Nigeria are aware of the existence of these health officers ? They were called sanitary inspectors back in the days , but today they seem to have disappeared . <p> It is only NGO programme officers that teach schoolchildren hand-washing ; that take them on ecological excursions ; that construct toilets for villagers ; that mentor displaced people . I am a witness to how two young Nigerians working under the platform of a then obscure NGO initiated the chain of events that saved lead-poisoned children in Bagega , Zamfara State . I am also aware of how the Lagos State Government totally depended on unknown , one-man-run NGOs to execute its Climate Change Club policy in Lagos secondary schools . <p> If the truth must be told , NGOs in Nigeria of today are like the informal sector ; they are employing youths , and giving many other adults the only hope for anything resembling entrepreneurship in a country ridden with infrastructure deficit and lacklustre political leadership .
