<h> Cities of the 21st century look at transport in terms of creating choices . Wellington does n't seem able to do this <p> ROLAND SAPSFORD <p> Last updated 05:00 , December 19 2017 <p> MAARTEN HOLL/STUFF <p> Roland Sapsford : Give the airport a target of 25 per cent of arrivals by public transport for passengers catching a flight and allow it to innovate to that end . <p> OPINION : Let 's Get Wellington Moving ( LGWM ) elicited a huge response from Wellingtonians . Thousands shared their thoughts , feelings and ideas , which gave rise to some good high-level goals . Yet , as they say , when the rubber hits the road , it 's a different story . <p> Back in 1963 consultants De Leuw Cather said Wellington would need an underground rail system soon , but that we should first build two tunnels and a trenched motorway . Fifty-four years later LGWM gave us much the same message about priorities ! <p> Imagine how much better our city would be if , in 1963 , instead of the current motorway we 'd had @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ underground rail loop as far as Newtown . <p> Fresh thinking seems beyond our civic leaders , who stick to outdated plans even in the face of new information . The trolley buses are the latest example . We were promised hybrids and electric buses , but ended up with 20-year-old diesels . We have a shortage of bus capacity , while perfectly functional electric buses are locked away and the near-new overhead network is dismantled . <p> LGWM is heading towards the same dead end . The problem is one of mind-set . LGWM is stuck thinking in traditional traffic engineering terms . It focuses on flows of traffic , addressing congestion and providing capacity for predicted trips . <p> Cities of the 21st century , on the other hand , look at transport from the perspective of human needs and creating choices . Focusing on why people travel the way they do , when they do , means we are much more creative about meeting those needs in ways that allow a city to function better for everyone . <p> As an example , a traffic engineering approach @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to the airport ? " A people-focused approach asks , " How do we help people catch aeroplanes more easily ? " <p> How indeed ? As it happens , only about 25 per cent of people in cars leaving the Terrace Tunnel are heading for the Mt Victoria tunnel . And between a quarter and a third of peak-time vehicles using the Mt Vic Tunnel are taxis and Uber heading between the CBD and the airport . If we can enable one-third of the people making those taxi and Uber trips to choose public transport instead , peak congestion at the Basin would virtually disappear . <p> So how could we achieve this ? <p> First , create downtown check-in and bag drops for travellers ; say , one at the railway station and one in the Courtenay Place area . <p> Second , create a guaranteed connection from these to the airport with excellent public transport ; say , with new articulated trolley buses running via the Mt Vic bus tunnel and the Coutts St tunnel under the airport ( yes , there is one ) with a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ target of 25 per cent of arrivals by public transport and allow it to innovate to that end . Without such a target , the airport profits from parking charges and has little interest in public transport . Within two years , we could see city-wide benefits . <p> Longer-term options include light rail and congestion pricing . Neither gets a serious mention from LGWM , which gave us only scenarios conceived of in narrow , traffic engineering terms , consequently recycling that same old 1963 motorway/tunnel plan . There is , quite literally , no Plan B. <p> LGWM 's inability to look beyond yesterday 's ideas has led to some almost comic inconsistencies . When asked why the existing Pirie St bus tunnel was n't considered for airport rapid transit , the answer was that it could be disruptive to people living nearby ; yet the 10-year disruption from building two tunnels and digging a four-lane trench across Te Aro does n't even register . <p> To get a fresh start , we need to stop arguing cars versus buses versus bikes , and recognise that people -- @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and ride buses and trains . <p> And a fresh start means having a clear vision about the future of our city and our shared goals . Most of us are only too aware of climate change , and the need for exercise and clean air . What we need are transport choices that make it easier to avoid driving . If we look at our city and its transport from this perspective we begin generating creative solutions . <p> What if we said we want 30 per cent less traffic by 2030 , and 70 per cent of what 's left to be electric ? To get there , let 's actually make use of the hundreds of good ideas that Wellingtonians have poured out over the last 50 years -- currently being ignored by ' experts ' stuck in 1963 . <p> Wellington has become a global leader of the worst sort . Since the Paris climate agreement was signed , we are the only city in the entire world to replace an electric transport system with diesels . <p> Hardly our " nuclear-free moment " . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ human needs and people 's choices , and get a broader range of cost-effective , creative options in front of Wellingtonians .
