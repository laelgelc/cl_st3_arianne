<h> Paris agreement could cost NZ $36b <p> Officials have warned the Government that meeting its greenhouse gas commitments under the Paris Agreement could cost up to $36 billion by 2030 . Eloise Gibson reports from the Briefing for the Incoming Minister for Climate Change . <p> New Zealand needs to start focusing as much on adapting to climate change as it does on preventing it , officials have urged in a briefing to the incoming Minister for Climate Change , James Shaw . <p> Given New Zealand 's climate is already changing , we should put more effort into readying for changes that could threaten our farm-heavy economy and our way of life , says the briefing . <p> Despite knowing what 's coming , the response so far has largely been reactive -- responding to floods , droughts , and coastal erosion as they happen , rather than the " deliberate , planned and coordinated " approach that is needed , says the summary . <p> Elsewhere , the briefing skewers a popular excuse often used by small countries to justify not acting strongly to curb emissions , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . It notes that while New Zealand generates less than 0.2 percent of global emissions " the sum total of small actors ' emissions is significant ... countries that individually contribute less than one percent of global emissions together make up over a quarter of global emissions . " <p> Our high per-capita emissions also pose a risk to " brand New Zealand " , as readers of the international news will already know . <p> But the cost of bringing emissions down is not small . The briefing puts the cost of meeting New Zealand 's first commitment under the Paris Agreement at $14 billion to $36 billion between 2021 and 2030 . That 's the estimated cost to remove about 220 million tons of carbon from our economy , by cutting domestic emissions , planting trees and buying carbon credits from overseas . The figures assume there is no carbon price applied to agriculture , no technological leaps , and conservative growth in forestry . <p> The briefing advises the Minister that there is only a short window to set a plan for meeting New Zealand 's first @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ below 2005 levels by 2030 . <p> It advises him to look at the " full range of options " for lowering agricultural methane and nitrous oxide emissions , including thoroughly weighing up bringing farming into the Emissions Trading Scheme . <p> The round-up notes that while New Zealand has invested in Research and Development , and farms have increased productivity , which lowers emissions-per-kilo of food , there are currently no direct measures aimed at reducing farming gases . <p> " The current approach also does not position the sector well to be able to demonstrate its contribution to reducing domestic or global emissions , should this be required through changes in market preferences , or to pursue new export opportunities , " says the briefing . <p> On the positive side , cleaning up rivers might lower greenhouse gases by the back door .
