<h> Auckland takes out global waste minimisation award <p> Last updated 09:47 , December 6 2017 <p> SUPPLIED <p> The Compost Collective 's Pip Beagley , centre , teaches a hands-on composting workshop . Auckland Council has been recognised for its initiatives to divert waste from landfill and promote recycling and composting . <p> Auckland has taken out a global award for its efforts to reduce waste . <p> The Cities4ZeroWaste prize is part of the C40 Cities Bloomberg Philanthropies Awards , which recognise cities that demonstrate climate action leadership . <p> It was announced at the North American Climate Summit in Chicago on December 5 ( US time ) . <p> MATTHEW CATTIN/FAIRFAX NZ <p> Mayor Phil Goff said Auckland 's approach to becoming a zero-waste city should serve as a model to other cities . " <p> However , the phased approach was necessary to identify areas for improvement , the application said . <p> Deputy chair of the Environment and Community Committee , Councillor Alf Filipaina represented Auckland at the Climate Summit in Chicago to collect the award . <p> " This is a result of close collaboration between our council leaders and local communities who both believe strongly in building a sustainable future for Auckland . " <p> Filipaina said they were working closely with local communities to inspire Aucklanders to think more about where their waste goes after they put it out on the kerb , and then ask how they can change their habits to produce less and re-use more . <p> Mayor Phil Goff said being selected strongly reinforced Auckland 's approach and progress towards becoming a zero-waste city by 2040 . <p> " Given the increasingly important role cities must play in driving climate action , Auckland 's approach to reducing waste should be considered as a model that other cities can @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ global cities share common challenges , our expertise and ground-breaking work towards waste reduction in Auckland is providing leadership in this space to another 90 of the world 's greatest cities helping to improve our odds in tackling climate change , " Goff said . <p> Ninety-two cities from around the world made applications for the awards . <p> New York City and Dar Es Salaam , Tanzania were recognised for their sustainable transportation , while Chicago and Copenhagen received awards for their focus on clean and renewable energy . <p> Paris Mayor and C40 chairwoman Anne Hidalgo said the winners showed " stunning innovation " in their efforts to shape the coming century . <p> " These projects should be considered blueprints that other cities can adapt to accelerate their own efforts .
