<h> Government to release long-delayed sea level rules <p> The new Climate Change Minister , James Shaw , will release long-delayed new guidance to councils on planning for new coastal developments and sea level rise on Friday . <p> But coastal developments that were approved during the guidelines ' long gestation period , after factoring in lower-than-ideal amounts of sea level rise , will have to be treated as existing development , says Shaw . That means they 'll be dealt with alongside the billions of dollars worth of homes and infrastructure that are already in the potential future risk zone . <p> The news comes after a Newsroom special inquiry found that , in the Coromandel alone , hundreds of new , permanent land titles were created on the coast in the past two years , after modelling at most 1m of sea level rise . <p> One development of 73 apartments factored in just 0.49m higher seas in its engineering and flood risk report . <p> Former Climate Change minister Paula Bennett told Newsroom today that , despite National not publishing the guidance , coastal councils already had @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the public by the Green Party , advised councils to look at the impact of 1.9m higher seas on flood risk before letting big , new housing developments proceed . <p> The draft was held back from being formally published so that councils could be consulted , says Bennett . <p> Asked if councils would have treated developments differently had the guidance been released sooner , Bennett told Newsroom that councils affected by rising seas " did have access to that information " . <p> " They were being consulted very early on and that 's why there was a delay in releasing it publicly ... we were consulting with those very councils , " she said . <p> Yet when Newsroom previously asked one council dealing with coastal subdivisions about the gap between the 1m higher seas it is using to test new developments , and the 1.9m test in the draft Ministry for the Environment guidance , Thames-Coromandel District Council said : " The guidance you refer to continues to be in draft form and the most recent formal guidance from the Ministry for the Environment continues to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ guidance being released officially before we update our assumptions as a council .
