<h> The Lost Species review : Christopher Kemp and the mystery of the unnamed <h> By Steven Carroll <p> 6 December 2017 - 11:15pm <p> The Lost Species <p> Christopher Kemp <p> The Lost Species . By Christopher Kemp . <p> Photo : Supplied <p> University of Chicago Press , $59.99 <p> Christopher Kemp , a natural scientist , thinks of all living species on earth as a symphony , each one a note . But of the estimated 10 million species ( from vertebrates to insects and plants ) only about 2 million have been named . All over the world in museums and universities there are collections of unnamed species -- such as a beetle collected by Charles Darwin , stored in London for 180 years , and recently christened in Chattanooga , Tennessee . A beetle might not seem important , but all notes in the symphony of living things affect each other . As Kemp says , from them we learn the big picture of how the eco-system works , and the impact of such things as climate change . It might seem a specialist text , but Kemp 's passion is infectious .
