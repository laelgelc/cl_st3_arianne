<p> Hobart City Council is to become one of only a handful nationally to sever ties with financial institutions that support the fossil fuel industry . <p> Monday night 's council meeting voted nine to three in favour of changing the council 's investment and borrowing policy . <p> In 2015 , the policy was amended to give higher priority to financial institutions not involved in the fossil fuel industry . <p> At the last full meeting for the year , aldermen agreed to change the policy , in line with calls from petitioners for action on climate change . <p> Lord Mayor Sue Hickey said it showed the council was getting on with the job of tackling climate change and taking a leadership role . <p> Greens alderman Anna Reynolds said the latest change meant council funds would no longer be held in any of the big four banks -- ANZ , NAB , Westpac and the Commonwealth Bank -- or any of their subsidiaries . <p> " This decision to shift from the banks that are investing in fossil fuels is a good progressive one but it 's @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " It is one based on good research and it 's based on what is happening in the financial markets at the moment . <p> " A lot of organisations and institutions are looking to invest their funds away from fossil fuels , so it 's a good decision for the future and a sensible one for the ratepayers as well . " <h> ' There are good mainstream banking alternatives ' <p> More than 30 councils across the country have adopted similar policies , but Hobart is one of only a few to fully divest funds away from financial institutions which support the oil and gas industry . <p> Alderman Reynolds said there were many alternative institutions to choose from . <p> " We put our money in term deposits with banks and this policy will mean that we do n't put that money with any bank that is investing in the fossil fuel industry , " she said . <p> " However the good news is there are many mainstream banks with good credit rating that do n't invest in fossil fuels any more .
