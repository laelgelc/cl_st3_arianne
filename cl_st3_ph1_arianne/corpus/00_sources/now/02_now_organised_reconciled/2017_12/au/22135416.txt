<p> THE saying that " there are plenty more fish in the sea " is no longer ringing true . <p> Unsustainable fishing , climate change and most concerningly , marine debris , is contributing to the disappearance of Australian aquatic life at an alarming rate . <p> Within the last 40 years , half of the world 's marine life has disappeared . <p> Approximately three-quarters of the rubbish along the Australian coast is plastic . Most is from Australian sources , with debris concentrated near urban centres . <p> In coastal and offshore waters , most of the floating debris is plastic , with the density ranging from a few thousand pieces per square kilometre to more than 40,000 pieces of plastic per square kilometre . <p> During a time when so many Australians embrace sea , sun and sand , the Foundation for National Parks &amp; Wildlife ( FNPW ) has launched a public appeal to support efforts dedicated to the removal and prevention of marine debris . <p> As the quantity of debris increases in the marine environment , so does the likelihood of impacts to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ volume of rubbish humans release into marine systems is growing at an exponential rate . <p> Even toothpaste and personal care products can have plastic microbeads in them , which can be mistakenly eaten by a range of marine species . <p> Plastic pollution has already affected at least 267 species worldwide , with some estimates suggesting that at least one million marine animals are killed each year from plastic pollution . <p> These fatalities are a result of ingestion , starvation , suffocation , infection , drowning and entanglement . <p> The ocean is such a beautiful and vast ecosystem but it 's fragile , its inhabitants are in danger and if we do n't do something now there wo n't be anything left for future generations . <p> By 2050 there will be more plastic in the ocean than fish .
