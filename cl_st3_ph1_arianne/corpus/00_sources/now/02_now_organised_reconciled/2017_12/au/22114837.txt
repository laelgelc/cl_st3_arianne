<h> Gold at the end of rainbow week <p> 8 December 2017 - 11:05pm <p> The applause that erupted on Thursday from MPs on both sides in the House of Representatives after the same-sex marriage bill was passed came like a cloudburst after a long drought . A year of stalemate and tedious political trench warfare was over . The burden of failure on a question of ordinary human decency had been lifted , and -- for a while anyway -- politicians were free to react like normal human beings . <p> The outcome is first of all a victory for the gay and lesbian community , for whom marriage represented more than just validation of the legal status of their relationships . Legalising same-sex marriage , after it had been delegitimised under John Howard , symbolised full social acceptance of their relationships , their sexual orientation , their very identity . That community should not have been put through the trial-by-plebiscite that was ordained for them , but it no longer matters ; they have survived it unscathed and have emerged triumphant . It suggests that , whatever the legal position , acceptance of gay and lesbian relationships was already so widespread and so well entrenched that Thursday 's final step of legitimisation was a very small , very easy one to take . Opponents who worry that it means the collapse of accepted values and the undermining of tradition can calm down . The values our society accepts today are not what they imagine ; tradition , as it always does , has already evolved to fit in with contemporary values .
