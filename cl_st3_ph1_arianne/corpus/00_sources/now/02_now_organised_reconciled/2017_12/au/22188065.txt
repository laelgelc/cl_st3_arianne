The legislation that underpins the ERF contains three such tests , which are actually very strong : <p> Newness : is a project new ? Has work on it already begun ? If it has , the project is ineligible , because it is considered already commercially viable . <p> Existing regulations : is a particular project or emissions abatement already required by law ? If so , the project is ineligible for ERF funding . <p> Other government funding : does a project have access to other sources of government funding ? If it does , the proponent should use those funds instead . <p> If these three tests were mandated for all projects submitted to the ERF , it would be filled with projects that truly deliver new environmental benefit .
