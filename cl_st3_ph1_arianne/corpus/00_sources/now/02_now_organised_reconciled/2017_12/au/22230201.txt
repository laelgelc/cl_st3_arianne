<p> UQ CEED postdoctoral researcher Dr Matthew Holden said the team identified a process aimed to overcome the deadlock on ivory . <p> " We believe there has n't been a resolution because each side of the debate has failed to recognise the other 's moral perspectives , " Dr Holden said . <p> " People are more likely to unconsciously challenge information coming from someone with a different moral view , like we see with climate change , " he said . <p> " Drawing on what we 've learned from other polarised debates , we developed a structured process aimed to help stakeholders better understand each other 's perspective , and make informed policy decisions . " <p> The researchers recognise the politics around ivory policy are challenging , but urge countries and organisations committed to elephant conservation to initiate such a process . <p> Lead author Dr Duan Biggs from Griffith University , who began the research as a UQ CEED postdoc researcher , said the policy impasse was wasting valuable conservation resources .
