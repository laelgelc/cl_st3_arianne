<h> The Environmental Protection Authority is asleep on the biggest job of all <h> Mark Wakeham <p> When oBikes were fished out of the Yarra River , the Victorian Environment Protection Authority ( EPA ) threatened the controversial share bike scheme with fines of more than $3000 . When a Coolaroo company washed chemicals into a stormwater drain , it got a fine of $7929 . And when someone gets caught flicking a cigarette butt out the window this summer , they can expect to cough up $317 . <p> So when Victorian power stations dump 15,000,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide pollution into the sky , contributing to climate change , what 's the penalty ? <p> Zero . The EPA lets them get away with it scot free . When it comes to the biggest environmental challenge we 've ever faced -- climate change -- our environmental watchdog is snoozing on the job . <p> This should have changed when the Andrews government introduced a $162 million reform package designed to bring the EPA into the 21st century . The reforms include strengthening the Environment Protection Act to put @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ EPA greater powers to prosecute with increased penalties , and creating a new Chief Environmental Scientist position . So far , so good . <p> But on a key recommendation relating to regulating greenhouse gases there 's been no action . This is despite EPA programs and policies in other states and countries , notably the US , being the main driver of cutting greenhouse pollution and cleaning up the economy . It 's also despite the recently strengthened Victorian Climate Change Act confirming that the EPA has the power to regulate greenhouse pollution . <p> We 're left with a glaring inconsistency . The EPA currently licences and regulates the state 's largest polluters for 13 different types of airborne emissions including benzene , sulfur dioxide and particulates ( though unfortunately not always to international standards ) . But emitting carbon dioxide , the largest contributor to global warming ? There 's no constraint at all . <p> Advertisement <p> It 's not for lack of options .
