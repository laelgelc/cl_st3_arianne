<p> No edition of AFR Weekend focused on megatrends would be complete without some analysis of how the financial world is responding to the one trend that could destroy the planet -- global warming . <p> It is refreshing to know that while Australian politicians have been running around in circles and failing to agree a policy for decarbonisation , the world 's largest institutional investors have been marshalling their forces to make boards of directors accountable . <p> This single-issue shareholder activism , which is on a scale never seen before , has spawned the creation of many powerful global lobby groups . <p> The largest of these is Climate Action 100+ , which represents 225 investors with more than $US26.3 trillion ( $34.3 trillion ) in assets under management . <p> It is working in partnership with the Asia Investor Group on Climate Change , the Sydney-based Investor Group on Climate Change , Institutional Investors Group on Climate Change and Principles for Responsible Investment . <p> Regulators were quick to put their views on the risks associated with climate change and how it may impact upon the balance @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> Bank of England governor Mark Carney led the charge with a seminal speech in 2015 .
