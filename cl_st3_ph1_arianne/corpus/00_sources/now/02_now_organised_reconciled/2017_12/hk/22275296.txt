<p> A group of central banks and financial industry supervisors from Asia , Europe and North America have established a network to help strengthen global response to meet the goals of the 2016 Paris agreement on climate change . <p> The group -- known as Network of Central Banks and Supervisors for Greening the Financial System -- will also help " enhance the role of the financial system to manage risks " , and " mobilise capital for green and low-carbon investments " , it says in a joint statement on December 13 . <p> According to the group , " financing the transition to a green and low carbon economy consistent with the ' well below 2 degree Celsius goal ' set out in the Paris agreement and promoting environmental sustainable growth are among the major challenges of our time . " <p> " In the process of responding to environmental and climate challenges , there are both opportunities and vulnerabilities for financial institutions and the financial system as a whole , " it adds . <p> It will conduct a " stock taking exercise " next year , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ The network comprises the central banks of China , England , France , Germany , Mexico , Netherlands and Singapore , as well as the French banking regulator and the Swedish financial supervisory authority . <p> According to Vivek Jamwal , practice head of risk and research at Stradegi Investment Management Consulting in Singapore , the move by the central banks and regulators will " significantly " help the cause of environmental , social and governance ( ESG ) investing . <p> " As the green financing is a relatively new development , it will help central banks to learn from each other , and share experiences on how green financing is effective , and distinguish green financing from traditional financing disguised as green , " Mr. Jamwal tells Asia Asset Management . " From an ESG perspective , this is a much needed push . "
