<h> Revival of springs in water-deficient Uttarakhand villages <p> People of two villages Jendo and Latau in Kalsi tehsil of Dehradun district are struggling with acute shortage of water and are compelled to meet their requirements by buying water from water tankers . <p> TNN Updated : Dec 9 , 2017 , 20:58 IST <p> DEHRADUN : People of two villages Jendo and Latau in Kalsi tehsil of Dehradun district are struggling with acute shortage of water and are compelled to meet their requirements by buying water from water tankers . International agencies - United Nation Development Program ( UNDP ) and Swiss Agency for Development and Cooperation ( SDC ) - are providing technical support for the project aimed at revival of springs in the area . The water department will provide the funds . The villages were selected as per survey conducted by the Climate Change and Environment department two years ago . <p> RN Jha , nodal officer of climate change and environment department , said , " Kalsi was found to be among the most susceptible areas , facing acute shortage of water , in a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to frame state climate change action plan two years ago . This is the reason , the pilot project of revival of springs was started in these two most affected villages of Kalsi . Once it becomes successful , it will be replicated in other villages which are facing similar situation in future . " <p> Subhrata Paul , expert with UNDP , said , " The villages are situated on the top of the hill from where the entire rainy water is draining off through steep slope . This is the reason the ground water is also not getting recharged , and springs have almost dried up . Under the project , new dug ponds , tanks and trenches would be created to store the rainy water which would recharge ground water and provide water supply to the villages through solar pumping . " <p> Vineeta Chauhan , gram pradhan of Jendo-Latau villages , said , " A water tank built by the district administration in 1957 in these two villages is in a dilapidated state . The pipeline was laid down but five km away from the village @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the way earlier to fetch water . But since the spring from where water is extracted has almost dried up , villagers are totally dependent on water tanks . " <p> Rashmi Bajaj , consultant with UNDP , said , " The water department has agreed to fund this project , costing around one crore . Before augmenting the project , a geo-hydrologican study would be conducted in order to increase the water sustainability . The project would benefit 800 hectare area including the fields near the villages . So people would be able to do farming and earn good income from it .
