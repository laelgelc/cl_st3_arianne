In fact , one survey estimates that today one in every three Indian households owns a two-wheeler . <p> What explains the enduring popularity of two-wheelers ? In one of the fastest growing economies in the world , two-wheeler ownership is a practical aspiration in small towns and rural areas , and a tactic to deal with choked roads in the bigger cities . Two-wheelers have also allowed more women to commute independently with the advent of gearless scooters and mopeds . Together , these factors have led to phenomenal growth in overall two-wheeler sales , which rose by 27.5% in the past five years , according to the Society of Indian Automobile Manufacturers ( SIAM ) . Indeed , the ICE 2016 360 survey says that two-wheelers are used by 37% of metropolitan commuters to reach work , and are owned by @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ rural areas . <p> Amid this exponential growth , experts have cautioned about two-wheelers ' role in compounding the impact of pollution . Largely ignored in measures to control vehicular pollution , experts say two-wheelers too need to be brought in the ambit of pollution control as they contribute across most factors determining vehicular pollution - engine technology , total number of vehicles , structure and age of vehicles and fuel quality . In fact , in major Indian cities , two-thirds of pollution load is due to two-wheelers . They give out 30% of the particulate matter load , 10 percentage points more than the contribution from cars .
