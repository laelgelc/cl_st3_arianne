<p> When I was growing up , in the 1960s and 1970s , we had what my nan used to call " proper weather " . Snow in winter , showers in spring , sun ( or at least , sunny intervals ) in summer and gales in autumn . Britain 's weather may have been changeable by the day , but the seasons were seemingly set in stone , with a reassuringly predictable regularity . <p> That certainly suited the country 's fauna and flora . Wild animals and plants , and by extension their habitats , evolved to cope with short-term unpredictability and long-term stability . If change did occur , it happened slowly , over decades or centuries ; rather than rapidly , in a single year . <p> Now , according to the latest annual report on weather and wildlife from the National Trust , published last week , all that has changed , with the weather in 2017 more topsy-turvy than ever .
