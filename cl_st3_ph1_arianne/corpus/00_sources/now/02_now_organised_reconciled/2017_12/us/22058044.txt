<h> Computer Simulations Reveal Roots of Drug Resistance <p> Bacterial efflux pumps , such as the P. aeruginosa MexAB-OprM pump shown here , are one of the dominant molecular mechanisms available to Gram-negative pathogens for removing toxins , including antibiotics . Inactivation of the pump assembly and function would be a major step for reducing bacterial multidrug resistance . One type of efflux pump , which until recently had only been studied in parts , was recently modeled in its entirety and simulated using supercomputers at Los Alamos National Laboratory . The findings , published November 28 in Scientific Reports , offer a better understanding of the motions and functions of efflux pumps . The work exploits the Laboratory 's extensive modeling and supercomputing simulation capabilities developed in support of its national security mission . <p> For this study , the researchers focused on efflux pumps inside the bacteria Pseudomonas aeruginosa , which can cause serious illnesses such as pneumonia and sepsis .
