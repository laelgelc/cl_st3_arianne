<p> In his Dec. 13 guest viewpoint , Travis Joseph , CEO of the American Forest Resource Council , claims that federal forests have been burning or dying at an alarming rate due to forest fires , disease and a lack of management . Echoing the timber industry 's archaic mantra for public forests -- log them or lose them -- Joseph declares , " We need to act now " ! <p> A Forest Service report , " Forest Health Highlights in Oregon 2016 , " presents a far less dramatic view of forest health . In the summary , unbiased experts reassure us that " a healthy forest is never totally free of damaging insects , diseases , and other disturbances . " <p> Noting a slight increase in " tree damage and mortality from diseases , insects and other animals , " the report attributes this to years of drought stress . <p> The report also notes that " average temperatures were higher in Oregon over the past 30 years and precipitation was below the 30-year average . Weather experts predict Western forest fires may be bigger and hotter due to human-caused warming , creating longer droughts . Late-season droughts like the one driving the huge Thomas Fire in California are becoming more common . Forests are n't more fire prone simply from " a lack of management , " but rather a lack of control over climate change . <p> Drought-driven wildfires , typically pushed by hot , low-humidity days and strong winds , often rip right through supposedly benign forest manipulations like thinning or fuel reduction . <p> Couched in candy-coated terms like " responsible science-based management , " logging is the timber industry 's profitable cure for everything that is said to ail our forests . <p> Joseph writes , " When we focus on whether we should be using more renewable resources , and where those resources should come from , we can have a more constructive dialogue about public forest policy . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ forest policy ? They are not ( and were never intended to be ) fast-growing , chemically sterilized tree farms . Managed by the Forest Service and the Bureau of Land Management for the use and enjoyment of all Americans , they 've been comparatively well-kept for more than a century . Richly diverse , they are full of clean waters , fish , wildlife and spectacular scenery . <p> Private forests account for three-quarters of Oregon 's annual timber harvest , with most of this volume coming from corporate lands .
