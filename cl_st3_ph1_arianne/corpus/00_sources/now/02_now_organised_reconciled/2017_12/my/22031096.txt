<h> Southern Chile iceberg splits from glacier , threatens navigation <p> A massive iceberg has caused problems for many people in southern Chile by splitting from the Grey glacier . <p> PUERTO NATALES : The recent calving of a large iceberg from a southern Chilean glacier threatens local ship navigation and could result in flooding for coastal communities , experts said . <p> An iceberg measuring approximately 350 by 380 metres ( 1,150 by 1,250 feet ) broke from the Grey Glacier in far southern Chile in late November . <p> The size of the break surprised local scientists who monitor the glacier . <p> " Events like this are part of a short-term irreversible tendency " due to rising global temperatures , said Ra ? l Cordero , a climate change expert at the Universidad de Santiago . <p> The iceberg now seems like a large chunk of ice , " but it will become a threat " since it will move out to sea and break up into smaller pieces , said Ricardo Jana , a glaciologist at the Chilean National Antarctic Institute . <p> Given its size @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ problems for area navigation , Jana said . <p> The icebergs will also contribute to a rise in the sea level , " putting coastal communities at risk for possible flooding , " Jana said . <p> The Grey glacier is located at the Torres del Paine National Park , located approximately 3,200 kilometres ( 2,000 miles ) south of the capital Santiago . <p> Over the past 30 years the glacier -- now measuring some 270 square kilometres -- has lost about two square kilometres of ice . <p> The glacier is part of the Southern Patagonia Ice Fields , the third largest land-based ice field after Antarctica and Greenland .
