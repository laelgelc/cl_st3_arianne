<p> If nations fail to meet global targets on climate change , a chunk of frozen soil bigger than Australia will melt , sending planet-warming gases into the atmosphere , British scientists say . <p> Permafrost - soil that has been frozen for at least two years - is more sensitive and susceptible to global warming than previously thought , the scientists found , in the first study to quantify how much permafrost could be lost due to climate change . <p> " The amount of permafrost that thaws under global warming is going to be very large , and this is the first time we 've really put numbers on it , " said Sarah Chadburn , the lead author of the study and a research fellow at the University of Exeter on Monday . <p> Permafrost , mostly found in high-latitude regions like the Arctic , stores large quantities of carbon dioxide and methane , which are released into the atmosphere if the soil melts and decomposes , according to the researchers from Exeter , the University of Leeds , and Britain 's official weather service , the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ journal Nature Climate Change , found that for every degree the world warms , 4 million sq km of permafrost would be lost . <p> An increase in global warming by 2 degrees Celsius would thaw more than 40 per cent of the earth 's permafrost , according to the study . <p> The 2015 Paris Agreement on climate change aims to limit this , by using the cooperation of almost 200 countries to attempt to keep the global temperature increase " well below " 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels . <p> But at the current level of warming , Chadburn said , " you could lose almost all of the permafrost " . <p> An estimated 35 million people currently live in cities or towns on top of permafrost , and thawed soil could cause the ground to become unstable , the scientists said . <p> The researchers said this would put buildings , roads and other infrastructure at risk of collapsing . <p> " The ability to more accurately assess permafrost loss can hopefully feed into a greater understanding of the impact of global warming and potentially @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a permafrost research scientist with the Met Office , added in a statement .
