<p> In an increasingly isolationist world , with governmental polices around the globe turning inward , we can not forget that nations must work together if we are to effectively tackle our most pressing environmental crises . Climate change , pollutants , chemicals -- none of these respect national borders as they travel freely between our countries , wreaking whatever havoc they may . <p> This is why Canada 's recent ratification of the Minamata Convention on mercury is an important step in the right direction . Canada , along with 127 other countries , has been a signatory to the Minamata Convention since 2013 . Now only ten countries need to ratify the agreement for its entry into force . <p> This treaty , named after the Japanese city of Minamata that experienced devastating incidents of mercury poisoning , is aimed at protecting humans and the environment from human-made mercury emissions . It would prohibit mercury mining and international trade in mercury and mercury-containing products . <p> Why does this matter ? <p> It has been hard to escape the human stories chronicling the horrific effects that ingesting mercury @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ from numbness in the hands and feet ; muscle weakness ; damage to vision , hearing , and speech ; to depression and suicidal thoughts . <h> Grassy Narrows tragedy is just a fraction of the global problem <p> Here at home , we have witnessed the tragedy of Grassy Narrows , where that community 's lakes and sources of food have been contaminated with mercury from a shuttered paper mill upstream , with terrible and continuing impacts on that population 's physical and mental health .
