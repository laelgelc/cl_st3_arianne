<p> The environmental and economic cases against oil exploration on Anticosti Island are by now obvious . It 's clear that the deal the former Parti Qu ? b ? cois government signed with a consortium of energy companies in February 2014 was a huge mistake . <p> The Couillard Liberals are not the ones who got Quebecers into this mess , but it is good to see they are now working to get the province out of it . The government confirmed this week that it is seeking to negotiate an exit from the contract , which the private firms have suggested is worth $200 million . <p> As Premier Philippe Couillard noted this week , the project has " zero social acceptability . " It is opposed by the island 's municipality , nearby First Nations and environmentalists , among others . The premier himself has also been long opposed . <p> Located in the Gulf of St. Lawrence , the huge island ( almost 8,000 square kilometres ) is home to deer , salmon , gorgeous scenery and a couple hundred people . Hydraulic fracturing , or fracking , would be used , a controversial process that , among other issues , has been linked to earthquakes and that would consume vast amounts of water , including from the rivers that are home to wild Atlantic salmon . With development , there would also be the problem of how to get the resource to market , something that could not be done without environmental damage . <p> And since the PQ government signed the deal , awareness of climate change has grown , spurring moves toward decarbonizing our economy .
