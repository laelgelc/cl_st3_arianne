<h> Water is about to change Our world <p> HOW high will sea levels rise ? The answers are worth understanding if we care about our children 's world . <p> Sea levels are rising . It is no longer a question of whether this will be a problem . It is a matter of how high and how fast . Recent science brings us both uncertainty and answers . Together they explain why geopolitics may soon become a more common word . <p> Science studies evidence of two kinds of steady sea-level increase driven by climate change . One : ( with some time lag ) as the Earth gets warmer , so do its oceans ; the water column expands and rises like liquid in a thermometer , but ocean water-columns can be kilometres high . The other : ( more visible and easier to understand ) as air temperatures rise , glaciers are melting . <p> A recent perspective by two American geoscientists in the leading journal Science critiques international studies of the sea-level rise that 's due to warming water and melting glaciers . Other cities , such as Miami , may be out of luck . Low-lying coral islands , such as Kiribati , could fare a bit better . There 's a reason why those islands all lie just above high tide : wave action built them . Left alone and given time , the rising ocean may give them a lift . <p> Before deciding how to invest ( or waste ) trillions of dollars , it would be useful to get a better handle on the problem we are setting out to solve . <p> And here 's the catch -- a third kind of increase is now known to be underway . The collapsing West Antarctic Ice Sheet ( WAIS ) will add much more than two metres to sea levels on many shores . <p> Nobody knows when this will happen . Simply put , the last great ice sheet left on Earth is now unstable . <p> It is not just melting ; it is falling . <p> The geoscientists ' perspective applies the **26;681;TOOLONG paradigm in which each thousand cubic miles of water falling in the oceans adds three-10ths of an inch to sea levels on all shores . That they mention the WAIS collapse at all is unusual . That they do n't mention effects of general relativity and crustal shape is par for the course . <p> But another study in the same journal shows these effects yield non-uniform sea-level rise from the half-million cubic miles of WAIS that 's mostly falling in . WAIS is so massive , its loss changes the shape of both the Earth 's surface and its gravitational field . <p> Bottom line : @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ levels rising by about four metres on top of those two metres from warming water and melting glaciers . <p> Washington , D.C. , may need to plan for at least six metres -- if the U.S. Congress thinks that city is worth saving , that is . <p> Though the crowded , low-lying deltas of the Ganges/Brahmaputra , Nile , Po , Irrawaddy , Yangtze , Mississippi and Indus rivers will see smaller total sea-level increases , plainly they ca n't be saved . These and other populated shorelines will be the source of millions of water refugees in our lifetimes . <p> War already has geopolitics and its economic consequences on the move .
