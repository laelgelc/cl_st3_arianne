<p> Vast swaths of the Saskatchewan landscape that have been protected from human interaction are expected to undergo significant " climate displacement " over the coming decades , according to the co-author of a new study on climate change . <p> New species of vegetation and wildlife will move into conservation areas , potentially driving those meant to be conserved into unprotected areas , said Marc-Andre Parisien , a researcher at Natural Resources Canada 's Northern Forestry Centre . <p> " The climate is changing , and it 's projected to change even more rapidly over the next 100 years , " Parisien said . " We wanted to know where the climates of those protected areas , of those parks , will be in the future . " <p> Published earlier this year in Global Change Biology , the study -- which Parisien conducted with U.S. and Spanish researchers -- examined hundreds of protected areas in North America , including national parks and smaller conservation areas . <p> " That gives us information we can integrate in our land use planning , " he said of the study 's @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ .
