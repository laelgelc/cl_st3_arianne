<p> Federal government scientists say they have devised an accurate way to directly measure air pollutants from oilsands mines and suggest industry estimates for certain harmful emissions have been much too low . <p> The research , published Monday in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences , focused on volatile organic compounds , or VOCs -- carbon-based substances that can be damaging to the environment and human health . <p> Oilsands companies have indirect ways of calculating their mines ' estimated VOC emissions . Methods include extrapolating from other substances they measure from smokestacks or from emissions associated with a specific activity , said lead author Shao-Meng Li , a senior research scientist at Environment and Climate Change Canada . <p> Li and his team set out to compare those figures against direct readings they took from the air above the mines . <p> Their experiment took measurements from a plane flown at various altitudes in a box-like pattern above oilsands mines in northeastern Alberta .
