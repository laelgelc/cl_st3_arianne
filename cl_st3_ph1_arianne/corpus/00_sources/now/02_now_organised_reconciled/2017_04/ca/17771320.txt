<h> Bold vision needed in the north <p> The Port of Churchill 's closure in 2016 was one of several setbacks for Manitoba 's northern region . <p> What causes uncertainty here in Manitoba and around the world ? While natural disasters , political upheavals and terrorism get much of our attention , insecurity can assume many guises . <p> Nowhere is this more evident than in the northern region of Manitoba . Here , the combination of resource extraction industries vulnerable to boom-and-bust cycles , geographic remoteness and the pressures of climate change are exposing this region , with already relatively high unemployment , to even more socioeconomic challenges and -- therefore -- more insecurity . <p> In 2016 alone , the Port of Churchill closed , rail services to communities along the Hudson Bay Railway were halved and The Pas nearly lost its paper mill , that town 's largest employer . Over the next three years , two northern mine closures are anticipated . Nonetheless , many northern residents are still anxious about what the future might bring . In the face of such uncertainty , the North needs a plan in place for better resilience . <p> In fact , the province did just that last year when it announced the Look North Strategy , a measure that will begin to address some of the North 's economic challenges . However , with Canada 's northernmost regions experiencing greater climate change and northern Manitoba 's reliance on the natural resources that may be affected , we need a plan that also considers ecosystems as a whole . <p> Such a plan would look at the bigger picture , considering the impact of major changes and developments to the area as a whole . A fraction of the north is included in the Partners FOR the Saskatchewan River Basin . All of this is good news , but this southern trend barely has reached the northern part of our province . <p> This is actually not such a novel idea . In 1999 , the concept was championed by the Consultation on Sustainable Development Implementation , a multi-stakeholder consultation convened by the Manitoba government . Among other things , it recommended that natural boundaries such as watersheds are a logical geographic delineation for planning efforts and that such initiatives could tie together economic , environmental and social considerations . <p> With ongoing challenges in the North , the region needs its own such effort . <p> Whether based on watershed geographies -- most of the population in the North lives in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in watersheds such as the Hayes and Seal -- or other boundaries , the people , industry and environment of the North all stand to gain from more comprehensive and integrated planning . <p> The federal government already is making some strides in this area .
