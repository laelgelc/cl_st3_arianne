<p> Monday , April 24 , 2017 , 1:05 PM - A California-based artist is adding a relevant twist to decades-old posters of U.S. national parks . Hannah Rothstein 's latest collection , National Parks 2050 , juxtaposes the old with the new to present the eerie reality of natural landscapes across the country and around the globe . <p> The original posters came into creation between 1938 and 1941 , when a team of artists from the U.S. Works Progress Administration created the serigraphs for the country 's national parks . Today , myriad copies lie in gift shops at National Parks across the U.S. , printed as posters or post card replicas of the original works . <p> Rothstein 's collection , however , is an altogether different version of the posters -- one that highlights a futuristic image of the parks based on the state of the climate in 2017 . <p> " I was rather worried about a number of issues after the election in November . " <p> Rothstein took time to consider how best to bring these issues to the public in a way that would connect to them . <p> " I , for a while now , have been making art projects and wanted to make one that addressed this topic in a way that felt familiar to people , " Rothstein says . Data on the issue , she adds , is abundant , but does n't always resonate with people . <p> " Words are powerful and numbers are meaningful and we certainly love data as a culture , but I think there 's something different in seeing the potential changes -- or likely changes , depending on how you look at it -- portrayed in a visual manner that feels a lot more emotional and a lot more close to home . " <p> Rothstein hopes her work sparks acknowledgement of the issue and meaningful discussion -- specifically , among those who find it easy to overlook the science of human impact on climate change . <p> " There are a lot of people , particularly people who are in positions of power , who are making decisions for a generation that 's going to be affected by this . They often do n't acknowledge that this is an issue , " Rothstein says .
