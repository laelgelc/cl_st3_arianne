<p> THE People 's Republic of Bangladesh recently commemorated its 46th year as an independent state with a formal banquet , attended by local government executives , foreign dignitaries and business leaders . <p> The country 's ambassador to the Philippines Asad Alam Siam spoke with the BusinessMirror about the significance of the celebration , as well as its plans to strengthen the relationship between Bangladesh and the Philippines . <p> Could you give a short perspective of this event ? We understand Bangladesh is celebrating a historic milestone . <p> Pakistan used to have two parts : the present-day Pakistan , and the other one is the East Pakistan . In between is India . Between the two " Pakistans " , they had nothing in common . We can say we are culturally different from the Pakistanis ; and in 1970 , there was an election for a democratic government which we won in majority , but we were denied democracy . The liberation we earned was for democracy . There was a nine-month liberation war which began March 26 of that year . About three million people @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ December 16 we finally achieved independence of the land . <p> What are your plans to strengthen the relationship between Bangladesh and the Philippines ? <p> Our countries are , in many ways , similar because we have similar challenges to overcome . So we can learn from each other 's good practices , while we in Bangladesh can replicate and implement them . A major objective of my assignment here is to bring the two countries closer in terms of training and experience , specifically addressing climate change and disaster management . Another is to recover the heist money of our Central Bank . <p> What are your initial impressions of the Philippines ? <p> I 'm finding this country very friendly , and the people are very nice . The environment is the same as that of Bangladesh .
