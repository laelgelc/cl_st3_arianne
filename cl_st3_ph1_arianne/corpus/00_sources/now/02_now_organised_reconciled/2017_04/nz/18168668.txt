<p> The state 's Ocean Protection Council on Wednesday revised upward its predictions for how much water off California will rise as the climate warms . The forecast helps agencies in the nation 's most populous state plan for climate change as rising water seeps toward low-lying airports , highways and communities , especially in the San Francisco Bay Area . <p> Discoveries that ice sheets are melting increasingly fast in Antarctica , which holds nearly 90 percent of the world 's ice , largely spurred the change . <p> As fossil-fuel emissions warm the Earth 's atmosphere , melting Antarctic ice is expected to raise the water off California 's 1,100 miles ( 1,770 kilometers ) of coastline even more than for the world as a whole . <p> " Emerging science is showing us a lot more than even five years ago , " council deputy director Jenn Eckerle said Thursday . <p> Gov . Jerry Brown has mandated that state agencies take climate change into account in planning and budgeting .
