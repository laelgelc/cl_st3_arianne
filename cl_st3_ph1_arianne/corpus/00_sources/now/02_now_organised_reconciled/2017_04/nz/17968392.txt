<h> The last dance of Carla van Zon <p> Auckland Arts Festival boss Carla van Zon has left the spiegeltent . Triumphant but also very ill , she has retired . Simon Wilson pays tribute . <p> The day I interviewed Carla van Zon she was due on a flight to Wellington , where the airport was closed due to fog . So she 'd managed to get a new flight to Palmerston North . The arts festival had finished and they were cleaning out their offices on Wellesley St : boxes to storage , wheelie bins clogging the hallways . Outside , the rain was so heavy it sluiced down the street . Stressful ? <p> It was , a bit . Van Zon needed that flight because she was due in hospital for an operation the next day . She has chronic kidney disease . <p> She said to me , when I plucked up the courage to ask her about it , " I have three percent kidney function . " <p> They have horrible offices . Tiny rooms , narrow angled hallways , an 80s rabbit @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ disguise , and this is because they have never spent festival money on themselves . And it was humid , the drear rain outside , everyone so exhausted they moved like they were wading . <p> Carla van Zon is a person who will either ignore you or get up close to you and talk , urgently , staring and grinning , speaking only of what 's important . Not today . Today she just sat there , slumped , measuring her speech , telling me what she must have told a hundred other people , about her options -- peritoneal dialysis , hemodialysis , a new kidney . As she talked the features seemed to move around in her face . <p> Peritoneal dialysis means connecting to a hose every four hours . She might not be able to do it because she 's had two lots of major surgery in her life ( including for a burst appendix ) and she thinks her peritoneum is too badly damaged .
