<p> A UK-based team of researchers has created a graphene-based sieve capable of removing salt from seawater . <p> The sought-after development could aid the millions of people without ready access to clean drinking water . <p> The promising graphene oxide sieve could be highly efficient at filtering salts , and will now be tested against existing desalination membranes . <p> It has previously been difficult to manufacture graphene-based barriers on an industrial scale . <p> Reporting their results in the journal Nature Nanotechnology , scientists from the University of Manchester , led by Dr Rahul Nair , shows how they solved some of the challenges by using a chemical derivative called graphene oxide . <p> Isolated and characterised by a University of Manchester-led team in 2004 , graphene comprises a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice .
