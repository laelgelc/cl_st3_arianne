<h> Planning Minister Simon Coveney urged to reverse seaweed licence <p> Planning Minister Simon Coveney is being urged to reverse a decision to grant a licence to a biotechnology company to mechanically harvest 1,860 acres of native seaweed forest in Bantry Bay , writes Claire O'Sullivan . <p> A 10-year trial licence to cut the large brown seaweed or kelp forest was issued by the Department of Planning to Tralee-based BioAtlantis in 2010 and is due to begin in the coming months . <p> According to Deirdre Fitzgerald of Bantry Bay -- Protect our Native Kelp Forest , the project , which includes sections of the shorelines of the Beara and Sheep 's Head peninsulas in West Cork , is " on a scale not seen before in Irish or UK waters " . <p> An environmental impact assessment was not required as the kelp farm is under water off the coast and so is governed by the country 's foreshore legislation . <p> " Fishermen have expressed concern that marine life could be affected as the forests provide shelter for marine life and this could impact shellfish fishing , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and trees , kelp act as carbon stores absorbing carbon dioxide and so are a vital part of the ecosystem .
