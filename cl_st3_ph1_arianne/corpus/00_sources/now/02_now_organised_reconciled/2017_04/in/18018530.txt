File Photo ) <p> Non-fossil fuels -- renewables , nuclear and large hydroelectric power plants -- will account for more than half ( 56.5% ) of India 's installed power capacity within the 10 years to 2027 , according to a draft of the third National Electricity Plan ( NEP3 ) released in December 2016 . <p> The draft notes that if India achieves its target to install 175 gigawatts ( GW , which consists of 1,000 megawatts ) of renewable energy capacity by 2022 -- as it has committed to under the 2015 Paris Agreement -- it will not need to install , at least until 2027 , any more coal-fired capacity than the 50 GW currently under construction . <p> The Ministry of Power produces a National Electricity Plan every five years , in which it reviews the progress made over the previous five years , and sets @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the overarching aim of achieving universal access to electricity across India and ensuring that power is supplied efficiently and at reasonable prices . <p> NEP3 outlines how the government expects India 's electricity sector to develop over the five years from 2017 to 2022 , as well as the subsequent five years to 2027 . <p> Renewables will grow faster <p> When the draft was released in December 2016 , India had installed just over 50 GW of renewable power capacity , of which wind energy made up 57.4% and solar 18% . This gave renewables a 15% share in India 's total installed capacity of just over 314 GW , while coal made up 60% -- the remaining being large hydropower , nuclear , gas and diesel . <p> Renewables will have to scale rapidly to meet a national target set in 2015 to increase capacity to 175 GW by 2022 -- 100 GW from solar , 60 GW from wind and the remainder from sundry smaller sources such as biofuels and biomass . <p> NEP3 projects that not only will the 2022 target be achieved , renewable power @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ three times the projection made in NEP2 , of 70 GW , and significantly more ambitious than publicly proclaimed targets . <p> Comparing NEP3 with India 's Intended Nationally Determined Contribution ( INDC ) under the Paris Agreement reached at the 21st Conference of Parties ( COP21 ) to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change ( UNFCCC ) in 2015 shows a higher level of ambition to reach a low-carbon economy faster . <p> In its INDC , India had said it planned to achieve 40% cumulative installed capacity from non-fossil fuel-based energy resources by 2030 . <p> NEP3 is significantly more upbeat , predicting that non-fossil power will make up 46.8% of total installed capacity by 2021-22 and 56.5% by 2027 -- 10 years from now . If the NEP3 target is met as per the projected timelines , total installed renewable capacity in India will surpass coal-based capacity some time around 2024 . <p> Such ambitions are underpinned by the rapidly changing economics of wind and solar , whose price is falling rapidly . Solar and wind are expected to reach grid parity -- when they cost as much as conventional power -- in the near future , perhaps as early as next year , as IndiaSpend reported on 28 January 2017 . <p> As for other zero emission sources like nuclear and large hydropower , NEP3 projects an addition of 7.6 GW of nuclear and 27.3 GW of large hydroelectricity capacity up to 2027 , up from 6.7 GW and 44.4 GW of hydro installed as of March 2016 , as ongoing and approved projects come online during 2017-22 . <p> Coal capacity addition will fall <p> While expecting renewable capacity to surge , NEP3 says no new coal-based capacity addition is required for the 10 years to 2027 beyond the 50 GW under different stages of construction and likely to come online between 2017 and 22 . <p> As per NEP3 projections , the 2022-27 period would require an addition of about 44 GW of coal-based capacity @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ adequately met by the 50 GW that will come online during 2017-22 . This leaves India with 6 GW of extra capacity . <p> The NEP 's projections for coal are different from India 's INDC , which suggested the country would require 100 GW , and perhaps as much as 300 GW , of additional coal-fired capacity by 2030 . <p> The downgrading of coal expansion is not unexpected because rapid expansion of renewables means fossil fuel-based power plants are already under-utilised . NEP3 shows the average coal plant load factor -- a measure of how much plants are used -- has fallen from around 70% to just over 62% in the last four years , an " exceptionally low " level , according to the Economic Survey 2016-17 . <p> India 's coal use might be far less than projected <p> While renewable capacity is rising globally , developing countries including India are widely expected to continue using cheap coal . <p> " Surging consumption of coal in power generation and industry makes India , by a distance , the largest source of growth in global coal use @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that provides key information and statistics about the international oil and energy markets , emphasized in its 2015 India Energy Outlook .
