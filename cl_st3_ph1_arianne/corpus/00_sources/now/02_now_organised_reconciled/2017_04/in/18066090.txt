<h> Livestock Farming vs Climate Change <p> Livestock is providing on an average 17% of food calories and more than a third of protein to human diets . Beyond food production for majority of people , it provides livelihood to millions of poor smallholder farmers . For the poor and undernourished people in the developing world , livestock products are crucial for protein supply . Based on estimates for growth of the world human population , increasing incomes and further urbanization , the global demand for animal products is expected to double by 2050 . The maximum demand will be in Asia especially in the middle-class of the society ; presently two of seven billion to become five of nine billion in due course . In J &amp; K , although 73 percent of the rural households own livestock yet the state is largely dependent on import of animals and foods of animal origin . A series of social concerns related to this intensification of farming practices provide an insight into the four basic principles ; One Health ; Customized Care and Credible Performance and No Nuisance . ' No Nuisance ' focuses on the environmental perspective . Livestock use 70% of the available agricultural land , consume 60% of the global biomass harvest and 8% of global ( 30% of agricultural ) water withdrawals . Around 30% of global biodiversity loss due to the spread of pasture land or turning over forests to feed production can be attributed to livestock production . This sector is also held responsible for about 12 to 18 percent of all anthropogenic greenhouse gas ( GHG ) emissions . Processing and transport of animal products give rise to further emissions , mostly related to use of fossil fuel and infrastructure development . <p> While livestock contributes significantly in environmental degradation , there are several ways in which they are in turn affected directly and indirectly by a changing climate . Fundamentally the animal production is affected by climate change in four ways : a ) feed-grain availability and price , b ) impacts on livestock pastures and forage crop production and quality , c ) distribution of livestock diseases and pests , and d ) the direct effects of weather on animal health , welfare , growth and reproduction . Heat stress directly impairs production ( meat , milk and egg yield and quality ) . Increased spread of existing vector-borne diseases and parasites , accompanied by the emergence and circulation of new diseases is evident . <p> Livestock sector located at the center of the discourse on climate change and agriculture is presently intertwined with many challenges .
