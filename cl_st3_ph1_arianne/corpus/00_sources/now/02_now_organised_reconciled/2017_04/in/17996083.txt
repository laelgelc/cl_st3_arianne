<h> Hot weather changing pattern of allergies <p> Lucknow : In what appears to be an outcome of changing weather conditions , the pattern of allergies affecting people is showing a new trend in the city . Problems like allergic conjunctivitis , allergic rhinitis and eczema have started appearing in the months of February , March and April . Earlier , such allergies were generally reported between July and September . <p> Allergic rhinitis , a kind of lung allergy , is a case in point . " Heat , humidity , and pollen concentration in air trigger allergic rhinitis . While humidity was said to be the main reason for allergic rhinitis between July and September , now rising temperature is the cause for its cases being reported from February to April , " said Prof Suryakant , head of pulmonary medicine department , KGMU . <p> He added that about a decade ago , very few cases of allergic rhinitis were reported between February and April , but these days 10-15% of cases are reported in KGMU 's OPD . " <p> City-based pulmonary physician Dr BP Singh said a combination of rising temperature with high pollen concentration is a known trigger for asthma and allergies . " Earlier , such cases occurred mostly at the time of change of seasons , but now they are being reported before the usual period of onset . Though Indian studies to prove this are yet to be taken , a problem always comes before a research , " he said . <p> Dr Arun Sharma of KGMU ophthalmology department said allergic conjunctivitis cases from February to September have recorded a four-fold increase in the past decade . " This problem is directly linked to increased ambient temperature . A decade ago , allergic conjunctivitis accounted for 10% cases in OPD , but today that has risen to 40-50% cases . The problem occurs because eyes are allergic to dry heat and ultraviolet rays , " he said . <p> As per the World Health Organisation-UN Environment Programme Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change report , " Allergic diseases @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ generally favour the production and release of airborne allergens ( such as fungal spores and plant pollen ) and , consequently , there may be an effect on asthma and other allergic respiratory diseases such as allergic rhinitis , as well as effects on conjunctivitis and dermatitis .
