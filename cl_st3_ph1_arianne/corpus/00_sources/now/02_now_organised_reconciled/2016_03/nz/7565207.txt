The region also has some of the world 's highest rates of violence against women , a challenge New Zealand shares . <p> While the picture postcards of white sand and gleaming water are familiar to us , we often forget that while New Zealand ranks 7th on the Human Development Index , some of our Pacific neighbours rate at 157th , still living in extreme hardship . <p> It is one of the most vulnerable regions in the world -- vulnerable to extreme natural events , to climate change , to economic fragility and the tyranny of distance . UN agencies , like UN Women , are working to ensure women can find dignity , safety and support in those emergency shelters . <p> The humanitarian response to our neighbours ' needs in such crises is vital , and New Zealanders are extraordinarily generous . But nevertheless , hard-won development gains and community progress can be wiped out in minutes by natural disasters . It is the work over months , years , even lifetimes , to lift countries out of the poverty and hardship that makes them so exposed to disaster . This is where women are powerful agents for change . The UN Economic and Social Commission for Asia and the Pacific has estimated that the Asia-Pacific region is losing US$40bn annually because women lack access to employment , and US$16-30bn a year because of gender gaps in education . Restrictive policies , healthcare restrictions and social taboos , along with poverty and lack of educational opportunities , make young girls vulnerable to a range of negative health outcomes . <p> But women who have the health , education , time and opportunity to earn an income can achieve so much .
