<h> Seas could rise higher than predicted - study <p> Global seas could rise nearly twice as much as previous , widely accepted estimates , according to a study published this week saying low-lying cities face possible disaster by the end of the century . <p> Sea levels could surge more than 0.9m ( 3 feet ) by 2100 from melting Antarctic ice alone , on top of a three-foot rise already predicted , said the study by two American researchers that appeared in the science journal Nature . <p> That same Antarctic ice melt could add nearly 15m of sea-level rise by the year 2500 , it said . <p> The earlier , commonly accepted prediction was made by the United Nations Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change in 2013 estimating global sea levels rising more than three feet by 2100 . <p> " This could spell disaster for many low-lying cities , " said co-author Robert DeConto , professor of geosciences at the University of Massachusetts at Amherst , in a statement . <p> Boston , for example , could see about 1.5m of sea level at @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Other low-lying cities often cited as being in jeopardy of rising sea levels include London , New York , Shanghai , Hong Kong , Sydney , and Venice . <p> The findings should sound an alarm bell for more greenhouse gas emission cuts , said co-author David Pollard , a senior scientist at Pennsylvania State University 's Earth and Environmental Systems Institute . <p> While the findings are " worst-case " possibilities , they " should be considered seriously , " he said .
