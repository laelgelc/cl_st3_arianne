<h> Westport hosts public talks on climate change and agriculture <h> Share This <p> How could we have responded to recent storms and extreme weather events differently ? How can we adapt our agricultural practices to cope with changes in our climate ? These topics and more will be discussed at two short talks organised by NUI Galway 's Geography Department , to be held in The Clew Bay Hotel , Westport , on Tuesday evening next , April 5 . Over the last three winters Ireland has been hit by an unprecedented number of extreme storm events , leading to widespread damage from coastal erosion and flooding . The impacts of these events are especially felt by rural communities , from the standpoints of both agriculture and tourism . Beaches like as Bertra Strand , outside Westport , have suffered significant erosion in recent years , while communities have been cut off or severely disrupted by flooding and the loss of local roads and bridges , like Knockaniska Bridge in Carrowholly . Rather than looking to Governments for guidance on how to protect themselves , communities could learn what the risks entail and how they can adapt their activities to both prevent damage and also to be in a position to avail of opportunities that may arise . The speakers at the Clew Bay Hotel event will be Philip Farrelly ( National Rural Network ) and Dr Conor Murphy , NUI Maynooth . Farrelly will describe how the new Green Low-Carbon Agri-Environmental Scheme ( GLAS ) will promote agricultural-production methods that are compatible with environmental protection and climate change mitigation in a talk titled ' The GLAS scheme : opportunities and adaptation in a changing climate ' . Meanwhile , Dr Murphy 's talk will ask , ' Are the recent extreme weather events changing the unwritten agreement between the Government and the people of Ireland ? ' .
