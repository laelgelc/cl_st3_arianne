<p> The World Bank has recently written to the government recommending a carbon tax as a " fiscally prudent measure . " <p> It is a welcome sign of growing consensus about the need for taxes on fuels that generate greenhouse gas emissions . <p> Such a move would fit in with national polices and international agreements to promote renewable energy , and would also help protect government revenue and promote price stability . <p> Although Bangladesh 's per capita carbon emissions are historically negligible and not barriers to efforts to industrialise our economy , it is imperative we try to do so sustainably . <p> This is vital both to prevent the negative consequences of climate change becoming even worse and to add further moral authority to demand better from the rest of the world in fighting climate change . <p> It is welcome to see signs of the government moving away from a policy of heavily subsidising fossil fuels , such as the prime minister 's statement in February that there would be no reduction in the prices of fuel oil until the all loans of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ bring in carbon taxes as well as cutting costly fossil fuel subsidies . Discouraging fossil fuel is essential to cut emissions and reduce dependence on a finite resource which can only get costlier to import in the long run . <p> It is also vital to stimulate more investment in renewable energy . <p> Bangladesh has already seen multiple social and economic benefits from the millions of solar home systems set up by organisations like IDCOL , which have brought cheap renewable electricity to rural households while limiting pressures on the grid . <p> A carbon tax will attract more new investment to help scale up renewable generation on a nationwide scale . It is an essential policy to help bring about the sustainable energy future that Bangladesh and the world needs .
