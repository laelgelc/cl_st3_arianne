<h> Population trends in the US put more at risk of sea level rise <h> Accounting for population growth can triple number of people at risk in 2100 . <p> Some of the impacts of climate change are conceptually complex , such as weather extremes , storm tracks , and ecosystem shifts . But one impact that 's easy to imagine is sea level rise . When the ocean comes up , stuff ends up underwater . So one way to project the future consequences is to simply calculate how many people and buildings are below a given height above the current sea level . <p> But sea level is n't the only thing changing -- so are the people and buildings . A new study led by the University of Georgia 's Mathew Hauer looks at population trends around the coastal US for a better estimate of how many people would be affected by rising ocean waters by the end of this century . <p> The researchers took census data from 1940 to 2010 for coastal counties , and they extrapolated the trends through 2100 .
