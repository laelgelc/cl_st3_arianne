<h> 13 million Americans at risk of floods from rising sea levels <p> A study finds that rising sea waters could put upwards of 4.2 million Americans at risk of flooding . <p> Doyle Rice , USA TODAY , KPNX6:36 PM . MST March 14 , 2016 <p> The mouth of the Miami River runs out to Biscayne Bay as it exits Downtown Miami . Floridians face the most risk from sea-level rise , where up to 6 million residents could be affected . ( Photo : Allison Diaz , for USA TODAY ) ) <p> Up to 13 million Americans along the coast are at risk of floods from rising seas due to climate change , says a new study out Monday . <p> Based on population forecasts for the year 2100 , a six-foot sea level rise would expose more than 13 million people to flooding and other hazards from rising seas . <p> Florida faces the most risk , where up to 6 million residents could be affected . One million people each in California and Louisiana also could be impacted . <p> " Projections @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ which significantly underestimate the effect of sea-level rise in the United States , " said study co-author Mathew E. Hauer of the University of Georgia . <p> " In fact , there are 31 counties where more than 100,000 residents could be affected by 6 feet of sea-level rise , " he said . <p> The study was published in the peer-reviewed British journal Nature Climate Change . <p> Millions of people may need to relocate , with the expected migration similar to that of the 20th-century Great Migration of southern African-Americans to the North , the study said . Relocating the people would come with a price tag of $14 trillion , study authors found . <p> If the seas rose by three feet , roughly 4.2 million Americans would have to move . <p> As the Earth 's temperature warms , so do the seas . Heat-trapping greenhouse gases cause more land ice ( such as glaciers and ice sheets ) to melt and water to expand . Warmer water simply takes up more room than cooler water .
