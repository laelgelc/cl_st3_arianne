<h> 13 million Americans at risk of floods from rising sea levels <p> Up to 13 million Americans along the coast are at risk of floods from rising seas due to climate change , says a new study released Monday . <p> Based on population forecasts for the year 2100 , a six-foot sea level rise would expose more than 13 million people to flooding and other hazards from rising seas . <p> Florida faces the most risk , where up to 6 million residents could be affected . One million people each in California and Louisiana also could be impacted . <p> " Projections are up to three times larger than current estimates , which significantly underestimate the effect of sea-level rise in the United States , " said study co-author Mathew E. Hauer of the University of Georgia . <p> " In fact , there are 31 counties where more than 100,000 residents could be affected by 6 feet of sea-level rise , " he said . <p> The study was published in the peer-reviewed British journal Nature Climate Change . <p> Millions of people may @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that of the 20th-century Great Migration of southern African-Americans to the North , the study said . Relocating the people would come with a price tag of $14 trillion , study authors found . <p> If the seas rose by three feet , roughly 4.2 million Americans would have to move . <p> As the Earth 's temperature warms , so do the seas . Heat-trapping greenhouse gases cause more land ice ( such as glaciers and ice sheets ) to melt and water to expand . Warmer water simply takes up more room than cooler water .
