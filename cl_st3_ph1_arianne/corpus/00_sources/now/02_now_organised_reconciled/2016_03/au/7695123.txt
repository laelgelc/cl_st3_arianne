<p> Australia 's climate zones are moving south , shifting 100-400 kilometres since 2000 and threatening to wipe out the south 's Mediterranean climate . <p> Rising ocean warmth and changing barometric pressures in a warming atmosphere have changed the balance between summer and winter rainfall over the lower half of the continent . <p> Research by Dr David Stephens of the Australian Export Grains Innovation Centre ( AEGIC ) found that the effect has been to shuffle south the north 's summer-dominant rainfall patterns . <p> " Rainfall between May to October over much of the heavily populated regions of southern Australia has decreased 10-30 per cent , while summer rain has increased up to 40 per cent in some areas , " Dr Stephens said . <p> A consequence is that over the past 16 years , the winter-dominant rainfall Mediterranean climate of southern Australia has shrunk significantly . <p> Before 2000 , the winter rainfall zone extended well north of the Victorian border , sometimes hundreds of kilometres north .
