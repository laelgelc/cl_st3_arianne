<h> Earth saw ' explosive ' annual growth in carbon dioxide in 2015 <p> The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration ( NOAA ) released a new report underlining the worrying spike in carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) levels last year . The greenhouse gas emission increased alarmingly , which is unquestionably bad news for the pace of global warming . <p> Atmospheric CO2 levels increased an average of 2 ppm per year <p> In 2015 , they spiked at 3.05 ppm , bringing the worldwide average to 402,59 ppm <p> In pre-industrial times , before 1800 , the global median was of 280 ppm <p> This is the biggest increase in the last 56 years <p> The development was observed with the aid of the Mauna Loa Observatory in Hawaii , which is the oldest station that has kept track of global atmospheric changes . In fact , it 's the benchmark site for worldwide shifts in temperatures , managed by the World Meteorological Organization ( WMO ) . The report included data gathered from 50 countries around the world . NOAA stated that the annual growth rate of atmospheric CO2 rose by 3.05 parts per million ( ppm ) in 2015 . That is the largest increase our planet has seen in the past 56 years of research . Why the growth is so significant is because the situation was already dire . <p> In the past 10 years , the amounts of CO2 emissions increased 2 ppm each year , so this is a new record of increases worldwide . As of February 2016 , the levels surpassed the benchmark average of 400 ppm and reached a highly alarming 402.59 ppm .
