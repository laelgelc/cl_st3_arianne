<h> Abbott and Credlin deserved a right of reply <p> Niki Savva had her reasons for not seeking a right of reply from Tony Abbott and Peta Credlin for her book , but by skipping this fundamental part of journalistic ethics she risks undermining political reporting more broadly , writes Sarah Maddison . <p> Political journalism is essential to democracy . Shining a light on the actions that lie behind parliamentary politics enriches public debate , providing much-needed scrutiny of the behaviour of our elected representatives and ( occasionally ) their staff . <p> In this sense , Niki Savva 's recent expose of the relationship between former prime minister Tony Abbott and his unpopular chief of staff , Peta Credlin , is just another in a series of necessary revelations about the attitudes and actions of our recent political leaders . <p> Such revelations are seen to be in the public interest because they help the rest of us make sense of political decision-making processes , including the decision to depose a sitting prime minister . <p> But the democratic role of political journalism is undermined if @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ One such standard is the presumptive " right of reply " that should be available to anyone about whom damaging public comment is made . <p> Savva certainly has a point . In her own history of crossing swords with Abbott and Credlin while " they " held office , Credlin attempted to have Savva fired from her newspaper on at least one occasion . If we believe the accounts contained in Savva 's book we can conclude that neither Abbott nor Credlin shy away from confrontation , and both have been prepared to use their political weight to protect their image or silence dissenting views . <p> But is such poor behaviour on the part of politicians justification enough for Savva 's failure to give Abbott and Credlin the right of reply ? I would say it is not . <p> The right of reply is what elevates journalism from scandal-mongering to the pursuit of truth . Without a right of reply , this kind of reporting risks being seen as little more than a mouthpiece for unsubstantiated leaks or a megaphone for someone with an axe to grind . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ report should add weight to investigative journalism rather than discrediting it . <p> Upholding such ethical standards is essential to the standing of political journalism more broadly . Public trust in news media continues to fall , with significant implications for its democratic role . If voters do not have faith that news media is following rigorous , ethical reporting standards they are less likely to engage in political discourse or to make informed political choices . <p> I am less concerned about the fact that Savva has broken another unwritten rule of the Canberra press gallery for which she has also been criticised ; that is , protecting the private lives of Australian politicians regardless of how scandalous the behaviour . Savva 's concerns about the " inappropriate " nature of Abbott and Credlin 's relationship quite rightly belong in the public domain . If politicians like Abbott are prepared to campaign on a set of traditional family values , and indeed to deploy their families as tools in an election campaign , they must also be prepared for their personal behaviour to be available for public scrutiny . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ effect of such revelations is less powerful , if the journalist themselves has not upheld the highest standard of professional ethics . Without a right of reply , journalistic scrutiny risks being dismissed as gossip or revenge . And without these robust standards the capacity of the media to hold politicians accountable for their behaviour is fundamentally diminished . <p> More than a decade ago , veteran political journalist Michelle Grattan gave a public lecture in which she observed the simultaneously adversarial and " parasitic " relationship between politicians and the media . There is no doubt that both camps need each other to survive ; the politicians in order to communicate their message to the voting public and the media for their bread and butter . <p> But if the focus of this relationship becomes one only of co-dependency then our democracy as a whole will suffer . Ethical standards in journalism , including the right of reply , ensure that the focus remains on the media 's higher purpose .
