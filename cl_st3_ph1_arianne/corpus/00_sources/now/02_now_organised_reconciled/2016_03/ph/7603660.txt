<p> Whenever I visit the Asia-Pacific region , I am impressed by its dynamism and the many successes that its people have achieved , not least of which was meeting the goal of halving the proportion of hungry people in region under the just-concluded Millennium Development Goals effort . <p> Now , the new successor to the MDGs -- the global Sustainable Development Goals ( SDGs ) -- has set the bar higher , placing the complete eradication of hunger and major improvements to nutrition as targets . <p> Globally , the will is there , and , with the energy that countries in Asia and the Pacific have shown in recent years , the region can again play a leadership role in this bold effort . <p> ADVERTISEMENT <p> But it 's not time to take a victory lap , not just yet . ( Asia is expected to account for most of that growth . ) Doing so , we 'll have to take better care of the environment , using ever-more-scarce natural resources with greater care , doing less damage to Mother Earth . <p> Meanwhile , climate change and the increasing frequency of natural disasters -- which affect the Asia-Pacific region more than any other -- are creating great uncertainty . This will make farming even riskier , especially for family farmers , as witness the recent droughts in Thailand and Cambodia and heavy flooding in Burma ( Myanmar ) . <p> And other new challenges have emerged . Producing more food , while important , is not the sole answer . We must feed people well . <p> Family farmers need to be much more integrated into food systems . So far , they have n't fully benefited from rapid economic growth in the region and the resulting surge in demand for higher-value food products . If we empower them as fully connected players in food value chains , they 'll be able to boost their output , earn more , eat better , and act as engines for rural economic growth . <p> Additionally , we must continue to innovate , trying out new tactics such as the Blue Growth approach to fisheries and aquaculture being widely embraced across the Asia-Pacific . Future successes in food production will require creativity -- and more research and development . Asia and the Pacific has shown that with human ingenuity and hard work , nothing is impossible .
