<p> MANILA - Former United States Vice President Al Gore has warned of the possibility that even stronger weather disturbances could hit the Philippines in the next few years owing to climate change <p> On the last day of the leadership training organized by the Climate Reality Project , Gore said that chances the Philippines will be affected by the impact of climate change are higher than for those of other countries . <p> As a result of rising temperatures and glaciers melting , sea levels are rising and some 13 million Filipinos in low-lying and coastal areas may need relocating . <p> He noted that the Philippines heads the list of those with the most number of weather-related events since 1994 . A prime example of this , of course , is typhoon Yolanda ( Haiyan ) , which struck in November 2013 and is tagged as the strongest cyclone to hit land ever . <p> " Super typhoon Haiyan or Yolanda in the Philippines crossed-over parts of the Pacific Ocean 3 degrees celsius warmer than normal that it became the most powerful and most destructible storm @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> Gore lamented that the Philippines relies heavily for energy on coal , a key driver of carbon emissions . <p> He made a pitch for harnessing more renewable energy in the Philippines .
