<h> Walls to Protect Resorts Threaten Fishing Communities <p> UROA , Tanzania -- A visitor to the Nungwi Peninsula in Unguja , one of Zanzibar 's twin islands , has their pick of luxury resorts , all promising access to golden beaches . <p> But resort owners trying to protect those beaches from coastal erosion , by erecting seawalls along the shore , are coming under fire from residents and marine experts , who say the trend destroys the environment and threatens the livelihoods of local communities . Built perpendicular or parallel to the beach , vertical seawalls divert waves away from the beaches to slow the natural erosion process . In some cases , concrete piles are placed in front of the walls to help weaken the waves before they hit the walls . <p> The problem , say environmentalists , is that while the structures help keep resort beaches intact , the walls can speed up erosion on other parts of the island by diverting the waves to unprotected sections of the coast . " The negative side is they accelerate the erosion by washing away the sand at the foot of the wall , " Mr. Letitre said . <p> Resort owners insist the seawalls are a matter of financial survival . According to official figures , in 2014 Zanzibar 's islands had around 260 hotels -- 20 of them boasting five stars . Together they drew a large number of the 175,000 tourists who visited the country that year -- and the tourism sector contributed between 25 and 27 percent to Zanzibar 's gross domestic product . <p> But the country 's beaches are under constant threat from erosion , a problem that is worsening as a result of sea level rise linked to climate change , scientists say . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of Unguja , villager Shaame Mcha Chambo said that the present beach line represents a loss of more than 40 meters of landmass " eaten by the encroaching sea " over the past five decades . <p> Resorts on the islands are so concerned about the encroaching sea that they feel they have no choice but to put up seawalls , prioritizing protecting their property over the uninterrupted sea views that their guests expect . <p> But when resorts build walls to stop their sand disappearing , local communities can pay the price . Mr. Chambo and two other Uroa villagers said that the construction of a seawall at one resort destroyed a major reef , while the erosion induced by the wall has almost cleared the trees that provided natural protection for the beach and has also indirectly contributed to the submerging of a key offshore sand dune . <p> " Our fathers and relatives who eke out their living from fishing used to stay at the dune as their transit point , " he said , referring to the practice of fishermen stopping on the dunes for a few @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ trips . " Look , the dune is no more and the fishing patterns of the people have been disrupted , " he said . <p> Mariyama Ussi Khamis , a member of the Uroa executive committee that helps run the village , said that in the five years since the lodge put up its seawalls , enough unprotected land has been lost to cause the village 's coastal graveyard to succumb to seawater flooding . <p> " If the trend of erecting such seawalls is not reversed , " she warned , the village of 3,050 people could see beach erosion threaten its burial sites , a market place and about 15 homes that sit along the shoreline . Zanzibar 's authorities seem to agree .
