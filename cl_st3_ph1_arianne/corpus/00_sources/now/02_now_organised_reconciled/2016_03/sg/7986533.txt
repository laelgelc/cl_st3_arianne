<h> Bad blood between political foes could hurt their party 's chances <p> Australia 's Prime Minister Malcolm Turnbull has cleared the way for an election within months and appears set to win aside from one potential obstacle : the simmering rage of the man he ousted , Mr Tony Abbott . <p> The two have been political foes for decades , but tensions increased after Mr Abbott declared he will stay in Parliament and then began to openly question Mr Turnbull 's policies . Mr Abbott , a staunch conservative , has criticised Mr Turnbull 's approach to national security and economics . The question now is whether Mr Abbott will prove to be Mr Turnbull 's " Kevin Rudd " and will leave the government and Australia facing a renewed period of political instability . <p> Mr Rudd , a former Labor leader , was toppled by Ms Julia Gillard in 2010 and undermined her until he finally wrested his job back in 2013 . The bitter feud between the pair led to the party 's crushing defeat at the last federal election in 2013 . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ replaced by the tensions between the Liberal Party 's Mr Abbott and Mr Turnbull , whose public sparring dates back decades to the debate in the 1990s over whether Australia should become a republic . <p> Mr Turnbull acknowledged last week that Mr Abbott could potentially cause serious political damage , and said that he hoped Mr Abbott will prove supportive in the coming election campaign . <p> " It depends on what he says , frankly , " Mr Turnbull told Radio 3AW . " Whether it 's a plus or minus depends entirely on the nature of his contribution . I hope that he will be supportive and he 's indicating that he will be , so that will be good . " <p> Mr Turnbull deposed Mr Abbott in September last year after the ruling coalition struggled in opinion polls . Mr Turnbull 's popularity has dipped in recent months but he remains the preferred prime minister of 52 per cent of the public , compared with just 21 per cent support for Labor leader Bill Shorten ; the remainder are uncommitted . <p> Mr Turnbull , a former investment banker and ardent republican , is more progressive than Mr Abbott on issues such as climate change and same-sex marriage , and has long been the more popular of the two . <p> After being ousted , Mr Abbott promised not to " snipe " or undermine the new leader . <p> But few believe he has stuck to his pledge , particularly as he publicly criticised decisions such as Mr Turnbull 's delayed purchase of a new submarine fleet . <p> Few also think Mr Abbott has any chance of making a comeback because of his own public unpopularity .
