<h> Glasgow 's SSE Hydro goes dark for Earth Hour <p> At 8.30pm last night millions of people in the UK joined hundreds of millions of people in 178 countries and territories to celebrate WWF 's Earth Hour and show they care about the future of planet . <p> Iconic landmarks including the SSE Hydro and the Kelpies were among the hundreds of landmarks to switch off their lights for one hour to shine a light on issues affecting our planet . <p> Jennifer Clements , spokesperson for WWF-UK said : " The future health and well-being of our planet is at risk , particularly from the effects of climate change . Climate change makes the global challenges , such as poverty reduction and wildlife extinction more difficult to solve . In a recent review , one in six species were shown to be facing increased risk of extinction because of climate change -- yet we have the knowledge and technology to reduce climate impacts and ease the pressures on the world 's most vulnerable animals and people . <p> " That 's why it 's fantastic to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ together this Earth Hour in a record-breaking number of countries . Earth Hour reminds us that we have the power to make change happen . It 's a visible demonstration of the enormous level of support there is around the world for a united effort to tackle issues affecting our planet . " <p> Over the last decade , WWF 's Earth Hour 's unique display of darkness has become a global phenomenon uniting hundreds of millions of individuals across the world .
