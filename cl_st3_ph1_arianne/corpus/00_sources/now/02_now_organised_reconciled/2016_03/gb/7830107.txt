<p> ENTIRE communities across Malawi who have been forced to drink dirty water for decades are now being given access to safe , clean drinking water for the very first time thanks to support from Scotland . <p> Charity Tearfund Scotland is marking World Water Day today by highlighting the plight of 1.8 billion people across the globe for whom drinking water contaminated by faeces is a daily reality . <p> Young mother Stera Muwa told Tearfund 's Scottish director Lynne Paterson during a recent visit to Malawi how her family had been ill for years after drinking water contaminated with human faeces . <p> She said : " We asked why the water was coming out red but nobody had the answer . We knew this was why we were sick . And then they came and told us it was contaminated with human faeces . This made us very worried and very angry . We had been drinking it for 19 years . <p> " I have 15-year-old twin daughters , and they have been sick their whole lives . Sometimes it was too much . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ I would put a child on my back and walk there . I did this every time they got ill and in our home someone was always ill . I was very worried that there was no way out . " <p> In Stera 's community in Salima district , around 800 people were drawing water from a water point which was pumping out contaminated water and leaving villagers caught in an ongoing cycle of ill health . <p> As a result , children across the region had their education disrupted and many had to repeat years or drop out altogether , and families were left feeling increasingly frustrated as they struggled to find the means to change their situation or get anyone to listen and respond to their concerns . <p> Stera added : " Our water was dirty water and my family would get sick . I spent so much time caring for illness that I could n't farm and make sure we had food on the table . " <p> " We used to get water from digging a hole and taking water from the nearby river . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ was very sad to find that for all those years it was producing contaminated water and we still were not safe . Whenever we raised the questions , it would always stop at that point . It would not go further . Nobody was prepared to help us and we did n't know how to change things . " <p> Tearfund Scotland was awarded funding through the Scottish Government 's Climate Justice Fund , and this has enabled the local community to take control of their situation and ensure a healthier future for the next generation . <p> Tearfund 's project is working with families in two regions of central Malawi to identify and restore contaminated water sources and educate local communities . <p> Members of the community are encouraged to set up water committees with responsibility for ensuring that water points are looked after . <p> Paterson said : " It is so easy for us in Scotland to take our access to clean water for granted , but for Stera and many others I met in Malawi , this was a luxury they felt would never become a reality @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ by the huge difference Tearfund 's project is making to thousands across Malawi . Having access to safe water to drink is a very basic right and it is amazing to see Scottish funds giving a voice and hope to people living in some of the poorest places of the world . " <p> Scotland 's Climate Change Minister , Aileen McLeod , praised the Climate Justice project and said it was the world 's poorest and most vulnerable who suffer the most because of climate change . <p> She said : " Scotland has a special relationship with Malawi , and support for Tearfund and its partners through Scotland 's innovative Climate Justice Fund has enabled over 31,000 Malawians to gain reliable access to clean , safe water for the first time .
