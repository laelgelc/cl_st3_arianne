<p> An aerial survey is revealing the worst bleaching ever seen on northern parts of the Great Barrier Reef , as the scientist leading the survey live-tweets the devastation and pleads for the world to take action on climate change . <p> Last week Terry Hughes , a professor at James Cook University and convener of the National Coral Bleaching Taskforce , told Guardian Australia he planned to hire a charter plane and map the bleaching to see how bad it was . <p> They would start from Cairns and fly north , he said . " We 'll expand that initial aerial survey to crisscross the whole northern region and map it all out . " <p> Now , using both planes and helicopters , Hughes has found what he says is the worst bleaching in the region he has ever seen . <p> On Tuesday , Hughes said he surveyed 600km of reef and found that more than 60% of it had been bleached . <p> That was just two days after the Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority raised the threat level of coral bleaching @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , indicating " severe regional bleaching " in the northernmost quarter of the 344,400 sq km marine park . <p> On Wednesday Hughes upgraded to a helicopter and continued the survey . <p> Bleaching occurs when coral gets stressed , usually by heat , and it expels the colourful algae that live inside it , giving it its brilliant colour and providing it with most of the energy it needs . If it stays bleached for long enough , the corals die and the reefs risk being colonised by other organisms such as seaweed . <p> This bleaching event is linked to the current El Ni ? o , which raises temperatures in the region , on top of climate change-induced warming . <p> While Hughes surveyed the reefs from the air , other teams were in ships , examining the coral up close to get a more in-depth picture of which corals were bleaching , he said .
