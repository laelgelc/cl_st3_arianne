<h> UN Agency to Extend School Breakfast Program <p> Khmer Times/Ros Chanveasna <p> Wednesday , 09 March 2016 <p> 175 views <p> The United Nations World Food Program ( WFP ) will continue providing free breakfasts to students at primary schools in rural areas for another two-and-a-half years , Foreign Affairs Ministry spokesman Chum Sounry said yesterday . <p> Mr. Sounry made the announcement following a meeting between Deputy Prime Minister Hor Namhong and WFP country director Gianpietro Bordignon at the ministry , during which they discussed extending the UN agency 's activities in Cambodia to ensure school children continued receiving nutritious meals . <p> The WFP began providing free breakfasts at schools in July 2011 , with a budget of $145 million for the project . Another $46 million has been earmarked to extend the project from July of this year to December 2018 , Mr. Sounry said yesterday . <p> " The WFP will extend its operations of providing food to Cambodia for another two-and-a-half years with an additional budget of $46 million , " he said , adding that the project 's goal is to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ attend classes . <p> " During the extension period , WFP will assist approximately 600,000 beneficiaries per year through school feeding programs that provide both hot meals in rural schools and stipends to families for their children 's regular school attendance -- especially the attendance of girls in higher grades of primary schools , " Mr. Bordignon said yesterday . <p> He said that the funds would also be used to improve drinking water , hygiene and sanitation in schools , as well as provide technical support to the government in the interest of scaling up its countrywide food assistance programs as well as providing training to rural communities regarding climate change adaptation . <p> At the meeting , Deputy Prime Minister Hor Namhong also asked Mr. Bordignon to help Cambodia combat the impact of climate change on food security , saying most Cambodian farmers depend solely on rain to grow crops due to the lack of irrigation systems .
