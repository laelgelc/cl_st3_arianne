<p> Bombardier makes a " fabulous " jet . That was Prime Minister Justin Trudeau 's description in an interview with Bloomberg News . He also believes the aviation industry is special , as it provides " high-quality jobs , " and seems to think taxpayers need to support it because " all the big aircraft companies around the world have always needed significant government support . " <p> Out of these few comments , it may be reasonably deduced that the new Liberal government is looking kindly at offering Bombardier its much-lobbied-for , $1-billion bailout . How could it not ? Any industry with high-quality jobs , making fabulous products , that is special in itself and has already received more than $1 billion from the government of Quebec , is surely eligible for $1 billion more from the rest of Canada . <p> Over the last five decades , Bombardier 's special treatment from the Quebec and federal governments has almost become something of a Canadian tradition , like singing the anthem at hockey games , or equalization payments . That industry , like Bombardier , has " high-paying jobs , " is " innovative " and while oil is not " fabulous , " it may claim an even greater virtue : it is necessary for almost every other industry . It is surely necessary for jets and cars . <p> The principle involved should be made explicit : the only carbon emissions that count " against " an industry are those that emerge from industries producing the product . Hence , oil discovery and extraction are villainous . Emissions from companies that are inextricably tied to the consumption of that product , emissions from companies whose products only function by actually releasing emissions -- cars , planes , jets -- do not count . <p> To call this a policy inconsistency is to overtax understatement . It is logically irreconcilable . Should Bombardier find swift favour with the new Liberal government , there will be far more at stake than the writing of the cheque . It will be this new government 's first really big choice .
