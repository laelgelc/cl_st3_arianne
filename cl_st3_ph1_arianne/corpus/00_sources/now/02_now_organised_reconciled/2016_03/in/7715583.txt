<h> One-fourth of all global deaths due to environmental factors : WHO report <p> New Delhi : Nearly one-fourth of all global deaths occur due to environmental factors , says a study by the World Health Organisation . <p> Non-communicable diseases have emerged as the biggest threat to life , primarily due to indoor and outdoor air pollution , climate change and exposure to synthetic chemicals . Air pollution is responsible for 25% of strokes and 19% of cancers . The highest proportion of deaths and disease that could be tackled through environmental improvements is in low- and middle-income countries . <p> The report titled ' Preventing disease through healthy environments ' showed that 12.6 million or 23% of people died as a result of living or working in an unhealthy environment in 2012 . As many as 100 diseases and injuries are related to environmental risk factors such as air , water and soil pollution , chemical exposures , climate change and ultraviolet radiation . <p> The most vulnerable populations are children under 5 years and adults aged 50-75 years , the report finds . Children die mainly due to lower respiratory infections and diarrhoeal diseases , while older adults are most impacted by non-communicable diseases . <p> Regionally , the report finds , low- and middle-income countries in the WHO South-East Asia and Western Pacific Regions had the largest environment-related disease burden in 2012 , with a total of 7.3 million deaths , most attributable to indoor and outdoor air pollution . <p> South-East Asia region , in which the WHO includes India , recorded the highest number of deaths at 3.8 million . Western Pacific Region was second with 3.5 million environment related deaths . The Americas and European Regions had lower burden of deaths due to environmental risks . <p> Poonam Khetrapal Singh , WHO regional director , said , " Few can be unaware of the growing region-wide burden of non-communicable diseases that are being caused by the environment . Unintentional injuries , for example , road traffic deaths , have the third-highest contribution with 1.7 million deaths annually and cancers contribute to 1.7 million deaths . <p> However , the report noted that with improved sanitation , deaths from infectious diseases have come down . <p> The report cites proven strategies for improving the environment and preventing diseases .
