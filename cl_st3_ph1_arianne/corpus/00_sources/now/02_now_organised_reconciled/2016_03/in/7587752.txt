<h> Experts want Bt tech in varieties and not hybrids <p> Having Bt in varieties allows farmer to produce his own seeds and help preserve the diversity in Indian cotton species <p> Mar 9 , 2016 , 03.27 AM IST <p> Nagpur : The pink bollworm resistance has apparently reduced effectiveness of Bt technology . The yield of Bt cotton in India has stagnated over the last 10 years or so . The obvious question is : What went wrong ? Bollgard-II is still effective against pink bollworms in USA , China and Australia despite 18 years of cultivation in these countries . How could it develop resistance in India within a short span of six years ? <p> Experts say it was a big mistake to adapt Bt technology only in hybrids , and this is what led to yield stagnation and rapid resistance development . India is the only country in the world which has the Bt ( Bacillus thuringiensis ) gene incorporated in hybrids . " <p> In India , about 95% area in cotton is under the Bollgard-II of multinational giant Monsanto which is considered by experts as the most potent technology to have ever been used for bollworm control . It should have ideally resulted in such high yields that India should have been the highest cotton producer until now . <p> But the reality is different . Despite a brilliant two-gene genetically modified ( GM ) Bt technology in the much touted high yield hybrids and the best of all available technologies , India ranks 32nd in productivity out of 80 cotton growing countries . This ranking leaves India behind at least 24 countries which do not have Bt cotton and behind 31 countries which cultivate only varieties and not hybrids . <p> What went wrong with hybrids ? Kranthi says , " Hybrids are inherently designed to perform best under high input conditions . " The problems of yield stagnation , doubling of fertilizer use and resurgence of insecticide usage over the past 6-7 years is primarily because of the insistence on hybrids for Bt and also because of the long list of more than 1,600 Bt-cotton hybrids which were approved for cultivation in rain-fed regions without ascertaining their suitability for rainfed soils that constitute 60% of Indian cotton area . <p> In the case of Bt gene in varieties , the genes will be present in homozygous condition of two copies of the gene in every cell which ensures that all seeds in bolls of the Bt-variety would contain Bt genes . In contrast , Bt genes in Bt-hybrids are present only as one copy per cell in heterozygous form . Because of this , about 25% of seeds in Bollgard Bt cotton hybrids and 6% in Bollgard-II hybrids do not contain any Bt toxins . This reduces efficacy of bollworm control and accelerates resistance development . <p> Pink bollworm is a winter @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ cotton , that is grown in the US , China , Brazil , Mexico and Australia , escapes pink bollworm . Thus in countries such as USA , China , Brazil , Mexico and Australia , pink bollworm has not developed resistance despite 18 years of cultivation , because of the short duration varieties of 150-180 days used there . Further , these countries obtain 2-4 times higher yield than India using high density planting of straight Bt-varieties , in climatic conditions that are far less suitable for cotton as compared to India . Long duration hybrids in India facilitated multiple cycles of the pink bollworm every year to accelerate resistance development . Another factor is suitability of short duration early maturing varieties for rainfed regions . A short flowering and boll formation window of 80-100 days will match the critical requirement of nutrients and water with moisture availability in the soil . The seed cost of Bt varieties is at least 8 times less than the cost of hybrid seed . But this would have resulted in the plants producing seeds that could be replanted year after year , which is something seed companies did not want . The companies wanted farmers to buy seeds from the market every year . <p> This would have resulted in Bt cotton hybrid seeds that are homozygous for Bt genes and all seeds in bolls of the hybrid plants would have contained Bt toxins . The seed companies may not have preferred this option primarily to prevent farmers from reusing the farm saved seeds which would be homozygous for Bt-toxins .
