TELANGANA <h> Development at the cost of nature ! <h> Huge quantities of rock and sand being extracted for constructing barrages across Penganga , Pranahita and Godavari rivers which will upset the ecological balance <p> Rock from the hillock near the historic ruins of Battisgarh fort is mined for the stone crusher unit at Nirmal in Adilabad district. -Photo : S. Harpal Singh <p> What possible justification can there be in plundering natural resources to create a new one ? This is a question that conservationists have been asking whenever there is a talk of constructing dams across rivers for irrigating agriculture lands . <p> The question has surfaced following signing of a Memorandum of Understanding on March 8 between the Telangana and Maharashtra governments for construction of a series of barrages across Penganga , Pranahita and Godavari rivers . That the work on the projects will commence soon is evident from the hurry with which the Telangana government is working on them . <p> Among the lesser focussed aspects associated with environmental destruction during construction of dams and barrages is the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ invariably upsets the ecological balance of the place where the quarries are located , usually far away from the given project site . <p> Take for example the proposed barrage of the Pranahita-Chevella project across the Pranahita river at Tummidi-Hatti in Koutala mandal of Adilabad district . Its non-overflow and gated sections require concreting to the tune of a mammoth 13.8 lakh cubic meters ( CBM ) . <p> For this , nearly 12.5 lakh CBM of rock and nearly 7.5 lakh CBM of sand is required , leave alone cement . In terms of lorry loads , it translates into nearly 80,000 tonne loads of rock and nearly 50,000 similar loads of sand . <p> The smaller Chanaka-Korta barrage across Penganga in Jainad mandal requires about 4 lakh CBM of concreting works , means it requires nearly 3.75 lakh CBM of rock or more than 23,000 lorry loads of it and 2.2 lakh CBM of sand or more than 13,500 lorry load of it . " The project cycle needs to be studied before going ahead with the project as extraction of construction material in huge quantities can upset the local eco-system , " she opines as she talks of the need for Environmental Impact Assessment to be done by the Ministry of Environment , Forests and Climate Change . <p> The eco-system of river Godavari in the district is already under considerable stress what with unchecked mining of sand even under drought conditions . The river is likely to be plundered more , given the excessively huge quantum of demand for sand in the wake of many projects taking shape in the coming months .
