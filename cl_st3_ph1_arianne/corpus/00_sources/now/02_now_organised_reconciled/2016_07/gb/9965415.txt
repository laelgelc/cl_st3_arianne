<p> The sound of the first cuckoo in spring is a familiar one in the British countryside . <p> But the summer visitor is in decline and , according to a new study , its migratory habits may be to blame . <p> Scientists have tagged birds leaving the UK and believe they take two different routes on their journey to spend the winter in Africa . <p> Surprisingly , survival is lower on the shorter route via Spain , they report in the journal , Nature Communications . <p> And this suggests that migration - as well as other factors such as loss of farmland and insect food - may be to blame for the cuckoo 's decline . <p> More than half of cuckoos in the UK have been lost over the past 20 years , according to the British Trust for Ornithology ( BTO ) in Norfolk , which led the research . <p> " That pattern of starting in the same place but taking two very different routes to get there has not been seen before in any birds , to the best of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> " We need to understand the full annual cycle of a migratory bird in order to understand its population decline .
