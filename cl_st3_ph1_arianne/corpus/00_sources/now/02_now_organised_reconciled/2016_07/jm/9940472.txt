<h> News <h> Ambassador Bernal appears before US congressional hearing on Carib <p> Former Jamaican Ambassador to the United States Dr Richard Bernal was among three expert witnesses who were invited by the United States Congress for a hearing Thursday on issues of concern in the Caribbean . <p> The House Committee on Foreign Affairs ' Subcommittee on Western Hemisphere hearing , entitled ' The Strategic Importance of Building a Stronger US-Caribbean Partnership ' , took place in Washington , DC , and was chaired by Congressman Jeff Duncan ( Republican , South Carolina ) who explained the rationale and timing . <p> Among US concerns about the Caribbean , Duncan mentioned that the region was seen as " a haven for drug and human traffickers , gun smugglers , and other transnational criminal organisations " . They fielded questions from members of Congress , who included long-standing supporter of the Caribbean , Congressman Meeks . <p> Members raised a wide range of issues among which were the Haiti-Dominican Republic situation , the impact of Cuba on tourism in the Caribbean , measures to be taken to prevent the further spread of the Zika virus , and the fallout from the uncertainty about PetroCaribe . <p> The three experts called on the US to give more attention to the Caribbean region , arguing that it was the third border of the US which was the Caribbean 's most important economic partner in trade , investment , remittances , and sources of tourists . <p> Bernal suggested that it was time for a bold new comprehensive policy initiative from the US that must address the debt situation which @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ maintain public sector investment in infrastructure , education and health . <p> Further technical cooperation and financial support from the US was needed to strengthen the capacity of governments in the region to combat transnational crime , narcotics trafficking and cybercrime . <p> Ambassador Bernal also highlighted the threat of climate change and sea-level rise as a clear and present danger to the coastal societies of the Caribbean where population , economic activity , infrastructure , and the built environment were concentrated on a narrow strip on the coast at sea level . <p> He stressed that ultimately national security was inextricably linked to sustainable economic development and crime fighting and climate mitigation measures were only a partial remedy .
