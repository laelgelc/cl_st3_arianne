But instead of destroying life on Earth , the death of several nearby stars may have prompted the rise of humanity . <p> Two studies earlier this year showed Earth has been caught in the fallout of supernovas twice -- roughly 2.5 million years ago and another roughly 8 million years ago . <p> Both appear to have coincided with periods of global cooling . <p> Now a new study uses computer modelling to show these supernovas likely blasted Earth with a long-lasting ' gust ' of cosmic radiation which buffeted our atmosphere . <p> " I was surprised to see as much effect as there was , " said Adrian Melott , professor of physics at the University of Kansas , who co-authored the paper in The Astrophysical Journal Letters . <p> " I was expecting there to be very little effect at all , " he says .
