<p> Countries worldwide could see declines in the numbers of migratory birds reaching their shores , and the Canadian and Russian Arctic islands may be the last refuges these species have . <p> We studied migratory shorebirds , superstars of global migration that cover tens of thousands of kilometres a year , and sometimes travel more than 10,000km in a single flight . These amazing birds breed in the Arctic and then fly south , stopping at known refuelling points en route to their non-breeding grounds , some of them in the Southern Hemisphere . Protecting these ultra-mobile species that cross international borders is a particularly difficult conservation challenge . <p> Shorebirds are embattled by habitat loss and hunting along their migratory routes . Nowhere is this more apparent than the route used by species that migrate to Australia : the East Asian/Australasian Flyway . Most species that travel between Australia and the Arctic stop off at mudflats in the Yellow Sea off China . But coastal habitat there is being rapidly destroyed and population numbers are already crashing .
