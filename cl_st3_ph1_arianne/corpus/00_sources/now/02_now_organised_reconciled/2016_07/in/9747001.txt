<h> ISRO-led study analysed satellite imageries of the country over an eight-year period <p> Nearly 30 per cent per cent of the country 's total geographical area is undergoing degradation , according to a study that analysed satellite imageries of the country over an eight-year period . <p> The degrading area has increased over 0.5 per cent to 29.3 million hectares during the period , as shown by comparative remote-sensing satellite imageries of the States for the periods 2003-05 and 2011-13 . Desertification increased by 1.16 million hectares ( m ha ) and stood at 82.64 m ha during 2011-13 . <p> Action plan <p> There was high desertification and degradation in Delhi , Tripura , Nagaland , Himachal Pradesh and Mizoram , while Odisha , Telangana , Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh showed some improvement . <p> The ongoing study , initiated by the Ministry of Environment , Forests &amp; Climate Change , was led by the Indian Space Research Organisation and involved 19 institutes . The atlas , adding 68 vulnerable districts , would form part of the country 's action plan to arrest the phenomenon and also be a status report to the U.N . body , ISRO said . The SAC had undertaken a similar study in 2007 . <p> Just nine States together account for nearly 24 per cent of desertification ; the other States have less than one per cent of this land . The culprit States in that order are Rajasthan , Maharashtra , Gujarat , Jammu &amp; Kashmir , Karnataka , Jharkhand , Odisha , Madhya Pradesh and Telangana . <p> Southern State Kerala figures among northern and northeastern States where less than 10 per cent land is degraded . With it are relatively greener States of Assam , Mizoram , Haryana , Bihar , Uttar Pradesh , Punjab and Arunachal Pradesh . <p> Water erosion <p> The bad news is that 3.6 million ha of productive land are getting lost , while on the positive side , some @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ been slowed down in a few other areas .
