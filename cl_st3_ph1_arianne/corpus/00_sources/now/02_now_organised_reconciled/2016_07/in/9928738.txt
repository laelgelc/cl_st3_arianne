<h> A breath of fresh air <p> Picture this : you wake up in the morning , get ready to go to work , have a healthy breakfast that keeps you going , you walk to work in peak hour traffic so you can get the exercise , and at the end of a busy work day , you hit the gym to keep that body of yours in good shape . Unfortunately , even the healthiest lifestyle could be undone by the increasing pollution levels in the air , particularly in large cities . This was demonstrated in a recent study that found a link between pollution and stroke incidence.The study was conducted using data from the United States ( US ) and China , two of the largest producers of greenhouse gases , and who are believed to contribute to over 33% of global warming . Data on air quality and particulate matter ( PM ) was collected from 1,118 counties in the US and from 120 cities in China . Dust , dirt , smoke particles and water droplets less than 2.5 microns in size @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , usually produced from fuel combustion . The results of the analysis revealed that there was a small but significant increase in the incidence of stroke ( 1.19% ) for each 10 ? g/m3 increase of PM2.5 . In regions where the PM2.5 levels were low , the incidence of stroke was correspondingly low . In addition , weather played an important role in predicting stoke incidence , with higher temperatures more likely to be associated with a greater risk of stroke . While the results did not provide conclusive evidence of the association between stroke and pollution , the study raises some important questions that should be addressed to combat climate change and global warming.So give your heart a fresh lease on life by taking a deep breath of unpolluted air during a morning walk in the park.Dr .
