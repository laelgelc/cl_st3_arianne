<p> The Indus Valley civilisation may be even older than initially thought . PHOTO : QUARTZ <p> New research shows that the Indus Valley civilisation may be 2,500 years older than initially believed . <p> Indian researchers have used carbon dating techniques on animal remains and pottery fragments to conclude that the Indus Valley settlements could be 8,000 years old , which would make the Indus Valley settlements even older than the Mesopotamian and Egyptian civilisations . <p> " Our study pushes back the antiquity to as old as 8th millennium before present and will have major implications to the evolution of human settlements in Indian sub-continent , " Anindya Sarkar , a professor at the department of geology and geophysics at IIT-Kharagpur , said in a statement . <p> Sarkar , along with his team of researchers from the Archaeological Survey of India , the Physical Research Laboratory in Ahmedabad and Pune 's Deccan College , used a technique known as the Optically Stimulated Luminescence , which measures the amount of light emitted from mineral grains to date past events . <p> The study , published in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Bhirrana in northern Indian state of Haryana , of which an extract reads : " Based on radiocarbon ages from different trenches and levels , the settlement at Bhirrana has been inferred to be the oldest ( &gt;9 ? ka BP ) in the Indian subcontinent . To check its validity we dated archaeological pottery from two cultural levels using optically stimulated luminescence ( OSL ) method and thus investigated the interrelationship between the cultural levels and climate change that occurred right at the settlement , a critical gap in information that exists in our present understanding of the Harappan civilisation . " <p> Archaeological studies had earlier suggested that the civilisation was centred around Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro in Pakistan , and Lothal , Dholavira , and Kalibangan in India , Sarkar explained . But the new findings suggest that it was actually spread over a much larger part of India , alongside River Saraswati , which is believed to have disappeared some 4,000 years ago . <p> A new theory for the decline of civilisation has also been proposed by researchers , which was earlier believed to have been caused @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ instead of shifting monsoon patterns , the decline of the civilisation could be attributed to a change in cropping patterns and storage of grains . <p> " Our study suggests that the climate was probably not the cause of Harappan decline , " the researchers said in a statement . <p> " Increasing evidences suggest that these people shifted their crop patterns from the large-grained cereals like wheat and barley during the early part of intensified monsoon to drought-resistant species of small millets and rice in the later part of declining monsoon and thereby changed their subsistence strategy , " they added . <p> But that shift in agriculture turned out to have devastating consequences , the researchers explained . Reason being that these later crops generally have much lower yield , and so , the organised large storage system of mature Harappan period was abandoned giving rise to smaller more individual household based crop processing and storage system .
