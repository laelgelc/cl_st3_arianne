" Like other establishment media , the Times has been alarmed by both the Donald Trump campaign and the " Brexit " vote and has struggled to understand the spreading " populist backlash . " <p> Political scientist Benjamin Barber 's mid-90s book , " Jihad vs . McWorld : How Globalism and Tribalism Are Reshaping the World , " provides a useful framework for understanding what is going on . In Barber 's account , " McWorld " represents the globalizing culture of capitalism . " Jihad " refers to reactions to the spread of this culture , not only Islamist extremism but all forms of ethnic nationalism or racist xenophobia . <p> Barber maintained that the two forces feed off each other , and the corporate media play a crucial role in this process . As he put it , " Jihad not only revolts against but @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and reinforces Jihad . " Whereas Jihad is fed by emotion , often reflecting yearning for a mythical past , McWorld offers the " rationality " of the market and illusions of a universal , just future . From the perspective of the market , Jihad is irrational and dangerous . From the perspective of Jihad , McWorld threatens one 's sense of place , one 's religious beliefs and traditions , even one 's identity . <p> Crucially , Barber argues , Jihad and McWorld interact to undermine civil society and the democratic institutions of the nation-state . That is the issue we face today : can we escape the no-win choice of Jihad &amp; McWorld and reclaim democracy ? <p> In this framework , Donald Trump and " Brexit " represent Jihad , and Hillary Clinton and the EU and British establishment represent McWorld . Trump has tapped into angry frustrations among sectors of the population who have long felt the world is leaving them behind . For decades , people drawn to Trump have been told that they are victims of the liberal establishment and its " Big Government . " <p> Ironically , however , Trump succeeds because of a foundational McWorld institution : the entertainment medium of television news , which spreads the culture of drama , personalities and conflict that grab our attention and play on our feelings . This is a world of images and sound bites , not sober political realities . Trump 's recent attacks on NAFTA or the Trans-Pacific Partnership ( TPP ) resonate well with people whose economic security has been shattered by these so-called " free trade " agreements . <p> In the context of Great Britain , similar dynamics are at work .
