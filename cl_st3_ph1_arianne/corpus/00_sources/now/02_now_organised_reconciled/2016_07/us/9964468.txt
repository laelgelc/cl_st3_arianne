<p> Between 2011 and 2014 , while humans were discovering dubstep and the wonder of selfies , Greenland was melting fast . It lost a trillion tons of ice in just three years , and the world neither noticed nor gave a damn . <p> That 's according to a new , open-access study published in Geophysical Research Letters , which used the European Space Agency 's CryoSat satellite , along with regional ice models , to show that recent melting of the Greenland ice sheet contributed twice as much to sea level rise ( roughly 2.5 millimeters over the study period ) as the prior two decades . In other words , Greenland -- which has been visibly losing ice since the ' 90s -- is melting at an accelerating rate . <p> This falls right in line with the picture Earth scientists have built by studying past ice sheet contractions . Namely , when ice starts melting , it does n't go in a plodding , linear fashion .
