<h> NZ MPs condemn Paris attacks <p> New Zealand 's Parliament has added its voice to global condemnation of the terror attacks in Paris . <p> French Ambassador to New Zealand Florence Jean-Blanc Risler was present in the debating chamber this afternoon to hear MPs across the House express solidarity with her country . <p> Acting Prime Minister Bill English , who moved the motion to condemn the attacks , said New Zealanders were deeply shocked by the brutal events that unfolded on Friday night . <p> " Our hearts go out to the ... people caught up in this despicable act of terrorism . <p> " In particular , we express our deepest condolences to those who lost loved ones as well as those who were injured , some of whom are still fighting for their lives . " <p> Echoing comments made by Prime Minister John Key last week , Mr English said New Zealand " stands shoulder to shoulder with France in the global fight against terror " . <p> Mr Key would express New Zealand 's condolences to the French President Francois Hollande in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ climate change talks . <p> Labour leader Andrew Little said Paris was a city which represented " the best about our civilisation " and had been attacked by people who " wanted to return us to the worst kind of barbarism " . <p> Mr Little said it was important to remember why terror occurred - to cause fear in civilised countries . <p> " They win when we succumb to the fear that they foster , " he said . " They win when we respond only with anger and hatred . <p> " They win when we turn on ourselves and on our peaceful communities . They win when our only response is revenge retribution . " <p> Green Party foreign affairs spokesman Kennedy Graham also expressed his sadness about the 132 deaths in Paris , while also recognising other victims in Lebanon , Turkey , Russia , USA , Palestine , Israel and Egypt . <p> " To paraphrase a valiant leader from a century ago , your sons and daughters so cruelly slain in the city of Paris this Friday evening are our sons and daughters today @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ are united in sorrow and respect . Our hearts go out to the families of the fallen . " <p> In a short speech , New Zealand First leader Winston Peters said he wanted the French people to know that New Zealand was with them in their hour of grief and anguish . " Vive la France , " he concluded . <p> The commemorations finished with a waiata led by Maori Party co-leader Marama Fox .
