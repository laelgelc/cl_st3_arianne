<h> Kervellerin recycles oyster shells <p> Nature is full of untapped renewable materials , like oyster shells . A small French company is finding highly innovative uses for them : as an ingredient for paint and for the filaments of 3D printers . <p> " We do n't pay enough attention to nature . You can find wealth even in the garbage " , says Martine Le Lu . In 1999 , this chemist concerned by environmental protection took over the small business founded by her father in Cl ? guer , a small town near Lorient , in Brittany . She left a good executive position in a pharmaceutical company and invested all her savings in the production of innovative sustainable materials . After three years of research , she decided to recycle oyster shells . " My factory is in Morbihan , a department full of oyster farmers . Until recently , many oysters were discarded as waste because they did n't reach maturity . Now , I pay to collect and transform them . The product costs twice as much as petroleum-based paste but it lasts far longer . <p> No matter how great Martine Le Lu 's scientific expertise is , she ca n't work entirely alone . So she partnered with scientific labs such as Bretagne Sud University in Lorient to help her break into the new 3D printing market . The powder from the crushed oysters is going to help produce the filaments needed to print objects . " It can be used in many different ways " , points out the manager , who does n't want to be more specific . <p> Her factory covers 3,000 m ? and employs a small team of eight people .
