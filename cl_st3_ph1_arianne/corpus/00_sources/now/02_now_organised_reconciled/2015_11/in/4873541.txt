<h> For climate action plans to succeed , focus should be on principles of equity <p> Paris , just recovering from dastardly terrorist attacks , has shown its resilience and has braced itself for fighting another major threat facing the world -- climate change . Beginning Monday , negotiators from 190 countries will converge on the French capital for fortnight-long annual exercise of protracted talks under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change . This round of talks has attracted much attention and raised expectations because of the change in strategy to fight climate change , which was adopted at the Warsaw round two years ago . Till now the approach and goal of climate negotiations was to arrive at an agreement or deal under which countries -- according to their level of development -- would be asked to limit or cut their greenhouse gas emissions . In other words , all countries should effect legally mandated emission reductions over a given time frame . Developed countries and some emerging economies had a problem with this approach . Kyoto Protocol , which had mandated legally binding cuts @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Therefore , a ' bottoms up ' approach was proposed at Warsaw . Instead of a legal treaty on emission cuts , all countries were asked to submit voluntary action plans to reduce emissions . This new tool was called Intended Nationally Determined Contributions or INDCs . Every country had two years to prepare this plan and submit it to the UN . Most countries have done this and Paris will review the progress in this regard . But we must remember that INDC is an interim tool and is voluntary . These are actions that countries pledge to take between 2020 and 2030 . The time period from 2015 to 2020 will be used to hold further talks about a climate change treaty that will take effect after 2030 . Basically , the world has bought some time in the form of INDCs and trying to see if voluntary action -- instead of legally binding action -- is sufficient to face the threat of climate change . That 's why Paris talks could turn out to be a milestone in deciding future pathways for climate talks . <p> As is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ but about economy and politics . Carbon emissions are directly linked to economic growth . Of course , it is possible to take a low-carbon growth trajectory but it requires new technology and additional finance . Second , climate talks are about equity . The carbon budget of the world is limited . The developed economies have already used up much of this budget since the industrial revolution . If developing countries have to achieve economic growth and development , they have to be accommodated in the limited ' carbon space ' that the world has . There is a limit to how much the world collectively can emit for the temperature rise to remain below 2 degree rise from the pre-industrial era . Climate science has predicted that we will witness catastrophic impacts of climate change if global temperature rise is more than 2 degrees . This is indisputable and accepted by all countries . <p> Climate actions -- whether voluntary or legally mandated -- of different countries have to be designed in such a way that they ensure equity . Developed economies will have to do more in the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ for developing and poor countries . This is necessary to correct historical inequities . Between 1850 and 2011 , America emitted 411 billion tonnes of carbon dioxide . It means a country with about five per cent of world population has emitted 21 per cent of world 's total carbon dioxide till 2011 . The 28-member European Union has emitted 18.4 per cent . In comparison , China has emitted 10.7 per cent and India just 2.8 per cent . Today China and India may figure in top five emitters , but their emissions still remain far below in historical context . From 1850 -- 2011 , the entire continent of Africa emitted 50 billion tonnes of carbon dioxide , which is 2.7 per cent of world 's total emissions . Global carbon emissions increased by 50 per cent since 1992 when climate talks began . The carbon dioxide concentration in the atmosphere increased from 356 parts per million ( ppm ) in 1992 to 400 ppm now . Yet the number of people without access to electricity remained almost same -- 1.5 billion in 1992 to 1.3 billion in 2012 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> It was because of such inequities that the framework convention was encased in the principle of ' common but differentiated responsibility ' . Over the years , developed countries have tried to give a go-by to this important idea , and are pushing for an agreement that treats all countries equally irrespective of their past emissions . When this did not succeed , the instrument of INDC was conceived . The effort on their part is to somehow to stave off legally binding targets . Paris will have to take a call if INDCs -- submitted by 150 countries till now -- add up to meet the target of keeping temperature rise below 2 degrees . What happens if voluntary actions proposed by different countries are not sufficient enough ? Will countries which have proposed lower reduction ambitions be asked to enhance their targets ? If yes , what will be the basis of doing so ? How will the progress on these voluntary actions be monitored ? Will developing countries get necessary finance and technology to move to low-carbon growth , as they have been demanding for several years @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ be breaking their heads in the next two weeks . <p> INDCs have already been examined by independent researchers to see if they really add up to something sizeable . Scientists compared the commitments to a world in which countries do n't act at all or start reducing greenhouse gas emissions only in 2030 . They found that if countries do nothing to reduce emissions , the earth has almost no chance of staying under the 2 degree limit , and it is likely that the temperature rise would be above 4 degrees . Much depends on what is done beyond these commitments post- 2030 . If countries implement their INDCs through 2030 and ramp up efforts beyond it , the world will have a much better chance of avoiding extreme warming and keeping temperature change below 2 degrees , the study has noted . Voluntary pledges should be treated just as a stepping stone for further action . Whatever may be the outcome of Paris , it is clear that climate change is real and that we need real action to reduce chances of it becoming catastrophic for future generations @
