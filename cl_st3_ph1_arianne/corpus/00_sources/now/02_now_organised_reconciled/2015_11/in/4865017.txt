<h> Corporate responsibility reporting on the rise , finds KPMG survey <p> Even though India tops in the number of firms reporting on corporate responsibility , much of this information falls short of international standards . Photo : Getty Images <p> New Delhi : India steals the show when it comes to responsibility reporting , suggests the 9th edition of a survey on corporate responsibility reporting by KPMG International Cooperative . <p> The Swiss company , in partnership with its affiliate KPMG across 45 countries , including India , covered 4,500 companies in the survey , the results of which were released on Wednesday . <p> Called ' Currents of Change ' , the survey on corporate responsibility reporting in 2015 culls out information from the top 100 companies in each country ( N 100 ) surveyed to map trends . The information is based on publicly available documents and is analysed on a qualitative scoring methodology devised by KPMG International . <p> The N 100 comprises the top 100 listed companies in the participant country . <p> The survey also puts out a list of the top @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ regard to corporate responsibility ( CR ) reporting and has a large section dedicated to carbon disclosures . <p> The key findings of the survey suggest that globally , 1 in every 5 high-carbon-footprint companies , like those in the mining or chemicals sectors , do not report on carbon . And , " less than 1 in 10 companies reports on carbon emissions from the use or disposal of their products " . <p> " We also analysed the quality of carbon disclosures in India . It was found that 62% of Indian companies identify climate change as a material issue and 42% of N 100 companies report on carbon emissions as compared to 80% of G 250 companies , " said Santhosh Jayaram , director-sustainability at KPMG India . <p> With the United Nations ' climate change talks , called the Conference of Parties ( CoP ) 21 , just weeks away , the importance of carbon disclosures is not lost on Indian firms . <p> Rajeev Dubey , group president ( HR and corporate services ) and CEO ( after-market sector ) of the Mahindra Group , said @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ depends on following a low-carbon path . Corporations play a very important role in ensuring that society gets onto this low-carbon path and carbon disclosures are a transparent way of achieving this objective . " <p> Jayaram hopes that the report will " encourage corporates to take more pro-active steps to help mitigate climate change " . He adds that the survey is akin to holding a mirror to companies , showcasing their non-financial reporting . <p> India has seen the biggest jump in overall corporate responsibility reporting -- from 20% in 2011 to 73% in 2013 and 100% in 2015 , the highest among the 45 countries . Reporting on sustainability governance has gained importance for companies in India due to additional disclosures required as part of the Business Responsibility Reporting ( BRR ) directive from capital market regulator Securities and Exchange Board of India in 2012 and Section 135 of the Companies Act , 2013 , which pertains to corporate social responsibility . <p> While the number of Indian companies reporting and disclosing non-financial information has seen a sharp rise , the quality of the reports has not kept @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the fact that India has the highest CR reporting percentage , Jayaram points out that India still lags behind the G 250 in terms of quality of reportage . " Indian companies lack in terms of making balanced reports , with only a limited number of companies talking about challenges and dilemmas as part of their disclosures , " he said . <p> Aditi Haldar , director , Global Reporting Initiative ( GRI ) India , believes that the first step to improving sustainability and corporate responsibility is reporting . According to her , Indian companies are keen to be seen as global players , which has been another factor in the improved CR reporting in the country . <p> GRI is an international organization that helps businesses and governments understand and communicate the impact of business on issues such as climate change , human rights , etc . <p> " India has emerged as a major global economic player and transparency is important for many foreign investors who are interested in Indian businesses , " Haldar said . <p> The importance of corporate responsibility reporting is gaining momentum across the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ India , explained , " Reporting on sustainability and responsibility indicators gives crucial insights to how a business will cope with the changing socio-economic environment . In India , it 's great that progressive regulations on BRR are in place and that companies are beginning to put in place transparent mechanisms to comply with these regulations . " <p> Oxfam India released the India Responsible Business Forum ( IRBF ) index for the top 100 NSE companies last month . <p> As per the KPMG survey , growth in CR reporting was driven by a surge in reporting within countries such as India , Taiwan and South Korea , where mandatory and voluntary reporting requirements were introduced since the last survey in 2013 . More than 79% companies in this region now report on CR , followed by the Americas with 77% and Europe with 74% . <p> While the findings of the 2015 KPMG survey are encouraging in certain aspects , in others , they bring to the fore a number of inconsistencies . The report , quoting Wim Bartels , KPMG 's global head of sustainability reporting and assurance @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ analysis is a view of fragmented , inconsistent approaches and patchy transparency . Key information is missing from many annual financial and corporate responsibility reports ... It is all but impossible to accurately compare one company 's carbon performance with another 's . " <p> However , Jayaram is optimistic that Indian companies are going to improve in the near future . According to him , the involvement of the top management will help improve the quality of CR reporting in the near future as it has for the quantity .
