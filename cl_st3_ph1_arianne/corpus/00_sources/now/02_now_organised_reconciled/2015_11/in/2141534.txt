<p> MELBOURNE : Adelie penguins may be benefiting from shrinking glaciers , according to a new study that found the population of the species in East Antarctica has risen 135 times than it was around 19,000 years ago . <p> The study led by researchers at the University of Tasmania in Australia looked at the DNA of Adelie penguins to identify population trends over the last 22,000 years . <p> It found that the penguin population in East Antarctica is 135 times larger now than it was when the last ice age ended around 19,000 years ago . <p> Adelie penguins nest on ice-free land along the Antarctic coastline and therefore benefit from deglaciation , while their foraging for food for their chicks is made easier by a reduction in sea ice . <p> Lead author Jane Younger said the research showed that when considering the effects of climate change , it is important to consider the long-term impact over millennia as well as the immediate effects . <p> " We found that the Adelie penguin population in East Antarctica was very small during the ice age , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ ice age ended , " said Younger . <p> " The increase started around 14,000 years ago , about the same time as glaciers were shrinking in East Antarctica . <p> However , Younger said the benefits for the East Antarctic Adelies could be balanced by the impact of climate change on its main food sources , and by less favourable conditions in other parts of the Antarctic . <p> " For Adelie penguin populations to expand they must have adequate food supplies to meet the requirements of the expanding population , " said Younger . <p> " Whether this will be the case in the future remains to be seen , as the impacts of climate change on Adelie penguin prey species , such as Antarctic krill , are unclear at this time , " she said . <p> " It is very important to note that Adelie penguin numbers are declining in some parts of Antarctica , it would therefore be incorrect to state that climate change is
