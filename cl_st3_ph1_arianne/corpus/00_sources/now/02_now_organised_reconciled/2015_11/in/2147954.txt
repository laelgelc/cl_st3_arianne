<h> The Road of Paris Climate Conference <p> From 1950 through 2012 , there have been more than 1159 Multilateral Environmental Agreements . Overtime , the environmental issues have been grouped into three categories , namely -- green ( deforestation , reduction of green cover ) , brown ( air pollution ) and blue ( water pollution ) . The tricolor of green , brown and blue has become an important symbol that both unites and divides the world . It unites , for it provides the world with a common cause which impacts everyone and at the same time motivates each entity to muster their resources to achieve a common ' superordinate ' goal . It divides , for it creates a new paradigm of looking at the world and thereby engenders new interest groups . The developed and the developing economies are seen as competing interest groups on the stage of environmental change and negotiations . Like any political situation , the environmental agenda runs the risk of becoming a battle between forces of cooperative growth and the forces of destructive petty self-interest . <p> Nevertheless , as Stephen Hawking has said , " Mankind 's greatest achievements have come about by talking , and its greatest failures by not talking " , hence , the only solution to the problem of bringing together the divergent ideas and interests regarding the critical issue of environment is through dialogue . Better late than never , the dialogues did start between governments back in the late 1970s regarding the issue of the survival of our planet , which culminated in the First World Climate Conference ( 1979 ) at Geneva . At this time , on an ' awareness-to-action continuum ' , the talks were primarily focused towards creating a general awareness of the magnitude of the environmental challenge . The creation of the World Climate Research Programme and later the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) in 1988 , were the results of the maturation of the ' awareness stage ' thus heralded by the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ scientific , technical and socio-economic information relevant to understanding the degree of risk of human-induced climate change " , the IPCC produced some extremely incisive research regarding climate change and global warming , that sent shock-waves in the international community and brought home the realization that something had to be done , that too urgently , in order to keep this planet an inhabitable place . <p> It took nearly a decade to graduate from the ' awareness stage ' of environmentalism to the beginning of the ' acceptance stage ' , which , it can be said , was inaugurated with the Second Climate Conference that met again in Geneva in 1990 . Indeed , as it has been pointed out by many , this conference was somewhat more political than the first one , for it made clear the writing on the wall -- that firstly , climate change had real impact on the lives of the people , the global economy and public health ; and secondly , the creation of unique interests in the global community was ineluctable.It became evident that rapid industrialization had brought the world @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ more of it would prove to be deleterious for the planet , on the other hand , those nations that had yet not tasted the forbidden fruit of industrial growth would be in a disadvantageous position . Developing nations expected to forgo industrial growth in the name of the environment expectedly protested and a not so laissez-faire mechanism of the ' carbon credit system ' was invented with the Kyoto Protocol of the year 1997 , as part of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change ( UNFCCC ) set up in 1992 . <p> With Kyoto , the ' acceptance stage ' of the environmental movement seemed to imperceptibly give way to the ' action stage ' . Kyoto Protocol could be christened as ' positive affirmative action for the developing world combined with responsible progress for the developed nations ' . Yet , with the beginning of the action stage , many a members of the global community felt shy of putting to action , what had been vowed in words . For example , in the United States , while the Clinton Administration signed the treaty , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ George W. Bush famously objected that " it ( Kyoto Protocol ) exempts 80% of the world , including major population centers such as China and India , from compliance , and would cause serious harm to the US economy " . In 2011 , Canada declared its withdrawal from the Protocol and Japan along with Russia also made evident their non-committal stance to any further Kyoto targets . Hence , the transition from the ' acceptance stage ' to the ' action stage ' for the environmental movement has not been a smooth one . <p> Since the 1992 UNFCCC treaty , eighteen Conferences of the Parties ( COP ) sessions have been held and in that time , global CO2 emissions have risen significantly even after a full running of almost two Kyoto commitment periods . While doomsayers speak of the end of the " age of environmentalism " , it must not be forgotten that the " keep talking " principle is still driving gradual , yet steady progress . What is heartening , is that there has been a conscious effort to bridge conflicting interests .
