<p> A new study says West Antarctic coastal snow rose 30 per cent in 20th century <p> Annual snow accumulation on West Antarctica 's coastal ice sheet increased dramatically by about 30 per cent during the 20th century , according to a new study . <p> The last three decades saw more snow build up on the ice sheet than at any other time in the last 300 years , researchers said . <p> The study used ice cores to estimate annual snow accumulation from 1712 to 2010 along West Antarctica 's coast . <p> Until 1899 , annual snow accumulation remained steady , averaging 33 and 40 centimetres of water , or melted snow , each year at two locations . <p> Annual snow accumulation increased in the early 20th century , rising 30 per cent between 1900 and 2010 , according to the study . <p> The researchers found that in the last 30 years of the study , the ice sheet gained nearly 5 metres more water than it did during the first 30 years of the studied time period . <p> " Since the @ @ @ @ @ Ice sheets start their lives as snowflakes . When snowfall builds up year after year , the weight of the snow compresses the bottom layers into ice , creating an ice sheet . <p> For the study , researchers collected two ice cores from Ellsworth Land , the strip of land that connects the Antarctic Peninsula to the rest of the continent . The ice cores contain layer upon layer of ice -- the remnants of yearly snowfall . <p> By measuring the thickness of the ice laid down each year , the researchers estimated annual snow accumulation for the past 300 years .
