<h> VOICE OF THE PEOPLE NOV. 24 , 2015 <p> On several occasions during rainfalls , an odour pervades Halifax Harbour in the area of Black Rock Beach in Point Pleasant Park . The probable cause is that HRM 's treatment plant is unable to cope . <p> Considering the popularity of the park and its value as a tourist attraction , it is time for HRM to consider updating the existing treatment plant . But HRM appears to be putting the proverbial cart before the horse in approving development before adequate municipal services are in place . <p> In HRM , combined sewer and storm water lines are frequently overloaded . With climate change , this situation will not improve . If , as suggested by Prime Minister Justin Trudeau , there will be increased funding for infrastructure , HRM would be well advised to consider such expenditures that can rectify this situation . <p> Haiven suggests providing Nova Scotian workers an actual living wage . Imagine that , a guaranteed living wage in this province . Thompson suggests rural Nova Scotia is resistant to the empty @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ many in this province have regarding the ongoing destruction of rural communities . <p> Thompson is right to suggest we are a resistant lot in these rural places , but she may well underestimate the extent to which rural Nova Scotia has been beaten down by politicians and those who sit comfortably in the living rooms of the one per cent . <p> It comes as a small surprise to me to discover that those at the top of the bureaucratic hubris in this province , in health care for instance , are making $350,000 annually from the public purse . This exceeds the median income of the one per cent by about $100,000 per year . To put this in perspective , this level of excess is equivalent to the take-home pay of about 25 to 30 citizens of Digby County . <p> Inequity is corrosive to the very soul of all Nova Scotia 's residents . Haiven is right to suggest it is time for a living wage in Nova Scotia . Yes , according to the Nova Scotia fisheries department , which has opened up the coastline of Nova Scotia to aquaculture development without placing restrictions on pesticides that kill lobster ( and sea lice ) . <p> The federal Fisheries Act now allows aquaculture companies freer access to pesticides to treat sea lice .
