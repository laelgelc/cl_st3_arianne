<h> News Local <h> Lively lake infested with algae <p> The Ministry of the Environment and Climate Change has advised the Sudbury and District Health Unit that water samples taken Nov. 3 from Makada ( Black ) Lake have tested positive for blue-green algae , which can produce toxins . <p> Algal blooms are not anchored and can move from one location to another in affected lakes . New blooms can also form . Blue-green algae has an unsightly pea soup appearance and foul smell . Residents should avoid using or drinking water from areas where blooms are visible . <p> The highest concentrations of toxins are usually found in blooms and scum along the shoreline . These dense accumulations pose the greatest potential risks to people and pets , as toxins can irritate a person 's skin and , if ingested , cause diarrhea and vomiting . If an individual ingests high levels of toxin , he/she could suffer liver and nervous system damage . <p> According to the city 's website , Makada Lake has 87 permanent residents , as well as 50 seasonal/recreational residents . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ to be on the lookout for algal blooms . If you spot a bloom near your property or water intake line , avoid using the water for drinking , bathing or showering , and do not allow children , pets , or livestock to drink or swim in the bloom ; avoid cooking with the water because food may absorb toxins from the water during cooking ; and exercise caution with respect to eating fish caught in water where blooms occur . Residents should abstain from eating the liver , kidneys and other organs of such fish . <p> Be aware that shallow drinking water intake pipes can pump in blue-green algae . Do not boil the water or treat it with a disinfectant , such as bleach , as that can break open the algae cells , which then release more toxins into the water .
