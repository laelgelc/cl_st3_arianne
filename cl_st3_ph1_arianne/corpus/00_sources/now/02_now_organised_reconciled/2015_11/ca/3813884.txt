According to an expert panel appointed by the U.S. Geological Survey , there will not be enough sea ice left in western Hudson Bay by mid-century to sustain this population of approximately 1,000 . <p> Signs of serious trouble began a decade ago when scientist Ian Stirling documented a 22 per cent decline in the polar bear population in western Hudson Bay from 1987 to 2004 . Although numbers appear to have stabilized since , Stirling warns against being optimistic . <p> Stirling has been conducting research on polar bears here and in other parts of the Arctic for more than 40 years . The animals he and his colleagues see in western Hudson Bay tend to be younger , shorter and thinner than the typical bear seen 20 or 30 years ago .
