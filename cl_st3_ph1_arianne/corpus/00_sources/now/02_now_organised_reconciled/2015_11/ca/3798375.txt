<p> CALGARY -- Change is coming to Alberta 's electricity sector , but industry watchers are divided on how it will affect rates for consumers already hit hard by the enduring global oil price slump . <p> Pointing to Ontario , TransAlta chief executive Dawn Farrell recently raised the spectre of rate hikes while pushing for the Calgary-based utility 's proposed plan to transition away from coal-fired power plants . <p> There is a " real risk to consumers , including Alberta businesses , of price spikes and volatility " as the province moves away from coal-fired generation and adds more renewable energy , she wrote in an opinion piece that ran in two Alberta newspapers . <p> In Ontario , she pointed out , electricity prices have climbed roughly 50 per cent over the past five years compared to 10 per cent in Alberta in the same period . <p> That 's " largely due to an aggressive program to retire coal generation and subsidize rapid renewable energy growth , " she wrote . <p> But
