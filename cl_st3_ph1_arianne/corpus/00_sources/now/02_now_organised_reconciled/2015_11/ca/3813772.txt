<h> Ship of state slow to change course <p> A long time ago , I read an article in Time magazine about super-tankers . <p> It discussed a man overboard situation and how long it takes a tanker to stop . These tankers have a particular pattern they employ during their 10 to 15 kilometres stopping distance which brings them back to the same point . <p> The article impressed on me the forces involved in stopping a massive ship . Momentum - mass times velocity - is a hard thing to deal with . The ship needs to overcome a massive amount of inertia . <p> Even just changing the direction of a tanker takes a lot of time , distance and planning . It takes foresight . <p> The same is true for governments . The ship of state has a lot of momentum . Turning it or stopping it takes a long time . Getting things going is equally difficult . <p> In the week following the federal election , this is the analogy which played in my mind .
