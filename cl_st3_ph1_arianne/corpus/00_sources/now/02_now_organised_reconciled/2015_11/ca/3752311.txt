<h> Sticky precedent ? Keystone XL rejection could be sign of more trouble to come for Alberta oilsands <p> In the fall of 2009 , not long after a group of Greenpeace protesters invaded a Shell site in northern Alberta , Edward Maibach gave an interview on the state of the oilsands in the American mind . <p> Maibach , the director for the Centre for Climate Change Communication at George Mason University , studies how global warming narratives and facts are communicated in the United States . At that point , he said , Alberta 's oilsands simply did n't register in the U.S. debate . <p> On Friday , Barack Obama , flanked by Vice-President Joe Biden and John Kerry , his Secretary of State , announced his rejection of the Keystone XL pipeline . In the process , he delivered a huge victory to a cross-border environmental movement that has homed in , with increasing focus , on the oilsands and their role in driving global climate change . <p> Few who follow the issue closely were shocked by the announcement .
