Burning fossil fuels emits greenhouse gases that trap more heat in the lower atmosphere . Since pre-industrial times the planet has already warmed 0.8 degrees C. This warming is increasing the rate of ice melt in Greenland , Antarctica and many of the world 's glaciers , adding more water to our oceans . <p> In addition , we know that heat causes liquids to expand . Coastal communities along the Atlantic Coast , from Charlottetown to Miami , are also feeling the pressure . Sea levels there are rising faster than the global average . <p> For low-lying communities , sea-level rise is n't the only challenge . Warming ocean waters are fuelling more intense hurricanes , which bring high winds , rain and strong storm surges . Combined with sea-level rise , the potential for flooding and property damage is amplified . <p> " One plus one can equal three , " said Radley Horton , co-author of a five-city study released in September that looked at the interaction of sea-level rise and storm surges from hurricanes . The study found that the two hazards , when taken together , could cause coastal flooding events to shoot up several hundredfold by 2100.
