<h> Taking charge of change <p> Climate change , species extinction , diminishing fossil fuel reserves , drought , carbon emissions , and ocean microplastics : it 's an extensive loss . Lately , I 've been noticing that the discourse concerning the environment seems underscored with a sense of unachievable solutions and fear , leaving the average MUN student , like myself , feeling quite powerless and overwhelmed . All of these concerns are certainly worthy of consideration and action , but what can I do about it ? The growing list of issues seems endless -- How does one choose what matters most ? <p> The first and most crucial step to postulating any possible solutions is to breathe . To realize that it is neither necessary nor possible to conquer all of the world 's nature nonsense at once . Secondly , it 's important to understand that every positive contribution to a cause is beneficial , no matter what the perceived impact . <p> A simple change I 've decided to take on is battery recycling . By properly recycling batteries , it saves harmful chemicals from possibly leaking into your garbage at home , the gasoline-filled garbage trucks , or the dump at Robin Hood Bay . And once in the landfill , does anyone truly know where the potent juices may eventually flow ? " Dead " batteries are still lively enough to create significant sparks , so it 's probably not the best idea to put them in that gas-filled garbage truck I mentioned before . They 're a household hazardous waste -- emphasis on hazardous -- and should thus be handled with care . <p> There are lots of other benefits to averting the landfill , but after looking into it even further , I realized that although recycling as much waste as possible is a daunting concept , there are other aspects involved in recycling that may be harmful in many complex ways . It takes a lot of energy and infrastructure , and therefore a whole lot of oil and green house gases , to recycle things . This presents ethical concerns as well , as our e-waste is diverted both nationally and internationally . <p> The more I look into it , the more complex and confusing the topic becomes . Here 's what I 've concluded : reduce battery use , and recycle when necessary . After all , the " four Rs " are not just coincidentally in a particular order : rethink , then reduce , then reuse , and then recycle . This way , there is less of an impact from excessive production and recycling , but you can still avert other harmful happenings by recycling when need be . <p> There are a surprising number of convenient locations that collect batteries for recycling . I find it easiest to drop mine off whenever I go to Staples , making sure to tape the terminals ( the ends of the batteries ) before transport .
