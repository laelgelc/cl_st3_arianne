<h> CBCP official urges gov't to ban coal-fired power plants <p> DAVAO CITY -- An official of the Catholic Bishops ' Conference of the Philippines ( CBCP ) has asked the Aquino administration to execute a moratorium on all coal-fired power plants as a concrete step to reduce greenhouse gas emissions . <p> The statement was made by National Secretariat for Social Action ( NASSA ) /Caritas Philippines Executive Secretary Fr . Edwin Gariguez during the Interfaith Dialogue on Climate Change held in Malaca ? ang Palace Thursday morning . <p> While the Philippine government has already pledged to reduce carbon emissions by 70 percent , Gariguez said that it can do more through changes on existing policies . <p> The commitment was made for the Intended Nationally Determined Contributions ( INDCs ) and submitted to the Conference of Parties meeting in Paris this December , <p> Gariguez said the Philippines has become heavily dependent on coal energy with at least 19 coal-fired power plants currently operating across the country and 20 more to be operational by year 2020 . <p> " Worse is , these coal projects @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ home to millions of Filipinos and are supported by rich ecosystems and bio-diversities , " he said . <p> Gariguez cited as examples the strong typhoons such as Typhoons Yolanda and Lando that are occurring more frequently . <p> " Truly , climate change-induced disasters spell indescribable miseries to our people . When we talk of climate change , we need to see real faces of suffering and feel the urgency of addressing the crisis , " he said . <p> The Catholic Church through the Caritas confederation has already reached out to 1.8 million people affected by Yolanda , and managed a total of P3.2 billion over the last two years . <p> But despite the rehabilitation efforts , Gariguez said that " climate change and its ensuing extreme weather conditions are still hurting the most at risk population .
