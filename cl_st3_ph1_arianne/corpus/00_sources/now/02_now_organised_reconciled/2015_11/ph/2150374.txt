<h> The world 's longest ice road <p> For most of the year , the world 's longest ice road is a series of lakes strung together by 64 portages ( short overland stretches ) . Tibbitt to Contwoyto Winter Road is an ice road located in the Northwest Territories and Nunavut in Northern Canada that connects Yellowknife , the territorial capital , to three diamond mines : Ekati , Diavik and Snap Lake . <p> The road has a length of 600 km and it was first built in 1982 . The ice road is the only overland resupply route for the mines and several exploration sites otherwise accessible only by air . They depend on it to truck in a year 's worth of supplies and equipment : Everything from fuel , ammonium nitrate for mine explosives , cement , equipment to supplies , and the ice road is open for only eight to nine weeks . <p> With most of it laid over lake ice , the road must be rebuilt each year . As climate change takes its toll , warmer weather has delayed ice road openings and made it harder to construct the roads . <p> When the ice reaches 107 cm , it is thick enough for a super B tanker , hauling two tanks of fuel weighing approximately 41-42 tonnes in total . Hagglunds , special amphibious tracked vehicles , measure the ice thickness everyday during the construction . Crews clear the snow and drill holes in the ice to flood certain areas and build up ice thickness . <p> Fully loaded trucks are allowed to travel at a maximum speed of 25 km/h and have to maintain a distance of 500 meters between each other . On some problem lakes , where ice is not thick enough , the speed limit is reduced to even 10 km/h.
