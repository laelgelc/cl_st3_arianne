<p> MANILA - The P10-billion budget for the Philippine hosting of the Asia Pacific Economic Cooperation should have gone to the rehabiliation of Yolanda-affected areas and the climate mitigation and adaptation efforts of the country , said Red Constantino of the Institute for Climate and Sustainable Cities . <p> Constantino participated in the Climate Vulnerable Forum Sherpa Senior Officials Meeting , a conference of 20 countries most vulnerable to climate change held in Manila earlier this week . These countries are pushing for their collective commitment on climate change before the Paris talks in December . <p> The Philippines could have opted out of the hosting right after Yolanda , when the country experienced the worst storm in history , Constantino said . <p> " Or we could have scaled it down , " he added . <p> The November 2013 super typhoon affected 16 million people , displaced 4.1 million , and killed 6,300 , with more than a thousand still missing although remains have continued to be found two years after . Twenty-one leaders , including from powerful nations like the United States , Russia , Japan , and China , will come to the Philippines to tackle economic issues in the Asia-Pacific region . <p> At the same time , Constantino asked if the Philippines has already allocated money for the Paris talks and for the actual mitigation and adaptation efforts against climate disruption . <p> Yolanda has been partly blamed on the changing climate patterns due to global warming , and the Philippines sits right smack on the corridor of typhoons from the Pacific Ocean and usually the first to bear the brunt of these destructive weather systems . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in the APEC hosting , noting the man-made disruptions of cancelled flights , lost incomes due to cancelled workdays , lost school days , heavy traffic due to road closures and rerouting , as well as displaced urban-poor communities and suppression of right to protest .
