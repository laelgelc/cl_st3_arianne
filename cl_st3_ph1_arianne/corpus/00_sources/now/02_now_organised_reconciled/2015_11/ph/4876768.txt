( photo by Gil Nartea , Malaca ? ? ? ? ang Photo Bureau ) <p> MANILA , Philippines -- Speaking at the leaders ' event of the Paris climate talks , President Benigno Aquino III once again emphasized the vulnerability of the Philippines , which " bear(s) a disproportionate amount of the burden when it comes to climate change . " <p> And yet , his brief speech at the Conference of Parties ( COP 21 ) leaders ' event disregarded the elephant in the room : when will the Philippines stop investing in coal and shift to renewable energy ? <p> Aquino said that for countries like the Philippines , the fight against climate change is a fight for survival . <p> He also mentioned the Philippines ' commitment to mitigate 70 percent of its carbon emissions based on a business as usual ( BAU ) model by 2030 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ fiscal limitations and despite the fact that the Philippines has one of the smallest carbon footprints . <p> " We work toward vital reforms to mitigate climate change , " he said . <p> Aquino also attended the Climate Vulnerable Forum ( CVF ) , an international partnership of 20 countries that are highly vulnerable to global warming , of which the Philippines is the current chair . The other countries are : Afghanistan , Bangladesh , Barbados , Bhutan , Costa Rica , Ethiopia , Ghana , Kenya , Kiribati , Madagascar , Maldives , Nepal , Rwanda , Saint Lucia , Tanzania , Timor-Leste , Tuvalu , Vanuatu , and Vietnam , <p> At the CVF , Aquino said that " building back better has become less of guarantee because the new normal can be replaced with another new normal that is more destructive . " <p> In both events , Aquino outlined the Philippine plans on how it plans to reach its mitigation plans and contribute to solving the climate crisis . He emphasized on planting 1.5 million trees on 1.5 million hectares and establishing anti-illegal logging campaign @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that the Philippine government has approved the building of more than 50 coal power plants this year , despite opposition from different environmental groups and communities where these plants are set to be built . <p> Fossil fuels like coal are the main driver of human-induced climate change . Other countries have started committing to phasing out fossil fuels . The United Kingdom , for example , has set a deadline to phase out coal over the next decade . The Cook Islands have also committed to becoming 100 percent powered by renewable energy by 2030 . <p> Aquino mentioned that the Philippines is increasing the share of renewables in the energy mix to 33 percent but this remains to be of little progress and of little significance given that the country already has a 30 percent share of renewable energy to begin with . <p> The Philippines is a vulnerable country to climate change and yet it is a country that continues to invest so much in coal . While Philippine commitments to the United Nations seem ambitious , the commitments remain to be just that : good on paper @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of coal power plants in the Philippines shows how much or , in this case , how little , the government truly wants to commit to sustainable development . The Philippines must not use its low carbon footprint as an excuse to justify its investment in coal power plants . <p> Without clear commitments from the government that it will stop investing in coal , the Philippines is treading a dangerous path -- the same path taken by rich countries that led us to where we are today . <p> As a vulnerable country to climate change , the Philippines must lead in this energy revolution . Or else there might come a time when we are hit by a typhoon even stronger than Yolanda ( Haiyan ) and there will be no one else to blame but itself .
