<p> MPA says the Maritime Energy Test Bed will support the maritime industry by " developing cleaner and more sustainable maritime energy solutions . " <p> The Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore ( MPA ) says it has partnered with the Nanyang Technological University ( NTU ) , Singapore Maritime Institute ( SMI ) , and other industry partners to launch an advanced maritime energy test facility , intended for the development of " innovative eco-friendly maritime technologies . " <p> Known as the Maritime Energy Test Bed , the facility is said to be intended to support the maritime industry in the context of stricter environmental regulations by " developing cleaner and more sustainable maritime energy solutions " in order to create more cost-efficient and competitive fleets . <p> The S$8 million ( $5.73 million ) facility , is reported to be jointly funded by SMI , which will contribute S$4.7 million ( $3.37 million ) over the next 10 years , and NTU , which is providing S$3.4 million ( $2.43 million ) , along with support from industry leaders such as Nippon Kaiji Kyokai @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Maritime Energy Test Bed represents a successful collaboration between the research sector and the industry , and is a timely development as the maritime sector looks to green and sustainable technologies to save on costs , mitigate the effects of climate change and address growing environmental regulations , " said Andrew Tan , CEO of the MPA . <p> " <p> This new facility puts NTU at the forefront of global maritime energy research . <p> Lam Khin Yong , Chief of Staff and Vice President for Research , NTU <p> The Maritime Energy Test Bed is said to be intended to conduct research and development for alternative fuels and energy sources , efficiency boosting fuel additives , as well as other technologies for cleaner and fewer emissions . <p> " The maritime industry is a crucial economic pillar carrying about 90 percent of the world 's trade .
