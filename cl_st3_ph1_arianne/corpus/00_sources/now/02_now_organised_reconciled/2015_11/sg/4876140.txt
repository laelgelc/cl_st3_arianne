<h> Newstream <h> World <h> U.N . chief aims to build defenses of poor against climate change <p> PARIS ( Thomson Reuters Foundation ) - U.N . Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon warned that climate-related disasters are taking an ever greater toll on the poor and can wipe out decades of development gains , as he launched a global partnership to boost the resilience of vulnerable communities . <p> The impacts of climate change - from intensifying droughts and floods to sea-level rise - are strengthening , as ecosystems and food and water supplies come under increasing pressure , he said . <p> Economic losses from disasters have increased by more than 50 percent over the past decade , he noted . <p> The hardest-hit are poor people , including small-scale farmers , fishing communities and indigenous people , he added . <p> " As climate impacts increase , we will see greater disruption to economies and international security , " he told an event on the sidelines of U.N . climate talks in Paris . <p> " It is imperative that we strengthen resilience and build adaptive capacity , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and most African nations , " he added . <p> His new A2R initiative will equip countries to better anticipate hazards , absorb shocks like extreme weather , and reshape their development to reduce climate risks , the United Nations said . <p> It will bring together businesses , such as insurance companies , governments , U.N . agencies , research institutions and others to mobilize financing and knowledge , coordinate their activities and develop new tools . <p> Freundel Stuart , the prime minister of Barbados , said his island country needed access to low-cost financing , or it " ca n't strengthen resilience to climate change " . <p> The Netherlands , a low-lying nation with much experience of defending itself against flooding , said it would contribute an additional 50 million euros ( $52.8 million ) to a program to boost resilience in developing countries .
