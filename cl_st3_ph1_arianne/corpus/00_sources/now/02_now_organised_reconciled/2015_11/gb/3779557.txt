<p> Thomas Piketty has called for investors to move their money out of fossil fuels ahead of landmark UN climate change talks . <p> The French economist , along with ' ecological economist ' Tim Jackson , authors of the respective bestselling books Capital in the Twenty-First Century and Prosperity Without Growth : economics for a finite planet , said that investors should divest from a sector with a business model " at odds with physical realities " . <p> In a letter to the Guardian , they wrote : " This is a rare and decisive moment in history . Science , ethics , and economics are intersecting to form a clear market signal : in the lead up to the COP21 climate talks , responsible investors should divest from fossil fuels . " <p> The economists warned that public wellbeing was at risk from continued investment in oil , coal and gas companies whose products are driving global warming . <p> " Set against a backdrop of record- ? breaking climatic extremes and weather events , capital continues to flow into the exploration and future extraction @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ future in which vast potentials of carbon reserves are available to be processed -- a bet against the public 's well ? being . " <p> In the letter Piketty and Jackson cite the growth of a global divestment movement which has now persuaded more than 400 institutions and 2,000 individuals worth $2.6tn to divest from oil , coal and gas companies . <p> They add : " At a time when the fossil fuel industry should be shifting their businesses to focus on renewable energy they are doing the opposite , doubling down on coal , oil and gas ... Meanwhile , clean , carbon- ? free energy is rapidly becoming cost ? -competitive with dirty energy .
