<h> DNV GL to study controlled release of CO2 from underwater pipeline <p> 24 November 2015 <p> DNV GL is set to perform the biggest controlled release of carbon dioxide ( CO2 ) from an underwater pipeline at its Spadeadam Testing and Research Centre in Cumbria , UK , to understand the environmental and safety implications . <p> The release is part of an international joint industry project ( JIP ) called Sub-C-O2 aimed at understanding the environmental and safety implications associated with the development of CO2 pipelines . It will begin in January 2016 . <p> Norway 's Gassnova , Brazil 's Petrobras , the UK Government 's Department of Energy and Climate Change , the UK 's National Grid and DNV GL are participating in the JIP , with Italy-based ENI expected to join in early 2016 . <p> According to the company , this is the second experimental phase and will continue for three months involving releases in a 40m diameter , 12m deep pond at the centre . <p> The first phase of experiments are underway and involve small-scale , controlled CO2 releases from @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 3m deep water tank . These experiments are expected to be completed by December . <p> DNV GL Spadeadam safety and risk vice-president Gary Tomlin said : " This is the largest experimental investigation to date of underwater CO2 releases , which will study the effects of depth on measured and observed parameters . <p> " The testing is designed around what is already known about underwater natural gas ( methane ) leaks and the possible occurrence of CO2 hydrates collecting on pipework . <p> " By using high-speed , underwater cameras and other measurement techniques , we can examine the configuration and characteristics of the released gas . It will allow us to see whether it reaches the surface and analyse what happens .
