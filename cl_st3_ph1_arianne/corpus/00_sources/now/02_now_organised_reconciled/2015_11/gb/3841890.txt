<p> What we know of the EU trade deals with Canada ( Ceta -- Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement ) and the US ( TTIP -- Transatlantic Trade and Investment Partnership ) points to significant increases in European fossil fuel imports from North America , and a restriction of the policy space to promote low-carbon economies and renewables . <p> Trade deals are particularly bad news when it comes to food and climate change . The global food system is already responsible for around half of global greenhouse gas emissions . The World Bank is projecting that emissions from agriculture and food could account for as much as 70% ( pdf ) of total allowable emissions to keep below a 2C temperature rise by 2050 , if governments continue to ignore the problem . <p> If we are going to deal with climate change , we have to overhaul the global food system . But a new generation of trade deals will amplify emissions from the food system . <h> Trade deals and emissions <p> At a most basic level , trade deals are designed to open up @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ meat and dairy . For example , TPP will boost US pork and beef exports to Asia and US dairy exports to Canada . TTIP is expected to open Europe 's border to more US beef . Australia 's dairy exports to China , up 300% in the past eight years , are a big reason why the two countries just signed a trade pact . The flow of cheap imports resulting from these deals will play a part in increasing global consumption of meats by 76% by 2050 . <p> Trade agreements also favour food production from intensive farms and large-scale plantations . When China joined the World Trade Organisation and opened its market to soybean imports the result was a dramatic expansion of soybean plantations in the forests and savannahs of the southern cone of Latin America and a corresponding rise in intensive pig production in China , fed on the imported beans . <p> New trade deals will likely do the same for maize imports .
