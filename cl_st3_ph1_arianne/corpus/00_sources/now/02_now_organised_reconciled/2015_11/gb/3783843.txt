<p> We Homo sapiens got lucky . Very lucky . Back in the 1920s , when looking for a " safe " gas to use in refrigerators , chlorine was the element of choice in a new family of manmade chemical compounds -- chlorofluorocarbons . In the 1970s , Paul Crutzen , Mario Molina and Sherwood Rowland discovered that while it was safe in our fridges , it was destroying the ozone layer , which is essential to protect all life on land . <p> But here luck comes in threes . Bromine is as good as chlorine for fridges and air conditioners , but about 40 times more corrosive to ozone . And by mere chance , the industry chose chlorine as the global standard . If this had not happened , the ozone layer could have been ripped apart before we even knew it .
