<h> Is the end of AIDS in sight ? 10 facts about HIV/AIDS ahead of World AIDS Day <p> Nov 30 ( Thomson Reuters Foundation ) - Despite major advances , HIV/AIDS remains one of the world 's most significant public health challenges , particularly in low and middle income countries , with new diagnoses every year and young women in sub-Saharan Africa seen as being particularly at risk . <p> World AIDS Day on Dec. 1 is used to unite people in the fight against HIV , the human immunodeficiency virus first identified in 1984 , to show their support for people living with HIV and commemorate those who have died . <p> The member countries of the United Nations agreed in September in a new set of global goals to end the AIDS epidemic by 2030 . <p> Here are some facts about AIDS in 2015 with data from the World Health Organisation , the United Nations children 's agency UNICEF , and UNAIDS : <p> 1 . Globally about 36.9 million people are living with HIV including 2.6 million children <p> 2 . The number of adolescent deaths from AIDS has tripled over the last 15 years <p> 5 . AIDS is the number one cause of death among adolescents in Africa and the second among adolescents globally <p> 6 . In sub-Saharan Africa , the region with the highest prevalence , girls account for 7 in 10 new infections among those aged 15-19 <p> 7 . At start of 2015 , 15 million people were receiving antiretroviral therapy compared to 1 million in 2001 <p> 8 . Despite widespread availability of HIV testing , only an estimated 51 percent of people with HIV know their status <p> 9 . The global response to HIV has averted 30 million new HIV infections and nearly 8 million deaths since 2000 <p> 10 .
