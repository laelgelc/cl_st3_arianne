<p> Campaign promises often fall by the wayside once politicians make it to office , but not for Canadian Prime Minister Justin Trudeau . Less than a month after his election , Canada 's head of state has put his pledge to ban oil tanker traffic into motion . <p> In a letter published Friday , Trudeau asked his Minister of Transportation to " formalize a moratorium on crude oil tanker traffic on British Columbia 's North Coast , " the Calgary Heraldreports . <p> Such a ban could potentially shut down the controversial Northern Gateway pipeline , in which crude oil from Alberta would be piped north to British Columbia and then shipped overseas . A coalition of environmental groups and aboriginal advocates applauded Trudeau 's decision . <p> " This ban ends the dangerous Northern Gateway pipeline proposal , " said Karen Mahon , advocacy director for environmental group ForestEthics . " Without tankers , crude oil has no place to go , and that means no pipelines , no oil trains moving tar sands to the northern B.C. coast .
