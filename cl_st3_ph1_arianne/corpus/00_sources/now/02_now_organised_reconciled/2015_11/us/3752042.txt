<p> One in 4 global corporations are already seeing water issues affect their business . The situation is only getting worse . <p> Before the world runs short of energy or food , it 's likely to run out of usable water . The U.N . says more than 1 billion people live in water-stressed areas , and every forecast shows the situation getting worse because of climate change and population growth . NASA said this year that 21 of the world 's most important aquifers have already passed replacement point : more water is being taken out than is going back in . <p> Such numbers are n't lost on the world 's largest companies , many of which rely on water to operate . According to a new survey from CDP , a London nonprofit , 27% of global corporations are seeing " detrimental effects " because of water-related issues . In 2015 , these impacts had a reported value of $2.5 billion . <p> For example , consumer products giant Unilever says the long-term drought in Brazil will create a " new normal " for @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ access to water is likely to result in a change of behavior such as reduced showering and clothes washing . And more than half of those have n't done a " comprehensive risk assessment " to understand where the water risks exist . Only 22% of publicly listed oil and gas companies responded to the CDP , even though that sector is often threatened : two-thirds of those reporting say they 're vulnerable to " substantive water risks .
