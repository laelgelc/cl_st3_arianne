<h> Aging U.S. Nuclear Plants Pushing Limits of Life Expectancy <p> The U.S. is set to become the first nation to decide whether it 's safe to operate nuclear power plants for 80 years , twice as long as initially allowed . <p> The majority of the nation 's 99 reactors have already received 20-year extensions to their original 40-year operating licenses . Now , operators led by Dominion Resources Inc. want to expand the time frame further , potentially creating a precedent for an aging global fleet at a time when the economics of the industry are undergoing dramatic change . <p> Dominion said earlier this month it will request an extension from the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission , which oversees the industry . The plan has already raised the ire of anti-nuclear campaigners who cite decades of wear and tear on the nation 's reactors , as well as the 2011 Fukushima disaster in Japan . <p> The NRC will release a draft report next month outlining safety measures needed to extend the time line . <p> " The reality of life is the risks go @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ director of the nuclear safety project at the Union of Concerned Scientists , a Cambridge , Massachusetts- based advocacy group . If allowed , Dominion 's Surry plant in Virginia would be the first to outlive the average human being in the U.S. with a lifespan of 78.8 years .
