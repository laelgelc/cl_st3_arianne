<p> JOHANNESBURG - As global leaders prepare for the United Nations Climate Change Conference , the South African Institute for International Affairs ( SAIIA ) at Wits University say South Africans are still struggling to understand how changing environmental conditions will affect them . <p> South Africa will play an active role at the COP21 gathering , acting as an advocate for other developing countries during talks around climate change . <p> However , the institute says many are unaware of what impact the country 's involvement in negotiations will have on them individually . <p> The institute 's Agathe Maupin says many drought stricken farmers have questions about what government can do to help them . <p> " If I really experience a couple of droughts in a row , what is my government going to do for me ? I think that 's what a South African farmer would be interested in. "
