<h> Greenpeace expects big action from the big economies at the Climate Summit <p> On this page <p> Press release - November 30 , 2015 <p> Johannesburg , 30 November , 2015 - As world leaders set out their response to climate change today in Paris , Greenpeace is calling on the leaders of the G7 and the BRICS countries to do more to transform their energy systems , and to switch to 100 percent clean renewable energy by 2050 . Their leaders ' fine words at the COP must be matched by action at home so global warming is limited to 1.5 Celsius or below . This is a matter of survival for millions of people . <p> The member countries of both the G7 and the BRICS ( Brazil , Russia , India , China and South Africa ) , together responsible for some 65% of global carbon emissions , have huge renewable energy potential . The ultimate question is why is the South African government not scaling up their ambition to tackle the twin crisis of water scarcity and climate change ? South Africa has some of the best renewable energy resources in the world and renewable energy technologies are able to deliver sustainable electricity , while at the same time substantially reducing the stress on South Africa 's water resources . What the current water crisis has made clear is that South Africa simply can not continue investing in coal , nuclear or any other unsustainable energy source in the face of extreme weather , brought on by climate change .
