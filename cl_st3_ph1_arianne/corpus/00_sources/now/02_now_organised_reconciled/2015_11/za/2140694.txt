<h> SA fails to meet water challenges <p> Johannesburg - A country like South Africa , where rainfall is variable and unpredictable , has to plan for the worst . Specifically , it has to plan to manage the impacts of drought . For cities , present practice is to store enough water to supply the needs of each region during the worst conditions likely to occur in 50 years . <p> To meet that target for the growing population of Gauteng , the country 's economic hub , a new dam must be built . The site has been chosen , the Polihali Dam , identified as Phase 2 of the Lesotho Highlands Water Project . <p> As so often in South Africa , the problem is not the plan but the delays in its implementation . According to the original planning , the dam should be completed by 2018 . Given slow progress , my understanding is that it is now not possible for it to deliver water before 2024 . <p> So , for at least six years , there is a risk that the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the country 's GDP , will not be met reliably . <p> It only took a few days of hot weather in Johannesburg for two things to happen : suburban reservoirs ran dry , and residents panicked . City authorities introduced restrictions on watering gardens and washing cars , and residents reacted as though this had never happened before , which in fact it had . <p> The extreme weather can serve a useful purpose : it should focus the attention of people and policy makers on the deadline to build the new dam . If no action is taken , policy makers will have only themselves to blame when failure of another vital service disrupts people 's lives , and the economy . <p> To understand why the Lesotho Project is so important , it is necessary to understand how droughts affect the country and what should be done about planning for water security in the face of drought . <p> Agricultural versus hydrological droughts <p> There is a difference between an agricultural drought and a hydrological drought . <p> Agricultural drought occurs when limited rainfall reduces soil moisture @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ five provinces . Hydrological drought is where a reduction in rainfall reduces the amount of water that flows into rivers and streams and into storage in dams and underground . <p> For farmers , three months of dry weather will ruin a year 's rainfed crops . While perhaps half of the country is already affected by an agricultural drought , it is not yet suffering a hydrological drought -- although one may have begun . <p> The role of El Ni ? o <p> Hot dry Novembers are not unusual in South Africa , although this year has been on the extreme side . In Johannesburg , temperatures reached 36 , the hottest in 30 years . Similarly , the temperature at the Irene weather station in Tshwane north of Johannesburg was 36.6 , the highest in 40 years . <p> The current weather was predicted some months ago by the South African Weather Service . Its June Seasonal Climate forecast reported that an " El Ni ? o " phenomenon was developing in the Pacific Ocean . It warned that El Ni ? o was associated with reduced rainfall @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ El Ninos of similar intensity happened in the 1981-82 and 1997-98 seasons . <p> In South Africa , the first was associated with a crippling drought that saw widespread crop failures and severe water use restrictions across much of the inland areas . The second caused significant crop losses but far less impact on water supplies . <p> So the occurrence of an El Ni ? o does not necessarily mean that there will be a major drought this time . It serves as a wake-up call to water managers and the community at large about the need to be prepared . And to speed up the construction of that damn . <p> The other weather pattern worth mentioning <p> South Africa potentially faces a very different set of weather challenges -- massive floods -- driven by a phenomenon with a much less catchy name : the Indian Ocean Dipole . It is a measure of temperature differences in the Indian Ocean and is considered to be a good predictor of monsoon rains in India as well as of cyclones in the south west Indian Ocean . <p> Those cyclones are @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Africa 's most extreme rainfall . They usually take place in the first few months of the year . <p> It 's happened before . Cyclone Domoina caused devastation in KwaZulu-Natal in 1984 and in 2000 when cyclone Eline filled the dams of Limpopo and Mpumalanga . <p> Even if the full force of such storms does n't reach the coast , it often causes rainfall as far inland as Gauteng . It was Eline that saw 500mm of rain fall in Levubu , Limpopo province in just three days and caused devastating floods in southern Mozambique . <p> So while El Ni ? o spells dry weather , fluctuations in the Indian Ocean Dipole may signal more tropical storms . In that case we may see floods as well as drought . <p> Pressures at the local level <p> Given the electricity shortages in the country and what euphemistically became known as load-shedding , many people have started to assume that long feared water-shedding had begun . The commentators who sought a more strategic view talked about an intense drought and the impacts of climate change . In Johannesburg 46 percent of household water supply is used for swimming pools and watering gardens . <p> If they do n't want to do this , municipalities have to improve their ability to persuade their citizens to use less water during times of stress .
