<h> News <h> Politicking may threaten outcome of Paris talks -- IPCC <p> TWO weeks before world leaders meet in Paris to hash out an agreement aimed at keeping global temperatures from rising beyond the point at which life can be sustained , climate scientists remain largely doubtful that negotiations will materialise into a plan that reflects their projections . <p> " We 've known for a long time that it would n't be legally binding and we 've known for a long time that it certainly would n't be below 1.5 degrees , " communications consultant with the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) Lance Ignon said in response to a question from the Jamaica Observer about the expectations from Paris . <p> Occasion was a media training event at University of Miami 's Rosenstiel School of Marine and Atmospheric Science Thursday . It was accompanied by a larger outreach on Friday designed to engage the public on climate change and the options for preventing and adapting to it , and to expose them to the IPCC process for assessing the science . <p> The @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 21st meeting of the Conference of Parties to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change . It 's considered especially critical because , according to scientists , the amount of carbon emissions already concentrated in the atmosphere is believed to be between 400 and 403 parts per million ( ppm ) . Projections are that at 450 ppm , the climate will warm by 2.6 degrees Celsuis -- which they refer to as the tipping point : a potentially irreversible point at which the climate will barrel untamed from one stable state to another . <p> At that point , according to IPCC author and professor of Tropical Island Ecology at the University of the West Indies , St Augustine , John Agard , " we will not be able to adapt to the consequences of what is unfolding " . As far as small island states are concerned , it will spell disaster for their populations , which largely live along the coast , and on their economies , which depend heavily on tourism . <p> " The evidence is overwhelming that something grave is afoot and island economies @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ This is going to affect people ... People are going to lose their countries and lose large portions of their territories , " professor Agard said in a presentation Thursday . " This is serious business ; the small islands are hanging on for dear life . " <p> The developed world has largely settled on capping warming at two degrees , but the push from scientists and small islands is to push emissions back to 350 ppm and keep global temperature increase for the rest of the century at or below 1.5 degrees . <p> But even as they approach Paris hoping for an agreement that will legally bind States to emission reductions , indications are that things may not go their way .
