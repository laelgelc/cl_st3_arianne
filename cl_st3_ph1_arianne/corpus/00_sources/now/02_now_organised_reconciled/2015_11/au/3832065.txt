<h> Disasters cause $110b agriculture damage <p> Natural disasters that struck developing countries over the past decade have caused at least $US80 billion ( $A110.38 billion ) worth of damage to agriculture , the United Nations Food and Agriculture Organisation ( FAO ) says . <p> The estimate comes from a 53-page report , The Impact of Disasters on Agriculture and Food Security , covering the 2003-2013 period and released in the run up to UN climate talks in Paris , starting on Monday . <p> " These production losses correspond to 333 million tonnes of cereals , pulses , meat , milk and other commodities , " shrinking the available calorie intake for people in developing countries by an average of seven per cent after each disaster , FAO said . <p> Asia took the biggest hit in absolute terms , with $US48 billion worth of losses , but worth only two per cent of total crop and livestock production . Africa suffered more in relative terms , with a $US14-billion loss representing six per cent of the total . <p> The Rome-based agency noted that compared @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ doubled during 2003-2013 , and more than a third of developing countries had suffered three or more extreme weather events over the period . <p> The Philippines was cited as having experienced 75 disasters between 2006 and 2013 - mostly typhoons , tropical storms and floods - which caused accumulated damage and losses to the agricultural sector of $US3.8 billion . <p> FAO 's report studied 78 natural disasters in 48 countries in Africa , Asia-Pacific , Latin America and the Caribbean . Among its recommendations was a call to earmark a greater share of development aid to help farmers in poor nations adapt to climate change .
