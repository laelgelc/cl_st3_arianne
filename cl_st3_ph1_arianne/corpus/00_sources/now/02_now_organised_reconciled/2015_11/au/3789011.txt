<h> Julian Cribb <p> National governments no longer have funds with which to govern . Photo : Andrew Meares <p> The 21st century will change much that people now take for granted . Among the more shocking possibilities is that it will ring down the curtain on the nation state as the primary instrument of human governance . <p> The nation state , established in Europe post-Napoleon , is now so firmly ingrained in our identities , customs and beliefs that many people are unable to imagine a world in which this edifice of self-organisation might disappear , or at least fade into insignificance -- like feudalism , monarchy , the ancient imperial systems , or the priest states before them . <p> Certain currents are now established in the stream of history whose confluence is threatening the nation as an entity . Not just in Australia , but everywhere . And it is time we talked about the possibility rather than assume " no change " . <p> The first is the rise and rise of transnational businesses , whose individual economic might now exceeds that of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ they are nearly all autocracies despite the occasional shareholder meeting -- now dominate more than half the world economy and do pretty much as they choose . They buy and sell politicians . They bend , break and change the laws more or less at will . And they pay only a fraction of their fair share of taxes . <p> Advertisement <p> This latter impact especially is slowly strangling national governments : they no longer have funds with which to govern . Or , at least , to do the things for their society which it demands or they 'd like to do for it . Their tax base is quietly withering as economics becomes more global . This loss of the fiscal power to govern is rapidly translating into the disillusion and disgust which many citizens feel for the politicians and governments who overpromise and constantly fail to live up to our expectations . As a result people are deserting political parties globally and fewer turn up to vote . <p> Second , there is the defiance of regulation . National governments can no longer control these giant businesses @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the chemical industry , which is quite strictly regulated in Western countries and which , rather than relinquish its profitable poisoning of our planet , is fleeing headlong into Asia where regulation is weak and officialdom often corrupt . National governments are powerless to stop its emissions from returning to toxify their own citizens . And if national governments can no longer enforce their laws to protect their citizens , then they are no longer governments by definition . <p> A third reason that the nation state is in trouble is the rise of social media . Yes , social media , that font of trivia and vapid opinion is rapidly becoming more potent as a determinant of political outcomes than the creaking " power centres " . Social media was closely implicated in the overthrow of oppressive regimes in Egypt and Tunisia in 2012 . Its lightning-fast reflexes ( literally -- it operates at light speed ) have foiled all sorts of dinosaurian government plans in countries round the world . In Australia it leapt to the defence of the Great Barrier Reef , mobilised a " peasants ' revolt @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , and rumbled the goofy Border Force plan to hunt down " illegals " on our streets . <p> There are countless examples , but the fact is that social media can respond with a freedom , flexibility , immediacy and agility that is simply beyond lumbering autocrats . Social media has the power to build and change public consensus on a raft of issues in a way that leaves governments stranded like proverbial shags on a rock . Nobody would claim it is carefully thought through , but at least it 's fairly democratic -- something fewer governments nowadays can truly claim . <p> A fourth reason why governments are failing is their impotence . So many issues today are global in nature or context , and individual governments -- even great federations like the EU -- are relatively powerless to control or influence them . Decisions about what to do about the atmosphere , the oceans , famines , refugee crises , the world economy etc are being taken by global consensus and through global institutions , emasculating the decision-making powers of national governments . Instead it draws in a class of bottom feeders , adept at every trick to gain and keep power , and who reward themselves and their mates with vast entitlements , allowances , perks and pensions . The concept of " public service " lapses into history as the administrative arm of government becomes politicised and subservient . This in turn accelerates the spiralling loss of credibility , power and popular support for national institutions . <p> A strident fifth reason nation states are crumbling is refugeeism . When the UNHCR was set up after World War II it had 1 million people to deal with . Today 60 million are displaced by war , famine , political and religious persecution -- and far more still are fleeing for economic opportunity elsewhere or the fear of impending disaster as their home state totters . <p> By mid-century , with climate-induced famines striking around the world and the outbreak of resulting conflicts , the displaced population @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ crises in northern China , north India and north Africa will unleash refugee tsunamis in all directions . At this point , populist measures such as Donald Trump 's Mexican border fence and Tony Abbott 's " stop the boats " are exposed as Canute-like fantasies . Borders become meaningless against such tidal population shifts . <p> If Africa erupts , then the nations of Greece , Italy and Spain will simply be swept away . If food and water supplies on the Indo-Gangetic or North China plains fail , Australia and our region will face an immigrant tide in the tens of millions , utterly transforming our society . Today 's refugee " crisis " is a mere foreshock of what the combined forces of resource scarcity , eco-collapse , peak people , climate change and inter-nation warfare will bring . <p> Faced with loss of wealth , loss of power , public disillusion and increasingly porous borders , the nation state is an entity fast approaching its use-by date . Something constructed on essentially 19th century Bismarckian ? principles is hardly up to the task of coping with a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , information and pollution -- flows globally . <p> For all their flags , anthems and patriotic bluster , nations have basically been a poor idea . Since the 1850s they 've slaughtered about 200 million humans -- mostly civilians -- in their wars . More , even , than religions . It was n't the people who started these wars -- it was their governments . Often in the face of majority citizen opposition . If we aspire to world peace in the Age of Peak People , and wish to avoid global nuclear conflict , we first need to replace the structures which lead to war . <p> The 21st century will see the citizens of the world coming together , peacefully , to order their affairs by mutual agreement , treaties , and consensual politics rather than the failing party systems of yore . The big decisions will be taken globally -- and locally , leaving a vacuum for the national tier of government caught in between . I have my own thoughts about how this might come about , but they are unimportant . The purpose of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ debate about what we -- the human species -- should do , as and when our national system falters and fails in the mid and late century . <p> It is only through timely global discourse that we can solve these problems and build the future . <p> Julian Cribb is a Canberra science writer and author .
