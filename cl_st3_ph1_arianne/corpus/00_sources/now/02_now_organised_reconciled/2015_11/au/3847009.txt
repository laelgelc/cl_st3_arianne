<p> Innovation means changing the way you do things . If you 're in business , it might mean producing a new product or service ; introducing a new technology or operational process ; instigating a new business model ; or just changing the way you market your products . It 's a sustainable way to raise productivity and enjoy a higher standard of living . <p> To understand why innovation is important , it 's helpful to think of a world without it . Imagine we 're back in 18th-century Europe and someone 's decided to stop all new inventions , or ban the conversion of ideas into goods and services . We 'd have no electricity , no water piped to houses , and no antibiotics . <p> Without the discovery of superphosphate and new seed varieties , we 'd only be able to feed , say , one billion people out of the current seven . Half our children would die before the age of five . It takes an amalgam of policies for a country to push ahead where others languish . <h> Lessons from abroad <p> Policies aimed at fostering innovation in the United States , Germany , Sweden and Switzerland -- to name just a few nations that do innovation well -- have been strategic and focused . In the US , for instance , government policies since World War II have pushed and pulled businesses and universities , often with a heavy hand , to interact with each other . <p> Programs via the defence , energy and health departments have created networks that identify and absorb external information ; interpret its meaning ; and then support the actions of its members by providing specialised intermediate services . This environment has substantially lowered the risks associated with innovation . <p> To become countries that successfully develop and commercialise ideas , these nations have used intellectual property laws and other ways to capture profits to ensure innovating is worthwhile . <p> And they do n't just rely on home-grown brains .
