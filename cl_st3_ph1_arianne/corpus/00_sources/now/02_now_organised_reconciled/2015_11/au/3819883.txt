<p> Coal still generates 40 per cent of American electricity . But 10 years ago , it was 50 per cent . <p> Construction of new coal-fired power plants has effectively ended in the US . A third of the plants that are still running have been slated to shut down . And they will not be replaced -- at least , not by more coal plants . <p> In the lead up to the Paris climate talks , it is tempting to look at the dramatic change in America 's coal industry through the lens of concern about global warming . <p> But that would be highly misleading . <p> While US president Barack Obama has repeatedly called climate change the greatest threat to the planet 's future , US opinion polls repeatedly show that while the American public is coming around to the idea that global warming is real , they do not regard it as a priority . <p> So why has the campaign to close down coal-fired power stations had such a dramatic impact ? <p> Because all politics is local . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " Sierra Club 's Beyond Coal campaign director Mary Anne Hitt said . <p> " Congress and the president have a lot of influence over policy .
