<h> Crispin Hull <p> Yes , there is a trickle down effect in the field of tax . Not in the Ronald Reagan sense , whereby tax breaks for the rich allegedly result in more employment . That effect was often likened to a trickle down from the bladder , whereby those at the bottom got worse than nothing . <p> Rather , this week , the CSIRO revealed a different trickle down effect when it comes to opinion . The CSIRO has been mapping attitudes to climate change for more than five years . It has found a remarkable correlation between attitudes to it and voting intention . <p> People may say they make up their own minds on issues , but on climate change the CSIRO work shows that a great many people follow the views of the leader of the political party they support . <p> Only one in four Coalition voters acknowledge human-induced climate change , as against 62 per cent of the population at large and an even higher percentage of non-Coalition voters . <p> Advertisement <p> The CSIRO report said : " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , despite the strong associations that opinions had with voting behaviour .
