<h> Birds in ' Goldilocks ' habitats have best chance against global warming <p> Birds living in a " Goldilocks " habitat where conditions are neither too harsh nor too comfortable have the best chance of adapting to global warming , research has shown . <p> The study looked at the ability of 12 European bird species to evolve and survive as the climate around them changed . <p> ( Andrew Milligan/PA ) <p> Scientists found that pampered populations living in the most favourable surroundings , usually at the centre of their species range , had poor " evolutionary potential " . <p> The same was true of species struggling to cope with the toughest conditions right at the edge of their range . <p> Birds that stood the best chance of adjusting to climate change lived between the two extremes . <p> Here , like the porridge enjoyed by Goldilocks that was neither too hot nor too cold but " just right " , conditions were ideal for evolutionary change . <p> ( Owen Humphreys/PA ) <p> Dr Regan Early , from the University of Exeter 's Penryn campus @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ find reduced evolutionary potential among birds living in the centre of a species ' range . <p> " The reasons for this are not clear , but high levels of competition in prime areas might lead birds with certain traits to survive -- meaning little genetic variety in the population and consequently little scope for evolution . " <p> Spanish lead author Dr Jesus Martinez-Padilla , from the University of Oviedo , said birds already struggling to survive at the edge of their species range would probably be killed off by climate change unless they moved . <p> ( Owen Humphreys/PA ) <p> " This is what might happen to populations of the Pied Flycatcher in southern Europe , " he said . <p> " These populations have little genetic variation compared to northern populations , so they wo n't be able to adapt to a changing climate . <p> " The birds living in places ( with ) neither the best nor the most hostile environmental conditions appear to have the best evolutionary potential . " <p> The research is published in the journal Proceedings of the Royal Society B.
