<h> eHealth project head says new digital system will result in better outcomes for patients <p> Electronic health records are to be rolled out for everyone availing of Irish healthcare services and will ensure better outcomes for patients , the chairman of the Government 's " eHealth " project , Prof Mark Ferguson , has said . <p> He said the project was going ahead in spite of concerns about personal information being stored digitally . <p> Prof Ferguson said the reality was that " electronic health in Ireland is not in a good place " but the changes being introduced would lead to better quality of GP and hospital care , and a seamless transition from one system of care to the other . <p> Under the plans , all people availing of healthcare would have a unique identifier and all their records would be stored digitally and there would be the capacity to carry out " genome sequencing " to chart their genetic make-up where appropriate , added Prof Ferguson , who is also the Government 's chief scientific adviser and head of Science Foundation Ireland . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , he said he appreciated eHealth was a sensitive area which had people " really worried " . <p> In contrast , he noted they did not give a damn about " a paper of record being lost in the back of a taxi " or paper records being kept in an unlocked room . <p> As a consequence , eHealth Ireland was engaging in a series of pilot projects to reassure the public and to demonstrate the advantages for them . <p> In one such project 20 people with epilepsy had their genotype profiled . In one case , it resulted in a person , who had an epileptic fit every day for 30 years , adopting a dietary change that meant they have never had a fit since . <p> He said the eHealthIreland project set up by the Department of Health had developed seven patient personas , which meant different things had to be delivered for different categories of people , informed by studies of " sickness " and " health " . This strong patient focus would be critical to the procurement process . <h> ' @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ educating people " so they are not frightened about control of their data " . <p> The initiative would also secure better participation in clinical trials and ensure proper consent , Prof Ferguson said at the conference hosted by the Avoca Quality Consortium , which co-ordinates collaboration on clinical trials . <p> Ireland 's approach to scientific research was being vindicated , he said , as it meant the State had become a global leader in chosen fields . This was a consequence of research excellence which was distributed in different places . <p> Through 12 highly-specialised research centres based in third-level institutions and close collaboration with industry , and backed SFI funding , Ireland was among the top five globally in areas such as nanotechnology , animal and dairy , chemistry , immunology and mathematics , he added . <p> Prof Ferguson said SFI was underlining the benefits of Ireland in the context of Brexit , though they were " not actively poaching people " . In addition , SFI was also strengthening relations with major funders of research in Britain , which would be important regardless of the Brexit outcome . <p> He cited the example of " a potential Nobel Prize winner " based in the US , reluctant to pursue climate change research there because of the Trump factor , who might have concerns about going to the UK , but might be persuaded by the joint approach involving Ireland .
