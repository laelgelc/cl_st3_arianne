<p> London : Britain 's Liberal Democrats leader Tim Farron announced his resignation on Wednesday , citing scrutiny of his religious views over gay rights during the recent general election campaign . <p> " To be a political leader ... and to live as a committed Christian , to hold faithfully to the Bible 's teaching , has felt impossible for me , " he said . <p> File image of Tim Farron . AFP <p> Farron , 47 , said he had felt " torn " between political and religious life after facing questioning over whether he agreed with his party 's positive view of same-sex unions . <p> A father of four , Farron was elected leader of the Liberal Democrats in July 2015 after the party , which had been in a coalition with the ruling Conservatives , was nearly wiped out in the general election , losing 48 seats . <p> However , the party created a surprise in last week 's snap election , increasing their number of MPs from nine to 12 , after campaigning for a second referendum on the terms of Brexit @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ headlines very early on the campaign and he was asked to clarify whether he thought being gay was a sin after a 2015 interview resurfaced in which he had given an evasive answer . <p> " At the start of this election , I found myself under scrutiny again ; asked about matters to do with my faith . I felt guilty that this focus was distracting attention from our campaign , obscuring our message , " he said on Wednesday . " I seem to be the subject of suspicion because of what I believe and who my faith is in , " he added . <p> Voting records show that Farron , an evangelical Christian , voted to allow registrars not to carry out gay marriages if they object on religious grounds . <p> He also voted against the Equality Act Regulations , which criminalised many types of discrimination against gay people back in 2007 , opposing his party 's official stance . <p> Former business secretary Vince Cable , Ed Davey , a former energy and climate change secretary and Jo Swinson , are the favourites to replace him .
