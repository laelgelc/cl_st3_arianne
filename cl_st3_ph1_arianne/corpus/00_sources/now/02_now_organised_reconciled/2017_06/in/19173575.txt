( Photo : Reuters ) <p> Erdene Sum , Mongolia : At 6:55 am on Monday , a man in rural Mongolia stood outside a yurt and looked down at his watch . <p> Five minutes remained until presidential election polls opened in the country of just three million wedged between Russia and China . Clad in a bright green traditional " deel " gown , Sharkhuu Gerelt-Od was ready . <p> The 39-year-old public servant was the chief organiser of a mobile polling station in Erdene Sum , a small city situated in Mongolia 's sprawling steppes , where nomadic herders parked their yurts - circular , collapsible tents - and let their livestock graze among the rolling hills . <p> The polling station was set up to accommodate these herders , who move with the changing seasons to find the best pastures for their goats , horses and yaks . <p> " We @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , " said Gerelt-Od . " We have to give them access -- it 's their constitutional right . " Soon he was joined inside the yurt by other polling booth officials , all garbed in colourful traditional outfits . <p> At the centre of the tent was a tall stack of bread carrying curd sweets , cheese pieces and a thick cream . It is Mongolian tradition to offer guests with the best food that one has on hand . Among herding communities , that food is often dairy . <p> " The voters are our guests in this yurt , " Gerelt-Od said with a smile . The first voters , a husband and wife herder pair whose yurt was stationed in the area , arrived wearing a suit and a printed dress . Dressing Up <p> Voting is a special event in the young democracy , which started holding elections in 1990 after decades of Soviet control .
