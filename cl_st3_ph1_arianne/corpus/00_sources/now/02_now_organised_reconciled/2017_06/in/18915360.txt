<h> China-Pakistan 's dam projects in Gilgit-Baltistan should worry India <h> One of the solutions is to renegotiate the Indus Waters Treaty <p> On the occasion of the opening of the One Belt One Road ( OBOR ) forum in Beijing , President Xi Jinping urged countries across the globe to join hands with China in pursuit of globalisation : " We have no intention to form a small group detrimental to stability . What we hope to create is a big family of harmonious co-existence . " <p> India has some doubts about the " big family " . Take the example of the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor ( CPEC ) , an OBOR offshoot ; it crosses Gilgit-Baltistan ( GB ) , considered by India an integral part of Jammu and Kashmir . <p> Not only has China pushed Islamabad to make GB a fifth province of Pakistan , but according to CNN-News18 , " Information and interviews exclusively accessed by CNN-News18 showed that the land was procured mostly by force by Pakistani generals for the CPEC and those resisting are either killed off or incarcerated without a trial @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Thinkers Forum , told the same channel : " Thousands had their land snatched and occupied by the military authorities and their agencies . Though described by WAPDA as a run-of-the-river ( RoR ) project , it is clearly not one , as it will have a 22-km-long reservoir which will inundate 12km of the road between Gilgit and Skardu . <p> The next dam is the Diamer-Basha HEP with a potential of 4,500 MW . A " lost " Saraswati river in the making ? <p> It has now been scientifically proved that big dams are not sustainable for several reasons ; the first one being the amount of silt retained behind the dam , which stops nourishing downstream areas .
