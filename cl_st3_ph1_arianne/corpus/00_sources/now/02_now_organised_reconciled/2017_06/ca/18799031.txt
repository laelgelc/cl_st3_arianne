<p> The expert panel report on federal environmental assessment processes , mandated by the Minister of Environment and Climate Change , was released earlier this spring . In 100-plus pages , the four-person panel sets out a " new vision " for federal reviews of large projects that can affect the environment . This is a big deal for B.C. and for Canada because the scope of the report covers fossil-fuel production , fisheries , forestry , mining , and a host of other key resource sectors that are central to Canada 's self-image and pillars of the Canadian economy . <p> The report 's authors conclude that " ... we need to improve the way we plan for development in our country " and do a better job anticipating the health , social and cultural impacts of proposed resource-development mega-projects . Few would disagree . However , the report offers little in terms of specific ideas about how to achieve this " new vision . Others remain skeptical , because similar messages have been provided to federal decision-makers for years . The 1994 Reference Guide for the Canadian Environmental Assessment Act , for example , requires that evaluations of potential impacts include " any change that the project may cause in the environment , including any effect of any such change on health and socio-economic conditions , on physical and cultural heritage , ( and ) on the current use of lands and resources for traditional purposes by aboriginal persons ... " Aside from highlighting the role of sustainability , have we made any progress over the past 23 years ? <p> This remains to be seen , based on the next steps determined by federal Environment Minister Catherine McKenna .
