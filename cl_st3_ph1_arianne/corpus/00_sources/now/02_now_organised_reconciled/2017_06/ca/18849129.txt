<p> In practical terms , U.S. President Donald Trump 's decision to formally withdraw from the Paris climate accord does n't much matter . Rather , it is a theatrical gesture that gives all actors in this drama a chance to strut their stuff . <p> The accord itself is largely aspirational . According to the United Nations , even if all the pact 's signatories -- including the U.S. -- delivered on their Paris promises , the world would still face catastrophic climate change . <p> In any case , it is not clear that signatories will deliver on their promises . Prime Minister Justin Trudeau 's Liberal government , for instance , is still distressingly vague about how it plans to meet its carbon reduction target , a target that Trudeau , when he was in opposition , used to deride as grossly inadequate . <p> If Canada or any other signatory fails to meet its goal , there will be no penalty . There is no enforcement mechanism in the Paris pact . <p> Trump understands this . He could have just as easily continued to sabotage the climate deal from within . <p> The answer , it seems , is that this veteran showman wanted to make a point that would resonate with his audience . <p> Certainly , there is a great deal of theatre involved in all of this . Major nations , including Germany , Britain , China and Canada , continue to pledge fealty to Paris . Given the inherent weakness of the accord , that is easy to do . Given the global unpopularity of Trump , it is also politic . <p> But it also allows these nations to portray themselves as being on the cutting edge of the new economy . Tesla founder Elon Musk responded to the move by quitting one of Trump 's advisory councils . In effect , he was saying : I ca n't afford to be associated with such an old dinosaur .
