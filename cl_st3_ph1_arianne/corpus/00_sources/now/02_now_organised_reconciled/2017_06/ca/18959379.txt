<h> At Endswell , Home Farm shares struggles of other BC Farmers <p> Corinna Headley says she was supposed to plant the first crop of outdoor greens at Endswell Farm in mid-March , but unusually cold winter weather forced her into the farm 's greenhouse . <p> This adjustment , was one of many Headley and the team from Home Farm Gardens have had to make at their market gardening operation . Flooding , both in the field and in the greenhouse has been another major struggle . <p> After planting their tomato seedlings , Headley noticed something starting to eat away at the small plants from the stem , up . It turns out there was a " wire worm " infestation . These pests stay in the soil for between two and six years , and farmers along the coast have all suffered from them this year . <p> " We learned , from an advisor at West Coast Seeds , that if you bury potato chunks down at the stem the worms will eat them instead of your plant , " says Headley . " It worked @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ out chunks of potatoes and add new ones to between 500 and 600 plants every day , but I 'd say we 've saved about 70% of our tomato crop . " <p> Islander Sarah Dent works with a resource network for young farmers and people who are new to the agriculture sector across BC ( called the Young Agrarians ) . She 's been observing the work at Endswell and says the team of farmers there have been amazing at responding to the varying crises they 've come up against . <p> " It 's been awesome to watch , actually , " says Dent . " They deal with things that would make people working in any other kinds of business just pull their hair out . Every year , there 's a new and major challenge for farmers to face , whether it 's drought , pests or too much water . I would predict that as climate change intensifies , so will these struggles .
