<h> Loss of rail service threatens Churchill <p> Thompson Citizen <p> June 14 , 2017 12:00 AM <p> With the latest in a series of bad news announcements and weather-related catastrophes that have plagued Northern Manitoba 's Hudson Bay seaport over the last year or so , Churchill is reeling . The news that the Hudson Bay Railway 's owners do not expect train service to be restored north of Gillam before winter could well prove to be , if not a death blow , a serious injury to the long-term future of the town as a viable year-round entity . <p> This latest disaster comes on the heels of recent flooding , a blizzard that prompted a state of emergency in early March , and last year 's decision by OmniTrax , which owns the railway , to stop shipping grain from the Port of Churchill , which threw a few dozen Churchillians out of work . Unlike the weather-related impacts , however , the decisions by OmniTrax -- the most recent one forced upon it by circumstances beyond its control -- are not the sort of blows that @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ assistance . <p> What form that assistance should come in depends upon whom you talk to , though . Churchill-Keewatinook Aski NDP MP Niki Ashton asked the federal government to nationalize the port in December , and presumably there are many who would extend that sentiment to include the railway as well , given that both are owned by the same company and the Hudson Bay Railway is the only permanent land link that some communities along its route , including the one that serves as the terminus , have to the rest of Manitoba . That course of action definitely has the advantage of seeming more practical than some others , such as convincing a private company to take over a port that does n't currently have any shippers making use of its facilities and a rail line that has washed-out track sections and several damaged bridges , with further damage possible as a more thorough assessment is completed . <p> A Winnipeg Free Press-Probe Research poll conducted in September found that 67 per cent of Manitobans supported the federal government taking over the port and railway , with @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ were even bigger supporters of nationalization , at 71 per cent and 85 per cent respectively , while those least supportive of such a move included rural residents , at 25 per cent and those with annual household incomes greater than $100,000 , at 27 per cent . The poll found no statistical differences in support between aboriginal and non-aboriginal respondents . <p> Coincidentally , OmniTrax 's decision to suspend operations between Amery , northeast of Gillam , and the end of the line at Churchill came just days after a First Nations consortium that includes Mathias Colomb Cree Nation at Pukatwagan said that the two entities had achieved a memorandum of understanding concerning the consortium 's purchase of the rail line and port for $20 million , to be financed in the form of a loan from OmniTrax , reportedly . If that deal proceeds , it would be a good one for OmniTrax , which purchased the port from the Canada Ports Corporation for a nominal fee after buying the rail line from The Pas to Churchill from CN for $11 million in 1997 . <p> Bat as @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in an op-ed column last summer , shortly after the decision to suspend grain-shipping operations , government dollars had still been flowing to the port and rail line while it was under private ownership , including $25 million from the federal government over five years beginning in 2012 and another million or so from Manitoba 's previous NDP government in 2015 as the company struggled to maintain service levels . If all of this could n't keep the business afloat , how much will it cost the federal government to run the railway ? While government obviously does provide services that are n't commercially viable in any number of areas , there 's no guarantee that it will wish to do so in this case . <p> Assuming that there are any easy solutions to the problems facing the port and rail line , which are affected by unpredictable factors such as weather and climate change , is obviously naive . But the provincial and federal governments do owe the residents of Churchill and the business owners who are trying to decide whether to forge ahead or cut their losses @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , one way or the other , sooner rather than later .
