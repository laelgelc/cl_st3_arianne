<h> Scrapyard in Cobbs Arm sits on the side of the ocean <p> Salvage by the sea <p> Published on June 13 , 2017 <p> COBBS ARM , NL -- One need not look too long to find something out of place on the stony shore of Cobbs Arm . <p> Sharing the shoreline with the fishing stages and saltbox homes typical of outport Newfoundland is a salvage yard . The site is tucked beside a saltwater pond , advertised as a storage centre for unwanted junk metal and cars . The former limestone quarry is visited two or three times a year by a mobile crushing unit . On site , the machine compresses the materials for transport . <p> Salvage yards themselves are not uncommon in the province , but one located on the shores of the ocean , on a bed of solid rock , is striking to witness . The owner of the scrapyard , Cobbs Arm resident George Saunders , readily confirmed the operation . The site has been a metal dumping ground for generations he says . <p> " Oh yes , long before @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Saunders told the Pilot . " There has been stuff left down there for years , old dump trucks and tractors , I disposed of most of that myself . " <p> When questioned on the location , and its proximity to the water , Saunders claims he tried to obtain a piece of land " out of the way . " The application was denied by the province says Saunders , and so the salvage yard by the sea has remained . <p> Saunders insists there is no oil going in the water . <p> The Pilot reached out the Department of Environment and Climate Change ( DECC ) to determine the scope of the operation , and environmental controls for the geographically unlikely site . <p> " A junk , scrap , or waste material yard is a registerable ( sic ) undertaking under the Environmental Assessment Regulations ( section 45 ) , " said DECC Media Relations Manager Lynn Robinson in an email statement to the Pilot . " As there is no record of an undertaking having been registered under the Environmental Assessment Division with the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , the department has not conducted any studies or placed any conditions on the site to date . " <p> Saunders however , tells things differently . He says provincial environment officers visit the site several times a year for inspection , and proudly states they have never found a problem . <p> " Maybe two or three times a year , he was here not too long ago , " said Saunders . " That 's the Province . " <p> Saunders seems surprised when advised the Province claimed there are no controls . The scrapyard operator knows his environmental officer by name , and says the relationship is open and direct . Saunders says he is performing a service for the people of Newfoundland and Labrador , and wants to be transparent . Saunders has even invited the Pilot to visit the site and witness first hand how the operation is run . The Pilot has agreed and will provide updates as more information becomes available . <p> Another response received by Service NL stated the first record that refers to this site being used to store wrecked @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> Service NL also stated no approval is required as this is a small operation that does not own crushing equipment . No environmental testing , no controls . <p> The Pilot also questioned the department on the perceived hazard such a site , alongside the ocean , could pose . At the time of publication the Pilot had not yet received a response .
