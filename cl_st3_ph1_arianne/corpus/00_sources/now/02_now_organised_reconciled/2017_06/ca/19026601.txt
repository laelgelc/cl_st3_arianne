And if introduced into a location without Lyme , there 's a three- to five-year lag before it shows up in the area 's mammals . <p> In Ontario , all public health units do passive surveillance , whereby people submit ticks , which helps pinpoint areas of concern . A growing number of units are also doing active surveillance , or tick dragging . Collected specimens are sent to PHO for identification and for bacteria testing to the National Microbiology Laboratory . This year in particular , both labs have been inundated with submissions . <p> Toronto started tick dragging in 2013 , focusing on one park .
