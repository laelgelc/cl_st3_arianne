<p> Mayors of the world are uniting in Montreal this week in one of the first major international gatherings of municipal leaders since the United States pulled out of the Paris Agreement this month . <p> The fight against climate change , and how cities can fill the void created by the U.S. , will figure high on the agenda at the Metropolis conference , hosted by an umbrella organization of 140 mayors from Accra to Zongo , currently led by Montreal 's own Denis Coderre . <p> Among the climate-championing civic leaders who will be in Montreal this week to brainstorm are Anne Hidalgo , mayor of Paris , and Rahm Emanuel , mayor of Chicago . <p> " If you want to get something done , ask a mayor , " Coderre quipped last week of the upcoming Metropolis conference . <p> The meeting was planned long ago , but the timing is fortuitous . When U.S. President Donald Trump recently abandoned all responsibility for the struggle to save the planet from the effects of global warming , the world 's mayors were among the most vocal critics @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ who lit up their city halls and landmarks with green lights to symbolize their continuing commitment to the goals of the Paris Agreement . And it is mayors who are redoubling their efforts to cut greenhouse gas emissions and promote the lifestyle and policy changes that can really make a difference . <p> When Trump quipped that he was ditching the climate accord because he was elected to represent Pittsburgh , not Paris , the mayors of those two cities issued a joint statement condemning his decision . Bill Peduto of Pittsburgh and Hidalgo of Paris said they will not be pitted against each other , and instead , reaffirmed the importance of joining forces to save the planet . Peduto noted that his city long ago moved on from its dirty , industrial past as a steel town and embraced renewable energy . He also said he will pledge his support to the " We Are Still In " movement , an ad hoc group of states , cities , businesses , institutions and organizations in the U.S. who remain committed to the Paris principles . <p> Words and @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ challenges ahead . But action from cities is crucial to curb climate change . Despite occupying just two per cent of the world 's landmass , cities are responsible for 70 per cent of emissions , according to the United Nations Human Settlements Programme . And since cities set policies that can make a huge difference in reducing harmful greenhouse gases -- from operating public transit to designing transportation networks to setting green building codes to enacting land-use laws that rein in sprawl -- their efforts are an essential part of the solution . <p> Fortunately , cities have already been leading on combatting climate change for some time . They were instrumental in clinching the critical mass of popular and political support that resulted in the Paris treaty , which includes every nation on Earth except Syria , Nicaragua and now the U.S. <p> And cities , from the First World to the Third World , have already been implementing the innovations and making the investments that will help put humanity on a course toward a greener future . Canberra is on pace to have zero net emissions from city operations by 2020 . <p> There is still much that needs to be done by cities -- and that includes mobilizing their citizens to make smart choices that will lead to lower emissions . Drawdown , a new book by climate scientist Paul Hawkins that offers peer-reviewed analysis of possible solutions , suggests individuals can actually have as great an impact as political leaders in reaching our climate goals . <p> So while Montreal , for instance , pioneered the Bixi bike share program , is installing electric car-charging stations and will be getting a new electrified light-rail line , there is much more the city can do to encourage drivers to get our of their cars , attract people to live in the core so they can walk and bike more easily , and plant more trees to reduce the heat-island effect of paved surfaces . <p> The U.S. abdication of its leadership role on saving the planet is a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ it 's that there are many , many other leaders ready and willing to take up the charge -- some of whom will be here in Montreal over the next few days . <p> Conferences like Metropolis are n't just schmooze-fests ; they are opportunities to share ideas , learn best practices from each other and strengthen resolve .
