<h> Decisions , not dithering , required from premier <p> It was 2005 when the Economist dubbed then-prime minister Paul Martin as " Mr. Dithers " for his inability to make tough decisions . Mr. Martin had only just taken over the helm of the federal Liberal party and , according to the magazine , the reality of governing seemed to have been a bit more overwhelming than the image Mr. Martin must have had when he so doggedly pursued the position . <p> Just more than a decade later in Manitoba , we may very well have our own Mr. Dithers . Since coming to office in April 2016 , Premier Brian Pallister has talked a big game about all the changes he would make . In reality , however , he has failed to deliver . <p> Mr. Pallister has actively cut expenditures and shrunk government . <p> Even though many of these decisions are contrary to his election pledge to avoid austerity , at least he has been decisive . On other files , however , Mr. Pallister has produced a decidedly Mr. Dithers-like performance . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ funding agreement and the national climate change framework to implement a carbon pricing scheme . Mr. Pallister withheld his support to protest the terms dictated by Ottawa . And while the provinces were bullied on both deals , it 's no longer clear why Manitoba is holding out or why , in the case of the carbon tax , the premier has not delivered on this province 's own solution . <p> But there 's more . Manitoba has yet to produce a policy on the legal sale and taxation of marijuana , despite a looming July 2018 federal deadline . The inertia in Manitoba and in other provinces as well , prompted Ottawa to threaten to offer mail-order access to pot for provinces that do n't act by next summer . <p> The dithering continues . Projects such as the Winnipeg Art Gallery 's $65-million Inuit Arts Centre and the $75-million Diversity Gardens at Assiniboine Park are stuck in limbo because Mr. Pallister will not confirm his government 's contribution . Such inaction threatens funds from Ottawa and from private donors , all of whom made their contributions dependent @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , Mr. Pallister again seems to be dithering . The private owners of the Hudson Bay Railway and Port of Churchill , who no longer wish to do business in Manitoba , have thrown the entire economic viability of the north into doubt with their leisurely pace in repairing flood damage to the rail link . Mr. Pallister has taken a hands-off approach that has left northerners fearing for their future . <p> Mr. Pallister has shown he can make tough decisions . His decision to reorganize Winnipeg hospitals -- which will result in some emergency rooms being closed and repurposed -- is bold . It will be some time before we know whether it 's a good idea , but at least he made a decision . <p> The dithering may be a reflection of the fact that Mr. Pallister is operating government with a skeleton staff . <p> He celebrated the fact he reduced the number of cabinet posts , giving rank rookies responsibility over two or three departments . But he also cut back on communications and political staff , which has impaired government 's overall capacity @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> The learning curve may have been steep , but it 's time for Mr. Pallister to get on with the business of governing .
