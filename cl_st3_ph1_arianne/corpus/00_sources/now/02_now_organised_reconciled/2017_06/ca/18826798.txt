<h> Foreign Minister Freeland 's speech will unveil a Canadian foreign policy rooted in multilateralism <p> OTTAWA -- Foreign Minister Chrystia Freeland will put her stamp on Canada 's overarching foreign policy for the first time in a speech Tuesday that urges multilateralism , trade , action on climate change and support for the world 's most vulnerable . <p> In the House of Commons Tuesday morning , just about five months into her tenure , Freeland will take half an hour to set up a framework with an outlook on Canada 's relationships with the United States , the Asia-Pacific , and the global south . The edict of predecessor St ? phane Dion -- " responsible conviction " -- will not be part of the discussion . <p> Freeland 's statement comes the day before the results of a defence policy review , and an expected funding announcement for the military , are to be unveiled by Defence Minister Harjit Sajjan . <p> The speech will begin with a history of Canadian foreign policy , particularly in the decades since the Second World War . Freeland will discuss @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ multilateral systems , and argue that Canada must again step up with real contributions and boundary-pushing if it wants to remain influential . <p> In listing her own priorities , Freeland will address the past , present and future of Canada 's relationship with the United States . She will highlight trade with Europe and opportunities in the Asia-Pacific . <p> Freeland will name international bodies Canada will continue to play an active role in : the North American Aerospace Defense Command ( NORAD ) ; the North Atlantic Treaty Organization ( NATO ) ; the G7 ; the G20 ; the World Trade Organization ; the United Nations ; the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation ; and the Organization of American States . She will reiterate that Canada is seeking a seat on the UN Security Council . <p> She will argue Canada is at a unique apex that separates it from other countries in the West , with both Commonwealth and Francophonie ties that connect it to much of the globe , including large swaths of the African continent . <p> While Freeland will not specifically mention Iran or her @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ -- despite officials having recently visited Tehran -- she will discuss the fight against Daesh ( ISIL ) in Syria and Iraq , as well as Russia 's annexation of Crimea from Ukraine . <p> Freeland will say instability in other regions can spill over and affect Canada , and that it is in the national interest to help quell insecurity and conflict elsewhere in the world , including in the Middle East . <p> She will condemn the seizure of sovereign territory and urge that the sanctity of borders be upheld more than ever , as worries rise over the perceived erosion of borders -- whether because of migrants and refugees , terrorism , or the Internet . Freeland will also mention technological evolution and how it inevitably affects the global economy . <p> While Canada 's history of joining peacekeeping efforts will be addressed by the speech , specifics about upcoming missions will not , since these will be addressed by Sajjan 's presentation Wednesday . <h> Related <p> Likewise , specifics on international assistance will pend the conclusion of a review being undertaken by International Development Minister @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , but it is expected to include specifics about the level and direction of funding . <p> The speech supports a motion to : " recognize that the government is committed to a foreign policy that supports multilateralism and rules-based international systems , human rights , gender equality , the fight against climate change , and economic benefits being shared by all . " <p> The motion also asks the House to recognize " that further leadership on the part of Canada is both desirable and required " and to support " the government 's decision to use the foregoing principles to guide Canadian foreign policy . " <p> Opposition parties will have an opportunity to debate the motion and offer their own takes on foreign policy .
