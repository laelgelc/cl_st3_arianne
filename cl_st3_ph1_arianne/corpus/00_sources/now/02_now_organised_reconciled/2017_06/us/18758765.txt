Andreassen/CAGE ) <p> Researchers working in the Barents Sea have discovered hundreds of craters on the Arctic Sea floor , some measuring over a kilometer in width . These craters , which date back to the end of the last Ice Age , were formed when large reserves of methane exploded in the wake of retreating ice sheets . Because methane is a potent greenhouse gas , this discovery is a potential warning of things to come in our warming world . <p> Around 12,000 years ago , the Barents Sea -- an area north of Scandinavia -- was covered by a thick layer of ice . When the planet warmed and these ice sheets receded , enormous quantities of methane trapped below were abruptly released . The result was a series of explosive methane blowouts that littered the seafloor with craters .
