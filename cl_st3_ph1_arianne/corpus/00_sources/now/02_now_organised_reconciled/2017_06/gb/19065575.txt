<h> Dr James Fenton : Nature is paying the price of development <p> We have only one planet and , as the ? American poet Gary Snyder says , " this living , flowing world is all we have -- forever " . <p> Why , then , do we not stand up for it more , instead of allowing developers to trash it , or , to use more measured language , cause continual attrition of its wildness ? <p> Why are we reducing the richness of the planet by forcing the animals and plants with which we share it into smaller and smaller corners , until they have nowhere left to go ? Indeed , why do we allow development everywhere , leaving no ? places where our grandchildren can ? experience nature in the raw ? <p> Recently , the John Muir Trust conservation charity had to pay ? 50,000 to Scottish &amp; Southern ? Energy for upholding the government 's own policy of keeping wild areas wild . Do the chief executives and shareholders not realise their descendants will have to live on the same planet , soon , through their own action , to be impoverished in wildlife and industrialised all over ? <p> There was a time of hope in the 1970s and 80s when environmental protection rose high up the political agenda and rules and regulations were introduced . These are the same regulations that politicians now argue are too restrictive and a barrier to growth . <p> While global warming has risen up the agenda , some of this relates to the economic opportunities brought by climate change mitigation . However , wildlife ? conservation has dropped off the agenda . <p> The lack of import given to wildlife is , to some extent , reflected in how different professions are rewarded . This gives the message that looking after wildlife is a frill that society can not afford . <p> Perhaps it is only when the whole planet is industrialised and wildlife is restricted to a few remaining pockets , that we will have realised too late that we had our values all skewed .
