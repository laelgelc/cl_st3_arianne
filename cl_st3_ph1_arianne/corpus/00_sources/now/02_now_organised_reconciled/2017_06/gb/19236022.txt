<p> The June heat waves that impacted much of the UK and Western Europe were made more intense because of climate change say scientists . <p> Forest fires in Portugal claimed scores of lives while emergency heat plans were triggered in France , Switzerland and the Netherlands . <p> Britain experienced its warmest June day since the famous heat wave of 1976 . <p> Human-related warming made record heat 10 times more likely in parts of Europe the researchers say . <p> During June , mean monthly temperatures about 3C above normal were recorded across western parts of the continent . France experienced its hottest June night ever on 21st when the average around the country was 26.4C . <p> That same day had seen the mercury hit 34.5 at Heathrow in what was the UK 's warmest June day for 40 years . <p> It was a similar story in the Netherlands which is set to have its hottest June on record while in Switzerland it was the second warmest since 1864 . <p> Now , researchers with World Weather Attribution have carried out a multi-method analysis to assess @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ record temperatures . <p> " We simulate what is the possible weather under the current climate and then we simulate what is the possible weather without anthropogenic climate change , and then we compare these two likelihoods which gives us the risk ratio , " Dr Friederike Otto from the University of Oxford , one of the study 's authors , told BBC News . <p> " We found a very strong signal . " <p> That signal , according to the authors , made heat waves at least 10 times more likely in Spain and Portugal . <p> Fires resulted in the deaths of 64 people in Portugal , while in Spain they forced the removal of around 1,500 people from holiday accommodation and homes . <p> In Central England , France , Switzerland and the Netherlands the intensity and frequency of such extreme heat was four times as likely because of climate change , the study says . <p> " We found clear and strong links between this month 's record warmth and human-caused climate change , " said Geert Jan van Oldenborgh , senior researcher at the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Local temperature records show a clear warming trend , even faster than in climate models that simulate the effects of burning fossil fuels but also solar variability and land use changes , " van Oldenborgh added . <p> Image copyrightGetty ImagesImage caption The heat wave in France saw the warmest June night on record <p> The researchers say their reported results on the impact made by human related warming are conservative in some ways . Their study indicated that in countries like Spain , Portugal and France , climate change could be increasing the chances of extreme heat by up to forty times . <p> The scientists believe that the chances of these extreme heat events becoming much more common will increase unless rapid steps are taken to reduce carbon emissions . <p> " Hot months are no longer rare in our current climate .
