<h> About 30% of the global population already faces lethal heat for 20 days a year or more . <p> By the end of the century , three-quarters of the human population will be exposed to potentially lethal heat for a significant period each year if carbon emissions are n't greatly reduced . <p> Even if significant cuts in carbon emissions are made , deaths from extreme heat will still rise to about half of the world 's population , finds a study published in Nature Climate Change . At the moment , 30% of the population is exposed to potentially lethal heat for 20 days a year or more . <p> The threshold for a lethal heatwave was measured by both the air temperature and the humidity . The more humid it is , the more lethal heat is -- so a warm but very humid day could be as dangerous as a hot but very dry day . <p> The study looked at research between 1980 and 2014 on deaths associated with heatwaves .
