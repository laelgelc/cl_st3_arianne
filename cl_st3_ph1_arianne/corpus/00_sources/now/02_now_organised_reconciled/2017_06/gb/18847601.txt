<p> Students from King James 1 Academy of Bishop Auckland created their installation outside of the Town Hall , joining artwork already created by students from Bishop Barrington as part of the same project . <p> Artists then helped the students with the application of hydrophobic spray that is used instead of paint , which means the designs are visible on the ground when it rains . <p> The project supports the aims of County Durham Climate Change group and the work of the Civil Contingencies Unit on raising awareness and understanding of climate change resilience issues , such as the potential for an increase in localised flooding . <p> Richard Hurst , sustainability education development advisor at Durham County Council said : <p> " This project has helped raise awareness of climate change amongst the students involved as well as giving them the opportunity to take part in an innovative art project . <p> " All those involved have worked hard on their designs and their creations will now help others gain an understanding of the impact climate change can have on our community .
