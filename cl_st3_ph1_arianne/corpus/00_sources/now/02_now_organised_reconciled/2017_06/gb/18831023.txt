<p> A new study has been launched to establish if there is a link between fish farming and dwindling numbers of wild Atlantic salmon . <p> The Atlantic Salmon Trust ( AST ) has identified a number of " likely suspects " ahead of its research into the " alarming " drop in numbers of the species in recent years . <p> Among them is " the impact of aquaculture through sea lice , pollution , disease and escapees . " <p> Other issues the study will examine include predation by seals , cormorants and dolphins , incidental capture in fishing nets , over-fishing of young salmon 's staple diet , climate change and moving sea currents . <p> The trust hopes the project will create the " most detailed picture yet " of the threats to wild salmon . <p> Launching the " suspects framework " study , AST executive director Sarah Bayley Slater said : " Because farmed salmon is now so commonplace in the supermarket , many people are unaware of the plight of the wild Atlantic salmon for which Scotland and Norway are so famous . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ suspects causing these high mortality rates , which will help us to stabilise and over time , reverse the decline of this important species .
