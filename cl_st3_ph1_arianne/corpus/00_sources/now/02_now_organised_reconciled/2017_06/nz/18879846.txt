<h> Port Nelson claim environmental award <p> Port Nelson has been nationally recognised after cutting the time its trucks are on the road by 10,000 hours . <p> It took out the Resilience to Climate Change category at the national Green Ribbon Awards in Wellington on Thursday night . <p> Its QuayConnect freight logistics model aimed to reduce fuel consumption and energy usage , with particular focus on the way wine is transported . <p> The Port worked with trucking company Central Express Ltd ( CEL ) , glass bottle manufacturer O-I New Zealand , and wine bottler WineWorks Marlborough to set up the distribution model . <p> Four truck and trailers move pallets of glass wine bottles from Port Nelson to Marlborough , and bring bottled wine back , rather than the traditional transport model where trucks would travel empty one leg of the journey . <p> Environment Minister Nick Smith presented the awards and said he was particularly proud this one went to the Nelson organisation . <p> " This transformative logistics model has made such a positive environmental impact in just its first year of operation , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ message to opponents of climate change initiatives , like President Trump , that such projects can make both business and environmental sense . " <p> Port Nelson CEO Martin Byrne said winning the award ahead of two other organisations working equally hard to reduce the impact of climate change was an incredible endorsement of the Port 's work . <p> " The significant difference QuayConnect has made to the wine transport sector 's energy output is hugely rewarding and we are enormously honoured to have won this award on behalf of the project 's collaborators , " Byrne said .
