<h> Dr Paul Duignan : Does Richie McCaw deserve a Nobel ? <p> DR PAUL DUIGNAN <p> Last updated 05:00 , June 29 2017 <p> MONIQUE FORD / Fairfax NZ <p> Imagine if we limited the Player of the Year award to rugby backs . Richie McCaw would have never won it . <p> OPINION : Imagine if in rugby the Player of the Year award could only be given to backs rather than forwards regardless of how well forwards performed . Well , it 's a bit like that in scientific research . <p> Recently I attended Professor Michael Corballis ' Rutherford Prize lecture at Te Papa . He 's a psychologist and , significantly , only the second social scientist to ever win the Rutherford Medal -- the peak scientific award of New Zealand 's Royal Society Te Aparangi . <p> It 's not just the Rutherford medal that has a bias against awarding social scientists recognition , the most important global award for scientists ; the Nobel Prize simply has no award for social scientists . Economists bought their way into the prize in 1968 -- they 're savvy like that -- but since then the Nobel Prize Committee has closed the door on recognising any of the other social sciences , or for that matter , another key discipline these days -- computer science . <p> As economists -- and my own discipline of outcomes theory -- tell us , public incentives matter . Things like the Rutherford medal , and obviously the Nobel Prize , are the peak high-status incentives for scientists . Universities promote themselves on the basis of how many Nobel Prize winners they have on their faculty . <p> Yes we need incentives to encourage scientists to work on : climate change , battling cancer , and feeding the world 's population , plus to grow our understanding of the universe works , <p> As a result , it seems to me we 're advancing by leaps and bounds on the physical and biological sciences front . Each week seems to bring : medical @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ particles discovered in weird and hyper-expensive accelerators ; and probes heading off into space to check out potentially civilisation-threatening asteroids . <p> But turning to developing evidence , analysis and techniques for solving the issues that the social sciences grapple with -- terrorism on the streets , the elite versus the mass , political polarisation , failed states , loss of confidence in democracy , corruption , political polarisation , ethnic conflict , the effects of automation and AI -- on these issues it 's clear we have not made so much progress . <p> It 's certainly great to reward physicists for getting particles to collide in spectacularly new ways , but should n't we also be rewarding researchers who are working out how to stop people on the streets of our cities colliding in equally spectacular , but humanly destructive ways ? <p> For people like me who work on social , government and corporate strategy , we need social scientists to provide the evidence base which organisations can then apply to ensure their strategies actually improve outcomes . <p> So more power to the Royal Society Te @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ up there in lights . <p> But , if you 're a physical scientist , or economist and get the call from the Nobel Committee in Stockholm , really the only decent thing to do at the moment is to turn it down until they make it a level playing field between the physical and social sciences . <p> Presumably you would want to be able to look your social science and computer science colleagues in the eye and say that you won it fair and square ? <p> After all , if you could get a Nobel for rugby , presumably no one would want Richie McCaw to miss out just because he was a forward .
