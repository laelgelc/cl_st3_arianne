<h> My Say : China a regional driver <p> When the 1970s dawned in China , the devastating decade-long Cultural Revolution was still running its course . Fast forward to today and China 's rapid economic rise has inspired awe and a combination of fear and admiration . <p> China is the world 's largest manufacturer and exporter . In Deloitte 's 2016 Global Manufacturing Competitiveness Index , China was ranked top . While the US remains the world 's largest economy , China is already remaking the landscape of international economics and development , society and , by extension , politics . <p> China 's post-1978 reforms marked the start of a gradual and highly coordinated transition to a socialist market economy . The reforms strongly relied on encouraging exports by attracting foreign direct investments . At the same time , it protected the domestic market through controlled liberalisation . <p> Coincidentally , China 's reforms began when the advanced capitalist world was transiting from state-led national development to a new world order of neoliberal economic globalisation . China successfully rode the neoliberal globalisation wave , and by the mid-1980s , had become the pre-eminent outsourced manufacturing destination . <p> Neoliberalism steadily lost credibility after the global financial crisis ( GFC ) of 2007/08 . Many economists blame neoliberalism for the GFC , which marked the end of the long US boom and interestingly accelerated China 's emergence as a global economic power . <p> The International Monetary Fund , in its October 2016 World Economic Outlook , warned that persistent stagnation in the advanced economies could fuel further anti-trade sentiment . This is precisely what has happened . Deep unease over stagnating living standards in the developed economies in the post-GFC period has led to rising populism and a return to nationalist inward-looking policies . <p> In the Brexit referendum held in June 2016 , England voted to leave . In January 2017 , US president Donald Trump formally scrapped the Trans-Pacific Partnership deal . As it is , the pace of integration has already been slowing since the GFC . Global trade growth , which outpaced global GDP growth over the 1990s and 2000s , has been expanding at a slower pace than global output . <p> Harvard University historian Niall Ferguson has argued that there are economic and political parallels between today 's global situation and the world of the early 20th century . Charles Kindleberger , in his book The World in Depression 1929 -- 1939 , blamed the unstable international economic system prevailing then for the 10-year global economic crisis . The British had not been up to the task of stabilising it , while the US was unwilling to assume responsibility . <p> Unfortunately , we are now living in a G-Zero world , according to Eurasia Group president Ian Bremmer , with no one single country or bloc of countries capable of driving a truly international agenda . This is where China , bent on achieving economic hegemony , comes into the picture . China is after all highly exposed to the global economic system and certainly feels it is a victim of it . It has , as part of its grand strategy to achieve economic hegemony , established the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank and the BRICS New Development Bank , rolled out its " One Belt , One Road " ( OBOR ) initiative and internationalised the renminbi . <p> Many people fear that China will use its financial clout to gain geopolitical subservience , especially in this region . However , it can not be denied that the region will stand to benefit a lot economically from China 's rise , especially if China focuses more on providing regional rather than global economic leadership .
