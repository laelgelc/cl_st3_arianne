It has been cast as a move to protect America , but in the long run it wo n't derail the world 's transition to a low-carbon economy , and instead the US will find itself lagging , not leading . <p> Trump 's repudiation of the Paris deal is regrettable for at least three reasons . First , because the US is a technological leader whose entrepreneurs are extremely well placed to lead the global low-carbon transition ; second , because America 's abdication of climate leadership weakens the global order and sends a wink and a nod to other fossil-fuelled recalcitrants like Saudi Arabia and Russia ; and finally because having the world 's second-highest emitter outside the agreement is a clear negative . <p> That said , US flip-flopping on climate is nothing new . But more importantly , the transition to a low-carbon future is not dependent on the actions of a single player . <p> The criteria for successful climate change policy are hard to achieve but easy to describe : success will come when non-emitting technologies economically outcompete fossil fuels , pretty much everywhere in the world , in the main half-dozen or so sectors that matter . <h> Beating the ' free-rider ' issue <p> A stable climate is what we call a " public good " , similar to fresh air or clean water . This is a situation in which one player playing well can determine the whole outcome , and as such is a much easier problem to solve . <p> We have seen technology play this role before , in other global environmental issues . The ozone hole looked like a hard problem , but became an easy one once an inexpensive , effective technological fix became available in the form of other gases to use in place of ozone-harming CFCs ( ironically , however , the solution exacerbated global warming ) . <p> Something similar happened with acid rain , caused by a handful of industrial pollutants .
