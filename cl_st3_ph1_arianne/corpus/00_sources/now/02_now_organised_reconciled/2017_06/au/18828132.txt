<p> Researchers undertaking a landmark study on the platypus are warning of significant population declines and localised extinctions across areas of southern Australia . <p> The study , which is still several years away from completion , seeks to establish a clear picture of the animal 's population for the first time . Current estimates on population figures vary from 30,000 to 50,000 mature animals , according to the International Union for Conservation of Nature ( IUCN ) . <p> " The platypus is notoriously difficult to study in the wild because it is a nocturnal animal and very elusive so there is no concrete population number , " Richard Kingsford , Professor of Environmental Science at the University of NSW , told Fairfax Media . <p> " But what we have uncovered so far is leading us to believe that the situation for platypus right now in Australia is critical . We can report with certainty that they have disappeared from the Adelaide Hills , the Bass River and Avoca River catchments in Victoria and they have declined precipitously in the Wimmera River catchment in the Murray-Darling .
