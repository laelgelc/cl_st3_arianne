<p> The National Electricity Market is a complex beast . Stretching from Port Douglas in north Queensland to Port Lincoln in South Australia , more than 40,000 kilometres of transmission lines provide electricity to 9.6 million customers . <p> Few of us ever take the time to understand how it works . But chief scientist Alan Finkel , along with an expert panel , has spent the past eight months doing just that -- to try to fix some major problems : soaring prices , declining reliability and falling energy security . <h> Why is the report required ? <p> The way we receive and use electricity is changing rapidly . The past year has seen the closure of two ageing coal-fired power stations at Hazelwood in Victoria and Port Augusta in South Australia . <p> Households are getting more efficient at using energy and are increasingly turning to solar panels and batteries to help meet their own energy needs . <p> Power companies are being encouraged to invest in large-scale renewable energy , through a federal subsidy known as the Renewable Energy Target ( RET ) . <p> But @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and wind farms built using the RET has not been despatchable . <p> Under the CET , government would set an emissions intensity target , which could be reduced over time to meet Australia 's international climate change obligations . <p> Electricity generators would be issued certificates for the electricity they produce in proportion to how far their emissions intensity is below the threshold . <p> Importantly , the scheme would be technology neutral -- that is , all forms of electricity generation would be eligible , including coal with carbon capture and storage or gas -- provided they are below the emissions intensity threshold . <p> Electricity retailers would be obliged to purchase certificates to demonstrate to the Government that a pre-determined share of their electricity came from low emissions generators . <h> Is it a price on carbon ? <p> Effectively , yes . Retailers will pass the cost of their certificates on to consumers . <h> What are the other options ? <p> The chief scientist and his expert panel also investigated an emissions intensity scheme ( EIS ) -- a different market mechanism . <p> But @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ scheme before Dr Finkel delivered his preliminary report . <p> An EIS works in a similar way to a CET to stimulate new investment in low emissions technology . <p> But it also penalises higher-emitting technology , thus hastening the departure of coal . <p> An EIS is a baseline and credit scheme . The Government sets a baseline of emissions intensity . <p> In contrast , a CET is all carrot , no stick . In the words of Dr Finkel : " There are no prohibitions , just incentives .
