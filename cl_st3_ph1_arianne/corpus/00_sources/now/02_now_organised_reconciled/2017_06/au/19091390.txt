<p> The attention of those at the forefront of the world 's technological battle to tackle climate change is inexorably being drawn to a small nature reserve about 60 kilometres off the Western Australian mainland . <p> There , on the barren and remote Barrow Island , the world 's most ambitious operation to permanently dispose of carbon waste underground is taking shape and is expected to start up later this year . <p> Helped by $60 million of funding from the federal government 's Low Emissions Technology Demonstration Fund , the project will be the largest worldwide to securely inject CO2 into a dedicated geological formation well below the surface of the Earth . <p> The world will be watching , both because of the sheer scale of the operation -- which will dwarf existing carbon disposal projects -- but more fundamentally because of the importance of the CCS process to help meet global greenhouse gas reduction commitments , experts say . <h> Related Quotes <h> Company Rather than just release all the CO2 into the atmosphere -- as is standard in most projects -- it will be extracted . <h> Gradually dissolve <p> The project involves three CO2 removal trains , each processing one-third of the total gas flow piped from Gorgon and Jansz about 65km and 130km , respectively , north-west of the island . A solvent -- activated methyldiethanolamine -- is used to remove carbon dioxide , as well as trace amounts of other substances . <p> After passing through a compressor , the CO2 -- now an oily substance -- will be piped through a 7km underground line to one of three " drill centres " and injected through one of nine CO2 wells to gradually dissolve into the saline aquifer .
