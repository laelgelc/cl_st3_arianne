<h> Study Finds Colorado Only Recycling 12 Percent Of Trash <p> That 's the conclusion drawn from the Colorado Public Research Interest Group 's annual State of Recycling report . In 2017 , Colorado created a record 9.3 million tons of waste while " flatlining " at 12 percent for recycling , compared to the national average of 35 percent . <p> That leaves over 8 million tons of trash going straight to the landfill , where it sits or gets shipped for disposal elsewhere . In many cases , the final resting place becomes the ocean or other natural environments . <p> The group estimates that the average Colorado resident produces 8 pounds of trash per day , or nearly 1.5 tons every year . <p> Over the past 10 years , Colorado has seen its waste production dip and rise to record levels , but diversion from landfills has not increased much , if at all , during that same span . <p> Aside from environmental concerns , decaying landfill waste also produces methane gas , a contributor to climate change . That makes it difficult to find out which Colorado cities or counties are lagging behind . <p> Summit County is only one of five counties that does track its own recycling , which was highlighted in the report . Summit 's recycling rate is at 23 percent , nearly double the statewide average . However , Summit still lags behind Boulder , which has a 40 percent recycling rate , and Pitkin at 30 percent . <p> Summit County 's Zero Waste Task Force has been aiming to get the county to 100 percent diversion or zero waste , but it has been slow-going with the changing economic landscape for recycling operations and lack of funding and infrastructure to do more efficient diversion . <p> The county has pledged to do more to educate residents and visitors about how to properly recycle .
