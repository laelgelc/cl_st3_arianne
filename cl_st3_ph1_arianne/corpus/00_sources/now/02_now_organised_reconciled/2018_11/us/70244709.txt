<h> Our view : Getting rid of the rat pack <p> We 're not talking about stool pigeons in the mob movies of old but the long-tailed rodents who are increasingly skittering across yards , driveways and basements across the North Shore . <p> No one is quite sure of the reason for the influx . Some point to the recent spate of development across the region -- all that blasting and digging is pushing the rodents out of the shadows and into our backyards . The building boom has meant more dumpsters and trash receptacles where the animals can find a ready meal . Others point to climate change -- warmer temperatures and milder winters mean the rats may reproduce more often . And more often is a relative term : A mother rat can have up to 14 babies , and that 's after a gestation period of a mere two weeks . <p> " You take one pregnant rat and you come back in one year , and all of the descendants of that pregnant rat will have become 15,000 to 18,000 rats , " rodentologist ( yes , it 's a thing ) Bobby Corrigan @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ residents are freaking out . <p> In Peabody , for example , rat complaints rose from 23 in 2016 to 103 in 2017 , according to Health Director Sharon Cameron . The city has already fielded 117 complaints this year . After dozens of residents spoke out on Facebook , Danvers set up an online system earlier this month that has already fielded plenty of reports . <p> " We have people that are just crying out for some kind of relief , " Mark Carleo , the town 's assistant director for public health , told reporter Kelsey Bode . " I do n't know what that relief is . " <p> The best approach finds residents and government working together . The online reporting systems used by Danvers and Peabody are a good start . They help health officials pinpoint problem areas quickly . Sometimes , the information leads to targeted extermination efforts in public areas . Peabody health officials will also walk through neighborhoods where there are complaints to talk to residents and pass out educational material . <p> That education is key , because there is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ is essential that property owners remove potential food sources -- uncovered trash cans , open dumpsters , even bird feeders and unattended vegetable gardens -- if they want to avoid their own personal infestation . <p> " This is not a problem that government can solve on its own , " Cameron said . " It really does require cooperation from everyone in the community to be aware of potential sources of food or harborage on their property and work to address those . " <p> It 's good advice .
