<h> Meet the bowhead whale hunters of northern Alaska <h> Each spring , local hunters sit on the edge of the ice and wait for whales -- a custom that 's at least 1,000 years old . <p> This story appears in the December 2018 issue of National Geographic magazine . <p> On the North Slope of Alaska , the culture of the Inupiat centers on whales . Each spring , men and women spend weeks on the tuvaq -- the ice near the water -- watching for bowhead whales migrating north from the Bering Sea to the Canadian Arctic . When one is spotted , a team pushes an umiak onto the water . There is typically one chance to harpoon the whale . If the hunt is successful , each person in the village can receive a share of the meat . <p> View Images <p> A butchered bowhead whale can yield thousands of pounds of food . The ninit -- community shares of meat and blubber -- are apportioned equitably to ensure that everyone benefits from a successful hunt .
