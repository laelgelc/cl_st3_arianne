This means putting a brake on oil , coal , and gas production and exploration . This means replacing the market-based , investor-focused approach to energy production with one that treats energy as a public good , while engineering a shift toward social ownership and management of energy supplies . <p> Rooted in energy sovereignty and self-determination , this approach would spur faster decarbonization , including by weakening vested interests ' power to resist change . It would also facilitate the restructuring of energy systems to serve social and ecological needs . <p> Another system-level transformation that would facilitate major emissions reductions would be the creation of a zero-waste circular economy , whereby everything we produce and consume returns safely to nature or is recycled and reused . <p> Consider textiles production , which in 2015 generated greenhouse-gas emissions totaling 1.2 billion tons of CO2-equivalents . The biggest gains would come from the introduction of less wasteful production processes . <p> Important steps should also be taken regarding land use ( encompassing agriculture and zoning changes ) . Their restoration would protect not just biodiversity , but also local people , including the indigenous communities whose land-tenure rights have been systematically violated .
