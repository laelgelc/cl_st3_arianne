<p> Taxonomies change , as keen birder Jonathan Franzen knows , in culture and in nature . As a species , the " Great American Novelist " -- as Time once called him -- seems more mythical than ever . The canon is undergoing a correction to admit diverse , once-suppressed voices ; critics and readers are rejecting notions of a single spokesperson or dominant culture . Franzen -- white , middle-class , male , cisgender , heterosexual , author of classical naturalist fiction -- does n't carry the authority he once did . <p> In fact he carries some rather troubling baggage . After a series of tragicomic mishaps worthy of one of his characters , he has over the years been labeled an elitist , a misogynist , a climate-change denier . He was criticized for dissing Oprah back when she selected 2001 's " The Corrections " for her book club . When that Time cover came out , Franzen found himself at the center of a social-media firestorm , stoked by Jennifer Weiner and Jodi Picoult , in which he became the love-to-hate avatar for a sexist publishing industry .
