<p> To the extent she was known at all before being turfed from the NDP caucus this week , Calgary-East MLA Robyn Luff was infamous for a picture of her outside the federal building in Calgary during a 2011 demonstration holding an anti-oilsands sign , " No More Dirty Oil . " <p> Luff , who was a junior high teacher and anti-oil activist before being elected to the legislature in 2015 , would later claim she had " recanted " her anti-oilsands stance . Was the " dirty oil " sign inappropriate ? " Yes , it absolutely was inappropriate , " Luff claimed after being elected , " and I regret making that decision . " <p> Luff insisted she had never , really , been anti-oilsands . She had merely opposed the manner in which the former Tory government had developed Alberta 's bitumen deposits and the way ( she felt ) they had ignored climate change . <p> " With our new government and our new initiatives , that 's not as much of a concern for me anymore , " Luff explained . <p> But which was Luff 's real position ? Or were those just words put in her mouth by party leaders ? <p> Luff got herself expelled from the provincial NDP caucus this week for claiming to be the victim of legislative bullying by party leadership , including Premier Rachel Notley . Specifically , she complained that her questions in the legislature were scripted by staff in the premier 's and ministers ' offices . And a bill she wanted to introduce had been heavily rewritten by leadership until it no longer resembled her initial legislation . <p> So , given Luff 's claim that individual MLAs are just puppets for the leadership , is n't it reasonable to assume her recantation was written for her , too ?
