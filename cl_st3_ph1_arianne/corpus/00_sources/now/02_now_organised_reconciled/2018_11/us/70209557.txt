<h> Tongass sees busy 2018 wildfire season ; rest of Alaska , no <p> Fire burns at the Funny River area on May 25 , 2014 . Wildfires in southeast Alaska are not the hazard they are elsewhere in the state . ( Creative Commons photo by Josh Turnbow/USFS ) <p> The country 's largest national forest saw an increase in wildfires in 2018 . <p> The U.S. Forest Service responded to 32 wildfires this year in Alaska 's Tongass National Forest , the Ketchikan Daily News reported . The forest averages 15 to 20 fires per year . <p> Wildfires in southeast Alaska are not the hazard they are elsewhere in the state . The Tongass is the world 's largest intact temperate rainforest . Ketchikan , near the southern end of the forest , bills itself as the Rain Capital of Alaska and receives upward of 150 inches of rain a year . <p> Tristan Fluharty , forest fire management officer for the Tongass , said warm , dry weather was behind the increase in 2018 . <p> " We really did spend a lot more time actually putting the fires out , " Fluharty said . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ largest fire , which occurred in fire in May and June near Berners Bay north of Juneau , burned more than 50 acres . <p> A fire at Moser Bay near Ketchikan burned for several days and cost the Forest Service about $126,000 to fight . <p> Most wildfires in the region are caused by humans , Fluharty said . Most of the 2018 fires were near the road system in Juneau , the region 's biggest population center . <p> The Tongass ' annual budget for fire preparedness is about $1 million , which pays for salaries , equipment , inventory , travel and training . The 2018 fire suppression costs were not available . <p> The busy season in southeast Alaska stood out in contrast to the rest of the state . <p> The state usually sees about 500 wildfires , said Tim Mowry of the state Division of Forestry . Lightning starts many of the fires in interior Alaska forests .
