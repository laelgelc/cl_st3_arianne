<h> Local authors tackle the problems and opportunities of just transition from a fossil-fuel economy <p> Book is a lucid , accessible account of the bind in which many fossil fuel industry workers find themselves , as well as an exciting and fundamentally hopeful sketch of some attempts to solve the problem of just transition . <h> Total Transition : The Human Side of the Renewable Energy Revolution <p> In October of this year , the United Nations Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change sounded yet another dire alarm . If major reforms in energy production and greenhouse gas emissions are not made in the next dozen years , the experts said , our average annual global temperature will spike up by over 1.5 degrees . Coral reefs will die , sea levels will go up and catastrophic climate events like storms and draughts will multiply . <p> Welcome , in short , to climate hell . <p> But many observers are concerned about the impacts on millions of workers of fighting climate change . Will workers be thrown into unemployment or low-wage precarious employment as the world makes the transition away from burning fossil fuels ? Will oilfield workers @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ suffer ? <p> This question , often formulated as the issue of " just transition , " lies at the centre of Total Transition : The Human Side of the Renewable Energy Revolution , an exciting and important new book from B.C. authors Sandeep Pai and Savannah Carr-Wilson . <p> Pai , an award-winning investigative journalist , and Carr-Wilson , who recently began work as a lawyer for a B.C. First Nation , met while doing graduate work together in an environmental science program in Hungary and Sweden . ( Full disclosure : While preparing for her career in law , Carr-Wilson was a student of my brother at the University Victoria , and I have met both authors . ) <p> Their book is a lucid , accessible account of the bind in which many fossil fuel industry workers find themselves , as well as an exciting and fundamentally hopeful sketch of some attempts to solve the problem of just transition . The authors take readers along as they travel to Jharia , an Indian coalfield town where underground fires have raged for a century , to the tar @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ soul- and planet- destroying oilfield work and destitution , and Vancouver Island , where the T'Sou-ke First Nation is leading the way in the transition to renewable energy projects . <p> Too often , critics of fossil fuels and the devastating effects those fuels are having on our climate can come off as over-earnest and self-righteous . There is none of that annoying tone in Total Transition . This book is a good-humoured , but serious . look at the core challenges we face in the 21st century , and a must read for all concerned citizens . <p> Tom Sandborn lives and writes in Vancouver .
