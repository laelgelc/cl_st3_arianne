Nick King/Lansing State Journal <p> A hundred years after Michigan granted women the right to vote , they 've organized to become a political force -- as seen in last week 's general ( midterm ) election . <p> Yes , Michigan led the nation in passing women 's suffrage in 1918 , two years before the federal government caught up . And women today are capitalizing on that freedom , running for office and being elected in higher numbers than ever before . <p> This is progress . <p> And it 's progress that is happening right now , right here in Greater Lansing -- at all levels of government . <p> Congratulations to Gretchen Whitmer , soon to become only the second woman to hold the office of Governor of Michigan . And to Sen. Debbie Stabenow ; the first female U.S. Senator ever elected in Michigan won re-election to a fourth term in Tuesday 's election . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ -- including Elissa Slotkin , one of five women elected to serve in Michigan 's 14 Congressional districts . <p> Also on the legislative front , a record 53 women will serve in state legislature next year -- including the five female representatives elected to one of Greater Lansing 's eight seats , all of which are currently or most recently held by men . <p> Local voters also elected three women to the Ingham County board , two women to the district court bench and two women to the MSU Board of Trustees . <p> This is monumental . It 's progress . And it 's great for our region , the state and the nation . <p> But there 's still far to go . Progress is great , but it 's slow and takes focus . <p> Gained ground must not be lost through complacency .
