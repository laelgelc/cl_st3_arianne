<h> Climate change damaging male fertility <p> Climate change could pose a threat to male fertility - according to new research from the University of East Anglia . <p> New findings published today in the journal Nature Communications reveal that heatwaves damage sperm in insects - with negative impacts for fertility across generations . <p> The research team say that male infertility during heatwaves could help to explain why climate change is having such an impact on species populations , including climate-related extinctions in recent years . <p> Research group leader Prof Matt Gage said : " We know that biodiversity is suffering under climate change , but the specific causes and sensitivities are hard to pin down . <p> " We 've shown in this work that sperm function is an especially sensitive trait when the environment heats up , and in a model system representing a huge amount of global biodiversity . <p> " Since sperm function is essential for reproduction and population viability , these findings could provide one explanation for why biodiversity is suffering under climate change . <p> " A warmer atmosphere will be more volatile and hazardous , with extreme events like heatwaves @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Heatwaves are particularly damaging extreme weather events . Local extinctions are known to occur when temperature changes become too intense . We wanted to know why this happens . And one answer could be related to sperm . " <p> The research team investigated the red flour beetle ( Tribolium castaneum ) to explore the effects of simulated heatwaves on male reproduction . <p> The beetles were exposed to either standard control conditions or five-day heatwave temperatures , which were 5 ?
