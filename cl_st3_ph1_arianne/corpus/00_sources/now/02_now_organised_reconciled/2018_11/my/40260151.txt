<p> Kuala Lumpur : The Energy , Science , Technology , Environment and Climate Change Ministry is targeting to grow renewable energy 's ( RE ) proportion of generation mix from the current two per cent to 20 per cent by 2025-2030 . <p> This bodes well with the electricity supply industry ( ESI ) , now grappling with the escalating global coal price . Eventually , this new target might allow for more stable tariffs in the future , analysts said . <p> Fifty-three per cent of Malaysia 's electricity comes from coal , 42 per cent from natural gas and the remainder from hydro and RE . Coal price is currently hovering above US$100 per tonne , up by more than 100 per cent after reaching a 10 year-low in 2016 when it fell below US$50 per tonne . <p> The price increase since last July has thrown a spanner in the ESI 's generation costs as coal is 100 per cent imported . <p> Over 60 per cent of the coal is purchased from Indonesia , and the rest from Australia , South Africa and Russia . <p> ADVERTISEMENT <p> " Coal demand in the next @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ levels , " said Hans van Cleef , Senior Energy Economist at ABN Amro . <p> " Although headlines in the newspapers may suggest that coal demand will peak soon , in reality , demand will remain solid in the coming years , " he added . <p> To prepare for such scenario , the industry has taken steps to achieve greater efficiency in power generation through coal power plants . <p> All new coal-fired power plants now use ultra-supercritical ( USC ) technology that burns less coal for more power , while complying with emission standards . -- Bernama
