<h> The inclement political weather <p> WHENEVER foreigners ask me what the weather in Malaysia is like , I tell them " It 's either sunshine or rain , but more sunshine " . <p> In temperate countries , like the UK , they always refer to the " inclement weather " . " Inclement " refers to unpleasant weather -- stormy , rainy or snowy . <p> I guess this can apply to any political environment too , which , in the global context , is witnessing clear effects of " climate change " . Political cultures are changing , with the newer generation of citizenry being more aware and informed of current developments . The parochial political culture of the old days are gone , when many were mostly uninformed , and even unaware of what their governments were doing and took little interest in the political process . <p> Today , there is an increasingly strong participant political culture , whereby the people are better informed and get involved in the political process . As such , those in positions of authority in decision-making , must have that necessary wider perspective , that global overview , so that decisions and their implementation must truly add value to nation building and long-term development , rather than inward looking , parochial , and often non-productive policies that can set the nation back . <p> Today , the " popular " clarion call is " country first " . It is of course , valid to put the nation first . <p> But there must be a context to that . And the context is the clearly unavoidable , undeniable and intricate linkages that countries like Malaysia have with the rest of the world . Policies can not be entirely inward-looking and domestic-orientated .
