<p> For the past 30 years , the Devon United Church thrift store has served members of the community and donated to and supported various charities . <p> At the centre of it all the thrift shop 's tireless volunteers have worked hard every week to help those who come through its doors . <p> Shannon Taylor , who has been a volunteer for past three years and keeps the shop running along with Tanis Kydd and Sharon McCann , president of United Church Women in Devon , said the shop 's volunteers are for the majority senior citizens who lend their free time to working in the shop and that the organization needs new helping hands . <p> " It was started by women who were much younger and basically the people that are the dedicated volunteers ... they 're getting older , " she explained . " We started thinking what 's a good way to recruit more volunteers ? " <p> After many hours of research , Taylor said she realized there was a way for local Devon businesses and the thrift shop to partner together . <p> " There is actually an industry that is @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , volunteerism melding with business . And it is actually very significant , " Taylor said . <p> With local businesses sending their employees to volunteer at the thrift store when they can be spared for a few hours , Taylor said the arrangement can be mutually beneficial . The thrift shop will have an extra set of hands , the employees ' will gain new skills , meet and interact with new people and go home with the feeling of having done good for their community . <p> " When you volunteer you come home feeling so good because you know what you have done . This is meaningful , " Taylor commented . <p> For local businesses , they gain some recognition for their support and involvement at the shop . <p> " These volunteers are people who would be coming from a business environment , so a lot of them are already up on the whole business end of things , " Taylor remarked .
