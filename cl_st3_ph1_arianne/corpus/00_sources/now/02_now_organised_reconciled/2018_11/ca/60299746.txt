<h> Translating climate risk into the language of investors <p> Yaelle Gang , the Canadian Investment Review November 28 , 2018 <p> A few decades ago , people were concerned about the depleting ozone layer . Then , in the late 1980s , the Montreal Protocol was ratified to phase out ozone-depleting substances . Fast forward to today : the ozone hole is shrinking and is expected to completely close by 2060 . <p> " That says to me that if you get policymakers , business leaders and scientists together you can come to solutions to these kinds of problems , " said James Davis , chief investment officer at OPSEU Pension Trust , speaking at the pension fund 's climate change symposium last week . <p> " We can get a handle on the risk of a recession , stock market volatility , even the systemic risk of a financial crisis that we experienced in 2008 , but climate change risk is even tougher , " he said . " We know it 's real , we know it 's slow moving . Its impact is not expected to be felt for many years . " The market is a very effective discount mechanism . " <p> Despite the reality of this risk , Davis noted it is n't being talked about more because responsible investing -- or environment , social and government -- professionals are n't speaking the same language as investment professionals . <p> OPTrust has a responsible investment committee with representation from every asset class . When Davis first joined the committee , the discussions were focused on compliance . " The conversations were n't making this risk feel real , " he said . " The problem was there was no measure of the impact of climate change-related risks that an investor could relate to . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ we need to find a way to measure it , " he added . " The reality is that climate change has the potential to impact our ability to pay pensions , " he added . <p> Pension plans need to price climate change risk into their investment decision process much better than they currently do .
