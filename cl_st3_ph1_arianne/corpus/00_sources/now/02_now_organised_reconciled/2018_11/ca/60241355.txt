Many EU countries have still not switched off the most polluting part of the energy mix : coal . <p> Coal continues to play a major role in the electricity generation for several EU countries : 80% in Poland , and over 40% in the Czech Republic , Bulgaria , Greece , and Germany . Even the gas-exporting Netherlands produces 35% of its electricity from coal . So far only a few EU countries have pledged to shut down their coal-fired power plants altogether : the UK , France , Italy and the Netherlands . <p> This needs to change , because coal 's lingering place in the EU energy system is disastrous for the climate , for the environment , and for human health . <p> From a climate perspective , coal is the worst way to generate electricity -- even compared to other fossil fuels . After all , a shift to electric cars will mean little if we power them with electricity from coal . <p> Coal is also bad for the environment and human health . Coal-fired power plants across Europe are responsible for the largest amounts of sulphur dioxide , nitrogen oxides and particulate matter released into the air . These pollutants can enter the human body and cause various health problems , from lung cancer to heart attacks . Air pollution causes 400,000 premature deaths in the EU every year . <p> And yet , several countries in Europe continue to support coal-fired electricity production . The country with the highest number of coal mining jobs is Poland , with 100,000 people employed . This represents a mere 0.7% of Poland 's total employment . In all other countries coal mining employment stands below 30,000 units , always representing less than 0.6% of total employment . <p> Of course the closure of mines will be painful for those few workers and communities who still depend on them .
