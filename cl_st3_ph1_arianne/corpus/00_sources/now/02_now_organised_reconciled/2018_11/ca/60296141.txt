Videos showing hostage beheadings by terrorists , a photograph of a model 's emaciated body to denounce anorexia in the fashion industry or , more recently , the image of a dying polar bear to call the attention to the consequences of climate change . These stories and images represent a kind of grotesque that claims to be accurate representations of our realities . <p> Every day , the media gives us a dosage of these grotesque images and stories -- grotesque because they are shocking , disgusting or horrific . Sometimes , the grotesque is linked to the exhibition of bodily functions or a deteriorating or dead body . <p> Art , literature , theatre and cinema have always used the grotesque to capture the attention of the public . <p> I call this grotesque tranparency : the strategic use of realistic grotesque images to achieve the goal of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ an environmental crisis or denouncing the questionable behaviour of an elected official . <p> Distortion and transparency <p> This communication strategy is somewhat paradoxical because it conveys something repulsive that could also be considered to be an exact representation of the situation ( think of the image of a dying patient suffering from lung cancer on a cigarette package ) . The realism of the image is enhanced by its disturbing effect . <p> This becomes problematic for two reasons . First , it reveals something to make us believe that we are seeing the " real thing " but it is used as a way to divert our attention or hide other things . Second , it is used to justify violence ( consider ISIS carefully staged executions of hostages ) , trivialize moral dilemmas ( how far a government or a corporation can go to make the public aware of a disease or prevent it ? ) or even legitimize questionable acts because they are considered to be " authentic . For example , the video ( originally recorded in 2005 and revealed in 2016 ) of then-presidential candidate Donald Trump 's dismissive remarks regarding women . <p> The objective of those who leaked the video was to denounce the questionable behaviour of Trump in relation to women . Certainly , the public disclosure of Trump 's infamous conversation with Billy Bush contributed to the polarization of the electoral campaign . <p> Despite Trump 's remarks in the video about his treatment of women , there was scant impact on the support he received from some women voters , particularly white women who favoured Trump over Hillary Clinton ( 52 to 45 per cent in favour of Trump ) . <p> Another case that illustrates this strategy was the video showing the late mayor of Toronto , Rob Ford , smoking crack . More interestingly , after the police confirmed the authenticity of the video , Ford 's approval rating rose slightly from 39 to 44 per cent , a sign again of the paradoxical impact of such disturbing disclosure . <p> Undermining the hero <p> The revelation of the horrific or disgusting has also been used to rewrite history . Such troubling images or stories could translate into active public support .
