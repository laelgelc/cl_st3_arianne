<p> When the release of a draft " withdrawal agreement " between the United Kingdom and the European Union plunged the British government into chaos in recent days , Canadian federal Conservative leader Andrew Scheer felt compelled to remind Canadians that he had been an early advocate of Brexit . In a June 2016 opinion piece in the National Post , Scheer had presented Brexit as a chance to rescue " British political traditions " from " the dictates of EU bureaucrats in Brussels . " <p> Faced now with evidence that leaving the EU is more complicated than the Brexiters had promised , Scheer could acknowledge that EU membership also comes with many benefits , which the British government is struggling to preserve . Instead , he has chosen to double down on his criticism of the EU , highlighting its " bureaucratic nature " and negative effects on sovereignty -- and making sure that this assessment is trumpeted out to his party 's 140,000 Twitter followers . <p> Regardless of the turmoil that Brexit has caused in the U.K. , Scheer likes to talk about the issue because it presents him with an opportunity to emphasize themes @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , sovereignty , skepticism of global ? lites . Depending on the details of the future relationship negotiated between the U.K. and the EU , Brexit might mean that the Canada-EU Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement ( CETA ) , in effect for little more than a year , ceases to apply to Canada 's closest economic partner in Europe . Canadian businesses who have invested in the U.K. to serve European consumers might lose unrestricted access to the EU 's single market . The EU might actively expand its capacity in security and defence policy , a development that the UK has long resisted , and that requires a carefully calibrated Canadian response . <p> In addition to these foreign policy challenges , Brexit also has the potential to cause divisions within Canada . With the U.K. as an EU member , Canada-Europe relations have been an area of relative political consensus . It was equally attractive to Liberals , traditionally keen to diversify Canada 's economic policy and to promote multilateralism , and to Conservatives , whose foreign policy has emphasized free trade and attachment to British traditions . Conducting a significant part of our transatlantic relations through the EU has also meant that potentially divisive debates about the relative importance of the " Anglosphere " and the " Francosphere " for Canadian foreign policy identity could be kept in abeyance . <p> There is nothing to be gained in politicizing Canada 's European relationships , with Liberals prioritizing the EU and Conservatives the U.K. Such a development would not only endanger the reliability of Canada as an international partner , it would also cause needless internal divisions , not least between Quebec and the rest of Canada . <p> The only sensible response to Brexit is to do the utmost to maintain good relations with both partners . <p> Canadian diplomats are working to meet this challenge .
