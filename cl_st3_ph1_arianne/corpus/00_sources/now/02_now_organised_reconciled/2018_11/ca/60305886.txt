The Shand facility would be designed to capture two million tonnes of CO2 per year , and would include a sulphur dioxide ( SO2 ) abatement . <p> The study was released at a conference in Edinburg , Scotland , on Nov. 28 by the Regina-based and SaskPower-affiliated International CCS Knowledge Centre . <p> Corwyn Bruce , head of technical services and lead author of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a conference call . <p> He opened by noting that BD3 had pioneered the way for CCS around the world , and said the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change has found that almost every pathway which leads to limiting global warming to 1.5 C includes carbon capture and storage . <p> Shand was considered because it is an open site with lots of room for a carbon capture facility , and it is the newest in SaskPower 's fleet of coal-fired power generating units ( excluding BD3 ) . Also , the turbine designs are very similar to that of BD6 and Poplar River Units 1 and 2 , so there could be similar results expected if those facilities were used . Shand also already has a Carbon Capture Test Facility , which can be used for piloting . <p> As for the 67 per cent cost reduction , per tonne , he pointed out that Shand , at 300 megawatts , would be twice the size of Boundary Dam , and there are " benefits of scale .
