<h> EDITORIAL : Make science user-friendly <p> Premium content <p> The Chronicle Herald <p> Published : Nov 07 at 6 a.m . <p> Updated : Nov 07 at 10:05 a.m . <p> - 123RF Stock Photo <p> Science is more than just a subject in school or university . It is more than E = mc2 or geeks in white coats working with test tubes in laboratories . It 's tied to our environment , health , food , economy , innovations and quality of life . <p> STEMfest is taking place in Halifax this week to celebrate science and raise awareness of its importance in our world today , and to make connections between the people and organizations that do science and are affected by it . ( STEM is the acronym for Science , Technology , Engineering and Math , the disciplines that come under the big tent of science . ) There will be talks , workshops and events with businessfolk and schoolchildren . <p> This is a good time for an event like this . Scientists often have a problem connecting with the general public . They are often perceived as geeks and elites trapped @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ makes them easier to dismiss . Yet their work and discoveries affect us all . And the urgency for a clearer understanding of scientific facts has never been greater . <p> In October , the UN Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change issued a grim report warning that the world is teetering on the brink of disaster . Temperatures are rising due to a buildup of greenhouse gases . If we do n't slow down global warming , we will face more violent storms , rising sea levels and flooding , droughts , forest fires , the destruction of ecosystems and eradication of species . <p> The problem is that too often scientists talk to us in their own jargon , which makes it difficult to fend off skeptics or sway decision-makers . Take a look at the official website of the UN panel on climate change . They sure do n't make it easy for the non-scientist to navigate it or to understand . <p> Scientists , whatever their discipline , must get smarter about how they engage people about their research and the collective challenges they see . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that could help with this : STEAM means Science , Technology , Engineering , Arts and Math . Putting the art in science could help scientists reach the people who need to hear their messages . Giving people -- in particular children -- the information they need in relatable language , graphics and other interactive installations could make the scientific work more accessible to the legions of non-scientists out there . <p> Perhaps the next event should be called STEAMfest . The more people know and understand what science is telling them , the more governments will be influenced to make those changes that scientists warn are necessary -- whether it 's on the legislative front or in the public policy sphere .
