<h> Governmental failure has left millions of Nigerians in the dark <p> Just 59 per cent of the country has access to electricity ( Source : Getty ) <p> Tim Worstall <p> Government is , as former President Barack Obama pointed out , just that name for what we do together . <p> Unfortunately , in some of the poorer parts of the world , it 's really the name for what does not get done at all . <p> Nigeria is a prime example . It is a large oil producer with plentiful resources . It is also energy-poor . <p> Only four million out of 32m households have access to private power supplies , while the World Bank and USAID estimate that just 59 per cent of the country has access to electricity . Nigeria is currently running its electricity generating plants at a third of capacity due to lack of resources and infrastructure . <p> The underlying problem is a well-known one . The production of oil always has with it some co-production of natural gas . What does not exist is a pipeline network to collect from production sites and deliver it across the country . <p> This is where we come back to the dangers of poor government . <p> In 2010 , Nigeria signed a contract with Process and Industrial Developments Limited ( P&amp;ID ) , an engineering and project management company started by two unlucky Irishmen , to capture natural gas at oil fields , process it , and deliver it to Nigerian power plants . <p> The power plants would receive the gas at no cost , while P&amp;ID would have the right to market by-products received from processing it . <p> The potential benefits to Nigeria were huge .
