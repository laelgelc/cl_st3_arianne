Already , more than three-quarters of personal trips in the city are not by car . <p> Advertisement <p> With the 2010 Walkable Kyoto Declaration , the city aimed to stop being a car-dominated society . Kyoto has an ambitious list of 94 projects promoting walkability . <p> The results so far are impressive , according to municipal government data . Use of public transport has increased significantly . Car traffic entering the city is declining year on year , as is use of car parking . Only 9.3 per cent of tourist movements in the city were by car compared to 21 per cent in 2011 . <p> As a result of this , emissions from transportation in 2015 were 20 per cent lower than 1990 levels . <p> Kyoto is determined to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ opportunities for cycling . <p> Cycling is the best way to see the city . An increasing number of the 50 million tourists a year are choosing to rent a bike . <p> Kyoto is a compact , flat city with a grid structure . This makes it easy to cycle and navigate . It now has close to 45km of official cycleways . While not as big as European cycling cities like Copenhagen ( which boasts 416km of cycleways ) , Kyoto 's network is relatively large for an Asian city . <p> On-street discarding of bicycles has declined 27-fold since 2001 . Everyone else uses the pavement , meaning neither pedestrians nor cyclists feel safe . <p> Second , cyclists across Japan are notorious for not following rules . The city 's population is projected to shrink by 13 per cent between 2010 and 2040 ( reaching around 1.28 million ) .
