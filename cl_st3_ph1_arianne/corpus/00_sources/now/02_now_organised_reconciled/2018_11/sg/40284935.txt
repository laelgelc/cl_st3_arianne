<p> IDENTIFY THE BIG PROBLEM <p> There is a long list of global problems to combat , including hunger , drought , poverty , bad health , polluted water and poor sanitation . <p> One that 's connected to all the others is the recent bombshell news that climate change is accelerating : Over the next 20 years , Earth 's atmosphere will reach average temperatures as much as 2.7 degrees Fahrenheit ( 1.5 degrees Celsius ) above preindustrial levels . <p> The scope of climate change gives companies like Google , Facebook and Amazon excellent opportunities to find specific approaches that would have meaningful effects . <p> TECHNOLOGY CAN MAKE A BIG DIFFERENCE <p> There are , of course , many elements driving climate change @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ all greenhouse gas emissions . <p> Farms emit the largest share and could benefit from a range of technologies , such as data analytics and artificial intelligence . As a bonus , innovating in agriculture could help feed more people . <p> Technological tools could help farmers collect and use data to manage their crops more precisely in ways that would reduce greenhouse gas emissions -- such as using less fertiliser and plowing and planting fields more efficiently . <p> Specifically , better data on soil and plant health could help farmers know where they need to increase or decrease irrigation or pesticide and fertiliser use . <p> These practices save farmers money and increase farms ' productivity , generating more food with less waste . <p> Farmers harvest cotton in a field in Nana Viramgam village in the western Indian state of Gujarat February 9 , 2015 . ( Photo : REUTERS/Amit Dave/File Photo ) <p> TECH COMPANIES CAN MAKE MONEY FROM GREEN OPPORTUNITIES <p> If companies are to get involved , there needs to be an opportunity to earn money -- and the more , the better . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ food practices that enhance productivity , promote sustainable methods and reduce waste could produce commercial opportunities and new savings worth US$2.3 trillion overall worldwide annually . <p> Our research estimated that of that US$2.3 trillion a year , US$250 billion could come from the application of artificial intelligence and other analytics for precision farming alone -- US$195 billion of which would be in the developing world , with US$45.6 billion in South Asia and US$13.4 billion in East Africa . <p> Other estimates for the effects of AI and analytics are less specific , but still within the same range -- between US$164 billion and US$486 billion annually . <p> There is indeed money to be made by technology companies interested in developing climate-friendly , productivity-improving interventions in agriculture . <p> INNOVATE TO OVERCOME THE MANY BARRIERS TO CHANGE <p> Before the commercial value can be unlocked , however , there are many barriers to consider . <p> Many rural areas , even in the developed world , do n't have affordable high-speed internet connections and , particularly in the developing world , the farming community is not as technology @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ have been handed down through generations and the idea of using data to make modifications to such long-held beliefs and methods can be countercultural . <p> In addition , there are many practical realities : 83 per cent of the world 's cultivated land is fed only by rain , with no irrigation systems to make use of better data . <p> Beyond that , in most parts of the world , seeds and fertiliser are not high-quality , lowering crop efficiency . Further , a lot of farms ' output is wasted because of lack of refrigeration and slow transportation from fields to consumers . <p> With all those obstacles , it is understandable that investments in data-driven agriculture dropped 39 per cent from 2015 to 2016 . <p> There are groups still working , though . FarmBeats is a Microsoft project that combines low-cost sensors in the ground with drones that both create aerial maps and act as wireless data relay points . <p> Nigeria 's Zenvus and India 's Aibono analyse soil data . Kenya 's FarmDrive develops credit scores for people without formal bank accounts or @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ phone and social media activity , together with local agricultural and economic information . <p> These are creative efforts to solve deep and complex problems , but clearly there is room for large , well-resourced technology companies to step in , make a difference with big ideas , deep pockets and global support . <p> INVEST IN PARTNERSHIPS <p> Technology entrepreneurs will need to develop business models and organisational structures that are better at collaborating with local agricultural communities and businesses , to navigate personal and political relationships as well as regulations and government programs . <p> Technology will not , on its own , be some sort of silver bullet that will unlock prosperity . <p> Changing technology companies into agents for widespread global good will not be easy -- and it can be done in areas beyond agricultural innovation , too . <p> There has been no shortage of talk about these ideas : 50 CEOs met with French President Emmanuel Macron to discuss socially positive technologies ; World Economic Forum events around the world discuss societal benefits of a Fourth Industrial Revolution ; and some companies , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ fulfilling United Nations goals for global sustainability . <p> We still have a long way to go .
