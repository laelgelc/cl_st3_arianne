Horticulture is considered the backbone of Kashmir 's economy and there are an estimated 144,825 hectares of land dedicated to growing apples in the region . The apple industry annually produces 1.7 million tons of apples , with exports worth US$857 million . Most apples in India come from Kashmir and demand increases in the festive season of September-January when Indians celebrate several feasts , sharing fruits and sweetmeats with friends and family . However , this year the snow in the peak festive season has cut off roads from Kashmir to mainland markets . " You can see the boxes now lying in the warehouse , left to get rotten , " said Shabir Hussain Dar , a fruit grower whose consignment worth US$7,000 has been delayed by the transport problem . The drastic change in Kashmir 's weather pattern is part of the impact of global warming across the globe , according to Prof. Shakeel Ahmad Ramsoo of the department of earth sciences at the University of Kashmir . " The unseasonal snowfall is the outcome of climate change that has already been hitting farmers hard , " Ramsoo said . Abid Ahmad , a sociologist in Kashmir , said the fruit industry has been the worst hit of all the state 's sectors . Besides weather changes , a continuing conflict resulting in strikes and curfews has also affected the harvest and transport of highly perishable produce . The region has been witnessing violence for the past 30 years after groups of Islamic militants took up arms to free it from Indian rule . Indian army efforts to counter the insurgency continue .
