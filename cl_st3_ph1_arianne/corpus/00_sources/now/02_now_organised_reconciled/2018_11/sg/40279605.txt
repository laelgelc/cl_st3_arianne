<p> PORT MORESBY - Small island states share many common interests and concerns , and thus have to " make common cause together " , said Prime Minister Lee Hsien Loong . <p> Mr Lee made this point during a dialogue between Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation ( Apec ) leaders and leaders from Pacific Island states on Saturday ( Nov 17 ) . <p> Singapore and the Pacific Island states are fellow small island nations with shared concerns such as defence , climate change and having to adapt to the changing external environment , he added . <p> While many Pacific Island countries are not part of the 21-member Apec grouping , the Pacific Islands Forum secretariat is an observer , and leaders and ministers from its countries are in Papua New Guinea this week for meetings with Apec leaders . <p> Noting the value of Saturday 's forum , Mr Lee said the need for small countries to work together and " advocate for our collective interests " was why Singapore set up the Forum of Small States at the United Nations in 1992 . <p> He added that the Republic regularly attends the annual leaders summit by the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , Fiji , New Zealand , Papua New Guinea , Samoa , Tonga and Vanuatu . <p> Singapore is also a member of the Small Island Developing States grouping . <p> Building relationships with Pacific Island states also provides opportunities for Singapore to share its developmental experience , Mr Lee said , noting that nearly 5,000 officials from these states have visited the Republic over the years . <p> The Republic shares its experience via the Singapore Cooperation Programme , which provides training courses for officials from other nations in areas such as economic development and e-commerce . <p> Mr Lee said he was very happy to welcome the continued participation of the Pacific Island states in the programme , adding : " We have picked up ideas from you too .
