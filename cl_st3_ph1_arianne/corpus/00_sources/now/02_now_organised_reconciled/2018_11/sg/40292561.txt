Image : Eco-Business <p> To date , Singapore has not introduced legislation on an issue now accepted by much of the world to be an environmental crisis that is getting out of control . <p> In August , New Zealand said it would ban plastic bags . In September , Malaysia said it would ban single-use plastic . In October , Thailand said it would ban plastic imports . <p> The main reason for Singapore 's inaction could be because plastic pollution is not seen as a problem by the powers that be . <p> This is because most the 763,400 tonnes of plastic produced last year -- 94 per cent of it , in fact -- is conveniently disposed of in incinerators and the ash dumped in a purpose built trash island just off the coast . <p> Out of sight @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ declines to take serious measures to reduce plastic consumption , the bigger the conspiracy theories get as to why the country is doing so little . <p> One is that pro-business Singapore does not want to upset the companies that make the stuff.Petrochemicals giants Shell and ExxonMobil have been in Singapore since before she was even a country -- 208 years of history between them . <p> Another is that Singapore 's incinerators rely on the high calorific value of plastic to burn trash more efficiently . <p> Another theory , according to a source who works at one of the plants , is that Singapore counts on high volumes of plastic waste to offset the high cost of the incinerators , since revenue is generated from fees collected from waste brought to the plants . <p> But the most likely reason is the political cost of a plastic tax -- the uproar that imposing even a small charge on plastic items would stir among a cost-conscious public . <p> The big supermarkets have tried to introduce a plastic bag charge before , and are now dead set against the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Australian supermarkets after a spat of " bag rage " in July prompted retailers to temporarily waive a 15 cents charge for a plastic bag . <p> Many Singaporeans feel that they are entitled to free plastic bags .
