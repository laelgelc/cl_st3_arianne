<h> U.S. report to detail costs of climate change as Trump downplays threat <p> U.S. President Donald Trump takes questions from the media after speaking via teleconference with troops from Mar-a-Lago estate in Palm Beach , Florida , U.S. , November 22 , 2018 . REUTERS/Eric Thayer <p> ( Reuters ) - The U.S. government on Friday will issue a new report detailing the threat climate change poses to the economy , in what is expected to be a dire warning at odds with the Trump administration 's pro-fossil fuels agenda . <p> The congressionally mandated report , written with the help of more than a dozen U.S. government agencies and departments , will supplement a study issued last year that concluded humans are the main driver of global warming , and warned of potentially catastrophic effects to the planet . <p> " It is extremely likely that human influence has been the dominant cause of the observed warming since the mid-20th century , " according to that report . " There is no convincing alternative explanation ... " <p> This year 's report is expected to detail costs of climate change and provide scientific assessments of the risks @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> The report is expected clash with policy under President Donald Trump , who has been rolling back environmental and climate protections to maximize production of domestic fossil fuels , including crude oil , already the highest in the world above Saudi Arabia and Russia . <p> Trump last year announced his intention to withdraw the United States from the 2015 Paris Deal agreed by nearly 200 nations to combat climate change , arguing the accord would hurt the U.S. economy and provide little tangible environmental benefit . Under the Paris pact 's rules , however , Trump can not officially withdraw from the agreement until after the 2020 U.S. presidential election . The U.S. government still sends delegations to U.N . climate negotiations . <p> Trump and several members of his cabinet have also repeatedly cast doubt on the science of climate change , saying the causes are not yet settled . <p> That is despite the fact an overwhelming majority of climate scientists around the globe agree that the burning of fossil fuels drives global warming , and is leading to rising seas , flooding , droughts @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , including from U.S. government scientists , has concluded that climate change could have severe economic consequences , including damage to infrastructure , water supplies and agriculture . <p> Severe weather and other impacts also increase the risk of disease transmission , decrease air quality , and can increase mental health problems , among other effects , according to U.S. and international studies . <p> Thirteen government departments and agencies , from the Agriculture Department to NASA , were part of the committee that compiled the new report .
