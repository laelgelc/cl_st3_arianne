<h> After six months ' recess ' , lobsters are up for grabs off Dege Beach <p> AN increase in the population of endangered marine animal species in the Indian Ocean has led to the first catches of marine invertebrates like prawns and lobsters in seven years along Dege beach in Kigamboni , Dar es Salaam , according to the World Wildlife Fund ( WWF ) . <p> WWF is implementing a conservation program involving the closure of fishing activities in specified areas along the Indian Ocean coastline as a means of boosting the populations of such marine invertebrates . <p> The fund 's fisheries co-management program also incorporates local communities with the aim of conserving marine resources and environs , and giving space to marine animals to multiply . <p> The marine invertebrates have now reproduced after almost a decade of scarcity caused by illegal fishing and other threats . <p> And yesterday , WWF reopened the popular Dege beach in Kigamboni for fishing activities after closing it for six months , where for the first time in the past seven years a significant lobster catch was made . <p> During the period , surrounding communities mounted up @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ at the area . years ago . <p> " There is a massive increase in fish stock overall ... the programme has also led to a sharp decrease in blast fishing , " said Mohamed . <p> He explained that previously , they were used to hearing blasts at least ten times a day , but such incidences have become rarer and rarer and now they can stay a year without hearing the sound of a blast . <p> According to Mohamed , yesterday 's lobster catch is an indication that the animals are now matured enough to be captured . <p> Previously , the lobsters were being fished out of the ocean waters at premature ages , thus
