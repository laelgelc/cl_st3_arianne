EPA-EFE/SERGEI ILNITSKY <p> The levels of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere , the main driver of climate change , have hit a new record high , the UN said Thursday , warning that the time to act was running out . <p> " Without rapid cuts in CO2 and other greenhouse gases , climate change will have increasingly destructive and irreversible impacts on life on Earth , " the head of the World Meteorological Organization Petteri Taalas said in a statement . <p> " The window of opportunity for action is almost closed . " <p> The Greenhouse Gas Bulletin , the UN weather agency 's annual flagship report , tracks @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ post-industrial era ( since 1750 ) . <p> This year 's report , which covers data for 2017 , puts the concentration of CO2 in the atmosphere at 405.5 parts per million ( ppm ) . <p> That is up from 403.3 ppm in 2016 and 400.1 ppm in 2015 . <p> " The last time the Earth experienced a comparable concentration of CO2 was 3-5 million years ago , when the temperature was 2-3 ? C warmer , " Taalas said . <p> Researchers have reliable estimates of C02 concentrations rates going back 800,000 years using air bubbles preserved in ice in places like Greenland and Antarctica . <p> But by studying fossilised material the WMO also has rough CO2 estimates going back up to three to five million years . <p> In addition to CO2 , the UN agency also highlighted rising levels of methane , nitrous oxide and another powerful ozone depleting gas known as CFC-11 .
