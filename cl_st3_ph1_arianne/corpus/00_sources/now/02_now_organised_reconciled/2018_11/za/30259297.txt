<h> Turn Africa 's cities into vertical farms <h> COMMENT <p> African cities , according to a World Bank report , are home to 472-million people -- almost half of Africa 's total population . This number is expected to double by 2050 . <p> One would expect these African cities to serve as hubs of productivity and hotspots of innovation where solutions to Africa 's problems , such as poverty , hunger and food insecurity , are born , incubated and implemented . One would also expect them to drive economic development and put the continent on a path towards achieving the United Nations ' sustainable development goals . <p> Yet residents of these rapidly urbanising cities face problems such as rising poverty , hunger , food insecurity and unemployment . Such difficulties generate stress for people , but also political , economic and environmental upheavals . These cities are ticking time bombs . <p> Nigeria , for example , was recently reported to have overtaken India as the country with the largest number of people living in extreme poverty . It is regarded as the sector that offers the greatest potential for reducing poverty and inequality . <p> African cites should consider the value of vertical farming .
