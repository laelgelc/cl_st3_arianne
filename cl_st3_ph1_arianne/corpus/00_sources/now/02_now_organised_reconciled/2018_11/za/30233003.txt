<h> Africa the odd one out at G20 summit <p> German Chancellor Angela Merkel , centre , opens a meeting as part of the ' G20 Compact with Africa Conference ' at the chancellery in Berlin last month . The blatant exclusion of African states in decisions affecting the global economy and other world issues is palpable , says the writer . File picture : John MacDougall/AP <p> Johannesburg - Overwhelmingly outnumbered is the sentiment for the upcoming summit in Buenos Aires , from an African perspective . South Africa is , as usual , the solitary African voice at the G20 summit where the world 's more " industrialised " ( read Western ) powers will discuss global matters . <p> In line with tradition criticised since the 1884 Berlin Conference , the major economies meet to discuss economic growth ; sustainable use of natural resources and climate change ; and promoting equal opportunities and inclusiveness . <p> The Berlin Conference was viewed , owing mostly due to Basil Davidson 's documentary , The Magnificent African Cake , as a crime against humanity because of its reduction of an entire continent 's people to unwilling participants in European @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ states in decisions affecting the global economy and other world issues is palpable . Each continent seems to have relatively adequate representation with at least two G20 member states , North America and Europe , having the lion 's share . <p> The clearly one-sided global directive coming out of these engagements is deliberate , because a significant addition of an AU to feign balance would provide support for the lowly South African voice . However , it would disturb the stage-hogging giants . <p> Weeks after the G20 curtain-raiser summit for finance ministers and Monetary Fund Governors of the G20 nations , the now official communications channel from the White House ( the daily tweet from the master bedroom lavatory ) , sent out a message about recent positive talks regarding trade with China . <p> The message was echoed by President Xi Jinping on CGTN about his openness to meet the US and foster a mutually beneficial partnership in the G20 's corridors . <p> These pre-emptive engagements are to be concluded in Argentina in a climate of diplomacy . The global markets reacted almost immediately after a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ where the US subjected Chinese products to tariffs to the tune of $250billion ( R3.5trillion ) . China responded with tariffs of its own worth $110bn . The Asian and US markets showed gains , indicating conflict and protectionism are not only anti-development but also bad for business . <p> The US and China trade relations will surely eclipse the more politically correct items on the Buenos Aires agenda - global profits and revenue maximisation tend to do more for informing international policy than people 's needs . <p> This is especially concerning for the sole African participant at the summit because the rhetoric of improving people 's lives rings no truer than in Africa . This continent is the most desperate for development and inclusive growth enjoyed by the rest of the world , but sadly is the most ostracised . The G20 's structure and mandate prove this . <p> A contingency plan at the 2017 Hamburg Summit was the G20 Compact with Africa ( CwA ) during the G20 's German presidency . It truly seems that profits garner more attention than people . <p> So , in Argentina , Ramaphosa will have to balance national interest and the African agenda .
