<h> Renewable Energy <h> Stop-start government approach puts a spoke in the wheel of renewable energy boom <p> By Melanie Gosling* 11 November 2018 <p> South Africa 's renewable energy programme has the potential to become the country 's single biggest infrastructure project , bringing in R25bn a year in direct foreign investment , delegates heard at the Windaba wind energy conference in Cape Town . But there are obstacles to this being realised . One is the debilitating ' stop-start ' nature of the government 's renewable energy programme . <p> At a recent conference in Cape Town , there was agreement that if the " stop-start " nature of the government 's renewable energy programme does not change , the industry will not fly and thousands of jobs and millions in foreign investment will not be realised . <p> The renewable energy industry is growing rapidly worldwide , driven largely by falling prices . It now accounts for 25% of global electricity generation . <p> South Africa 's fledgling industry started out well . " <p> With the change of administration and President Cyril Ramaphosa 's appointment of Jeff Radebe as energy minister , the renewable energy programme got back on track and government earlier this year signed up 27 new renewable energy projects . <p> But the two-year hiatus had harmed the industry . <p> Some of the smaller companies had closed up shop , and a local renewable energy supply chain industry was unable to get off the ground . <p> Now , just after the renewable energy programme seems to be back on track , another hiatus is looming on the horizon -- this time @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the form of the government 's draft Integrated Resource Plan ( IRP ) , which lays out the country 's future electricity mix until 2030 . <p> While the draft IRP makes provision for more wind energy to come online during this time , it does not do so in an incremental , year-on-year manner .
