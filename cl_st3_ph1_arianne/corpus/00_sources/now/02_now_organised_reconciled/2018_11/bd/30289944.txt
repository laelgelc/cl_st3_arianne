<p> The research by scientists at Harvard and Yale universities , published in the journal Environmental Research Letters , proposes using a technique known as stratospheric aerosol injection , which they say could cut the rate of global warming in half , reports CNN . <p> The technique would involve spraying large amounts of sulfate particles into the Earth 's lower stratosphere at altitudes as high as 12 miles . The scientists propose delivering the sulfates with specially designed high-altitude aircraft , balloons or large naval-style guns . <p> Despite the technology being undeveloped and with no existing aircraft suitable for adaptation , the researchers say that " developing a new , purpose-built tanker with substantial payload capabilities would neither be technologically difficult nor prohibitively expensive . " <p> They estimate the total cost of launching a hypothetical system in 15 years ' time at around $3.5 billion , with running costs of $2.25 billion a year over a 15-year period . <p> The report does , however , acknowledge that the technique is purely hypothetical . <p> " We make no judgment about the desirability of SAI , " the report states .
