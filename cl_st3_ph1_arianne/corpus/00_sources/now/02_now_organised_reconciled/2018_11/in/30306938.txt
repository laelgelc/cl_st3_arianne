<h> Globally , 153 billion hours of labour were lost due to heat exposure in 2017 . <p> India lost nearly 75 billion hours of labour in 2017 , according to 2018 Lancet report on health and climate change . <p> The country lost about 43,000 million hours in 2000 . This means India saw an increase of over 30,000 million hours of labour lost over two decades . <p> Globally , 153 billion hours of labour were lost due to heat exposure in 2017 , an increase of 62 billion hours relative to 2000 . In addition , these changes were concentrated in already vulnerable areas in India , southeast Asia , and sub-Saharan Africa , and South America . <p> Around 80 per cent of these losses were in the agricultural sector ( 122 billion hours lost ) , 17.5 per cent were in the industry sector ( 27 billion ) , and 2.5 per cent were in the service sector ( 4 billion ) . <p> Rising temperatures are a risk in occupational health , and as temperatures regularly increase above physiological limits , sustained work becomes more difficult or impossible . <p> " Vulnerability to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , " said Joacim Rocklov , at Umea University in Sweden . <p> " This has led to vast losses for national economies and household budgets .
