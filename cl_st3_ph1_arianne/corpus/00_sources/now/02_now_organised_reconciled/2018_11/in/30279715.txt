Indian social activism has assumed gigantic proportions , but often , emotive activism , devoid of objectivity , derails the smooth functioning of institutions of governance and creates confusion . The latest in a series of such instances is the shooting of a man-eating Tigress in Maharashtra 's Yavatmal district . For the past few weeks , animal rights activists and organisations have been castigating the Maharashtra Government for the killing of Tigress Avni in Pandharkawada forests of Maharashtra 's Yavatmal district . They have charged the forest department for her cold-blodded killing , so much so that Union Minister Maneka Gandhi dubbed her killing as a straight case of crime and raised the hackle by demanding the sacking of Maharashtra Forest Minister Sudhir Mungantiwar . <p> The State Government on November 10 set up an inquiry committee @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ examine the facts from the point of view of rule of law as also from the point of view of overall animal welfare , moral and professional ethos . <p> Tigress Avni was eliminated on the night of November 2 , 2018 , with help from a sharp shooter , Asgar Ali . During the last two years , the tigress killed 13 people . Due to public pressure , the Chief Wildlife Warden seemed to have ordered that the big cat be either captured or killed as she was found to be dangerous for human life . Even the Mumbai High Court , after initial refusal , allowed the shooting . The Supreme Court , however , refrained from interfering . <p> Strictly speaking , the forest department was within the legal boundaries to neutralise the man-eating tigress .
