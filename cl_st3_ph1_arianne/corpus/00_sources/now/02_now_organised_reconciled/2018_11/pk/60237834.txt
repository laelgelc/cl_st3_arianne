The recently released Statistical Update on Human Development in 189 countries provides new information and points to ways to act on those priorities . <p> As measured by the Human Development Index , people 's lives have improved in Pakistan , with the HDI increasing from 0.404 in 1990 to 0.562 in 2018 . This is a rate higher than average for developing countries . <p> While Pakistan progressed from a " low human development country " in 1990 to a " medium human development country " today , the rate of progress is slower than in other countries in South Asia , eg Nepal , which has overtaken Pakistan , jumping to 149th place . <p> The critical dimensions measured in the HDI are health , education and income . Regarding income , Pakistan is ahead of its neighbours : its per capita Gross National Income of $5,311 is among the highest in South Asia . But on measures of education and health , Pakistan is lagging . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 8.6 years is lower than India 's 12.3 years . Among regional countries , Nepal has the highest public expenditure on health at 6 per cent of GDP . Pakistan 's public expenditure on education stands at 2.2pc -- higher than Bangladesh 's but lower than both Nepal 's and India 's . <p> But it is not just the amount spent that impacts human development , it is also quality . The 2017 census shows an annual growth of 2.4pc , significantly above the earlier believed rate of 1.9pc . Nepal 's population growth rate is 1.1pc , India 's 1.2pc and Bangladesh 's 1.1pc . This means that for Pakistan to increase its share of people with access to education and health services , it has to move faster than its neighbours . It also means there are unsustainable strains on scarce resources such as water when Pakistan also has to adapt to the rapid effects of climate change . <p> The statistics also show that rising inequality is causing slow growth in human development . There are reasons for this . One is gender . Pakistan has a Gender Inequality Index value of 0.541 and is ranked 133rd out of 160 countries . These disparities exist across all three dimensions of human development . The expected years of schooling for women are 7.8 against 9.4 for men . The lagging areas , therefore , need to increase public investments and access to social services .
