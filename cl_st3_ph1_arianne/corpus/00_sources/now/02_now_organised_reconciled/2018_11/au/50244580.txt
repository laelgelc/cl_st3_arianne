<h> UN report reveals Paris Agreement targets now out of reach <p> Global efforts to tackle climate change are falling dangerously short according to a new United Nations report that details the first rise in CO2 emissions in four years . <p> The UN 's emissions gap report has found that economic growth is responsible for a rise in emissions in 2017 while national efforts to cut carbon have faltered . <p> By the end of this century , the world is forecast to experience many of the worst case scenarios that this target was hoping to avert , the report warned . <p> Global temperatures are expected to rise to 3.2 degrees Celsius by 2100 based on the current emission targets for all countries . <p> That would accelerate the sea level rise , wreak havoc on the world 's economy , and bring about dire consequences on human health . <p> The report shows that there is a great gap between individual country contributions to the Paris Climate Agreement and what will be necessary to keep global warming well below 2 degrees Celsius over pre-industrial temperatures . <p> " Global peaking of emissions by 2020 is crucial @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " the report stated , " but the scale and pace of current action remains insufficient . " <p> The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) gave similar findings in its October " Special Report on 1.5 Degrees " , which showed that the world urgently needed to bridge this gap and that time was quickly running out . <p> The world emitted 1 billion tons of CO2 , the largest ever amount in history , the UN report revealed . <p> Global emissions need to be 25 per cent lower than this figure by 2030 in order to limit warming to 2 degrees Celsius and 55 per cent lower in order to limit to 1.5 degrees , the report claimed . <p> It outlined that countries must increase cutting CO2 emissions by three times in order to meet the 2 degrees goal and by five times to meet the 1.5 degrees goal by 2030 . <p> Australia is listed as a G20 country that will not meet its 2030 target -- alongside Canada , Argentina , the European Union , South Korea , Saudi Arabia , South @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ agreement , Australia aims to reduce emissions by 26 to 28 per cent based on 2005 levels . <p> The authors of the report concluded that this was unlikely to happen and pointed to smaller governing bodies and private entities to help countries reduce gas emissions . <p> Environmental figures will meet next week as part of COP24 to establish a new plan to combat climate change on the back of this report .
