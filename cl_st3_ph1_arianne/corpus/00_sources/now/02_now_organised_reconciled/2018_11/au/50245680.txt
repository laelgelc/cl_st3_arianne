<h> In Jordan , Flash Floods are a Dramatic Reminder of Environmental Challenges <p> In October and November , a series of deadly floods swept across Jordan , creating one of its worst natural disasters in decades . Sudden deluges in Petra , Amman and near the Dead Sea were responsible for dozens of deaths , while thousands of tourists were forced to flee Petra . The floods have come as heavy rains have impacted much of the Middle East , causing flooding in countries such as Saudi Arabia and Kuwait . Although November is usually the start of the region 's rainy season , the frequency and intensity of the rain has made the flood risk especially dangerous . <p> Comment <p> Despite generally being known as an arid , water scarce country , Jordan frequently experiences heavy flooding , especially in the south . These floods generally occur during the transition between seasons , typically October-November and March-May . Alarmingly , the frequency of the strong rains that cause flash floods has increased in recent years . This trend is likely to continue , as Jordan 's climate is particularly sensitive to climate change . During these periods , the intensity and frequency of heavy rain is likely to increase , making flash floods more likely . <p> The Jordanian climate and geography combine to make flash flooding possible , but political and administrative mismanagement and regional instabilities have significantly exacerbated the risk . In the immediate aftermath of this year 's floods , the Tourism and Education Ministers offered their resignations , due to the perceived roles their ministries played in the deaths caused by the floods . While they were convenient political scapegoats , other elements of the government are similarly culpable , due to a lack of planning and a failure to have the necessary infrastructure in place to mitigate the impact of natural disasters . <p> Rapid population growth , which is partly driven by an influx of refugees from neighbouring countries , has caused urban areas to increase in size , with little planning or oversight . Many public works in Jordan are undertaken without proper geological or hydrological feasibility studies ; consequently , many urban areas have been built in valleys and other flood-prone areas . <p> Agriculture also amplifies the impacts of flash flooding ; in part , because of upstream deforestation . Importantly , the factors that increase the severity of flash floods have also degraded Jordan 's agricultural sector . Sprawling urbanisation has converted agricultural land to urban and industrial use , which is made worse by a lack of national land-use plans . Though the agricultural sector has expanded in recent years , limited availability of land and water has curtailed its growth and its sensitivity to climate change may threaten it further . <p> A sudden increase in population has also seriously challenged Jordan 's food security .
