She knows exactly what her cotton-growing neighbour sees when he leans over her rickety fence with that courteous " Good morning " . He longs for her death . Then he can buy-up her property as he has just about every other old property in the district and plant more of the water-gulping cotton . <p> Esther drives her old Dodge across her land enjoying and understanding the naturalness of the scrubby areas , the useless outcrops . She farms as her grandparents farmed but she is fully aware of her last-century life and knows it has to change . But while she 's on her feet she has agency and she intends to make the best of use of her time . Thank heavens she is fond of Mitch . <p> Rosalie Ham 's love affair with the Australian landscape is a heartbeat on every page . She 's at her best seeing through the eyes of Mitch who could never be anything but the farmer he is . He loves the paddocks cut with the no-nonsense irrigation channels , loves the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the brown water ripping down the channels , loves the feel of hard work in his body after a day drenching the sheep . <p> What he does n't love is the water authority , an official organisation rife with crooked people always with an eye to their own gain . Water means nothing less than life in the country and the only reason these farmers have been able to make a living is because of irrigation , because of those beautiful channels and those clever wheels . With climate change , with the corporatisation of water and the big money makers from the city determined to make it profitable , farming can never again be simple . <p> The love story between Mitch and his old flame Neralie gives richness to the story but Ham 's novel is really a heartfelt disclosure of what it is to be a farmer aiming to make a living off the land in years of drought . The prospect of climate change glowers behind every tiny decision . <p> Ham 's voice is never sentimental , it is sardonic and frequently mean . @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ The Year of the Farmer seems destined to grip readers the way the radio serial Blue Hills did in the 1950s when lunch was timed so that everyone could come in to listen , when " Shush , Blue Hills is on " echoed around the country for a decade . It is also like watching any episode of Australian Story . Subtlety is n't a motivator for Ham . She observes a world that is changing as human nature remains unchanged . So the farmer bringing figs to Cleopatra Kardashian herself could well be Mitch . You have to wish him luck . And more .
