<p> Yes , carbon . That pesky element so often talked about alongside climate change . But what does the carbon in your grandma 's lamingtons have to do with climate change ? Well , depending on where your leftovers go , quite a bit . <h> GASBAGGING <p> All that carbon in your food is found in different kinds of molecules like carbohydrates , proteins and fats . When the carbon in your food goes to landfill , microorganisms break them down . This decomposition process produces methane gas . <p> View LargerFood is made of carbon-based molecules like carbohydrates , proteins and fats . Methane traps significantly more heat than carbon dioxide , the most infamous greenhouse gas . That 's bad news for Planet Earth and everyone on it . <h> COMPOSTING TO THE RESCUE <p> Luckily , this problem can be solved with some clever science . While SMRC has been composting organic waste ( collected from general mixed waste ) for 15 years , problems with contamination have reduced the quality of compost . <p> SMRC is now trialling the Food Organics Garden Organics ( FOGO ) program with the City of Melville where 7000 homes separate their food waste out from general waste . Instead of just dumping food waste into their main bin , homeowners separate their food waste into a small kitchen caddy with a compostable liner made of cornstarch . <p> This makes it easy for homeowners to put all their organic material -- both from the kitchen and garden -- in one bin for composting . <p> " On average , between 20% and 30% of waste collected through kerbside bins could be classified as food waste , " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ much cleaner stream of organic waste , resulting in the production of a higher-quality compost while reducing processing costs . The only difference is that , by controlling the chemistry a little better , it ensures the process does n't produce methane . <p> Imagine you 're making a cake . If you want to win the blue ribbon at the Royal Show , you need to make sure you 've got the ingredients just right . It 's the same with composting . And the vital ingredient for clean composting is oxygen . <p> Keeping a supply of oxygen to the composting process stops methane from being produced . But if organic material is starved of oxygen by being buried underground in landfill -- well , the ground gets gassy . <p> ... and ensures the final compost is a rich , nutritious meal for plants in in future . <p> SMRC 's composting process also ends up with a mix @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ are great at helping plants grow , meaning your composted kitchen waste can be used back on the farm to grow your next meal . <h> DOES IT WORK ? <p> Does it ever ! Since SMRC 's Waste Composting Facility was introduced in 2003 , it has produced substantial environmental benefits . In 2017/18 alone it processed approximately 78,000 tonnes of waste and diverted 39,000 tonnes from landfill . That has saved 32,000 tonnes of greenhouse gases from going into the air . <p> " This is the equivalent of taking 7000 cars off the road , " says Tim . <p> By properly separating out organic food and garden waste , SMRC is aiming to divert even more organic material from landfill , further reducing greenhouse gas emissions . <h> SCALING UP <p> Of course , it 's still a challenge to pull this off on a council-wide scale . <p> Like all great ideas , we 're glad to see that this one is bound to go further .
