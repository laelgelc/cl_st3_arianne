<h> Turning pollution into profit <h> Add to ... <p> Maori landowners are seeking new opportunities to reduce greenhouse gas and carbon emissions . At the same time , they are finding ways to turn pollution into profits by planting trees . <p> The move comes amid new international warnings that rising temperatures driven by greenhouse gas emissions will worsen climate disasters by 2030 . <p> Pastoral farms account for half of New Zealand 's greenhouse gas emissions , much of it methane from cows and sheep flatulence . However , farms are also one of our largest export sectors and vital to the Maori economy . <p> Te Uranga B2 is a Maori Trust near Taumarunui which has two dairy farms and a beef and sheep unit . <p> Twenty-five years ago Te Uranga B2 chairman Derek Wooster and his team laid the foundations for clean farming on their land . They have reduced stock numbers and nitrogen fertiliser . <p> They also set aside 117 hectares to protect native ecosystems on their property . This creates carbon credits- the dollar value earned from each tonne of greenhouse gases stored in a tree . <p> Carbon credits can be sold for up to NZD$25 per tonne of carbon dioxide to polluters- to absorb or offset their carbon emissions . <p> For Maori landowners , their roles as kaitiaki are a natural fit in the fight against climate change . <p> On the East Coast near Ruatoria , another land incorporation has been planning for the future . <p> Ten years ago , Ngati Porou Forestry planted a forest sink on mostly marginal land on the East Coast . Their goal was to absorb carbon dioxide and trade their carbon credits to polluters . <p> Today their foresight is taking off .
