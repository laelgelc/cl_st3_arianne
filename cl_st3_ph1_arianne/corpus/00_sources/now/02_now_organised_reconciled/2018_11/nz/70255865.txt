<h> Editorial : Unsettled spring is not that unusual <p> A photograph taken on Sunday at 3.50pm , five minutes north of Ashburton , showing the massive tornado . <p> EDITORIAL : What on earth has happened to our spring , for heaven 's sake ? <p> Plainly , your experience of the season so far will vary , depending on what part of the country you live in , but that might well be a question that has been on the lips of many , in a range of locations . <p> The answer , it seems , may be a slightly qualified " not as much as you think " . <p> The word " unsettled " has been widely used . Unusually high November rainfall has hit some parts of the country , with lower than normal temperatures , and hail has been widespread , from the lower South Island , where it made some stretches of road look like they had been snowed on , to the lower reaches of the North Island . It hailed in our capital on Monday afternoon . A couple of weather systems bringing actual snow down to low levels @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> On Sunday afternoon , there were widespread reports , accompanied by some dramatic video footage , of a tornado hitting farming country north of Ashburton . There was some debate about whether or not the dramatic funnel formation stretching earthwards from heavy cloud was actually a tornado , or a large " dust devil " , but meteorological opinion seems to have settled conclusively on the former . Certainly the thunderstorm activity that is required for a tornado to develop was present . <p> All of that certainly makes our weather sound fairly unsettled , so it might be surprising to hear the analysis from Metservice duty meteorologist Tui McInnes on Monday that the most unusual weather feature of the spring so far was actually the high-pressure system that produced settled weather for much of the country last week . <p> Low-pressure systems , and thus fairly unsettled weather , are typical for spring , he said . <p> " In Wellington , October and November are the strongest wind months , by a reasonable degree . " <p> What had been " quite unusual " was the snow @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ level , and even lower in some areas . It was " not unheard of " , but not common . " The cold temperatures are the most unusual aspect of the spring weather . " <p> Naturally , and especially coming so soon after a major report released by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) , our spring weather has prompted debate about climate change . We 've been told more extreme weather events , particularly flooding -- which is tied to sea level rise , making coastal communities more vulnerable -- and drought , are to be expected . Which is why the need to limit the increase in global temperatures is so urgent . <p> But in the wake of Sunday 's Mid Canterbury event , McInnes says the country is not seeing an increase in the frequency of tornadoes , which still represent a " small-scale phenomenon " in New Zealand . <p> " Meteorologically , they have n't increased , " he said . Factors such as population increases and the ease of communications -- such as the ability of people to @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ meaning news of them spread further -- outweighed " anything else measurable " with regards to tornado activity , he said . So there 's no need to rush off and build a tornado bunker . <p> Forecasts indicate temperatures may return to more normal levels by next week , as the onset of summer approaches . <p> Of course , this is all rather short-term , so while some relief from unsettled weather conditions may be ahead , our long-term climate challenges have n't changed .
