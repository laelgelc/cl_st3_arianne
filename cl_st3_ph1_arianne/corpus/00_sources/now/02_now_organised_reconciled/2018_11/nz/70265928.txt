<h> Vanuatu could take legal action against climate change culprits <p> Vanuatu is putting the fossil fuel industry and the states that sponsor it on notice . <p> The government of Vanuatu is looking at ways to take legal action against corporations and companies that cause climate change . Photo : Laszlo Konya/ 123rf <p> In what could be world-first legal action by a climate-vulnerable country , Vanuatu 's Foreign Minister Ralph Regenvanu said his country is considering legal action against the world 's most polluting fossil fuel corporations and countries . <p> His comments came during the Climate Vulnerable Forum 's Virtual Summit , which is taking place online . <p> Vanuatu , which is home to 260,000 people , is made up of 82 volcanic islands across 1,280km of sea . Many of the islands sit less than a metre above sea level . <p> " The injustice of climate change is that the impacts are felt first and hardest by those with the least responsibility for its causes , " Mr Regenvanu said . <p> " Vanuatu is on the front lines of climate change and yet we have benefited least from the exploitation of fossil @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ therefore today putting the fossil fuel industry , and the states that sponsor it , on notice that the climate loss and damages ravaging Vanuatu will not go unchallenged . " <p> The Vanuatu government is exploring ways to use the judicial system , possibly under international law , to shift the costs of climate protection back onto fossil fuel companies , financial institutions and governments that " created this existential threat to Vanuatu " . <p> People from Peru , the Philippines and several American cities , counties and states are also seeking to hold oil , gas and coal companies accountable over climate change , but Vanuatu could be the first nation state to do so .
