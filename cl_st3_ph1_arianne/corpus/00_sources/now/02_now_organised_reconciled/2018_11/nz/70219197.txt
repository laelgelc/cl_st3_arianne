<h> Oil and gas ban passes into law - Greenpeace <p> Greenpeace today welcomes the passing of legislation that puts the ban on new oil and gas permits into law . <p> The Crown Minerals ( Petroleum ) Amendment Act , which passed last night 63 to 55 , formalises the Coalition Government 's April announcement that there will be no new offshore permits available to oil and gas companies . <p> Greenpeace Climate and Energy Campaigner , Kate Simcock , says this is a huge win for the climate and for people power , and it is a law New Zealand should be proud of passing . <p> " Today we have passed an incredibly important law for the global climate . This law means that around four million square kilometres of the earth 's surface is now off limits to oil and gas companies , and any deposits under our deep seas will stay in the ground where they belong , " she says . <p> " The science is very clear - we only have 10 years to halve our use of oil , gas , and coal or face the displacement of millions of people @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and mass species extinction . That 's going to mean massive change - not business as usual - and we need to be prepared to take bold steps like this to protect humanity and the planet . <p> " As a nation , we should be very proud of being one of the first countries in the world to ban climate-destroying oil exploration . Just like when New Zealand became nuclear free , we have taken an important stand for our principles and our future . " <p> Simcock says there was clear public mandate for the legislation banning new oil and gas permits . <p> " Over the past eight years , tens of thousands of New Zealanders have demanded an end to offshore oil and gas , and this year the Government has listened . The desire for our Government to show leadership on climate change is clear from the overwhelming public support for this Bill , " she says . <p> The Bill was opposed by the National Party and was heavily debated in the house .
