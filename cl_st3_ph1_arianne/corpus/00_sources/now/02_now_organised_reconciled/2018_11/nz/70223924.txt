<h> Decarbonise , decentralise and digitalise <p> Victoria University of Wellington 's Professor Alan Brent assesses how well New Zealand is doing with the trinity of energy priorities necessary to tackle global warming . <p> The October release of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) special report paints a stark reality of what is to come if we , as a civilisation , do not make curb global warming to below 1.5 ? C and reach net zero CO2 emissions by 2050 . The IPCC report argues for ambitious policy support to transform how we use energy , including doubling our investment in low-carbon ( renewable ) energy technologies and our energy efficiency over the next 20 years , and decreasing our investment in fossil-fuel extraction and conversion by a quarter . <p> So how does New Zealand stack up against this challenge , and what should be our priorities ? However , a number of challenges must still be addressed . <p> A first priority is to facilitate more electricity decentralisation . Electricity decentralisation is shaping up to be the dominant energy sector macro theme over the next two decades . It also enables more consumers to become prosumers , by using , for example , solar panels . Contact Energy is already investigating this potential market development in Wellington . <p> Victoria University of Wellington 's research in the built environment have shown that commercial buildings and industrial sites can be significant market players in this area , or , at the very least , can reduce much of the ( morning and evening ) electricity peak demands , and we @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ can play in the electricity market . <p> As highlighted by the Productivity Commission report , a second priority is the transformation of the transportation sector . A key focus for this area is switching use of light transportation , using internal combustion engines , to higher use of electric vehicles . <p> This will have major implications for the electricity grid , which Transpower has analysed , and it will have to be controlled carefully with smart grid technology -- an area that is , fortunately , a key focus for engineering research in New Zealand . Where we have n't reached consensus is what to do with heavy transport and aviation , which our economy ( like any other ) highly <p> depends on . For KiwiRail to switch from electric to diesel locomotives just does n't make sense -- unless , of course , they operate on 100% biodiesel .
