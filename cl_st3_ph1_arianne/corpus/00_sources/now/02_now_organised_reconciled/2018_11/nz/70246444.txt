<h> Bring back science , for it holds the dam truth <p> Morgan Williams says reducing certain risks by providing adequate water storage is what the 17-year Community Dam project has been all about . <p> OPINION : We appear to have forgotten about the science . Maybe because it is boring , or complicated , or we have been distracted by the noise , but we must come back to some science if we are to see sense in the dam conversation . <p> In an interview on RNZ National recently a mountain climber talked of climbers as the true climate change believers , with global warming dangerously changing their well-mapped routes . Our regional growers are similar to these climbers , as they too have experienced the impacts of our now rapidly changing climate first hand . For several decades they have often lead debates on water storage needs and options -- not because they are the only ones who need water storage to smooth out the increasingly variable rainfall , but because they have already felt the pain that the rest of us are yet to , but will , experience first-hand . <p> This climate @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ report on global warming highlighted that our 1.0C warmer world is already seeing more extreme weather ; floods , droughts , sea level rises and other major changes - many shown daily on our TVs . It called , yet again , for vastly more effort to limit future increases to 1.5C . It stressed that people and ecosystems need room to adapt - because it 's now clear we must adapt . <p> Tim Cuff <p> Tasman resident and former Parliamentary Commissioner for the Environment Morgan Williams <p> Meanwhile , Westpac 's Climate Change Impact Report published earlier this year highlighted a need for the physical impacts of climate change to be better understood and planned for . By planning for a storage dam now , we are ahead of the game , but only just . <p> The common thread in all calls for action on climate change is that they are based on vast amounts of research and sound evidence of the stark risks we face . For example , a report on drought security provided to Nelson City Council recently was considered to be ' politically driven ' -- two words that are easier to latch onto and believe than a 20,000-word report . It 's a responsibility of our politicians to consider evidence relevant to the risks their communities face and take the opportunities for actions needed . Attempts to flippantly discredit proven research pose major risks to collective decision-making when dealing with a threat as large as climate change . <p> A common thread in climate research is that increased rainfall variability , as our planet 's atmosphere warms , causes more droughts and more floods . Both disrupt water supplies and cause damage , but droughts can have much greater impacts because we depend on reliable water supplies for so many things in our daily lives . <p> With drought , we are not talking about the familiar and normal summer restrictions on water use that pose the big risks , @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in processing facilities , businesses with public health requirements ( cafes ) or the production of vegetables and fruits . Facilities such as hospitals and rest homes will require emergency supplies . These can all be very costly - both short and long term - as highlighted in submissions to the Parliamentary Select Committee considering the local bill . <p> Reducing certain risks by providing adequate water storage is what the 17-year Community Dam project has been all about . <p> We all know water storage in various forms is a solution to drought in combination with efficient use . Riverside pond storage and household water tanks have a place , but they address very different scales of water supply risks compared to a dam that can support our existing large natural water system and ecological and community needs . <p> Sound science shows that the Waimea aquifers are the plains ' main water tank , fed by the Waimea River and plains rainfall . By sharing costs and risks with landowners , Central Government and Nelson City , it is possible to afford the most cost-effective large storage infrastructure . We will be ready , as recommended by proven science and as experienced firsthand by those already facing climate change impacts . <p> - Morgan Williams is a Tasman district resident and former Parliamentary Commissioner for the Environment
