<h> CAP reform ' is falling short ' of EU auditors ' ambitions <p> The proposed reform of the Common Agricultural Policy ( CAP ) after 2020 " falls short " of the EU 's ambitions for a greener and more robust performance-based approach , according to a statement published today ( Wednesday , November 7 ) , by the European Court of Auditors . <p> The auditors have identified a number of other issues with the proposal , notably in terms of accountability . <p> When the European Commission published its proposal for the new CAP after 2020 , it stressed that environment and climate objectives would be a high priority . <p> The auditors acknowledged that the proposed reform includes tools to address these objectives but say that these are neither clearly defined nor translated into quantified targets . <p> In addition , the Commission 's estimate of the CAP 's contribution to EU climate change objectives appears unrealistic , say the auditors . <p> Advertisement <p> The auditors noted that many of the proposed policy options are very similar to the current CAP . <p> The statement said that the largest part of the budget would @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a given amount of hectares of land owned or used . <p> However , the auditors believe that this instrument is not appropriate for addressing environmental concerns , nor is it the most efficient way of supporting viable income . <p> The proposal introduces key changes in the way that policy would be put into practice . <p> There is a shift from an emphasis on compliance towards a focus on performance , which the auditors have welcomed . <p> The new CAP needs more incentives for performance and objectives that are clearly linked to outputs , results and impacts , concluded the auditors .
