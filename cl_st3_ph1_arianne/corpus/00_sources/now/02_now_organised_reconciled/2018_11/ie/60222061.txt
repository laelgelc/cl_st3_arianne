When buying your veg , always seek out Irish vegetables and try to avoid the price promotions remembering that there is always a cost to cheap food down the line . <p> The basics -- growing yacon <p> Similar to potatoes , yacon is grown from tubers from last year 's crop . <p> Yacon grows two types of tubers -- the knobbly ' stem ' tubers that grow just under the surface around the stalk of the plant and look a lot like Jerusalem artichokes , and the larger , smooth edible tubers that grow outside of these . <p> The former are the ones used for propagating the following year 's crop , while the latter are for eating . <p> In the late autumn , after frost , dig the whole plant carefully -- the yield should be 5-10 large tubers per plant . Snap these off . They will store in a frost-free shed in a box of sand and will sweeten further over time . <p> Cut the stem of the plant back to about 10cm and store this ' crown ' with @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , also storing in sand . <p> Separate the knobbly tubers in spring , making sure you have a growth point on each . Plant each one into a large pot with good quality potting compost . Place the pot on a heated bench or in a sunny place in doors . <p> Plant out in the ground in May , being careful of the weather -- a good guide would be to only plant them out when you 're happy to plant out your tomatoes . Space one metre apart . Water regularly . <p> The plants can be slow to get going but in the summer will get to a height of 2m .
