<h> Election posters belong on ' scrap heap of history ' <h> Portlaoise councillors call for total ban on election posters <p> Potential candidates may have to rely on social media to promote themselves in the forthcoming elections , as local councillors have proposed a complete ban on election posters in the Portlaoise area . <p> " A throwback to a different era " which " should be consigned to the scrap heap of history " was how Cllr Noel Tuohy described the posters , at today 's meeting of the Portlaoise Municipal District . <p> Proposing a motion calling on the council to implement a no postering policy during the upcoming local and general elections , Cllr Tuohy said : " Posters come from a political era when there was only one TV station and one radio station and when Gay Byrne was the voice and conscience of the public mood . There were no mobile phones , no internet , no social media , no online platforms and in order to get the GAA , soccer or rugby fixtures you had to go out and buy the local paper . However , he added that it will only work if everyone agrees . <p> " Banning election posters wo n't save the planet , but it will help , " he said . <p> Supporting the motion , Cllr Catherine Fitzgerald said there had been a Public Participation Network meeting recently and it was voted by a huge majority to ban posters , especially within the 50km/h speed limit areas . <p> She acknowledged that new candidates may be at a disadvantage due to this , but pointed out that there had been " a lot of skullduggery " during the last election with posters being burnt or taken down .
