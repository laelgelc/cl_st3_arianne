<h> New green heating system could slash emissions from homes : climate advisers <p> Thursday , November 22 , 2018 - 12:01 AM <p> Carbon emissions from heating could be slashed by installing hybrid technology that does not require major changes to people 's homes , Government climate advisers have suggested . <p> The potential for hybrid heat pumps using electricity most of the time and then hydrogen to meet peak demand on cold winter days has been a " light bulb moment " , the Committee on Climate Change said . <p> While the Government has had success in cutting emissions from electricity , carbon from heating buildings has been a much more intractable problem because of the impact on people 's lives of changing how homes are heated . <p> But a new report from the Committee said installing hybrid heat pumps for buildings on the gas grid , along with boosting energy efficiency and making new homes more efficient , could cut emissions to near zero by 2050 . <p> Hybrid hydrogen at a later date to almost completely remove fossil fuel use in buildings . <p> And the use of the hybrid heat pumps would need far less hydrogen than a wholesale switch of the gas grid to the cleaner fuel would require . <p> It is a system which has been tested in a pilot in Bridgend , South Wales , with positive feedback from households , the committee said . <p> Hybrid heat pumps are the best of both worlds <p> The report also found hydrogen could play a role in cutting fossil fuels from heavy goods vehicles , industrial processes and to replace gas power to manage peaks in an " <p> The new analysis has found it would not be a more expensive solution than a full switch of the gas grid to hydrogen or a move over to electrifying heating . <p> Government must develop a full strategy for removing carbon emissions from heating in the next three years , including clear signals on future use of the gas grid and support for carbon capture and storage , the committee said . <p> Pilot schemes and public engagement will also be needed to communicate the need to cut carbon from heating @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ . <p> Lord Deben , chairman of the Committee on Climate Change ( CCC ) , said : " The future now rests on Government making a quick decision and fully committing to low-carbon heat within the next three years . <p> " This is important to achieving the existing 2050 emissions target , but even more important as we consider whether it is possible for the UK to reach ' net-zero ' emissions in the future .
