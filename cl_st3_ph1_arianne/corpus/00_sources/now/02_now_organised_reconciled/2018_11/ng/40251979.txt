<h> Africans Unite To Promote Food Sovereignty <p> That Africa can feed itself and the world was the crux of discussions by organic farmers , researchers , smallholder farmers , fisherfolks , faith based organisations , women and youths at the recently concluded three-day conference on the future of food systems in Africa , themed African Food Systems and the SDGs held in Dakar . RUTH TENE NATSA was there for LEADERSHIP Friday ... <p> The Conference had five thematic areas , which included urban food systems , food systems , climate change and soil , policy change on food systems in Africa , the future of food systems in an increasingly complex world and African cultural food systems . <p> The conference was organised by the Alliance for Food Sovereignty in Africa ( AFSA ) , in partnership with the Ecological Organic Agriculture Initiative for Africa ( EOA-1 ) , AfroNet and IFOAM-Organics International . <p> Speaking to LEADERSHIP , AFSA Coordinator , Dr Million Belay , said the conference was aimed at creating solidarity among participants so that Africa can move into the future together . He added , " AFSA was an open system which opened @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ programmes to help member organisations in the future . " <p> Speaking on the choice of themes , the coordinator said , " urban system was chosen because it is growing as an unplanned growth , claiming rural land , influencing what is being produced in rural areas , while the youths continue to flee to urban areas and create lots of problems in the urban areas ( including health and scarcity of available resources ) . <p> He added that the second theme , complexity shows that Africans exist in a social and ecological complexity , where no one knows what will happen next , so what kind of food system should be adopted in the continent to ensure the types of soil as well as the types of policies that we should have as well as the challenges the African food system has . <p> He noted that the conference , which was the second to be organised by the organisation , had in attendance over 70 organisations and 40 countries from Africa ( including Nigeria ) , as well as other countries from Europe . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ from all stakeholders , we have agroecology as a solution to the challenges facing Africa 's food systems . He stressed the need to further interact and partner with African government in the future . <p> Also speaking , Nigerian environmental activist and director , Health of Mother Earth Foundation ( HOMEF ) , Nnimmo Bassey , said his take away from the conference was their views at the complexities surrounding food systems and production in Africa and the many false solutions and arguments . She however , noted that their rights to food and food sovereignty are rarely linked together and if we manage to realise our rights to food , we have food sovereignty . <p> The right to food should be included in the policies and programmes development , because when people have adequate rights to food , they are able to create wealth and move forward .
