<h> Stakeholders Blame Nigeria 's Receding Waters On Coal Mining <p> As Nigeria continues its struggle to acquire alternative energy source through 30 per cent coal power generation , communities have lamented the increasing impact , coal mining has on their water sources , RUTH TENE NATSA writes <p> Executive Director , Global Rights and host community activist , Ms Abiodun Baiyewu , said mining is a water intensive activity that requires access to a lot of water , noting that the water needs of mining companies and artisanal miners will always challenge the conflicting needs for potable water of their host communities . <p> She said coal adds an added dimension to the mining , agriculture and energy dynamics , noting that while the rest of the world is divesting from coal , Nigeria has decided that it would generate 30 per cent of its energy need from coal . <p> Speaking at the stakeholder 's engagement on contextualising Nigeria 's water resource management in mining and energy policies in Abuja , yesterday , she said the dramatic shrinking of lake chad in less than 40 years to 1/10th its size as well as , the rapidly shrinking @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ all ominous signs of the fate of these natural resources in Nigeria and it is true that it is largely due to climate change . <p> Ms Abiodun said the essence of the programme was to bring together core stakeholders on our natural resources in Nigeria , with particularly water and other ministries that somehow impact our water resources in Nigeria .
