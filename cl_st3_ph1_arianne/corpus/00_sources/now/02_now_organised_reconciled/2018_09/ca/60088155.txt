<h> Low-income Canadians benefit most from federal carbon tax : study <p> TORONTO -- Canadians could come out ahead financially with a federally imposed carbon tax , or so concludes the latest study on the policy as understanding of emission-reduction options continue to evolve . <p> The study by Mark Cameron , executive director of the pro-carbon tax group Clean Prosperity , found people in almost all income brackets would take in more money than they pay in carbon taxes because businesses subject to the tax would n't get a refund . <p> Prime Minister Justin Trudeau has committed to imposing a carbon tax on provinces without their own form of emissions pricing , starting at $20 per tonne next year , and said he could send the revenues directly back to citizens . <p> The Cameron study released this week found lowest-income households would benefit most from such a policy . Those earning under $20,000 would receive a net benefit of $195 in Ontario and $831 in Saskatchewan next year , two provinces without a carbon tax .
