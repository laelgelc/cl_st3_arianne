Dave Abel/Toronto Sun/Postmedia NetworkDave Abel / Dave Abel/Toronto Sun <p> As I prepare myself for another year at university , working towards a bachelor 's degree in the social sciences , I am met with a depressingly acute pessimism about the scholarly destructive university culture that awaits me . <p> My contempt for the school year ahead can be attributed to the ideological shift that has occurred in the social sciences . Once thought of as rigorous scholarship that equipped minds with critical thinking skills , the social sciences have been exploited by a hodgepodge of so-called progressive positions on timely issues with an outright hostility toward differing positions or ideas . <p> There is an inherent viciousness of ideological discourse that is rampant in today 's academic climate of dogma-driven scholarship , where university professors yield little regard for differing opinions when it comes to a whole host of hot-button issues ranging from religion to gender politics to climate change . <p> The assumption that those who disagree with @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ facts , and the urge to make up for weak argument by shouting more loudly -- seem to be core values of many students graduating with degrees in social sciences today . <p> This should be catching the attention of university administrators and sounding the alarm bells . Instead , much of the blame for stifled discourse on university campuses lies squarely on the shoulders of the professors and edu-crats who are blind to the ideological groupthink pervasive in their profession and within the halls of higher-learning institutions . <p> The Campus Freedom Index , a project of the Justice Centre for Constitutional Freedoms , publishes annually an index that assesses the free speech climate on Canadian university campuses . Of sixty campuses analyzed in 2017 , only six were awarded A grades for upholding a " clear and unequivocal commitment to free speech on campus . " Thirty-eight campuses , nearly two-thirds , received F grades for employing policies that limit free speech , stifle debate , and restrict events or expressions that cause emotional harm to others . <p> The result of stifled debate on university campuses has @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ variation of a progressive credo that preaches from a place of self-righteousness , resembling an old-fashioned religious pulpit . <p> What is a university degree if not the opportunity to engross oneself in a diversity of thought , ideas , and methodology ? If professors are able to decree their scholarly opinions from pulpits impenetrable by opposing arguments , then social sciences become little more than dead dogmas adhered to religiously by future generations of scholars who never developed their own perspective . <p> During my time at university I have witnessed most students fold like cheap garden-chairs under the pressure of the ideological groupthink that lurks over them . What more could be expected of young adults who are both naive and unfamiliar with the complex social ideas that they study ? Students would sooner regurgitate the opinions of their professor in an essay than dare to offer up their own well thought out argument . <p> The University culture that exists today , left unchanged , will surely reduce students to mindless pawns who graduate with nothing more than the ability to opine on issues with a clarion @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ " Truth , " as Oscar Wilde quipped , is " rarely pure and never simple .
