<h> Circular Economy : A promising strategy for a healthier , more prosperous Canada <p> Imagine a future where a billion people have been lifted to the economic middle class but where , as a result , we 're awash in food , materials and water shortages , supply-chain interruptions , fewer jobs and lower manufacturing output . Some leading academics say if we continue to follow our business-as-usual model of " take , make , dispose " that 's what Canadian households may face by 2050 . <p> The way that we are realizing economic prosperity is causing these unintended , economically and environmentally unsustainable consequences . <p> Now imagine a future where improved business is innovating to lower these risks and is becoming more globally competitive in doing so . Efficiency and productivity are increased . New jobs are created , new sources of revenue are realized , and a new generation of consumers have more choices in buying goods and services that reflect their values in a shared twenty-first century economy . <p> This future is possible if Canada moves from the current linear model -- extract , use and discard resources -- to a circular @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , just the opposite , it is already underway . Businesses know that producing less waste and realizing greater energy and other efficiencies results in stronger bottom-line results . Energy efficiency alone employs millions of people in the United States . Companies like Google , Facebook , IKEA , Unilever , Coca-Cola and others are taking concrete actions to produce less waste and benefit from more renewable energy . <p> Perhaps most important , businesses in a circular economy are designing products so they are ' made to be made again . ' Economic activity is driven by the reuse , repair , remanufacture or ( in the last resort ) recycling of your broken widgets . Apple has promised that all of its consumer products will reflect a circular economy model in the future , with all components sourced from reused materials . <p> It 's not a new concept around the world , but it is largely unknown in Canada . This July , China and the European Union signed a memorandum of understanding on cooperation toward a circular economy . <p> Canada , and North America as a whole , is way behind accessing the benefits of economic circularity . Those benefits include opportunities for Canadian businesses to lower input and manufacturing costs , create new jobs , and reduce pollution and environmental damage caused by waste management and resource extraction . <p> Research indicates those benefits could add up to billions of dollars in projected GDP growth . One study by the U.K.-based Ellen MacArthur Foundation showed that by adopting principles of the circular economy , Europe could increase its projected 2030 GDP by ? 900 billion more than the growth based on a linear economic model . <p> No doubt , this requires a shift in the way we live , work and do business . But it will provide the foundation for Canadians to enjoy more prosperity in a healthier nation .
