<h> Marchers call for citizen action on climate <p> Marchers of the global Rise for Climate rally get moving along Memory Lane at the Kiwanis Bayshore Trail Saturday , September 8 , 2018 in Belleville , Ont . Speakers called for everyone , not just politicians , to reduce their impact on the planet and call for more sustainable practices . Luke Hendry/Belleville Intelligencer/Postmedia Network <p> Everyday citizens , not just governments , must help fight climate change -- and must do it now . That was the message heard Saturday at the Rise for Climate March along Belleville 's Kiwanis Bayshore Trail . A little more than 40 people participated . It was one of more than 900 events staged that day in 95 countries on seven continents and involving more than 250,000 people and organized globally by partnerships calling for climate action , including 350. org , GreenFaith and the Climate Action Network . Participants in the Global Climate Action Summit in San Francisco , which begins Wednesday , will also ask political leaders to strengthen their commitments to reducing carbon emissions and taking other steps . Belleville mayoral candidate Egerton Boyce , who chairs the city 's green task force , spoke of that team 's accomplishments and citizens ' increasing support of green programs . The first Trash Bash garbage pickup day in 2009 attracted six people , he said . This year there were about 1,700 . The task force has helped in the creation of community gardens , offering tree seedlings and rain barrels and establishing bicycle repair stations . Its summer " Quench Buggy " reduces local demand for drinks in single-use containers by offering water at events and selling hundreds of refillable bottles .
