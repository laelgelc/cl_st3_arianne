<h> Study of fossil teeth sheds light on Jurassic marine reptiles <p> A study of fossilised teeth has shed new light on how reptiles adapted to changing seas more than 150 million years ago . <p> Palaeontologists at the University of Edinburgh found that marine predators living in deep waters during the Jurassic Period thrived as sea levels rose , while species that dwelled in the shallows died out . <p> The research reveals how the structure of food chains beneath the sea has remained largely unchanged since the Jurassic era . <p> The team analysed the shape and size of teeth spanning an 18 million-year period during which diverse reptiles lived in tropical waters stretching from present-day northern France to Yorkshire in the north of England . <p> They found that as global sea levels rose , species that lived in shallow waters and caught fish using thin , piercing teeth declined drastically . <p> Meanwhile , larger species in deeper , open waters with broader teeth for crunching and cutting prey began to thrive . <p> These species may have benefited from major changes in ocean temperature and chemical make-up which could have increased levels of nutrients @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> The study , which also involved the University of Bristol , could shed light on how species at the top of marine food chains today might respond to rapid environmental changes such as climate change , pollution and rising temperatures . <p> Davide Foffa , of the University of Edinburgh 's School of GeoSciences -- who led the study , said : " Studying the evolution of these animals was a real -- and rare -- treat , and has offered a simple yet powerful explanation for why some species declined as others prospered . <p> " This work reminds us of the relevance of palaeontology by revealing the parallels between past and present-day ocean ecosystems . " <p> Colleague Dr Steve Brusatte added : " Teeth are humble fossils , but they reveal a grand story of how sea reptiles evolved over millions of years as their environments changed . <p> " Changes in these Jurassic reptiles parallel changes in dolphins and other marine species that are occurring today as sea-levels rise , which speaks to how important fossils are for understanding our modern world . "
