<h> Irish agri output up 21% since 2010 -- but Brexit looms large <p> Gross output of Irish agriculture is up 21% since 2010 , showing a stronger sector in recent years , but Brexit and profitability pose risks , according to the Central Bank of Ireland <p> The Central Bank today ( Tuesday , September 18 ) published new research on the economic impact of Irish agriculture and the challenges facing the sector . <p> To launch the research deputy governor , Sharon Donnery , visited the National Ploughing Championships 2018 and the Central Bank 's exhibition . <p> An economic letter by Central Bank economist Thomas Conefrey finds that , following a period of stagnation from 1990 to 2010 , agricultural output has increased significantly over recent years . <p> However , the research also identifies a number of challenges facing the industry , including profitability , Brexit , possible future Common Agricultural Policy ( CAP ) reform and climate change . <h> Key findings <p> In 2010 , the value of agricultural output in Ireland was ? 5.5 billion , only marginally higher than the value of output achieved in 1995 ( ? 5.2 billion ) @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ 2017 , the overall volume of gross output in agriculture was 21% higher than in 2010 ; in value terms , output increased 46% to just over ? 8 billion . <p> Much of the rise in output has been driven by the dairy sector following the removal of milk quotas in 2015 . <p> In the border region , more than one in seven workers is employed in the agri-food sector , almost twice the national average of one in twelve .
