the first to open in this former textile manufacturing powerhouse in half a century - aims to reuse that waste , harnessing pioneering recycling technology to try to make the fashion industry more sustainable . <p> " These technologies may be the gateway to a fashion industry decoupled from the use of virgin natural resources , " said Erik Bang , who heads @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ by the family , founders and main owners of H&amp;M Group . <p> The clothing retailer has already placed a first order at the mill , as part of its bid to become " fully circular and renewable , " according to Cecilia Br ? nnsten , the group 's environmental sustainability manager . <p> The Novetex Upcycling Factory in Tai Po Industrial Estate , when it opens this month , will use new technology to separate fabric blends in waste garments and produce yarn . <p> It comes as clothing companies around the world doubled the amount of garments they made from 2000 to 2014 , according to a 2016 report by management consultancy McKinsey &amp; Company . <p> Over the same period , the number of garments bought each year , per person , jumped 60 per cent , the report said . <p> That has led to a stream of clothing - such as old uniforms or hotel curtains - are sanitised , with buttons and zippers removed , then sorted and stored . <p> Once an order comes in for a certain colour , the material is UV-sterilised before being cut into pieces and spun into yarn . No water or dye is needed , and only small amounts of virgin material are used , Chan said . <p> But the mill will also test a system to separate cotton and polyester blends using only heat , water and a @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ turned into cellulose powder while the polyester fibre is used for spinning and making new fabric , Chan said . <p> If Novetex can show on the factory floor that the technologies work to make high-quality yarn , it could drive demand globally for recycled yarn and wider use of the technology , its backers say . <p> " As we scale up and make this technology freely available to the industry , we will reduce our dependence on limited natural resources to clothe a growing global population , " Bang said . <h> ' Fast fashion ' <p> A 2017 report from the Ellen MacArthur Foundation , a UK-based sustainability charity , said the total greenhouse gas emissions from producing textiles , at 1.2 billion tonnes annually , is more than those from all international flights and maritime shipping . <p> An average of 23 kgs of greenhouse gases are generated to make one kilo ( 2.2 pounds ) of fabric , according to McKinsey . <p> Customers are keeping their clothes half as long as they did 15 years ago , the report added , sometimes wearing them @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> This move towards fast fashion - where more clothes are produced but at a lower quality , making them less robust - must be reversed for a sustainable future , researchers from the Mistra Future Fashion program wrote in The Handbook of Sustainable Fashion . <p> A sustainable fashion survey by auditor KPMG , released at the Fashion Summit this month , showed more buyers are becoming more conscious about the industry 's impact on the planet . <p> More than three-quarters of 5,000 consumers surveyed in Hong Kong , Shanghai , London , New York and Tokyo said they were concerned about the environment , pollution and waste . <p> However , only 13 per cent of consumers in the survey said they were willing to pay a higher price for sustainable fashion . <p> Sixy per cent said they would prefer sustainable fashion if the price was the same as normal fashion .
