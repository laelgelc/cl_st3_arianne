Aris Oikonomou/Pool via REUTERS/File Photo <p> By Alastair Macdonald <p> STRASBOURG ( Reuters ) - An annual address to the European Parliament on Wednesday by European Commission President Jean-Claude Juncker puts a spotlight on EU elections next year . <p> WHO 'S VOTING AND FOR WHAT ? <p> More than 350 million adults in the European Union 's 27 member states can vote , most on Sunday , May 26 , 2019 -- two months after Britain becomes the first country to leave in a move that has spurred nationalist , anti-EU groups across Europe . <p> By proportional representation , they will elect 705 members to the European Parliament , which divides its time between Brussels and Strasbourg . Ranging from tiny Malta with 6 seats to Germany with 96 , for five years these MEPs will check and amend laws proposed by the European Commission , subject to approval by national governments in the EU Council . <p> @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ from spending , though the EU budget is equal to just 1 percent of member states ' combined GDP , to climate change and labour rights . But some who want the Union broken up see it as a Brexit-style referendum on the EU 's very survival , pitting advocates of historic , ethnic-based nations against the idea of pooling sovereignty to defend Europe 's wealth and values in a world of rising authoritarian powers and global corporations . <p> Caught up in this centre-versus-states debate are refugees . Nationalists blame the EU for a surge in arrivals in 2015 . Federalists say only cooperation can control migration . <p> Leaders of some eastern states such as Hungary and Poland slam Brussels over migrants and its complaints that they are undermining EU rules on democracy in Warsaw and Budapest ; some westerners speak of cutting their EU subsidies in retaliation . <p> ARE THERE EU POLITICAL PARTIES ? <p> Yes . And no . Eight party groups sit in the current 751-seat chamber , which is about to get smaller due to Brexit . Well , maybe . Parliament 's leaders say they are the heart of European democracy . National leaders scoff at the 43 percent turnout in the 2014 EU elections . In practice , states wield most power and little happens that big countries do n't like . <p> The EU executive Commission is led by Jean-Claude Juncker . A historic power struggle between Parliament and Council will get an airing in the election . Parliament has pledged to force the Council to nominate as Juncker 's successor a lead candidate from a winning party in May 's vote .
