A climate-change mitigation and adaption program , the Expanded National Greening Program , is aimed at enhancing the natural defense of vulnerable communities against natural calamities . <p> The Philippines has been assessed as one of the " highly vulnerable " countries to climate-change impacts . An archipelago situated in the so-called typhoon belt , the Philippines receives an average of 18 to 20 typhoons every year . <p> Against the backdrop of intensifying typhoons , storm surge and excessive rains during the wet season , massive floods and landslides have been predicted to happen , prompting the Philippine government to identify which areas are prone to what particular geological hazards to prevent disaster . <h> Going to the details <p> As early as 2014 , the DENR completed the geohazard maps and two years later , made the detailed landslide and flood geohazard maps available for viewing and downloading for free from the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ of the maps , with a scale of 1:10,000 , were also distributed to various LGUs . <p> These maps are considered bigger and more detailed as they can pinpoint the barangays that are susceptible to floods or landslides , and thus , should be able to help concerned national and LGU officials plan ahead to prevent deaths . <p> Experts from the DENR-MGB also started to train LGU officials and concerned employees on how to properly use the geohazard maps as early as 2015 . <p> However , the use of these geohazard maps has not been fully appreciated , as certain areas continue to experience tragic deaths as a result of flash floods and landslides , such as what happened in Barangay Ucab , in the municipality of Itogon , a mining town in the province of Benguet ;
