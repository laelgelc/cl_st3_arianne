<p> Scotland is outperforming the rest of the UK on reducing greenhouse gas emissions but needs fresh measures to meet its own recently introduced targets , according to parliament 's climate change watchdog . <p> The Committee on Climate Change 's annual progress report on carbon reduction north of the border is published today ( 24 September ) . <p> It says Scottish net emissions were 41.5 MtCO2e in 2016 , the last year for which data is available , below the climate target figure of 44.9 MtCO2e . Total emissions fell by 10 per cent in 2016 alone . <p> This means that net emissions , which account for the trading of allowances in the EU Emissions Trading Scheme , were 45 per cent below 1990 levels . Scotland is outperforming its interim target of a 42 per cent reduction by 2020 , the report shows . <p> Actual emissions in 2016 were 49 per cent below 1990 levels , meaning that Scotland is on track to meet the separate target in the Scottish parliament 's own Climate Change Bill for a reduction of at least 56 per cent by 2020 . <p> The biggest factor in the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ generation provided the lion 's share of this latest drop in emissions with 17.8 per cent of Scotland 's total energy in 2016 coming from renewable sources ; a higher proportion than in the UK and EU overall . <p> However following the closure of Longannet , the final coal plant located north of the border , Scotland 's strategy must now move on decisively , says the CCC . <p> And given the lack of " significant " emission reductions outside electricity generation and waste over the five years to 2016 , other sectors need to pick up the slack in order to meet Scotland 's ambitious climate targets . <p> The Scottish bill has set a target of cutting emissions north of the border to 90 per cent of 1990 levels , compared to the figure of 80 per cent enshrined in the UK-wide Climate Change Act . <p> The CCC judges that the 90 per cent reduction target is " at the limits of feasibility identified to date " and that more measures may be required to achieve this goal . <p> Low-carbon heat and transport are @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ , including a clear plan for rolling out electric vehicle charging infrastructure . <p> Lord Deben , chairman of the CCC , said : " Scotland continues to lead the UK in reducing its emissions and has ambitious targets which aim to go further . <p> " Decarbonisation of Scotland 's electricity sector , and reductions in emissions from waste , have seen Scotland outperform the UK overall as emissions continue to fall year-on-year to nearly half of 1990 levels . <p> " The Scottish government has made some progress on tackling issues raised in the committee 's report in 2017 .
