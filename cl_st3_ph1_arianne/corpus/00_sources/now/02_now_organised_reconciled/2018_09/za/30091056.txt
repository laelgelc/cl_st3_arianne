Recent research for HSBC by East &amp; Partners , a research and analysis firm , shows that investors and companies are increasingly incorporating environmental , social and governance ( ESG ) factors into their investment strategies . At the moment there are significant barriers to market growth . Inconsistent definitions of " ESG " , " sustainable " and " green " are an issue for all parties , and investors are n't getting good or consistent enough information on which to base decisions . The failure to provide comparable information makes it hard for the market to discriminate on environmental grounds , preventing the accurate pricing of risk and delaying the transition to a low-carbon world . This leads directly to a lack of investment opportunities and identifiable " green " assets . <p> Disclosure informs investment <p> The best remedy for investors ' carbon addiction is better disclosure . Providing the market with better quality and more consistent information about companies ' climate strategies and preparedness would give institutional investors -- the managers of trillions of dollars in assets -- what they need to direct capital into the right areas . <p> The G20 Financial Stability Board 's Task Force @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ a workable standard on which to base this global effort . For now , though , just one in 10 investors and issuers have even heard of the TCFD recommendations . Central banks , financial institutions and investors need to start raising awareness fast . Big corporates , regulators and governments need to work together -- and quickly -- to provide better guidance on what good disclosure looks like , using TCFD as the basis . <p> Companies need to take responsibility , too .
