<h> Coffee industry may be up for change <p> Places where certain coffees are grown will change but climate change wo n't stop coffee from being grown , says one roaster . David Gray Reuters <p> Cape Town - Climate change has the potential to alter the way coffee is produced , change the price and even the quality of the bean . <p> South Africa 's coffee industry is small with just seven coffee farms located in the country . None are in the Western Cape but coffee culture has exploded in Cape Town in recent years with boutique cafes springing up across the city , selling their brews at premium prices . <p> According to the scientific journal Climatic Change , half of the land used to produce high-quality coffee could be unproductive by 2050 . <p> Bean There coffee founder Jonathan Robinson said personal relationships with coffee growers were necessary to maintain a sustainable coffee industry . <p> " Ensuring the sustainability of the coffee industry requires coffee companies to form direct relationships with their growers . <p> " Farmer education , more sustainable techniques and technologies as well as ongoing research are needed in order @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ <p> Bean There became one of the first coffee roasters in South Africa to be certified by Fairtrade . <p> The Fairtrade label was established to serve the interests and rights of farmers , workers and producers . Paying fair prices motivates farmers to grow their businesses and innovate how and where they grow their crop . <p> " We pay consistent , fair prices for our beans and we conduct regular trips to meet with , receive feedback from and support training programmes for growers in the co-ops we purchase from , " said Robinson . <p> David Donde from Truth Coffee Roasting said he saw no value in the Fairtrade label . <p> " We have hundreds of bags here and no one knows the labels on them .
