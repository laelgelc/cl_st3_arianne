<h> Food insecurity rising in Africa <p> A third of all people living in sub-Saharan Africa face severe food insecurity . They do not have enough money , or the resources to grow food , and regularly go for more than a day without food . <p> The bad news is the situation is getting worse . Of the just under a billion people in sub-Saharan Africa , 230-million were undernourished last year , an increase of 10-million from 2016 . That 's also an increase of 25-million since 2014 . <p> That 's according to the United Nations ' Food and Agriculture Organisation . Its State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World report has become an annual marker of how the world is dealing with feeding its nearly eight billion people . The 2018 edition , released last week , makes for grim reading . <p> " New evidence continues to signal a rise in world hunger and a reversal of trends after prolonged decline . Predictability is key to growing enough food to feeding your family and climate change is removing that predictability . <p> The UN report mentions farmers in the Afram Plains region of Ghana , where the rainy season is arriving later than usual . That means a shorter growing season . But heatwaves and heavy rains in the middle of the growing season are damaging crops . So farmers either lose their crop or have to live off lower yields . <p> Changes in rainfall are a particular problem on the African continent . Some 80% of farmers and herders rely on rainfall . When it does n't rain , crops and cattle die . This is accelerating desertification and has resulted in the Sahara and Kalahari deserts expanding , thus reducing farmland . <p> Farmers are far less resilient when weather shocks come along . In the case of sub-Saharan Africa , those shocks are usually droughts . But climate change means people have to deal with more climate extremes -- such as floods and droughts -- at the same time and more regularly . The UN report says the occurrence of three or more different climate extremes at the same time has increased by 160% . This means farmers are facing a new norm where floods and drought are all happening during the same time that the growing season is shortened . <p> When crops fail , countries have to import critical foodstuffs , such as maize , wheat and rice , which pushes the prices up .
