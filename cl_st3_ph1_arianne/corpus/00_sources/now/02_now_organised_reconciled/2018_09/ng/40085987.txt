<h> Malnourishment On The Rise In Nigeria <p> The number of people suffering from hunger and malnutrition has increased astronomically , particularly in the last few years . About 82 million people globally were undernourished in 2017 , that is about one person in every nine , and nearly 151 million under-fives , or 22 percent of the global total , have their growth stunted by poor nutrition . Difficulty in accessing nutritious food is contributing to the challenging state of food insecurity and nutrition the world over . Accordingly , the frequency of extreme climate in the form of heat , storms and draughts has doubled since the early 1990s and this has sent a clear message that climate variability and exposure to more complex , frequent and intense climate extremes are threatening to erode and even reverse the gains made in ending hunger and malnutrition over the past years . Climate extremes have a direct impact on crop yields and food availability , but can also reduce the number of healthy people available to grow and harvest crops . Climate variability and extremes , in addition to conflict and violence in this part of the world are key drivers behind the recent rises in global hunger and one of the leading causes of severe food crises . <p> Disturbingly , this is the third consecutive year of rising hunger . The inescapable fact is that climate change is now leaving people around the world without enough to eat . Hunger is significantly worse in countries hit by severe droughts and flooding . A hotter world is proving to be a hungrier world . The people behind these stark statistics need urgent help . The level of poverty in Nigeria today , particularly in the north of the country , is worrisome , with well over 80 per cent living below the poverty line of $1 a day . The rate of poverty in Zamfara State is 92 per cent ; it is 88 per cent in Bauchi , 82 per cent in Katsina , and the same dismal statistics apply to other states in the region .
