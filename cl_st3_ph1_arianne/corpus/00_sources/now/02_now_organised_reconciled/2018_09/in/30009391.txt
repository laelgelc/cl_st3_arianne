<h> Climate negotiator warns world ' out of time ' to save islands <p> Bangkok , Sep 7 Island nations like Fiji and the Maldives are almost at the " point of no return " because of rising sea levels , a leading climate negotiator warned Friday . <p> As well as losing land and infrastructure to encroaching oceans as the planet heats up , many islands are also facing extreme flooding and damage from tropical storms , warned Amjad Abdulla , head negotiator for the Alliance of Small Island States ( AOSIS ) . <p> With experts from across the planet locked in key talks in Bangkok aimed at breathing life into the Paris Agreement on climate change , Abdulla said time had " already run out " for some countries . <p> " Our islands are at risk . We are doing more than our fair share with our limited resources , " he told AFP on the sidelines of the six-day conference . <p> " What we are saying to the international community is that we do not have adequate financial , technological or human capacity in terms of alleviating the issue of climate change -- @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ our islands . " The 2015 Paris deal -- which must be adopted by signatory nations by December -- aims to limit global temperature rises to " well below " two degrees Celsius and to less than 1.5 degrees if possible . <p> The most persistent sticking points in negotiations revolve around money . <p> The Paris Agreement has promised USD 100 billion annually from 2020 to poor nations already coping with the effects of climate change . <p> Developing countries favour outright grants from public sources , demand visibility on how donor nations intend to scale up this largesse , and object to under-investment in adapting to climate impacts . <p> Rich countries want more private capital in the mix and prefer projects with profit potential . <p> Although responsible for only a tiny percentage of global greenhouse gas emissions , the very existence of island nations are under threat from rising sea levels . <p> " We are very behind .
