Read More <p> The Union ministry of environment , forests and climate change notified Prevention of Cruelties to Animals ( Pet Shops ) Rules 2018 on Wednesday . The notification , which aims to set standards for animal housing and care in pet shops , mandates all traders to get a certificate of registration from the Animal Welfare Board of India . And while it has brought cheer to activists , those in the trade say some of the provisions are impractical . <p> " Animals sold in pet shops are treated as commodities and the cruelty they are subjected to is unthinkable . The situation in pet shops demanded a regulation , " said Gauri Maulekhi , trustee , People for Animals . <p> Animals in pet shops are denied vet care and newborns are separated from their mothers . An estimated 40% of animals die in captivity or during transport , said Human Society International welcoming the order . <p> But pet shop owners said some clauses were vague . A R Vijai , who plies the trade in Chennai , said , " The word enclosure did not specify the dimensions where birds are to be kept . Another example is the use of the word ' aviary ' which means a large enclosure to keep birds . How can a pet shop provide such a huge space ? " <p> In Chennai , the pet trade industry has an annual turnover of Rs 10 crore , said wildlife officials . Most pet shops deal in exotic birds which are not covered under the Wildlife Protection Act , 1972 . Officials said more than 400 pet shops function across the city . But many shops and private farms sold local species illegally , and the menace is prevalent across the state . <p> Even though notified , the rules will have limited effect in Tamil Nadu as there is no agency to enforce them .
