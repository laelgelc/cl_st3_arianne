<h> ' Tanks can be revived with people 's participation ' <p> TNN Sep 19 , 2018 , 00:45 IST <p> Founder , Sundaram Climate Institute , and author of " The Climate Solution , India 's Climate Change Crisis " , Mridula Ramesh , had recently conducted a study into the water and waste in Madurai . She talks about the tanks in the district in a chat with TOI Q : How would you rate the condition of the tanks in Madurai ? <p> A : Madurai 's tanks have been dying for many decades . Today , both in number and size , they are far smaller than what they were . <p> Q : Do you think these tanks have a chance for revival ? <p> A : Perhaps . Some tanks have been destroyed because of the large buildings that have been constructed on them . Some have died because of no water feeding because the feeder canals are choked with waste . If the waste can be cleared and the tanks desilted , we can revive them . <p> Q : Why is tank revival crucial at this juncture ? <p> A : @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ in Madurai , we found out that 40% of the city 's population buys water for some part of its needs . We also found out that many neighbourhoods had run out of ground water . Reviving tanks can help in addressing this water shortage . Treated sewage from a neighbourhood , which makes it usable , can be channelled into the tanks , which would help in tank revival . For example , the Sellur tank is surrounded by households . Their sewage , if treated appropriately , can be used to revive the tank . This will also help in ground water recharge in the Sellur area . <p> Q : Who should do it , the government or the people ? <p> A : We are a raucous democracy . This means more than commenting , or even just voting . People need to take the initiative to revive the tanks and restore them , only then would it yield maximum results . This is what has happened in so many other places in India .
