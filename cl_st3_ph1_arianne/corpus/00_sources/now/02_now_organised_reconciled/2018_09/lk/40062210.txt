<p> Rising temperatures and changing rainfall patterns pose a threat to Sri Lanka and could reduce GDP and living standards , a new World Bank report says . <p> The report ' South Asia 's Hotspots : The Impact of Temperature and Precipitation Changes on Living Standards ' flags that almost half of South Asia 's population now live in vulnerable areas called hotspots that could see their living standards decline as a result of falling crop yields , lower labour productivity , or related health impacts . <p> Some of these hotspots , the report notes , are already vulnerable as they are less developed , suffer from poor connectivity , and are water stressed . <p> According to the report , in Sri Lanka , areas in northern and north-western provinces are the most likely to experience increases in average temperature and more variable precipitation by 2050 . <p> The analysis concludes that Sri Lanka 's average annual temperatures could rise by 1.0 ? C to 1.5 ? C by 2050 even if carbon emission reduction measures are taken as recommended by the Paris Agreement of 2015 .
