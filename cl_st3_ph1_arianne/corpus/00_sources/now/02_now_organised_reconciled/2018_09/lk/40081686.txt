<h> How can Sri Lanka better protect its people against disasters ? <p> About two thousand years ago , the country built one of the world 's first irrigation systems to control its water supply . <p> This feat of engineering , which boasted hundreds of kilometres of channels , tanks and innovative valve pits , helped the great kingdoms of Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa flourish into sophisticated societies and protect their people against hunger . <p> Not unlike these early civilizations , modern social protection programmes have sheltered those affected by disaster through financial assistance and other forms of support . <p> Today , building resilience to natural disasters and other shocks is more critical than ever . <p> Since 1980 , the frequency of natural disasters worldwide has increased by 250 percent and the number of affected people has more than doubled . <p> Sri Lanka is no exception . The country ranked fourth most vulnerable to climate change in 2016 . <p> Further to that , a recent World Bank report indicates that nine out of 10 of Sri Lankans may live in climate hotspots -- or areas highly prone to floods or droughts -- by 2050 @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ disasters have long-lasting consequences . <p> Children , especially , can suffer permanent damages if they are not educated or fed correctly in their critical early years . And the loss of assets , livestock and crops can severely hurt small business owners and farmers and further discourage them from investing . <p> Sadly , natural disasters hit the poor the hardest as they tend to live in disaster-prone areas , work in agriculture and usually do n't have savings or access to credit . <p> When a shock hits , the well-being declines as people cut back on food and other essentials due to their loss of income or the high cost of rebuilding their homes . <p> And while some people gradually restore their standards of living , some never fully recover and get stuck in poverty . <p> But the poor are n't the only ones who need to worry about shocks . <p> Today , a third of Sri Lankans are just a shock away from falling into poverty . <p> Our analysis of the 2016 Household Income and Expenditure Survey reveals that a 20 percent @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ would more than double the poverty rate : almost one in 10 Sri Lankans would be poor . <p> If the shock triggered a 50 percent decrease in consumption , one in three Sri Lankan families would fall into poverty . To its credit , the Sri Lankan government already has a well-established disaster management system . <p> But social protection programmes -- such as cash transfers , social insurance and care services -- must also play a role in the aftermath of disasters to prevent more people from becoming poor . <p> To achieve that , social protection programmes must adapt to reach more people and help them grow more resilient to a fast-changing and riskier world . <p> This is currently not the case in Sri Lanka , as programmes have fixed budgets and rules that prevent them from increasing benefit amounts or enrolling additional households . <p> Generally , social protection programmes provide a regular amount of money or other kinds of support for the poorest . After a disaster , some may need extra assistance . <p> Adaptive social protection programmes can provide a top-up grant @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ may also need temporary support until they recover . <p> So , how can Sri Lanka 's social protection programmes become adaptive ? <p> To fully protect its people , Sri Lanka must design its social protection programmes , so they assist anyone whenever they need it , rather than serving as an entitlement to a select few . <p> Programme rules and budgets need to be more flexible , to allow new beneficiaries to enrol as soon as their welfare falls below a given level . And the country needs a social registry to decide who needs assistance the most using objective and transparent criteria . <p> Social registries are standard in many countries and aim to make social protection programs fairer and more effective in reaching the poorest and lifting them out of poverty .
