<h> Cars or livestock : Which contributes more to climate change ? <p> WHAT we choose to eat , how we move around and how these activities contribute to climate change is receiving a lot of media attention . In this context , greenhouse gas emissions from livestock and transport are often compared , but in a flawed way . <p> The comparison measures direct emissions from transport against both direct and indirect emissions from livestock . The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change ( IPCC ) identifies and monitors human activities responsible for climate change and reports direct emissions by sectors . <p> The IPCC estimates that direct emissions from transport ( road , air , rail and maritime ) account for 6.9 gigatons per year , about 14% of all emissions from human activities . <p> These emissions mainly consist of carbon dioxide and nitrous oxide from fuel combustion . By comparison , direct emissions from livestock account for 2.3 gigatons of CO2 equivalent , or 5% of the total . <p> They consist of methane and nitrous oxide from rumen digestion and manure management .
