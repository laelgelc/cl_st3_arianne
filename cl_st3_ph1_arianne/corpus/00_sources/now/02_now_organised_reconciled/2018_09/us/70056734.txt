The sharks , attracted by the skyrocketing seal population , have become a threat to swimmers , attacking several and leading to beach closures . <p> It 's a wonder there have n't been more incidents . Sharks have been sighted along much of the national seashore and in Cape Cod Bay , the 600-square-mile body of water on the sheltered side of the cape . Tourists repeatedly have seen sharks attacking or dining on seals , which are protected from human interference under the 1972 Marine Mammal Protection Act . <p> The landmark law covers a variety of mammals that were being killed inadvertently , such as dolphins caught in tuna nets , or deliberately , such as seals for their meat and fur . In 1972 , the number of seals was dwindling . Seals compete with other species and commercial fishermen for food -- the Cape Cod Commercial Fishermen 's Alliance notes that the mammals follow no fisheries management plan -- and they produce huge amounts of waste that foul the cape 's waters . <p> One cape official is calling for a culling of the seal population , something impossible under the 1972 federal law .
