<p> The hurricane is massive : at 400 miles wide , its hurricane-force winds stretch across a 160-mile span , as ABC reported . <p> Yet the long-ranging , torrential winds are not the primary concern . It 's the sheer volume of water , in the form of tidal surges , rain , and anticipated flash flooding that make this Category 1 storm unusually dangerous . As meteorologist Janice Dean put it , " The legacy of the storm is not going to be the winds . It 's going to be the rain . " <p> Florence may drench the Carolinas with an unthinkable amount of water this weekend : 18 trillion gallons , or enough to fill the Chesapeake Bay . As of early Friday afternoon , 20 inches of rain had already fallen in parts of North Carolina -- and some resolution models are predicting that by Sunday , the southeastern part of the state could see 50 inches of rain . <p> We 've seen a slow-moving storm like Hurricane Florence before .
