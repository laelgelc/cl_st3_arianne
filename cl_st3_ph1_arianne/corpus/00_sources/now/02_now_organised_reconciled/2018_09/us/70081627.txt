<p> The lower Detroit River becomes one of the largest vessels of passage during the autumn months for hundreds of thousands of migrating hawks , eagles , falcons , and vultures as they make their way to South America . <p> In the past three days , more than 13,000 hawks have been spotted near Detroit , most of them being broad-winged hawk and turkey vultures , according to HawkCount , a database monitoring raptor populations . <p> Interested in watching thousands of hawks circling the sky now , during the height of migration season ? Head out to Lake Erie Metro Park 's boat launch , where raptor watchers gather almost every day , according to the Detroit River Hawk Watch 's Facebook group , which has nearly 700 members . Many in the group also are raptor photographers . <p> Or wait a week and head to the Hawk Migration Association of North America ( HMANA ) conference , Oct. 12-14 , at the Detroit Metro Airport Marriott Hotel . The conference includes several field trips to Holiday Beach Bird Observatory ( Ontario ) , Marshlands Museum , Detroit River International Wildlife Refuge and features keynote speakers @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ and Todd Katzner of the Geological Survey . Conference topics include : weather patterns and climate change to shifts in migration patterns and conservation threats . <p> " Tens of thousands of raptors migrate seasonally through the region . Up to 10,000 birds can be seen in a single day , " said Jane Ferreyra , HMANA 's executive director in a news release . " You just have to look up ! " <p> HMANA will also soon open its first headquarters at the Detroit River International Wildlife Refuge Visitor Center in Trenton . <p> Tickets for the three-day conference range from $125 to $200
