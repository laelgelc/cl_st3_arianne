<p> A rare and extreme tsunami ripped across an Alaskan fjord three years ago after 180 million tons of mountain rock fell into the water , driving a devastating wave that stripped shorelines of trees and reached heights greater than 600 feet , a large team of scientists documented on Thursday . <p> The October 2015 cataclysm in Taan Fiord in southeastern Alaska appears to have been the fourth-highest tsunami recorded in the past century , and its origins -- linked to the retreat of a glacier -- suggest that it 's the kind of event we may see more often because of a warming climate . <p> The new study even bluntly calls it a " hazard occasioned by climate change .
