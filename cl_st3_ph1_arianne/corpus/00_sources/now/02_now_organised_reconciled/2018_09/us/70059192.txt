<p> Carrington 's featherwort is an unusual plant by any standards . Tiny , between 2cm and 5cm in height , it clusters on high ground in north-west Scotland . Crucially , every single plant found in this secluded Caledonian enclave is male . By contrast , the only other substantial colonies known to botanists are located in the Himalayas -- and are made up of females . <p> Carrington 's featherwort would now be extinct were it not for the fact that the species can also propagate nonsexually . New plants form out of fragments of existing featherworts , producing colonies of clones . <p> " The trouble is that populations created this way may well lack genetic variation , leaving them vulnerable to infestations or ecological damage , " says Dan Mead , a geneticist at the Sanger Institute near Cambridge .
