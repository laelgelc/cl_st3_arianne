<h> DOC to manage tourist numbers and cut aircraft noise at parks <p> Managing tourist numbers and cutting aircraft noise are among a list of proposals for two South Island national parks , the Department of Conservation says . <p> Mt Cook/Aoraki National Park . Photo : 123rf.com <p> The department has just released its draft management plans for the Aoraki-Mount Cook and Westland-Tai Poutini National Parks , which describe how the parks would be managed and take into account the impacts of climate change and increased tourism . <p> Each plan included proposals to manage high visitor numbers in the parks whose ecological systems and natural features were part of the Te Wahipounamu World Heritage Area . <p> Aoraki-Mount Cook operations manager , Brent Swanson said a proposed park and ride system was one possible solution to managing visitors to the Aoraki-Mount Cook Village and Hooker Valley . <p> A new approach to authorising aircraft activity was also in the draft , Mr Swanson . <p> " As well as a zone approach for aircraft landings , we have been working with the University of Canterbury to develop a tranquillity mapping tool , to enable DOC and stakeholders @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ soundscapes and tranquil places . " <p> The draft plans have been developed in partnership with Te Runanga o Ngai Tahu and Runanga Arowhenua , Waiho and Moeraki who are affiliated with Aoraki . Development has also included Makaawhio who are affiliated with Tai Poutini , as well as conservation boards and the community . <p> Westland-Tai Poutini operations manager Wayne Costello said the impacts of climate change would mean changes to the way fragile areas are looked after . <p> " For example , in the Franz Josef and Fox Glacier valleys , climate change means the glaciers are further back - there 's lot of instability , in the Fox valley in particular , and that might change how we have to access the valleys . " <p> Mr Costello said there was a plan to allow people to walk dogs on some tracks in the Westland park . <p> " One of the points that was really important to locals who live alongside the park in Franz Josef , were some opportunities to walk dogs on a couple of tracks in the national park . <p> " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ In fact dogs are not permitted in national parks . " <p> Consultation was now open for the next two months . <p> Mr Costello said every bit of feedback would be fed into the draft plans . <p> " They will ensure the views of New Zealanders and how they want to see the parks managed into the future , are represented . " <p> Public hearings were scheduled at the close of submissions . The revised plans would then considered by the conservation boards before going to the New Zealand Conservation Authority for approval .
