<h> Editorial : New Zealand 's Antarctic efforts need to be refocused <p> Last updated 05:00 , September 4 2018 <p> Iain McGregor/STUFF <p> A frozen iceberg in the Ross Sea , Antarctica . New Zealand 's efforts there have been brought into sharp focus by recent stories . <p> EDITORIAL : It does n't seem unreasonable to suggest there 's a word that begins with F currently on the minds of many concerned with the operation of Crown entity Antarctica New Zealand . Not to mention those in government to whom its leaders must answer . <p> Yes , it 's focus . Because a series of recent stories on New Zealand 's operations on the frozen continent have raised questions suggesting that focus on the core business of the taxpayer-funded organisation , responsible for New Zealand 's activities supporting science and environmental programmes , has drifted a little . <p> At a time when the information and data unearthed by scientists working at the bottom of the world is more crucial than ever to our understanding of complex issues like climate change , that is worrying . <p> " Science is the currency of Antarctica , " @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Stuff last week . Not that there 's any suggestion our scientists are n't carrying out research , with four universities running Antarctic programmes , Antarctic scientists employed in Crown research entities , and two major research programmes , as part of a new Antarctic science platform , being set up , with field activities to start in the 2019-20 season . As in other fields of scientific research , the contestable nature of some funding means scientists can often be required to spend a chunk of their time trying to secure or retain funding , which has led to a sense of New Zealand 's Antarctic effort being fragmented . <p> However , stories about the recent sacking of charismatic AntNZ chief executive Peter Beggs for serious misconduct have focused attention on the organisation , which gets about $20 million a year from the Ministry of @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ scientific activities itself , but looks after Scott Base , and facilitates the work of scientists based there . <p> There was a testy relationship between Beggs , who " lacked the background in scientific research that 's needed " , and Antarctic scientists , Storey told Stuff . His departure also turned the microscope on the makeup of the AntNZ board , which contains several high-profile business figures , including former Air New Zealand and Icebreaker chief executive Rob Fyfe , and Dame Therese Walsh , widely known for prominent roles in the staging of the Rugby and Cricket World Cups in New Zealand in 2011 and 2015 respectively . It is noticeably short on scientific expertise and , despite Christchurch 's location as the so-called gateway to the ice , contains no members from Canterbury . <p> A report last week said scientists had been concerned the board was treating Scott Base like something of a resort . That had seen a steady stream of celebrities and VIPs , with questionable connections to the science being carried out there , making it down to the ice . On @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ Next Top Model host Sara Tetro , who married Fyfe earlier this year , had travelled to the ice in 2016 . Board member Tony O'Brien said she had been hired as a consultant and worked on the " invited visitors programme " . Fyfe was not there . <p> All of which suggests a major reset , with the focus squarely on scientific efforts in Antarctica and how best to achieve them , is well overdue . A revamped board , and a genuinely science-focused replacement for Beggs , may be just the first steps , if taxpayers are to see our involvement on the ice as money well spent .
