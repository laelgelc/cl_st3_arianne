<h> UN expert : Argentina should avoid dismantling family farming <p> UN expert : Argentina should avoid dismantling family farming at times of economic crisis <p> GENEVA / BUENOS AIRES ( 21 September 2018 ) -- The Government of Argentina should support the country 's family farming sector in order to protect food availability , says the UN expert on food and human rights . <p> Family farmers represent almost 80 per cent of farmers in Argentina and produce nearly half of the vegetables and fruits consumed in the country . <p> " I understand the challenges faced by Argentina , but I am critical of the Government 's decision to take advantage of the ongoing economic crisis to dismantle support for the country 's family farming sector by laying off almost five hundred workers and experts from the Ministry of Agroindustry . This action seems to be targeted to further promote export-orientated industrial agriculture mainly of soybean and maize , " said Hilal Elver , the Special Rapporteur on the right to food as she presented a statement at the end of a 10-day fact-finding visit to the country . <p> " The adoption of such policies in @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ already significantly increased poverty and diminished the purchasing power of the poor , will intensify the impact on Argentina 's realization of the right to food . <p> " In crisis situations with acutely high inflation , people who are already vulnerable such as landless peasants , agricultural workers , migrants , and indigenous peoples are further hit , with the livelihoods of many put at risk , " the Special Rapporteur added . I have observed an increasing number of people going to soup kitchens , or skipping meals , and children being forced to rely for their daily meals entirely on school feeding programmes , " Ms Elver said . <p> The Special Rapporteur met with members of the Qom communities in Chaco , who had migrated from their traditional lands to nearby cities in order to survive , yet still unable to sustain themselves because of systemic discrimination . <p> " It is a legal obligation of the government and a matter of human rights to uphold the right to food in times of a national economic crisis and the Government should take into greater account the @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ accessibility of food for the poorest members of Argentine society , " she added . <p> Argentina claims that the country produces enough food for 450 million people , yet almost four million of its own citizens face serious food insecurity . <p> Ms Elver argued that the country 's present agricultural development model based on intensive export oriented commercial farming , with genetically modified soya beans and maize as its main product , is not beneficial to the right to food in a number of ways . <p> " Industrial agriculture is causing dangerous rates of deforestation , at around 27 million hectares each year . Moreover , the enormous increase in the use of agrochemicals , including glyphosate , pollutes water and soil . As a result some 60 per cent of Argentinians are overweight or obese , including 40 per cent of children , making them more susceptible to diseases like diabetes . <p> Ms Elver urged the authorities to ensure that nutrition policies supported well financed healthy school meal programmes .
