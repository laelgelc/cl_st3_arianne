<h> Pacific needs hard hats , not silly shirts <p> OPINION : This week we celebrated another momentous Pacific Islands Forum , notable mostly for our RNZAF 757 having to make a special flight for Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern to attend for a day . <p> Needs must , and Ardern is Prime Minister and is doing her job . However , had she given the forum a miss , she would have been in good company as the leaders of Fiji , Australia , Palau and Papua New Guinea failed to turn up . <p> In any event , apparently important things were achieved , namely the Boe Declaration , named after the area of Nauru where it was signed . <p> Generally official communiques coming from global meetings are not worth the paper they are printed on , but , given the media coverage and general speechifying , I assumed something significant was agreed . <p> Online I found the " unscrubbed version " of the declaration cleared by leaders at 7.45pm , on September 5 . <p> It starts with a preamble in which forum leaders recognise , reaffirm , recall and respect various things like @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ affairs and regional security co-operation . <p> Good stuff but hardly requiring an expensive conference on a clapped-out phosphate pit in the middle of the Pacific . <p> But then comes the declaration , in which the kicker is right at the end . <p> " We commit to strengthening the existing regional security architecture to account for the expanded concept of security ... , " it says . <p> A reaffirmation asserts that climate change is the single greatest threat to Pacific peoples . In fact obesity , according to the document , appears to be a bigger one . <p> So what has been achieved apart from a bit of relationship-building and giving of loose commitments that generally will have to be funded by Australian and New Zealand taxpayers ? <p> Not much , it appears , except maintenance of the fiction the forum is a collection of functioning states with special Pacific values , all deserving equal respect and status . <p> And given climate change was affirmed , reaffirmed , recognised , asserted and respected as the main threat it was rather ironic the various delegations @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ the greenhouse gases causing all the climate change in the first place . <p> Having Nauru as the venue made it worse because aircraft had to land and then fly away again because the airfield could not accommodate many aircraft . <p> So that was the 49th Pacific Islands Forum and before the 50th it might be time for a rethink of how they are run and what they should achieve . <p> For instance , if climate change is the biggest threat to the Pacific , maybe the leaders should all get to Australia or New Zealand on regular flights and share transport . Maybe meetings should be held in one central place capable of hosting large groups to avoid wasting more resources . <p> Tuvalu , about a quarter of the size of Waiheke Island , with a population of about 10,000 , is the next venue . <p> And maybe the 50th forum could recognise and reaffirm some hard truths about most Pacific Island nations , many of which are essentially failed tinpot states surviving on foreign aid , remittances from abroad and favours . We do not want our military involved in peace-keeping and nation-building all over the Pacific and we do n't want refugees from conflicts or economic distress . <p> We also do n't want our Pacific friends getting too cosy with countries who might not have our best interests at heart .
