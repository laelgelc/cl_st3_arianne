<h> Will electric locomotives on main trunk line be scrapped ? <p> Roger Blakeley : The decision by KiwiRail in December 2016 to switch from electric to all diesel locomotives on the North Island Main Trunk ( NIMT ) could have profound implications for New Zealand . <p> OPINION : Before the election , on August 22 , 2017 , Michael Wood , Labour spokesperson for transport , announced : " Today I have written to the KiwiRail CEO to provide fair notice that if elected , a Labour Government will issue a clear letter of expectation to cease work on de-electrification . " <p> The Labour-led Government has now been in power for 10 months , but there has been no announcement of whether this election promise will be honoured . <p> KiwiRail made a decision in December 2016 to switch from electric to diesel locomotives on the North Island Main Trunk ( NIMT ) from Te Rapa , north of Hamilton , to Palmerston North . This could have profound implications for New Zealand . <p> It is in direct conflict with the Government 's climate change mitigation target of reducing carbon emissions to net zero by @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ @ that " climate change is the nuclear-free issue of my generation " . Also , it could jeopardise any future opportunity to electrify the whole NIMT . <p> In a briefing note to the minister of state owned enterprises dated March 21 , 2018 , KiwiRail chief executive Peter Reidy advised that no further correspondence had been received regarding Wood 's " cease and desist " notice on the de-electrification programme . <p> He said , " KiwiRail continues its work to decommission the electric locomotive fleet by 31 March 2019 . " <p> There is still time for the Government to honour Labour 's commitment . Under current planning , the electric locomotives will not be decommissioned until March 2019 . This still allows several months for the Government to direct KiwiRail to rescind its decision . <p> There has been remarkably little public debate for such an important decision . It is worth analysing whether KiwiRail 's decision to scrap the electric locomotives on the NIMT stacks up . <p> The lowest-cost option would be refurbishing the 17 electric locomotives currently used on the NIMT . It would require financing of only $4m a year over three years . <p> * What are the greenhouse gas emissions of the two options ? <p> KiwiRail says that new diesel locomotives would be more reliable and would save time , and therefore they would be able to attract freight by rail instead of by road , which would reduce carbon emissions from diesel trucks . <p> This is a fallacious argument , because the same reliability , time-saving and mode-share shift of freight from road to rail would be achieved by refurbished electric locomotives travelling between Te Rapa and Palmerston North . <p> KiwiRail will burn an extra eight million litres of diesel per year using diesel locomotives on the electrified section of the NIMT , and add 12,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide to the atmosphere each year . The decision involves keeping the lines maintained , inspected and energised , at an estimated cost of $2m-$3m per year . <p> However , this maintenance funding could be at risk from future budget cuts , which could then jeopardise any future opportunity to electrify the whole NIMT . <p> In summary , refurbishment of the existing 17 electric locomotives is the option with the lowest cost , best value , lowest greenhouse gas emissions , and that which best secures the option for any future electrification of the whole NIMT . <p> Dr Roger Blakeley is a former Secretary for the Environment , and Distinguished Fellow , Engineering New Zealand .
